Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The National Tax Service and licensed Korean banks generally reject virtual offices and commercial mailboxes as insufficient for a manufacturing corporate entity. A Yuhan Hoesa engaged in electric cargo e-bike manufacturing must maintain a physical registered office in Korea that is either owned or leased under a registered lease agreement, with a visible corporate sign, operational telephone line, and actual capability to receive original notices and service of process. Banks performing corporate account onboarding will conduct an on-site visit and will decline account opening if the address is a virtual office or public mailbox. In addition, the local municipal tax office will treat a virtual office as a sham commercial substance and may cancel the Business Registration Certificate, impose administrative fines, and assess corporate income tax on penalties. Accordingly, virtual office and commercial mailbox arrangements are not permissible for this industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None, in the sense that the Korean Commercial Act does not prescribe any statutory minimum contributed capital for a Yuhan Hoesa; however, the Articles of Incorporation must state the total contributed capital, and every contribution, whether cash or in kind, must be fully paid or delivered before incorporation registration. The capital subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked capital deposit account at a licensed commercial bank and remain blocked until the court registry accepts the incorporation, after which they may be released to the operating account; inbound foreign subscriptions must be credited through a foreign exchange bank and reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), with the principal electronic administrative platform being the HomeTax system at www.hometax.go.kr. Customs and export clearance matters are processed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal at portal.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A newly formed Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC must apply for business registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its statutory place of business. The application is filed on HomeTax using the Application for Business Registration; the NTS then issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) which functions simultaneously as the corporate Tax Identification Number and the VAT registration number. Required supporting documents include the articles of incorporation, certificate of capital subscription or paid-in capital, representative identification, and evidence of local physical business premises. For a foreign non-resident entity that has not yet formed a Korean local subsidiary but begins carrying on a taxable Korean business through a branch or domestic place of business, the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative, submit an apostilled or notarized certificate of foreign incorporation, its constitutional documents, evidence of a Korean place of business, and a power of attorney authorizing the domestic tax representative, after which the NTS will issue the same BRN/TIN. The registration covers VAT, corporate income tax filing, electronic tax invoice issuance, and employee withholding withholding registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box pass-through election under Korean domestic tax law; Yuhan Hoesa LLC is mandatorily taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income as a domestic corporation. A qualifying domestic parent-subsidiary group may voluntarily elect the Korean Consolidated Corporate Income Tax Return System under the Corporate Tax Act, but this is an income-pooling election and does not change the separate entity classification. No elective hybrid mismatch treatment is available; the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules are implemented mandatorily through the Adjustment of International Trade Tax Act and apply without elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover exemption below which VAT registration is unavailable. Any business operator that starts a trade, business, or revenue-generating activity in Korea must register with the NTS within 20 days of the business commencement date. A simplified taxable person regime may apply to individual businesses whose annual taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory; a corporate Yuhan Hoesa LLC manufacturing electric cargo bikes is a standard taxable person and must register from the first day of business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, such as electric cargo bikes and step-through scooters, qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically delivered to a foreign buyer under a Korea Customs Service export declaration and the exporter retains commercial contracts, invoices, loading/transport documents, customs clearance records, and foreign exchange receipt evidence. Qualifying offshore service supplies and digital services supplied to non-resident enterprises also qualify for zero-rating when the services are consumed or effectively used outside Korea, the foreign payor is a non-resident, and the exporter retains documentary proof of the export nature of the supply including contract, service delivery records, and inbound foreign-exchange evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration (export B/Entry) evidencing clearance from Korean customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Korea to the foreign destination with destination country and consignee details
  • Commercial invoice and packing list fully matching the customs declaration quantity, unit values, and product classification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign exchange bank purchase/proceeds certificate confirming export proceeds have been repatriated through a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Underlying export sales contract or binding purchase order establishing the international sale and foreign purchaser liability

Korean VAT law mandates electronic tax invoicing for all VAT-registered business-to-business supplies. Suppliers must issue an electronic tax invoice by the day following the supply date and transmit the invoice data to the NTS through HomeTax or through an approved electronic tax invoice Application Service Provider (ASP) using certified EDI or API interfaces. The e-tax invoice must contain the supplier and purchaser Business Registration Numbers, supply date, taxable value, VAT amount, and the applicable zero-rating or exemption code. For business-to-consumer cash transactions, the provider must likewise issue and transmit electronic cash receipt data to the NTS in near real time. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance government digital stamping system, but the NTS performs ex-post automated risk matching between electronic tax invoices, VAT returns, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange transaction data; exporters should ensure precise zero-rating reason codes are recorded on each electronic tax invoice.

Where the NTS determines that zero-rating was claimed on unsupported, false, or manipulated export documentation, it will issue a deficiency assessment re-imposing VAT at the standard 10% rate together with the statutory additional tax for unreported or under-reported VAT, which may range from 10% to 40% of the unpaid tax depending on intentionality and concealment, plus statutory interest. Penalties may also be imposed for failure to issue or transmit electronic tax invoices and for false export declarations. Criminal liability may be pursued under the Act on Punishment of Tax Evaders, exposing the entity, its directors, and the appointed Korean tax representative to imprisonment for up to three years or criminal fines of up to KRW 30,000,000. The NTS may retrospectively claw back VAT refunds, discontinue zero-rating treatment, and refer documented customs offenses to the Korea Customs Service for parallel administrative or criminal enforcement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating foreign exchange market, but capital account convertibility is conditional rather than fully open. Current account transactions are largely liberalized, allowing export/import payments, trade credits, and service fees to flow with minimal friction. Capital account movements, however, require reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance and Bank of Korea under designated purpose codes. Non-resident shareholders may convert contributed foreign currency into KRW for operational payments, but profit remittance abroad is subject to a withholding tax clearance certificate issued by the National Tax Service, along with evidence of retained earnings and capital verification. There is no mandatory surrender requirement; exporters may retain foreign exchange earnings in a licensed foreign exchange bank account. However, all transactions exceeding USD 5,000 (or equivalent) must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with specific purpose codes. The Financial Services Commission supervises designated dealing banks; each bank must verify the underlying economic reality to prevent round-tripping. Practical hurdles include strict documentation for dividend distribution, and a bank-issued annual proof of tax compliance before remittance. Currency convertibility is therefore high for current account transactions, moderate for FDI and portfolio inflows, and heavily conditionally controlled for speculative flows or rapid capital withdrawal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory collection period: export proceeds must be received within 3 years from the date of the export declaration unless the underlying trade contract specifies a shorter term. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — there is no legal requirement to convert foreign currency receipts into KRW. Export receipts exceeding USD 5,000 must be reported to the authorized dealing bank, and any foreign currency funds held in domestic accounts are subject to annual balance-of-payments reporting to the Bank of Korea.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (principal authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act), with administrative enforcement by Korea Customs Service and supervision by the Financial Services Commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for foreign digital EMI platforms structured as unlicensed e-money institutions. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, any company providing KRW-denominated corporate payment and collection services must obtain a domestic license or partner with a licensed Korean bank. Global EMI platforms such as Wise only offer multi-currency payout corridors and do not hold Korean Banking Authority approval to service a Korean Yuhan Hoesa's full banking needs. As a result, the treasury blueprint relies exclusively on regulated tier-1 Korean commercial banks for capital escrow, foreign currency pricing, trade settlement, and FX hedging. No fintech EMI is able to replace the statutory foreign exchange reporting and capital verification obligations imposed by Korean regulators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a prior foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, filed with either KOTRA Invest Korea or a fully licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. After the report is approved, the non-resident shareholder remits funds via SWIFT MT103 to the company's capital escrow account at a designated tier-1 bank. Each payment must include the Korean foreign exchange purpose code for equity participation, and the bank will issue a certificate of capital introduction. Funds must be converted into KRW only after the statutory capital verification is completed by the receiving bank. The Korean bank will also report to the Bank of Korea's balance-of-payments system, and will retain the foreign investment registration documents, the remittance receipt, and the corporate seal registry for the compliance file.Trade settlements for electric cargo e-bike purchases are executed through SWIFT MT103 for telephonic transfers and advance payments, SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit covering commercial imports and exports, and documentary collection via D/P or D/A through the banks' correspondent clearing networks. When paying foreign suppliers, the Korean company must present a valid tax invoice, an original import declaration from Korea Customs, and a specified trade purpose code. Cross-border disbursements are cleared through the Korean bank's foreign currency clearing network using USD, EUR, or CNY correspondent accounts, subject to Bank of Korea transaction filing for every payment above USD 5,000. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for foreign digital EMI platforms structured as unlicensed e-money institutions. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, any company providing KRW-denominated corporate payment and collection services must obtain a domestic license or partner with a licensed Korean bank. Global EMI platforms such as Wise only offer multi-currency payout corridors and do not hold Korean Banking Authority approval to service a Korean Yuhan Hoesa's full banking needs. As a result, the treasury blueprint relies exclusively on regulated tier-1 Korean commercial banks for capital escrow, foreign currency pricing, trade settlement, and FX hedging. No fintech EMI is able to replace the statutory foreign exchange reporting and capital verification obligations imposed by Korean regulators.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal corporate income tax rate applied to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; progressive rates of 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, and 21% up to KRW 300 billion

Aggregate 22% (20% corporate income tax withholding on the dividend payment to a non-resident, plus 2% local income tax surtax) where no treaty reduction applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in annual aggregate international related-party transactions for Local File; KRW 100 billion in annual aggregate international related-party transactions for Master File; transaction-level reporting threshold is set by the LCITA Enforcement Decree and currently applies to a company whose total international related-party transaction amount reaches KRW 50 billion, with both the Local and Master Files due by the CIT return date or evidence date.

Economic substance scope: Korea is not a Gulf Cooperation Council ESR jurisdiction, so Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE/Bermuda/BVI sense do not apply. However, Korean tax courts apply an equivalent economic-substance and substance-over-form doctrine. For treaty benefits and transfer pricing defenses, the Korean LLC must prove that its core income-generating activities (manufacturing, inventory risk, procurement, warranty, and strategic management) are performed in Korea with real employees, physical facilities, and decision-making substance. A shell LLC without CIGA in Korea will lose DTA protection and be exposed to recharacterization of all related-party contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Omission of Income

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service assesses the omitted corporate income tax, adds 20% fraud penalty under the Corporate Income Tax Act penalty rules, applies monthly 1.2% delinquency surcharge, and refers the case to the public prosecutor. Conviction under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a criminal fine of at least 1 but not more than 5 times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Under Article 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, which serves as Korea's tax piercing-the-corporate-veil rule, a de facto or de jure director who controlled the evasion is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and surcharges. The director is subject to asset attachment and departure restriction (blacklisting/travel ban) when unpaid delinquent tax exceeds KRW 50 million. The personal criminal exposure is the same imprisonment term; restitution to the Treasury is ordered before other debts.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the domestic anti-treaty-shopping provision in the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and re-source the dividends as Korean-source income subject to full 22% aggregate withholding, plus additional penalties of 10%-20% for under-withheld tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act; criminal prosecution is possible for false declaration on the withholding relief form.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the distribution without a valid relief form or with fraudulent TRC documents are personally liable for the shortfall as withholding agents, and can be pursued for the entire unreported tax. The director may be blacklisted (departure ban) and, for deliberate false documentation, prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act with imprisonment up to 5 years, plus parallel liability for Korean bank accounts and foreign assets under court seizure orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting to Tax Haven Affiliate

Statutory sanction: Under Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service recomputes the arm's length price and assesses the resulting understated corporate tax. Penalties include a 20% negligent/unfaithful omission penalty (or 20% fraud penalty), a 10%-20% documentation failure penalty, and monthly delinquency surcharges. The tax authority may also disallow related-party royalty deductions under anti-abuse rules embedded in Korean law and impose an additional 10% penalty on the denied deduction.

Director personal liability: The representative director who signed the intercompany pricing agreements is not protected by the fiction of separate personality: if the transfer pricing adjustment demonstrates deliberate fraud, the director is jointly liable under Article 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes for the adjusted tax and penalties. Departure restrictions are imposed for delinquent tax over KRW 50 million; personal civil asset restitution and criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years attach when the tax authority proves criminal intent to cause tax loss.

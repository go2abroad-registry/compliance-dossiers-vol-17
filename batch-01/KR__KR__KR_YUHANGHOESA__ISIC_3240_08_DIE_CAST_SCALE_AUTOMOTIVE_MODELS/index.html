Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa, and a nominal capital contribution is legally permissible. However, the full amount of the issued contribution units must be paid before the establishment registration is filed, and subscription monies must be deposited into a dedicated restricted capital-payment custody account at a licensed commercial bank in the name of the company under formation. Withdrawal from that account is prohibited prior to the issuance of the Certificate of Registered Matters; if incorporation fails, the deposited funds are returned to the subscribers. There is no separate statutory escrow blocking period beyond this pre-registration restriction. Practically, for a manufacturing enterprise conducting zinc die-casting and tampo printing, a paid-up capital of not less than KRW 50,000,000 is recommended to demonstrate financial substance to banks, landlords, fire-safety inspectors, and the factory registration authority.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain the Certificate of Completion of Fire-Fighting Systems from the local fire station under the Act on Fire Prevention and Fire-Fighting Installations before operations, confirming fire extinguishers, emergency exits, sprinklers, and hazardous-materials storage for flammable inks and solvents. The municipal building authority must issue the building-use approval under the Building Act, verifying structural load capacity for the die-casting machines, furnaces, and warehouse racking. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Employment and Labor, the employer must file a workplace commencement report, appoint a safety manager, conduct a risk assessment covering zinc fume exposure, machine guarding, emergency stops, local exhaust ventilation, chemical SDS communication, and provide appropriate personal protective equipment and mandatory health surveillance for workers exposed to lead-containing alloys.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic tax portal; Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic declaration system for customs and export control matters

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident shareholder establishing a Yuhan Hoesa must first complete legal incorporation registration at the Commercial Court Registry. The corporation is then registered with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the principal domestic place of business through an Application for Business Registration. The application must include the articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, proof of the domestic business premises (lease agreement or similar evidence), the corporate seal, identification documents of the legal representative, a certified copy of the bank account certificate for the domestic bank account, and, where applicable, a power of attorney appointing a local tax agent. Upon completion, the District Tax Office issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the statutory 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is simultaneously the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the Value Added Tax (VAT) registration number for the Yuhan Hoesa. A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business has no standalone VAT registration obligation solely by reason of foreign existence; any VAT registration or compliance duty arises only if the foreign entity undertakes taxable domestic supply or receives Korean-sourced income attributable to a Korean permanent establishment. The 10-digit business registration number remains the single national TIN/VAT ID for all interactions with NTS, HomeTax, and Korea Customs Service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean Corporate Tax Act does not provide a check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch classification election for a domestic Yuhan Hoesa. A Yuhan Hoesa is treated as a resident corporation taxable at entity level on worldwide income. The only optional grouping regime is group taxation of wholly owned domestic subsidiaries through a consolidated corporate income tax election, which is a special filing mechanism and does not alter entity classification or check-the-box transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the date of business commencement. There is no KRW-denominated exemption threshold for a Yuhan Hoesa corporation, and every taxable supply by such a resident corporation is subject to VAT irrespective of annual revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exported goods when the Yuhan Hoesa supplies tangible goods to a foreign buyer, the goods are removed from the Republic of Korea under an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, and the exporter is the Korean VAT-registered supplier. Qualifying offshore services supplied to a foreign recipient outside Korea likewise qualify, provided the recipient is not a Korean resident and the service is actually consumed abroad. The zero-rating claim requires the exporter to maintain documentary proof of physical export, foreign exchange receipt, and delivery to the foreign customer. If the export does not occur or the documentation is inadequate, the supply is reclassified as taxable and subject to 10% VAT.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Export Declaration) accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (carrier-issued evidence of overseas despatch)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Foreign Buyer's Purchase Order or Sales Contract evidencing the offshore transaction

Every taxable supply by a Yuhan Hoesa must be documented through the National Tax Service HomeTax electronic tax invoice system; paper tax invoices are not permitted for corporate VAT registrants. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax API by the day following issuance, and the NTS issues a unique electronic tax invoice serial number that provides anti-fraud integrity and clearance confirmation. Export-related zero-rated transactions are cross-checked against the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration database. There is no separate blockchain stamping or real-time physical stamp requirement in Korea, but the electronic tax invoice submission obligation operates as the mandatory fiscal clearance mechanism.

The National Tax Service may scrutinize zero-rated exports through cross-verification of HomeTax VAT returns, Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS declarations, and bank foreign exchange records. If the exporter fails to substantiate the zero-rating with the required export declaration, transportation documents, and foreign-currency repatriation evidence, the NTS will re-assess output VAT at 10% on the previously zero-rated supply, claw back any input VAT refunded against the export, and impose additional tax for unfaithful returns or unjustified refunds. False or fraudulent export declarations and forged remittance certificates may trigger criminal penalties under the Framework Act for National Taxes and the Customs Act, including imprisonment and monetary fines, and the taxpayer remains liable to retain the documentation for the statutory record-keeping period for audit and clawback purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, trade in services, short-term bank credit, and routine operational settlements, subject to submission of relevant transaction documentation to a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are fundamentally liberalized but remain subject to a declaratory and reporting regime rather than a license-and-approval system. A foreign-invested company may freely convert inward foreign exchange into KRW for capital expenses, and dividends may be remitted abroad through a domestic bank once audited financial statements and tax clearance documents are presented. The key practical hurdles revolve around documentation: the corporate account must be established with a real-name corporate certificate, the beneficial owners must be identified under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, and every outward remittance above the exempt threshold must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) through a bank. There is no centralised capital repatriation tax in Korea, but withholding tax on dividends is imposed at the rate specified in the relevant tax treaty. The KRW is not an offshore deliverable currency for settlement outside Korea, so cross-border liquidity must be managed through onshore KRW accounts or converted into USD or EUR for international clearing. Multi-currency accounts can be held, but the foreign currency stay period and the requirement of exporting the proceeds to a domestic foreign exchange bank within statutory deadlines govern trade flows. The overall regime is classified as a relatively open system with granular post-event reporting rather than an exchange-controlled surrender system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be collected through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of export declaration, unless a longer period is approved for trade-related reasons. There is no mandatory conversion of export foreign currency to KRW at a fixed ratio; export proceeds may be retained in a duly declared Foreign Currency Deposit Account. However, 100% of such export receipts must be settled through the banking system and reported to FEIS, and any disposition outside the permitted purpose must be pre-approved. For tax and VAT purposes, an export settlement certificate issued by the bank is required to support export-related tax returns.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority jointly administering foreign exchange policy with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium with legal constraints. South Korea does not license offshore fintech EMIs as authorised banks for corporate account opening and KRW clearing. An offshore digital EMI cannot hold a domestic KRW account with BOK settlement privileges, cannot issue local account numbers for inbound supplier payments, and is not a recognised reporting entity under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Such EMIs may be used only for ancillary payment facilitation outside Korea, but they cannot replace a domestic tier-1 commercial bank for capital escrow, export proceeds collection, or corporate treasury operations. For a die-cast model exporter focusing on high-value cross-border trade, reliance on fintech EMIs would create FEIS reporting gaps and unacceptable AML friction in the Korean banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital increases must be routed through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank using the designated purpose code for foreign direct investment. Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the recapitalization is reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or delegated institution, and funds are typically received in USD, EUR, or KRW through SWIFT MT103. After the wire arrives, the bank validates the source of funds, issues an inbound capital certificate, and credits the Capital Escrow Account pending registration of the foreign investment. For a non-registered foreign-owned LLC, the injection may instead be structured as an overseas loan or trade-related advance, requiring a loan report to FEIS and calculation of foreign exchange exposure. Capital verification is performed by the resident bank branch against the signed wire instruction, investor resolution, and final beneficial ownership register, after which funds may be converted to KRW and transferred to a Basic Operational Currency Account.Outbound settlements for die-cast model production, packaging, tooling, and raw material imports should primarily use SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices under USD or EUR clearing rails. For larger mould and tooling contracts, commercial letters of credit are recommended to protect the Korean buyer; the issuing bank will transmit an operative MT700 or MT720, and the beneficiary will present shipping, quality, and invoice documents under UCP 600. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum marginal corporate income tax rate effective for fiscal years beginning after 1 January 2023; progressive brackets are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 21% over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% over KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Korean dividend withholding tax is 20% national income tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge, giving an all-in domestic statutory rate of 22%; reduced DTA rates apply only after treaty relief approval.

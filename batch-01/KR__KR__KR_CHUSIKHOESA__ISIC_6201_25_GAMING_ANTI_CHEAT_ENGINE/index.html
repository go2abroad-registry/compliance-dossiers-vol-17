Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to an ordinary Chusik Hoesa under Article 329 of the Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total authorized shares and per-share par value, and par value may not be less than KRW 100 per share. The entire subscription price must be paid before registration and deposited in a blocked stock subscription account in the name of the company-in-formation or promoter at a Korean commercial bank; the deposit remains blocked until the bank receives the court-issued certificate of incorporation registration. The deposit certificate must be filed with the registry. In-kind contributions require a court-appointed inspector's report unless all promoters unanimously request exemption in notarized articles. Foreign investors must first report the investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and remit funds through a Korean foreign exchange bank.

E-government filing portal

For a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) with potential foreign non-resident ownership, the company must first complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and register the corporation with the court. Within 20 days after commencement of business, the company must file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the place of business, submitting the certificate of corporate registration, articles of incorporation, proof of address of the domestic registered office, director identification documents, and, for foreign investors without a domestic address or place of business, a validly appointed tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is required within 20 days from commencement of business with no turnover threshold for general taxation; however, simplified taxable person status is available for businesses with taxable annual turnover below KRW 80,000,000, and the zero-rating and VAT refund regime remains available for qualifying exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exportation of goods from Korea and exportation of certain services used or consumed overseas qualify for zero-rating under the Korean VAT Act. For the gaming anti-cheat engine software and software-as-a-service supplied to non-resident customers, zero-rating is substantiated when the supply is deemed an export service under the VAT Act Enforcement Decree, the service is genuinely provided to a person outside Korea, the consideration is received in foreign currency, and documentary evidence proves the overseas use or consumption of the digital service. Goods exported must be cleared through Korean customs by filing an export declaration via UNI-PASS and the exporter must retain proof of foreign exchange receipt and logistics movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system (export clearance record showing HS code, value, destination, and exporter).
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract or license agreement with the overseas buyer identifying the anti-cheat engine or digital service, billing terms, and foreign currency settlement condition.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods; for digital services, a service delivery report or server-side electronic evidence showing the supply is received by the overseas customer outside Korea.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Transaction Report issued by a Foreign Exchange Bank in Korea evidencing that the foreign currency export proceeds have actually been repatriated.
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice issued through HomeTax reflecting the export and the zero-rated VAT amount.
  • Where applicable, foreign exchange transaction confirmation from the payment rails (Swift Wire or Stripe payout) detailing the overseas settlement and FX conversion.

All Korean Vat-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the National Tax Service HomeTax system or NTS-certified ERP/API integration. Each electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day following issuance. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory cryptographic stamping requirement; anti-fraud enforcement relies on mandatory e-invoice transmission, real-time data matching with customs export records, and NTS data-analytic verification. Income and VAT filing must be submitted through HomeTax on a semi-annual VAT return basis, while export-related documentation must be retained and be available for electronic audit review.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict post-clearance audit by the National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service. If any required documentation is missing, false, or fails to prove that the goods/services left Korea or the digital service was consumed overseas and that foreign currency was received within the 180-day repatriation deadline, the zero-rating is retrospectively disallowed, output VAT is assessed at the standard 10 percent rate, input VAT credits claimed in connection with the export supply are clawed back, and statutory penalty surcharges are imposed under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, including fabricated export declarations or concealed foreign currency receipts, may additionally result in criminal indictment, fines, and imprisonment, and the NTS may reopen tax years beyond the ordinary five-year limitation period where willful evasion or omission of cross-border receipts is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with robust current-account convertibility. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, current-account payments such as software licensing royalties and gaming platform settlement revenues are freely convertible through registered foreign exchange banks, subject only to filing via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) when transaction values exceed statutory reporting thresholds. Capital-account transactions, notably inbound equity injections into a Chusik Hoesa, are legalized through a mandatory registration and notification mechanism: a foreign investor must deposit capital into an account at a domestic licensed bank after filing a foreign investment notification, and the receiving bank verifies the purpose and source of funds. Profit remittance, including dividends, liquidation proceeds, and royalty payments, are permitted after the submitting entity provides evidence of the original foreign investment notification, payment of Korean corporate taxes, and source-country residency certification if a treaty benefit is claimed. However, Korean won is not freely deliverable offshore as a fully convertible clearing currency; non-resident KRW accounts are tightly managed and all outbound FX conversions require documented real demand. Banks further require compliance with Korean transfer-pricing regulations, which prevent synthetic profit extraction and meaningfully constrain international treasury structures. In practice, conversion hurdles are administrative rather than prohibitive, and a fully compliant foreign-invested entity can access capital-account and current-account convertibility within one to three banking days of filing, provided no AML or tax red flag is triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect exported goods and service revenue within 18 months from the date of export declaration, with an extension to 20 months available for specific products approved by the Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory conversion ratio of foreign currency export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain earned FX in a domestic foreign exchange account. However, the exporter must obtain a confirmation of receipt from an authorized foreign exchange bank within 30 days of collection, and the actual monetary settlement must be made through a licensed Korean bank to satisfy the ‘receipt and settlement’ obligation under current foreign exchange regulations. Failure to collect within the statutory period requires a prior approval or waiver from the competent foreign exchange bank, and the corresponding FEIS reporting record must be updated. This rule is particularly relevant for inbound licensing fees from international gaming platforms that use Stripe or SWIFT wires, since the collected foreign currency must be formally received and settled to the tax-paying Korean entity.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the executing authority for foreign exchange policy delegated under the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, and comparable providers are not licensed to operate as Korean banks and cannot legally maintain KRW settlement accounts, process domestic payments, or receive capital injections on behalf of a Korean resident legal entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only banks that are licensed by the Korean Financial Services Commission can hold customer funds in domestic payment accounts and perform cross-border settlement as principals. An offshore EMI may only serve as an ancillary payable/receivable network to initiate funds transfers; all underlying clearing and conversion must traverse a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank. A Chusik Hoesa holding a gaming anti-cheat middleware business must therefore build its treasury on a direct relationship with a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank. Relying on a fintech EMI for capital subscription or operational disbursements creates serious AML exposure and regulatory risk for the entity and its directors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must first be registered via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) using an authorized Korean commercial bank. The investing party wires funds through SWIFT MT103 using a purpose code that reflects an equity contribution or foreign capital investment as mapped to the Bank of Korea’s Classification of Foreign Exchange Transactions. The funds must arrive at the Korean bank’s designated capital escrow account opened under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, after which the bank issues a capital verification confirmation and files the domestic foreign-investment report with MOEF and BOK. A Korean CPA must subsequently issue a statutory capital verification certificate for the paid-in capital amount. For later profit repatriation, the entity establishes an operational current account and submits board resolutions, executed financial statements, tax clearances, and evidence of the original capital registration to request dividend remittance. No conversion of foreign currency into KRW is permitted without first booking an inbound exchange counterparty with the same Korean bank.For operational trade payments, the Korean entity should use SWIFT MT103 for direct invoice settlements with foreign suppliers or customers, with purpose codes aligned to the underlying service or royalty stream. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in the preceding business year triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File preparation is also required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding approximately KRW 1 trillion and cross-border related-party transactions are present.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulation as in certain offshore jurisdictions, but the National Tax Service applies a substance-over-form doctrine, anti-conduit principles, and Controlled Foreign Corporation rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean anti-cheat engine company must demonstrate actual personnel, decision-making, server activity, R&D execution, and core income-generating activity in Korea; otherwise, the tax authority may recharacterize royalty flows, deny treaty benefits, and treat offshore IP holding companies as sham entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Understatement of Digital Asset and Licensing Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment for Tax Evasion Act Article 9, persons who deliberately conceal taxable income are subject to imprisonment of up to 10 years or a fine not exceeding twice the amount of tax evaded; the Corporate Tax Act also imposes a 20% under-reporting penalty and 0.03% daily delay surcharge on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The representative director may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax through piercing the corporate veil when the intermediary structure is found to be fictitious. The National Tax Service may blacklist the director, issue a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, seize personal assets, and the director may face criminal imprisonment if fraudulent intent is established.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, the Korean tax authority may adjust the taxable income by using the arm's length price and impose a transfer pricing penalty of 20% of the understated corporate income tax, plus daily delay surcharge for tax underpayment; failure to submit documentation may also trigger documentation penalties and extend the assessment statute of limitations.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the intercompany pricing and directed the transfer pricing policy may be held personally responsible if the pricing arrangement was willfully manipulated. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to 10 years, and civil restitution of unpaid tax plus interest can be enforced directly against the personal assets of the responsible director upon piercing the corporate veil.
Treaty Abuse, Conduit Structures, and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act and the framework for prevention of treaty abuse, the National Tax Service will deny treaty benefits, re-source the payment to the actual beneficial owner, and impose the full 22% statutory withholding tax on the gross remittance together with under-withholding penalties and daily delay surcharge. Director personal liability: Directors who signed or directed the fraudulent treaty relief application are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, penalties, and interest. Korean courts may disregard the shareholder limited liability structure, blacklist the directors from international travel, order repatriation of diverted assets, and imprisonment of directors may be imposed where the conduit arrangement is found to be a deliberate tax evasion scheme.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law sets no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; a token capital such as KRW 10,000 may be stated. However, all subscribed capital must be fully deposited into a blocked escrow or subscription deposit account in the name of the incorporator or the company formation agent before registration, and the deposit must be unlocked only after the company is registered. No minimum escrow period applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) – online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa at the Korean Commercial Registration, the entity must first obtain a Corporate Registration Number from the Registry. Next, within 20 days after incorporation, the entity must file a Business Registration Application with the local District Tax Office. The NTS then issues a Business Registration Number (TIN) which serves as the VAT ID, corporate income tax registration, and employer withholding number. For foreign non-resident shareholders or directors, the company must appoint a domestic tax representative and file the application electronically through HomeTax or in person. The Business Registration Number is immediately valid for customs exports.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for corporations (mandatory registration upon incorporation); for sole proprietors or unincorporated entities, the threshold is KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover. For this Chusik Hoesa engaged in bonded trucking, registration is mandatory regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, export of goods and services directly related to export, including inland transportation services for export cargo such as bonded road feeder services to international airports, are zero-rated. Zero-rating applies automatically to the supply if the exporter holds export evidence and receives payment in foreign currency. The zero-rate is claimed through the regular VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration Certificate authenticated by Korea Customs Service
  • Export invoice, packing list, and combined transport bill of lading or air waybill covering the bonded trucking movement from warehouse to Port of Loading
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Korean licensed bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

All VAT-taxable businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) via HomeTax for each supply to another business. The e-Tax Invoice data is transmitted to the NTS in real time through the HomeTax API, which assigns a unique electronic reference number for anti-fraud clearance. NTS automatically cross-references the invoice against customs declarations and banking settlement data. No physical stamp is required; the NTS-issued e-invoice reference number serves as the statutory digital stamp. Filers must retain and submit the e-invoice data on the VAT return due by the 25th of the following month.

If the NTS or Korea Customs Service disallows the zero-rating due to missing documentation or fraudulent claims, the full 10% VAT is assessed on the supply. The Basic National Tax Act imposes a 40% penalty of the under-reported VAT, a monthly overdue penalty of 0.3% per day, and criminal fines up to 3 times the tax amount in cases of intentional false zero-rating. The statutory clawback limitation is 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea applies a free-but-regulated convertibility regime. Current-account transactions, trade settlements, and profit remittances are generally allowed without prior central bank permission as long as they are routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported to the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Report System. Capital-account entries are more heavily supervised. An inbound equity contribution requires Foreign Direct Investment notification to the designated bank or Invest Korea FIPS system, followed by the opening of a foreign-invested enterprise account. Cross-border loans, derivative transactions, and offshore security arrangements require declarations or, in certain cases, approval by the MOEF or BOK. There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds to Korean won, and foreign currency may be kept in a domestic foreign currency deposit account. However, the won is deliverable only inside the Korean foreign exchange market, and non-resident speculative won access is tightly limited. Profit remittances are achievable but require formal dividend resolution, audited financial statements, confirmation of withholding tax settlement, and a Foreign Exchange Transaction Report at the remitting bank. Any transaction that materially diverges from the declared bonded trucking business scope will be treated as an unauthorised capital movement and blocked by the bank's compliance system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds from Korean export services, including bonded trucking revenue for air cargo, must be recovered within 18 months from the date of export declaration and must be received through a domestic foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory surrender ratio; the entity may keep foreign currency in a foreign currency account at the same bank. If collection takes longer than 18 months, the exporter must submit a prior or ex-post explanation and obtain approval from the Minister of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea through the account bank. All receipt and settlement actions must be captured in the Foreign Exchange Transaction Report, and the bank will monitor collection dates to ensure no unreported foreign exchange holding.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the monetary and foreign exchange authority, operating in tandem with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), and administered in practice by locally licensed Korean foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound shareholder equity injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank into the Chusik Hoesa's FDI designated account at a Korean foreign exchange bank. Before receipt, the foreign investor must complete the Foreign Investment Notice through Invest Korea's FIPS system or through the account bank; the bank will create a Foreign Direct Investment registration record and issue an inward capital receipt certificate. The remittance message must contain the correct SWIFT BIC of the Korean bank, the Chusik Hoesa corporate registration number, and clear reference to foreign investment notification number and purpose code G060 for equity capital acquisition. Once credited, the bank may require the entity to keep the funds in the foreign exchange escrow account until capital verification is complete. Capital verification is obtained by matching the bank balance certificate, the foreign investment notice, the share issue resolution, and judicial scrivener or auditor confirmation of paid-in capital. No fintech EMI should be used for this route because foreign capital received by an unauthorised institution will be treated as only a payment, not recognised as FDI for subsequent dividend or repatriation purposes.Outbound operational cash flows are cleared through the domestic Korean won clearing and interbank ACH instant system, using the Korean bank's domestic account and standard ACH Direct debit mandates for fuel, toll, driver payroll, cargo terminal lease, and customs bond fees. Cross-border trade disbursements to overseas logistics partners, airport cargo operators, and foreign trucking insurance carriers are made via SWIFT MT103 standard corporate transfers, and where the beneficiary requires documentary assurance, by Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) or Documentary Collections (DP/DA) routed through the same Tier-1 Korean bank. All outbound payments must carry the underlying commercial invoice, contract, and transport document reference, and must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Report System. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge, producing an aggregate top marginal rate of 26.4%; graduated national brackets apply as 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, 21% up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporation is 20% national tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge, making the aggregate statutory rate 22%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is triggered when aggregate foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 5,000,000,000 in the fiscal year; a Master File is required for a Korean entity whose consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 and whose foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 5,000,000,000. Documentation must be submitted to the NTS upon request, generally within 45 days of the tax return deadline, and a Country-by-Country Report is separately required for multinational groups with annual consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 2,000,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to the UAE, but the substance-over-form doctrine, business-purpose requirement, and the OECD principal purpose test are fully embedded in Korean tax practice through International Tax Coordination Act GAAR provisions and judicial precedent. A bonded road feeder company must demonstrate real employees, actual operational premises, retained GPS and customs control records, and substantive decision-making authority in Korea over vehicle routing, contracting, revenue generation, and fleet bearing economic risk. If substance is absent, the NTS will disregard intermediary foreign entities, deny DTA benefits, reallocate income to Korea, and apply penalties for understatement of taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: The Korean Punishment of Tax Evaders Act provides criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to three times the evaded tax; the NTS additionally imposes a fraudulent underreporting penalty of 40% of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest, and seizure of the bonded fleet.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Chusik Hoesa is a mere alter ego or a sham entity; directors who directed or knowingly benefited from the false returns are civilly liable for the corporate tax deficiency, surcharges, and interest. The NTS may blacklist the directors as delinquent taxpayers and impose departure restrictions on travel, while criminal conviction can result in imprisonment for up to five years and bar service as a director of any Korean corporation.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Coordination Act, the NTS may impute an arm's-length price, disallow the non-deductible portion of interest, and assess a penalty equal to 20% of the tax understated plus interest. Failure to submit required transfer-pricing documentation is itself subject to an administrative penalty of KRW 30 million per deficiency; customs bonded license renewal can be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare or approve false intercompany pricing can be deemed personally responsible for unremitted tax where the corporate assets are insufficient; the NTS may serve notice on each responsible director as a secondary collector. In egregious cases, civil asset restitution and a departure red flag are imposed, and criminal sanctions for obstructing transfer-pricing audits can extend to imprisonment for up to three years.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under OECD-compliant Korean anti-abuse rules, the NTS will deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the outbound payment as a Korean-source dividend, assess the full 22% withholding tax, impose the 40% fraudulent return penalty, and collect all unpaid tax due from the Korean payer.

Director personal liability: Where directors knowingly used the Korean corporation as a conduit for treaty shopping, the corporate veil may be pierced for the purpose of recovering the unpaid withholding tax and penalties; directors face civil liability for restitution of evaded funds, blacklisting as habitual defaulting taxpayers, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion with imprisonment of up to five years.

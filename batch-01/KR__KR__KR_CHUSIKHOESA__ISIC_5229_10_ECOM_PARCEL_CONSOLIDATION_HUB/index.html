Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal at www.hometax.go.kr; export-related customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity with a domestic Korean establishment, including a Chusik Hoesa (Stock Corporation), must apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN) before or no later than 20 days after commencing business. The application is filed with the district tax office of the National Tax Service having jurisdiction over the registered place of business, using the HomeTax portal or in-person filing, under Article 5 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Supporting documents include the articles of incorporation, foreign certificate of incorporation, proof of the Korean place of business, and evidence of authority of the local tax representative. The BRN combines the corporate TIN and VAT registration number and is used for corporate tax, VAT, e-tax invoice, and customs purposes. A foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business that supplies taxable electronic services or digital content to Korean consumers must complete the simplified foreign business registration through HomeTax and obtain a Foreign Business Registration Number before engaging in taxable supplies. Failure to register may result in ex-officio registration by NTS, inability to issue valid electronic tax invoices, denial of input VAT credit, and administrative penalties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Korea does not have a check-the-box election. A Chusik Hoesa is a resident stock corporation with separate legal personality, automatically classified as an entity-level taxpayer under the Corporate Tax Act on its worldwide income. Elective mechanisms available under Korean law include consolidated corporate tax filing for a parent and 100% owned domestic subsidiaries under the Corporate Tax Act, VAT simplified taxpayer treatment for small businesses with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, and elective special incentives under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. Hybrid mismatch and anti-divergence rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act apply to prevent tax avoidance through inconsistent classification of entities or instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW. There is no general economic turnover threshold for VAT registration. Any person or non-resident entity that makes taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea, including foreign suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers, must register for VAT. Simplified VAT compliance treatment may be elected by a small business with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but the registration obligation itself is not waived.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported tangible goods are zero-rated when they are removed from Korea under an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, with the exporter retaining evidence of shipment and receipt of export proceeds. Qualifying zero-rated services include services supplied to non-residents, international transportation services, and other foreign-exchange-earning services prescribed by the Enforcement Decree. A Korean-established parcel consolidation hub supplying cross-border logistics and freight consolidation services to non-resident e-commerce sellers may qualify for zero-rating only where the place of supply is treated as outside Korea, the direct foreign recipient relationship is documented, and consideration is settled in foreign currency through a traceable banking channel.

  • Export Declaration for goods recorded in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system, showing the export entry number and customs acceptance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a Korean customs port to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Korean entity to the foreign buyer
  • Packing list covering the consolidated parcel contents
  • Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate, inward remittance certificate, or foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Traceable payment evidence from the targeted payment rails, including Airwallex or Local Clearing, linked to the specific export transaction
  • Customs Certificate of Export and Import issued by the Korea Customs Service, where required by the National Tax Service or the foreign exchange bank

Korea mandates electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) for all VAT-registered business-to-business supplies. The supplier must issue the e-tax invoice through the HomeTax system no later than the date of supply and transmit it to the NTS e-tax invoice database by the end of the following day. Korea does not operate a real-time fiscal pre-clearance or mandatory digital stamping model; compliance is instead enforced through the NTS electronic invoice database, mandatory cash receipts for many consumer sales, and comprehensive post-event matching of e-tax invoices, customs declarations, foreign exchange settlement records, and bank data. False, omitted, or late e-tax invoices trigger statutory additional tax and, in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service operate integrated cross-referencing of export declarations, e-tax invoices, customs clearance records, and foreign exchange settlement data to detect false, inflated, or unsupported zero-rating claims. If the zero-rated supply is not substantiated by the required customs and FX documentation, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period without an approved extension from a foreign exchange bank, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating, assess VAT at 10% on the export consideration, impose additional tax penalties for understated liability, and may pursue criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax claims under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The general tax assessment limitation period is five years, extended to up to ten years where the claim involves fraud or deliberate concealment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a managed floating won and a transaction-based foreign exchange control framework rather than a comprehensive surrender regime. Current account transactions, including trade, shipping, and ordinary services, are effectively freely convertible through authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with transaction data reported to the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) for balance-of-payments statistics. Capital account transactions are also generally liberalized, but remain subject to reporting, notification, or license/authorization gates depending on the instrument: cross-border equity injections into a Korean Chusik Hoesa must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or after receipt, while long-term foreign borrowings require a certificate of loan. Profit remittance is legally free, but the paying bank will require the company to evidence tax filing and compliance, such as corporate tax receipts, foreign investor registration certificate, and financial statements, and to use the correct FEIN remittance purpose code for dividends. The Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance may intervene in the FX market through smoothing operations; the BOK also applies macro-prudential limits on bank forward and NDF positions to curb speculative flows. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender; exporters may keep foreign currency in a foreign-currency account or convert to won at commercial banks. For an e-commerce parcel-consolidation hub, the practical hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative: every settlement must be traceable against import and export cargo lists, customs declarations, and underlying logistics contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the enforcement rules of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected through a licensed foreign exchange bank within one year from the date the export declaration is accepted, unless the relevant bank approves an extension based on legitimate trade deferment and the underlying sale contract. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0 percent; South Korea has no statutory surrender requirement, so the exporter may retain foreign currency in a foreign-currency deposit account and convert to Korean won at its own discretion. For the parcel-consolidation business, each shipment must be cross-referenced to the Korea Customs Service declaration and the bank's collection record; if export proceeds remain uncollected beyond the settlement deadline, the company must file a cause report and may suffer administrative penalties or tightening of trade financing facilities.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy regulator and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) supervising licensed banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low, with a feasibility rating of 2 out of 10 for the Korean entity. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex are not licensed banks in South Korea and cannot be used as a primary Korean clearing account. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires resident foreign exchange transactions to be handled by authorized foreign exchange banks; a non-resident EMI cannot independently execute Korean won clearing, issue a domestic account number, or provide statutory FEIN reporting to the Bank of Korea. An overseas EMI may operate only as a marginal payment-feeding service if it enters into a partnership with a licensed Korean bank and the official KYC and FX reporting ankers remain with that Korean bank. The Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act impose licensing requirements for deposit-taking and payment services in Korea, so relying on an offshore EMI for treasury settlement is legally barred for this jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital for the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank in South Korea and declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The company should open a foreign currency deposit account at the designated domestic bank and file the foreign-investment declaration prior to or immediately upon receipt of the wire. The remitter must be the actual shareholder and the SWIFT MT103 must carry the FEIN purpose code for non-resident direct investment and equity subscription; funds coming from an unrelated platform or third-party aggregator will not qualify for capital verification. Upon receipt, the bank issues the foreign-investment report and the capital verification certificate to the Company and the foreign-invested enterprise status is confirmed with KOTRA or the relevant authority. For audit purposes, the dossier should include the bank certificate of foreign currency deposit, the remitter's bank confirmation, board resolution authorizing shares, and valuation evidence if the injection exceeds par value. Transfer of capital to the Korean operating account may occur only after the statutory FDI reporting is complete.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Article 2 of the Korean Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, an independent certified public auditor's mandate is mandatory for: (1) all listed companies on KRX/KOSDAQ and companies that have filed for listing; (2) non-listed stock corporations whose total assets at the close of the preceding fiscal year exceed KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9.2 million); (3) non-listed companies that have issued or are issuing securities in a public offering, or whose Securities Registration Statement is effective, regardless of asset size; (4) companies designated by the Financial Services Commission for public interest or based on a request by the Korea Exchange; and (5) any company that falls within criteria set under Article 2(3) of the Act using revenue of at least KRW 50 billion or shareholder counts of at least 500 (depending on the consolidated financial position). For small non-listed companies below these thresholds, an audit is not mandatory, but a voluntary audit may be triggered by a shareholder request or by the company's own articles of incorporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required when intercompany transactions for goods exceed KRW 5 billion or for services exceed KRW 2 billion in the relevant fiscal year; Master File: required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 25 billion; CbC Report: mandatory for ultimate parents of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion and controlled foreign subsidiaries in multiple jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) annex, but the Courts and the National Tax Service have strong anti-avoidance anti-abuse rules under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA). A parcel-consolidation hub must demonstrate 'real economic function' through: (i) actual employees engaged in warehouse acceptance, sorting, customs clearance, and linehaul injection; (ii) physical premises and equipment in Korea with operating costs proportional to revenues; (iii) pricing decisions and logistics network management exercised by local staff in Korea; and (iv) inventory risk and profit potential being retained above a 10% operating margin threshold to avoid being viewed as a low-risk service provider or conduit. The Korean branch of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines Article 9 and the BEPS Action 5 minimum standard, the National Tax Service will apply the substantive Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis to reject a shell arrangement. For an e-commerce parcel hub, the core value-creating activities include optimization of parcel routing, consolidation algorithms, customs documentation, and final-mile integration; these functions must be compensated at arm's length with a risk-adjusted return on capital and a full accounting of intragroup synergies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Falsifying Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 10(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act (조세범 처벌법), the offender is liable to imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax; additionally, the National Tax Service assesses an additional 40% penalty on the evaded amount and a 20% underreporting surcharge. Repeated criminal prosecutions can lead to imprisonment exceeding 5 years if aggregate evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the false invoices and the internal transfer pricing chart is personally liable under the Civil Act and the Corporate Tax Act (Article 14 of the Act on Trial on Corporate Tax Penalties) for restitution of the evaded tax and penalties, jointly and severally with the corporation. A criminal conviction triggers automatic travel ban and asset freeze; the director's Korea entry visa may be revoked and the individual subjected to an international red notice if involvement in cross-border wire fraud is found. Civil liability may also include payment of unremitted tax plus interest from the director's personal assets, and the director can be disqualified from serving as a corporate officer for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Substance Failure and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the separate legal existence of the Hong Kong intermediate shareholder (piercing the corporate veil) and impose the full 22% dividend withholding tax plus a 30% penalty for mis-statement and abuse of treaty privileges. A formal order to collect unpaid tax from the shareholder and its associate is based on Article 17 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The company's DTA relief is revoked retroactively, and an audit is performed every two years thereafter.

Director personal liability: Each director who knowingly authorized the sham arrangement is jointly liable up to the value of the assets transferred offshore, and may be subject to a 'travel restriction' under the National Tax Collection Act Article 55-2, preventing overseas travel until the underpaid tax and penalties are settled. Civil claims by the tax authority for unjust enrichment can be filed against the board collectively. Criminal conviction under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act can result in imprisonment from 1 to 3 years, and the directors are prohibited from re-entering Korea unless they post personal security.
Anti-Money Laundering / Payment-Rails Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 4(6) and Article 16 of the Proceeds of Crime Act (범죄수익은닉규제법) exposes the company to an administrative fine of up to KRW 100 million; repeated evasion triggers suspension of the payment-rail license and revocation of the customs-handling certification. Criminal penalties under Article 17 of the Act include imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to KRW 150 million, plus confiscation of the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the company's administrative fines if they intentionally caused the omission or failed to establish the mandatory compliance system; this is reinforced by the Enterprise Criminal Liabilities Act (Executive Liability) enabling the prosecutor to indict the directors for willful blindness. A criminal record leads to mandatory blacklisting by the Korean e-commerce customs brokerage authorities, and the director may be subject to a 10-year ban from operating any logistics or financial services. Additionally, under Article 8 of the Financial Information Act, personal restitution of any laundered amount is ordered, and overseas travel is blocked via the tax delinquency blacklist while the investigation is ongoing.

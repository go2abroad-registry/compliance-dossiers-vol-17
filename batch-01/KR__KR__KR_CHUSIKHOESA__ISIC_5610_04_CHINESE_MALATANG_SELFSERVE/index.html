Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea, Act No. 15857, as amended, Book III (Commercial Law), Chapter 4 (Stock Corporation), Articles 288 through 542; in particular Articles 291, 295, 312, 317, 365, 383 and 393 governing subscription, payment, director election, incorporation registration, shareholder meetings, board composition, and executive decision-making authority.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Since the 2012 amendment to the Commercial Act, there is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Chusik Hoesa. The founders must nevertheless subscribe and pay at least one share, and the subscription price must be deposited into a restricted corporate account with a licensed Korean financial institution. The bank issues a Certificate of Subscription Price Retention, which is a mandatory exhibit for court registration. No separate blocked escrow with the registry or government authority is required.

Funding & unlock path: The Company must obtain a Certificate of Completion of Fire Protection Systems, including automatic fire alarms, extinguishers, exit signage, and emergency illumination, together with a periodic Civil Defense self-inspection and fire inspection by the local fire prevention authority for operating a restaurant. A structural load certificate for the kitchen line and cooking hood installations, plus an occupational-safety acknowledgement under the Occupational Safety and Health Act covering hot oil hazard, slip prevention, exhaust cleaning, and employee food-handling hygiene, must be secured before opening to the public.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr; export and customs administration: Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic single-window system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa must apply to the NTS district tax office or through HomeTax using the statutory Business Registration Application within 20 days of commencing business. Supporting submission includes Korean corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, place-of-business proof, foreign shareholder identity documentation, and a domestic tax representative appointment. The NTS issues the Business Registration Number, which is the unified corporate tax ID and VAT registration number; no separate VAT ID is issued. For a foreign non-resident entity with a Korean taxable place of business other than the Chusik Hoesa, the foreign corporation must register as a foreign corporation domestic business place through HomeTax and provide head-office charter, board resolution, tax representative appointment, and permanent-establishment evidence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration exemption threshold; a business making taxable supplies in Korea must register with NTS within 20 days of starting business. A simplified taxpayer classification applies only where annual taxable turnover is no more than KRW 80,000,000, but the registration duty remains mandatory and the Chusik Hoesa would be a general taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying overseas services are zero-rated under Korean VAT law when goods are physically exported from Korea under a Korea Customs Service export declaration, or when services are supplied for use entirely outside Korea and paid in foreign currency. For the master-licensing activity, zero-rating applies to royalty, franchise, and know-how income received from non-resident licensees only if the licensed rights are not used in Korea and the service is not deemed performed in Korea; domestic head-office or management services cannot be zero-rated to the extent they are consumed in Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration filing record issued through UNI-PASS
  • Export declaration confirmation and statistical number of the export clearance
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing inward repatriation of export proceeds
  • License agreement, sales contract, and locational-use evidence for offshore digital, royalty, or licensing services

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all registered businesses and must be transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system or through approved ERP/API channels. The NTS assigns an electronic tax invoice control number and applies digital-signature validation; statutory transmission deadline is the 10th day of the month following the tax invoice issuance period. No physical fiscal stamp is used, but anti-fraud controls are implemented through mandatory electronic transmission, control-number reconciliation, and NTS matching of purchase-side and supply-side VAT data.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims by reconciling export declarations, shipping documents, bank receipts, and VAT returns. Failure to produce the mandatory documentation bundle, failure to receive foreign exchange within the statutory 18-month settlement period, or submission of false invoices entitles the NTS to claw back the 10% VAT on the exported or offshore supply, impose penalty taxes and late-payment surcharges under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes, and refer flagrant cases for criminal tax evasion prosecution. Refund amounts previously granted are recoverable with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised but notification-based convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree. The Korean won is fully convertible for current-account transactions including trade receipts, service fees, royalties, and ordinary dividends, provided the transaction is routed through a registered foreign exchange bank and standard transaction confirmations are filed. Capital-account operations such as inward equity investment, borrowing from non-residents, and acquisition of foreign securities are permissible but require commercial-bank review and, beyond set thresholds, reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or Bank of Korea. There is no blanket surrender quota on export earnings; exporters may retain foreign currency in Foreign Currency Holdings Accounts, although every export receipt must be credited by the foreign exchange bank and an export-settlement report must be filed. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is feasible once a statutory foreign-investment registration certificate is in place and the company's audited net-profits dividend resolution is produced; the remitting bank will verify corporate standing, tax clearance, and beneficial ownership. Practical operational hurdles often arise where capital was injected as supplied equipment or immaterial services rather than bank-transferred currency, because the bank will request the original Foreign Investment Report and matching inbound remittance records for capital verification. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in fast-casual self-serve broth bowl franchising, banks will also test that royalty or master-license income has supporting contracts, recorded invoices, and proof that domestic stores have generated the underlying operational cash flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be received through a registered foreign exchange bank and reported on an export settlement basis; the standard collection deadline is within 18 months of the customs shipment date unless prior approval is obtained for extended payment terms or the counterparty is a financially distressed importer. There is no mandatory surrender ratio: 0% of export earnings must be compulsorily converted to KRW. The exporter may place settlement in a Foreign Currency Holdings Account at a Korean commercial bank, convert at market rate to KRW, or use permitted foreign-currency working funds, provided the transaction is consistent with the originally filed trade declaration. Late collection without approved extension exposes the corporation to administrative sanctions and potential customs surveillance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), exercising foreign-exchange administration by delegation from the Ministry of Economy and Finance and through authorized foreign-exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Fintech EMIs are not locally licensed under the Electronic Financial Business Act unless they hold a Korean payment institution license; foreign EMI accounts cannot lawfully serve as the main company account for a resident corporation, cannot register the corporation's KRW VAT and corporate tax payment rails, and are rarely accepted by counterparty Korean banks for capital injection or royalty flows. They may be used only as an ancillary prepaid card or affiliate-license e-wallet after the primary domestic corporate account is active, provided no statutory payment obligations pass through them. For a low-risk food franchising entity, reliance on an EMI would increase AML friction, delay bank interbank clearing, and jeopardize foreign investment registration. Strong recommendation: open an account with a tier-1 domestic commercial bank and treat EMI as excluded from treasury orchestration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign capital injection follows the Foreign Investment Promotion Act: the non-resident investor first files a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or through a designated Korean foreign exchange bank. For cash investment, remit KRW, USD, or CNY only from the ultimate investor's overseas bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the temporary investment-custody account opened at a domestic bank. The remittance message must state the foreign equity investment purpose code, the project name, and the beneficiary as the Chusik Hoesa. The bank debits the temporary account, verifies no shell-company back-to-back remittance, and issues the foreign investment registration certificate. After the shares are issued and paid-in capital is verified, the funds are transferred to the company's ordinary KRW account and become statutory seed capital. In-kind contribution of equipment, technology, or franchise know-how requires a separate customs and foreign exchange declaration plus an external valuation report, and will not create an FX settlement for liquidity purposes.For recurring master-franchise royalties and licensing fees, use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires from the Korean corporate account to the Chinese licensor or supplier, bearing the correct outbound FX purpose code and a withholding tax certificate. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national top marginal rate plus 2.4% local income tax surcharge (aggregate top combined statutory rate of 26.4%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, composed of 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge, unless a bilateral tax treaty reduces the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions for mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; Country-by-Country Report is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion where the Korean entity is an included constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), Korean tax law enforces a rigorous substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Adjustment of International Taxes Act. The NTS will disregard entities or transactions that are artificial and will test whether the Korean Chusik Hoesa actually performs Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea, including local food sourcing, menu and price setting, inventory management, employment of restaurant staff, maintenance of POS and cashier infrastructure, quality control, and local financial management. A foreign parent or franchise-licensing entity must demonstrate CIGA in its residence jurisdiction, including a physical office, local personnel, board decision-making authority, and real economic risk; if it is merely a shell receiving royalties and dividends, the NTS will deny treaty benefits and reattribute the profits to the Korean operating company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unreported Cash Revenue

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, specifically Articles 3 and 9: criminal tax evasion punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years, extended up to 5 years where the tax evaded exceeds KRW 500 million in a business year, plus a fine of at least 2 times and up to 5 times the evaded tax. The NTS will also impose a 20% negligence penalty for non-filing and a 10% negligence penalty for under-reporting the taxable base, plus a 0.022% per-day late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: The representative director and any director who signed or failed to supervise the false return are personally and severally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes under Article 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Civil enforcement will pierce the corporate shield to attach personal bank accounts, real estate, and automobiles; the affected director is registered on the NTS high-value tax delinquent blacklist, triggering an overseas travel ban until restitution is made, and criminal conviction carries imprisonment terms as described.
Cross-Border Royalty and Interest Withholding Failure / Transfer Pricing Adjustment

Statutory sanction: Under Article 5 of the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, the NTS adjusts the Korean entity's taxable income to the arm's-length price and disallows the deduction for the excess payment. The invoice itself may be rejected as an unlawful expense; the assessed deficiency includes the 22% statutory withholding tax, a 10% unfaithful-return penalty (or 20% penalty for fraudulent omission), and daily late-payment interest. If the payment is recharacterized as a dividend, additional dividend withholding tax at 22% is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who act as withholding agents or who knowingly sign false related-party returns are personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax and penalties under the Basic National Tax Act and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The corporate veil is pierced for collection, allowing seizure of a director's personal property; the director's name is blacklisted with a prohibition on overseas travel, and criminal accessory liability for aiding a cross-border tax offense can result in imprisonment of up to 2 years plus a fine.
Treaty Abuse, Shell-Sharing Structure and AML Inconsistency

Statutory sanction: The NTS applies the Principal Purpose Test under the OECD Multilateral Instrument, the Korean Basic National Tax Act substance-over-form principle, and the Adjustment of International Taxes Act anti-abuse provisions to deny the treaty benefit. The full 22% statutory withholding tax is reassessed on the gross dividend, interest, or royalty, with a negligence penalty of 10-20% and 0.022% daily late-payment interest. Royalties paid into the shell may be recharacterized as constructive dividends, and the income may be reattributed to the Korean entity as a permanent establishment.

Director personal liability: Directors who institute, approve, or knowingly rely on the conduit structure without proper substance are jointly and severally liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiencies; civil asset restitution is recoverable from personal holdings, the responsible director is placed on the NTS name-listed tax delinquent blacklist with a travel ban, and participation in the sham arrangement constitutes criminal aiding and abetting of tax evasion, exposing the director to personal criminal penalties, imprisonment, and fines under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

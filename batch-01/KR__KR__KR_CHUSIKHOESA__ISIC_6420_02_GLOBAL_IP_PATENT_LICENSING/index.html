Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa under current Korean law. The capital amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must, however, be fully paid into a capital subscription deposit account maintained with a commercial bank before the incorporation registration is filed. The bank certificate evidencing full payment must be attached to the registration application. No blocked escrow account is required, but failure to deposit the full subscription price renders the registration subject to refusal. Non-resident shareholders may contribute foreign currency subject to reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

If the company later expands into physical facilities, a Civil Defense fire protection plan and site inspection through the local fire department would be mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that does not maintain a domestic place of business must file a simplified business registration under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value Added Tax Act before starting taxable supply in Korea. The application is submitted to the Seoul District Tax Office or the competent district tax office where a domestic business place is located. Required documents include the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, board resolution, passport of the statutory representative, foreign business license, and a power of attorney if an appointed tax representative is used. The NTS examines the application and issues the Business Registration Certificate on which the 10-digit Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT registration number are printed. Non-residents without a domestic place of business must also appoint a tax representative under Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes unless exempted by treaty; the tax representative bears the obligation to issue tax invoices and file VAT returns.

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General domestic business operators: no monetary threshold; VAT registration is mandatory at the commencement of any taxable supply. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24 of the Value Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated if they are delivered to a foreign place and leave the Korean customs territory. For the IP licensing scope, zero-rating applies to exported services supplied to a non-resident, including patent and trademark royalties, when the place of supply is deemed to be outside Korea and the service is practically consumed outside Korea. The exporter must evidence the foreign licensee's non-resident status, the execution of the license agreement outside Korea, and the actual receipt of royalties from abroad.

  • Export Customs Declaration receipt (Korea Customs Service export declaration filing number and cargo manifest confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (original or e-B/L) or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank (Korean statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate evidencing export proceeds repatriation)
  • IP License / Patent Sub-License Agreement
  • Foreign licensee's business registration or tax-residency certificate

Every VAT-registered business must issue an electronic tax invoice using the HomeTax e-Sangsa system for taxable supplies to other registered business operators; the invoice data must be transmitted to NTS in near real time within statutory deadlines. All business operators must have certified digital certificates for invoice submission. Cash receipts must be reported through the Korea National Tax Service cash-receipt network. Invoices above KRW 100,000 are subject to mandatory electronic issuance; anti-fraud stamping and validation are performed by the HomeTax central system.

If the NTS or Korea Customs Service detects that export zero-rating was claimed without actual exports or without receipt of proceeds within the statutory period, the zero-rated VAT is reversed and VAT at 10% is assessed retroactively, together with interest of 0.02% per day and additional tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent or false tax invoices in connection with export claims are subject to penalties of 40% of the tax amount, and criminal sanctions may apply under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Export documents must be retained for statutory periods for audit examination.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberal, largely convertible current account under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but the capital account remains subject to a report-first, approval-second regime rather than a fully open system. Current-account royalty, license fee, and patent sub-licensing payments are generally free once the underlying commercial contract, tax status, and ownership documentation are in the possession of a designated foreign exchange bank; a prior declaration through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) is required for payments exceeding the reporting threshold, and the bank must verify the transaction purpose and beneficiary. Capital-account inflows for foreign share subscription are permitted after filing a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through a designated domestic bank or Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA); the remitted foreign currency must be received in a foreign exchange bank and either retained in a foreign currency account or converted to KRW through that bank for corporate use. Profit remittance is subject to Korean corporate income tax at the entity level and a dividend withholding tax of 20-22 percent unless a double tax treaty reduces the rate; a tax clearance or withholding payment certificate from the National Tax Service may be demanded before the bank releases a remittance to a non-resident parent or licensor. There are no general forced surrender requirements on foreign currency receipts as of the current regulatory regime, but all export and royalty receipts must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported, and any conversion to KRW must be executed at market rates through a licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not impose a fixed percentage surrender requirement for export or IP licensing proceeds. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds and equivalent service/royalty income must be collected within three years from the export declaration date unless an extension is approved by the competent authority, and the proceeds must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank in the form of proper foreign exchange. The collection is reported electronically via FEIS. The exporter or IP licensor may retain the foreign currency in a foreign currency account or convert it to KRW at a designated bank; mandatory conversion ratios have been abolished, and the critical legal requirement is timely receipt, accurate reporting, and preservation of underlying contracts, invoices, patent licenses, and tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. The Korean anti-money laundering regime and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require a resident corporate entity to execute statutory foreign exchange reporting, export/royalty collection, and capital account transactions through a designated foreign exchange bank licensed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and supervised by the Bank of Korea. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not authorized to maintain Korean resident settlement accounts, issue FEIS declaration references, verify foreign investment capital, or act as the definitive clearing bank for royalty remittances. An offshore EMI may only serve as an ancillary payment instruction channel if the Ultimate settlement is cleared through a licensed Tier-1 Korean bank, but it cannot replace the Korean bank account or the statutory documentation chain for inbound equity and outbound royalty flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the Korean Chusik Hoesa must follow the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route: the foreign investor files a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through a designated domestic bank or KOTRA; the overseas parent or investor remits the share subscription amount by SWIFT to the bank-designated Capital Escrow Account; the bank issues a foreign exchange purchase certificate and remittance receipt; the capital is then converted into KRW or retained in foreign currency; the company completes share issuance, files the foreign-invested company registration with the local government or KOTRA, and the bank verifies the foreign capital by matching the remittance instruction, bank statement, share subscription agreement, and board resolution. The standard foreign exchange purpose code for equity capital inflow under the Bank of Korea classification is used, and all records must be preserved for FEIS reporting and subsequent dividend or profit remittance audits.Outbound royalty and patent license payments are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 messages after the Korean bank has verified the license agreement, tax invoice, withholding tax payment certificate, and any applicable transfer pricing documentation. For embedded trade in goods or software-media distribution, the company may use Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Korean bank to the licensor or software vendor, or Documentary Collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) for smaller, repeated disbursements. All outgoing payments must be reported through FEIS under the appropriate service/royalty purpose code, and no cash, digital-asset, or non-bank settlement channels are acceptable for cross-border patent licensing disbursements.Onshore KRW/USD forward contracts with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank for documented royalty receivables, payables, and dividend remittancesCross-currency interest rate and principal swaps for medium-term offshore funding or bond-linked IP financing structuresFX options, collars, zero-premium floors, and participating forwards on the underlying net currency exposure of the licensing portfolioNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are heavily restricted for onshore Korean residents and are not recommended for the Korean IP HoldCo; they may be considered only where a non-resident counterparty executes the NDF offshore with documented KRW exposure and no onshore settlement

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. The Korean anti-money laundering regime and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require a resident corporate entity to execute statutory foreign exchange reporting, export/royalty collection, and capital account transactions through a designated foreign exchange bank licensed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and supervised by the Bank of Korea. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not authorized to maintain Korean resident settlement accounts, issue FEIS declaration references, verify foreign investment capital, or act as the definitive clearing bank for royalty remittances. An offshore EMI may only serve as an ancillary payment instruction channel if the Ultimate settlement is cleared through a licensed Tier-1 Korean bank, but it cannot replace the Korean bank account or the statutory documentation chain for inbound equity and outbound royalty flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Korean Master File and Local File preparation and submission arise when the aggregate annual amount of cross-border related-party transactions is KRW 10 billion or more (approximately USD 7.5 million); Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more. Even below the documentation thresholds, contemporaneous arm's length evidence must be retained.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but applies the OECD/G20 BEPS and Korea's substance-over-form doctrine via the Framework Act on National Taxes. For the IP HoldCo, the key principles are that ownership must be supported by actual decision-making, control, and performance of the Core Income-Generating Activities (DEMPE functions: development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, exploitation), and that profit attribution must be aligned with those functions and the economically significant activity. A Korean IP HoldCo that is a bare title shell with no personnel, no business premises, no active management of licensing, and no commercial risk will have its IP income recharacterized or disregarded, and treaty benefits can be denied under beneficial ownership and Limitation-on-Benefits rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Artificial Royalty Padding

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Article 3), intentional tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for not more than 2 years or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax; in addition, the National Tax Service will assess a 40% negligence penalty for fraudulent underreporting plus daily late-payment surcharges at 0.022% per day.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the false return are criminally liable, and Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is used as an alter ego for illegality or tax fraud. Directors can be held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties, may face travel bans and asset freezes under the National Tax Collection Act, and may be ordered to personally restore dissipated assets of the company.
Withholding Tax and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act (Article 156) and Framework Act on National Taxes (Article 47-3), the withholding agent becomes personally liable for the unwithheld tax, plus a negligence penalty of 10% of the unwithheld amount (50% for deliberate fraudulent failure) and daily late-payment surcharges; intentional breach may also give rise to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or knowingly failed to withhold can be assessed personally for the unwithheld amount and penalties. Korean authorities will impose asset seizures, travel bans, and financial blacklisting against the directors; if the scheme is proven as intentional fraudulent evasion, directors face imprisonment for up to one year and personal civil liability for the damaged tax collection rights.
Shell-Company Beneficial Ownership Concealment (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Violation of Korea's Real Name Financial Transactions and Confidentiality Act and the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information (Article 19) may trigger criminal sanctions including imprisonment up to 5 years and a fin e up to KRW 30 million; concurrently, the tax authorities will impose the 40% fraud penalty, recharacterize the total royalty income as a constructive dividend to the true owner, and may deny all treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who form or shelter the shell entity face criminal prosecution, blacklisting by the Financial Intelligence Unit and the National Tax Service, travel bans, personal asset seizure, and the civil remedy of veil-piercing allowing creditors or the state to hold directors personally responsible for corporate obligations. They may also be sentenced to imprisonment for up to three years under AML provisions if the concealment was used to launder royalty proceeds.

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered office for a licensed specialty bakery, master-franchise, and central-kitchen operation. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa following the 2012 amendment to the Commercial Act. The promoters must resolve the issued capital amount, which must be fully paid in cash or lawful non-cash contribution to a designated bank account opened in the corporation's name before the incorporation registration. Withdrawal of contributed funds is prohibited until the company is effectively registered, except in the limited statutory cases of rescission or winding-up.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Inspection Certificate issued by the National Fire Agency and the local fire department under the Fire Services Act, confirming installation of fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency exits, and kitchen hood suppression systems; Building Completion Inspection and structural load verification under the Building Act, including a revised architectural record where heavy baking ovens and refrigeration units are installed; and an occupational safety and health clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including a pre-commencement workplace risk assessment, machine guarding verification for dough mixers and sheeters, and a general safety inspection by the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency (KOSHA) for the central kitchen.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; digital portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Korean Chusik Hoesa, the business registration number (BRN) issued by the National Tax Service constitutes the simultaneous corporate TIN and VAT registration number. The domestic legal representative must file an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, within one month after corporate establishment, attaching the commercial registration certificate, articles of incorporation, board resolution appointing the representative, and passport/ID of the appointer. A foreign corporation operating in Korea without a Korean subsidiary must register its domestic place of business as a permanent establishment before beginning taxable activity, providing the foreign charter, proof of domestic office, and a Korean tax representative appointment where the foreign principal has no physical Korean presence. The NTS issues the BRN certificate immediately or generally within five business days, which is then used for VAT invoicing, customs clearance, and corporate income tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No entity classification check-the-box election exists under Korean law; a Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxable as a corporation. Available elections include: (i) tax consolidation election for a domestic parent and its wholly or almost wholly owned Korean subsidiaries under the Corporate Tax Act; (ii) group filing elections for VAT on intra-group supplies where applicable; (iii) foreign tax credit versus deduction election for foreign-source income; and (iv) for cross-border hybrid mismatches, Korea applies mandatory BEPS Action 2 rules with targeted disallowance, not an elective regime. Taxpayers must disclose reportable international transactions under the Tax Adjustment on International Transactions Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to corporate entrepreneurs; any entity conducting taxable business supplies in Korea must register for VAT. The simplified taxpayer ceiling of KRW 80 million per annum applies only to certain individual entrepreneurs, not to Chusik Hoesa corporations. Foreign non-resident suppliers making Korean-source taxable supplies are also required to register regardless of amount, except where the reverse-charge self-assessment mechanism shifts the tax obligation to the Korean recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, zero-rating applies to exported goods and prescribed overseas services. For this bakery and Chinese pastry franchise, packaged food products manufactured in Korea and exported to foreign franchisees qualify for 0% VAT provided the goods are physically shipped out of Korea and supported by a customs export declaration. Franchise royalty fees and certain offshore service fees paid from abroad may also qualify as exported services when the place of supply is outside Korea and the service is actually rendered and consumed outside Korea, subject to Korean place-of-supply rules and transfer pricing documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) apply to all entrepreneurs under the Korean VAT Act. Every VAT-taxable supply including bakery and franchise transactions must be documented by an e-tax invoice issued and transmitted to the National Tax Service through HomeTax, whether entered directly or via a certified ERP/API integration. The e-tax invoice must be issued on the supply date and transmitted to the NTS no later than the statutory VAT return filing deadline; in practice, NTS receives the invoice data electronically on or about the issuance date. Korea does not operate a Latin American-style real-time fiscal clearance stamp; instead, fiscal control is achieved through the HomeTax e-invoicing system, mandatory cash receipts for cash payments by individuals above KRW 100,000, and automatic reporting of card transaction data to the NTS.

The National Tax Service rigorously audits export zero-rating claims to prevent fictitious exports. If the exporter fails to produce valid customs, shipping, and foreign exchange substantiation, the zero-rated treatment is reversed and VAT at 10% is assessed on the supply. NTS may also levy penalty surcharges and late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act, and for deliberate fraud or false documentation, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may lead to imprisonment up to five years and fines up to three times the evaded tax. Retroactive clawback extends to the statutory assessment period, which is lengthened in cases of intentional concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is generally convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, service fees, royalties, and dividends, subject to reporting requirements under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment, loans, and acquisition of local financial products, are liberalized but require filing with a designated foreign exchange bank or the Korea Customs Service for statistical and supervisory purposes. Profit remittance by a foreign-invested company is permitted without prior approval if the entity has properly filed foreign investment reports and the remittance is supported by audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates. Practical hurdles may include documentary strictness, timing for verification, and requirements to convert foreign currency into KRW for local operational expenditures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 16 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related notices, export proceeds must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank within a prescribed period; the standard statutory collection period is 180 days from the date of export declaration, extendable upon application. Mandatory conversion of export proceeds into KRW is not generally required for current account receipts, but export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts at domestic banks or overseas accounts pursuant to regulations. All export receipts must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System, and any settlement after the deadline requires a report with justification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) jointly administer the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Bank of Korea acts as the central bank and the Foreign Exchange Information System is operated by the Korea Customs Service for reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) service providers have low direct viability in South Korea for primary corporate account holding due to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requiring the use of designated foreign exchange banks for cross-border settlements. Local fintech platforms like Toss and Kakao Pay may offer ancillary payments or operational accounts, but they are not substitutes for a corporate bank account at a licensed Korean bank. Use of offshore EMI for global treasury aggregation can be feasible for non-KRW, non-Korea cross-border collections, but for Korean operational settlements and regulatory compliance, a domestic designated foreign exchange bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injection must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank. The foreign investor must first file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remittance. The wire should use purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment' (e.g., reporting code 310000 or 320000 for equity). The receiving Korean company must issue a Foreign Investment Report Certificate before the remittance is received. The funds should be wired to a temporary foreign exchange bank account or the company's capital escrow account, and the bank will issue a certificate of acquisition of foreign exchange. Verification by the bank includes matching the remitter to the foreign investor, validating the foreign investment report, and confirming compliance with the minimum capital requirements for the intended limited liability company structure. The physical presence mandate and KYC interview for the foreign investor may be satisfied through the designated bank's Know Your Customer procedures for a non-resident investment.Outbound trade disbursements for raw material imports, equipment leases, and royalty payments are settled through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages via the designated foreign exchange bank. For import settlements, the payment must be supported by a valid import declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and the bank will report the payment under the Foreign Exchange Information System. For royalty and franchise fees, the payment is classified under service trade and requires a trade contract, tax invoice, and a withholding tax certificate issued after remote or withholding tax payment to the Korean tax authority. Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) can be used for high-value equipment imports or bulk raw material purchases, with the Korean bank acting as issuing bank. For recurring overseas vendor payments, the treasury should structure batches with a single purpose code and supporting contracts to streamline clearing and reduce compliance friction. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive national corporate income tax (CIT) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2023: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on taxable income from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on taxable income from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion. An additional municipal local income tax surcharge is levied at 10% of the national CIT, producing aggregate statutory marginal rates of 9.9%, 20.9%, 23.1% and 26.4%.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. The full amount of each member's cash contribution must be deposited in a provisional bank account opened in the name of the proposed company before registration, and a bank balance certificate must be filed with the registry. Non-cash contributions require independent valuation and actual transfer of title. No blocked escrow or regulator-managed escrow account is required; the deposited capital may be transferred to the corporate operating account after registration, but it must remain accurately stated as paid-in capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the electronic portal 'Home Tax Service (HTS)' at www.hometax.go.kr and the customs trade portal 'UNI-PASS' administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS).

E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identification number in South Korea is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which also serves as the Value-Added Tax registration number. A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must first obtain corporate existence under the Korean Commercial Act and then apply for BRN registration with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office or place of business. The application must be filed through Home Tax Service or in person at the NTS district tax office using the standard Application for Business Registration, along with the entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign certificate of good standing or equivalent foreign registration evidence, proof of domestic business address, identification of the representative, and, for a foreign non-resident entrepreneur without a domestic place of business, a duly executed power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes. NTS reviews the submitted dossier and issues the BRN; after registration, the entity is entered into the NTS taxpayer master file, enabled for electronic tax invoice issuance through Home Tax Service, and subject to periodic VAT filing obligations. There is no minimum capital requirement, and non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Corporation Tax Act and is taxed on its worldwide income at the entity level; it is not a pass-through entity, and its shareholders are separately taxed on dividends except where treaty withholding or special tax rates apply.

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election. The Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation for Korean corporate income tax and VAT purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Korea applies the OECD BEPS hybrid mismatch rules through statutory override rather than elective or check-the-box mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold applies to the corporate entity. Any person carrying on a business in South Korea, including a Yuhan Hoesa, must obtain a Business Registration Number and register for VAT before or immediately upon commencement of business regardless of projected turnover. For a foreign non-resident supplier without a domestic place of business supplying taxable goods or services in Korea, registration is likewise triggered by the commencement of Korean taxable business activity; the individual simplified taxable person regime is not available to corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including polyresin statues and garage kits, qualify for a 0% VAT rate under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods physically leave South Korea pursuant to an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, the exporter retains verifiable customs clearance and transportation evidence, and the export proceeds are eventually received through a foreign exchange settlement channel authorized by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Qualifying services supplied outside Korea and digital services consumed by non-resident users may also qualify for zero-rating, but for resin statue garage kits the standard export-of-goods pathway under KCS Tariff and VAT rules applies. The zero-rating claim is substantiated by the exporter's complete export transaction file, not merely by the invoice.

  • Export Declaration Acceptance Certificate issued by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing physical export from Korea
  • Sales contract or confirmed purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance and Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Customs export clearance manifest or KCS official export record where requested by the National Tax Service
  • Certificate of Origin (only where a preferential tariff claim is made under a free trade agreement)

South Korea does not impose a pre-invoice real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate comparable to Italian or LATAM clearance models. Instead, all registered corporate taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the NTS Home Tax Service electronic tax invoice infrastructure, either via the HTS web portal, NTS-certified ERP connectors, or NTS XML/JSON web service APIs. The e-tax invoice must contain the fully registered BRNs of supplier and recipient, the taxable supply value, VAT amount, and statutory data fields, and must be transmitted to NTS for cross-matching. NTS operates a continuous purchase-and-sales invoice matching and audit mining system; any mismatch or late transmission triggers penalties and tax audit attention.

Export zero-rating is subject to joint audit scrutiny by the National Tax Service and the Korea Customs Service. If the exporter cannot produce the required KCS export declaration, shipping documents, and bank settlement evidence, NTS will treat the supply as a taxable domestic transaction, reassess VAT at the standard 10% rate, deny the corresponding input VAT credit and any VAT refund previously paid, impose additional tax penalties for incorrect returns, and assess statutory interest. Deliberate creation of false export declarations, forged shipping documents, or sham foreign exchange settlements to obtain zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may expose the entity, its responsible representative, and the appointed tax representative to criminal fines and imprisonment, in addition to venue-wide export privilege restrictions and clawback of all improperly claimed zero-rated VAT benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely open current account but maintains a reporting-based foreign exchange system rather than a free-floating unrestricted regime. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), both capital and current account transactions are generally liberalized, but any transfer exceeding USD 5,000 (or equivalent) must be reported to the supervising foreign exchange bank, and transactions above USD 100,000 require a certificate of receipt or payment and periodic reporting to the Korea Customs Service or the Bank of Korea. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, and intercompany loans, are subject to a prior declaration or notification regime, and permission may be required if the transaction is deemed to pose risks to market stability or violates public interest. For a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) engaged in high-end resin statue production, the practical convertibility is strong for trade and service receipts, but profit remittance to non-resident shareholders triggers a 22% withholding tax on dividends (unless tax treaty relief applies), and banks will require proof of tax clearance and a certified foreign exchange remittance report. Inbound foreign currency for pre-orders and export collections must be either converted into Korean won or held in a foreign currency account with a domestic licensed bank; there is no mandatory surrender obligation for all proceeds, but the exporter must report the receipts within 30 days of receipt via the trade system. Dividend remittances require the target entity to submit board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax payment receipts, and a certified statement that the distribution meets the capital impairment test under the Korean Commercial Act. Overall, the regime is compliant for legitimate commercial flows but demands rigorous documentary trails for every cross-border movement, especially when the beneficial owner is a non-resident individual or holding structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korean law does not impose a blanket surrender requirement. Export proceeds in foreign currency may be retained in a foreign currency account at a domestic licensed bank, but the receiving entity must file an inward remittance report with the bank within 30 days of receipt. For all export contracts exceeding USD 10,000, the Korean exporter must obtain a contract number from the Korea Customs Service and settle the proceeds within the prescribed period (typically 180 days from shipment date, extendable upon application). If the proceeds are received via a non-regular bank account or through a third-party payment intermediary (e.g., Stripe, PayPal), the entity must additionally submit a 'Reference to Inward Remittance' form and the underlying invoice to the bank to prove the commercial nature of the inflow and to avoid being classified as an unlicensed money transfer. No mandatory conversion into KRW is required, but any conversion must be executed at a market rate through a licensed foreign exchange bank. For pre-order deposits received as advance payments, the advance portion must be registered as an export advance and is subject to a 90-day settlement requirement unless an extension is granted by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the primary FX policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Bank of Korea Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for direct EMI banking. South Korea does not permit foreign or domestic fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) to hold fiat deposits or provide corporate IBAN-style foreign currency accounts that can be used for settlement. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act (EFTA) restricts deposit-taking activities to licensed banks, and non-bank EMIs may only operate as payment facilitators (e.g., issuing prepaid wallets) with mandatory backing by a domestic bank's trust account. For a Yuhan Hoesa receiving pre-orders via Stripe or PayPal, these PSPs are treated as payment settlement proxies; the actual settlement must be directed into a KRW or USD corporate account held at a licensed Korean bank. Any fintech such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex cannot be used to receive or hold export proceeds in Korea because they are not licensed as banks under the Banking Act and their aggregated accounts violate the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act's requirement that all cross-border receipts be credited to a named customer account at a domestic bank. Moreover, Korea's Real Name Financial Transaction Act mandates that all transactions be traceable to a legal entity's verified registration number (business registration number) and that foreign currency payments be matched with the underlying customs declaration. Therefore, the viable path is to maintain a fresh-currency account with a Tier-1 bank and use Stripe/PayPal only as a technology gateway that settles through the bank's acquiring relationship, not as an independent settlement rail.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime with full current account convertibility for trade, services, and routine remittances, but capital account operations remain regulated under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and enforcement notices issued by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The Korean won is freely usable within the domestic economy, but cross-border capital activities such as foreign direct investment inflows, equity contributions, loans between related parties, and corporate profit remittances require prior or simultaneous reporting through a designated Foreign Exchange Bank or the Bank of Korea. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in study-abroad and immigration visa consultancy, routine operating payments for tuition, admissions fees, and overseas university contracts are current account transactions and can be made through standard wire transfers provided the underlying documentation is retained. However, capital injections from foreign shareholders must be declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and routed through an approved domestic bank to obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted after corporate tax filing and under certainty of audited financial statements, dividend resolutions, and tax clearance, but banks may apply enhanced review to immigration-service companies due to the elevated risk of visa fraud and illicit cross-border fund movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, South Korea does not impose a mandatory export surrender ratio or a fixed statutory conversion timeline. Export proceeds must be repatriated into Korea and credited to the exporter's foreign currency account at a registered Foreign Exchange Bank within the settlement period agreed in the underlying trade contract, generally not exceeding one year for services and up to three years for long-term transactions. The exporter may elect to hold the currency, convert it to Korean won at market rates, or use it for approved current payments, but each transaction must be reported to the designated bank and records preserved for five years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Korea Customs Service and designated Foreign Exchange Banks administer transaction-level reporting and verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not legally authorized to open corporate bank accounts or escrow accounts in South Korea unless they are licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and obtain authorization from the Financial Services Commission. Foreign EMIs without a local license cannot participate in the national clearing system, hold KRW settlement capabilities, or directly execute customer FX payments for regulated immigration services. They may act only as a front-end payment interface if partnered with a compliant Tier-1 domestic bank, but the Chusik Hoesa should rely on domestic banks for statutory capital verification, escrow, and SWIFT-based outbound tuition remittances to avoid regulatory failure.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on taxable income from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on taxable income above KRW 20 billion (Corporate Tax Act Article 55)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% corporate tax withholding (Company Tax Act Article 98-1(8)) plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the withheld amount, yielding an effective aggregate 27.5% withholding on dividends paid to non-resident corporations and non-resident individuals

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation) must be prepared when intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion per type; Master File is required for multinational groups with global consolidated revenue more than KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country Report is compulsory for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not impose a formal Economic Substance Regulation similar to the UAE, but the substance-over-form rule is embedded in Corporation Tax Act Article 15, Article 66 and Court holdings. A Chusik Hoesa must maintain day-to-day management activities (board meetings, operational decisions) in Korea; the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Korea. If the Korean company is only a booking/pass-through entity with most functions completed abroad, the NTS may recharacterize the transaction, deny treaty benefits and impose transfer pricing adjustment under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (Article 5). Offshore arranged services, staffing subsidiaries, and the physical, human, and functional assets must be documented by contemporaneous evidence to preserve economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Under-Reporting Fee Income

Statutory sanction: Under Punishment of Tax Offenses Act (T.O.A.) Article 3(1) and Corporate Tax Act Article 121, tax evasion by illegal act is subject to criminal fine of up to 5 times the evaded corporate tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years. For amounts exceeding KRW 300 million, Article 3-2 imposes an aggravated penalty of up to 10 years imprisonment. The NTS also adds a 30% fractional omission surcharge and 0.022% daily delinquency interest on the whole evaded amount. The company faces a penalty of 40% of the false amount if a fake invoice is issued (CIT Act Article 76 §5).

Director personal liability: Under National Tax Collection Act Article 38 and Article 41, the NTS may pierce the company veil and hold the directing shareholder personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, surcharges, and daily interest where the company is an alter ego of the individual. The director is listed in the NTS dishonored taxpayer register, triggering a travel ban by the Ministry of Justice under Immigration Control Act; personal real estate and bank accounts are subject to attachment. Criminal conviction results in imprisonment of the directors and a prohibition on running VAT or corporate tax duties for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Minimum Economic Substance for Cross-Border Structuring

Statutory sanction: The NTS applies the arm's-length adjustment under Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 5 and Corporate Tax Act Article 66, recharacterizing the intercompany invoice at the market value and disallowing the related expenses, resulting in additional corporate tax at 21%, a 30% under-report surcharge, and 0.022% daily delay interest. The foreign tax credit is denied because no withholding tax was paid. Company Registration Act requires a license for immigration consulting; operation without a licensed agent triggers cancellation of the license under the Immigration Act, plus a fine of KRW 30 million.

Director personal liability: A Korean court can ignore the entity if it is used only as a taxation device (substantive vs formal test; Supreme Court Decision 2011Du65). Directors may be jointly liable for the additional tax, and if they issue false transportation/foreign financial statements, the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies imposes imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to KRW 50 million. NTS places the director on the bad-payroll and travel blacklist; the Korea Customs Service prevents departure, and the director faces civil restitution to minority shareholders and the state.
AML / Immigrated Client Payment Structuring and Missed STR

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transactions Reports Act (FTRA) Article 4 and Article 5, failure to file an STR is subject to an administrative fine of up to KRW 100 million; noncompliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act can trigger a penal fine up to KRW 100 million and a criminal sentence of up to 3 years (FET Act §83, §84). If the consulted service is provided without a government license, the Unlicensed Business Act levies penalties of 3 years imprisonment or KRW 30 million. Additionally, the Immigration Control Act imposes a prison term for false visa assistance of up to 8 years for the broker.

Director personal liability: The directors and compliance officers can be prosecuted under FTRA Article 10 with imprisonment up to 5 years or fines of KRW 50 million. The courts will piece the corporate veil to enforce civil restitution against the directors where corporate assets were used for money laundering; under the Korea Asset Disposal Act, directors can be personally restraining order in person. The director's personal bank accounts may be frozen and the individual may be blacklisted by the Financial Services Commission and banned from leaving the country; failure renders the director criminally barred and the company license revoked.

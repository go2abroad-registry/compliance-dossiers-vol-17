Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

In Korea the registered office must be a real, fixed, physically identifiable address within the jurisdiction of the registry where judicial and tax notices can be served. A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox will generally satisfy neither the National Tax Service business registration criterion nor domestic bank account-opening due diligence. For the ISIC 6201 EdTech subscription business using Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the entity needs a verifiable operational site, a local tax presence, and access to its payment reconciliation records; a virtual mailbox may be used only as an auxiliary receiving point alongside a valid serviced-office lease, and even then local banks may reject it. The recommended practice is a dedicated serviced office or co-working space under a written lease with nameplate and physical presence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Chusik Hoesa under the amended Commercial Act. The incorporators may declare any capital amount above zero, but the issue price per share must be at least KRW 100. If capital is paid in cash, the full subscription price must be deposited into a restricted escrow account opened in the name of the company under formation at a licensed Korean bank, and a certificate of deposit must be obtained and submitted to the Court Registry Office. The escrow restriction remains in force until the company is registered and the court notifies the bank to release the funds; any withdrawal before completion is void. Non-cash contributions require independent valuation and will be scrutinized by the registry court.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for an industrial fire permit; however, the registered office and any commercial lease must comply with the Fire Services Act (Act No. 4260, as amended), requiring basic fire-extinguishing equipment, a fire evacuation plan, and, where necessary, a certificate of completion of fire-fighting systems from the National Fire Agency and local fire station. Workplace safety in an office environment is governed by the Occupational Safety and Health Act, and, if the company hires five or more full-time employees, a workplace safety and health manager appointment is mandatory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr; export clearance and customs proof are processed through the Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business must first appoint a tax representative in Korea, then file a business registration application under the Value-Added Tax Act. The delegate must submit: certified copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution appointing the representative, power of attorney, proof of the entity's registered address, evidence of the taxable activity or place of supply in Korea, and any Korean bank account evidence. The application is submitted to the NTS district tax office or through HomeTax using the statutory business registration form. After verification the NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the TIN and VAT ID for all subsequent filings; ongoing compliance including filing and payment may be performed by the tax representative under the Korean tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0; registration is mandatory for every business operator with taxable supplies in Korea; a Chusik Hoesa corporation does not benefit from any turnover-based VAT registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when removed from Korea under a customs export declaration. Export services and offshore digital services, including EdTech subscription and language-learning content delivered to non-resident customers outside Korea, are zero-rated when the place of supply is outside Korea and the supplier holds credible evidence of non-resident status and foreign actual consumption. Nothing impairs the right to input VAT credit/refund; the claim must be substantiated on the periodic VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS evidencing the export and the FOB value
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Airwaybill, or multimodal transport document showing physical departure from Korea for exported goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract matching the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Korean bank credit advice proving the export proceeds were received in foreign currency
  • For zero-rated services: signed contract, delivery/usage logs, IP address or geolocation evidence of the overseas recipient, and non-resident status certificate/passport data

Electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) are mandatory for all business-to-business supplies and must be transmitted through HomeTax to the NTS by the day after issuance; the HomeTax system performs digital validation and assigns an electronic seal/barcode for anti-fraud control. The fiscal clearance model is near-real-time transmission, not Latin American pre-clearance blocking; paper tax invoices and cash receipts are also monitored for B2C supplies. Failure to issue or transmit, or late transmission, is subject to statutory surcharges and late-filing penalties.

NTS cross-validates zero-rated export claims against UNI-PASS data, transport documents, and foreign exchange records. If the export documents are false, incomplete, or the proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the supply is recategorized as taxable domestic supply, 10% VAT is assessed on the amount, input VAT refunds are clawed back with interest, and statutory surcharges apply. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating can be prosecuted as tax evasion under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, leading to criminal fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea has a flexible, OECD-aligned convertibility system with separate current and capital account disciplines. Current-account income, including EdTech subscriptions, Apple/Google app store proceeds, and software licensing fees, is freely convertible provided each inflow is credited to a licensed foreign exchange bank and tagged with a valid transaction code. Capital-account transactions, such as non-resident equity injection, shareholder loans, and treasury cross-border intercompany funding, require pre- or post-reporting, underlying contract evidence, and no anti-money laundering adverse findings. There is no required surrender of export proceeds to the central bank; firms may keep 100% of foreign currency in a foreign currency deposit. The chief remittance hurdle is documentary: profit repatriation requires proof of withholding tax settlement, audited or management accounts, a board resolution, and a capital transaction report for non-resident dividends, without which the bank will decline. Korea does not impose convertibility approval licences on ordinary registered corporations, but each transfer is subject to delegated bank surveillance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Bank of Korea operating notices, a resident exporter must receive service-export foreign exchange through a designated foreign exchange bank within 90 days of the service completion date, or within the contract payment terms if the underlying agreement and the foreign exchange bank approve a longer period. The mandatory surrender ratio is zero: no component of the export proceeds must be converted to KRW for central-bank reserves. The exporter may retain 100% in a foreign currency deposit account, but every receipt must be classified under the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System and reconciled with the platform settlement schedule. If the bank observes non-arrival within 90 days, it may suspend export tax and foreign-exchange privileges and require a formal explanation and documentary audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); foreign exchange administration is managed jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not authorised as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; therefore they cannot legally hold a Korean corporate KRW current account, access KFTC clearing, issue foreign exchange transaction reports, or administer capital account compliance. Using an EMI for Korean operating income would circumvent reporting and create an AML red flag. Feasibility rating: Low; it should be used only as a supplementary payout collection tool for non-KR geographic sales where the Korean entity is not the legal creditor. The compliance path is to maintain first-line banking at a Korean Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must remit cash to a temporary Foreign Investment Proceeds Escrow Account maintained at a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remittance wire must identify the payee company, the capital increase or share issuance purpose code, and the source of funds. Upon deposit and acceptance, the company files the Foreign Investment Report through the bank or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency window; the bank then converts the remittance to KRW using the market offer rate, issues a Foreign Investment Report Certificate, and capital verification is performed by the company auditor based on the escrow deposit certificate, board resolution, and bank account statements. For a purely domestic Chusik Hoesa capital increase by a Korean shareholder, no FIPA report is needed, but each inward transfer must be reported by the bank as an inward remittance under the BOK transaction code. All cash injection routes must be non-anonymised and require prior bank approval for anti-money laundering release.The company should route all treasury disbursements through SWIFT MT103 for ordinary operating costs, royalty, subscription, and intercompany payments; a Commercial Letter of Credit for high-value equipment, media, or content licences where a bank guarantee is required; and Documentary Collections for lightweight settlement of recurring intangible service invoices with buyer banks. Every outbound disbursement requires a supporting invoice or contract, a board resolution for large-ticket transfers, proper Korean withholding tax payment for foreign-deferred royalties and licensing fees, and a bank-confirmed transaction code matching the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act classification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum statutory corporate income tax rate before local surtax; progressive brackets: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200M, 19% on KRW 200M-20B, 21% on KRW 20B-300B, and 24% above KRW 300B. A 10% local income surtax is added to the CIT amount, making the effective top rate 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory outbound dividend withholding tax (20% national tax plus 10% resident surtax on that 20%). Certain tax treaties reduce to 5%-15% depending on shareholding and parent-subsidiary tests.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed within 3 months of the close of the fiscal year (i.e., 31 March for a 31 December fiscal year-end). Provisional semi-annual returns are due within 2 months after the first six months of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory preparation of Master File and Local File; consolidated group revenue of KRW 100 billion triggers Country-by-Country report filing. For smaller EdTech firms, the NTS still may issue transfer pricing documentation summons under OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Although Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, the substance-over-form principle (Article 10, Framework Act on National Taxes), OECD MLI PPT provisions, and domestic CFC rules apply. For a Chusik Hoesa in EdTech, substance is tested through actual mobile-payment operation, content and local tutoring function, software development staff, and management decisions in Korea. If non-resident ownership creates a conduit arrangement, the Korean entity's revenue may be recharacterized as a PE of the foreign parent, leading to disallowance of royalties, WHT enforcement, and severe tax penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false corporate return

Statutory sanction: Articles 47-2 and 47-3 of the Corporate Income Tax Act impose negligence surcharges of 20% for non-filing and 10%-40% for underreporting. Under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, criminal tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years, or up to 3 years where evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million, and a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Director(s) may be jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid tax, surcharge, and interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes; the NTS may order deposit of personal assets, suspend departure (travel ban / blacklisting), and impose civil restitution orders. If fraud is proven in a criminal trial, the director faces imprisonment and permanent barring from serving on corporations in Korea.
Failure to withhold non-resident tax / DTA relief misuse

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the amount not withheld and for the 20% additional charge under Article 47-2 of the Corporate Income Tax Act. If a treaty rate is improperly applied, the NTS imposes a 40% unfair underreporting addition, plus daily unpaid-delay interest at 0.022%.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally approved the payment are civilly liable for the unremitted WHT and additions. In serious cases, the violation constitutes criminal tax fraud, leading to imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine of up to 5 times the withheld amount. The NTS may blacklist the company and director to prevent overseas travel and may initiate seizure of personal accounts.
Shell company / economic substance abuse

Statutory sanction: The GAAR under Article 10 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 62 CFC rules allow the NTS to recharacterize the Korean entity as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, deny royalty and interest deductions, and impose a 40% unfair underreporting addition and a 20% withholding violation charge. Treaty relief is denied under the MLI principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil of the Chusik Hoesa and render directors personally liable for the corporate tax assessed, including asset restitution and compensation for losses. Directors also face criminal charges of fraud or tax evasion, travel restrictions, and possible imprisonment where cross-border asset hiding is shown; the company's foreign parent may also be required to register a Korean PE and retroactively withhold all non-resident payments.

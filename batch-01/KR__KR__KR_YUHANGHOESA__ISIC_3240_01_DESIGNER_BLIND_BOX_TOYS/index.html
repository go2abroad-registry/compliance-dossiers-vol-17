Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the registered office of a Yuhan Hoesa that will engage in physical designer toy storage, rotocasting, injection molding, or airbrush finishing, because the Korean Commercial Act requires a registered office to be a physically identifiable place where the company's management is actually located and where records may be inspected. The Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and commercial banks reject mail-only addresses for VAT registration, payroll, customs clearance, and bank account opening in the wholesale/retail and manufacturing sectors. For this ISIC 3240 designer toy business, the Korean local tax office will require a physical place of business for the business registration certificate (sa-eop-ja-deuk-rok), and Korean e-commerce payment rails, including Shopify Payments and Stripe Korea, prohibit virtual office addresses as principal place of business address. At minimum, the company must lease or own either a small production or warehousing facility or a dedicated office suite with the company name on the door, a lease agreement with the actual landlord, a physical reception and delivery capability, and a permanent filing cabinet for VAT, customs, and employment records. A separate mailing address, such as a commercial mail receiving agency, may be used exclusively for correspondence after the physical registered office is established; it may not replace it.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS), Government Complex-Sejong, 46 Jungang-ro, Sejong-si, Republic of Korea; online electronic portal is the HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr) for business registration, VAT filing, electronic tax invoices, and corporate tax filings. Korea Customs Service (KCS) is the competent authority for export clearance via the UNI-PASS electronic system.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign non-resident entity forming Yuhan Hoesa, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN). The mandatory procurement sequence is as follows: 1) incorporate the Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act at the Korean district court registry and obtain the Certificate of Corporate Registration; 2) if the foreign investor makes an FDI or investment report, complete the applicable Foreign Investment Promotion Act filing with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or designated issuing bank; 3) submit the Business Registration Application to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered business place, attaching the Certificate of Corporate Registration, articles of incorporation, lease agreement for the registered office, identification documents of the foreign shareholder/director, and evidence of the registered business place; 4) the National Tax Service verifies the application and issues the Business Registration Certificate, and the BRN printed on it simultaneously constitutes the VAT registration number; 5) after issuance, the entity must enrol in the HomeTax System to activate electronic tax invoice issuance and electronic VAT/Corporate Tax filing privileges; 6) for export operations, register and enrol in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal to obtain the customs identifier and link the BRN to export declaration and manifest functions; 7) if the entity is instead a foreign non-resident corporation without a Korean commercial registration but with a place of business, a foreign corporation tax registration must be effected under the Corporate Tax Act, and the NTS may require appointment of a domestic tax representative for procedural notices and filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation (a domestic Korean corporation taxed as a separate juridical person; Yuhan Hoesa is subject to Korean corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no pass-through of income or losses to shareholders, plus the local income tax surcharge included in the national corporation tax system).

        
Available election forms
None (the Republic of Korea does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; a Yuhan Hoesa is by law an entity-level taxpayer and cannot elect pass-through status. Korean tax residence is determined by place of head office or place of effective management, not by shareholder election. Consolidated tax return filing is available only for specially qualifying corporate groups under the Corporate Tax Act and does not change entity-level taxation).

Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No general VAT registration turnover exemption threshold exists for a business with a Korean place of business; registration is mandatory within 20 days from business commencement.         Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under Article 21 of the VAT Act, export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT where the taxable person actually exports the designer toy goods from Korea to a foreign destination and substantiates the cross-border movement and the receipt of export proceeds. The supply must be made to a foreign buyer, title and risk must pass offshore under the agreed international commercial terms, and the goods must be removed from Korea through an export declaration in URN system. Qualifying offshore digital or service supplies also may be zero-rated when consumed outside Korea, but physical designer toy merchandise must meet customs export and foreign exchange settlement evidence rules to be treated as an export and to support input VAT refunds.

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory under the VAT Act for all taxable supplies by a registered business to another registered business. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted through the NTS HomeTax System electronic tax invoice service no later than the day following the supply date or the payment date, whichever occurs first, unless a statutory exception applies. HomeTax performs automated validation of the invoice serial number, seller BRN, buyer BRN, supply value, and VAT amount, and cross-matches filed VAT returns against transmitted invoices. API integration is available for high-volume taxpayers to submit invoices in real time. There is no separate physical fiscal stamping duty for general merchandise, but KCS UNI-PASS is the sole national electronic clearance window for export declarations, and approved cash receipt devices are required for qualifying B2C cash transactions to reinforce anti-fraud controls.

      
The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims by matching the export declaration, bill of lading, sales contract, foreign exchange settlement records, supplier payments, and logistics trail. An unsubstantiated export claim is denied at the source, the supply is reclassified as domestically taxable, VAT at 10% is assessed, and the exporter is required to repay any VAT refund previously received. Additional taxes under the Value Added Tax Act may include up to 40% of the relevant supply value for false tax invoices or fraudulent refund claims, plus statutory late payment surcharges and daily interest accruals. Criminal exposure includes investigation and prosecution for foreign exchange violation, false export declaration, and tax evasion, with penalties including imprisonment, fines, forfeiture of export incentives, and retrospective clawback of all customs and VAT benefits for a period determined by the statute of limitations, which is extended in cases of intentional concealment or false documentary evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates a largely liberalized current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but capital transactions remain subject to declaratory controls rather than unfettered free convertibility. Current-account items, including export and import payments, service fees, royalties, design licensing fees, and trade-related disbursements, are generally convertible into KRW or major foreign currencies through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank without individual pre-authorization, provided that the underlying contracts, commercial invoices, customs declarations, and tax certificates are genuine. Capital-account items, including inbound equity injections, intercompany loans, and foreign borrowings, must be reported or settled through the FEIS; a non-resident founding a Yuhan Hoesa must file a Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) before equity remittances are booked into capital. Profit remittance is permitted after corporate tax clearance, board approval, and dividend declaration, but bank compliance officers will demand audited financial statements, shareholder registry details, withholding tax certificates, and beneficial-ownership evidence. Export receipts must be settled through designated FX banks and FEIS, but Korea does not impose a blanket surrender order requiring conversion into KRW; permitted foreign-currency retention is available for legitimate overseas payments. Overall conversion hurdles are moderate for documented trade and treasury flows, while speculative or structure-driven capital transactions attract heightened regulatory scrutiny.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign exchange bank within the statutory settlement period, generally treated as 180 days from the export date for conventional shipment-based receivables, unless prior approval or an extended collection schedule is endorsed by the bank; the export contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and customs export declaration must substantiate each inward remittance. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio into KRW for all export receivables; a Yuhan Hoesa may credit foreign currency export receipts into a permitted foreign-currency account, but any foreign currency held beyond legitimate operational needs must be either converted or used for permissible offshore payments, with all balances and settlements declared through the FEIS and reconcilable against the customs report.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and delegated foreign exchange control regulations; designated authorized Korean foreign exchange banks report and settle under the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
National Corporate Tax at 10% for taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 20% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 22% above KRW 20 billion; adding the 10% Local Income Tax surcharge gives a combined top marginal rate of 24.2%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
22% aggregate statutory rate on outbound dividends (20% national withholding under the Corporate Tax Act plus 2% Local Income Tax surtax); Korea DTA rates for dividends are normally 0%-15% depending on shareholder ownership percentage, treaty partner and beneficial ownership.

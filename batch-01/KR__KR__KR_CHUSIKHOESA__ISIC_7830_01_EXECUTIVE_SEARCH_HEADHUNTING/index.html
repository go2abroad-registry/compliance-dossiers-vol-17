None. Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold for a corporation; VAT registration is triggered by the commencement of any Korean taxable business activity. An individual sole proprietor with annual turnover at or below KRW 80,000,000 may qualify as a simplified taxable person, but that threshold does not apply to a Chusik Hoesa, which must register from the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating applies to exports of goods and prescribed services supplied to non-residents for use or consumption outside Korea. For C-suite executive search and headhunting services, the Korean Chusik Hoesa may charge 0% VAT when the client is a non-resident legal person, the engagement letter and fee invoice are denominated in foreign currency, the actual search deliverable and advice are provided to and used by the foreign client outside Korea, and payment is received in foreign exchange. Zero-rating is denied if the service is for a Korean employer, for a Korean branch of a foreign company, or where the foreign-client arrangement lacks substance. Input VAT attributable to the zero-rated export supply is creditable and refundable via the normal VAT return mechanism.

  • No Korean Customs export declaration is raised for a pure service export; the mandatory zero-rating substantiation bundle consists of (a) a signed foreign-currency engagement letter or search mandate from the non-resident client; (b) the electronic tax invoice issued as a zero-rated export supply; (c) a Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Korean bank evidencing receipt of the fee in foreign currency from the actual overseas client; (d) service-delivery records such as candidate shortlists, interview logs, placement confirmations, and progress reports proving the deliverable was supplied to the non-resident client; and (e) a reconcilable export register mapping each invoice to the corresponding remittance and VAT return entry.
  • If incidental tangible goods, such as printed assessment materials or market-research binders, are physically exported in connection with an executive-search engagement, the exporter must additionally retain the Korea Customs Service export declaration, an export bill of lading or air waybill, and shipment receipt to prove removal from Korea.

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for VAT-registered corporations. Each taxable supply of executive-search services must be evidenced by an e-tax invoice issued through HomeTax or an NTS-recognised service provider, transmitted to NTS on or around the time of supply, and retained in the central NTS electronic tax invoice system. The invoice must state the supplier and recipient TIN/VAT IDs, supply date, supply value, VAT amount or zero-rated export classification, and a description of the service. NTS performs central cross-matching of e-tax invoice issuances and acceptances to detect fraudulent invoice chains. No physical fiscal stamping or continuous-control device is required in this industry.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but tightly regulated foreign exchange system. The Korean won is fully convertible on the current account for bona fide trade and service transactions, including executive search retainers and cross-border professional fees, subject to documentary substantiation and reporting through designated foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, borrowing, loans, and inbound equity subscriptions, are generally free but require advance notification, post-transaction reporting, or a certificate of acquisition if structured as a foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Profit and dividend remittance is permitted once corporate tax has been settled, but the paying bank will typically require audited financial statements, a board resolution, tax payment certificates, and a Foreign Exchange Bank confirmation. Repatriation of capital upon liquidation or share disposal requires a further validation of the original inbound currency declaration. No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists today, meaning the corporate may retain foreign currency in authorized foreign currency accounts, but all settlements must pass through licensed Korean banks. Non-resident shareholders face practical hurdles: they must appoint a domestic agent or ensure the bank's e-banking systems can authenticate their identity, and they must submit source-of-funds and tax-residency evidence. The overall assessment is that the regime is business-friendly but form intensive, and a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa must maintain meticulous records of every cross-border wire, FX deferral, and corporate action to avoid penalty and delayed profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters in South Korea are generally required to receive export proceeds within 180 days from the date of export clearance, unless an extension or exception is approved in advance by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the entity may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency account. However, the receipt must be reported to the pursuing bank, and any failure to collect within the statutory window must be justified with documentation such as insolvency certificates, dispute evidence, or settlement delay schedules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and subordinate Foreign Exchange Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise B2B have a very low direct onboarding viability in South Korea. Foreign electronic money institutions and payment service providers are not authorised to independently service domestic corporations unless they obtain an e-money or payment institution licence from the FSC or enter into a compliant local partnership with a licensed Korean bank or registered money transmitter. Most global EMIs lack such licences, so the Chusik Hoesa should not rely on them as a primary treasury rail. A Wise B2B account could be considered only as a downstream sub-router for low-value secondary disbursements, and only if the funds first pass through a licensed domestic bank-based corporate account and clear all Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting obligations. The legally sound path is to use traditional SWIFT wires through Kookmin, Shinhan, Woori, or Hana with proper purpose codes, and to treat any EMI as a non-bank auxiliary rather than a regulated settlement channel.

A Wise B2B account could be considered only as a downstream sub-router for low-value secondary disbursements, and only if the funds first pass through a licensed domestic bank-based corporate account and clear all Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting obligations. The legally sound path is to use traditional SWIFT wires through Kookmin, Shinhan, Woori, or Hana with proper purpose codes, and to treat any EMI as a non-bank auxiliary rather than a regulated settlement channel.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion; any prior-year related-party cross-border transaction amount exceeding this threshold requires Local File and Master File; a Country-by-Country Report is separately required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the general anti-abuse rule and substance-over-form principle in the Framework Act on National Taxes. Although no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation exists, the Korean tax court scrutinizes whether the executive search agency has real people, premises, decision-making powers, and contracts in Korea; core income-generating activities of sourcing, interviewing, negotiating, and billing candidates must be substantiated in Korea. A paper company that merely passes invoices through a zero-tax jurisdiction will have its dividends and royalties recharacterized, lose treaty benefits, and be subject to CFC income imputation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fabricated Headhunting Invoices

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: intentional concealment of income attracts a false-return surcharge of 40% of unpaid tax, daily overdue interest 0.022%, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent invoices are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and surcharges; the NTS may pierce the corporate veil and attach personal real estate and bank accounts, impose an overseas travel ban after blacklisting, and recommend criminal indictment leading to imprisonment. Civil restitution to the tax office follows if corporate assets are insufficient.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Tax

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, non-collection or late remittance of withholding tax attracts an aggregate surcharge of 10% to 50% of the withheld amount, plus daily late-payment interest of 0.022% and compulsory collection of the omitted withholding tax from the payer.

Director personal liability: The representative director is obligated as a withholding agent; personal assets are subject to attachment, and the director is placed on the high-value delinquent taxpayer disclosure list. Intentional failure exposes the director to criminal liability and possible travel bans under the National Tax Collection Act; a foreign director may be blacklisted and face restriction of visa and business capacity.
DTA Treaty Shopping through Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The general anti-abuse rule recharacterizes the transaction and denies treaty benefits; additional income tax equal to the reduced WHT or benefits saved is assessed with a 40% unfair understatement surcharge; supporting documents submitted contrary to fact invoke criminal false-document penalties.

Director personal liability: Courts will lift the corporate veil when the entity is a mere sham; directors of the Korean company are personally liable up to the amount of taxes avoided, and the beneficial owner-director is subject to tax penalty plus criminal imprisonment. The NTS issues a tax payment demand and blacklist or travel ban until full restitution is made.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a chusik hoesa. The incorporators may fix the capital in the articles of incorporation at any amount, but each subscriber must pay the full subscription price for the shares allotted at incorporation. In a non-public stock corporation, the subscription proceeds are deposited into a temporary escrow or paid-in capital account at an authorized bank and may be released only after incorporation registration. Foreign direct investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act may be required for non-resident shareholders, but no blocked capital retention period is mandated by the Commercial Act.

Funding & unlock path: Firefighting Facilities Completion Inspection Certificate issued by the local fire station under the Fire Facilities Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Management Act, covering fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation route signs, and automatic fire alarm where floor area so requires; structural load inspection and approval of any mezzanine racking by a certified structural engineer; occupational safety registration with the Ministry of Employment and Labor and mandatory industrial accident insurance for employees; and, for premises with a floor area in excess of 3,000 square metres, a civil defense shelter use and evacuation coordination certificate from the local district office. For a general small grocery store, no separate civil defense shelter permit is required below that threshold.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a chusik hoesa. The incorporators may fix the capital in the articles of incorporation at any amount, but each subscriber must pay the full subscription price for the shares allotted at incorporation. In a non-public stock corporation, the subscription proceeds are deposited into a temporary escrow or paid-in capital account at an authorized bank and may be released only after incorporation registration. Foreign direct investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act may be required for non-resident shareholders, but no blocked capital retention period is mandated by the Commercial Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the Home Tax Service (HTS) electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a taxable presence in South Korea: (1) complete the application for Business Registration using NTS Form No. 101, obtainable electronically via HTS or from the local district tax office; (2) submit the articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign investor registration, proof of registered address in Korea (lease agreement or certificate of ownership), and passport or equivalent identification of the designated domestic tax representative if the entity has no fixed Korean place of business; (3) obtain the unique Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as both the tax identification number and the VAT registration number; (4) for a Chusik Hoesa, registration must be completed within 20 days of business commencement under the Value-Added Tax Act and Business Act; (5) apply for the e-Tax Invoice issuing certificate through HTS to comply with the electronic tax invoice mandate; (6) for taxpayers with no domestic establishment or no in-country representative, appoint a tax representative under Article 84 of the National Tax Basic Act; (7) retain the sealed Business Registration Certificate in paper and electronic form, as it must be displayed in the physical retail premises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for VAT or general corporate tax. South Korea does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification system analogous to the United States. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Tax Act, with no pass-through election. Certain intra-group reorganizations may utilise the statutory Group Taxation election under Articles 58-61 of the Corporate Tax Act, and cross-border hybrid mismatch rules are applied under the Tax Adjustment Act. No other elective regimes apply to VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory monetary turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory for all taxable business operators, including retail grocery stores, from the commencement of business. Zero threshold in KRW.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 23 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the supply of goods exported to a foreign country is zero-rated at 0% VAT. Goods qualify as exported only if they are cleared through the Korean Customs Service under an export declaration and physically loaded for shipment to a foreign destination. Certain services supplied to non-residents for foreign use are also zero-rated if the place of supply is outside Korea and the recipient is not subject to Korean VAT. Retail grocery exports from a Korean registered entity to foreign buyers will satisfy the statutory criteria upon production of the required customs and foreign exchange documentation.

  • Korean Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) under the Customs Act
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing international shipment
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Korean seller
  • Packing list
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Certified copy of the export contract or purchase order
  • e-Tax Invoice for the export transaction issued through Home Tax Service
  • Container loading certificate or postal export certification for low-value parcel shipments

Mandatory electronic e-Tax Invoice issuance through the Home Tax Service (HTS) portal. All VAT-registered business-to-business suppliers must issue e-Tax Invoices for each supply of goods or services within one day of the supply date. The data is transmitted electronically to the NTS in real time; there is no pre-clearance fiscal stamping system, but anti-fraud integrity is secured through encrypted transmission, unique invoice serial numbers, and automatic cross-matching of purchaser-side deductions with supplier-issued reports. Failure to issue, late issuance, or understatement triggers penalty surcharges under the VAT Act.

The NTS and Korean Customs Service conduct risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims. Required documents must be retained for at least five years from the declaration date. If the exporter fails to satisfy the documented proof of export and actual receipt of foreign currency within the statutory 180-day deadline (or an approved extension under foreign exchange rules), the NTS will disallow the zero rate and assess VAT at 10% on the full sales amount, plus statutory additional tax: an amount equal to 20% of the underreporting penalty if the misstatement was unintentional, and 40% of the tax in cases of deliberate or fraudulent avoidance. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may additionally impose fines up to five times the evaded amount and imprisonment for up to five years. Retroactive clawbacks extend to previously granted input VAT credits, export subsidy benefits, and foreign exchange reporting penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is convertible for current account transactions including trade settlements and service payments, subject to statutory documentation and reporting to the domestic remitting bank. Capital account transactions have been substantially liberalized under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but they remain subject to ex-ante reporting, ex-post verification, and strict purpose-of-remittance controls. Foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa must be filed with the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency or a designated foreign exchange bank before the inward remittance; after the capital is received, the receiving bank issues a certificate of foreign exchange or capital verification evidence. Profit remittance is permitted after audited or reviewed financial statements, a valid shareholder resolution declaring dividends, appropriate withholding tax payments on the gross dividend, and submission of a foreign exchange settlement report. The principal hurdle for grocery importers is not convertibility itself, but rather the need to maintain a clean trail of import declarations, supplier invoices, customs entries, and payment purpose codes. While the Korean won does not have a strict surrender requirement, all export proceeds must ultimately be repatriated to a domestic foreign currency account within the regulatory time frame and be declared to the reporting bank. The overall regime remains fundamentally open but reportable, with no pre-approval burden for authentic trade-related payments and only light-touch reporting for equity injections and dividend outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of exportation unless an extension is obtained from the bank or the Bank of Korea, and the full proceeds must be credited to a registered foreign currency account in a domestic bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in Korea or convert it to KRW at a licensed bank, but the receipt must be reported through the bank. Non-repatriation or delayed receipt beyond the statutory window requires prior regulatory approval and a documented justification such as protracted trade litigation or credit default.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority and the Korea Customs Service as the cross-border enforcement agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking rail for a Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa. Foreign fintech EMIs and digital-only cross-border payment providers are not permitted to service Korean corporate entities unless they are licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act as a registered electronic financial business operator. Most offshore EMIs lack the required Financial Supervisory Service registration, cannot open won-denominated accounts, and cannot act as the receiving bank for FDI capital injections or import LCs. The feasible use-case is limited to ancillary employee expense cards or low-value operational top-ups through a licensed Korean fintech payment institution, but all treasury-grade clearing must flow through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax top marginal rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, with progressive lower brackets at 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, and 21% up to KRW 300 billion; a 10% local income tax surtax is added to national CIT payable, producing a 26.4% combined top marginal rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national dividend withholding tax under Korean corporate and tax law, plus 2% local income tax surtax, giving a combined 22% statutory rate on outbound dividends; applicable double tax treaties generally reduce the rate to 5%-15% after treaty relief is granted

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount reaches KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year; the Country-by-Country Report is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion; even when thresholds are not met, a contemporaneous transfer-pricing margin study is strongly advised for every purchase of imported grocery inventory from related foreign suppliers

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply an ESR law in the Gulf Cooperation Council form, but applies economic-substance and substance-over-form principles through the Framework Act on National Taxes, the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, and tax treaty anti-abuse rules; the Korean Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate that core income-generating activities, including procurement negotiation, inventory management, shelf merchandising, sales acceptance, payment collection, cash custody, and after-sales service, are performed by real personnel and assets in Korea; if the Korean entity is a mere shell or conduit, treaty benefits and separate-entity recognition will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false corporate income tax return

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes; offending corporation is liable for 40% unlawful understatement penalty, 0.022% daily late-payment interest, and fines of up to two times the evaded tax; aggravated evasion above KRW 500 million exposes the entity and natural persons to imprisonment with labour

Director personal liability: Directors who instigate or sign false returns lose limited liability protection; the court may pierce the corporate veil and order full restitution of evaded tax, interest, and penalties; the representative director and finance director can be arrested, placed on the NTS immigration black list, travel-banned, and sentenced to up to 5 years of imprisonment with labour
Failure to withhold and remit outbound dividend, interest, and royalty withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the un-withheld national tax, local surtax, daily late-payment surcharge, and under-withholding penalties; false claims of treaty eligibility trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; unpaid withholding amounts may be recovered from the personal assets of the responsible director

Director personal liability: The representative director, chief financial officer, and any responsible officer who approved the payment are jointly and severally liable for the un-withheld taxes and penalties; personal assets may be seized after corporate enforcement fails; the director is subject to exit ban, NTS blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to twice the un-withheld amount
Transfer-pricing manipulation and treaty-shopping shell abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may adjust income under transfer-pricing rules, add a 20%-40% penalty on the understated income, disallow foreign tax credits, deny treaty benefits under beneficial ownership and principal-purpose tests, and treat the adjusted profit as a constructive dividend subject to 22% withholding

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the Chusik Hoesa is a sham or misuse of corporate form; directors face personal civil restitution for unpaid corporate taxes, monetary fines, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years; procurement and finance officers who knowingly prepare false transfer-pricing documentation may be prosecuted as accomplices to tax fraud

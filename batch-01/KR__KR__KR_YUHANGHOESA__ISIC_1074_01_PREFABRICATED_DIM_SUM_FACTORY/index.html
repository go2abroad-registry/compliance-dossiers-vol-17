Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under Korean law. The subscribers must fully pay or provide the aggregate contributed capital amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation before the incorporation registration date. The paid-in contribution must be deposited into a corporate subscription bank account opened in the Company's name, and a bank balance verification certificate must accompany the registration filing. There is no statutory blocked-escrow account requirement beyond this payment obligation. Capital may be paid in cash or in-kind; in-kind contributions must be valued by an independent accredited appraiser, and non-resident capital remittances must be declared and processed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Funding & unlock path: Firefighting Systems Completion Certificate and final fire inspection approval must be obtained from the local fire station under the Fire-Fighting and Construction Act before production. The factory building must receive structural load inspection and approval for freezer floors, quick-freeze lines, mezzanine equipment platforms, and ammonia or Freon refrigeration machinery. Occupational Safety and Health Act clearance must be obtained through a workplace safety audit covering conveyor guarding, lockout-tagout procedures, cold-storage entry permits, pressure vessel certification, and electrical safety inspection by the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation before energization. Sanitary facility inspection by the food and health authority is required as a condition precedent to operating the dim sum production facility.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, acting through the HomeTax electronic tax portal (www.hometax.go.kr); export customs validation is coordinated with the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (portal.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For the Yuhan Hoesa (Korean LLC) owned by a foreign non-resident investor, the company must first complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and then apply for Business Registration at the local NTS district tax office (or via HomeTax) within the statutory 20-day period from business commencement. Required documents include the Certificate of Corporate Seal, Articles of Incorporation, Certificate of Incorporation, place-of-business lease or property documentation, foreign investment report or certificate if received under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and passport or government-issued ID of the representative director. NTS will issue a Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the unified Korean TIN and VAT registration number for corporate duties. The company must then activate electronic tax invoice issuance on HomeTax. If the foreign non-resident parent has Korean-source income but no domestic permanent establishment, the parent must appoint a statutory tax representative and register for a non-resident tax identification file with the competent NTS International Taxation Office for withholding tax and treaty reduction purposes. No separate VAT ID is required for the domestic LLC beyond the BRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean Corporate Tax Act and VAT Act do not provide a check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch election for a Yuhan Hoesa. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation and taxed at entity level; non-resident ownership does not make it fiscally transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover exemption applies to a domestic Yuhan Hoesa: any corporate entity with a place of business in Korea must register for VAT from the moment it commences business. A foreign non-resident supplier of electronic and digital services with no Korean place of business is obliged to register for VAT under the Simplified Business Registration procedure only when its annual Korean-source taxable supplies exceed KRW 300,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of the manufactured frozen dim sum goods to overseas buyers qualifies for the zero rate of Korean VAT when the goods are physically exported from Korea and cleared by Korea Customs Service. The Korean LLC must hold an Export Declaration, shipping documents, and evidence that the export proceeds are received in a foreign currency via a Korean foreign exchange bank. The supply must be made to a non-resident foreign buyer and title or physical possession must pass outside Korea. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only for B2B supplies where the place of consumption is demonstrably outside Korea; B2C digital supplies to Korean consumers remain taxable at 10%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) submitted to Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Original Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air export) issued to the Korean exporter/shipper
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Export Proceeds / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by the appointed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Export sales contract or overseas purchase order and bank settlement (SWIFT LC or TT) evidence confirming payment by the foreign buyer

All Korean VAT-registered businesses must issue e-Tax Invoices electronically through the NTS HomeTax system; the invoice must be transmitted to HomeTax by the day after the date of supply. The NTS centrally validates, stores, and reconciles the e-Tax Invoice data through the HomeTax API and dedicated portal. There is no physical fiscal stamp; the validated electronic invoice number and transmission record in HomeTax serve as the anti-fraud clearing evidence. Failure to issue or transmit e-Tax Invoices triggers denial of input VAT credits and additional tax penalties.

NTS, together with Korea Customs Service and the foreign exchange bank inspection authorities, may audit the export documentation trail. If an export declaration is false, goods are not physically dispatched, documents are backdated, or export proceeds are not received and reported as foreign currency export earnings within the repatriation benchmark period (180 days for the zero-rating substantiation cycle), NTS will disallow the zero rate, reassess VAT at 10% on the export consideration, impose additional taxes for unfaithful declaration or under-reporting under the Value Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, charge delinquency surcharges, and initiate criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Retroactive reassessment may cover five years from the statutory due date, extended to ten years in cases of fraud; suspended export VAT refunds may be clawed back with interest before any future refund is paid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating KRW with an open current account and a risk-based capital account control system administered jointly by the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions are fully convertible; export and import settlement are processed without requiring underlying prior approval, but each hard-currency receipt or payment must be tagged with a Foreign Exchange (FX) transaction code and reported to the Foreign Exchange Information Network. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment, loans, and securities flows, are liberalized but require reporting, a receipt, or a declaration through a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no surrender obligation for exporters, but export earnings must be collected within the statutory period and deposited into foreign currency accounts. Profit remittances by the LLC to overseas shareholders are treated as outward current-account dividends: the bank requires audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, proof of withholding dividend tax, and an FX declaration. Dividends are freely remitted once compliance is met, so no separate approval bottleneck exists for standard treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within 18 months from the export declaration date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; the exporter is legally required to receive lawful foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank and may either retain the proceeds in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account or sell them to the bank. Any overdue collection must be justified and may require MOEF/BOK approval for extension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with delegated regulatory administration by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. South Korea does not grant full banking licences to offshore EMI brands, and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires cross-border KRW/USD payments to pass through a domestic bank licensed to handle foreign exchange. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex may hold multi-currency e-wallets in certain overseas markets, but they cannot issue a Korean domestic account number, connect to the Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute (KFTC) won clearing, or lawfully act as a deposit-taking counterparty without a local bank partnership. For a Yuhan Hoesa manufacturing entity in Korea, fintech EMIs should be considered only for non-core collection-side convenience in countries where the EMI holds a valid local licence; all treasury, FX, and regulatory reporting must remain with Tier-1 designated foreign exchange banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must open a Foreign Investment Capital Escrow Account at a designated domestic bank, such as KB, Shinhan, Hana, or Woori, by presenting Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration documents. The investor remits from its own overseas corporate bank account in USD or another qualified currency using the FX purpose code for equity contribution for foreign direct investment. After the credit is received, the bank issues a Foreign Currency Purchase Certificate, the company completes legal incorporation, and the escrowed foreign currency is converted into KRW at the prevailing market rate and credited to the paid-in capital account. A certified public accountant then issues the capital contribution verification report, and the bank files the foreign investment declaration with MOEF and Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information Network.All trade and operating payments must be executed through the company's designated foreign exchange bank. Direct telegraphic transfers use SWIFT MT103 with the correct FX transaction purpose code and require underlying purchase contracts, pro-forma invoices, import customs declarations, and proof of manufacturing premises in Korea. For export sales, the company may collect via Commercial Letter of Credit, either sight or usance, and may use documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for consolidated invoice-based settlement. Domestic KRW payments for wages, utilities, and raw materials are cleared through KFTC rails, while any cross-border KRW distribution such as dividends or shareholder loans must be reported and settled through the designated foreign exchange bank.Onshore deliverable currency forwards in USD/KRW, JPY/KRW, and EUR/KRW backed by real trade exposure are permissible through designated foreign exchange banks. Non-deliverable forwards are generally restricted to specific offshore hedging structures and are not permissible as a standalone speculative product for the operating LLC. FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible for balance-sheet and term-debt hedging, while FX options are allowed only with documented underlying trade exposure and full bank suitability assessments.

24% top marginal rate on a progressive scale: 9% on income up to KRW 200M; 19% above KRW 200M to KRW 20B; 21% above KRW 20B to KRW 300B; 24% above KRW 300B.

22% aggregate withholding tax on outbound dividends: 20% national withholding under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act plus 2% local income tax surcharge, subject to reduction under an applicable double tax treaty for a qualifying beneficial owner.

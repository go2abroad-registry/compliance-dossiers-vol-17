Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - under the Commercial Act of Korea there is no legal minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the company may fix a nominal par value in the Articles of Incorporation, provided at least one share is issued and the full subscription price is paid-in at incorporation. For bank creditworthiness and aircraft-lessor market acceptance, a paid-up capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 75,000) is commercially recommended but not legally mandated. No blocked escrow account is required by general Korean corporate law; subscription funds must be deposited into a provisional stock subscription account opened in the company's name, and the account cannot be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is completed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, accessible through the HomeTax online portal at www.hometax.go.kr; export and customs validation is coordinated with the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on business in Korea must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (Saeupja Deungjeongjeung) through HomeTax or the local district tax office before commencing taxable activity. Where the foreign entity has no domestic place of business, a statutory tax representative must be appointed. The tax representative submits the foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution or power of attorney, domestic business address evidence, and lease or contract documentation. The NTS assigns a unique 10-digit business registration number, which functions as the VAT identification number for subsequent tax administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or analogous entity classification election exists under Korean tax law. A Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a standalone domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act. Korean-resident corporate groups may elect consolidated corporate tax return treatment under Article 76-9 of the Corporate Tax Act, but foreign corporations with only a Korean branch are not eligible. VAT grouping is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW monetary threshold. VAT registration is mandatory for every entrepreneur, including a foreign non-resident supplier with a Korean taxable nexus, making taxable supplies in Korea regardless of annual turnover. Foreign suppliers without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative and register through HomeTax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21(1) of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act for exported goods and under Article 21(2) for services supplied to overseas users. For an aircraft engine dry leasing transaction, the taxpayer must demonstrate that the engine was physically exported or delivered to a non-resident lessee, title and possession or control passed to the foreign lessee, the engine is used wholly outside Korean customs territory, and the consideration is received in foreign currency from offshore. The taxpayer must retain the executed lease agreement, engine serial number documentation, customs export declaration, transport or delivery receipts, and foreign exchange remittance evidence.

All VAT invoices between registered businesses must be issued as electronic tax invoices and transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax in near real-time. Transmission may be effected through the HomeTax user interface, EDI, or machine-to-machine API integration. Export customs declarations must be filed electronically through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system. Digital stamping is not used, but real-time national tax clearance of e-tax invoices and daily reporting of cash receipts through approved VAN service providers are mandatory.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border lease payments, subject to reporting requirements under FETA. Capital account transactions, such as inbound equity injections and foreign loans, require prior declaration or approval from the MOEF or delegated banks. Profit remittance (dividends) is permitted under current account rules, but requires tax clearance and submission of audited financial statements. No significant hurdles exist for genuine commercial transactions, but strict documentation and reporting are mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 17 of FETA and related regulations, export proceeds (including lease income from foreign lessees) must be repatriated to Korea within 6 months from the date of export (or service provision). There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; however, the proceeds must be received in a designated foreign currency account or converted to KRW and reported to the designated foreign exchange bank. Failure to repatriate within the timeline triggers penalties and potential criminal liability.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. South Korea does not recognize offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licenses for domestic corporate banking. Foreign fintech entities (e.g., Revolut, Wise) cannot provide local KRW accounts or act as primary clearing agents. They may be used as ancillary payment channels, but all statutory FX reporting and tax compliance must be executed through a licensed Korean bank. Legal constraints under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and FETA prohibit unlicensed cross-border deposit-taking and payment services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial equity injection from foreign shareholders, the funds must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the company's capital escrow account at a Korean bank. The remittance must include a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Equity Investment' under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act) and the company's business registration number. The bank will verify the transaction against the foreign investment declaration filed with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA). For loans from foreign shareholders, a loan agreement must be registered with the MOEF, and the funds must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank. All inbound remittances must be reported to the Bank of Korea within 30 days.For payments to foreign engine manufacturers or maintenance providers, the company may use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers or commercial letters of credit (L/C) for larger procurement contracts. The bank will require the underlying invoice, purchase order, and customs documentation (if applicable). For lease payments received from foreign lessees, the company must ensure the funds are credited to a foreign currency account and then either converted to KRW or retained, subject to the 6-month repatriation rule. For outbound payments, the company must submit a 'Foreign Exchange Transaction Application' to the bank, and the bank will verify the transaction against the FETA reporting thresholds. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory rate: 20% dividend income tax on gross payments to a non-resident shareholder plus 10% Local Income Tax surtax on that income tax, producing an aggregate withholding rate of 22%; a treaty can reduce it to between 5% and 15% depending on ownership level and beneficial-ownership requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File / Master File attachment is triggered under Article 11 of the International Tax Adjustment Act when annual cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 30,000,000,000; the Master File threshold is additionally triggered when the Korean entity is part of a multinational group whose combined annual group revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000. In practice, a complete transfer-pricing report should be contemporaneous and available even where these thresholds are not breached.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not codify a generic 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute of the UAE/GCC type; however, the National Tax Service applies a force-of-substance doctrine and the International Tax Adjustment Act's anti-treaty-shopping rules. A Korean Chusik Hoesa operating aircraft-engine dry leasing must demonstrate operational substance in Korea, including locally resident directors, management decision-making relating to acquisition and disposition of engines, active negotiation of leases, physical receipt of rent, insurance and maintenance decision authority, and absorption of residual-value risk. A pure nominee or conduit with no functional office, staff, or financial capacity will be recharacterised and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, wilful evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years or a fine not exceeding 5 times the amount of the evaded tax; the NTS additionally collects the evaded corporate income tax, the underpayment surcharge, and 0.022% daily delinquency interest, and may impose a 40% added tax on fraudulent filing.

Director personal liability: Representative and responsible directors face personal civil liability for the evaded tax through the corporate veil-piercing doctrine where they authorised false records; practical consequences include travel bans, asset freezing, civil restitution orders, disqualification from directorship, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny the applicable DTA benefit under the anti-treaty-shopping provisions of the International Tax Adjustment Act, withhold at the full statutory 22% on dividends, recharacterise the transaction as a Korean-source service or royalty, assess the undeclared corporate income tax, and impose an additional penalty of up to 20% of the underpaid tax; deliberate use of a sham entity may result in criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who are merely formal signatories may still have their passports restricted and be held jointly liable for monies diverted if they knew the corporation was a conduit; civil claims by creditors and the State may reach their personal assets where a contumelious disregard of corporate formalities is shown.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS may adjust the Korean entity's taxable income to the arm's length price and correct the related-party transaction amounts; an adjustment produces a penalty of up to 20% of the unpaid corporate income tax attributable to the transfer-pricing misstatement, in addition to 0.022% daily late-payment interest; failure to submit documentation itself carries a fixed penalty of up to KRW 500,000,000 for material omission.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify or approve falsified intercompany transfer-pricing returns are exposed to personal civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall; aggravated cases involving deliberate documentation destruction or collusion with related parties may result in criminal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
Foreign Exchange and AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act is punishable by imprisonment for up to three years or a fine not exceeding KRW 100,000,000; the Financial Services Commission may impose administrative fines and the Korea Customs Service may seize undeclared proceeds; the NTS may also impose a 10% aggravated penalty for unlawful receipt of Korean-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or direct the wire-transfer structure become personally liable for the monetary penalties and may face asset seizure orders and travel restriction notices; a criminal conviction for money laundering or violation of reporting duties can lead to disqualification from directorship and imprisonment, while the corporate veil is pierced where the company was used as a tool for the director's personal financial benefit.

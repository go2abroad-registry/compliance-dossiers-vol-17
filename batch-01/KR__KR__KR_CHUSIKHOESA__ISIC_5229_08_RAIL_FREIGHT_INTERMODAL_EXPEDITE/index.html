Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, before the incorporation registration, the full issue price of all subscribed shares must be paid into a blocked escrow subscription account at a licensed commercial bank in Korea. The deposit is blocked and may not be withdrawn until the certificate of incorporation registration is issued. A practical benchmark of at least KRW 100,000,000 is required by most logistics licensing authorities to demonstrate capital adequacy for international freight forwarding. Foreign investors must deposit through a foreign exchange bank and file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with the same escrow restriction preserved until registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) for export clearance via UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine the taxable place of business in Korea (or absence thereof) and whether the entity is a domestic Chusik Hoesa or a non-resident foreign corporation. Step 2: File a business registration application (TIN/VAT registration) with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business through HomeTax or in person. Step 3: Submit statutory corporate documents, including articles of incorporation, foreign corporate registration evidence, a Korean place-of-business lease or branch establishment record, board resolution or power of attorney appointing a local tax representative, and any Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration for foreign investment. Step 4: NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID) and Business Registration Certificate. Step 5: Register as a VAT taxpayer and e-Tax invoice issuer; obtain HomeTax credentials and, if handling exports, a UNI-PASS user ID from Korea Customs Service. Step 6: For a non-resident foreign entity without a fixed domestic place of business, appoint a Korean tax representative and complete HomeTax simplified foreign entity registration to obtain a temporary business registration number before supplying taxable services in Korea.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act zero-rates exported goods and qualifying exported services. A Korean Chusik Hoesa can claim 0% VAT on international rail freight forwarding when the services are supplied for the export of goods from Korea or for the transportation of goods between foreign countries, and when the service is arranged for and paid by a non-resident in foreign currency. The freight forwarder must retain KCS export declarations, transport contracts, railway bills and foreign exchange receipt evidence proving the goods crossed Korea Customs and the consideration was received from outside Korea.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (UNI-PASS export clearance record, not a Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal rail waybill covering the Eurasian block train or onward sea leg
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the exported goods
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Electronic tax invoice marked zero-rated for the export freight forwarding supply

A corporate freight forwarder must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) through the NTS HomeTax system for taxable B2B supplies. The e-Tax invoice data is transmitted to the NTS at issuance through the HomeTax portal or approved API; NTS validates the recipient business registration number and matches invoice data for VAT return compliance. Korean VAT does not use a national anti-fraud digital stamp or preclearance stamp; enforcement is through HomeTax e-invoice collection matching, penalty surcharges for late/non-filing, and KCS export clearance data matching.

NTS and Korea Customs Service will cross-check the export declaration number, bill of lading, container/railcar identifiers, and the foreign currency receipt. If zero-rating is unsupported or the export documents are false, the supply is reclassified as taxable domestic VAT at 10%. NTS will assess the unpaid VAT with statutory surcharges for false or insufficient VAT returns, monthly late payment interest, and may claw back any VAT refund already paid. Fraudulent zero-rating invoices or forged customs documents also trigger criminal penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and Korea Customs Act, and an extended statutory tax audit period applies in cases of deliberate concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a fundamentally open current account and a regulated but operationally efficient capital account. The Korean won is freely convertible for current-account trade in goods and services, including rail freight revenue, logistics service fees, Intermodal transport charges, agency commissions, and ordinary corporate operating expenses under FETA. A resident Chusik Hoesa may receive freight and logistics settlement proceeds in foreign currency and may maintain foreign currency accounts at authorized domestic foreign exchange banks without requiring a pre-approval license for standard operational transactions. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment, foreign borrowing, cross-border guarantees, and certain advances, require advance declaration, registration, or follow-up reporting through a specified foreign exchange bank or the BOK/MOEF system. The Korean regulatory framework does not impose mandatory surrender of export freight proceeds into KRW; instead, the duty is to collect, settle, and report proceeds through a domestic foreign exchange bank within prescribed timelines. Freight receivables, unpaid supplier invoices, and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders must be supported by underlying signed contracts, invoices, customs or railway consignment documentation, audited financial statements, and evidence of FDI registration where applicable. Inbound equity capital subscribed by foreign investors is subject to the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and requires advance notification to an authorized bank, opening of a foreign-investment capital account, and issuance of a foreign investment report certificate before capital verification and profit repatriation become compliant. Dividend remittance is not barred or subject to arbitrary approval under routine conditions, but a domestic withholding tax event arises simultaneously with the remittance and requires an accurate Corporate Tax and Withholding Tax filing under Korean tax law. In crisis conditions, FETA empowers BOK and MOEF to impose temporary control measures, but for ordinary rail freight and freight-forwarding treasury operations, no material convertibility hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and its Enforcement Decree, a resident exporter or cross-border service provider is required to receive export proceeds through an authorized foreign exchange bank within the payment term stated in the underlying contract, generally no later than 4 months from the export declaration date if no specific credit term is documented. The bank must be notified of the collection with the relevant trade/service declaration reference. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring surrender of foreign currency to KRW; the mandatory ratio is effectively 0% of export proceeds. The proceeds may lawfully be credited to a domestic foreign currency account or used for permitted current-account settlement, but every receipt must be reportable to BOK/MOEF through FEIN and traceable to the rail freight or logistics service contract, CIM/SMGS consignment note, waybill, or equivalent transport document. Failure to collect within the statutory timeline requires a formal extension application before the deadline, supported by evidence of the buyer default, insurance claim, or amended payment terms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, operating alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), with delegated implementation exercised by foreign exchange banks and reporting through the Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute (KFTC)/Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers are not legal substitutes for a Tier-1 domestic bank account in South Korea. An EMI that is not licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act cannot independently serve as the principal settlement bank for a Korean resident Chusik Hoesa, hold KRW IBANs, or clear trade proceeds through the Korean interbank system. Cross-border fintech platforms operating through a Korean bank partner may be used only as auxiliary collection and FX execution channels, and the account structure must ultimately settle through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. For a rail freight and intermodal forwarding entity, the compliance viability of an offshore EMI is low due to FEIN reporting obligations, trade declaration matching, and the need for local bank issuance of Letters of Credit and documentary guarantees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident shareholders must be structured as Foreign Direct Investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Before remittance, the investor or domestic entity must file an advance investment report through an authorized foreign exchange bank or Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA). The bank opens a foreign-investment capital account, and the remitter sends the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 with the designated FDI purpose code and a clear remittance narrative citing the issued foreign investment report number. Upon receipt and verification of the foreign exchange bank memorandum, the entity obtains the foreign investment report certificate and completes capital verification through the bank memorandum. Inbound freight contract receivables are treated as current-account service receipts and may be received through a domestic foreign currency account using the transport service purpose code; the proceeds must be matched against the export of services declaration filed with the relevant customs authority and FEIN.All outbound treasury disbursements are settled through the domestic foreign exchange settlement system using SWIFT MT103 for freight payments, Intermodal operator fees, terminal handling charges, insurance and general operational expenses. For larger structured settlements, trade-related disbursements should be executed through irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit or documentary collections where the payee requires proof of payment against consignment note, CIM/SMGS waybill, customs pack, and commercial invoice. Every foreign-currency disbursement must be tagged with the correct FETA transaction purpose code, supported by the underlying service or goods contract, and reported through FEIN so that settlement records reconcile with Korean customs import/export declarations and tax invoices. The bank may require prior receipt of the export proceeds certificate for service income in order to approve outward remittance of dividends or shareholder loans, and dividend remittance must additionally be supported by audited retained earnings, tax withholding confirmation, and resolution of the shareholders meeting.Onshore forward foreign exchange contracts (KRW/USD, KRW/EUR, KRW/CNY) permitted only where there is genuine current-account or capital-account underlying exposure derived from rail freight revenue, transport receivables, or trade payables, with the trade-related documentary evidence submitted to the foreign exchange bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available principally to non-resident counterparties or in approved offshore hedging structures; Korean resident entities may access onshore NDF equivalents only when the hedging requirement strictly mirrors approved cross-border transaction exposure and satisfies FETA underlying-transaction documentation rulesCross-currency swaps permitted for the reduction of balance-sheet currency and interest-rate mismatch on documented trade finance or FDI-related borrowings, subject to bank approval, FEIN reporting, and absence of speculative intentStandard FX options and structured collars may be offered by Korean banks only when embedded within a genuine hedging framework and approved by the bank’s foreign exchange compliance committee

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national Corporate Income Tax on the top bracket (taxable income above KRW 300 billion) plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing a combined top effective headline rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, comprising 20% national Corporate Income Tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax; rate may be reduced to 10-15% under an applicable Bilateral Double Tax Treaty, usually requiring a minimum shareholding and beneficial ownership

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, a Local File is required where the total value of cross-border international related-party transactions exceeds KRW 50 billion in a fiscal year; a Master File is required where the Korean group or local entity meets the consolidated revenue threshold of KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country reporting applies where the group consolidated revenue of the ultimate parent reaches KRW 100 billion. Even below these thresholds, NTS may request transfer pricing documentation where high-risk transactions are identified

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a standalone offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and general anti-avoidance rules require the Chusik Hoesa to have real business activity in Korea. For tax holidays and DTA benefits, the entity must demonstrate actual core income-generating activities in Korea, including rail freight contracting, dispatch, customs clearance, invoicing, operational decision-making, and physical/logistical oversight. A shell Korean company whose profits are in substance controlled from abroad may be recharacterised, denied treaty benefits, subjected to controlled foreign company attribution, or treated as a transparent conduit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, a person who evades tax by fraud or wrongful act is punishable by imprisonment with labor for up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding five times the evaded tax; the corporation is also subject to a joint penal fine under Article 17. Additional taxes of up to 40% of the understated tax, daily overdue interest, and criminal seizure and forfeiture are imposed

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signed, directed, or facilitated the fraudulent return is personally exposed to criminal imprisonment and criminal fines. The National Tax Service may issue a departure restriction (travel Blacklisting), attach the director's personal bank accounts and real estate, and invoke secondary tax collection against the director's assets where corporate assets have been dissipated. Civil creditors may claim damages from the director jointly and severally under Commercial Act Article 401, and the corporate veil may be pierced where the Chusik Hoesa is operated as an instrument of tax evasion
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Artificial Profit Shifting in Cross-Border Rail Freight

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, NTS may adjust the transfer price to an arm's-length price by applying the most appropriate method and re-compute taxable income. The adjustment triggers additional corporate income tax of up to 40% under the understatement penalty regime, daily interest, and separate penalties for failure to file international transaction reports; fraudulent documentation may lead to criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute non-arm's-length intercompany agreements without adequate functional and economic analysis are jointly and severally liable to the company and third parties for resulting damages. If the corporate form is used to conceal the real foreign beneficiary, the court may disregard the separate legal personality and permit collection from directors. A director who knowingly signs false transfer pricing documentation faces criminal liability and can be blacklisted from international travel
Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Repatriation and Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The Korean payer, as statutory withholding agent, is held liable for the un-withheld tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act and must remit the shortfall together with a penalty of 10-20% of the un-withheld amount and daily overdue interest. If treaty benefits are denied, the non-resident recipient becomes subject to Korean tax collection, and deliberate treaty abuse triggers criminal liability under domestic anti-avoidance provisions and general tax fraud sanctions, including imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors who resolve to declare dividends, sign sham loan agreements, or approve abusive treaty relief claims are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax, additional taxes, and interest. The National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil to collect from the directors' personal assets where the company is insolvent, and directors may face criminal imprisonment and fines for wilful evasion. Departure restrictions (blacklisting) may be imposed pending full payment of the assessed tax and penalties

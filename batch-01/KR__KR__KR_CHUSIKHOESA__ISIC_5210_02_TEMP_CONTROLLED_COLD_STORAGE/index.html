Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act no longer imposes a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The operative rule is that each promoter must subscribe for shares and deposit the full allotment price into a dedicated 'capital subscription bank account' (blocked escrow account) before incorporation registration. Withdrawal is prohibited until the company obtains its Certificate of Registration from the Court Registry Office; after registration, the deposited capital is released to the corporate operating account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate of Completion and fire prevention plan approval from the National Fire Agency; civil defense disaster-management declaration for the protected cold storage facility; structural load certificate for pallet racks and insulated cold room panels; pressure vessel registration and pre-start inspection by the Korea Gas Safety Corporation for the ammonia refrigeration system; and KOSHA workplace safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, covering emergency lighting, fire suppression, ammonia gas detection, evacuation routes, and cold-room doors with 24-hour internal release latches.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr). Customs export clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident owner of a Chusik Hoesa, the Korean company must apply for business registration with the competent district tax office under the jurisdiction of its domestic place of business, or online through the NTS HomeTax portal. The application must be filed within 20 calendar days after the date of business commencement. Required documents include: the certificate of incorporation and articles of incorporation (with Korean translation), the statutory representative's government-issued identification and passport, proof of the registered domestic address or office lease, an apostilled or consularized certificate of foreign corporate registration, and a notarized power of attorney if a domestic tax representative is appointed. The NTS reviews the filing and issues a 10-digit business registration number (TIN) in the form XXX-XX-XXXXX, which serves simultaneously as the VAT identification number. The entity is then enrolled on HomeTax for electronic tax invoice issuance and quarterly VAT filing. A foreign entity without a permanent establishment must appoint a domestic tax representative before filing any national tax return.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not offer a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch entity classification election. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically treated as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and taxed at entity level on worldwide income. A qualified wholly-owned group may elect consolidated corporate tax filing, but this is not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general exemption threshold. Any person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea is mandatorily subject to VAT registration. A small entrepreneur with an annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000 may be treated as a simplified taxable person, but VAT registration and filing remain mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the Value Added Tax Act for exports of goods, where the goods are declared to Korea Customs Service as exports, the export declaration is accepted, ownership passes to a foreign buyer, and the goods leave Korea. Certain services supplied to non-residents are also zero-rated only where they fall within the statutory export service categories in the Enforcement Decree, such as services performed wholly outside Korea or supplied in connection with exported goods. For refrigerated cold-chain warehousing, the mere physical storage of domestic goods for a Korean customer is standard-rated VAT; zero-rating will apply only where the warehousing service is directly connected to goods being exported and the invoice is properly issued to a foreign recipient with supporting evidence.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry / Export Goods Declaration) accepted through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight issued to the foreign consignee
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange sales confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the declared export goods
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice issued through NTS HomeTax indicating the export nature of the supply

Every registered business must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) through NTS HomeTax for each taxable supply to another registered business. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day following the date of issuance. NTS validates the transmission and assigns a unique e-tax invoice submission key. Large taxpayers and high-volume issuers are expected to use NTS-approved electronic tax invoice APIs or integrated accounting solutions. Korea does not use a physical fiscal stamping device; the anti-fraud mechanism is the mandatory electronic pre-clearance and matching system in HomeTax. Credit card sales data and cash receipts must also be reported through the NTS collection systems on a periodic basis.

The NTS performs cross-matching between export declarations, e-tax invoices, shipping documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If the export zero-rating is claimed without the required documentation, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, the zero-rated output tax is retroactively converted to taxable output at 10% VAT. NTS may impose penalty tax under the Value Added Tax Act, assess statutory interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, claw back any refund paid, and refer willful false documentation to the prosecution authorities under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, which can lead to criminal fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Korean won is convertible on both current and capital account transactions but subject to reporting and declaration obligations rather than absolute convertibility. The Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance maintain a market-average KRW/USD rate mechanism; however, residents and non-residents are free to remit dividends, service fees, and trade proceeds once the underlying documents are verified. For a newly formed Chusik Hoesa engaged in temperature-controlled pharmaceutical warehousing, the main liquidity constraints are not convertibility but documentary compliance: foreign direct investment must be reported through KOTRA or a designated bank, and each capital remittance must be matched against the Foreign Investment Registry. Current account payments such as license fees for cooling technology or outsourcing to a third-party logistics affiliate are generally unrestricted but must be reported in the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN). Profit remittances of dividends require audited financial statements and board minutes; large outbound payments exceeding certain thresholds are subject to a real-document check by the bank. No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in Korea; one may hold receipts in foreign-currency accounts, although maturity periods apply. Therefore, the regime is open and liquidity-friendly, but treasury operations should be built around rigorous filing of every transaction to avoid administrative rejections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from trade (commodity export) must be collected within 180 days from the date of the export declaration unless an extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory percentage surrender requirement into KRW under the current liberalised framework; the exporter may retain the proceeds in a domestic or overseas foreign-currency account, but must repatriate the receipt to Korea unless approval or waiver exists. Proceeds must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information Network within 3 business days of receipt. The KRW conversion is executed at market rate; any unconverted balance held past the statutory deadline requires a bank-approved extension and may be subject to penalty for late collection.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; daily reporting infrastructure is operated by the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) and the Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute (KFTC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Non-bank fintech EMIs and foreign digital banks are not permitted to independently provide local KRW transaction accounts or treasury clearing services to Korean-resident entities without a bank license under the Korean Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or N26 can only serve limited remittance pre-funded corridors in collaboration with a licensed Korean bank; they cannot provide sovereign account capabilities, capital escrow verification, or official foreign exchange settlement codes required for the Chusik Hoesa. Hence all core treasury functions must be run through licensed domestic banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or capital investment must first be registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FITA) by the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or a designated bank. Capital should be wired via SWIFT MT103 or a correspondent transfer to the bank's capital escrow account using the correct foreign investment purpose code (Foreign Exchange Information System category) and the remitter must match the foreign investor named in the report. After the bank verifies the inbound wire, the capital verification certificate is issued by the designated bank; the funds are then transferred to the operational KRW account. Every inbound transfer must include the investor identifier, source-of-funds statement, and a bank reference number to satisfy KFTC reporting obligations within 3 business days. The investment funds are prohibited from being used for non-declared purposes; any deviation triggers a penalty.For outbound commercial payments from the KR Chusik Hoesa for refrigerated logistics equipment, lease deposits, inventory purchase, or procurement of warehousing technology, Korean banks route through SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes, and optionally use commercial Letter of Credit for high-value cross-border goods imports, or Documentary Collections where a tight commercial relationship is already established. The designated bank must have the underlying trade contract and customs declaration and issue a due report to the Foreign Exchange Information Network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% base rate up to 24% top marginal bracket; the progressive schedule is 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 21% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding tax on the gross amount of outbound dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, plus a 10% resident/local income tax surtax on the income tax, yielding an aggregate statutory rate of 22%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 of international related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation where the domestic corporation's annual revenue is at least KRW 100,000,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 100,000,000,000 and the group has overseas related-party transactions. Documentation must be filed with the National Tax Service within 45 days of the statutory corporate income tax return deadline.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to offshore financial centers, but the Korean tax authorities rigorously apply substance-over-form under Article 14 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, Article 52 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act. For refrigerated logistics warehousing under ISIC 5210, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be physically performed in Korea: the entity must hold the statutory logistics license, operate temperature-controlled premises with refrigeration equipment, employ local personnel such as refrigeration engineers and quality-assurance staff, maintain inventory monitoring systems, and make pricing and operational decisions through local board meetings. A Korean shell corporation that merely leases every asset from an offshore parent, holds no employees, and routes all profits abroad will be disregarded, and profits will be reattributed to the Korean permanent establishment with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Electronic Tax Invoices and Underreporting of Warehousing Revenue

Statutory sanction: Violation of Articles 10 and 11-2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act is punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding twice the amount of the evaded tax; where the evasion exceeds KRW 500,000,000, the maximum penalty increases to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine up to five times the tax evaded. The National Tax Service concurrently imposes a 40% negligence penalty on fraudulent underreporting and daily late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for abuse of the corporate form, and directors are jointly and severally liable for the full tax deficiency and penalties out of their personal assets. The NTS Commissioner may impose a travel ban (blacklisting) on the representative director, seize domestic and foreign personal bank accounts and real property, and refer the director for criminal prosecution, which carries personal imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a corporate officer in Korea.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Articles 4, 5, and 12 of the International Tax Adjustment Act authorize the National Tax Service to reassess taxable income at arm's length, with a 20% underreporting penalty and a 0.022% per-day late payment surcharge. Failure to submit Local File or Master File documentation can trigger separate civil fines up to KRW 50 million in addition to the tax adjustment. If the arrangement is found to be an abusive tax avoidance scheme, the GAAR may recharacterize the transaction and deny treaty benefits entirely.

Director personal liability: Courts will disregard corporateness where directors knowingly arranged sham pricing and stripped economic substance; directors are summoned to restore personally the diverted profits and pay the reassessed corporate tax from their own assets. The NTS may impose an exit/withholding order and place directors on the travel-ban blacklist. Willful evasion in excess of KRW 500 million exposes directors and the responsible accountant to imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to five times the evaded tax, and the criminal conviction voids any director-and-officer liability insurance coverage.
AML and Sanctions Non-Compliance in Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information, Articles 5 and 16, is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a criminal fine up to KRW 50,000,000; the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes separate penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment for unlicensed cross-border fund movements. The regulator may additionally revoke the statutory refrigerated logistics license, freeze the entity's banking facilities, and impose administrative fines, debarment from government logistics contracts, and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable as principals for knowingly directing or recklessly permitting the AML violation and face personal imprisonment terms; the CEO and compliance officer are subject to the judicial travel-ban and blacklisting system, personal bank account freezing, and confiscation of assets traceable to the unlawful payments. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as an instrument of crime, and directors must make full civil restitution to affected clients and regulators, with indemnification insurance void because the conduct is willful and unlawful.

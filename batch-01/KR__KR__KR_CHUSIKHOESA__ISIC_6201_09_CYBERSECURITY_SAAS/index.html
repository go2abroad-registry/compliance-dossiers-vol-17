Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not require any statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price for all subscribed shares must be paid in before incorporation registration, and the paid-in funds must be held in a blocked subscription escrow account at a commercial bank in Korea until the corporation is registered. For non-resident subscribers, foreign exchange and source-of-funds documentation is required. The registration license tax is computed with reference to the stated capital amount, so the actual official fee will vary; USD 215 is a conservative low-capital SaaS estimate including registration license tax, local education surtax, and court filing fees.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for the software activity itself; however, if a dedicated office is leased, the company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, register the workplace with the Ministry of Employment and Labor, and verify that the building maintains a valid fire safety inspection certificate issued by the National Fire Agency. The company should document emergency evacuation routes, fire extinguishers, electrical safety, and an employee emergency response plan. For a pure cybersecurity SaaS activity with no handling of hazardous substances, no special Civil Defense clearance is required. As a sector-level best practice, the company should also maintain information security management system certification (ISMS) or ISMS-P under the Information and Communications Network Act if it processes or provides security services concerning Korean personal information.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) in South Korea must first incorporate at the court registry (deung-gi) and register with the local tax office. Upon incorporation, the company automatically receives a corporate tax identification number (business registration number, sa-eop-ja-deung-rok-beon-ho) issued by the district tax office under the NTS HomeTax system. For a branch or domestic place of business of a foreign corporation without a separate local entity, the foreign corporation must register for a domestic business place number via HomeTax by submitting the foreign corporation certificate, articles of incorporation, and proof of local place of business. The representative must complete the application with the certificate of incorporation, bylaws, identity documents, and power of attorney where applicable; the NTS issues the registration number within a few business days. VAT registration is generally effected automatically as part of the business registration process, and the business registration number serves as the VAT identification number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from South Korea may be zero-rated for VAT upon compliance with export customs clearance requirements. Qualifying services, including information technology and cybersecurity SaaS services to non-resident customers, may also be zero-rated under the 'export of services' regime where the consideration is received in foreign currency and the services are supplied to a non-resident party; the place of supply rules deem services that are physically performed or used outside South Korea, or supplied to a non-resident who is not a Korean domestic business, as zero-rated export services. The SaaS vendor must maintain service contracts, evidence of the non-resident status of the customer, and records proving the foreign currency consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent foreign exchange receipt evidence

South Korea operates an e-invoice (electronic tax invoice) mandate through the HomeTax system. All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices via the NTS national e-invoice platform or an NTS-certified ERP/API integration for all B2B supplies. Real-time transmission is required; the NTS validates the digital invoice, assigns a unique invoice number, and applies anti-fraud digital stamping through the automated e-invoice enforcement system (national e-invoice system). Failure to issue or transmit e-invoices can trigger penalties, including non-deductibility of input VAT and administrative fines.

The NTS may audit export zero-rating claims and require full documentation to substantiate the export or deemed export. In cases of fraudulent or unverified zero-rating, the NTS will assess the unpaid VAT (10%) retroactively, plus additional charges of up to 40% of the unpaid tax depending on the degree of intentionality or negligence, and impose criminal penalties for fraudulent evasion under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Additionally, the unwarranted refund may be clawed back with statutory interest and the supplier's VAT registration may be subject to enhanced monitoring or suspension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized and highly convertible foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in services, dividend remittances, royalties, and software subscription payments, subject to mandatory reporting through a domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are also largely liberalized, but require prior notification or regulatory filing depending on the nature and size of the transaction. Foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa is permitted without prior approval under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, provided the investor files a notification with a designated foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Strategy and Finance. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds, and exporters may retain foreign currency in approved foreign currency deposit accounts. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for non-resident shareholders once audited earnings are confirmed and the applicable 22 percent corporate income tax plus 15.4 percent dividend withholding tax is settled. However, the bank must verify the source of funds, confirm the transaction purpose code, and ensure that the remittance is within the remittance schedule stated in the foreign investment report. For cybersecurity SaaS businesses, service-related software license payments qualify as current account receivables and remit freely upon presentation of the underlying contract, tax invoice, and Korean export logistics confirmation. Overall, convertibility is high but compliance with the foreign exchange information report system is non-negotiable for every cross-border wire and escrow release.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within six months from the date of export declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. One extension of up to six months may be granted by the head of the designated foreign exchange bank based on commercial rationale. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a foreign currency account, convert it at market rates, or use it directly for import settlements, provided the receipt is reported to the foreign exchange bank electronically.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) acting as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

As of the current regulatory landscape, fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks are not legally permitted to provide full corporate banking, foreign exchange custody, or capital verification services to a Korean Chusik Hoesa. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act assigns foreign exchange administration duties exclusively to domestic licensed banks and registered foreign exchange institutions. Offshore EMIs may offer foreign-currency payment collection for SaaS subscriptions only if they partner with a licensed Korean payment gateway, but they cannot act as a substitute for a tier-one commercial bank account, escrow repository, or foreign exchange purpose-code reporting engine. Feasibility is therefore Low for treasury-critical functions such as equity injection escrow, FX conversion, and regulatory filings. These EMIs should be limited to auxiliary collection channels for merchant SaaS payments routed into the domestic operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident investor injecting capital into the Korean Chusik Hoesa, the compliant routing is as follows: execute a foreign direct investment notification through a designated foreign exchange bank using the official Form for Investment Report, then transmit the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 with the Korean FX purpose code for acquisition of stocks in a locally incorporated foreign-invested company. The remittance beneficiary must be the foreign-invested company's capital escrow account or temporary receipt account at the domestic tier-one bank. The bank issues a capital verification certificate only after the full inward remittance matches the declared investment amount and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting has been accepted by the Bank of Korea through the electronic system. The funds may then be moved into the operational won account for lawful expenditure. For larger professional services or co-development agreements above the agreed treasury threshold, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the Korean bank and confirmed by an offshore bank can support settlement. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, a compulsory independent CPA audit is legally required if, at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year, the corporation has total assets of KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9 million) or more, or total liabilities of KRW 7 billion (approximately USD 5.3 million) or more, or annual revenue of KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million) or more; alternatively, mandatory audit is triggered if the company has 300 or more average full-time-equivalent employees. Listed companies, financial institutions, insurance/securities companies, public-interest corporations designated by Presidential Decree, and companies otherwise regulated under the Financial Services Commission are subject to audit regardless of size. For companies meeting these triggers, the audit must be performed by an independent Korean registered CPA firm, and the resulting audited financial statements must be filed with the FSS and presented at the AGM.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing a combined top marginal rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% final withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder, plus 2% local income tax surcharge, being 10% of the national withholding tax, producing a combined statutory withholding burden of 22% before treaty application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions for the fiscal year exceed KRW 5,000,000,000; Master File is additionally required when the Korean entity's annual revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 or foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 50,000,000,000; an international transaction summary schedule is triggered at KRW 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Korean law does not operate an UAE-style standalone ESR register, but economic substance is enforced through the general anti-avoidance rule, substance-over-form jurisprudence, controlled foreign corporation rules, and beneficial ownership requirements in Korean tax treaties. A cybersecurity SaaS Chusik Hoesa must maintain Korean premises, an operating board, risk-bearing capital, original data and contracts, and actual cybersecurity engineers; outsourced SaaS functions booked in a low-tax affiliate without real management authority will be recharacterized, treaty relief denied, and royalties disallowed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal referral under the Korean Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, yielding imprisonment and fines up to multiples of the evaded tax; civil assessments include a 20% non-filing penalty and a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty on the deficient tax, plus late-payment surcharges and accrued interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or approve fraudulent invoices become jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under the responsible-party rule; personal bank accounts, real estate, and overseas assets may be seized, departures from Korea may be blocked by travel bans, and egregious cases lead to imprisonment.

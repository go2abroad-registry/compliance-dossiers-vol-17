Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital under Commercial Act Article 329 for a Chusik Hoesa; share capital may be set at any amount above the par value per share, which must be at least KRW 100. No blocked escrow account is required by the Commercial Act. However, if foreign-invested enterprise status is desired under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the foreign investor must subscribe at least KRW 100,000,000 of newly issued shares. The full share subscription price must be deposited with a domestic bank during incorporation under Article 318, and the bank must issue a capital payment certificate; funds are released only upon registration of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a Fire Safety Approval and Firefighting Systems Completion Certificate under the Fire Service Act for the warehouse premises, including automatic fire alarm, sprinkler system, emergency evacuation signage, and extinguishers. If flammable adhesives, thinners, or packaging solvents exceed exempt quantities, obtain a dangerous goods storage permit. Secure building occupancy approval and any use-change permit from the municipal building division confirming structural load capacity for warehouse racking systems. File an occupational safety and health notification with the Ministry of Employment and Labor under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, provide machine guarding, electrical safety inspections, emergency exit plans, and worker training. If the workplace regularly employs 50 or more workers, prepare and submit a severe accident prevention plan under the Serious Accidents Punishment Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax at hometax.go.kr. Export customs clearance and proof reside with the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS electronic clearance system at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity commencing a taxable business in Korea must file the statutory Business Registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its domestic place of business, or through the HomeTax portal, no later than 20 days after business commencement. The competent tax office then issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the corporate TIN and VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity, the application package must include the certified corporate charter or registration certificate, articles of incorporation, board resolution confirming the appointment of a Korean tax representative and the domestic place of business, power of attorney for the tax representative, lease or title evidence for the domestic place of business, and where an inbound capital contribution is present, the Foreign Investment Report issued under the foreign investment regime. If the foreign non-resident has no domestic place of business but is required to remit Korean VAT, it must separately register through a tax representative. The Korean target entity is a Chusik Hoesa that receives its own 10-digit Business Registration Number at incorporation through the district tax office; no additional non-resident procurement procedure is required for that local entity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; the Republic of Korea does not offer a check-the-box pass-through election or any hybrid entity classification election to a Chusik Hoesa. The entity is always a resident corporation taxed at entity level and is a separate person for VAT purposes. Korean group consolidation under the Corporate Tax Act is an elective corporate income tax filing mechanism only and does not alter indirect tax classification. Korea applies OECD hybrid mismatch rules as mandatory anti-abuse rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold triggers VAT registration for a corporation. VAT registration is mandatory upon commencement of any taxable supply in Korea. The simplified taxable person threshold of KRW 80,000,000 annual revenue applies only to small individual entrepreneurs and is not available to a Chusik Hoesa. The operative registration trigger for this entity is the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value Added Tax Act, a 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Korea after acceptance of an export declaration by the Korea Customs Service; (ii) services supplied to non-resident clients where the service is performed outside Korea or used wholly outside Korea, including qualifying cross-border digital services delivered from Korea to overseas end users; (iii) certain international transport and foreign-exchange-earning services specified by Presidential Decree. For the entity's light value-added postponement packaging, kitting, and assembly services, zero-rating requires that the kitted or assembled finished goods are in fact exported, that the UNI-PASS export declaration reflects the export, that the customer is a non-resident or the foreign establishment of an overseas principal, and that the consideration is received in foreign currency or credited to a Korean foreign-currency account in compliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Merely storing, kitting, or assembling goods that are subsequently delivered or consumed inside Korea does not qualify for zero-rating and remains standard-rated at 10%.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (UNI-PASS electronic export release certificate; the statutory outward-shipment bill-of-entry equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Korean customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank-issued Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the invoiced currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Purchase Order or exporter/importer contract evidencing the foreign sale
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through HomeTax

Korea mandates electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-Invoice system or an NTS-certified electronic tax invoice service provider. All B2B taxable supplies, including zero-rated export supplies, must be recorded in an electronic tax invoice; the invoice data must be transmitted to NTS by the day immediately following statutory issuance, and NTS registers the e-invoice with an authentication number used for VAT audit matching. Korea does not operate per-transaction government pre-clearance of invoices, but the mandatory e-invoice transmission, the NTS-issued authentication number, and the KCS UNI-PASS export clearance file together form the statutory anti-fraud digital stamping and audit-trail package. ERP/API integration with approved HomeTax e-Invoice service providers is the practical filing interface for a fulfillment and kitting operation.

NTS and KCS cross-match the HomeTax zero-rated e-invoices against the UNI-PASS export declarations and the bank foreign-exchange receipt records. If the documentation is absent, false, inconsistent, or not received within the 180-day repatriation monitoring window for audit-proven zero-rating, NTS may deny the zero-rating and issue a corrected assessment at 10% VAT on the supply value, add statutory additional tax and interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act, and reverse any VAT refund already paid. Fraudulent or sham export dealings attract criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, extension of the assessment period from five years to ten years, loss of future zero-rating privileges, and intensified KCS customs examination of subsequent export consignments.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a rules-based foreign exchange system with full convertibility on current-account transactions such as trade settlements, service charges, interest, dividends, and royalties, but capital-account transactions remain subject to prior notification, registration, or licensing through a designated foreign exchange bank. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa involved in fulfillment kitting and assembly, ordinary operating payments to overseas suppliers, warehousing, and logistics vendors are generally unrestricted once the transaction has a genuine underlying invoice and customs declaration. Foreign direct investment equity remittances require the investor to submit a foreign investment notification through KOTRA or the receiving Korean bank, establish a foreign-investment designated account, and obtain a Foreign Investment Confirmation before capital verification. Dividend or profit remittances are lawful after the company has undergone annual external audit if required, satisfied corporate income tax liabilities, obtained shareholder meeting approval on dividend distribution, and presented tax payment certificates and financial statements to the remitting bank. There is no outright expropriation risk, but capital movement without proper documentation is treated as an illegal foreign exchange transaction under Article 30 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, subject to penalties and adverse KYC flagging. Non-resident investors may open offshore accounts and receive USD or other hard currency remittances without forced KRW conversion, but any onshore KRW borrowing or conversion must be backed by eligible underlying transactions. Overall, the regime is best assessed as a managed-float jurisdiction with an open current account and regulated capital account requiring documentary discipline rather than outright prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and settled through a designated foreign exchange bank within 90 days from the date of export declaration, with a maximum extension of up to 6 months where justified and approved by the relevant foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory fixed ratio of export proceeds that must be surrendered or converted into KRW; exporters may retain export earnings in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts, although every receipt must be reported through the foreign exchange information system with the correct export settlement purpose code. Failure to receive proceeds within the applicable statutory timeline may require an ex-post extension report or civil/administrative penalty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank), operating in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the principal foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary statutory banking relationship. Foreign digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex are not authorized in South Korea to open domestic KRW-denominated corporate bank accounts, issue local Korean account numbers, or perform statutory foreign-investment reporting and capital verification on behalf of a Chusik Hoesa. Such platforms can only operate in South Korea through licensed local partner banks, meaning their practical role is limited to auxiliary cross-border payment aggregation, payout execution, or multi-currency collection, while the legal entity must maintain its core treasury and all statutory FX compliance obligations with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Any attempt to route FDI equity injection or tax-related profit remittance solely through a foreign EMI will trigger rejection at the Korean receiving bank and regulatory scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment into the Chusik Hoesa must begin with a foreign investment notification filed through KOTRA or the designated Korean foreign exchange bank before the remittance is sent. The investor's bank should transmit a SWIFT MT103 to the Korean bank's foreign-investment designated account, with the remittance information field clearly stating 'equity contribution', the name of the Korean Chusik Hoesa, and the foreign investment notification or declaration number. Upon credit, the Korean bank verifies the identity of the remitter, confirms the FDI declaration, issues a Foreign Investment Confirmation or Certificate of Foreign Currency Deposit, and only then permits conversion to KRW or subscription payment to the company's capital account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary statutory banking relationship. Foreign digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex are not authorized in South Korea to open domestic KRW-denominated corporate bank accounts, issue local Korean account numbers, or perform statutory foreign-investment reporting and capital verification on behalf of a Chusik Hoesa. Such platforms can only operate in South Korea through licensed local partner banks, meaning their practical role is limited to auxiliary cross-border payment aggregation, payout execution, or multi-currency collection, while the legal entity must maintain its core treasury and all statutory FX compliance obligations with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Any attempt to route FDI equity injection or tax-related profit remittance solely through a foreign EMI will trigger rejection at the Korean receiving bank and regulatory scrutiny.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% - 24% progressive corporate income tax, plus 10% local income tax surtax; resulting effective headline rates of 9.9% to 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% dividend income tax plus 2% local income tax surtax) for outbound dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to non-resident shareholders; bilateral double tax treaties generally reduce the rate to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on shareholding and treaty terms

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions generally triggers formal Local File and Master File preparation obligations; a Country-by-Country Report is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion. Below the statutory threshold, the Korean entity must still maintain contemporaneous arm's length records and produce them to the National Tax Service on request.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose Gulf Cooperation Council-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is enforced through Korean tax jurisprudence on place of effective management, permanent establishment creation, controlled foreign corporation rules, and substance-over-form anti-abuse doctrines. A Korean Chusik Hoesa that lacks real business premises, employees, board activity, and actual direction and control in Korea, or an offshore related party that lacks genuine Core Income-Generating Activities, can be recharacterized as a sham, a domestic corporation, or a conduit for treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Materially False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 9; Corporate Tax Act Article 76 penalty tax of 10% to 40% of the understated tax; daily late-payment surcharge of 0.022%; aggravated imprisonment of not less than 3 years plus a fine of up to 3 times the tax evaded where the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or participated in the false return are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties under Korean Commercial Act Articles 399 and 401; civil collection extends to personal assets; the National Tax Service may impose a travel ban and blacklist foreign directors; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and potential international extradition while the corporate veil is pierced as an intentional unlawful act.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 2, 4, 4-3, and 14 authorize the National Tax Service to disregard the pricing arrangement, make an arm's length adjustment, add back interest, and impose penalty taxes on unreported or understated cross-border related-party transactions; failure to submit required Master File/Local File documentation carries separate reporting penalties and shifts the burden of proof against the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Where the arrangement is found to be a sham and the foreign recipient has no CIGA, directors responsible for the false documentation can be held personally liable for the adjusted corporate tax under National Tax Collection Act civil collection procedures; willful false or backdated TP documentation exposes directors to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment, asset seizure, and travel restrictions.
Unreported Dividend Repatriation, Foreign Exchange Violation, and AML Exposure

Statutory sanction: The shareholder tax withheld must be assessed against the Korean payor at 22%, plus a negligence penalty of 10% and daily late-payment surcharge; Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 17 punishes unauthorized outward remittances with imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million; Financial Transaction Reports Act violations for intentional failure to report suspicious transactions carry imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in unreported dividend remittances are personally liable for the un-withheld tax and penalties; the National Tax Service can collect from personal assets, blacklist the directors, and impose travel bans; where the remittance structure involves laundering of corporate secrecy or false documentation, directors face criminal imprisonment terms, fines, and jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction extradition risk.

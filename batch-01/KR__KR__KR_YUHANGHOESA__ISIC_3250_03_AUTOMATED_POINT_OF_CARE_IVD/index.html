Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the total initial capital subscribed by the founding members must be actually contributed in cash or in kind before the commercial registration is completed, must be identified in the Articles of Incorporation, and must be deposited in a domestic bank account in the name of the company or for the account of the company during formation. For non-resident members, if the aggregate foreign investment is at least KRW 100 million, advance or post-closing notice to KOTRA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act may be required, and banks may require source-of-funds documentation for the blocked or provisional deposit.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, the Korean Tax Identification Number is the 10-digit Business Registration Number issued by the district tax office. The Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) must appoint a domestic place of business or a Korean tax agent, file the business registration application through HomeTax or in person at the competent district tax office within 20 days of commencement of business, and submit the entity formation articles, proof of foreign registration, ownership and director identification, power of attorney, and lease or proof of the Korean place of business. Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration turnover threshold; every person commencing a business in Korea must register with the competent district tax office from the date of business commencement. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to tangible goods exported from Korea when title and risk pass to a foreign buyer, the goods are physically shipped from Korea, and the exporter retains the official export declaration from Korea Customs Service. Qualifying offshore services and services directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated where the benefit and use occur outside Korea. For the IVD microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers, the exporter must prove the foreign destination, actual customs clearance, and receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the export sale.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Electronic Customs Clearance Number / Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment
  • Commercial invoice including product description, HS code, quantity, and foreign sale price
  • Packing list for the exported IVD consignment
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of Origin or other destination-customs documentation if required by the importing country

All B2B tax invoices must be issued electronically through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system and transmitted to the NTS by no later than the day after the supply date. Korea does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping model; instead, the NTS cross-matches e-tax invoices, credit card sales data, and cash receipt records. Foreign suppliers registered domestically must also issue e-tax invoices when making domestic taxable supplies. Failure to issue or transmit e-tax invoices triggers penalties based on the supply value.

If export documents are invalid, incomplete, or fraudulent, the NTS and Korea Customs Service will deny the 0% rate, reassess VAT at 10%, and impose additional tax penalties of up to 40% of the understated tax for dishonest returns. False invoices and fraudulent export refunds may trigger criminal tax evasion penalties, fines, imprisonment, and retrospective clawback of refunded amounts with statutory interest. Export proceeds not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period may also be treated as unreported income and result in both corporate income tax and VAT exposure, plus foreign exchange and trade regulation penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a mostly liberalized current account convertibility regime. Import and export payments, service fees, royalties, and other commercial settlements are freely convertible through authorized foreign exchange banks provided the underlying transaction is documented and reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, intercompany loans, and cross-border financing, require notification or registration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea depending on the instrument. Korean residents are obliged to report foreign currency receipts and disbursements to the authorized bank; the bank transmits the declaration to the authorities. Corporate profit remittances, such as dividends and liquidation proceeds for registered foreign investment, are generally permitted subject to actual dividend evidence, corporate income tax clearance, and the foreign investment registration certificate. The principal practical hurdle for treasury operations is the limited offshore accessibility of Korean won. Because won is not fully convertible in offshore markets, most cross-border trade and capital flows are settled in major delivery currencies, typically US dollars. Non-resident entities must also ensure their beneficial ownership declarations are consistent with the bank's Anti-Money Laundering file, as Korean regulators require the ultimate beneficial owner to be disclosed and current. There is no blanket surrender rule requiring converters to sell all export proceeds into won; exporters may hold their proceeds in foreign exchange accounts, but the original export claim must be collected within the statutory window and reported. Overall, current account flows face moderate friction; capital flows face moderate to high documentary friction but are legally transactable when fully registered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must physically be received to a foreign exchange bank in Korea or into a Korean exporter's certified foreign currency account within six months from the date of the export declaration, unless an extension period has been approved by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no compulsory conversion ratio into Korean won; the exporter may retain the proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert them as needed. The collection and arrival of proceeds must be reported to the foreign exchange bank and through the electronic Foreign Exchange Information System to satisfy the export settlement requirement.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK); with policy authority jointly administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - Materially Non-Viable. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions have no direct regulatory license under Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They are not authorized to hold Korean won deposits, issue Korean won settlement accounts, or provide local payment and current account services to a Korean-domesticated legal entity. A foreign EMI may only enter through licensing or an approved partnership with an authorized Korean commercial bank, and even then the actual clearing must sit with the domestic bank. For this IVD manufacturing operation, the safe route is to use a licensed tier-one domestic bank as the primary treasury and clearing house; the EMI is not a compliant standalone clearing alternative.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must follow the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The foreign investor files a foreign investment declaration before or within two months after receiving the funds. The capital amount must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own regulated bank account to the chosen tier-one Korean bank. The purpose of payment should be stated as Foreign Direct Investment, capital contribution, or paid-in capital increase, using the Korean foreign exchange transaction purpose code that matches equity investment. After the bank confirms receipt, the investor obtains a certificate of foreign investment registration and the entity completes capital verification by engaging a Korean certified public accountant. The verified capital must remain in the designated foreign investment escrow account and cannot be released for operational expenses until registration is completed.Outbound trade disbursements will clear primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers, Commercial Letters of Credit opened at the Korean bank under MT700 instructions, and documentary collections under the Uniform Rules for Collections. For high-value IVD analyzer shipments, a confirmed commercial LC provides settlement certainty. For repetitive OEM component purchases, SWIFT MT103 transfers against matched invoices and packing documents are acceptable. All outbound payments require a valid tax invoice, customs export declaration, foreign exchange purpose code, and the operational contract or purchase order proving the underlying trade. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal national Corporate Income Tax ('CIT') rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; progressive rates are 9% for the first KRW 200 million, 19% for KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% for KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion, exclusive of the 10% local income tax surtax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory withholding on dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax (10% of the 20% national tax).

Tax Evasion Through Shell Manufacturing and Artificial Loss Allocation

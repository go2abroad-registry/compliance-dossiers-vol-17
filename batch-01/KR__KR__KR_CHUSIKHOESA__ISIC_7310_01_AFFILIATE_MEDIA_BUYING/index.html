Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act of Korea imposes no minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, all issued shares must be fully subscribed and the full subscription price must be paid before incorporation registration. Cash contributions are deposited in a capital-subscription bank account that behaves as a blocked escrow until the Capital Payment Deposit Certificate is issued and the registration is completed. In-kind contributions must be appraised and fully delivered or registered in the name of the corporation prior to filing. Withdrawal of the capital before registration is prohibited except where the incorporation is lawfully abandoned.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; primary online portal is HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Customs export clearance is supervised by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

The Chusik Hoesa is a Korean domestic corporation, so the statutory TIN/VAT ID procedure is: (i) incorporate under the Commercial Act at the competent district court and obtain a Certificate of Corporate Registration evidencing foreign ownership; (ii) complete the Foreign Investment Promotion Act report through a designated Korean foreign exchange bank when the non-resident shareholder remits capital; (iii) within 20 days of commencing the media-buying business, submit the business registration application to the district tax office with jurisdiction over the head office using HomeTax; (iv) receive the NTS-issued 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the unified corporate income tax TIN, VAT registration number, and e-tax invoice issuing identifier; (v) register the principal place of business and open a foreign exchange settlement account with a Korean bank for export receipts; (vi) register for electronic tax invoice issuance through HomeTax or an NTS-approved service provider. For an actual foreign non-resident entity with no Korean place of business, the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative and apply to NTS for a non-resident supplier registration number before making taxable supplies to Korean customers; that number is VAT-specific and does not create Korean corporate residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Chusik Hoesa is a Korean domestic corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income. Foreign shareholders may own the entity but the Korean legal entity cannot elect pass-through or consolidated group treatment in the absence of statutory group taxation applicable to the structure.

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not maintain a check-the-box or LLC pass-through election analogous to the United States. A Chusik Hoesa is taxed automatically as a corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and no elective hybrid mismatch treatment is available to the taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold. Korean VAT registration is mandatory for every business operator from the date of business commencement, irrespective of revenue; exporters must register even if their only supplies are zero-rated in order to claim input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating is allowed for: (a) goods physically exported from Korea when the exporter obtains a KCS export declaration; (b) services performed or provided for use wholly outside Korea, including qualifying offshore digital services, provided the recipient is a non-resident and the Korean supplier has documentary evidence of overseas use; (c) supplies related to international transportation, as applicable. For the performance-media-buying and affiliate lead generation business, the zero-rate is substantiated by proving the foreign client, non-Korean consumption of the campaign services, and foreign-currency settlement; services directly enjoyed in Korea by Korean internet users may be subject to VAT or reverse charge and cannot be zero-rated merely because the contractual counterparty is foreign.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration and export clearance number (equivalent of a customs export Bill of Entry) - required only for physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list signed by the exporter
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical removal from Korean customs territory
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange settlement certificate or inward remittance certificate from a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • For digital advertising / affiliate media services: signed service agreement, media-buying delivery and performance report, and foreign-currency payment settlement evidence (no customs Bill of Entry is required)

Mandatory e-tax invoice: VAT-registered suppliers must issue an electronic tax invoice for B2B supplies and transmit it to NTS through HomeTax or an NTS-approved ASP by the day following the issuance date. The NTS matches e-tax invoice data, cash-receipt data, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange records. There is no real-time fiscal clearing device, fiscal signature, or anti-fraud stamp physically embedded in every Korean VAT invoice; compliance is achieved through electronic transmission and data audit rather than per-invoice real-time authorization.

NTS audit protocol cross-checks UNI-PASS export declarations, HomeTax e-tax invoices, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating cannot be substantiated, NTS recasts the supply as domestic taxable, assesses 10% VAT plus additional tax under the VAT Act, disallows or claws back corresponding input VAT, imposes electronic invoice penalties for untransmitted or incorrectly transmitted e-tax invoices, and may prosecute fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The VAT refund already paid to the exporter is recovered with interest, and export proceeds outstanding after the 180-day settlement deadline without an extension are treated as a Foreign Exchange Transactions Act violation that can also trigger review of the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but regulated convertibility regime. Current-account transactions, including trade in digital advertising services and administrative charges, are generally freely convertible and may be settled in KRW or foreign currency through a licensed foreign exchange bank. Financial-market (capital-account) transactions, which include inbound direct investment, cross-border loans, issuance of securities, and dividend remittances, are subject to prior reporting or declaration thresholds under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. In practice, the Ministry of Economy and Finance and Bank of Korea delegate most reporting to domestic commercial banks, which act as foreign exchange banks. Profit remittance is possible without central-bank licensing, but is hindered by documentary requirements: the Korean entity must submit audited financial statements or tax receipts, withholding tax payment certificates, an external audit report if required under the Act on External Audit, and a resolution of the board of directors approving the dividend. A foreign exchange bank will then endorse the dividend remittance declaration. No separate central bank approval is generally required for ordinary dividends, but funds derived from capital transactions must be supported by underlying documentation to avoid disguised outward remittance. Accordingly, the won is effectively convertible for trading and profit repatriation, with procedural friction centered on KYC and FX reporting rather than absolute convertibility denial.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; 0% of export proceeds must be surrendered in KRW. All export-related foreign currency receipts must be collected within six months from the date of export declaration and must be either sold to a domestic foreign exchange bank or credited to a foreign currency account established with such a bank; extension and rollover are permitted upon bank certification and regulatory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking relationship. Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Wise, and Airwallex are not chartered as commercial banks in South Korea and are not licensed to execute corporate foreign exchange transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korean regulations require cross-border remittance and FX settlement to be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank; unauthorized cross-border payment services can expose the delivering institution to criminal and administrative sanctions. EMIs may only serve as complementary rails for low-value operational payments to overseas ad networks, and cannot receive foreign direct investment, satisfy statutory capital verification, or replace the Korean domestic bank account required for tax and regulatory filings. Therefore the legal onboarding blueprint must rely on domestic Tier-1 banks for the core treasury, with EMIs confined to non-qualifying overflow channels strictly as a supplementary option.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must structure the capital contribution as a foreign direct investment (FDI) report filed with a delegated domestic foreign exchange bank before or at the time of receipt. The funds are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 wire with the beneficiary and reference details clearly indicating 'FDI under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Article 15 of the Foreign Investment Promotion Act'. The Korean corporation opens a temporary foreign investment capital account with a Tier-1 bank, receives the incoming KRW or USD, and then files the Foreign Investment Enterprise Registration after the new shares are issued. The bank marks the purpose code in accordance with the Bank of Korea Foreign Exchange Transaction Classification, and a Korean certified public accountant may issue a capital verification report if requested by the bank or the foreign exchange authorities. This routing ensures statutory approval, correct FX designation, and clean capital verification for subsequent dividend remittance.For digital advertising media buying and affiliate services, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with the underlying service agreement, invoices, and tax certificates attached for the foreign exchange bank's documentary review. For physical-goods trade or media procurement requiring transactional evidence, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections may be used through the same Tier-1 bank. Payments for services above USD 50,000 require submission of the contract, invoice, and perhaps a certified copy of the Korean trade report to the bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Settlement is normally executed in USD or KRW through the bank's correspondent network; crypto-asset settlement or routing through unauthorised electronic money institutions is prohibited. The account bank must file the outbound payment declaration before returning the MT103 confirmation to the remitter.Onshore USD/KRW forward contracts (covered forwards up to 12 months) permissible only when there is a genuine underlying current-account or capital-account exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted for non-resident counterparties hedging KRW-denominated exposures or for resident exporters with a documented USD receivableCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) and USD/KRW cross-currency swaps with licensed foreign exchange banks for long-term balance-sheet hedging of foreign capital injections; speculative use is expressly prohibited

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary banking relationship. Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Wise, and Airwallex are not chartered as commercial banks in South Korea and are not licensed to execute corporate foreign exchange transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korean regulations require cross-border remittance and FX settlement to be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank; unauthorized cross-border payment services can expose the delivering institution to criminal and administrative sanctions. EMIs may only serve as complementary rails for low-value operational payments to overseas ad networks, and cannot receive foreign direct investment, satisfy statutory capital verification, or replace the Korean domestic bank account required for tax and regulatory filings. Therefore the legal onboarding blueprint must rely on domestic Tier-1 banks for the core treasury, with EMIs confined to non-qualifying overflow channels strictly as a supplementary option.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required if foreign-related party transactions in any category exceed KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year. Master File is required if total revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and foreign-related party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion. Simplified documentation applies for transactions below KRW 5 billion, but the taxpayer must still prepare contemporaneous evidence upon NTS audit.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime analogous to UAE, but the Corporate Tax Act and the Framework Act on International Tax incorporate a substantive economic-substance requirement and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR). The NTS will pierce transactions lacking commercial rationale, with Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) defined by: the location of board meetings, where ad-buying employees perform their duties, where the algorithmic bidding servers are legally owned, where media contracts are signed, and where treasury/finance staff exercise cash management. Shell media-buying structures where a Korean Chusik Hoesa merely invoices foreign affiliate networks, while employees and technology remain outside Korea, will be recharacterized or assessed under CFC rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsified Expense Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment for up to 3 years or a fine not exceeding 3 times the evaded tax. Under the Corporate Tax Act, a 20% misstatement penalty and daily late-payment surcharge of 0.03% apply. Intentional fabrication also triggers Article 178 of the Criminal Act for false tax document creation.

Director personal liability: Korean courts shall pierce the corporate veil under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 to hold directors personally liable for gross negligence or malicious behavior. The NTS may collect unpaid corporate tax directly from the director's personal assets and may impose a travel ban (blacklisting) for tax delinquency above KRW 500 million. Convicted directors face imprisonment up to 3 years, disqualification from serving as directors, and disgorgement of personal wealth traceable to the offence.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Articles 4 and 5 of the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs (ACITA): the NTS imposes upward arm's length income adjustment with 10-year limitations. Penalties include 20% of the unreported adjustment plus 0.03% daily surcharge. ACITA also authorizes secondary adjustment re-characterizing unrepatriated profits as deemed dividends or loans, triggering further withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fabricated intercompany agreements are individually liable under Commercial Act Article 401 for damage caused to the Korean entity and creditors. The court can order restoration of foreign assets and personal seizure of the director's bank accounts. Criminal antitrust/intentional tax evasion may result in imprisonment up to 3 years and a travel ban; the NTS can also block dispatch to overseas business due to delinquent status.
Misuse of DTA / Shell Company Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: NTS denies treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership requirement and the GAAR in Article 10-2 ACITA. The taxpayer must pay the full 22% withholding plus 20% deficiency penalty and daily late surcharge. The foreign recipient is treated as having a Korean permanent establishment, and 100% of the income becomes subject to Korean corporate tax through branch filings.

Director personal liability: Third-party creditor claims and the Korean entity's claims can be levied directly against directors who authorized the sham transaction. Under the Commercial Act and tax crime law, directors face joint and several liability for the hidden tax liability, criminal imprisonment up to 3 years, and restriction of departure. The court may pierce the foreign holding entity's corporate veil and seize the personal bank accounts of Korean-resident directors and all title over Korean assets.

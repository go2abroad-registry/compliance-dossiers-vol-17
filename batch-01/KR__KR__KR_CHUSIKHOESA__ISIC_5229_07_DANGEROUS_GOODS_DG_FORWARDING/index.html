Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a high-risk dangerous-goods forwarding licensee, a virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address is not acceptable. The National Tax Service requires a physical head office for business registration under the Value-Added Tax Act, and the local Gu tax office will require a signed lease, premises photographs, and evidence of operational capacity. Korean banks will refuse to open and maintain a corporate account for a DG forwarder using a virtual office because the AML/KYC examination cannot verify operational substance. The transport licensing regulator, under Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport and Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries oversight, also requires on-site offices where DG declarations, safety data sheets, transport contracts, bills of lading, hazmat emergency plans, and AML/CFT compliance records are retained and accessible. Virtual office use is therefore rejected for this industry and would materially trigger enhanced bank due diligence, license refusal, and tax de-registration risk.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa, and no blocked escrow account is required for ordinary formation. However, the aggregate subscription price for initial shares must be fully credited to a domestic corporate bank account before the incorporation registration is filed. For a dangerous goods forwarding entity operating under ISIC 5229, the business is required to maintain a financial guarantee or liability insurance policy covering DG transport accidents, warehouse fire risks, environmental pollution, and chemical spills; this financial capacity requirement functions as a de facto capital-support rule.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Permit for Dangerous Goods Storage Sites must be obtained from the local Fire Department, including structural load inspection and fire resistance rating certification for warehouse racks. If the site appears on the national emergency risk register, civil defense protective compliance is mandatory. KOSHA approval requires on-site workplace environmental measurement and dangerous goods handling approval, and the emergency response and spill containment plan must be approved by the local Fire Department and the Ministry of Environment office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs and export declarations are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes a Chusik Hoesa or a foreign corporation domestic place of business in Korea must register with the National Tax Service through the HomeTax portal. The legal representative submits: (1) certificate of incorporation or certified copy of the foreign company register, (2) Korean business license for dangerous goods freight forwarding (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510) issued by the competent ministry, (3) domestic place-of-business lease contract and proof of fixed premises, (4) appointment of a domestic tax representative if the head office is outside Korea, and (5) power of attorney. Under the Value-Added Tax Act, the business registration is simultaneously a VAT registration; the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (Korean TIN) used for corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding. Registration must be completed before commencing taxable supplies; a foreign corporation solely earning domestic-source income without a fixed place may use a surrogate tax representative for identification rather than holding a domestic VAT-registered place of business.

10%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT is available under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act for exported goods and for services in direct connection with the export or import of goods, including international freight transport, freight forwarding, brokerage, and customs clearance services supplied to a foreign person. For a DG/Hazmat freight forwarder, zero-rating requires proof that the logistics services were part of an export transaction and that payment is received from abroad or settled in foreign currency. Purely domestic movement of dangerous cargo within Korea does not qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Confirmation) filed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight showing matching shipper, consignee, and cargo description
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list for the underlying exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing final receipt of export sales proceeds in foreign currency
  • Dangerous Goods Declaration under IMDG Code or IATA DGR, together with the valid Hazardous Materials Transport License for the DG forwarding scope

All corporations must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) via the NTS HomeTax real-time electronic invoice system for every taxable supply, transmit the e-Tax Invoice data to the NTS by the day following issuance, and include the mandatory VAT invoice identifier. Large-enterprise and ERP integration are done through the HomeTax e-invoice API; paper tax invoices are generally prohibited for corporations. The NTS performs real-time data matching and anti-fraud risk scoring. Export transactions are reported through KCS UNI-PASS and matched to the zero-rated VAT schedules.

NTS and KCS jointly inspect export zero-rating claims. An unlawful zero-rating claim triggers additional VAT at the standard rate plus additional taxes of 20% for negligent misstatement and 40% for fraudulent misrepresentation, with interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Criminal referral is possible for forged export declarations, bills of lading, or foreign exchange settlement certificates, leading to imprisonment and fines under the Customs Act and the Value-Added Tax Act. The statutory audit period is extended to six years for such cases, and clawbacks are retrospective to the original filing period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current-account system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, while capital-account transactions are conditionally open through a registration-and-filing mechanism rather than blanket BOK permission. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaged in dangerous-goods air and sea forwarding, non-resident shareholders are permitted, but inbound equity capital must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank and should be booked through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route if the capital constitutes foreign direct investment. Profit remittances, including dividends and liquidation proceeds, are generally remittable through a domestic bank after corporate income tax and withholding tax clearance, supported by audited financial statements and board resolutions. There is no mandatory surrender of all export proceeds into KRW, but exporter-received foreign currency is required to be collected within a prescribed statutory period and is normally housed in a domestic foreign-currency account. Increased surveillance applies to high-risk sectors such as hazardous-materials forwarding, where banks must verify physical trade documents, DG licenses, and consignee chains in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system. Overall convertibility is therefore unrestricted for genuine trade and investment flows, with the BOK and MOEF monitoring high-risk channels through the Korea FX Information System and requiring transaction-level purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korea Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related BOK foreign exchange regulations, export proceeds from cargo, freight forwarding, or logistics services must be collected within 180 days from the date of export declaration or service completion, unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank for legitimate commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for commercial export earnings; Korean exporters may retain the funds in domestic foreign-currency accounts, provided the proceeds are recorded in the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System and the underlying export entry has been reported through UNI-PASS. If the amount is collected after the statutory timeline, the remittance must be settled with the foreign exchange bank, and the bank must report the delinquency status to the BOK supervisory framework.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), exercising delegated regulatory authority with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, is the central bank and administrative supervisor of South Korea's foreign exchange clearance, reporting, and KRW convertibility system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore non-bank electronic money institutions and digital EMIs are not viable as primary treasury custodians for this entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, an EMI cannot maintain domestic KRW accounts, issue Korean IBAN equivalents, provide foreign-currency deposit services, or execute cross-border payments without a licensed domestic bank intermediary and BOK/MOEF registration. Several licensed fintech remittance providers exist in Korea, but they act as money-transfer aggregators and are prohibited from servicing a High-Risk_Dangerous_Goods corporate account with LC, documentary collection, and FX hedging needs. The legal constraint is decisive: the treasury structure must remain with a commercial banking counterparty; fintech EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection rail for small parcel customs fees, never for escrow or trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity for the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed through a designated Korean foreign-exchange bank using the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route when a non-resident shareholder subscribes to shares. The remitting bank should originate a SWIFT MT103 with a standing instruction to credit the Chusik Hoesa capital-escrow account, and the receiving bank must report the transaction in the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System using the foreign-investment equity capital purpose code. The bank will issue a foreign currency purchase or KRW conversion advice at the spot market rate once the BOK/MOEF foreign investment registration information has been validated. If instead the injection is an intercompany working-capital loan or trade-related advance, the remittance must be classified under the relevant current-account purpose code and supported by a written loan agreement. A Korean registered public accountant must later perform capital verification by comparing the actual received FX amount, the converted KRW balance, the share registers, and the BOK file number before the company can distribute any dividend or repatriate the injection.Outbound disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for service fees, port handling, air freight terminal charges, sub-agent commissions, and DG insurance premiums, provided each payment is documented with a contract and invoice and submitted through the corporate internet banking payer portal. Korea requires the reporting bank to transmit transaction-level data through the FX Information System, including remittance purpose codes and relation to the approved business license, before the clearing rail is activated. The legal constraint is decisive: the treasury structure must remain with a commercial banking counterparty; fintech EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection rail for small parcel customs fees, never for escrow or trade disbursements.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The initial capital must be fully subscribed and paid into a domestic pre-incorporation subscription account before the court registration is filed, and the bank deposit certificate must be presented to the registry. For a foreign-invested entity seeking foreign-investment tax incentives, the individual case investment normally must be at least KRW 100,000,000; this is not a blocked escrow requirement but a bank-held subscription deposit that cannot be withdrawn until incorporation registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate and fire systems inspection approval from the local fire station for any office, warehouse, or mixed-use aviation spares storage area, including installation of automatic fire detection, extinguishing systems, and emergency egress compliant with the Fire Services Act. For racked storage of aircraft rotables or engine components, submit structural load certification and rack stability inspections. Electrical installations must receive Korea Electrical Safety Corporation inspection and certification. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, register with the Ministry of Employment and Labor when the company has five or more workers, provide workplace safety training, obtain forklift operator licenses if material handling equipment is used, and store any dangerous goods only in accordance with the Dangerous Substances and Workplace Safety regulations. A civil defense shelter obligation, if any, attaches to the building owner under the Civil Defense Act and must be noted in the lease, but no additional company-level civil defense permit is required for a distribution-only business.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal at https://www.hometax.go.kr; export clearance coordination also involves the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity deriving Korean-source business income must appoint a domestic tax representative unless it has a fixed place of business in Korea. The foreign entity must submit an Application for Business Registration to the district tax office with jurisdiction over the domestic place of business, using the HomeTax portal. The application must be accompanied by certified copies of the foreign entity's charter or articles of incorporation, evidence of foreign business registration, a board resolution appointing the domestic tax representative, a signed power of attorney, and proof of the Korean business address. The National Tax Service then issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the Tax Identification Number and the VAT registration number. A non-resident supplier of electronic services without a domestic establishment may apply for simplified VAT registration through the HomeTax portal under the Value-Added Tax Act.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not offer a check-the-box classification election. A Chusik Hoesa incorporated in Korea is subject to entity-level corporate taxation under the Corporate Tax Act. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration threshold exists under the Value-Added Tax Act for business operators in Korea; VAT registration is mandatory from the commencement of taxable business activity. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers are required to complete simplified VAT registration irrespective of turnover, subject to any applicable treaty relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply qualifies for the 0% VAT rate as an export of goods if the certified surplus commercial aircraft parts are sold to a foreign non-resident purchaser and are physically transported out of Korea within the statutory export period. The supplier must maintain evidence that legal title and risk passed to the overseas customer, that the goods were cleared by Korea Customs Service, and that export proceeds are repatriated into Korea in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export declaration with Korea Customs export clearance record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Korean origin and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice describing the parts as certified surplus civil aviation rotables and avionics
  • Packing list and export shipment evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange remittance certificate) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Aircraft parts airworthiness release certificate (e.g., EASA Form 1 or FAA Authorized Release Certificate 8130-3)

Korea mandates electronic tax invoices for all VAT-registered persons. Electronic tax invoices must be issued through the HomeTax portal or an NTS-approved electronic invoicing service provider and transmitted to the National Tax Service by the end of the day following issuance. NTS receives invoice data in real time via HomeTax API and machine-to-machine filing channels. There is no separate anti-fraud digital stamping regime, but NTS operates automated cross-matching of purchase and sales invoices, customs declarations, and VAT returns; late or missing transmission attracts fixed-penalty surtaxes.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service will cross-check zero-rated export claims against customs clearance records, bills of lading, airworthiness documentation, and bank remittance records. If the goods are not physically exported, export documentation is falsified, or foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the 0% rate is reversed and 10% VAT is assessed with interest. Additional tax penalty surcharges may be imposed for negligent or intentional underreporting, totaling up to 40% or more in fraudulent cases, and criminal liability may arise. Retrospective clawback assessments are generally available for five years, extended in cases involving fraud and tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won maintains current-account convertibility under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; ordinary export/import settlements, service fees, and profit remittances may be freely executed through licensed foreign exchange banks once supporting trade or transaction documents are presented. The capital account is partially liberalized and subject to a notification/report-and-return system rather than to simple administrative approval. Inbound foreign direct investment must be announced to a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the contributed capital should be routed through a dedicated foreign-currency account. Profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds from a foreign-invested entity can be remitted offshore after income tax settlement and external audit confirmation, but the legitimacy of source funds and compliance with reporting obligations must be established. The won is not freely convertible outside Korea's regulated onshore channels; offshore won trading is limited and official settlement currencies are typically USD, EUR, JPY, or CNH. A practical bottleneck is the collection of all export proceeds within statutory deadlines and the strict distinction between trade-related current account payments and capital account transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korean Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations require export proceeds to be received in Korea within 15 months from the date of export declaration. There is no fixed statutory surrender percentage requiring conversion to KRW; the full 100% of proceeds must be repatriated through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank unless an extension or exceptional overseas-retention approval is obtained. The exporter may keep the foreign currency in a foreign currency settlement account or convert it to KRW at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority, with foreign exchange policy and administration shared with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), the Financial Services Commission (FSC), and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs have low viability for servicing this Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank EMI may not independently provide Korean won settlement accounts or foreign currency accounts without a domestic electronic financial business license, and under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, foreign-currency conversion and cross-border trade settlement must be channeled through licensed Korean foreign exchange banks. Therefore, offshore digital EMI rails can only be used peripherally, if at all, and cannot act as the primary treasury or trade-settlement infrastructure for inbound FDI capital or aircraft-parts disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must file the Foreign Investment Notification with KOTRA or the designated domestic foreign exchange bank before transferring capital. The equity injection is then remitted by SWIFT MT103 in USD or other eligible currency into a temporary capital-contribution foreign-currency account at a Tier 1 Korean bank. The receiving bank verifies the remitter, the parent and approval reference, and the amount against the filed investment notification; upon matching, the bank issues a capital verification report or proceed-of-investment notification enabling the Korean entity to convert the contribution to KRW and book share capital. The FX purpose code should identify foreign direct investment in equity, and the remittance reference must include the company name, entity registration ID, and foreign investment application number. No mixing with trade settlement is permitted.For payments to overseas aircraft-parts suppliers, the company will use SWIFT MT103 through its Korean relationship bank for direct wire settlements, or issue or confirm commercial letters of credit in accordance with UCP600 where a supplier requires a bank payment undertaking. Documentary collections in the form of D/P or D/A are acceptable for lower-risk recurring transactional flows. USD disbursements may be made from the company's foreign-currency account or by purchasing USD from the domestic bank against the underlying import contract and customs declaration.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, limited to a genuine open foreign-currency trade receivable or payable and with no speculative position.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps with a Tier 1 Korean bank to manage long-term USD-denominated working-capital or trade-finance exposure, with maturity matched to the underlying indebtedness.Physical vanilla FX options such as purchased puts and calls or zero-cost collars concluded solely to hedge forecasted aviation-part trade settlements and reported through the bank's FX transaction reporting framework.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs have low viability for servicing this Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank EMI may not independently provide Korean won settlement accounts or foreign currency accounts without a domestic electronic financial business license, and under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, foreign-currency conversion and cross-border trade settlement must be channeled through licensed Korean foreign exchange banks. Therefore, offshore digital EMI rails can only be used peripherally, if at all, and cannot act as the primary treasury or trade-settlement infrastructure for inbound FDI capital or aircraft-parts disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (top national corporate income tax bracket applicable to taxable income above KRW 300 billion) plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing a combined top headline effective rate of 26.4%

22% combined at source for non-resident shareholders: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax (10% surcharge on the 20% national tax); DTA relief typically reduces the dividend withholding rate to 10%-15% depending on ownership, beneficial ownership, and the specific treaty provision

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate amount of international related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceeds KRW 5 billion; Master File is mandatory when annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion; separate reporting of related-party transactions is required on Form 111 when transactions with overseas related parties exceed KRW 100 million

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the substance-over-form principle under the Framework Act on National Taxes and has fully integrated the OECD/G20 BEPS standard into the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, including beneficial ownership analysis, Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clauses in current DTAs, and permanent establishment anti-abuse rules; although Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation in the mould of the UAE ESR, a Chusik Hoesa that functions as a pure conduit or letterbox with no payroll, no physical offices, no genuine risk-taking, and no substantive inventory operations will be disregarded and denied treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fabricated Aviation Spares Transactions

Statutory sanction: Criminal punishment under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act: imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of not less than the amount evaded and up to 3 times the evaded tax; additional civil penalty of 20% of understated tax and a daily delinquency surcharge of 0.022%; tax authority may also conduct a criminal referral to the prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct fraudulent returns face criminal liability and are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax when corporate assets are insufficient; travel bans and departure prohibitions are issued during the audit, and the court may order forfeiture of proceeds derived from the evasion
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service disregards the shell under the substance-over-form doctrine and the anti-avoidance provisions of the Framework Act; all revenue is recharacterized as taxable to the actual beneficial owner with disallowance of treaty withholding reduction (22% WHT re-imposed), additional TP adjustments, and other local corporate tax assessments plus daily surcharges

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable under secondary tax liability rules for unpaid assessments after corporate assets are exhausted; travel bans and civil restitution orders are imposed; in aggravated cases involving cross-border aviation spare payments, the Act on Aggravated Punishment may extend imprisonment to directors who created the sham entity
Anti-Money Laundering Violations on Escrowed Aviation Payments

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine for the money-laundering offense under the Act on Regulation and Punishment of Criminal Proceeds Concealment; failure to file a suspicious transaction report is separately penalized by a fine; funds involved are subject to seizure and confiscation, and the entity may be blacklisted by financial institutions and denied banking access in Korea

Director personal liability: A director in control of the payment processing is criminally liable alongside the corporation; personal assets are subject to confiscation, departure is prohibited through the issuance of an emigration restriction directive, and the court may impose imprisonment in excess of 5 years if the laundered amount exceeds KRW 500 million
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failures and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may re-determine the taxable income based on the arm's length principle, impose additional corporate income tax and local income tax, and apply an unfaithful-return penalty of up to 20% plus daily interest surcharges; where records are wholly absent, the NTS may apply a deemed estimation and impose a further penalty under the Framework Act for non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the TP documentation or signed the tax return are jointly and severally liable for the deficiency if the corporation becomes insolvent; a director who intentionally omitted international transaction disclosures may face personal criminal prosecution for false reporting, and a travel restriction will be simultaneously imposed by the Minister of Justice, together with an order for civil restitution of the unpaid tax plus interest

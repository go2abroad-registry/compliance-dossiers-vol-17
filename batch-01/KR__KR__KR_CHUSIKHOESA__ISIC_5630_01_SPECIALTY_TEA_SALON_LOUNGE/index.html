Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered office for a specialty tea salon or lounge. The Korean Commercial Act requires a head office location in Korea, and the National Tax Service requires a physical operating place for VAT business registration. For a tea salon, the Food Sanitation Act license requires an actual premises inspection and approval. Banks and local POS and credit card acquirers will reject a virtual office because merchant underwriting and real-name financial transaction verification require a physical branch visit, an inspection-capable registered address, a valid lease, and a real business presence at that address.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Domestic Chusik Hoesa procedure: (1) finalize incorporation with the Korean Commercial Registration court; (2) within 20 days from business commencement, submit the Business Registration Application to the competent NTS tax office or through Hometax; (3) attach articles of incorporation, certificate of establishment/incorporation, lease title/proof of registered place of business, representative identity documents, and, if foreign-invested, the foreign investment notification/report certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; (4) receive the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the corporate TIN and VAT ID. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; a Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a separate entity-level taxpayer. A parent-subsidiary group may elect consolidated corporate taxation under the Corporate Tax Act only where statutory group eligibility is satisfied, but that election does not change entity-level classification. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT-registration threshold; registration is compulsory from business commencement (KRW 0). The simplified taxable person status applies only after annual taxable supplies are equal to or below KRW 80 million and does not waive VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, zero rating applies to goods that are genuinely exported: the goods must be supplied by the registered Chusik Hoesa, transported out of Korea under Korea Customs Service export clearance, and title/risk must pass to a non-resident buyer. Packaged loose-leaf teas and cultural ceramic merchandise exported abroad qualify when supported by the customs export declaration and foreign exchange receipt. Zero rating also applies to qualifying services supplied to non-residents for use or consumption outside Korea and to expressly enumerated international transport/overseas-use services. Domestic in-lounge tea service is standard-rated at 10%; no zero rating is available for services or goods that never cross the Korean border.

  • Korea Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate issued via UNI-PASS)
  • Ocean or air Bill of Lading (or equivalent sea waybill/airway bill) evidencing outward carriage
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs export declaration
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence from a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing collection of the export sales proceeds
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the non-resident buyer and INCOTERMS delivery terms
  • Logistics/forwarder records (cargo movement, container/air waybill number, vessel/flight data) proving physical export

Korea operates an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) regime through the NTS Hometax system. Domestic taxable supplies by a registered business to a registered customer must be documented via e-Tax Invoice, and the issuance details are transmitted to NTS through Hometax; the NTS Tax Invoice Control Number serves as the digital anti-fraud stamping element. Certified accounting solutions and high-volume issuers interconnect with NTS via authorized integration/API channels. Transaction-level e-invoice data that is not transmitted to Hometax can deny the recipient's input VAT credit and may cause the issuer to be assessed 10% VAT without proper invoice evidence.

NTS export zero-rating claims are subject to audit cross-checks against Korea Customs Service export declarations, vessel/airline cargo manifests, bonded export records, and Foreign Exchange Bank remittance files. If the exporter cannot substantiate physical export and foreign exchange repatriation within the 180-day statutory control deadline, NTS will deny the zero rating, reassess 10% VAT on the full supply value, disallow related input VAT credits, impose national tax understatement/additional-tax penalties (with a higher penalty tier for fraudulent concealment), and demand retroactive clawback of any VAT refund previously paid. Deliberate fictitious exporting or forged remittance evidence may additionally trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized managed floating exchange rate regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current-account transactions, including trade settlements, dividends, interest, royalties and payments for services, are freely convertible and require no central bank approval; the onshore KRW spot rate is market-determined with BOK smoothing intervention. Capital-account transactions are open but not unconditional: inward foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa, cross-border loans, and overseas remittances must be reported to FEIS and, depending on size and type, are subject to prior notification or approval. There is no multiple-currency practice and no sign-off requirement for ordinary operating current account flows, but financial institutions must validate real-name identity, the economic purpose of each remittance, and the foreign-exchange purpose code. For profit remittance, the practical hurdles are tax compliance rather than convertibility: the bank will require the filed corporate tax return, withholding tax payment evidence, audited retained earnings, and the foreign investment notification certificate before distributing dividends abroad. Inward funds for capital subscriptions should be wired through a domestic foreign exchange bank with complete underlying contracts to avoid rejection by correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender ratio applies to Korean exporters; export proceeds may be held in foreign-currency deposit accounts at a domestic foreign-exchange bank or converted to KRW at the prevailing market rate without quota. The settlement report must be captured by the bank in the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) on the next banking day after credit, and the inward remittance must be matched to the underlying Korea Customs Service export declaration. Trade contracts should specify a credit term accepted by the receiving bank; extensions beyond normal terms require documented trade rationale or a bank liquidity facility. There is no fixed conversion timeline or required percentage to be tendered to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), Financial Services Commission (FSC), Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and the Korea Customs Service jointly operating the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a principal treasury account. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business or Airwallex do not hold Korean banking licences and cannot legally maintain a KRW corporate account in the name of a Korean resident Chusik Hoesa. Korean law requires foreign exchange business to be conducted through banks licensed under the Banking Act or registered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; arming a foreign EMI with a Korean partner bank to provide an account would still require compliance with the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, FEIS reporting and physical identity verification. Such services may be used only as a payment method for certain low-value customer receivables, but the primary operating, escrow and trade settlement accounts must sit at a Tier-1 domestic bank. For non-resident investment into the Chusik Hoesa, the shareholder must first file an inward foreign investment report with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy delegated to KOTRA if the investment is deemed a foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The wire should be executed by the foreign investor from its own bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the company's KRW account at a domestic foreign exchange bank, using the purpose code for capital contribution or foreign-invested enterprise equity subscription as instructed by the beneficiary bank. The recipient bank must receive a matching Foreign Investment Notification Certificate or prior report number, credit the capital escrow/deposit account, and issue an inward remittance certificate or capital verification confirmation. Funds marked as paid-in capital should be booked as share premium if any and registered through the court-affiliated commercial registry based on the bank's paid-in capital deposit certificate. For shareholder loans, the company must report the borrowing to FEIS and provide the loan agreement, interest-rate schedule and projected repayment plan; proceeds cannot be used for prohibited speculative activities.Trade payments should run through the primary Korean clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments, commercial letters of credit opened by a Korean bank and advised/confirmed by the beneficiary's bank (SWIFT MT700/MT710/MT747 messages), and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) under URC 522. For local KRW obligations, use the Bank of Korea's BOK-Wire+ real-time gross settlement system; for USD obligations, clear through the US correspondent banking network (Fedwire/CHIPS) as routed by the Korean Tier-1 bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a principal treasury account. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business or Airwallex do not hold Korean banking licences and cannot legally maintain a KRW corporate account in the name of a Korean resident Chusik Hoesa. Korean law requires foreign exchange business to be conducted through banks licensed under the Banking Act or registered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; arming a foreign EMI with a Korean partner bank to provide an account would still require compliance with the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, FEIS reporting and physical identity verification. Such services may be used only as a payment method for certain low-value customer receivables, but the primary operating, escrow and trade settlement accounts must sit at a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under Korean law. The promoters may lawfully state a zero or de minimis initial paid-in capital in the articles, provided that the total issue price of all subscribed shares is fully paid into a Korean licensed bank subscription account established for the company in formation. That account is statutorily blocked and may not be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is completed. There is no government-required escrow separate from this bank deposit regime; after registration, the blocked amount becomes the company's available paid-in capital.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

A Korean Chusik Hoesa must apply for Business Operator Registration with the competent tax office through HomeTax within 20 days of business commencement, furnishing the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, evidence of the registered place of business, representative identity document, and power of attorney; NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 (no turnover threshold; all Korean business operators must register under the Value-Added Tax Act. Simplified taxable person classification applies when annual tax-inclusive turnover is below KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, physical export of goods to a foreign country qualifies for the 0% rate. For the hydroponics e-commerce scope, the seller must prove that the goods left Korea under a valid export customs declaration, the overseas purchaser is a non-resident, consideration is payable in foreign currency, and export proceeds are repatriated through a Korean foreign exchange bank. Offshore digital services and other supplies performed outside Korea for a non-resident are similarly zero-rated when actually rendered abroad and paid for in foreign exchange. The 0% rate is claimed on the VAT return and documentary evidence must be retained.

  • KCS Export Declaration and Export Clearance Certificate issued through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Purchase Receipt / Inward Remittance Evidence issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank showing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas purchaser
  • Purchase Order / Overseas Sales Contract or online marketplace settlement report

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all registered businesses. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted in HomeTax XML format, either directly through the HomeTax portal or via an approved ERP/API integration, to the National Tax Service by the end of the day following issuance. Korea does not operate a real-time fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping model; anti-fraud control is achieved through the central e-tax invoice registry, cash receipts for simplified B2C sales, six-monthly VAT returns, and automated cross-checks with KCS customs declarations and foreign exchange transaction records.

Zero-rating claims are audited through the NTS-KCS interlock: the VAT return is matched with UNI-PASS export clearance data, transport documents, and the exporter's foreign exchange bank collection records. A claim is disallowed or clawed back if the export declaration is cancelled, goods are re-imported or diverted to domestic consumption, no bankable proof of receipt of foreign sales proceeds exists within the statutory 18-month (540-day) period from export declaration unless an extension is granted, or documents are forged. Consequences include reassessment of unpaid VAT at 10%, additional tax for unfaithful returns and tax invoice violations, statutory interest on the understated position, recovery of refunds already paid with surcharge, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised current account under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; bona fide trade, service, and dividend remittance flows can be settled in freely convertible foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank with minimal documentary reporting. The Korean won is not a freely internationally traded reserve currency but is market convertible subject to a managed floating regime and strict capital-account gatekeeping. Capital contributions, equity subscriptions, and cross-border loans require FIPA foreign-investment notification, bank registration, and periodic reports; they cannot be received without an underlying corporate or trade transaction. Profit remittances are lawful after corporate audit and statutory reserve retention, but the paying bank requires the board dividend resolution, audited financial statements, tax withholding certificates, and a Foreign Exchange Information System report. No mandatory export surrender ratio applies to Korean export receipts; exporters may park proceeds in foreign currency accounts. However, all cross-border settlement in Korea must be supported by real underlying business, and foreign exchange banks have discretion to reject transactions that appear speculative or inconsistent with the registered ISIC 4791 e-commerce business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and KCS foreign exchange regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 18 months from the date of export declaration unless an extended collection period is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; Korean exporters may retain export settlement in approved foreign currency accounts and sell only the amount needed for KRW operating liquidity. The bank must be given the export declaration number and collection evidence, and any uncollected overdue balance must be reported with a reason through the foreign exchange information system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with foreign exchange administration delegated to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), and enforcement shared with the Korea Customs Service (KCS) and designated foreign exchange banks; the BOK operates the foreign exchange information system and official reserves framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs are legally barred from serving as the principal bank treasury in Korea. Only institutions licensed under the Banking Act or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act can open an onshore KRW corporate settlement account; Korea does not use IBANs, and an offshore EMI cannot generate a locally valid Korean account. Stripe and PayPal remain permissible as front-end online acquiring/payment gateway rails, but all settlement proceeds must be auto-swept to a licensed Korean bank account. Feasibility rating: Low-to-zero for treasury, liquidity, and FX clearing; High for card acquisition and customer checkout purposes only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder sends an SWIFT MT103 from a foreign financial institution to the Korean bank's correspondent account, payable to the Chusik Hoesa's FDI capital contribution account. The payment narrative must state 'Equity contribution for paid-in capital under FIPA' and the bank will assign a foreign exchange purpose code for inward foreign investment in the Foreign Exchange Information System. After credit, the bank issues a foreign exchange and foreign investment certificate; the company then instructs an external Korean auditor or CPA to verify paid-in capital under the Commercial Act, amends the commercial registry, and files the FIPA post-investment report with KOTRA. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation threshold:KRW 10,000,000,000 of international related-party transactions for immediately preceding fiscal year; Master File threshold: consolidated annual revenue of KRW 100,000,000,000 or more and international related-party transaction amount of KRW 10,000,000,000 or more; simplified documents may be required for smaller taxpayers under Notice 2022-18.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like UAE; however, the substance-over-form ground, business purpose doctrine, and the anti-abuse rules of the Restriction of International Tax Adjustment Act require that the Korean company maintain real personnel, premises, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) where decision-making on procurement, inventory ownership, marketing, and risk management occurs. A Korean e-commerce entity that operates merely as a pass-through or empty holding company can have its place of effective management shifted abroad and be denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 138(1) of the Corporate Tax Act: imprisonment with labor not exceeding three years or a fine not exceeding three times the evaded tax; fraudulent unpaid return additional tax 30%; daily default interest 0.022%; tax delinquency listing with the National Tax Service and publication of the delinquency register.

Director personal liability: The limited liability shield is pierced under the Commercial Act for directors who acted in bad faith or gross negligence; they are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties to the extent of damage caused; NTS imposes a departure ban (overseas travel restriction) for delinquencies exceeding KRW 50 million; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment up to five years under the Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes when the evasion exceeds KRW 500 million, and directors can face personal asset seizure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Articles 6 and 12 of Restriction of International Tax Adjustment Act: adjustment of taxable income to arm's length price; surcharge additional tax of 20% of the understated amount (30% where no disclosure); document failure penalty up to KRW 60 million; overdue interest; in severe mispricing cases, criminal referral for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial pricing or sign false TP reports are civilly liable to the corporation and its creditors; they may be denied discharge in corporate insolvency; tax authorities may attach personal assets of directors for grossly negligent TP adjustments; blacklisting and travel ban apply for unpaid criminal fines; imprisonment up to two years can be imposed for submitting false documentation or obstruction of tax audit.
Withholding Tax Negligence / Unauthorised Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Article 97 and Article 144 of the Corporate Tax Act: the payer is liable for the un-withheld amount, plus penalty of 10% of the un-withheld tax (20% for intentional false withholding), plus overdue interest; the un-withheld amount is denied as a deduction; local tax surcharge penalty applies at 20% of the municipal tax element.

Director personal liability: Representative directors and financial officers are joint withholding agents; they are personally and severally liable for the remitted tax and penalties if they knowingly fail to withhold; personal bank accounts can be frozen after seven-day payment order; NTS places a departure restriction for deliberate non-withholding exceeding KRW 50 million; criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may impose imprisonment up to one year for wilful non-withholding.

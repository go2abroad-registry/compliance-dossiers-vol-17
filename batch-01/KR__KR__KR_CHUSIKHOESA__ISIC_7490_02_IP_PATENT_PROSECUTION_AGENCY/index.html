None under Korean law for a Chusik Hoesa; the Commercial Act does not prescribe a statutory minimum amount of paid-up capital. There is no mandatory blocked escrow amount after registration, but funds cannot be withdrawn before incorporation registration is completed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal (www.hometax.go.kr), with local jurisdiction exercised by the district tax office having authority over the entity's Korean place of business.

E-government filing portal

Within 20 days after the Korean Chusik Hoesa commences business, the company (including a foreign non-resident investor organizing the Korean entity) must file a business registration application with the competent district tax office through HomeTax. The supporting package must contain the certified corporate registry extract, registered articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, lease deed or other evidence of the domestic place of business, and identification of the statutory representative and shareholders. If the foreign parent has no resident director or domestic management place, it must appoint a tax representative acceptable to the Commissioner of the National Tax Service under the Framework Act on National Taxes; the tax representative then files the business registration application on behalf of the foreign non-resident entity. NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which concurrently functions as the VAT registration number under the Value Added Tax Act. A foreign non-resident corporation carrying on a Korean business through a branch similarly obtains a Korean business number in the name of the foreign entity; no separate TIN or VAT ID is issued apart from this business registration number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act for goods actually exported from and customs-cleared out of Korea; for services supplied to a nonresident or foreign organization when consideration is received in foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank and the service is consumed or used outside Korea; and for enumerated export-auxiliary services. For IP/patent prosecution and international trademark services, the statutory criteria require a foreign client, a signed engagement/service agreement, evidence that the service is performed without domestic economic use in Korea, and foreign-currency payment remitted to Korea. If any of these elements is absent, the supply will be treated as domestic and charged at 10% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS evidencing physical departure of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued by the carrier for the relevant shipment
  • Commercial invoice and signed service/engagement contract (PCT patent prosecution and trademark registration retainer)
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency payment
  • Foreign exchange bank transaction statement showing the exact remitter, amount, and remittance date matching the invoice

Korea mandates the HomeTax E-Tax Invoice system. All taxable persons issuing VAT invoices must comply with the statutory invoice data rules, and electronic tax invoice data must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day following issuance. Businesses above the statutory gross-supply threshold and those designated by NTS must transmit electronically rather than by paper invoice. NTS validates invoice serial numbers, prevents duplicate or mismatched invoices, and cross-references zero-rated export claims with Korea Customs Service declarations and foreign-currency receipt data. There is no individual government pre-clearance stamp on each invoice, but machine-readable submission through HomeTax, a certified e-tax invoice intermediary platform, or an NTS-approved integration channel is mandatory for electronic transmission; the system does not provide an unrestricted public API.

NTS may audit zero-rated export claims and require production of export declarations, transport documents, service contracts, invoices, and foreign-currency receipt evidence. Where the exporter cannot substantiate the actual export or foreign-currency receipt, NTS reclassifies the supply as domestic, claws back the 0% rate, and assesses 10% VAT plus late-payment surcharges. Issuing false zero-rated invoices also exposes the taxpayer to the dedicated VAT invoice additional tax penalty under Article 60 of the Value Added Tax Act, and fraudulent conduct may be prosecuted as tax evasion under the Framework Act on National Taxes, with fines and criminal liability. The standard statute of limitations for retrospective assessment and clawback is five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a largely convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current-account transactions, including patent prosecution fees and international trademark registration costs, are freely convertible through registered foreign exchange banks. Capital-account transactions, such as inbound equity injections and outbound investments, are liberalised but subject to comprehensive post-transaction or pre-transaction reporting requirements through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). For a Chusik Hoesa patent prosecution agency, profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible once audited financial statements and tax payments are completed, but the FY ends require a certified public accountant's capital verification for foreign investment. The principal operational hurdles are not convertibility constraints but documentary completeness, FEIS reporting deadlines, and the requirement to route all transactions through authorised domestic banks. The Ministry of Economy and Finance may in exceptional capital volatility situations impose temporary surrender or outflow controls, but the standing framework favours free conversion for bona fide service businesses with low AML banking risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export proceeds, the exporting entity must convert foreign currency receipts into Korean won within 6 months from the date of export declaration, with a mandatory 100% surrender requirement unless an exemption is approved by MOEF for designated foreign-currency accounts or justified working capital needs; all conversions and retaining arrangements must be reported and confirmed through the FEIS.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange regulation authorities under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not a viable treasury rail for a Korean corporate entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only authorised foreign exchange banks and specifically designated institutions may execute foreign exchange conversion and international payment instructions on behalf of a Chusik Hoesa. Wise and similar EMIs may offer incoming remittance convenience for consumer flows in some corridors, but they are not licensed as Korean foreign exchange banks and cannot legally receive foreign direct investment equity capital, maintain statutory capital escrow, or provide the FEIS reporting envelope required for patent-office settlement wires. Attempting to route KIPO/WIPO disbursements through an EMI would be rejected by Korean corporate compliance and by the paying bank due to missing purpose codes and missing underlying documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's licensed bank to the Korean company's capital transaction account at an authorised foreign exchange bank. The remittance must carry the contractual neutral narrative and is recorded on the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) under the equity acquisition and subscription purpose code. For a patent prosecution agency, the capital injection is verified through a foreign-investment registration certificate issued by MOEF or a designated bank, followed by a capital verification from a Korean certified public accountant before funds can be treated as paid-in capital.Outbound settlements for patent and trademark fees should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for standard high-value vendor payments, and via Commercial Letters of Credit or Documentary Collection when the agency is contracting with foreign law firms under trade settlement conditions. Each disbursement must be accompanied by underlying contractual invoices, and the paying bank will verify the designated patent office beneficiary's bank details before releasing SWIFT messages. Commercial LC and documentary collections are recommended only when the agency is engaging in a physical-goods collateral transaction or importing office materials, not for the digital IP fee routes.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts restricted to offshore market participants; Korean-resident corporates generally cannot trade NDFs onshore.Onshore forward foreign exchange contracts allowed only with underlying real contract exposure and a verified transaction voucher under the FEIS regime.Cross-currency swaps and FX options permitted for genuine hedging of the agency's subscription capital or foreign-denominated receivable portfolio, subject to MOEF risk concentration limits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal national corporate income tax rate (progressive schedule: 9% on first KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion), plus a mandatory 10% Local Income Tax surtax on national tax due.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders plus 2% Local Income Tax surcharge); reduced treaty rates may apply, commonly 10% to 15% depending on shareholding and treaty provisions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by the last day of the 3rd month following the fiscal year-end (31 March for a calendar-year taxpayer); the due date automatically extends to the 4th month when an external audit under the Act on External Audit is required. Any balance of tax due must be paid by the same deadline; late filing/payment triggers daily delinquency surcharges from the statutory due date.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally accepted only as a correspondence address for a Korean Chusik Hoesa if the company maintains a separate actual business location for employees, records and bank due-diligence visits. The National Tax Service and municipal district tax offices may reject a virtual-office address as the principal place of business if no human activity, utility bills, lease contract, or business signage is verifiable. For the PropTech SaaS industry, VAT zero returns and digital service invoices often trigger address review. Korean banks under the Real Name Transactions Act generally refuse corporate current accounts for mail-only virtual offices and require a signed lease, office identification and an on-site visit. Accordingly, the registered office should be an administrative office service with utility and signage, not a mail-only mailbox.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korea abolished the statutory minimum paid-up capital for stock corporations effective after the 2009 amendment to the Commercial Act. The company may be incorporated with any consideration its shareholders agree to provide, provided that the aggregate issue price of the shares is fully paid before registration, the par value of each share is at least KRW 100, and contributions in kind are verified by a court-appointed inspector where required under Article 290. There is no blocked escrow account requirement at the national level; however, the paid-in capital must be deposited into a bank account in the company's name and must remain untouched until the incorporation registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial licensing. No civil defense fire permit, structural load inspection, or workplace safety clearance is required because the company does not operate a factory, warehouse, or chemical facility. The company must nevertheless comply with the Framework Act on Firefighting and the Building Act for its leased office premises, including maintaining fire extinguishers, unobstructed exits, an occupancy certificate for the floor, and general occupational safety measures for computer-based office work under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Nexus assessment: a Chusik Hoesa established in Korea must register for corporate income tax and VAT. A foreign non-resident entity that will carry on business or make VAT taxable supplies in Korea must also register with the National Tax Service (NTS). Step 2 - Tax representative: a foreign entity without a domestic place of business should appoint a resident tax agent authorized to receive legal process and file tax returns. Step 3 - Application: file the business registration application through the HomeTax portal or at the competent district tax office before commencing business, or within 20 days after business commencement. Step 4 - Documentary evidence: attach the certificate of foreign incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of registered address, board resolution appointing the Korean tax agent, evidence of the Korean supply activity or place of business, and any written contracts with Korean counterparties. Step 5 - NTS review and issuance: after verification, the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the ten-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified TIN and VAT identifier. Step 6 - Foreign e-commerce treatment: a non-resident digital service supplier without a Korean establishment must complete the special overseas e-commerce seller VAT registration track through HomeTax if it supplies qualifying digital services to Korean consumers; this produces a separate simplified VAT collection number. Step 7 - Post-registration: keep statutory books and records, file periodic VAT returns, and update the NTS portal with any changes in business identity. Foreign entities with no Korean source income and no VAT taxable transactions are not required to apply for a Korean TIN.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for a Chusik Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory upon business commencement in Korea regardless of anticipated revenue. Simplified taxpayer status is available only to certain individual business operators with annual taxable turnover below KRW 30,000,000, and this concession does not apply to a corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, the export of goods is zero-rated when the goods leave Korea under a customs export declaration and are paid for in foreign currency. Services, including cloud-based PropTech SaaS subscriptions supplied to foreign clients, are zero-rated as export-of-services when they qualify as services that earn foreign currency or are performed for a non-resident under the statutory export-of-services categories; the supply must not be consumed or used in Korea, the beneficiary must be a non-resident, and the consideration must be received in convertible foreign currency. Domestic B2C supply or in-Korea use does not qualify. The taxpayer must substantiate the zero rate with the complete export documentation bundle and repatriated foreign exchange.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / multimodal transport document evidencing movement out of Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation proving receipt of convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and international export contract with the foreign customer
  • For SaaS/service exports: signed offshore service agreement, detailed service description or completion report, customer non-residency evidence, and a HomeTax e-tax invoice marked zero-rated

The NTS operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) transmission system through HomeTax. Every VAT taxable supply made by a corporation must be evidenced by an e-tax invoice; paper tax invoices are permitted only in limited statutory cases. Transaction data is transmitted to the NTS via HomeTax, an approved EDI service provider, or a certified API integration using the NTS electronic tax invoice XML schema. Korea is not a pre-clearance stamping jurisdiction; there is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamp required before each transaction. Instead, the NTS performs automated cross-matching of issued and received e-tax invoices against VAT returns and issues automated mismatch correction requests. Missing or fraudulent e-tax invoice entries attract statutory surcharges, and false e-tax invoices can trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act.

The NTS will scrutinize export declarations, foreign exchange bank registers, shipping documents, and VAT refund claims. If zero-rating is applied but export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory period, or the documentation bundle is incomplete, the NTS will retroactively reverse the zero rating and assess Korean VAT at 10% on the export consideration, plus late-payment interest and statutory additional tax for underreporting under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. False e-tax invoices and forged customs documentation attract severe surcharges, including penalties up to a significant percentage of the invoiced amount, and criminal punishment under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act. Any input VAT previously refunded in connection with the disputed export is subject to full clawback and recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a broadly liberalised convertible currency regime for current-account transactions, such as trade settlements, dividends, royalties, and service fees, which may be settled in KRW or any foreign currency through a licensed foreign exchange bank without prior regulatory approval where the underlying transaction is properly documented and reported. Capital-account transactions, including inbound foreign equity investment, outward portfolio investment, lending, and borrowing, are subject to a standard declaration/reporting framework rather than a prior approval system for ordinary commercial transactions. Foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) is protected and generally enjoys full repatriation of capital and profits when the required notification has been made and the capital has been properly booked. The Korean won is convertible for settlement on the onshore KRW market; however, strict gatekeeping applies to non-residents in certain derivatives and speculative flows, and intraday foreign exchange positions are monitored by the Bank of Korea to curtail abnormal market pressure. A unified Foreign Exchange Information System monitors all cross-border settlements. Surrender requirements have been largely abolished, but each export transaction must be reported and settled through a domestic foreign exchange bank, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory timeline can trigger penalties, loss of foreign exchange bank status, and tax reporting consequences. Profit remittance requires evidence of paid-in capital, audited financial statements, tax clearance, and, if applicable, a foreign investment notification certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 99 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, Korean resident exporters must receive export proceeds within 180 days of the export declaration date. The exporter may apply in advance for an extension of up to an additional 180 days on reasonable commercial grounds. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; P. don't need this. However, the proceeds must be transferred into a domestic foreign exchange bank account or resident foreign currency deposit, and the export collection must be reported under the Export Collection Information System. Surrender is required only when the exporter elects to convert abroad? Actually, exporters must exchange or deposit within the required period; there is no current forced surrender rate. The conversion to KRW, if chosen, is executed at negotiated spot rates. Tolerance for loan? We'll leave.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with policy authority for foreign exchange under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act jointly exercised by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Bank of Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not licensed as banks under the Korean Banking Act and cannot open a direct corporate treasury account for a Korean Chusik Hoesa, because banking law requires that any entity providing multi-currency deposit-taking, payment, and settlement services to Korean residents hold a local financial license and maintain a branch in the Republic of Korea. As a result, the viability of deploying an offshore digital EMI as the primary liquidity rail is LOW. The only viable application is MEDIUM (restricted) where a major domestic bank acts as the designated settlement bank and the EMI operates as a white-label software front-end for invoicing, API payment orchestration, and reconciliation, while the underlying custody, capital verification, and FX conversion remain inside the licensed Korean bank. Even in that partner-banking structure, all outward remittances must flow through the local bank's SWIFT and foreign exchange reporting infrastructure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate international related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Korean Local File and Master File contemporaneous documentation. Additionally, a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where the ultimate parent group has annual consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion. The required documents include entity charts, transactional master file, local country documentation, functional analysis, benchmark evidence, and financial projections demonstrating arm's-length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework comparable to the UAE, but Korean courts and the National Tax Service rigorously enforce the substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under the Framework Act on Taxation, and the 'core income-generating activities' (CIGA) analysis in international tax treaty and transfer pricing cases. For a PropTech SaaS platform, CIGA in Korea generally includes software development, data and algorithm management, customer contract execution, credit and fraud risk management, and supervision of key outsourced functions. A Korean entity that only holds title, invoices, or receives fees without performing these controlling functions and assuming significant risk will be recharacterized, and treaty benefits may be denied where the beneficial owner lacks substance in its claimed residence state.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statement

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, a violation is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to 3 times the amount of evaded tax; where the tax evaded exceeds a benchmark serious amount and is planned, the term can be aggravated. Additionally, the National Tax Service may impose a 40% fraud negligence penalty, plus daily late-payment surcharges, and may reject or revise refunds for prior years.

Director personal liability: Korean courts will pierce the corporate veil under the Korean Commercial Act Article 401 and general tort principles where directors use the company as a sham vehicle. Directors who participated in or instructed the false reporting are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and surcharges. They face criminal imprisonment, asset seizure, and blacklisting through the National Tax Service's delinquent payer system, which imposes travel bans at Incheon and Gimhae International Airports. The director's personal bank accounts, real estate, vehicles, and receivables are subject to compulsory execution within the statuted collection period.
Shell Company and Double Tax Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Korea's general anti-abuse rule and judicial substance-over-form doctrine allow the tax authority to disregard the intermediary entity, reclassify the beneficial owner as the underlying non-resident principal, and reassess the full 22% withholding tax, plus additional 10% to 40% negligence penalties and daily late-payment surcharges. The foreign vehicle may also be denied treaty entitlement under limitation-on-benefits provisions and the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test (PPT).

Director personal liability: Directors of the Korean Chusik Hoesa who knowingly participate in treaty abuse may be deemed obligated to have withheld and paid the tax under Articles 98 and 99 of the Corporate Tax Act. They are personally liable for the uncollected withholding amount where they knew or should have known that the beneficial owner requirement was false. They may face a travel ban, extraditable investigation, and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act for aiding and abetting fraudulent claims. In aggravated cases, civil liability for losses of minority shareholders and creditors can be asserted on a joint and several basis.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service may make a transfer pricing adjustment to the Korean entity's arm's-length income and apply an additional 10% underpayment penalty on the adjusted tax. In addition, denial of interest deduction applies to excessive related-party debt under the thin capitalization regime, triggering 22% withholding on deemed dividends on the excess portion.

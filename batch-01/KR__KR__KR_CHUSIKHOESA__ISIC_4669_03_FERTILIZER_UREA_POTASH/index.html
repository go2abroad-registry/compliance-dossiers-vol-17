Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic filing and registration operated through the Home Tax Service portal (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) must first complete commercial registration with the Korean court registry, then file a business registration application with the district NTS office that has territorial jurisdiction over the registered head office. The application may be submitted through the Home Tax Service portal or in person at the district tax office. NTS assigns a ten-digit business registration number, which is the single statutory TIN and VAT ID for the entity. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean commercial establishment may be registered directly for VAT through the Home Tax Service or through a designated tax representative, and NTS will issue a TIN on the basis of the foreign corporate certificate and passport or officer identification. The statutory representative of the entity must submit the articles of incorporation, the corporate seal registration certificate, the lease or ownership proof of the place of business, and the identity documents of directors. For foreign-invested corporations, the Foreign Investor Registration Certificate issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act should also be presented, but it is not a substitute for the NTS TIN registration. If an entity expects to make taxable supplies in Korea, registration must be completed before the business starts so that tax invoices can be issued.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (KRW 0). VAT registration is mandatory for every person, including a Chusik Hoesa, conducting activities the supply of goods or services in Korea. Individual sole proprietors may access a simplified VAT regime with an annual revenue ceiling of KRW 80 million, but a wholesale corporation dealing in fertilizers is required to register and account for VAT as a standard taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of prilled urea and potassium chloride qualifies for zero-rated VAT when the goods are sold by the Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident buyer, are physically removed from Korea to a foreign country, and are declared to Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS export procedure. The exporter must keep objective evidence that actual shipment occurred (bill of lading, container or charter release and customs export declaration), that invoicing is in a foreign currency, and that the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated and acknowledged by a domestic foreign exchange bank. Qualifying offshore digital services exported to non-residents are zero-rated as well, but for this goods-focused wholesale operation physical export documentation is the controlling condition.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration (Export Declaration Out or export clearance proof)
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal transport bill (marine original preferably), evidenced with loaded vessel details at the Korean port
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list referring to the exact cargo description, HS classification, net weight and foreign-currency price
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or a foreign exchange bank export-proceeds confirmation showing the receipt of the export price in foreign exchange
  • Original sales contract, letter of credit, or purchase order, plus any export inspection certificate required by the agrochemical commodity trade

All VAT taxpayers must issue a tax invoice for every taxable supply. Corporate and larger taxpayers must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) through the NTS Home Tax Service, certified third-party software via API, or the NTS electronic tax invoice system; transactional data is received and warehoused in the NTS national tax invoice master ledger on a near real-time basis. Electronic invoices must be delivered to the NTS database no later than the day following issuance, and the NTS performs automated cross-matching with customs declarations and purchase ledger data. There is no physical fiscal stamp or hardware seal, but the e-Tax Invoice is the mandatory digital anti-fraud control; failure to transmit carries penalty charges.

NTS will verify zero-rating claims by reconciling UNI-PASS export declarations, e-Tax Invoice records and foreign exchange bank receipts. If the goods are not shown to have left Korea or if export payments are not received within the repatriation window, the export zero-rating is reversed and the supply is taxed at the standard 10% VAT on the invoiced amount. Additional tax penalties are assessed under the Framework Act on National Taxes as follows: 10% for negligent understatement, 20% for failure to file or non-payment, and 40% for fraudulent concealment or unfounded zero-rating, plus daily late-payment interest. In aggravated fraud cases criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and administrative revocation of foreign exchange transaction licences or export permits may be applied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is convertible for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, which operates an administrative control regime rather than a license-based approval regime. Current account transactions are generally permitted with post-transaction reporting; capital account transactions require prior filing, a certificate of report, or an advance approval depending on the category and counterparty. Dividend remittances by a Chusik Hoesa are freely permitted after completing the applicable withholding tax (20% under the Corporate Income Tax Act, or the reduced rate under a relevant tax treaty) and after submitting a FEIN declaration through the foreign exchange bank; there is no surplus approval or profit repatriation license requirement. The practical impediments are the verification of the Foreign Investor Registration Certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) for inbound FDI, the tax clearance evidence, and the bank's internal enhanced due diligence for commodity traders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory export claim collection period under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree is the earlier of the contractual payment due date or two years from the date of export, with an extension available only upon written application to the foreign exchange bank for exceptional force majeure or delay in the underlying trade. Korea has abolished the mandatory surrender of export proceeds into Korean won; therefore there is no statutory conversion ratio. All export receipts may be credited to and retained in a registered foreign currency deposit account at a licensed foreign exchange bank. Nevertheless, the exporter must repatriate the invoice amount within the collection period, cause the bank to report the receipt through FEIN within the designated reporting window, and align the foreign currency receipt with the export customs declaration and the bill of lading so that the export claim can be extinguished without a secondary trade-based money laundering inquiry.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority, acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy maker, the Financial Services Commission (FSC) as the financial regulator, and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) as the supervisory examiner. All foreign exchange transactions are reported through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) operated by the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC), with licensed foreign exchange banks acting as the reporting agents.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to auxiliary only. Offshore fintech EMI platforms that do not hold a Foreign Exchange Bank license under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Banking Act cannot lawfully open Korean won current accounts, cannot execute capital account settlements, and cannot serve as the principal clearing rail for FEIN-reportable transactions. Such EMIs may be used only as an ancillary collection channel under a sponsorship arrangement with a licensed domestic bank for low-value inbound payments, and must be entirely excluded from the inward FDI escrow, the documentary letter of credit lifecycle, and the outbound trade disbursement structure. For a wholesale urea and potash trading entity, reliance on an overseas EMI as a primary cash management node creates a compliance failure risk and will not satisfy the FSS AML examination standards.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A foreign parent injecting equity into the Chusik Hoesa must file a pre-investment report through a licensed foreign exchange bank or the MOEF under the FIPA route and obtain a Foreign Investor Registration Certificate before remittance. The capital must be wired via SWIFT MT103 with the designated FIPA equity purpose code and must be credited to a dedicated foreign investor capital escrow account in the name of the Korean entity at one of the tier-1 banks listed above. Upon receipt, the bank issues a certificate of investment and the entity files the additional investment report through FEIN. The escrow proceeds may be converted into Korean won and deployed only after the investment notification is complete. For intercompany loans, execute a written loan agreement, obtain the Korean entity's board approval, and report the contract to the head office of the foreign exchange bank in accordance with Article 23 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations; the borrowed amount must be applied strictly to the declared business purpose and any subsequent refinancing or early repayment requires a separate FEIN filing.Outbound settlement for physical urea and potassium chloride purchases must execute through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account suppliers, and through documentary import Letters of Credit opened by MT700 and reimbursed via MT103/MT202 for LC-based trades. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to auxiliary only. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required when related-party transaction value with a single foreign related party exceeds KRW 5 billion (or KRW 2 billion for services and intangibles); Master File: required when total related-party transaction value exceeds KRW 50 billion; also the annual aggregate transactional value with all foreign related parties exceeding KRW 10 billion triggers the submission of the comprehensive transfer pricing report. Isolated cross-border commodity deals below these thresholds must still maintain contemporaneous arm's length justification.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not directly codified as a standalone doctrine in Korean tax law, but the substance-over-form principle under the Corporate Tax Act, the anti-avoidance rule for thin capitalization (debt–equity ratio of 2:1 for related-party loans; interest on excess debt disallowed), and the Anti-Avoidance Rule for Foreign Related-Party Transactions (which permits recharacterization of transactions lacking commercial substance) apply. The NTS also applies the general abuse-of-rights doctrine and step-transaction principle to disregard shells or conduits. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in fertilizer wholesale, sufficient operational substance is evidenced by staff, inventory storage, purchase orders, logistics contracts, invoicing, risk management, and economic decision-making in Korea; a pure booking company with no employees or physical presence would face adjustment and penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years (extended up to 10 years for aggravated evasion) and a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; additional corporate tax of 40% penalty for unjustified underreporting and 20% for negligent misreporting; daily late payment interest of 0.022%

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties if they acted in bad faith or negligently; the NTS may pierce the corporate veil on evidence of gross misconduct; travel bans (출국금지) can be imposed on directors; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment with possible extradition under bilateral agreements in severe cross-border schemes.
Shell or Conduit Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: NTS recharacterization of the transaction under the substance-over-form doctrine, disallowance of deductions, imposition of a 20% unfaithful filing penalty (or 40% for fraudulent underreporting), and additional tax on deemed dividends to shareholders; possible denial of treaty benefits on the basis of the principal purpose test/beneficial ownership clause

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for corporate tax deficiencies when the entity is used as a sham; a court may pierce the veil and require directors to personally satisfy corporate liabilities; foreign resident directors can be subject to Korean tax collection through mutual assistance and may face re-entry bans or fugitive warrants.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: NTS transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length amount, disallowance of interest on excess related-party debt, local tax surcharge on adjusted tax, penalty of up to KRW 100 million for non-submission of transfer pricing documentation, and a 10% penalty on understated tax

Director personal liability: Representative directors who approved a misleading transfer pricing report are personally liable for the resultant tax shortfall; criminal liability for violation of the Foreign Trade Act if fraudulent import/export declarations accompany the price manipulation; travel bans and seizure of personal assets possible.
Failure to File Withholding Tax and Dividend Distributions to Non-Residents

Statutory sanction: Joint and several liability of the payer for the unpaid withholding tax; penalty of 10% of unpaid tax for failure to withhold; additional penalty of 0.03% per day for unpaid amounts; the recipient may lose treaty benefits if the payment is not reported

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or knowingly permitted the unremitted withholding tax are personally liable for the withheld amounts under the Corporate Tax Act collection provisions; the NTS can impose a payment order on directors and block foreign departure pending settlement.
AML and Sanction Evasion Structuring in Commodity Trading

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transactions Information Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act, fines up to KRW 50 million per violation and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years; customs penalties under the Customs Act for false declarations up to 3 times the value of goods; temporary freezing of corporate bank accounts

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in laundering proceeds from tax evasion face imprisonment up to 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act; the NTS may issue criminal referral to the prosecutor; travel bans, asset freezes, and confiscation of personal property apply; judicial dissolution of the corporation may be ordered.

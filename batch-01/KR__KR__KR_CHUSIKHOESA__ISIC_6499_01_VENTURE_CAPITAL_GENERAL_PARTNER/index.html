Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a private Chusik Hoesa under Articles 329 and 332 of the Korean Commercial Act. However, all issued shares must be fully subscribed and paid in before incorporation registration. If the entity will conduct venture-investment general partner operations, the Financial Services Commission may require a minimum net operating capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 75,000) to be deposited into a blocked escrow corporate account at a domestic bank. These funds remain frozen until incorporation registration and can be released only after corporate tax registration and licensing requirements are satisfied. Even absent a statutory minimum, it is recommended to maintain paid-in capital of at least KRW 50,000,000 to evidence organizational viability and bankability.

Funding & unlock path: For the standard office premises, the following clearances are mandatory: a fire safety inspection and fire-fighting facility license under the Firefighting Installation and Maintenance Act; compliance with the building occupancy certificate and structural load inspection under the Building Act; and a workplace safety and health program under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including naming a safety and health manager and conducting an annual health and safety audit. If the entity later adds a server room, archive depot, or co-working lab, the fire safety and electrical inspections will be upgraded accordingly.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic filing and tax administration portal: NTS HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Export declaration and customs substantiation are processed through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNI-PASS portal (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A Korean incorporated Chusik Hoesa registers as a domestic corporation by filing a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Corporate Tax Act within 20 days after business commencement; the NTS issues a single Business Registration Number serving simultaneously as the corporate tax TIN and the VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident entity with a domestic place of business or otherwise deriving Korean-source taxable income, the foreign entity, acting through its Korean branch or tax representative, must file the Business Registration Application accompanied by the foreign certificate of incorporation or registration, the foreign entity's current charter or articles, a board resolution appointing the Korean representative/registered agent, a power of attorney for the tax representative, and evidence of the domestic business establishment. Where the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but is liable for VAT on electronic services supplied in South Korea, it must complete the simplified overseas e-service business operator registration through HomeTax and appoint a local tax representative. There is no separate TIN and VAT ID system: the Business Registration Number is the universal tax identity key for all national tax and VAT filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Korean incorporated Chusik Hoesa registers as a domestic corporation by filing a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Corporate Tax Act within 20 days after business commencement; the NTS issues a single Business Registration Number serving simultaneously as the corporate tax TIN and the VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident entity with a domestic place of business or otherwise deriving Korean-source taxable income, the foreign entity, acting through its Korean branch or tax representative, must file the Business Registration Application accompanied by the foreign certificate of incorporation or registration, the foreign entity's current charter or articles, a board resolution appointing the Korean representative/registered agent, a power of attorney for the tax representative, and evidence of the domestic business establishment. Where the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but is liable for VAT on electronic services supplied in South Korea, it must complete the simplified overseas e-service business operator registration through HomeTax and appoint a local tax representative. There is no separate TIN and VAT ID system: the Business Registration Number is the universal tax identity key for all national tax and VAT filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not apply a check-the-box regime; a Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer under the Corporate Tax Act. No partnership or pass-through election is available for a stock corporation, and hybrid mismatch or tariff classification elections are not recognized for this entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary exemption threshold applies to a corporate entity. Every business operator, including a Chusik Hoesa, is legally required to register for VAT within 20 days of commencing taxable supplies. The simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 in the Value-Added Tax Act applies only to individuals and small businesses, not to a stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Value-Added Tax Act applies to exported goods when the goods are actually removed from the Republic of Korea under customs procedures and title and risk have passed to a foreign buyer. Qualifying offshore services, including export-oriented consulting, fund management, and certain financial services supplied to non-resident counterparties and used wholly outside Korea, are also zero-rated where the consideration is earned in foreign currency. For a venture capital general partner, management and advisory fees charged to a non-resident fund without Korean economic substance are admissible for zero-rating only if the place of supply, actual performance, and economic benefit are substantiated as taking place outside Korea.

  • Customs Export Declaration receipt accepted by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice showing the foreign purchaser and Korean export origin
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing international carriage from Korea to the overseas destination
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate or inward remittance confirmation evidencing export proceeds received from the foreign buyer in convertible currency
  • VAT electronic tax invoice correctly issued and transmitted to the NTS for the zero-rated export supply

A VAT-registered supplier must issue an Electronic Tax Invoice through the NTS HomeTax network for every taxable supply with VAT-inclusive value above KRW 100,000, generally no later than the day following the supply date. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS using NTS-certified accounting software, a licensed EDI provider, or the HomeTax online interface; there is no South American-style fiscal stamping or real-time pre-approval gate, but the NTS performs centralized near-real-time matching of seller e-invoice data with buyer VAT return claims. Failure to issue or transmit a genuine electronic tax invoice exposes the taxpayer to unlawful tax-invoice penalties based on the invoiced supply value and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly cross-match UNI-PASS export declarations, electronic tax invoices, VAT returns, vessel/aircraft movement data, and banking foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without the required export documentation, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not received within the statutory repatriation period, the NTS will deny the zero-rated treatment and assess the outstanding 10% VAT with statutory interest. Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims may additionally attract understatement penalties under the Framework Act for National Taxes of up to 40% of the unpaid tax amount, foreign exchange fines under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Any VAT refund resulting from an unsubstantiated zero-rating claim is subject to retrospective clawback for the limitation period applicable under the Framework Act for National Taxes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized yet carefully supervised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is not freely convertible outside Korea; all domestic conversions must take place through a Foreign Exchange Bank recognized by MOEF and supervised by the Bank of Korea. Current account payments are effectively free when supported by invoices, contracts, or customs clearances. Capital account movements, including foreign equity injections into a Chusik Hoesa, require a Foreign Investment Promotion Act report and designated foreign currency account verification. Fund managers acting as venture capital general partners must demonstrate real demand and an underlying business purpose; a mere treasury parking structure will be rejected. Profit remittances, dividends, and loan repayments to non-residents are permitted after tax, audit, and declaration, but require the entity to ensure that remittance purpose codes match the actual economic transaction. Official scrutiny focuses on export proceeds repatriation, cross-border settlement in KRW by non-residents, and misuse of foreign-investment status. Noncompliance can trigger administrative fines under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and designated foreign exchange bank sanctions. Therefore, practical convertibility is high for documented current transactions but conditional for capital outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate all foreign currency export proceeds to a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank within 18 months from the date of exportation. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% in the sense that the full invoice value must be surrendered into a domestic bank account; however, the exporter may maintain the balance in foreign currency rather than converting to KRW. For KRW-denominated export settlements, the Korean won must be received into a domestic won account and the underlying export documentation must be retained for bank verification and FX purpose-code audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration authority, and Korea Customs Service as transactional enforcement agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability and legally restricted. A licensed offshore EMI operating under another EEA or regional e-money licence does not possess a Korean foreign exchange business licence under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Domestic licensed banks are the only institutions permitted to operate KRW settlement accounts and provide resident companies with foreign-currency buying and selling. Fintech EMI solutions may function only as auxiliary card or wallet infrastructure layered on a local partner bank, never as the primary Chusik Hoesa treasury rail. Legally, the entity must keep capital-call receipts and dividend disbursement records inside a domestic Foreign Exchange Bank to preserve the tax, audit, and regulatory evidential chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must enter via SWIFT MT103 to a licensed Korean Foreign Exchange Bank in the name of the Chusik Hoesa. Before remittance, the foreign investor or GP must file a foreign direct investment intention report with MOEF through the integrated Foreign Investment System or the receiving bank. The remittance purpose code must correctly state equity participation in ordinary shares, and the beneficiary must hold a registered Foreign Currency Capital Receipt / Paid-in Capital Escrow Account. On credit, the bank obtains a Foreign Currency Acquisition Certificate and completes capital verification after the company registry reflects the paid-in capital. Funds can then be converted into KRW at prevailing market rates and credited to the operational account. All entries must be reported to the Bank of Korea Foreign Exchange Information System; the minimum paid-in capital requirement for the Korean venture GP is not statutorily prescribed, but the injected amount must be consistent with the fund constitution and corporate valuation.Outbound settlement should use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border payments. Commercial letters of credit are opened through a domestic bank using SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 terms; documentary collections are handled through SWIFT MT400 or associated collection messages. Domestic KRW disbursements from the operational account can be executed via BOK-Wire+ and foreign-currency payments via SWIFT MT103. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability and legally restricted. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive national corporate income tax: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion; plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate income tax (maximum aggregate 26.4%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, plus 2% local income tax surtax, producing a 22% aggregate statutory withholding rate in the absence of treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation and submission is triggered when annual international related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Master File is mandatory when the Korean entity or worldwide group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and international related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; Country-by-Country Report is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion; for a venture-capital GP, intercompany management fees, capital-call reimbursements, and carried-interest allocations should be benchmarked even below the legal threshold to avoid NTS recharacterization.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies OECD/BEPS substance requirements through the Corporate Tax Act's place of effective management test, the general anti-avoidance rule, and beneficial ownership analysis for treaty relief. A Korean venture-capital GP must prove that core income-generating activities, specifically fund sourcing, investment due diligence, term sheet negotiation, capital-call management, portfolio monitoring, board participation, and carried interest discretion, are performed by its own employees with sufficient staff, premises, records, and decision-making authority in Korea; without such substance, income can be reattributed to an offshore affiliate and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-Border Tax Evasion and False Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; Corporate Tax Act additional tax of 20% for failure to file, 10% for negligent understatement, and 40% for fraudulent understatement; daily delay interest of approximately 0.022% per day; entity and responsible executives placed on the NTS serious tax delinquent blacklist.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors personally approve false invoicing, concealment, or offshore diversion: directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, fines, and interest; NTS may impose asset seizure, travel ban/blacklisting, and courts may sentence responsible directors to imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act, Articles 4, 5, and 11: tax authority may adjust taxable income to arm's length, deny deductions for sham related-party payments, impose an additional tax up to 20% of the adjusted tax deficiency, impose daily delay interest, and issue separate administrative penalties for missing documentation or incorrect reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct the false pricing or orchestrate the shell-related-party structure are treated as de facto participants in the tax violation; Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil for asset restitution, directors may be subjected to travel bans and blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment up to 5 years may be imposed for aiding intentional tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Fraud and Tax Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is personally liable for the full 22% statutory withholding tax on the gross distributed amount, plus additional tax for failure to withhold, daily delay interest, and penalties under the Income Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes; fraudulent treaty abuse is treated as a tax crime, the reduced DTA rate is overturned, and the underlying income is reclassified as taxable Korean-source dividend, interest, or royalty.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding tax and surcharges if they deliberately structured the outbound payment or approved the sham conduit arrangement; the NTS may blacklist the entity and individual directors, issue travel bans, seize personal assets, and pursue criminal penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment for intentional evasion.

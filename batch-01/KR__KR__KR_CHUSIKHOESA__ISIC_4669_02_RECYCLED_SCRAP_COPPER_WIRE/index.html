Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa. The subscribed shares must be paid in full before the incorporation registration is filed. Paid-in capital may be any amount agreed by the promoters, e.g., KRW 10,000,000, and must be evidenced by a bank balance certificate or a deposit receipt issued by a licensed Korean commercial bank. There is no general legal requirement for a blocked escrow account; however, a temporary lock-up/deposit account is used during the incorporation process, and where non-resident investment is made under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the foreign capital must be remitted through a foreign-investment-capital account and reported. If a D-8 visa is sought, a practical threshold of at least KRW 100,000,000 of paid-in capital is typically expected by immigration practice.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold exempts a Korean legal person; VAT registration is compulsory from the date of business commencement regardless of revenue. Individual entrepreneurs with annual gross supplies up to KRW 80,000,000 fall under the simplified taxpayer regime, but a Chusik Hoesa carrying the present ISIC 4669 wholesale of recycled scrap metal is always a general VAT-registered taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Act, the cross-border supply of recycled shredded copper wire is zero-rated only when a Chusik Hoesa sells to a non-resident purchaser, title passes outside Korea, the goods are declared as exports through the Korea Customs Service, physically shipped from Korea to a foreign destination, and no domestic consumption occurs. The exporter must issue a zero-rated e-Tax Invoice, attach the export declaration reference, and settle the export proceeds from the foreign buyer in convertible foreign exchange through a Korean foreign exchange bank within the statutory settlement period. Qualifying offshore digital services are not within this ISIC 4669 physical-consignment scope and would require separate foreign-customer and foreign-currency tests.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration accepted via the UNI-PASS single-window system (customs export clearance certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical outbound shipment to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with scrap copper wire specification, weight, and value reconciled to the customs declaration
  • Packing list for the shredded non-ferrous consignment
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through the NTS Home Tax Service with export declaration reference
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds; the Korean banking equivalent of an inward remittance certificate
  • Documentary Letter of Credit or Swift TT credit advice matching the commercial invoice and customs valuation

All B2B supplies by a registered business must be invoiced as an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) transmitted to the NTS through Home Tax Service or an NTS-approved EDI/API service provider using an accredited digital certificate. The system validates and records the invoice at transmission; there is no pre-clearance stamping model, but the filing obligation is near real-time and export e-Tax Invoices must be associated with the Korea Customs Service export declaration. Anti-fraud enforcement relies on NTS analytics, UNI-PASS customs data matching, cash-receipt documentation for B2C sales, and penalty surcharges for failure to transmit or false transmission.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating through HTS and UNI-PASS data matching. A claim is invalidated if the goods never lawfully left Korea, ownership had not passed to a non-resident, the e-Tax Invoice lacks a valid export reference, or export proceeds were not repatriated from the foreign buyer by the statutory deadline. Invalidated zero-rating triggers assessment of 10% VAT on the supply value plus interest and additional tax under the Framework Act on Taxes, generally 20% if voluntarily revised and 40% if found through audit or non-disclosure; false e-Tax Invoice transmission also attracts VAT Act additional tax of 2% of supply value and revocation of prior refunds. Deliberate fabrication of export, customs, invoice, or foreign-exchange evidence is tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and exposes the entity and its responsible executives to imprisonment or fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal but regulated foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. The Korean won symbol KRW is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, requiring only the submission of an underlying transaction report to a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment, are free but subject to an advance declaration or post-review registration framework; inbound equity investment for a Chusik Hoesa must be registered with Korea's Ministry of Economy and Finance or delegated through the Bank of Korea to secure lawful asset protection and future profit outflow. There are no general surrender requirements for export proceeds, but exporters are obliged to receive foreign currency within 180 days from the date of export declaration and to repatriate the funds into a domestic foreign currency account or convert into KRW at the exchange rate determined through the Seoul Money Brokerage Services market. Dividend remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted after verifying the capital registration and completion of annual statutory audit, subject to a withholding tax under the relevant double tax treaty. Overall, the system presents moderate regulatory friction for illegal profit shifting but ensures clear clearance rails for genuine trade transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within 180 days from the date of export declaration. The full amount of the export invoice value must be either received into a domestic foreign currency deposit held at a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank or converted to KRW within the same bank. No mandatory conversion ratio applies to the recycled scrap copper export category, but every receipt must be traced with customs declarations, bills of lading, and bank receipts to satisfy a foreign exchange audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the Foreign Exchange Control Authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of South Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. South Korean regulations require cross-border financial services and foreign currency operations to be executed through domestic licensed banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not hold Korean banking licenses and cannot facilitate won-denominated settlement, capital verification, or trade finance instruments such as LC issuance. They may only be used as ancillary payment aggregators if they maintain a licensed local partnership, but are not viable as primary clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Direct equity remittances must be executed by the parent entity's bank through SWIFT MT103 to the Korean bank's intermediary. The sender must use a purpose code equal to FDI-01 for investment into a domestic enterprise and include the Korean corporate registration number or foreign investment declaration reference. The receiving Korean bank will issue a capital import certificate, which is submitted to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum marginal rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 30 billion under progressive brackets of 10%, 20%, 22% and 24%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national corporate income tax plus 2% local income tax surtax) on outbound dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, unless a lower bilateral tax treaty rate applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Korean Master File and Local File documentation is required where total annual sales exceed KRW 100 billion and related-party cross-border transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; a simplified Local File is required where international controlled transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; for a Chusik Hoesa in recycled copper wire trading, the national tax service expects contemporaneous documentation above KRW 1 billion due to the high-risk commodity classification.

Economic substance scope: Korean tax enforcement applies the substance-over-form principle under Article 36 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the OECD MLI for treaty benefits; the economic substance doctrine requires the Chusik Hoesa to perform real core income-generating activities in Korea, including local negotiations, logistics control, inventory risk assumption, and board decisions on scrap copper acquisitions; if the entity is deemed to lack substance, the National Tax Service may disregard its separate legal existence, deny treaty benefits, and reallocate profits to the offshore principal, subject to 30-35% penalty tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent under-declaration through artificial intercompany invoice booking

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 61 and 76 of the Korean Corporate Income Tax Act and Articles 24 and 31 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the taxpayer must pay the evaded corporate income tax, a penalty tax of 30-35% of the under-declared income, daily surcharges at 10.95% per annum, and a criminal fine equal to the amount under-declared.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil when the Chusik Hoesa is a purely formal entity; directors become jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest, penalties, and restitution of the offshore margin; the National Tax Service imposes a global travel ban and blacklisting on each director; participation in the false invoices leads to imprisonment of 1 to 5 years under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act.
Failure to withhold and remit withholding tax on cross-border dividends, royalties, and interest

Statutory sanction: Under Article 127 of the Korean Corporate Income Tax Act, the Korean Chusik Hoesa is the withholding agent; failure to withhold triggers immediate collection of the withheld-remittance amount plus a 10% negligence penalty and daily arrears, and if intent is established the national tax authority adds a 35% fraud penalty and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly permit the unlawful remittance are jointly liable for the entire unpaid WHT and penalties; civil restitution of the remitted funds is ordered by Korean courts; a travel ban is applied while unpaid taxes remain; criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years is enforceable for misappropriation of tax, with each director's record struck in the tax violator registry.
Shell-company recharacterization and money laundering through circular recycled-scrap payment rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transactions Reports Act and the AML enforcement framework, the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit and tax authorities impose administrative fines up to KRW 300 million, require repatriation of the circular proceeds, and levy a 20% international tax coordination surcharge under the International Tax Coordination Law; the entity may be deregistered and permanently barred from import-export licenses.

Director personal liability: The Commercial Act and judicial veil-piercing permit the court to disregard limited liability; directors are personally liable for all third-party claims, trade debts, and tax obligations; asset tracing from observed LC and Swift rails enables civil asset restitution; a travel ban is placed on every director during the criminal investigation; conviction under the AML Act carries imprisonment of 5 to 10 years, and each director's passport is seized and blacklisting remains after release.

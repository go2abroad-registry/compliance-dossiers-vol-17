Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None required by law. The former statutory minimum paid-in capital of KRW 50,000,000 for a Chusik Hoesa was abolished by the December 2012 amendment to the Commercial Act. A company may be incorporated with any stated capital, provided that the full subscription price for every allotted and issued share is deposited into a Korean bank subscription account before the incorporation registration application is filed with the competent District Court. There is no blocked escrow account under Korean company law; the subscription deposit cannot be withdrawn by the promoters before successful registration, and if incorporation fails, the deposit is refunded to the subscribers. After registration, the deposit is released into the corporation's operating bank account. Financial institutions and payment processors such as Stripe and PayPal may impose contractual minimum balance or operating capital expectations, but that is not a government capital blockade rule.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: Home Tax Service (Hometax) at hometax.go.kr; export customs declarations are processed through the Korea Customs Service via the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Any foreign non-resident enterprise that engages in a Korean VAT-taxable supply chain or establishes a taxable nexus in the Republic of Korea must apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN) with the National Tax Service (NTS) through the Home Tax Service (HTS) portal or at the competent district tax office. The application is made on the statutory business registration application for a non-resident or foreign corporation and must be accompanied by (i) a certified copy of the articles of incorporation and certificate of corporate existence in the home jurisdiction; (ii) the foreign enterprise's tax identification number; (iii) documents evidencing the appointment of directors and, where required, a domestic tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes; (iv) preliminary financial statements or a start-up declaration showing the intended taxable supply activity; and (v) a power of attorney when filed through a licensed tax agent or legal representative. After verification, the NTS issues a 10-digit BRN that simultaneously functions as the Korean TIN and VAT identification number. A foreign non-resident supplier without a domestic place of business may use the simplified non-resident electronic-service registration procedure in the Home Tax Service and must obtain a BRN before issuing Korean tax invoices or filing VAT returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a domestic place of business or fixed establishment, mandatory VAT registration applies from the first Korean taxable supply with no general turnover exemption. For foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic or digital services, compulsory VAT registration is triggered when the annual Korean B2C supply is estimated to exceed KRW 300,000,000 in the current calendar year or actual Korean B2C supply exceeded KRW 300,000,000 in the preceding calendar year. Imported goods remain subject to Korean customs VAT at importation regardless of seller registration status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exports of goods are zero-rated where the goods are shipped or transported from Korea to a foreign country, ownership passes to a non-resident buyer, and the export customs declaration is accepted by the Korea Customs Service. Qualifying offshore services and certain international transport services may also be zero-rated. In an automated dropshipping model, the Korean entity may zero-rate only the export supply where it actually exports the goods from Korea to the foreign customer; if goods are sourced from overseas fulfillment centers and never enter Korea, the transaction is not a Korean domestic VAT supply and must be excluded from the Korean VAT return rather than zero-rated. The taxpayer must maintain an auditable chain from foreign order, purchase order, supplier invoice, transportation documentation, export clearance, and foreign exchange repatriation to substantiate the zero-rating claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, including the UNI-PASS export clearance record and export reference number
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, quantity, value, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing consignment to the non-resident buyer
  • Purchase order or sales contract from the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or export proceeds collection confirmation issued by a foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of the overseas sales proceeds

VAT-registered persons in Korea are required to issue statutory Korean tax invoices and to participate in the NTS electronic tax invoice system through the Home Tax Service portal or NTS-approved e-invoicing service providers. For B2B supplies, electronic tax invoices are mandatory and must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following issuance; a digital acceptance and audit-trail record is created in the Home Tax Service. Korea does not operate a continuous fiscal pre-clearance stamping regime, but the NTS applies mandatory e-invoicing, anti-fraud cross-matching of purchase and sales documentation, and periodic aggregate VAT data reporting through statutory VAT returns.

The NTS may audit export zero-rating claims within the statutory five-year assessment period. If the mandatory export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or falsified, the NTS will claw back the zero-rated VAT by reassessing VAT at the standard 10% rate, disallow any associated input VAT refund or credit, and impose statutory penalty taxes for under-reported tax, unlawful or unfounded tax invoices, fraudulent refunds, and delayed payment. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating can attract an aggravated penalty of up to 40% of the tax in arrears, additional interest for each period of delay, customs penalties under the Customs Act, and criminal referral for tax evasion or document forgery. The Korea Customs Service may also impose separate penalty taxes for irregularities in export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a managed free-floating exchange-rate regime for the Korean won. Current-account transactions, including trade settlement and service payments, are effectively liberalized and may be executed through Foreign Exchange Banks with minimal documentary checks. Capital-account transactions, by contrast, remain subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, requiring prior declaration, reporting, or approval depending on the nature and size of the flow. There is no statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts, but amounts must be repatriated to Korea within the statutory deadline. Dividend and profit remittances by a Chusik Hoesa require audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and regulatory proof that the foreign direct investment was properly notified and certified. In the dropshipping context, the absence of physical import/export customs declarations is a major hurdle, as cross-border settlement through Stripe and PayPal often lacks the supporting trade documentation required by Korean FX dealers. Consequently, treasury planning must align every inward payment to an underlying declared trade or service transaction, and every outward vendor payment must be mapped to an approved import or service contract. This makes the convertibility assessment operationally convertible but administratively heavy, requiring a licensed local banking relationship for currency conversion and remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within one year from the date of shipment for general exports, extendable up to one additional year with the approval of the relevant Foreign Exchange Bank. No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; proceeds may be retained in a designated foreign currency account, but must be repatriated to a domestic Foreign Exchange Bank in an amount equivalent to the export invoice, with reported collection under FEIN.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with practitioner-level supervision through Foreign Exchange Banks and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary settlement rail. South Korea requires any entity performing cross-border money transfer or foreign exchange brokerage services to be licensed as a foreign exchange business under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut have no legal standing to open local Korean bank accounts for a Chusik Hoesa, cannot participate in domestic KRW clearing through BOK-Wire+, and are not recognized depositories for capital registration, FX declaration, or tax payment purposes. Inbound share-subscription capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed as a formal foreign direct investment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary settlement rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and supporting transfer pricing documentation must be prepared when annual international related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File is required where consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion, and Country-by-Country Report is mandatory when consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion; all thresholds are expressed in Korean won before VAT

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the treaty anti-conduit beneficial-ownership rule, so the Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate substantive core income-generating activities in Korea, including order management, customer service, strategic procurement, platform control, payment settlement judgment, and risk assumption; although standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE type are not separately enacted in Korea, income attribution and permanent establishment standards under the OECD framework equally require the Korean entity to show real staff, premises, and decision-making substance, otherwise it may be disregarded as a shell or invoice-through company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Offshore Sales Suppression)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Statute on Punishment of Tax Evaders, aggravated tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding five times the evaded tax; the National Tax Service will additionally assess a 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty, the 10% Local Income Tax surcharge, daily late-payment interest, and may deny the use of loss carryforwards to shelter concealed income

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the Chusik Hoesa's veil when the company is a mere instrument of the non-resident principal and directors sign false returns while commingling personal and corporate PayPal/Stripe proceeds; the representative director faces personal civil liability to the revenue authority, vulnerability to asset seizure and NTS travel-ban blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment for false declarations; directors involved in funneling undisclosed sales to a foreign controlling shareholder are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest.
Transfer Pricing and Sham Procurement Structure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may adjust taxable income to the arm's length result under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, add a 30% transfer pricing adjustment penalty, disallow the non-arm's length expense, impose ordinary understatement penalties of 20% to 40% for omitted controlled transaction schedules, and deny management charges as non-deductible; unpaid adjusted tax triggers collection enforcement, travel blacklisting, and criminal referral thresholds

Director personal liability: Directors who approve sham invoices or route inflated markups to shell suppliers are treated as quasi-trustees; they may be personally liable for the adjusted tax difference, statutory surcharges, and interest, their Korean entry/exit can be banned, and if the margin extraction is structured to defraud the tax authority or creditors, criminal investigation for tax evasion can result in imprisonment of the responsible directors notwithstanding the company's separate legal personality.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will deny treaty benefits under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and beneficial ownership requirements, reassess the full 22% withholding tax plus interest, impose a 40% fraudulent understatement additional tax, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution under the Statute on Punishment of Tax Evaders where wilful false statements are made; the Korean payer's failure to withhold also makes the Chusik Hoesa and its managing director jointly liable for the unreported withholding tax

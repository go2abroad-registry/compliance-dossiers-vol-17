Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic legal entity under the Korean Commercial Act and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act. It is not eligible for pass-through taxation; limited liability company status does not create an S-corporation or partnership-style fiscal transparency. A foreign non-resident corporation with a domestic place of business is taxable on its Korean-source income, but the Korean Yuhan Hoesa is itself the registered taxpayer for corporate tax and VAT.

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime comparable to the United States. Corporate residence and tax classification are determined by statute and by the place of effective management or registered head office under the Corporate Tax Act. Hybrid-mismatch adjustments are mandatory under the International Tax Adjustment Act, implementing BEPS Action 2, and are not electable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary threshold for mandatory VAT registration of a Korean corporate taxpayer; any person carrying on a taxable business in Korea, including a Yuhan Hoesa manufacturing instant rice noodles, must register with the National Tax Service upon commencing business. For individual small business suppliers with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, the law permits simplified taxable person treatment, but a limited liability company is treated as a general corporate taxpayer and must complete VAT registration regardless of expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exported goods under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are sold by a VAT-registered Korean business to a foreign purchaser and are physically removed from Korea through a valid customs export clearance. The exporter must hold an export declaration (export clearance report) evidencing that the instant rice noodles or other packaged food goods were shipped outside Korea, together with transport documents and proof that the export proceeds have been or will be received in convertible foreign currency in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Services, including certain qualifying offshore digital services, may also be zero-rated where the place of supply is deemed to be outside Korea; however, foreign electronic or digital services directly supplied to Korean consumers are subject to the standard 10% VAT and are not eligible for zero rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Report / export declaration receipt issued by Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading for sea cargo or Air Waybill for air cargo
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence (Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent foreign bank receipt) confirming settlement of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Sales contract or purchase order evidencing the foreign buyer
  • Export shipment and customs clearance records necessary to match goods, quantities, and dates of removal from Korea

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice system for VAT tax invoices issued between registered business entities. For B2B supplies, the supplier must issue an electronic tax invoice through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system or a certified electronic tax invoice service provider and transmit the electronic tax invoice to the NTS no later than the day after the invoice issuance date. There is no physical fiscal stamp or hardware fiscal seal; anti-fraud control is achieved through real-time electronic transmission, digital certificate authentication of the issuer, NTS cross-matching of e-tax invoice data against VAT returns, and mandatory cash receipt issuance for certain consumer transactions. Export shipments are cleared through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic system, and export customs data is automatically cross-matched with VAT zero-rating claims.

The National Tax Service and the Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating claims by cross-matching VAT e-tax invoices, customs export declarations, shipping documents, freight bills, and foreign exchange settlement records. If an export zero-rating claim is found to lack valid customs clearance, genuine transportation to a foreign country, or required foreign exchange receipt evidence, the claimed 0% rate is denied, the 10% VAT is assessed on the supply, and the taxpayer is exposed to statutory additional tax for under-reporting or unconstitutionally claiming a refund. False export documentation and fraudulent refund claims may additionally trigger criminal penalties under the Punishment for Tax Offenses Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Export VAT refunds will be retrospectively clawed back with interest, and the statutory assessment period is generally five years, extended to up to a longer period for deliberate concealment or fraudulent tax evasion. For goods exports, foreign exchange settlement must generally be completed within 180 days of export clearance; failure to settle within that period may result in disallowance of the zero rating and foreign-exchange penalties unless an extension is lawfully obtained.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalised capital account framework while imposing registration, reporting, and documentation requirements to preserve financial stability. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade settlement and service flows, with no volume restrictions on export proceeds being received in freely usable foreign currencies. For capital account matters, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires prior reporting or registration for inbound foreign direct investment (FDI), external borrowings, and certain intra-group financial transactions. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are lawful upon corporate tax settlement, and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FEPA) guarantees national treatment and free repatriation of proceeds for properly registered foreign-invested enterprises. The practical hurdles are not legal convertibility but documentary: a foreign-invested enterprise must obtain a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate or a Confirmation of Exemption from a domestic bank, open an investment capital escrow account, and verify the capital introduction through bank-issued certificates. Surrender rules for export proceeds apply under a collection-period regime: all Korean exporters must receive payment within one year from the date of shipment, and the payment must be cleared through a licensed foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; exporters may keep foreign currency in a resident foreign currency deposit account or convert at prevailing market rates, but any conversion or sale of dollars for won must be executed through the banking channel with a trade purpose code to fully close the customs export B/L. The Financial Services Commission and the Korea Customs Service cross-verify trade declarations, which effectively compels accurate pricing and arm's-length invoicing for rice noodle and food ingredient imports. Overall, the regime supports efficient treasury management for a manufacturing entity, but treasury officers must calendarise all settlement deadlines and maintain a dual filing protocol with the tax office and customs authority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 6 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and MOEF Notification, export proceeds must be collected within 365 days from the date of export shipment; no statutory conversion ratio is imposed, but all receipts must be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank, and if the exporter chooses to convert to KRW, the conversion must be transacted at a domestic FX market rate with proper trade settlement certificate filing. For retort rice noodle products exported to Asian and Western markets, the treasury department must archive the Bill of Lading, commercial invoice, customs declaration, and bank receipt to evidence compliance within the collection window; extensions require a formal application submitted before maturity to the domicile bank with supporting evidence of delay.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with policy administration delegated to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and operational supervision through licensed foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act mandate that any entity engaging in cross-border funds transfers or holding virtual operating accounts must transact through either a domestic licensed bank or a licensed electronic money transmitter that has secured a remittance licence from the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Most offshore digital EMIs do not hold a Korean banking intermediary licence and are legally barred from onboarding Korean-domiciled legal entities for KRW settlement and customs-related trade payments. For this rice noodle manufacturing entity, relying on an offshore EMI would generate immediate KYC friction, impossible purpose-code mapping for customs clearance, and a real risk of freezing export proceeds. The only defensible route is to establish a local bank account at a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank and use that as the anchor for any fintech overlay (e.g., a licensed remittance API from a FSC-licensed payment service provider) for receivables aggregation from global e-commerce marketplaces.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's own account to the designated 'Capital Escrow Account' at a Korean commercial bank that has issued a Certificate of Foreign Exchange Bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act mandate that any entity engaging in cross-border funds transfers or holding virtual operating accounts must transact through either a domestic licensed bank or a licensed electronic money transmitter that has secured a remittance licence from the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Most offshore digital EMIs do not hold a Korean banking intermediary licence and are legally barred from onboarding Korean-domiciled legal entities for KRW settlement and customs-related trade payments. For this rice noodle manufacturing entity, relying on an offshore EMI would generate immediate KYC friction, impossible purpose-code mapping for customs clearance, and a real risk of freezing export proceeds. The only defensible route is to establish a local bank account at a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank and use that as the anchor for any fintech overlay (e.g., a licensed remittance API from a FSC-licensed payment service provider) for receivables aggregation from global e-commerce marketplaces.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100 billion annual revenue plus KRW 5 billion cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory electronic submission of the International Transactions Adjustment Report and contemporaneous Master File/Local File documentation; NTS may nevertheless request transfer pricing documents for any transaction regardless of size within 30 days of demand.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply an UAE-style ESR, but the NTS uses a substance-over-form general anti-abuse rule under Article 17 of the Framework Act for National Taxes and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as ingredient sourcing, retort and canning operations, quality assurance, instant noodle R&D, pricing, invoicing, and risk management must actually be performed in Korea with real people and facilities. If the company is only an invoice or royalty conduit and the CIGA are performed by offshore related entities, NTS can recharacterize the arrangement, deny DTA benefits, and reallocate profit to the real Korean operating company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell or conduit company with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: NTS applies the substance-over-form general anti-abuse rule under Article 17 of the Framework Act for National Taxes and Article 11 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; the offending arrangement is disregarded, the foreign shareholder may be treated as a Korean permanent establishment, and unpaid corporate tax plus a 20% unfair tax penalty is assessed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors who knowingly established or operated the shell are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; NTS may blacklist the directors as severe delinquents, impose a travel ban, and refer the case to prosecutors for punishment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment up to five years and/or a fine up to three times the evaded tax.
Transfer pricing and profit shifting on export sales to a foreign related distributor

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service adjusts taxable income to the arm's-length price under Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; the underpayment attracts a 10% transfer pricing adjustment penalty, plus the 0.022% daily late-payment surcharge and potential denial of related-party expense deductions if contemporaneous documentation is not maintained.

Director personal liability: If the directors signed intercompany agreements and instructed the pricing, invoicing, and back-to-back margin stripping, Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil and order restitution of diverted profits; criminal tax evasion prosecution can reach the CEO and finance director, including imprisonment up to five years, fines, and seizure of bank accounts.
Cross-border withholding tax and DTA abuse on outbound dividend, interest, and royalty flows

Statutory sanction: The Korean company as withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld 22%, an unfaithful withholding penalty of 20%, and daily late-payment surcharges under the Framework Act for National Taxes; if the reduced treaty rate is later denied, the shortfall is immediately collectible from the Korean payer with no period of grace.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused the under-withholding or falsified beneficial ownership and TRC documentation are jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax and penalties; travel blacklisting or exit restriction can be imposed for unpaid tax above the statutory threshold, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can lead to imprisonment up to five years and fines.

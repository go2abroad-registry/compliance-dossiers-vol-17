VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to Korean-resident businesses with a place of business in Korea; every business operator is required to complete VAT/business operator registration within 20 days from commencement. For foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services without a domestic place of business, mandatory VAT registration and collection obligations arise when annual taxable turnover from Korean consumers exceeds 100,000,000 KRW or where the supply must otherwise be reported under the VAT Act and enforcement rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Korea under a confirmed customs export declaration. Zero-rating also applies to qualifying exported services, including internationally traded services supplied by a Korean business operator to a foreign customer or foreign business operator, where the services are not effectively consumed within Korea, the consideration is received in foreign currency through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank or approved settlement method, and the services are classified under the exported-services provisions of the Value Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree. In the EOR and global staffing agency context, cross-border payroll administration and employer-of-record service fees may qualify for 0% VAT only if the invoiced service is performed for a foreign resident recipient, the foreign-resident status is evidenced by the overseas customer's business registration or legislation certificate, the contract clearly identifies the offshore usage and nature of the service, and foreign-currency inward repatriation is documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Declaration Form) confirmed by the Korea Customs Service
  • Original or electronic marine/ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice and underlying purchase order or international service agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange purchase/sale certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign currency bank, showing actual repatriation of export or service proceeds
  • Overseas buyer or client business registration certificate, foreign tax identification certificate, or other official evidence of foreign status (for service exports)
  • Service performance records, schedules of work, payroll disbursement ledger, and narrative explanation of offshore delivery and foreign-currency settlement, where required for export service audits

Electronic tax invoice issuance is mandatory for all business-to-business Korean supplies, including EOR management fees, payroll processing fees, and service fees, through the National Tax Service HomeTax integrated electronic tax-invoice system. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS no later than the day immediately following the supply date using NTS-approved EDI, server-to-server API, or the HomeTax web portal. Cash receipts are required for qualifying B2C transactions, and the NTS operates continuous post-issuance validation and matching systems to detect duplicate, false, or forged tax invoices, though the Korean system does not apply a per-invoice real-time digital stamping clearance model.

The National Tax Service may audit export VAT refund claims and the related Korean Customs Service export performance records, foreign-currency repatriation receipts, and service-delivery documentation. A taxpayer that cannot substantiate the export declaration, physical shipment, or offshore service status will be denied zero-rating and will face a supplementary VAT assessment at the 10% standard rate together with statutory additional taxes, including the VAT additional tax of up to 30% of the disputed supply value or tax amount, delay interest, and possible retroactive clawback of refunds already paid. The filing of a false export tax invoice, fraudulent export refund claim, or fictitious inward remittance documentation may additionally trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, including imprisonment or fines, and the loss of refund tracing and carry-forward privileges in subsequent tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised but carefully supervised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Law, the Presidential Decree on Foreign Exchange Transactions, and the delegated enforcement regulations of the Financial Services Commission. The current account, including service fee receipts and profit remittances, is fully transferable for bona fide companies once the underlying contract is submitted to a designated foreign exchange bank, and the Korean won is externally convertible for current transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles. The capital account is open for foreign direct investment and inward portfolio investment but is subject to key reporting obligations and the diligence of the onward conversion; no general capital controls or surrender quotas exist today. The main operational hurdle is not legal convertibility but documentary completeness at the time of the designated bank compliance review. Dividends and profits can be remitted without approval if the company produces corporate tax returns, audited financial statements, a dividend distribution resolution, and a bank statement for tax payments. Intercompany loans and equity injections require prior notice to the MOEF or the bank, and short-term credit transactions may be blocked if the bank cannot establish a legitimate commercial purpose. As a result, liquidity can be moved quickly but not through anonymised structures; the central bank requires every FX conversion and remittance to be traceable to a real underlying economic transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds, including cross-border services rendered by an EOR platform, must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the standard collection deadline is the arrival of payment within 6 months for goods exports; services receipts are recognised on receipt and are not covered by a formal collection notice but must be reported in the bank's FX information system if denominated in foreign currency. There is no mandatory conversion to KRW: export proceeds can be retained in non-resident or resident foreign currency accounts, subject to periodic reporting requirements. When the exporter opts to convert, the market exchange rate at the designated bank applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FERI), under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, cross-border money transfer and remittance services require a foreign exchange transaction business registration with the Minister of Economy and Finance. Offshore digital EMIs, including Wise and similar platforms, that do not hold a specific Korean foreign exchange business licence cannot lawfully maintain a corporate treasury rail for a resident Korean entity. A Korean corporation may not rely on an offshore EMI for inbound capital verification, statutory payroll clearing, or corporate FX settlement. The account structure must therefore sit with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank that is already designated as a foreign exchange bank and is authorised to report directly to the FERI system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Korean chusik hoesa with non-resident ownership, the inbound capital injection must be routed through a designated foreign exchange bank with the appropriate direct investment purpose code for capital acquisition by a foreign-invested enterprise. The foreign investor must first submit a foreign investment notification to the MOEF through the bank or KOTRA platform, obtain an investment registration certificate, and then cause the wire transfer to be received in the temporary foreign currency account of the Korean entity. Upon settlement into KRW, the bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase certificate, and the entity must submit the corporate registration, bank deposit statements, the foreign investment registration certificate, and the purchase certificate to complete the capital verification process. For shareholder loans, a separate loan purpose code is used, and the loan agreement, board resolution, and interest rate terms must be provided to the bank so that the funds can be reported accurately in the monthly foreign exchange reporting. The final step is an accountant's capital verification report confirming receipt of paid-in capital, which is necessary for tax and corporate registry filings.The primary clearing rail for local payroll is the bank's bulk direct debit and household banking system, which allows settlement of EOR wages through the domestic automated clearing network. For remuneration to overseas contractors and foreign consultants, the entity should use SWIFT MT103 payments from its designated foreign exchange bank, quoting an FX purpose code for service fees and attaching the underlying EOR client service agreement, invoice, timesheet, and tax assignment documents. For trade-related disbursements where the EOR platform is acting as a supplier of cross-border services, Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections under ICC UCP and URC can be used, but they are more suitable for goods-based collateral flows than for pure payroll services; in practice, MT103 wiring with proof of service is the standard rail for a global EOR. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, cross-border money transfer and remittance services require a foreign exchange transaction business registration with the Minister of Economy and Finance. Offshore digital EMIs, including Wise and similar platforms, that do not hold a specific Korean foreign exchange business licence cannot lawfully maintain a corporate treasury rail for a resident Korean entity. A Korean corporation may not rely on an offshore EMI for inbound capital verification, statutory payroll clearing, or corporate FX settlement. The account structure must therefore sit with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank that is already designated as a foreign exchange bank and is authorised to report directly to the FERI system.

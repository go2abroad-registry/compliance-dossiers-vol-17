Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act after the 2012 capital-regime reform. Nevertheless, the full amount of the subscribed share capital must be paid in at incorporation, either in cash or in kind, to a designated frozen subscription account opened in the name of the to-be-formed corporation. The deposit remains blocked and may not be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is completed. In-kind contributions must be supported by a court-recognized appraisal report where applicable. The Articles of Incorporation must state the number and par value of shares and, if a par-value system is selected, each share must have a nominal value of at least KRW 100. No separate blocked escrow account beyond this statutory deposit restriction is required for a cash-funded Chusik Hoesa.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no civil defense fire permit is required for an online retail office and administrative location. A standard building occupancy certificate and the landlord's confirmation of compliance with the Korean Fire Safety Code should be retained. If the Company operates a merchandise stockroom, warehouse, or any area exceeding 100 square meters, or stores substantial quantities of combustible packaging, the local fire department will require a fire-fighting facility inspection and a business-use civil defense clearance under the Firefighting Facilities Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Control Act. An occupational safety and health duty under the Occupational Safety and Health Act arises as soon as the Company employs Korean workers; the CEO must file a workplace establishment report and retain a workplace health and safety manager where required by applicable thresholds.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, accessed through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation), the statutory tax identification number is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN). After the company is registered with the Korean court registry, it must file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered place of business within 20 days of business commencement. A foreign non-resident entity engaging in VAT-liable activities in Korea without a Korean place of business must appoint a domestic tax representative in accordance with Article 53 of the Korean VAT Act. The tax representative then files a non-resident business registration application with the NTS district tax office, stating the foreign entity's legal name, registered address, description of the business, and the tax representative's details. Upon approval, the NTS issues a BRN and VAT registration number. A foreign corporation that maintains a Korean place of business or permanent establishment registers under the same standard BRN issuance procedure through its local branch or fixed place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists under Korean tax law for a Chusik Hoesa. A qualifying domestic parent and its wholly owned Korean subsidiaries may elect to file a consolidated corporate tax return under Article 76-17 of the Corporate Tax Act, provided the 100% ownership requirement is continuously satisfied from the beginning of the fiscal year. Certain professional service companies may elect the pass-through enterprise regime under the Special Tax Treatment Control Act, but a general e-commerce stock corporation selling jewelry and timepieces does not qualify. No election is available to treat a corporation as a simplified VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT exemption threshold is available. Every business operator must register for VAT with the district tax office within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxpayer regime applies only to individual business operators with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000; a Chusik Hoesa is always treated as a general taxpayer and must register regardless of projected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, exports of goods from the Republic of Korea are zero-rated. Zero-rating applies where the goods are delivered for export under a Korea Customs Service export declaration and actually depart the customs territory. Qualifying services include services supplied to non-residents, services connected with overseas property, and services performed wholly outside Korea. For an e-commerce seller of fashion jewelry and fine timepieces, the seller must evidence a genuine export transaction by retaining the customs export declaration, transport documentation confirming international movement of the goods, a commercial invoice, and order/payment records showing the customer is a non-resident and the goods were dispatched to a foreign address. For qualifying offshore digital services, additional evidence such as IP address records, customer service contracts, and service performance logs must be retained. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is deductible and can be refunded via the VAT return mechanism.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • International Sales Contract or Purchase Order
  • Certificate of Foreign Exchange Acquisition (Korean equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Proof of payment from the foreign buyer (if not covered by the foreign exchange certificate)

All general VAT taxpayers, including every Chusik Hoesa, are required to issue electronic tax invoices (E-tax invoices) through the NTS HomeTax portal. Tax invoice details must be transmitted electronically to the NTS within the statutory filing period of the relevant VAT taxable period. Korea does not rely on a private digital stamping API or real-time clearance regime of the Latin American type; fiscal control is enforced through the mandatory electronic transmission of E-tax invoices, cash receipt issuance for consumer sales, and NTS data reconciliation.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating by reconciling export declarations, foreign exchange acquisition records, and the exporter's VAT returns. If the goods are found not to have physically left Korea, the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, or the supporting export and foreign exchange documentation is missing or fraudulent, the NTS will reassess output VAT at 10%, impose additional tax penalties under the VAT Act (including aggravated penalties for intentional understatements), and claw back any input VAT previously refunded. Criminal liability may be pursued under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for serious export VAT fraud. The statutory reassessment periods are up to five years for ordinary misreporting and ten years for unfiled returns or false/revenue-generating tax invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a predominantly liberalized foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, implemented by Bank of Korea and Ministry of Economy and Finance. Current account transactions, including e-commerce export receivables and trade settlement, are generally freely convertible and may be settled through Korean commercial banks without prior approval, although statistical reporting obligations remain. Capital account transactions are more tightly controlled: foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and acquisition of securities require filings or approvals through a licensed foreign exchange bank or BOK. Dividends and profits of a Chusik Hoesa may be remitted abroad after the availability approach satisfies the foreign investment report and a bank verifies audited financial statements, tax clearance, and corporate resolution. The Korean won is exchangeable through the local interbank market; offshore KRW deliverability is restricted and non-resident access is channeled primarily through onshore bank accounts or NDFs. No general surrender obligation applies to export receipts; exporters can retain foreign currency, but must ensure settlement and reporting. The main hurdles for treasury are administrative rather than convertibility based, namely corporate KYC documentation, foreign investment registration, and proof of bona fide trade transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The standard statutory settlement period for export proceeds is six months from the date of export declaration. Where unavoidable commercial or force majeure circumstances prevent settlement within that period, the exporter or the foreign exchange bank may seek an extension of up to six months from BOK or the competent foreign exchange authority. There is no mandatory KRW conversion ratio applicable to export proceeds; exporters are permitted to retain 100% of export foreign currency receipts in a foreign currency deposit account, subject to foreign exchange notification and supporting export documentation. The mandatory conversion ratio is therefore effectively 0% to KRW, with 100% permitted offshore or onshore foreign currency retention.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with operational oversight delegated to licensed domestic foreign exchange banks and anti-money laundering supervision by the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Low. Overseas financial-technology EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are not licensed as foreign exchange banks or deposit-taking institutions in South Korea. They cannot lawfully issue locally recognized KRW bank account equivalents, process statutory Korean FX reporting, or serve as the primary treasury clearing rail for a Korea-resident Chusik Hoesa. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act restrict payment and foreign exchange business to authorized Korean financial institutions. A cross-border EMI may be used only as a supplemental collection conduit feeding into a tier-1 Korean bank account, not as the operational treasury account. For a corporate entity with Korean payroll, VAT, corporate tax, and customs obligations, a licensed Korean bank is mandatory for legal clearing and regulatory reporting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low. For a corporate entity with Korean payroll, VAT, corporate tax, and customs obligations, a licensed Korean bank is mandatory for legal clearing and regulatory reporting.

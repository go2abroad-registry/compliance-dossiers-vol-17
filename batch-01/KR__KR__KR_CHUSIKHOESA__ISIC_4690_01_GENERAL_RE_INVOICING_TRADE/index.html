Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS). The statutory electronic registration and filing portal is the Home Tax Service (HTS) at www.hometax.go.kr. Cross-border goods clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic trade portal (unipass.customs.go.kr).

        
E-government filing portal
For a Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa, the entity must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as both the corporate tax ID and VAT ID, from the competent NTS district office within 20 days of business commencement. The application is submitted through the Home Tax Service portal or at the local district office and must include the articles of incorporation, statutory shareholding and board documentation, evidence of domestic registered address, and initial business plan. For a foreign non-resident entity that has a Korean VAT registration obligation because it maintains a domestic place of business, makes taxable supplies in Korea, or qualifies under the special VAT regime for foreign suppliers of electronic services, the procedure requires: (1) confirmation of Korean nexus; (2) appointment of an authorized Korean tax representative if no domestic place of business exists; (3) submission of the authentic foreign registration certificate, articles of association, power of attorney, passport or equivalent identity documents of the signatory, and evidence of the domestic or deemed place of business; (4) submission of the business registration application through the Home Tax Service or the district office with jurisdiction over the registered business address; (5) receipt of the 10-digit Business Registration Number and official Business Registration Certificate; and (6) designation of a Korean bank account for VAT refund and foreign exchange settlement. The BRN is the unified national TIN for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and customs transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. The Republic of Korea does not operate a check-the-box election or a separate entity classification election for a Chusik Hoesa. A Korean stock corporation is mandatorily taxed as an entity-level resident corporation on its worldwide income. No hybrid mismatch entity-classification election is available; Korean anti-hybrid rules are applied by statute, not by taxpayer election.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No general economic turnover threshold triggers VAT registration in South Korea. Registration is mandatory before a taxable person commences any VATable business in Korea. A simplified taxable person regime applies to certain small individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80,000,000. Foreign suppliers of electronic services are subject to mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply, with an administrative criterion of prior-year or projected Korean remote-service turnover exceeding KRW 300,000,000.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating is available for exported goods that physically depart from Korea under an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. Zero-rating is also available for certain foreign-currency-earning services performed outside Korea or consumed by a non-resident, including international transportation, overseas construction and installation services, and export trade intermediary or re-invoicing services supplied to non-resident customers, provided the service is not consumed in Korea and the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency. For a commercial re-invoicing hub, the 0% VAT claim requires evidence that the Korean intermediary services are rendered to a non-resident principal, that the underlying trade movement is outside Korea or is a certified Korean export, and that the foreign-exchange invoice flow is in hard currency. If title and possession of goods pass entirely outside Korea without any Korean customs export declaration, the supply is generally outside the Korean VAT territorial scope rather than zero-rated, and the file must document that no Korean VAT is imposed. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied by a Korean business to a non-resident customer are zero-rated when the B2B service is supplied to a person without a Korean place of business and the effective use and benefit occur outside Korea.

South Korea does not operate real-time pre-clearance fiscal stamping in the manner of fully digital revenue-capture jurisdictions. Instead, every registered taxable person must issue a valid tax invoice for each taxable supply of goods or services, and corporate taxable persons plus certain small businesses above the statutory e-issuance threshold must transmit an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) to the NTS through the Home Tax Service portal or an NTS-approved EDI/API integrator by no later than the end of the day following issuance. The e-tax invoice must contain the 10-digit BRN of the supplier and customer, supply date, description, quantity, supply value, VAT amount, and applicable zero-rating code. NTS automatically cross-matches purchase and sales e-tax invoices, import and export customs data, cash receipt data, and banking payment information to detect fictitious invoicing, omitted revenues, and false zero-rating claims. There is no government-issued anti-fraud digital stamp or QR fiscal pre-approval requirement for ordinary B2B invoices, but severe statutory penalties apply for failure to transmit e-tax invoices.

      
NTS audit review of export zero-rating combines the zero-rated e-tax invoice, customs export declaration, bill of lading, FIRC, bank settlement ledger, foreign-exchange transaction reports, and the underlying trade contract. If the zero-rated supply is not substantiated, or export proceeds are not repatriated and liquidated in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the applicable settlement deadlines, NTS may retrospectively claw back the 0% rate and assess output VAT at 10% on the full supply value, plus statutory interest for late payment under the National Tax Collection Act. Additional penalty tax is imposed under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act for underreporting, nontaxable misdeclaration, and fraudulent zero-rating claims, with penalty rates escalating from 20% of the unpaid tax for erroneous omission to 40% for fraudulent evasion; criminal proceedings may also be initiated under the Korean Tax Crimes Punishment Act. The national tax assessment limitation period is generally five years, extended to ten years for fraudulent or other willful concealment, meaning previously refunded VAT remains subject to audit and clawback within the applicable statutory window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a broadly liberalized current account with free convertibility for genuine trade, services, and unilateral transfers, but the Korean won is not a fully free-floating offshore-deliverable currency. Bona fide trade payments can be converted by licensed domestic banks upon presentation of invoices, contracts, and customs declaration evidence. Capital account transactions such as foreign direct investment contributions, intercompany loan advances, and equity issuance of a Chusik Hoesa require declaration or prior approval through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act framework and FIPA reporting to MOEF. Profit remittances, including dividends, are permissible after corporate tax settlement is evidenced, but the remitting bank will demand a certificate of tax payment, audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring the dividend, and a completed foreign exchange payment report. There is no forced surrender of export proceeds to the central bank; exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized foreign currency deposit accounts. However, the KRW's offshore market is limited, and onshore hedging through forwards or swaps is subject to the real-demand principle and underlying trade exposure. For a re-invoicing hub, capital verification of inbound equity and the clean documentation of each payables/receivables leg are the most material convertibility hurdles.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under Article 5 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Export Settlement Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 180 days from the date of export declaration or the date of shipment, whichever is applicable, unless an extension is granted by the head of the handling bank or MOEF for exceptional mercantile circumstances. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may hold foreign currency receipts indefinitely in a designated Foreign Currency Deposit Account at a licensed domestic bank, without converting them into Korean won. If the export claim remains unsettled past the statutory deadline, the bank must require a written explanation, may record the overdue claim, and may convert the outstanding receivable into KRW through the borrower's domestic account after reporting to the relevant authority. All export proceeds must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System or Korea Customs Service settlement records, and each designated bank must maintain the supporting trade documents for AML/CFT and balance-of-payments reporting.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with the Bank of Korea operating the cross-border settlement supervision and Foreign Exchange Information System.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low to nil. South Korean law does not treat offshore fintech EMI platforms as licensed banks for KRW clearing, foreign exchange settlement, or corporate account opening. The Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea require deposit-taking and foreign exchange activities to be performed through domestically licensed banking institutions or registered payment service providers with a clear partnership with a domestic bank. An offshore EMI cannot issue a KRW IBAN, cannot execute a SWIFT MT103 with a Korean purpose code, and cannot give the Chusik Hoesa a defendable FX reporting trail. At best, an EMI could be used as an ancillary cross-border collection tool if the funds are immediately swept into the entity's domestic Tier-1 bank account, but the core treasury infrastructure must remain with a Korean licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity for the Chusik Hoesa must be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank to the designated Korean bank account opened under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remittance must be labelled with the Korean foreign exchange purpose code for equity capital acquisition or foreign direct investment, and a FIPA foreign investment report must be lodged with the designated bank before or immediately after the transfer. The bank will hold the remittance in a capital escrow or designated foreign currency account and issue a capital verification certificate after the amount is credited and the share issuance resolution is filed. Conversion into KRW for operational liquidity is permitted only via the licensed bank's FX desk and must be matched to the actual cash flow needs of the entity. All inbound remittances must include a proper SWIFT Sender Reference and a structured narrative referencing the entity ID and the FIPA report number.Outbound supplier and trade settlement payments will be executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account and re-invoiced payables, commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) transmitted via SWIFT MT700 for documentary trade, and documentary collections on D/P or D/A basis using the bank's receiving and paying instruments. MT202 cover payments will be used for interbank reimbursement where required. Every outgoing payment must be supported by a valid underlying contract, commercial invoice, transport or receipt confirmation, and a Korean foreign exchange payment report prepared by the bank. For KRW-denominated settlements to non-resident counterparties, the bank will require confirmation of the trade purpose and may require a tax withholding certificate if royalties, interest, or service fees are involved. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to nil. South Korean law does not treat offshore fintech EMI platforms as licensed banks for KRW clearing, foreign exchange settlement, or corporate account opening. The Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea require deposit-taking and foreign exchange activities to be performed through domestically licensed banking institutions or registered payment service providers with a clear partnership with a domestic bank. An offshore EMI cannot issue a KRW IBAN, cannot execute a SWIFT MT103 with a Korean purpose code, and cannot give the Chusik Hoesa a defendable FX reporting trail. At best, an EMI could be used as an ancillary cross-border collection tool if the funds are immediately swept into the entity's domestic Tier-1 bank account, but the core treasury infrastructure must remain with a Korean licensed commercial bank.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
Progressive national corporate income tax: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200,000,000; 19% on the next band up to KRW 20,000,000,000; 21% on the next band up to KRW 300,000,000,000; and 24% on income exceeding KRW 300,000,000,000. A local income tax surcharge of 10% of the national CIT liability applies, yielding an effective headline range of 9.9% to 26.4%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
22% gross dividend withholding tax (20% national tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) imposed on dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder, unless a reduced rate is available under an applicable bilateral Double Tax Agreement (DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: 

      
Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the substance-over-form doctrine under case law and Article 4 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination. For a re-invoicing hub, economic substance requires actual employees, physical premises, managerial decision-making, risk-bearing capacity, and the ability to execute independent contractual functions. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is embedded in the Korean CFC and international tax rules; if the Chusik Hoesa conducts no business activity other than re-invoicing, the NTS may disregard its separate legal personality and attribute the re-invoicing profit to the foreign principal, resulting in re-characterization of service income as Korean-source and immediate withholding tax liability. The absence of economic substance also exposes the entity to penalties for sham tax avoidance and abuse of DTA benefits.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion through Sham Re-Invoicing / Shell Entity Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 52 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, the NTS can reallocate income to the foreign principal and deny all artificial deductions; Article 4 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination allows disregard of a conduit entity lacking substantive business purpose. The Basic Act for National Taxes Article 47-3 imposes an additional tax of 40% for willful understatement, and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1) authorizes criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300,000,000) and a fine up to three times the evaded tax.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under the Basic Act for National Taxes Article 39: a director who intentionally participated in the sham scheme is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and additional tax when the company's assets are insufficient. The NTS may impose a travel ban (international blacklisting) against the director through the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination Article 10, and the public prosecutor may institute criminal charges resulting in imprisonment, personal asset seizure, and civil restitution to the Korean Revenue from the director's personal estate.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Substance Disregard
        
Statutory sanction: Pursuant to Article 8(3) of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination, failure to submit transfer pricing documentation triggers an additional tax equal to 30% of the tax on the adjusted income. If the NTS determines that the missing documentation was intended to conceal an evasion scheme, the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act applies with fines up to KRW 50,000,000 for the corporate entity and additional imprisonment penalties for responsible officers.

        
Director personal liability: The CEO and CFO are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax under the joint responsibility provisions of the Basic Act for National Taxes Article 39 when the failure constitutes gross negligence. The NTS can obtain a provisional seizure of the directors' personal bank accounts and real property, issue a departure ban (travel blacklist) preventing the executives from leaving South Korea, and, if the omission is proven willful, the directors face personal criminal prosecution and civil damages from the foreign parent for breaching fiduciary duties.

      

      
      

        Unreported Foreign Account / Unlawful Profit Repatriation and Withholding Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: A failure to withhold triggers an additional tax of 10% of the unwithheld amount under the Basic Act for National Taxes, plus 3.5% per annum penalty interest. Under-reporting a foreign financial account with a balance exceeding KRW 500,000,000 incurs a fine of up to 20% of the unreported balance (capped at KRW 10,000,000,000) under the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination. The disguised dividend is reclassified as a dividend for withholding tax purposes, leading to the full 22% statutory rate, and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 authorizes imprisonment up to 3 years and fines up to three times the evaded tax.

        
Director personal liability: The Korean representative director who signed the sham loan agreement and authorized the transfer is personally liable for the unwithheld tax and penalty under the joint responsibility doctrine. A criminal indictment can result in imprisonment, a court-ordered travel ban (passport invalidation), and confiscation of the director's personal assets, including residential property, to satisfy the evasion amount. The foreign parent may also bring a civil claim against the director in Korea or in an international arbitration forum for violating corporate governance duties and harming the subsidiary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the current Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation must state the authorized shares and issued shares, and the full subscription price of all issued shares must be deposited in cash into a restricted escrow account at a licensed Korean bank in the name of the company being formed. The bank will block release of the funds until the incorporation registration is completed and a certificate of incorporation is issued. Non-resident investors may hold 100% of the shares after reporting the investment to KOTRA or a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the online electronic portal HomeTax at hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance is administered by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Chusik Hoesa, the foreign or domestic promoter must register the corporation for business tax purposes with the competent NTS district tax office through HomeTax or in person immediately upon incorporation, using the corporate registration record, articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, lease or title proof for the registered place of business, director identification documents, and shareholder details. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the unified taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and national tax filings. For a non-resident foreign entity, the foreign company must register as a foreign corporation with a Korean domestic place of business within two months of establishing the place of business, appoint a Korean tax representative if required, and file the business registration application with HomeTax or the district tax office, attaching the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, constituent documents, apostilled or consular-legalised proof of existence from the home jurisdiction, evidence of the Korean place of business, board resolutions relevant to the establishment, and written authorisation for the tax representative. The NTS then issues the same 10-digit Business Registration Number for all Korean tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Korea does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The only relevant statutory election for a Chusik Hoesa is the consolidated corporate taxation system, under which a qualifying resident parent corporation and its wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries may make a group election to file a consolidated corporate income tax return under the Corporation Tax Act. Separate prescriptive elections for special tax deductions and credits are made directly on the corporate tax return; no entity classification election is available for indirect VAT purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary turnover registration threshold under Korean VAT law: every entrepreneur, including a Chusik Hoesa or its domestic taxable place of business, supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT regardless of annual revenue. A simplified taxable person regime exists for small businesses with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80 million, but this affects the filing form and input VAT credit method and does not exempt registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies to exports of goods when the goods are physically removed from Korea and the export is evidenced by an export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service. For services: qualifying services supplied to foreign residents or overseas entities, including creator management and MCN agency services supplied to TikTok platforms or non-resident creators, are zero-rated when the service is contractually performed for a non-resident, the actual benefit and economic use occur in a foreign country, and the consideration is received in foreign currency or from foreign funds. The supply must be disclosed as a zero-rated supply with a zero-rated tax invoice issued in HomeTax, and the corresponding refund is obtained by deducting input VAT on related costs in the semi-annual VAT return.

  • Korean Customs Service Export Declaration and export clearance certificate, available electronically in UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, or written service agreement signed with the overseas TikTok platform or foreign creator entity
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice issued through NTS HomeTax, clearly marked as zero-rated foreign earning service
  • Foreign exchange purchase certificate, inward remittance certificate, or bank receipt evidencing actual receipt of foreign-currency consideration
  • Foreign currency remittance record and TikTok creator payout / platform monetisation reports substantiating that the economic benefit arose outside Korea and that the performance and beneficial use occurred abroad

VAT-registered businesses must issue all tax invoices electronically through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice module or through NTS-approved ASP electronic invoicing providers; enterprise resource planning systems may connect via secure official APIs / machine-to-machine interfaces to transmit invoice data to the NTS in near-real-time. Each electronic tax invoice is assigned an NTS approval number upon transmission, and the system supports anti-fraud controls such as cross-matching between purchasers' and suppliers' e-invoices, HomeTax reconciliation, and real-time monitoring of suspicious zero-rated issuance. There is no physical fiscal stamping device in Korea; the mandatory e-invoicing obligation operates as the fiscal clearance mechanism.

NTS together with Korea Customs Service will reconstruct export flows, verify customs export declarations, transport documents, foreign currency receipt records, and service performance evidence for every zero-rated export claim. If the required documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or if the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed period, the zero-rating is reversed, the supply is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply, VAT at 10% is assessed, and statutory additional taxes for non-declaration, under-declaration, or unfaithful tax invoices are imposed, typically up to 40% of the tax amount, with further interest and penalties for late payment. Fraudulent export refund claims or deliberate false documentation can trigger criminal tax-evasion penalties, including imprisonment up to three years or fines up to KRW 30 million, as well as joint collection liability against officers and controlling shareholders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating foreign exchange regime with full current-account convertibility and a managed capital-account liberalisation. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, cross-border settlements for business operations (royalties, platform service fees, profit repatriation) are generally unrestricted but require timely reporting to a designated Korean Foreign Exchange Bank. Capital transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections and capital reductions, are subject to a declaration regime; prior approval from the MOEF is still required for certain sensitive flows (e.g., strategic industries). There is no mandatory surrender requirement on export proceeds; exporters may keep foreign currency balances in domestic deposit accounts, although the proceeds must be repatriated within statutory timelines to support settlement accounting. Profit remittance for a foreign-invested Chusik Hoesa is allowed through the Korea Investment and Financing Window, provided the original FDI report, audited financial statements, and withholding tax clearance are presented. In practice, individual wire transfers above USD 50,000 trigger additional AML review and documentary verification; higher-value remittances require bank compliance committee sign-off, but no material delays occur for properly documented treasury operations. The KRW is a freely convertible currency in the global market, though offshore non-resident speculative positioning in the onshore FX market is tightly supervised. Overall, the system is operationally open, with the main friction arising from rigorous document authenticity checks rather than convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Korea within six months from the date of customs clearance (or service completion, as applicable) and must be credited to a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank account. The exporter must submit a settlement declaration to the receiving Foreign Exchange Bank within thirty days of receipt. There is no mandatory conversion into KRW; the foreign currency proceeds may be held in an eligible foreign currency deposit account. If the exporter has obtained local currency trade finance, repatriated proceeds must be converted to KRW to repay the trade loan. The Bank of Korea may grant extension for delayed repatriation upon written application with substantive justification (e.g., trade dispute, litigation).

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) is the central bank; exchange control powers are administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) with BOK acting as the operational agent under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise B2B, Airwallex) are not licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act as settlement institutions able to hold Korean Won balances or connect directly to Korea's interbank clearing system. Consequently, the Chusik Hoesa cannot rely on an EMI for its primary treasury operations, including KRW payroll, VAT settlement, and corporate tax payments. EMIs can serve only as supplementary inbound/outbound facilitation through hosted partner-bank rails, with the usual three-to-five-day correspondent delay. The viability for primary banking is Low, and for cross-border payout backup it is Medium, subject to a full KYC being performed on the Korean entity and authorised signatories.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial equity injection, the remitter must send an SWIFT MT103 using overseas direct investment purpose code (FDI) and must include the name of the Korean receiving bank, the Chusik Hoesa's investment account, and an explicit narrative referencing the MOEF FDI acknowledgement number. The Korean bank will verify the incoming credit, capture Foreign Exchange Transaction data, and issue a capital verification certificate (Customary Capital Verification) to the company within one business day. If the source country has a capital control regime, the remitter must provide local central bank clearance proof. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

South Korea national corporate income tax: 9% on tax base up to KRW 200 million; 19% over KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% over KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion. A local income tax surcharge equal to 10% of national CIT is added, making the aggregate top statutory rate 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national corporate income tax WHT plus 2% local income tax surcharge, totaling 22% aggregate withholding on gross dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders. Treaty rates may reduce this to 5% or 10% for beneficial owners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered when aggregate cross-border transactions with foreign related parties equal or exceed KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year. Country-by-Country reporting is required for multinational groups whose consolidated revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Although Korea has no separate jurisdictional ESR, the DTA relief and Foreign Investment Promotion Act incentives require the Chusik Hoesa to demonstrate real commercial substance: a physical office, employees, actual strategic decision-making, and core income-generating activities in Korea. A shell entity with only title to TikTok contracts and no local operational functions is disregarded, and its treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Foreign-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, willful evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to KRW 100 million. The tax authority also imposes a 40% dishonesty underreporting penalty under the Basic Act on National Taxes plus daily overdue interest on the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Where the court pierces the corporate veil, directors are jointly liable for the corporate tax deficiency and may face attachment of personal assets, civil restitution of diverted funds, a departure ban placed by the National Tax Service, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 66 of the Corporate Tax Act, the National Tax Service may re-compute the taxable income using an arm's length method and impose a transfer pricing adjustment penalty up to 10% of the understated tax base, plus document-related penalties and daily interest. Willful false documentation may trigger criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent TP documentation can be held personally liable for the re-assessed tax when the entity is found to be a sham. The NTS may block personal bank accounts and register departure restrictions, and the directors face corporate-veil-pierced civil restitution and incarceration for repeat offenses.
Treaty Abuse, Shell Company, and Withholding Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 and the Principal Purpose Test, treaty benefits are denied and the statutory 22% aggregate WHT will be applied retroactively. The Korean payer acting as withholding agent is liable for the unpaid tax, 10% additional WHT negligence penalty, and daily interest; criminal evasion may be charged if the scheme is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured the shell entity or authorised non-withholding are regarded as withholding agents and can be personally sued for collection. The NTS may freeze, blacklist, and impose travel bans; personal criminal liability includes imprisonment for aiding tax evasion, and civil asset restitution will be ordered against directors who moved the funds abroad.

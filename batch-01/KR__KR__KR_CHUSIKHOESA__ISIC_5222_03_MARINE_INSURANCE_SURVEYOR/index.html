Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea – online electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) – UNI-PASS electronic clearance system for export and customs formalities

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident contributor forming a Chusik Hoesa must ensure the Korean company obtains a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the single tax identity for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and electronic tax invoicing. The statutory procedure is: (1) complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and remit paid-in capital through a Korean bank; (2) within 20 days of commencing business, file the national business registration application with the competent NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business, or through HomeTax where a tax representative is appointed; (3) attach the certified articles of incorporation, apostilled/consularized foreign certificate of incorporation or corporate good-standing certificate, board resolution approving the Korean entity and tax representative appointment, power of attorney naming a Korean tax representative if the non-resident has no Korean permanent establishment, proof of registered office address, and identity documents of the statutory representative; (4) obtain the NTS-issued 10-digit BRN after validation. There is no separate VAT registration number: the BRN is concurrently the VAT Taxpayer Reference Number and the Corporate Taxpayer Reference Number. A foreign entity with no domestic place of business but with VAT obligations in Korea must appoint a tax representative and register the same BRN procedure for VAT purposes.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide a check-the-box, hybrid mismatch, or pass-through classification election for a Chusik Hoesa; the entity is per se taxable as a domestic corporation on worldwide income. A parent resident corporation holding at least 90% of a resident subsidiary may elect consolidated corporate income tax filing for qualifying wholly-owned domestic subsidiary groups, and a corporation may, with NTS approval, use a fiscal year other than the calendar year; these are filing and accounting-period elections, not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW turnover threshold for a Chusik Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory within 20 days of business commencement regardless of revenue level. The KRW 80,000,000 annual revenue threshold is relevant only for simplified taxable person status (small business VAT simplification), not as a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act and the Presidential Decree applies only to: (i) goods physically exported from the Korean customs territory, supported by an accepted KCS Customs Export Declaration; (ii) services supplied and consumed/used wholly outside Korea; and (iii) services specifically designated as foreign currency earning services (e.g., certain international trade and export-related services listed in the Presidential Decree) that are provided to a non-resident foreign insurer and paid in convertible foreign currency. For a marine insurance surveyor inspecting vessel hulls or cargo damage in Korea, 0% VAT cannot be assumed merely because the insurer is a foreign underwriter; the service must satisfy the statutory place-of-supply test or fall within a designated foreign-currency-earning category. Qualifying exported digital content or offshore services also require that the recipient is outside Korea and that the supply is not consumed in Korea. Payment must be received in foreign exchange and repatriated into Korea through a domestic foreign exchange bank.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) – Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export clearance record
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill for air cargo) evidencing physical departure of goods from the Korean customs territory to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (ETI) must be issued through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system for all VAT taxable supplies, including B2B supplies; transmission to the NTS must occur by the following business day after issuance. Commercial accounting systems connect through the NTS Open API. There is no pre-issuance real-time fiscal clearance or physical/crypto anti-fraud stamping requirement comparable to Latin American fiscal voucher regimes; instead, the NTS performs near-real-time validation of invoice metadata (BRN, supply value, VAT amount, line-item descriptions), cross-references ETI records against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations and foreign-exchange bank receipts, and applies anti-fraud risk scoring to flag abnormal or missing documentation. Export invoices must carry the 0% VAT category code and identify the supporting export document reference.

The NTS conducts post-export audits by matching electronic tax invoices against UNI-PASS customs declarations and FIRCs. If the 0% rate is unsupported, omitted, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the zero-rating is revoked and the taxpayer is assessed the unpaid 10% VAT plus refund clawbacks with interest. Administrative penalty taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes follow: 10% for underreported tax bases, 20% for false reporting, and 40% for deliberate concealment, along with delay penalties for overdue VAT. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act may be initiated for forged export documents, fabricated FIRCs, or intentional VAT refund fraud, and the taxpayer may lose eligibility for future zero-rated export treatment and be excluded from NTS compliance-good-benefit programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, South Korea maintains a semi-liberalised regime: current account payments are generally convertible and freely remittable when supported by an underlying trade or service contract. Cross-border service receipts from marine insurance surveying, cargo inspection, and claims adjudication are treated as current account earnings and may be settled through an authorized foreign exchange bank without prior approval, provided the bank receives the invoice, service confirmation, and related evidence for purpose code classification. Capital account transactions, however, require structured reporting and in some cases prior declaration or approval. Foreign direct investment into a Korean Chusik Hoesa must be registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with the invested capital recorded after remittance and verification by the receiving bank. Dividend repatriation is permitted for registered foreign-invested companies after local corporate tax settlement, audited financial statements, and bank confirmation of distributable reserves, with applicable withholding tax and any double tax treaty relief applied. The Korean won is not freely tradable offshore; onshore deliverable liquidity is restricted, and offshore non-deliverable won instruments require genuine underlying exposure. There is no general mandatory conversion of service export proceeds in Korea, but companies must collect export/survey earnings within the statutory settlement timeline and report all movements through the banking system. Profits can be remitted once the foreign investment entity registration is valid and the tax clearance obligations are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For marine insurance surveying services exported by a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the service provider must receive the foreign-currency settlement within six months from the date the survey report is issued. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in a foreign currency deposit account at an authorized foreign exchange bank. The bank must receive a copy of the survey contract, the invoice, the proof of performance, and the payment instruction. If payment is not received within the statutory six-month period, the exporter must submit an extension application to the foreign exchange bank explaining the commercial dispute or bank delay. Delayed receipts require a retroactive foreign exchange declaration and risk administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Administration; day-to-day compliance reporting is channelled through authorized foreign exchange banks and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Under Korean foreign exchange legislation, only an authorized foreign exchange bank may hold and settle corporate Korean won treasury operations for a domestic Chusik Hoesa. Wise and other unlicensed international EMIs are not authorized foreign exchange banks and are legally barred from serving as the primary clearing or operating account provider for a Korean resident corporate entity. The entity may use an EMI only as an ancillary outbound disbursement channel for very low-value payments to foreign beneficiaries outside Korea, and even then the Korean bank may require the underlying invoices to be disclosed. The statutory treasury blueprint must be anchored on a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank; the EMI rail cannot support capital verification, VAT refund processes, foreign exchange reporting, or dividend outbound remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be wired to a designated foreign exchange bank account in the name of the Korean Chusik Hoesa using SWIFT MT103 or MT202. The remitter must be exactly the foreign investor of record under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the payment narrative must contain the foreign investment report number, the investor identifier, and the purpose code for foreign direct investment. After receipt, the bank issues a certificate of foreign currency payment, the company completes capital verification with an external Korean CPA, and the registration of the foreign-invested company is updated with the MOEF and relevant tax authority. For operational service inflows, payments from marine underwriters should be denominated in USD or another convertible currency and coded as service export earnings, supported by the survey contract, invoice, and bank arrival notice. All transactions must be reported by the bank through the Foreign Exchange Information Network and must be traceable to the underlying commercial purpose.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade, surveyor fee, subcontractor, reinsurance premium, and claims-related disbursements is SWIFT MT103 through the Korean foreign exchange bank. The Korean bank will create a foreign exchange settlement report and assign a purpose code based on the invoice, the beneficiary country, and the nature of the payment. For high-value or documentary transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Standby Letter of Credit can be issued in favour of a foreign sub-surveyor or equipment supplier when the recipient requires banking payment confirmation. Documentary Collections may be used only where the transport and waiver documents are needed before payment release, which is rare in a marine survey fee context. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Korean Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, a Chusik Hoesa is subject to a compulsory independent audit by a certified CPA if any of the following applies: (a) it is a listed company (KOSPI, KOSDAQ or KONEX) irrespective of asset size; (b) it is an unlisted company whose total assets at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year are at least KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.7 million at the KRW/USD exchange rate used for the compliance cycle); (c) it is a supervised financial company, insurance company, securities company or other special-law company, regardless of asset size; or (d) it is licensed as an insurance claims adjuster or marine surveyor under the Korean Insurance Business Act and designated by supervisory regulations as subject to external audit. These tests are balance-sheet-based; the Korean External Audit Act does not use turnover or headcount as audit triggers for private unlisted corporations. A company below the threshold is not legally required to appoint an external CPA auditor, but such an audit may be voluntarily adopted by shareholder resolution or required under a foreign parent group governance policy.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax plus 10% local surtax (total 27.5% marginal rate; progressive rates 10-25%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% corporate income withholding + 2% local income surtax) on dividends paid to foreign corporations, unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty (commonly 5-15%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: aggregate cross-border related-party transaction value ≥ KRW 10,000,000,000; Master File: consolidated group revenue ≥ KRW 1,000,000,000,000 and Korean related-party transactions ≥ KRW 5,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not maintain a Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style ESR regime, the substance-over-form doctrine applies under the Corporate Tax Act and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA). A Korean marine insurer surveyor must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea if it contracts with global reinsurers; otherwise the NTS may recharacterize the entity as a shell and deny treaty benefits. Actual place of effective management (APEM) is crucial for residency. Maintain local contracts, personnel, decisions, bank accounts, and claim settlement functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion (fraudulent under-reporting)

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (조세범처벌법) Article 3 - imprisonment with labor up to 3 years, or fine up to KRW 30,000,000 (with aggravated fine up to 5 times total evaded tax if combined with false tax invoices); additional surcharge tax of 20-40% of omitted underpaid tax is assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the company's unpaid taxes where the veil is pierced due to fraud. They face asset seizure, overseas travel ban (출국금지), inclusion on the high-risk delinquent taxpayer list, disqualification from future board position, and criminal sentence of imprisonment up to 3 years in their personal capacity.
Transfer pricing filing violation and arm's length breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA) Article 11 and Enforcement Decree, non-submission of documentation may attract penalty up to KRW 50,000,000; the NTS may disregard the transaction and make upward adjustment to taxable income together with 10% additional charge on under-reported tax and daily delinquency surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing documentation face civil liability for restitution of the corporation's tax losses; personal liability may be imposed on the director who signed the disguised agreements. In aggravated cases, the NTS may report to the prosecution for obstruction of tax collection, leading to imprisonment up to 2 years, plus a travel ban and blacklisting of the corporate group.
Shell company / abusive treaty shopping with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: NTS may apply the beneficial ownership test and substance-over-form doctrine to deny treaty relief under Article 14-2 LCITA; the company is re-assessed at the 22% withholding rate as dividend/royalty distribution, plus a 20% unfaithful-declaration surcharge. If foreign account reporting is breached, Real Name Financial Transaction Act may impose fines up to 80% of the amount.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing exposes the directing minds (including foreign non-resident directors) to full personal liability for tax arrears and restitution; travel bans are imposed until taxes are settled; criminal charges of fraud under the Criminal Act (Article 347) or special tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment between 1 and 7 years.

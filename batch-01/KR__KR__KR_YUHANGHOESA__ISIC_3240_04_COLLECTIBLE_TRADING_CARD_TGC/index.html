Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law does not prescribe a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The total contributed capital set out in the Articles of Association must be fully subscribed and remitted, before incorporation registration, into a blocked temporary corporate nominee account opened in the company name at a Korean financial institution; the bank issues a certificate of deposit balance which must accompany the registration filing. Those funds are escrowed and cannot be withdrawn until issuance of the certificate of incorporation, after which the funds are released to the corporate operating account.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, online portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); customs duty and import VAT clearance are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS e-clearance portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) owned directly or indirectly by non-residents, the entity must first complete formation at the relevant Korean court registration office; it must then apply for mandatory business registration with the district tax office of the NTS. Submit the court-issued certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, certificate of initial paid-in capital if any, board resolution appointing the Korean domestic business operator or tax representative, power of attorney, proof of Korean place of business (lease agreement or branch office registration), and the representative's identification/passport. The application is made through HomeTax (or at the NTS tax office with jurisdiction over the Korean place of business) under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act. The NTS issues a 10-digit Registered Business Number which is concurrently the VAT identification number and corporate income tax reference. If the foreign entity has no Korean place of business and only supplies imported digital/electronic services, it must use HomeTax's simplified non-resident VAT registration and appoint a domestic tax manager; if a foreign entity has a Korean fixed place of business, it must also file an 'opt-in' or ordinary registration and obtain a 10-digit business number. After VAT registration, the entity must apply to the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS for an exporter/importer code by submitting its business registration certificate. No notarization apostille is required for domestic NTS filings if the documents are in Korean; foreign documents need apostilled Korean translation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; the Republic of Korea has no check-the-box election for classification of local entities. A Yuhan Hoesa is statutorily taxed as a separate resident corporate taxpayer and a separate VAT taxable person. The only domestic 'election' addressing group tax burden is the consolidated/group taxation regime under the Corporation Tax Act, available to resident companies that meet the 100% parent-subsidiary ownership and lock-in requirements; it does not override separate entity classification for VAT or foreign entity treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules are applied through domestic deduction-disallowance rules for payments characterized inconsistently by another jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold; Korea mandates VAT/business tax registration from the first taxable business activity. Newly formed local businesses, including a Yuhan Hoesa, must register regardless of turnover. A smaller taxpayer with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million may be classed as a simplified taxpayer for filing/simplified invoice purposes, but this does not operate as an exemption or registration threshold. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services (e.g., digitally delivered content) must complete simplified VAT registration through HomeTax from the first B2C supply to Korean non-registered consumers; imported goods become subject to VAT automatically at customs clearance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act applies to physically exported tangible goods, including collectible trading cards produced and sold to foreign customers, provided the goods are delivered outside Korea, move under a valid Korean customs export declaration, and are settled in foreign exchange in satisfaction of export-payment rules. Directly related services to exported goods (for example, custom security-holographic cold-foil design and printing work applied to goods that are physically exported) are also zero-rated if the service is supplied to a non-resident and the goods are exported. The seller must be registered with NTS and issue an electronic tax invoice marked as export zero-rated. If the supply takes place in Korea or the goods are ultimately used or consumed in Korea, the supply is not zero-rated and remains taxable at 10%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Customs Clearance Status) issued through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS channel
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill (for air freight) showing the Korean exporter as shipper
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the collectible trading cards and their export value
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation (bank-issued Foreign Exchange Purchase/Receipt Certificate) demonstrating collection of export sale proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice transmitted through HomeTax to the NTS
  • Sales contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Proof of the foreign buyer's non-resident status, such as its overseas tax registration or passport/trade license, when NTS requests additional evidence

Korea operates a mandatory e-invoicing platform called HomeTax e-invoice (electronic tax invoice). VAT-registered suppliers must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices to the NTS for all B2B supply of goods or services. The NTS provides an open API and certified ERP interfaces for real-time secure transmission. There is no separate pre-approval fiscal stamp or third-party fiscal clearance authority, but the NTS real-time validates invoice numbers and matches invoice line items against VAT returns and customs export declarations. For retail cash sales to individuals, cash receipt devices are required, and consumers can verify receipts on HomeTax; receipts are transmitted to the NTS by the approved cash receipt service provider.

The NTS automatically reconciles HomeTax e-invoice data, UNI-PASS export declarations, and Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmations. If the exporter fails to provide the required customs declaration, transport document, or evidence of foreign exchange settlement within 180 days (or within an officially granted extension), the NTS will assess VAT at 10% on the gross supply amount. Negligent failure or omission triggers additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act and daily statutory interest; a fraudulent export invoice triggers additional tax of up to 100% of the evaded tax under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and criminal liability under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes, including imprisonment and fines up to three times the evaded amount. Tax assessments for genuine omissions generally run for five years; deliberate fraudulent zero-rating claims extend the clawback window to ten years. Korea Customs Service may also impose customs penalties, interest, and seizure/confiscation for false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised yet notification-based foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions for trade in goods and services, including collectible trading card exports, licensing royalties, and e-commerce settlement inflows from Stripe or Authorize.Net, are fully convertible without prior central bank approval; however, every payment routed through a designated foreign exchange bank must be accompanied by a foreign exchange declaration (foreign exchange remittance justification form) evidencing the underlying commercial contract. Capital account transactions, including the non-resident equity injection into the Yuhan Hoesa LLC, require a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, after which the Bank of Korea automatically records the subscription and the commercial registry reflects the paid-in capital. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted after a statutory external audit; banks will require a dividend resolution certified by the board, the audited financial statements, and a National Tax Service tax clearance certificate. In practice, conversion of KRW into USD, EUR, or JPY on the Seoul Money Brokerage market is unrestricted within licensed banking channels, but any single settlement exceeding USD 500,000 requires a supporting transaction file. For a collectible trading card manufacturer, royalty and license outflows to non-resident IP holders are treated as current account service payments subject to withholding tax reconciliation, and must satisfy arm's-length pricing documentation under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs to prevent disguised capital flight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Republic of Korea has abolished mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Under Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Trading Guidelines, an exporter must settle export proceeds within 90 days from the date of the export declaration; settlement means the receipt, collection, and disposition of the foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank. The export receipt may remain in a foreign currency account without mandatory conversion to KRW. Extensions beyond the 90-day statutory window are available through the head of the relevant Korea Customs Service office for commercial disputes, inspections, or contractually deferred payment terms. Non-settlement without an approved exemption triggers administrative fines, automatic reporting to the Korea Customs Service and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU), and may result in revocation of the exporter's trade-related banking privileges.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, jointly administered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with operational foreign exchange reporting executed through designated foreign exchange banks in Korea.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Cross-border financial technology EMI servicing is Not Viable as a substitute for a domestic banking relationship. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the specific foreign exchange business guidelines issued by the FSS, non-bank electronic money institutions headquartered outside Korea (including Wise, Revolut, Neat, and similar EMIs) do not possess a license to open KRW-denominated domestic transaction accounts or provide Korean won clearing services to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC registered in Korea. Domestic digital banks (KakaoBank and Toss Bank) are subject to the same corporate onboarding and foreign exchange reporting obligations as commercial banks, but their foreign currency and cross-border trade finance product suites remain immature for corporate exporters. Stripe and Authorize.Net operate exclusively as card acquiring and payment gateway processors; they do not constitute banking rails and cannot replace the statutory foreign exchange declaration channel. The compliant architecture is to use a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank as the master settlement bank, with Stripe and Authorize.Net as incoming card acquiring rails that settle directly into the bank's foreign currency account under the merchant-of-record framework.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on the first KRW 200 million of taxable income; 19% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion; plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the corporate income tax liability, producing combined effective headline rates from 9.9% to 26.4%.

20% national corporate income withholding plus a 2% local income tax surtax (10% surtax on the 20% national withholding), producing an aggregate statutory Korean dividend withholding tax rate of 22%, reduced only under an applicable Double Tax Treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korea applies OECD-aligned documentation thresholds in KRW: CbC report is required when MNE group consolidated revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion; the Master File is generally required when the group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 100 billion; the Local File is required when annual cross-border related-party transactions reach the statutory KRW 50 billion threshold, with lower ministerial thresholds for certain intercompany services and intangibles. Even below these thresholds, contemporaneous arm's-length records must be maintained and submitted upon NTS request.

Economic substance scope: There is no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime in Korea, but the NTS applies the substance-over-form doctrine under National Tax Basic Act Article 14 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule. A Yuhan Hoesa must possess real personnel, premises, books and records, decision-making authority, and business risks in Korea; shell arrangements will be disregarded and transactions recharacterized where core income-generating activities are not performed in Korea, particularly when testing permanent establishment status and treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1) imposes imprisonment up to 2 years and/or a fine up to twice the evaded tax; National Tax Basic Act Article 47-3 adds a 40% corporate tax penalty for fraudulent understatement; the unpaid tax also accrues 0.022% daily.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil where the Yuhan Hoesa is a mere facade or is used to commit fraud; directors can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and third-party civil damages under Commercial Act Article 401, and can face NTS blacklisting, an overseas travel ban, asset attachment, and personal criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Articles 5 and 12 authorize the NTS to re-determine taxable income at the arm's-length price and deny deductions for missing documentation; National Tax Basic Act Article 47-3 can impose additional tax of 10% to 40% of the deficiency plus 0.022% daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certificates or deliberately conceal related-party transactions are liable for resulting corporate losses under Commercial Act Article 401; the NTS may blacklist the directors, issue an overseas travel ban, and pursue personal assets, while criminal tax evasion sanctions apply if the false documentation is used to hide Korean-source profit.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: National Tax Basic Act Article 14 (substance-over-form) and Article 15 (GAAR) allow the NTS to disregard the offshore conduit and reclassify the payment as dividend or royalty income taxable at the 22% aggregate withholding rate; Corporate Tax Act Article 98(1) imposes withholding liability on the Korean payer; false treaty claims trigger a 40% unfair underreporting penalty and 0.022% daily delinquency surcharge.

Director personal liability: If the Yuhan Hoesa is used as the alter ego of foreign principals to evade withholding tax, the corporate veil is pierced: directors and controlling shareholders become personally liable for unpaid withholding and penalties, personal assets can be attached, travel bans and blacklisting can be imposed, and criminal imprisonment for aiding and abetting tax evasion is available under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

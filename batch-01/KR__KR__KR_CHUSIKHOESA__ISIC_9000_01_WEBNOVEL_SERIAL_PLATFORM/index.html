Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Korean Commercial Act, a registered office must be a determinate location within the territorial jurisdiction of the relevant district court. A licensed virtual office or serviced-office address may be accepted at incorporation if a genuine service agreement, sublease, or landlord consent is produced. However, the National Tax Service requires an actual place of business for VAT and corporate tax registration, and major Korean banks generally reject a mailbox-only virtual office for operating-account opening because they require domicile proof, lease contracts, and often a physical visit. For this low-risk web novel subscription platform, a virtual office may satisfy the court registry but carries a high probability of being disputed as a disguised office by tax authorities and banks. A dedicated serviced suite with signage, meeting facilities, mailroom access, and a documented work location is strongly preferred; a purely virtual mailbox without local substance is not recommended.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; however, the full subscription price for all issued shares must be credited to a dedicated deposit account at a Korean commercial bank before registration, and the bank must issue a Certificate of Payment of Subscription Price to attach to the court filing. No blocked escrow account is legally mandated. Foreign investors must report inward capital remittance under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Court registration tax is ad valorem on stated capital and therefore cannot be stated as a fixed USD amount in the absence of a chosen capital figure.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic online portal: Hometax (www.hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal and associated electronic data interchange systems.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business, the statutory Korean Tax Identification Number and VAT registration procedure is as follows. First, determine whether the foreign entity maintains a Korean domestic place of business; if a domestic branch or establishment is present, the entity must file an ordinary business registration application with the competent District Tax Office through Hometax. The required evidence set includes: the completed Hometax business registration application form; certificate of incorporation or equivalent attestation of foreign legal existence; articles of incorporation and board resolutions; passport or alien registration card of the legal representative; proof of the Korean business address, lease title, or other evidence of a physical establishment; and, where a professional tax agent is engaged, a power of attorney with apostille or consular legalization. For an offshore foreign supplier of electronic services such as a WebNovel subscription platform, the entity must register under the simplified VAT registration regime for non-resident electronic service suppliers before commencing supply to Korean consumers; the simplified registration application is filed through Hometax through a designated Korean tax representative. After review by the competent District Tax Office, the NTS issues either a standard 10-digit Korean business registration number, which simultaneously functions as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number, or a simplified VAT registration number for an offshore non-resident supplier. The assigned number must be used on all e-invoices, VAT returns, foreign exchange settlement reports, and any applicable customs export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Korean corporate law does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for a Chusik Hoesa; the entity is irrevocably classified as a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level on worldwide income. The only relevant elective mechanism for group structures is the consolidated corporate taxation election available to a domestic holding company with wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries, which requires prior approval from the National Tax Service. There are no elective hybrid mismatch regimes; Korea applies mandatory anti-hybrid adjustment rules under its tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a domestically incorporated Chusik Hoesa, the threshold is KRW 0; registration is mandatory upon commencement of business and is not triggered by turnover. For a foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services, the threshold is also KRW 0; simplified VAT registration is required before the first supply of digital content to Korean consumers. A simplified VAT taxpayer status is available for very small individual and micro businesses with annual taxable turnover below approximately KRW 80,000,000, but it is not an exemption and generally does not apply to a stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, a 0% export VAT rate is available for exported goods and for services supplied to non-residents where the services are performed or consumed outside Korea and the consideration is received in foreign exchange. For pure digital WebNovel subscription services sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the Korean platform must demonstrate that the end subscriber is a non-resident individual or foreign business, that the digital content is accessed or downloaded outside Korea, and that the remittance received from the foreign app store operator or foreign payment processor is in foreign currency. If the same platform supplies digital subscriptions to Korean residents, those supplies are domestic taxable supplies subject to 10% VAT even if payment is routed through a foreign app store.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the Korean export declaration equivalent to an export bill of entry) approved through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system for exported physical goods, or a digital export evidence record where no physical movement occurs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical export movement out of Korea for goods; for pure digital services, the app store delivery and download report evidencing consumption outside Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Settlement Report issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing actual collection of foreign currency receipts
  • Commercial invoice, service agreement, and app store settlement statement identifying the non-resident recipient, the digital content description, the consideration, and the payment route
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the foreign subscriber or foreign app store operator, such as a foreign registration certificate, passport, business license, or independent IP address and device-location data
  • Apple IAP and Google Play Billing platform agreements, payout reports, and remittance statements showing the foreign currency proceeds repatriated to the Korean platform

The NTS operates a mandatory national e-invoice system via Hometax. Taxable persons must issue tax invoices in the prescribed statutory form; electronic tax invoices must be issued through NTS-certified channels, including the Hometax API or a certified e-invoice service provider, and must be transmitted to the NTS within one day after issuance. Korea does not require a continuous real-time fiscal stamp on every transaction, but the NTS operates anti-fraud validation of unique e-invoice serial numbers, duplicate detection, digital signature certification, and substantial penalties for non-issuance, delayed issuance, or issuance of fictitious or duplicate tax invoices. For B2C app-store sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, Apple and Google payment receipts are acceptable commercial documentation, while the Korean platform remains obligated to file periodic VAT returns and maintain full transaction-level evidence in Hometax records.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to NTS documentary scrutiny and cross-verification with Korea Customs Service and foreign exchange bank records. If the exporter cannot substantiate foreign exchange repatriation, non-resident consumption, or customs clearance, the claimed 0% rate is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply; the NTS will assess VAT at 10% on the gross proceeds, together with unfaithful declaration additional taxes, statutory interest, and possible refund clawback of any previously refunded VAT. Fabricated, backdated, or fraudulent export declarations, FIRCs, app store settlement reports, and bills of lading can trigger criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, including longer assessment windows, monetary fines, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but fully monitored convertible-currency regime. Current-account settlements, including web-novel subscription remittances, royalties, service fees and dividends, are generally free of quantitative limits, while capital-account movements such as foreign direct investment equity injections and intercompany loans are permitted through report-and-channel rules rather than blanket exchange controls. A Chusik Hoesa may therefore receive app-store payouts or export receipts in USD and KRW without central bank conversion approval; the BOK receives transaction-level data through the Foreign Exchange Information System. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, so the company may retain foreign currency balances in a licensed foreign currency account and convert at commercial need. Profit remittance is lawful after corporate tax and dividend withholding tax are paid, supported by audited financial statements, a shareholder dividend resolution and a bank reporting slip; no additional MOEF permit is required. The practical hurdles are documentary: correct BOK transaction codes, taxable proof on service payments, active foreign-investment registration for paid-in capital, and reconciliation between app-store platform reports and bank deposits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Digital-content export proceeds must be received through a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of export performance, with bank-approved extensions of up to 12 months for long-term subscription contracts. South Korea imposes no mandatory conversion ratio: the statutory surrender requirement is effectively 0%, and 100% of export foreign-currency receipts may be held in a registered Foreign Currency Account. Conversion into Korean won is optional and executed at market rates; every receipt must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Information System transaction code matching the App Store Connect and Google Play payout evidence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act control architecture

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally constrained. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed as Korean foreign exchange banks and cannot provide registered KRW accounts, issue capital verification evidence, or file Foreign Exchange Information System reports. Using a fintech EMI for FDI contribution or export-proceeds collection would fail the designated foreign exchange bank requirement in the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and create an AML red flag. The webnovel platform may use such EMIs only as a supplementary payment facilitation layer behind a licensed domestic bank, never as the treasury settlement rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.5% combined top effective rate (national corporate income tax rate of 25% plus 10% local income surtax on corporate income tax). Progressive national rates: 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 20% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 22% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 30 billion; 25% above KRW 30 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax), subject to reduction under an applicable double tax treaty.

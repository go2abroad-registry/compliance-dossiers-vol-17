Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Korean registry will accept a serviced office address for corporate registration, but a mail-only virtual office is not sufficient for full commercial substance in the tabletop gaming dice manufacturing industry. Korean tax authorities and commercial banks, and in practice Stripe, PayPal, and local acquiring banks, require a verifiable physical operating location or demonstrable production and fulfillment premises. A pure virtual office creates a material risk that the National Tax Service will reject business registration amendments, that a bank will refuse account opening under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, and that payment processors will hold funds pending address verification. The recommended structure is a physical workshop or fulfillment space registered as the business establishment, with a commercial correspondence service used in addition, not instead, of that physical place.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act or the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The subscribed contribution amounts are stated in the Articles of Incorporation and must be deposited into a Korean bank provisional deposit account before registration; the bank issues a capital contribution verification certificate presented to the court. The deposit is blocked only until the registration is completed, after which the funds become freely available for corporate use. Court registration tax and local education tax are assessed on the actual contributed capital, generally at 0.4 percent and 0.2 percent respectively, plus any minimum court stamp fees.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic tax portal; Korea Customs Service (KCS) handles export declaration processing through the UNI-PASS national customs portal.

E-government filing portal

Under the Korean VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes, a foreign non-resident entity carrying on any VAT-taxable business in Korea must file an application for Business Operator Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its domestic place of business, or with the Seoul Central District Tax Office if there is no fixed domestic place of business. The NTS then assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified TIN and VAT ID. The submission package for a foreign entity must include: (1) the prescribed business registration application submitted through HomeTax or in person; (2) a certified copy of the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or registration, plus articles of association or equivalent constitutive documents, notarized and apostilled where required; (3) a board resolution or power of attorney appointing a tax agent in Korea, and, where the entity has no domestic business office, an express designation of a domestic tax representative; (4) evidence of the domestic place of business such as a lease agreement or ownership deed when applicable; (5) for digital commerce, evidence of the foreign legal representative appointment and the consent for service of process under Korean tax law; and (6) supporting documents showing the actual commencement date of the business. Registration must be completed before starting the Korean taxable activity, and failure or delay in registration may expose the entity to unregistered-business additional tax penalties. The same 10-digit registration number is used for VAT and for KCS exporter registration, enabling the applicant to file export declarations in UNI-PASS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold: any entrepreneur supplying goods or services in Korea in the course of business must register for VAT from the first taxable supply. A simplified taxable person regime exists for small business operators with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but this is a special accounting and relief regime and does not exempt the entity from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under the VAT Act when goods are actually exported from Korea to a foreign country, evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service and by credible transport documents showing the goods left the Korean customs territory. Services, including offshore digital services, may also qualify for zero-rating where they are supplied to a non-resident or foreign corporation, are performed or consumed outside Korea, and the statutory supporting evidence is retained by the Korean exporter. For goods, the exporter must complete export within the legally permitted period and repatriate export proceeds in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For services and digital supplies, the taxpayer must retain the contract with the foreign customer, proof of the customer's non-resident status, evidence that the service is consumed outside Korea, and bank or remittance records proving settlement in foreign currency.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration certificate (Export Entry) issued through UNI-PASS
  • Original Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or equivalent transport document
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for exported goods
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-confirmed foreign currency receipt record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Signed contract or purchase order with the overseas customer
  • Proof of non-resident status and overseas consumption for zero-rated services or digital products

All VAT-registered businesses are obliged to issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax Electronic Tax Invoice System, in accordance with Article 32 of the VAT Act, and to transmit the issuance data to the NTS through the HomeTax API or an accredited special service provider. There is no physical fiscal stamp or real-time transaction-level clearance; instead, NTS conducts system-based validation, cross-matching of electronic tax invoice data against VAT returns, and anti-fraud reconciliation. Late submission or non-transmission of electronic tax invoice data triggers electronic tax invoice penalties, and issuance of false electronic tax invoices is subject to criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service coordinate to verify export declarations, transport documents, and foreign currency receipts. If an export zero-rating claim is not fully substantiated with the required documentation, the supply is reclassified as taxable and 10% VAT is assessed on the gross supply value, with additional tax imposed under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes for underreporting or non-reporting. Frail, false, or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective assessment over the statutory 10-year limitation period for tax evasion, interest, additional tax of up to 40% or more depending on the degree of fraud, and criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. In addition, false or forged export documents and electronic tax invoices trigger severe statutory penalties and can result in suspension of exporter privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but supervised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, including export of assembled RPG dice, import of raw materials, service fees, royalties, and ordinary trade payments, are free to execute through authorized foreign exchange banks and require only reporting or post-event verification. Capital account transactions remain subject to a notification, declaration, or approval regime managed by the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance; inbound foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, overseas borrowing, securities investment, and external guarantees must be filed before settlement. The Korean won is fully convertible for legitimate current account purposes onshore, but the authorities can impose temporary requirements, such as surrender or conversion restrictions, in a balance-of-payments or financial crisis. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa is permitted yearly or upon dissolution after audited financial statements are approved; the bank verifies corporate authorization, tax clearance, and the underlying foreign investment registration. A non-resident shareholder's dividend or liquidation proceeds may be converted and sent abroad through the domestic bank using purpose code for overseas remittance. There is no fixed percentage surrender requirement for export receipts, but the exporter must repatriate and report the full export value onshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, an exporter must receive export proceeds within six months from the date of export clearance unless an extension is granted by the competent authority. The statutory repatriation obligation is 100% of the invoiced export value; proceeds must be received through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio forcing all foreign currency receipts into Korean won; the exporter may hold the funds in a registered foreign currency account or sell them to the bank at the prevailing market rate. The bank reports receipt through the Korea Customs Service and the Bank of Korea's electronic foreign exchange reporting system, and any unrepatriated or overdue balance must be justified with supporting evidence or face administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with Ministry of Economy and Finance exercising Foreign Exchange Transactions Act policy oversight and Korea Customs Service handling trade-related reporting

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low; offshore fintech EMIs are not legally viable as primary treasury banks for a Korean entity. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal national corporate income tax rate (tax base over KRW 300 billion); lower bands: 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% for KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% for KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax, producing effective top combined rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on outbound dividends paid to foreign shareholders, plus 2% local income tax surtax, making the aggregate statutory rate 22%; treaty rate may reduce the aggregate rate if a complete DTA relief file is lodged.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: International transaction report required for aggregate foreign related-party transactions of KRW 500 million or more; Local File required when the Korean entity's foreign related-party transactions reach KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year; Master File and CbC reporting generally apply when consolidated group revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use a separately labeled Economic Substance Regulation in the UAE sense, but the substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial ownership gatekeeping, controlled foreign company rules, and tax treaty anti-abuse provisions require the Yuhan Hoesa to carry on real Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea. A foreign-owned LLC must show a Korean office, employees, inventory control, order processing, and management decisions in Korea; if the Korean entity merely routes dice royalties or pre-orders to a shell shareholder, tax authorities may disregard the entity under the piercing-of-veil doctrine and allocate income to the real owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Fraudulent Underreporting of Digital Sales

Statutory sanction: Article 47-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 76 of the Corporate Tax Act: 40% unfaithful-return penalty on the evaded tax plus overdue interest. Tax Evasion Punishment Act Article 3 adds criminal liability: imprisonment for up to five years or a fine of up to five times the tax evaded; both penalties can be imposed cumulatively.

Director personal liability: The Commercial Act Article 401 makes the responsible director personally liable for damages to the company and third parties caused by intentional or negligent breach of duty. The NTS may pierce the corporate veil, hold the director jointly liable, impose a departure-restriction/blacklist order, and the signing director faces criminal imprisonment if the fraudulent return was signed with knowledge.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding obligations arise under the Corporate Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes; failure to withhold makes the company and its responsible officers jointly liable for the uncollected tax plus penalties. Additional-penalty tax can reach 100% of the evaded withholding tax when the omission is intentional; the Tax Evasion Punishment Act criminal provisions additionally apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct the treasury to make untaxed outbound distributions become personally liable for the withheld-but-not-remitted tax; the NTS can seize personal bank accounts and real estate, issue a departure-restriction/blacklist order for unpaid withholding debts exceeding the statutory threshold, and recommend criminal prosecution for individual directors, with imprisonment exposure.
Transfer Pricing Sham and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Article 5 and Article 9 of the Framework Act on International Taxation permit the NTS to adjust Korean taxable income to the arm's length price and impose penalties for filing failures; a misreporting penalty of 20% to 40% under the Framework Act on National Taxes, fines for failure to submit TP documentation, and interest are all assessable. If the structure is a sham, substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules recharacterize the supposed non-resident owner.

Director personal liability: Directors face joint secondary liability if the corporation is unable to pay the adjusted tax because they intentionally stripped assets; the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil permits the NTS to attribute the unpaid tax to the controlling individual. The director can also be criminally prosecuted as a conspirator in tax evasion and blacklisted from foreign travel while tax delinquencies remain unpaid.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in share capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the full amount paid for issued shares must be deposited with a Korean bank before incorporation registration, and the bank must issue a balance certificate naming the corporation; such deposit must remain blocked until registration is completed. The fee estimate above assumes a nominal issued capital of KRW 100,000,000, yielding approximately USD 385 in consolidated registry tax, education surtax, and court stamp duty. No separate escrow-account rule is imposed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; primary electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) at https://www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

For a Chusik Hoesa incorporated in Korea, the company obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT Business Registration Number through the standard Korean business registration process. After court registration at the local Commercial Registration Office, the statutory representative files a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business; registration is submitted in person or electronically through the Home Tax Service. The district tax office issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the ten-digit Korean TIN, which simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean registered address that is nonetheless liable for Korean tax or VAT must appoint a tax agent with a domestic address and file the Korean non-resident business registration application with the NTS district tax office; the NTS then issues the same Business Registration Number where a permanent establishment or VAT collection obligation exists. Foreign electronic-commerce operators can use the simplified HTS foreign entity registration window to obtain a VAT registration number for services supplied to Korean customers when no domestic establishment is present.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a separate pass-through election for a Chusik Hoesa. A Korean stock corporation is mandatorily treated as a separate corporate income taxpaying entity under the Corporate Income Tax Act. The Korean hybrid mismatch rules operate by compulsory statutory adjustment rather than taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to a Chusik Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory from the commencement of business, regardless of turnover. A simplified VAT registration threshold of KRW 80 million annual gross revenue is limited to qualifying individual sole proprietors and is not available to an incorporated stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Korean Chusik Hoesa may claim the 0% VAT rate for physical goods that are delivered to a foreign destination as genuine exports under the Value-Added Tax Act, including recurring subscription boxes dispatched by international courier or freight forwarder. The claim requires that the goods leave the Korean customs territory and that payment is made or settled in foreign currency by the overseas buyer. For bundled digital content, advertising services, or domestically consumed services accompanying the subscription box, an appropriate apportionment is required and the zero-rating applies only to the exported tangible goods component, not to local services or rights that are used in Korea.

  • Electronic Export Declaration acceptance issued by Korea Customs Service through the UNIPASS customs clearance system
  • Commercial Invoice describing the subscription box contents, harmonized system codes, quantities, and export unit prices
  • Packing List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Subscriber order records and recurring billing schedule demonstrating the cross-border commercial transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Currency Receipt Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • For postal or small-parcel exports, the postal export customs declaration or international shipment tracking documentation accepted by Korea Customs Service

Tax invoices must be issued for all VAT taxable supplies. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS through the Home Tax Service certified electronic invoicing system or an NTS-certified API-connected ERP system. The NTS does not employ a pre-clearance fiscal stamp model, but taxpayers must transmit e-tax invoice data to the NTS by the end of the next business day after issuance to enable near-real-time cross-matching. The NTS also requires mandatory tax invoice key fields, digital signature or certification, and multiple issuances are monitored for abnormal invoice sequences under the integrated tax information system.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the customs declaration, transport evidence, or foreign-exchange receipt evidence is missing, false, or inconsistent, the claimed 0% rate is clawed back and the full 10% VAT is assessed on the export supply, together with statutory interest and overdue additional tax. Falsifying export documentation or claiming zero-rating on goods not actually exported may subject the entity to further additional taxes, civil penalties for fraudulent tax invoices, and criminal referral under the Frame Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory period may also cause the sale to be recharacterized as a domestic supply and expose the company to exchange-control penalties under Korean foreign exchange regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, royalties, and ordinary business remittances, subject to documentary verification by designated foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions are more tightly controlled. Foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa requires reporting under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the foreign exchange bank must verify the inward remittance against the investment report before issuing a capital verification certificate. Profit remittances such as dividends are permitted once corporate tax is settled and board shareholder resolutions are documented, but the remittance must carry proper purpose codes and be processed through a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no general surrender requirement forcing conversion of all export proceeds into KRW; exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts, but all receipts must be repatriated through the domestic banking system and reported. For a subscription box business, recurring inbound payment flows from overseas e-commerce gateways such as Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net create temporary suspense balances that must be reconciled and sequentially traced to the underlying sales invoices. The Bank of Korea maintains exchange rate stability through managed floating, and any capital outflow beyond prescribed thresholds requires a certificate of tax payment and remittance approval from the foreign exchange bank. Practical hurdles include strict anti-money-laundering (AML) profiling of foreign shareholders, physical presence expectations for signatories, and the need for clear source-of-funds dossiers for every capital injection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act rules, export proceeds for goods or services generally must be collected within six months from the date of export, unless an extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank due to justified commercial reasons. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; exporters are allowed to deposit foreign currency proceeds into a domestic foreign currency account, but the receipt must be reported and settled through a bank authorized to handle foreign exchange transactions. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers regulatory inquiry and possible administrative penalties, so a subscription box operator must reconcile gateway settlement batches and convert/repatriate proceeds in compliance with the six-month rule.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Domestic Korean financial regulators do not license foreign digital EMI platforms to hold KRW deposits or provide local clearing account IBANs. Offshore digital banks and EMI issuers cannot substitute for a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Payment gateways such as Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net can be used only for the collection of card payments from end customers, and the settlement proceeds must be routed into a Korean licensed bank account. Using an unlicensed fintech EMI for treasury operations would create licensing, AML, and foreign exchange reporting violations. A supervised partnership with a domestic Tier-1 bank is mandatory for capital verification, export receipt processing, and outbound supplier payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the overseas parent or investor to the Chusik Hoesa’s designated foreign exchange bank account. Each transfer must reference the Foreign Investment Report filed with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and use the appropriate purpose code for equity investment. The receiving bank will verify the beneficial owner, reconcile the remittance message to the share subscription resolution, issue a capital verification certificate, and report the transaction through Korea’s Foreign Exchange Information System. After capital verification, the funds are transferred from the temporary escrow account into the operating KRW account.

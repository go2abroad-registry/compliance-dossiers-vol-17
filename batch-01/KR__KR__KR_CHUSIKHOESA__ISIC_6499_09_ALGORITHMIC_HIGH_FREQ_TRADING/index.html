Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act for ordinary stock corporations: no statutory minimum paid-up capital is required; however, the total issue price of shares subscribed at incorporation must be paid in full into the promoter's deposit account at a bank before the incorporation registration; the depository bank must issue a share capital payment certificate and the funds remain blocked (escrowed) until the company is registered; for a Proprietary Algorithmic HFT entity, the Financial Supervisory Service may require initial financial soundness capital under the FSCMA depending on the precise licensing category; if seeking registration as a financial investment business entity, minimum capital requirements under Article 12 of the FSCMA and subordinate regulations may apply, with a minimum paid-in capital of not less than KRW 500 million for certain investment advisory or dealing licenses, but for an unlicensed proprietary trader the general zero-minimum rule applies.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered through the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); export declarations are administered by the Korea Customs Service via the UNI-PASS single-window system.

E-government filing portal

For a Chusik Hoesa incorporated in South Korea, the company must first complete incorporation registration with the court registry; within 20 days after commencing business, the representative must file a Business Registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, providing the Certificate of Incorporation, articles of incorporation, corporate seal certificate, address evidence, and directors' identification. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that is simultaneously used as the VAT registration number and employer withholding number. The BRN is activated on the HomeTax system and linked to UNI-PASS customs access. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean place of business should register through its domestic fiscal representative for VAT purposes only when it makes taxable supplies in Korea, using the simplified BRN procedure; ultimate legal liability remains with the foreign entity and each registered place of business requires its own BRN.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for a Chusik Hoesa; all business operators, including local branches of foreign corporations, must obtain a Business Registration Number and VAT registration. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Value-Added Tax Act applies to goods physically exported from Korea and to specific services supplied to non-residents in a foreign currency where the statutory export-service conditions are met, including verification that the service is performed or consumed outside Korea and is paid in foreign currency. Proprietary ultra-low-latency algorithmic market making is a VAT-exempt financial service rather than a zero-rated export; the entity may not claim 0% VAT on own-account trading profit. A 0% rate may only attach to separately invoiced eligible exported goods or qualifying overseas services, not to the principal trading business.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration / Export Declaration Certificate obtained through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or remittance receipt issued by a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Service-export substantiation file (where applicable): signed offshore service contract, invoice and foreign-exchange receipt

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the HomeTax system. E-tax invoices must be issued and the data file transmitted to the NTS no later than one day after issuance, using delegated accounting software or direct EDI/API integration; paper invoices are exceptional. There is no manual fiscal stamp, but NTS performs near-real-time cross-matching of e-invoices, cash receipts, and sales data, and imposes penalty additions for issuance or transmission errors.

On audit, NTS will disallow zero-rating if the statutory documentation bundle is incomplete, the foreign-exchange proceeds are not received within the 180-day deadline, or the service is not genuinely supplied and consumed outside Korea. The consequence is retrospective reassessment at 10% VAT, plus statutory additional taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, including interest and penalties for unreported or false tax invoices, and possible criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for willful export zero-rating fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a dual-system exchange control framework. Current-account transactions are generally liberalized and do not require individual license, but capital-account transactions, including equity, debt financing and financial derivative trades, require prior or simultaneous reporting to a designated foreign exchange bank. The KRW is not a freely deliverable offshore currency; settlement of securities and HFT treasury flows must be executed through onshore banks and the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute. Profit remittance for dividends is subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, which requires remittance evidence of audited financial statements, undistributed earnings calculations, board minutes, and withholding tax receipts. In addition, non-resident shareholders must register their acquired equity directly on the foreign exchange bank's ledger after capital verification. There is no blanket prohibition on capital conversion, but foreign exchange banks must verify the underlying transaction and may suspend the remittance if the stated purpose code is inconsistent with payment to a counterparty in the algorithmic trading chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be settled through a designated foreign exchange bank. The statutory receipt period for most ordinary trade transactions is 1 year from the date of export declaration; extensions up to 24 months may be granted by the bank based on commercial payment terms and related customs agency approval. The mandatory conversion ratio is currently 0% for funds credited directly to a foreign currency account maintained with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank; however, if the exporter does not maintain such a foreign currency account and requests KRW settlement, the receiving bank will convert 100% of the credited receipt at the prevailing buying rate. Proprietary algorithmic trading receipts are not export goods proceeds and therefore do not qualify for special export deferral unless classified within a licensed trade-in-services or financial investment settlement code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Financial Services Commission (FSC) as co-regulators under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; operational examination is delegated to the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Korean law does not recognize fintech EMI as licensed deposit-taking or foreign exchange institutions. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act permits payment gateway companies to process certain payments, but they cannot clear cross-border corporate treasury flows, hold corporate currency accounts, or carry out foreign exchange intermediary functions. Any offshore EMI must rely on a fully licensed Korean commercial bank as the settlement sponsor and sub-account holder; standalone EMI onboarding for a Chusik Hoesa is legally barred.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.2% combined top marginal corporate income tax rate (22% national corporate income tax under Corporate Tax Act Article 55(1) where taxable income exceeds KRW 400 million, plus 10% local income tax surcharge under Local Tax Act Article 103(1)(1))

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined outbound withholding tax for dividends paid by a Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder without treaty relief (20% national withholding under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 and Income Tax Act Article 129, plus 2% local income tax surcharge under Local Tax Act Article 103-2)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korean Master File and Local File documentation is mandatory when annual revenue is at least KRW 100 billion and aggregate cross-border related-party transactions reach KRW 50 billion; Country-by-Country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above KRW 1.5 trillion. Even below these thresholds, the NTS may demand a comparable analysis under Article 12 of the Framework Law on International Tax Coordination; related-party transaction amounts aggregate sales, purchases, loans, guarantees, royalties, interest, service fees, and other cross-border dealings with foreign related parties

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain zero-tax offshore centers, but the NTS applies the general anti-abuse rule, substance-over-form jurisprudence, and Article 13 of the Framework Law on International Tax Coordination to disregard arrangements lacking genuine commercial purpose. A Chusik Hoesa performing algorithmic high-frequency trading as principal must demonstrate that board control, risk management, algorithmic code maintenance, co-location equipment, trading-floor personnel, execution systems, and revenue booking are located in Korea. When a foreign jurisdiction applies its own ESR or a treaty partner asserts CIGA analysis, the Korean entity must prove that its core income-generating activities are performed in Korea; failure to maintain economic substance may cause the denial of foreign tax credits, recharacterization of profits, transfer pricing reset, or deemed distribution to non-resident shareholders

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Non-Identification of Algorithmic Trading Profits and Off-Book Trade Settlement

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Taxpayers Act Article 3(1) and Article 3-2: imprisonment not exceeding 2 years, or a fine not exceeding 3 times the evaded tax; where the fraudulent evasion in a taxable year exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax may be imposed. The NTS also adds a 40% dishonest-return penalty under Basic National Tax Act Article 47-3(2), plus the full underpaid tax, daily delinquency surcharges, and collection costs

Director personal liability: The representative director may be personally liable by piercing the corporate veil where the corporation was used as an instrumentality; Article 4 of the Punishment of Taxpayers Act imposes joint personal liability on directors who willfully or negligently participated in concealing trading profits. Civil liability includes restitution of evaded tax and penalties, seizure of personal assets, departure restrictions under the immigration blacklisting procedure, and criminal imprisonment. The court may also order forfeiture of personal brokerage accounts and disqualification from serving as director in any Korean corporation
Artificial Profit Repatriation and Treaty Shopping via Excessive Royalty or Interest Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Article 13 of the Framework Law on International Tax Coordination, the NTS disregards the intermediary and recharacterizes the disguised payment as a dividend, imposing 22% withholding tax plus deficiency penalties and a 40% fraud penalty in intentional cases; transfer pricing adjustment also imposes a 10-30% penalty on the corrected income. The withholding agent who fails to withhold is personally liable for the unwithheld tax under Corporate Tax Act Article 98(8) and Basic National Tax Act Article 21(2)

Director personal liability: The director who signs the false treaty application, beneficial ownership certificate, or remittance instruction is jointly and severally liable for withholding tax. The NTS may blacklist the director as a high-value delinquent taxpayer when unpaid national tax exceeds KRW 50 million, impose an exit ban, revoke passports, and initiate criminal proceedings under Article 30 of the Punishment of Taxpayers Act for false attestation. Civil asset restitution and disqualification orders may extend to the foreign beneficial owner where the director acted as nominee
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure with Unregistered HFT Activity under Capital Markets and AML Rules

Statutory sanction: The Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million for unlicensed financial investment services; AML violations may result in business registration cancellation and a block on future financial investment business approval. The NTS may disregard the shell's separate existence, assess worldwide profits to Korea under substance-over-form, deny tax treaty benefits, and impose penalties for unregistered foreign exchange transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where capital is nominal, assets are commingled, corporate formalities are ignored, and the entity operates solely as an instrumentality. Directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, financial regulatory fines, civil tort damages under Civil Act Article 750, and AML penalties. Directors face travel bans, listing on the NTS delinquent taxpayer disclosure, passport revocation, and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years for unlicensed financial investment business activity; if the shell vehicle facilitated tax fraud or market manipulation, extradition may be sought for cross-border economic crimes

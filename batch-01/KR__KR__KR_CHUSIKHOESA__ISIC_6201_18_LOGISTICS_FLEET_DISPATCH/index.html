Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding addresses are generally permissible to physically register a Korean Chusik Hoesa only if they are provided by a licensed corporate service provider with a real lease and a designated office room or dedicated cubicle that the company can access on short notice, but the National Tax Service and the local tax office (세무서) increasingly reject business addresses that are merely commercial mailboxes or that are shared by more than a threshold number of registered companies without separate signage or a dedicated workspace. Quantified risk: if the registered address is a virtual office without an actual employee or desk, the probability of the relevant local tax office requesting documentary evidence, a phone inspection, or excise visit is approximately 50% to 70% within the first fiscal year.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) for border/customs export declaration administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that, like the Chusik Hoesa, establishes a domestic corporation or a domestic place of business in the Republic of Korea must first complete Commercial Act incorporation or foreign branch registration, then file a corporate business registration application (Business Registration Certificate application) with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office or domestic place of business. The application should be submitted through the South Korean company registration system or HomeTax within the statutory period, generally within two months of incorporation or commencement of business. Upon acceptance, the NTS issues the 10-digit Korean Tax Number and Corporate Business Registration Number, which jointly serve as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number. If the foreign entity has no domestic place of business but is required to file Korean VAT on electronic services supplied to Korean consumers, it must register as a foreign electronic service supplier directly through HomeTax and receive a VAT registration number before commencing taxable supplies. A foreign corporation without a domestic establishment that only earns Korean-source investment income is instead registered in the NTS foreign non-resident taxpayer register rather than under the business-registration regime, and corporate tax is collected through withholding where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Chusik Hoesa is treated as a Korean domestic corporation, separately taxable on worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act; it is not pass-through, and there is no general entity-classification election for U.S.-style check-the-box treatment).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 registration threshold for legal entities: a Chusik Hoesa or any corporation supplying goods or services in Korea must register as a general VAT taxpayer before commencement regardless of turnover. For individuals only, simplified taxable person status may apply when the prior-year taxable turnover is KRW 30 million or less; this simplified regime is unavailable to corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act, zero-rating applies to exported goods, services supplied outside Korea, and certain services provided to non-residents in foreign currency. For the fleet-telematics SaaS and ISIC 6201 activity, the zero rate may be claimed where the place of supply is outside Korea, meaning the foreign customer receives or uses the software and real-time telematics services outside Korea, and the supply is made to a non-resident counterparty. If the supply is instead treated as supplied to a Korean customer or consumed by a fixed establishment in Korea, standard 10% VAT applies. The supporting position must be evidenced by commercial documentation, technology or service-usage records, and foreign-exchange receipts.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration (Export Declaration Certificate or electronically transmitted export declaration) for goods; for services, a substitute document package is required
  • Commercial invoice and executed service agreement stating the non-resident customer, the nature of the SaaS and telematics service, and the place of use outside Korea
  • Proof of export or delivery: Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods; for services, server or service-use logs, or a signed statement from the foreign customer confirming use outside Korea
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: a Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or certified Foreign Remittance Receipt from a Korean designated foreign exchange bank showing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • HomeTax export-settlement reporting input matching the invoice amount, customer name and date of payment

All registered VAT businesses must issue tax invoices for taxable supplies to other VAT-registered businesses. Under the mandatory e-tax invoice regime, tax invoices must be transmitted electronically to the NTS through the HomeTax portal or an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice service provider. The NTS clears each e-tax invoice into its central invoice database and performs cross-matching among issuers, recipients, cash-receipt data and periodic VAT returns. There is no physical anti-fraud stamp; integrity is enforced through mandatory transmission of encrypted electronic invoice XML, sequential invoice-number controls, and NTS transaction monitoring for mismatches and false invoices. Cash sales to individuals must also be reported through the HomeTax cash-receipt system.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service scrutinize zero-rated export claims by matching VAT return e-invoice data, customs export declarations, bank non-resident remittance records and service-consumption evidence. If zero-rating is denied because the documentation is incomplete, the supplies are not actually exported, or the service is found to be consumed in Korea, the NTS will assess unpaid VAT at 10% retrospectively, impose an unfaithful-return additional tax of up to 40% of the underpaid VAT, and charge statutory late-payment interest from the original filing due date. False or fraudulent export evidence can lead to tax evasion investigation under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, clawback over the 10-year fraudulent-assessment limitation period, and criminal liability. Immediate reversal of any input-VAT refund previously claimed on the export cost base will also be required.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly open but regulated foreign exchange system. The Korean won is freely convertible on the current account for genuine trade, service, royalty, and dividend flows, provided the underlying transaction is declared through a reporting institution (a licensed foreign exchange bank). Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and equity subscriptions, are liberalised but require formal filings and may be subject to ad hoc restrictions when market volatility creates national emergency. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in logistics SaaS, the practical constraint is the need to prove the 'real business purpose' of every substantial cross-border flow. Dividends and profit remittances to foreign shareholders require cumulative investment registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a certified dividend resolution, and proof of non-resident tax withholding paid to the National Tax Service. The bank will also review that the original capital entered via an inbound foreign direct investment route under a proper purpose code; if the share capital was funnelled through an offshore account or a third-country intermediary without sequential payment, the withdrawal path will be blocked. Periodic BOK statistics reporting applies to foreign exchange positions above certain thresholds, and Korean banks enforce strict anti-fragmentation rules to detect round-tripping. Overall, current account convertibility is unrestricted, while capital account freedoms demand documentary diligence rather than monetary restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the FETA and the enforcement decrees on exports, export proceeds from software, telematics, and fleet-dispatch services must be received within twelve months from the export declaration or invoice date; the period is extendable by up to eighteen months with prior Bank of Korea approval or where a recognised foreign exchange bank has been notified. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won for private commercial exporters; the Chusik Hoesa may retain 100% of export receipts in Korean won onshore foreign-currency current accounts. However, every collection must be remitted into a domestic foreign-exchange bank account and reported in the Electronic Export Ledger; any deviation is treated as an unapproved disposal and can trigger cancelation of trade entitlements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, acting as executor and supervisory authority for the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and Foreign Exchange Business Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for any non-Korean fintech EMI or offshore digital e-money institution. The Chusik Hoesa cannot legally maintain treasury or settlement accounts with EMI licensees that are not recognized foreign exchange banks under the FETA. Offshore EMIs are barred from opening multi-currency corporate accounts in South Korea, and they are not permitted to execute foreign exchange payment orders on behalf of a Korean resident entity. Even domestic-tier online banking platforms such as Toss or Kakaobank cannot serve as the primary FX clearing bank because they lack the full delegated authority to assign BOK foreign exchange purpose codes and to issue foreign-investment certificates. Consequently, the treasury blueprint must centralise all cross-border liquidity through the tier-1 banks; EMIs can only be used as invoicing or API middleware, never as the regulated clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscription must be routed from the ultimate shareholder's bank account directly to the foreign exchange bank that will issue the Foreign Investment Certificate, with a SWIFT MT103/200 message including the phrase 'FIPA - Foreign Direct Investment' and the appropriate BOK purpose code for capital investment (e.g. article 18, type 101). The routing cannot pass through a personal account or a foreign EMI. The bank verifies the currency transfer against the incorporation documents, then files the foreign direct investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the tax office. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100 billion annual revenue triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; KRW 50 billion in cross-border intercompany transactions triggers annual information return disclosure.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain separate ESR for domestic law, but substance over form, beneficial ownership, and OECD Pillar Two anti-avoidance rules apply. A Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain substantive operations: office, employees, contracts, decision-making, and actual B2B SaaS dispatch activity in Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Group and Fraudulent Filing)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 10 times the evaded tax; administrative 40% penalty; public disclosure as a serial tax evader

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind corporate veil: civil joint and several liability for corporate tax, criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and personal asset seizure
Abusive Tax Avoidance / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief; 40% penalty; recharacterization under substance-over-form; retroactive assessment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors who approved transaction structure subject to personal liability for penalties and collusion-based criminal sanction
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to KRW 10 million per missing document; adjustment of taxable income with 10–30% additional tax; possible criminal referral if intentional misstatement

Director personal liability: Responsible directors secondarily liable under Corporate Income Tax Act; penalties may be collected personally
Shell Company / Substance Failure (DTA Benefit Claim)

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefit; full 22% withholding; penalties 40% of unpaid tax; cancellation of tax benefits retroactively

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid withholding and subject to criminal penalty if found to have used shell to evade tax
AML / Foreign Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to KRW 200 million for unreported FX transactions; AML penalties under Financial Transactions Reports Act — imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to KRW 50 million, or both

Director personal liability: Officers criminally liable for AML violations; travel bans and extradition; personal restitution of foreign assets

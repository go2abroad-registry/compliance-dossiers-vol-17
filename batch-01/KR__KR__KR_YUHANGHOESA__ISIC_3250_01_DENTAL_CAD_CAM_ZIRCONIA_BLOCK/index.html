Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The capital amount is set freely in the Articles of Incorporation and must be fully paid by the members before the incorporation registration is filed. There is no legal requirement for a blocked escrow account; however, the capital contribution must be evidenced by a formal capital deposit certificate issued by a Korean bank, and the deposited funds must not be withdrawn for operations until the company is registered and the Board of Directors has passed a disbursement resolution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing and registration portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) via hometax.go.kr. Export customs control and clearance portal: Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm that the Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation for Korean tax purposes because its place of effective management and/or registered office is in Korea. Non-resident ownership is permitted and there is no statutory minimum capital requirement. Step 2: Appoint a Korean tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes if the foreign parent is not physically present in Korea, and register a fixed place of business address with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean seat. Step 3: Prepare the statutory corporate package: certificate of Korean corporate registration issued under the Commercial Act, articles of incorporation, board resolution establishing Korean activities, opening financial statement, proof of Korean place of business, and power of attorney for the tax representative. Step 4: File the Business Registration Application with the NTS district tax office through the Home Tax Service portal or in person, including foreign shareholder details, director details, and tax representative designation. The registration is due within 20 days from business commencement. Step 5: The NTS assigns the ten-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the corporate tax identification number, VAT identification number, and electronic tax invoice key. The Business Registration Certificate must be retained and kept current in the HTS profile. Step 6: For export operations, separately obtain the Korea Customs Service exporter registration and customs identifier via UNI-PASS, and designate a Korean foreign-exchange bank account for export settlement proceeds under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not apply a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Once created as a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act, the entity is classified as a domestic corporation and taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Corporate Tax Act and the OECD Inclusive Framework apply to deny deductions for hybrid mismatch outcomes where the counterparty treats a payment as deductible in another jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no economic turnover exemption for corporations; the Yuhan Hoesa must register for VAT from the commencement of Korean business activity. An eligible individual operator may qualify as a simplified taxable person only for annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but a corporation is always a general taxable person and must file full VAT returns and e-Tax Invoices regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Act, a 0% rate applies to exported goods if the goods are sold to a non-resident purchaser, physically transported out of Korea, and covered by an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. For the dental CAD/CAM zirconia block business, each shipment must have a valid export declaration, shipment evidence, and proof that export proceeds are received through a Korean foreign-exchange bank in an approved settlement currency. The zero-rated e-Tax Invoice must be issued with the NTS export reason code and linked to the customs export declaration number. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only if the place of supply is outside Korea, the recipient is located abroad, the service is effectively used outside Korea, and equivalent foreign-exchange receipt evidence is retained.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration filed through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice linked to the export declaration number

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses under the NTS Home Tax Service. The seller must record every taxable zero-rated or standard-rated supply on an e-Tax Invoice and transmit the invoice data to the NTS portal no later than the day after issuance. The NTS assigns an electronic tax invoice management number and validates the counterparty business registration number; no physical tax stamp is required. For exports, the export declaration identifier and e-Tax Invoice are electronically matched through UNI-PASS and HTS, and VAT refunds are cleared through the NTS e-Tax Invoice data warehouse. Certified EDI and API-integrated solutions may submit e-Tax Invoice data to HTS machine-to-machine for near-real-time acceptance.

Korea Customs Service export declarations are integrated with NTS VAT data. If no matching export declaration, Bill of Lading, or foreign-exchange receipt is found within the required period, the zero-rating is overturned, the 10% VAT is assessed as domestic output tax, and any input VAT refunded or offset is clawed back with statutory interest. The NTS may also impose surcharges for false or unsubstantiated zero-rated e-Tax Invoices, including an additional tax of 2% of the falsely issued invoice amount and an unlawful-refund surcharge of up to 40% of the fraudulent refund amount under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent export claims may be referred to the public prosecutor under the Act on Punishment of Tax Evaders, with imprisonment and fines proportionate to the evaded tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but regulated current and capital account regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for settled current-account transactions such as trade payments, service fees, dividends and royalties, provided the underlying transaction is reported through a Domestic Foreign Exchange Bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment inward remittances, outward loans and equity purchases, do not require separate central bank approval but are subject to prior or timely reporting to the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information System. There are no general capital controls blocking repatriation, but profit remittances require proof of paid-in capital, tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a withholding tax certificate. A Yuhan Hoesa financed by non-resident shareholders may be deemed a foreign-invested enterprise if capital is contributed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, in which case FDI reporting, capital verification, and registration with a designated Foreign Exchange Bank are prerequisites before dividends or foreign-currency loans can be serviced. Export proceeds are not compulsorily surrendered; exporters may hold foreign currency in authorized accounts but must collect and report the proceeds within the statutory deadline. Overall, convertibility is high for operational flows and FDI, while discretionary foreign exchange risk positions are restricted under actual-demand hedging rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and associated Exchange Regulations, export proceeds must be received into a foreign currency account at a designated Foreign Exchange Bank no later than 18 months from the date of the export declaration. If the deadline cannot be met, the exporter must apply for an extension through the Foreign Exchange Bank or the MOEF. There is no mandatory forced conversion ratio: 0% of export receipts must be converted for the Korean won. The exporter may retain foreign currency in authorised Foreign Exchange Bank accounts or convert to KRW at market rates. All conversions are recorded and reported by the bank to the Foreign Exchange Information System, and the exporter must retain the export declaration, packing list, invoice and beneficiary evidence to support each credit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; Korea Financial Intelligence Unit supervises AML/KYC compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Jurisdictional viability is Low. An offshore digital EMI, unless separately licensed under Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act and registered as a Foreign Exchange Bank or a designated paying agent, cannot provide a legally valid KRW settlement rail for this Yuhan Hoesa. It cannot report FDI inward remittances or export proceeds, cannot issue domestic Korean account numbers for customs and export proceeds, and cannot complete MFDS-linked dental trade settlement. It may be used only for ancillary customer collections if a local e-money license is obtained, but it must not be used for capital injection, trade settlement, or profit repatriation. The treasury backbone must remain a Domestic Tier-1 Commercial Bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion, exclusive of the 10% local income tax surcharge.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on gross outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. A Double Tax Treaty may reduce this rate, commonly to 5% for holdings of at least 10% voting rights or 15% for portfolio dividends, provided beneficial ownership is established.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the end of the fiscal year; for a December 31 year-end, the deadline is March 31 of the following year. Non-calendar fiscal year-ends use the same three-month rule. Interim estimated corporate tax returns are due within one month after the first half of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50,000,000,000 (KRW 50 billion) in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions during the fiscal year triggers mandatory preparation and submission of a Local File and Master File to the National Tax Service. A simplified report is required for entities with related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion but below the KRW 50 billion threshold.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a codified Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to UAE-style ESR, but the National Tax Service applies substance-over-form, OECD BEPS Action 5 CIGA principles, and anti-abuse rules under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs. Any intercompany entity or IP holding must have actual employees, functions, and office premises commensurate with the income claimed; otherwise the benefit or treaty rate may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA), the National Tax Service may substitute the arm's length price and adjust taxable income, imposing a 10% negligence penalty on the understatement (40% for fraudulent understatement) plus late-payment interest of 0.022% per day. Failure to submit documentation can trigger a correction to the highest comparable result and an administrative fine of up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may face corporate-veil piercing under substance-over-form doctrine and Civil Code tort liability. Each director who knowingly approved the manipulated transfer prices can be jointly and severally liable for the recovered tax and penalties. The National Tax Service may impose travel bans (restricted taxpayer list) and refer the case for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to three years and fines up to twice the evaded tax.
Unlawful Outbound Dividend and Royalty Withholding Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 98(1) and (3) of the Corporate Tax Act triggers a recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the Korean payer plus a 10-30% unfaithful-reporting penalty and daily late interest. The National Tax Service also imposes a 40% fraud penalty if the non-payment is intentional. Criminal prosecution can follow under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: The directors who declared the dividend and authorized the royalty payment without withholding are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. They can be blacklisted as delinquent taxpayers, subjected to asset seizure, and criminally prosecuted. Conviction for intentional evasion can result in up to three years imprisonment, a fine up to twice the evaded amount, and disqualification from professional or corporate roles.
Tax Evasion through Fictitious Expenses and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, fraudulent evasion exposes the entity to a penalty of 40% of the understated corporate tax plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.022% per day; the statute of limitations extends to ten years for dishonest evasion. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to two years or a fine of up to KRW 10 million, or both, for each act of evasion.

Director personal liability: Because the directors actively participated in the false invoice scheme, the corporate veil is pierceable under the Commercial Act and tax law principles. Directors can be held personally liable for the entirety of evaded tax and penalties, suffer civil asset restitution to the Republic of Korea, be placed on the National Tax Service travel-ban blacklist, and be sentenced to imprisonment for up to two years with criminal fines reaching 200% of the evaded amount.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The articles of incorporation must state the number and par value of shares, and all issued shares must be fully subscribed. Before registration, the total subscription money must be deposited into a blocked incorporation deposit account maintained at a domestic financial institution; such funds may not be withdrawn until the Commercial Registry completes the incorporation registration. After issuance of the certificate of incorporation, the blocked capital is released to the company's operating account. Non-resident investment is permitted and is separately reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, but no minimum block amount is imposed.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - the company has no physical premises requiring civil defense fire inspection or structural load certification. As a consultancy with an office, fire-safety compliance is limited to the building's existing consolidated fire safety certificate issued under the Act on Fire Prevention and Safety of Buildings, and the employer's general duties under the Occupational Safety and Health Act to maintain a safe workplace. If the company later operates a warehouse or assembly site, it must obtain the appropriate National Fire Agency permit and workplace approval from the Ministry of Employment and Labor before commencing those operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr). Export declarations are separately processed through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the entity is a domestic corporation (Chusik Hoesa incorporated under the Korean Commercial Act) or a foreign non-resident entity. Step 2: For a Korean domestic corporation, the representative must file an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business prior to commencing business. The application can be submitted through the HomeTax portal (hometax.go.kr) or in person at the competent district tax office. Attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, resolution appointing directors, proof of place of business (lease contract or ownership certificate), and, where there are non-resident shareholders, a tax residence certificate and Korean-language translations of relevant corporate documents. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident entity, the NTS assigns a Business Registration Number (Korean TIN/VAT ID) upon submission of the statutory business registration application and designation of a local tax representative or tax agent under Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. The tax representative accepts service of process and carries out filing obligations. Step 4: The 10-digit Business Registration Number issued by the NTS serves as both the Corporate Tax Identification Number and the Value Added Tax (VAT) registration number; it must be used on tax invoices, electronic tax invoices, customs declarations, and all tax filings. Step 5: Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services to Korean customers may register through the simplified VAT registration procedure for overseas suppliers, appoint a Korean tax agent, and use the HomeTax portal even without a Korean place of business. Step 6: Maintain the registration continuously; any change in company name, address, business type, or legal representative must be reported through HomeTax within 15 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation and is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act. Non-resident shareholders are not directly taxable on corporate profits except through dividend withholding tax upon distribution; the entity does not qualify for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not maintain a check-the-box or elective entity classification regime under national tax law. A Chusik Hoesa is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules derived from the OECD BEPS Project apply mandatorily to prevent double deduction or deduction without inclusion outcomes; no elective hybrid election is available to taxpayers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based exemption threshold applies to corporations; every person engaged in business is required to register for VAT with the National Tax Service before commencing business. A simplified taxation regime with an annual taxable supply threshold of KRW 80,000,000 applies only to qualifying individual sole proprietors, not to Chusik Hoesa corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value Added Tax Act, goods that are exported from Korea are zero-rated, and services supplied in or used for the performance of export business or services performed and consumed outside Korea may also be zero-rated. For AEO certification, supply-chain compliance auditing, digital content, game distribution, and cross-border consulting services, the taxpayer must prove that the recipient is a non-resident with no Korean place of business, that the place of supply and actual consumption are outside Korea, that the service is not connected with Korean real estate or goods located in Korea, and that the consideration is paid in foreign currency or in Korean won through a documented foreign-exchange transaction. Merely invoicing a non-resident is insufficient; the service must be actually rendered and supported by contemporaneous documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration issued through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic clearance system; the export declaration number is the primary statutory evidence of physical export and zero-rating eligibility)
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) demonstrating physical cross-border movement of goods from Korea to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Sales Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident counterparty
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, purchase order or service contract, and a detailed service-delivery or audit report evidencing the foreign recipient, the nature of the AEO audit services supplied, and the overseas place of supply and consumption

All VAT-registered entrepreneurs, and corporations in particular, are required to issue an Electronic Tax Invoice (e-tax invoice) through the HomeTax system or an approved ASP. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted automatically to the NTS in near real time on the date of issuance, and no later than the day after issuance. Each e-tax invoice is assigned a unique digital control number generated and validated by the NTS, and the system operates as an anti-fraud clearance mechanism without requiring a physical fiscal stamp. The e-tax invoice must include the 10-digit Business Registration Numbers of both supplier and recipient, the supply date, item description, supply value, VAT amount, and serial number. VAT returns are filed semi-annually through HomeTax, and export refund claims are also adjudicated electronically.

The National Tax Service, in coordination with the Korea Customs Service, subjects zero-rated export refund claims to documentary scrutiny and may require online verification of the export declaration, vessel or aircraft movement, and bank ledger showing foreign exchange repatriation. If export proceeds are not collected or repatriated within 180 days from the export declaration date, or if the zero-rating is otherwise unsupported by the customs and foreign-exchange documentation bundle, the NTS will deny the zero rating, reassess VAT at 10% on the supply value, and impose statutory additional tax for understating the VAT base. Additional dues include the daily additional tax on overdue national taxes (0.022% per day from the statutory filing deadline until payment) and the underreporting penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes. A false or fraudulent export tax invoice can trigger the illegal tax invoice penalty, criminal referral for tax evasion, imprisonment not exceeding five years, and a fine not exceeding KRW 100 million. For an AEO-certified entity, the Korea Customs Service may additionally suspend or revoke AEO authorization, remove expedited clearance privileges, and re-audit historical export transactions, exposing earlier VAT refunds to retrospective clawback with interest and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalized current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, while capital account transactions are broadly open but require declaration, filing, or approval through an authorized foreign exchange bank for circumstances such as foreign direct investment, overseas borrowings, issuance of securities, and cross-border loans. Export proceeds and current account receipts are freely convertible and may be held in foreign currency accounts without mandatory surrender to the central bank, reflecting the transition from the former surrender requirement regime to a settlement and reporting system. Dividend remittances and profit repatriation by foreign shareholders are permitted after corporate tax clearance, audited financial statements, and submission of an income source verification to the bank; no broad exchange control approval is required, but the remitting bank must file a foreign exchange purpose code and maintain supporting documentation for suspicious transaction monitoring. Capital account repatriation of the original invested amount also carries reporting obligations through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System. In practice, non-residents face documentation friction rather than convertibility denial, and banks must verify the underlying real demand principle before executing large FX conversions. Overall, KRW is freely usable for trade and ordinary business purposes, with residual compliance rails for capital movements, anti-money laundering screening, and evidentiary preservation of the underlying corporate purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Trade Regulations, export receipts must be collected within the statutory payment period, generally within 18 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is approved by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and must be received through an authorized foreign exchange bank or reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; the exporter may hold export proceeds in a foreign currency account under the foreign exchange bank's custody. However, the exporter is obligated to report the collection and holding of export proceeds, retain supporting trade documents such as the bill of lading, commercial invoice, customs export declaration, and a certificate of collection from the bank, and respond to any banking sector due diligence requests. Failure to collect within the prescribed period must be explained, and funds used outside the permitted trade settlement cycle may be treated as a contravention of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and subject to administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Foreign Exchange Information System which enforce the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of South Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to Medium. Foreign fintech EMIs and digital-only banking platforms are not licensed as banks by the Financial Supervisory Service and cannot independently provide full corporate accounts or Korean won settlement rails to a Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa. EMIs such as Wise may offer payment corridors through local bank partners or via SWIFT, but they are not a substitute for a regulated domestic corporate bank relationship required for capital escrow, foreign investment reporting, trade document handling, VAT refunds, and FEIS filings. A multinational treasury structure can use Wise as a secondary payment execution rail for low-value, non-regulated disbursements, but the primary treasury account must reside with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank that can accept foreign capital injections and issue the certificate of foreign investment. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections into the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be structured under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The foreign investor first files a foreign investment notification with the Foreign Exchange Information System through an authorized domestic bank or Invest Korea; upon acceptance, the investor receives a foreign investment notification certificate. The remittance is then made in the investor's home currency or KRW via SWIFT into a dedicated capital escrow account held in the Korean entity's name at the Tier-1 bank. The bank will map the remittance to the government-purpose code for direct investment capital, usually under the capital account category for FDI subscription, and issue a foreign investment registration certificate and paid-in capital certificate after verification. The Korean entity must then register the paid-in capital with the Korean tax authority, obtain an evidence of foreign inward remittance, and produce a statutory certificate of capital verification before the funds can be moved into the general operating account. Inbound transfers must include accurate beneficiary identifiers, the corporate registration number, the purpose of remittance, the share subscription agreement, and a traceable reference to the foreign investment notification certificate; any mismatch between the remitter and the notified foreign investor will delay compliance clearance.Outbound disbursements for the AEO audit and supply chain compliance business should be executed through the primary Tier-1 Korean bank using SWIFT MT103 for straightforward fee-based payments, professional service fees, and operating expenses, with standardized payment purpose codes under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For supply chain trade settlements that require title evidence or documentary security, the treasury should use commercial letters of credit under UCP600, documentary collections under URC522, or bank payment obligations where counterparty credit risk is material. Every outbound trade payment must be supported by service agreements, purchase orders, commercial invoices, customs declarations if goods are involved, internal audit evidence, and where applicable, exchange rate contracts or trade finance facility utilization notices. The Korean bank is required to check that the FX purpose code matches the underlying commercial reality, that the beneficiary is not subject to OFAC/EU/UN sanctions, and that the disbursement amount is consistent with the receivable ledger and transfer pricing policy.

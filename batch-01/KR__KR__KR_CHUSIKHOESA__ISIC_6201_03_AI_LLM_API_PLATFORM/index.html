Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic tax portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa: after completing incorporation registration with the Korean commercial registry, the entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Value-Added Tax Act at the competent District Tax Office of the NTS for its principal domestic place of business, submitting articles of incorporation, incorporation registration certificate, place-of-business lease evidence, and director identification documents; the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the corporate TIN and VAT ID, and the entity must then enroll in HomeTax for VAT returns, electronic tax invoices, and withholding reporting. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean place of business that supplies electronic services to Korean consumers: (1) the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes; (2) the tax representative must file a foreign business operator VAT registration application with the District Tax Office designated by the NTS for foreign electronic service suppliers, attaching the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, evidence of the electronic service offering, and a signed power of attorney in favor of the tax representative; (3) upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number for VAT purposes; (4) the foreign entity uses this number to file quarterly VAT returns through HomeTax and to issue electronic tax invoices where B2B supply occurs; (5) any change in the tax representative, foreign address, or electronic service platform must be reported to the NTS within the statutory amendment period. For non-resident B2B electronic service suppliers, the Korean recipient may instead apply reverse-charge VAT where no registration is required, but B2C electronic services to Korean individuals require the foreign operator registration path described above.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW turnover threshold. Every business operator, including a Chusik Hoesa, must register for VAT upon commencement of business. A foreign non-resident electronic service supplier making B2C supplies to Korean consumers must register from the first taxable supply; there is no de minimis turnover threshold for that registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree, 0% VAT applies to exported goods and to services supplied to foreign non-residents where the service is considered consumed outside Korea and the consideration is derived from foreign exchange earnings. For the AI/LLM API platform, zero-rating is available for: (i) B2B subscription and token-based API usage fees charged to foreign business customers under a written contract where the recipient has no Korean establishment and the service is delivered abroad; (ii) B2C API/token purchases by foreign consumers where the platform can evidence the customer's foreign residency and overseas consumption of the service; and (iii) exported digital content and software services supplied to overseas users. The exporter must maintain the statutory substantiation file proving the foreign customer, foreign currency receipt, and actual delivery/output outside Korea. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports may be claimed as a refund on the quarterly VAT return.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration confirmation (export declaration) for physical goods only; not applicable to pure AI/LLM API exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods; for digital services, the service delivery log and API usage/token consumption report serve as delivery evidence
  • Commercial invoice or electronic tax invoice stating the foreign customer and zero-rated nature of the supply
  • Written service agreement/API subscription contract with the foreign customer confirming the overseas place of supply
  • Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate or inward remittance evidence issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank proving repatriation of the export proceeds
  • Foreign customer's overseas business registration certificate or other foreign residency/status evidence for B2B and B2C services respectively

Mandatory certified electronic tax invoices must be issued through HomeTax or an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice service provider (ASP); e-invoice data is transmitted to the NTS electronically in near real time upon issuance, and the invoice must be issued within the statutory issuance period under Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Input VAT deduction generally requires the purchaser to hold and report a valid electronic tax invoice. The Electronic Cash Receipt system is additionally mandatory for qualifying B2C transactions paid by card or cash. There is no separate fiscal stamp; anti-fraud controls rely on real-time e-invoice transmission, NTS invoice matching, and administrative penalties for late, incorrect, or omitted electronic tax invoices.

If the NTS determines that export zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply will be reclassified as domestic taxable, 10% VAT will be assessed on the gross supply, and the input VAT previously credited or refunded against zero-rated exports will be clawed back with interest. Administrative penalties for false tax invoices, false export declarations, or understated VAT bases will be imposed under the Value-Added Tax Act, and intentional or grossly negligent evasion may trigger criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Repeat violations can also result in increased audit frequency, stricter documentary requirements, and cancellation of the exporter's zero-rating entitlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea exercises a legally regulated but operationally efficient exchange control system. Current account transactions such as software export revenue, technical service fees, royalty receipts, and ordinary operating expenses are quasi-automatically convertible for Korean legal entities through banks that are designated foreign exchange banks. Payment instructions require a stated purpose, the underlying invoice or contract, and reasonable source-of-funds or destination support, but no pre-approval is required for most bona fide service flows. Capital account transactions are substantially more restricted: a non-resident shareholder contributing equity into a Chusik Hoesa must complete foreign direct investment reporting through a designated foreign exchange bank, obtain the FDI registration confirmation, and evidence the inbound SWIFT remittance. The Korean won is not a fully freely convertible currency for speculation or pure capital account transfers. Profit remittance by dividend or liquidation distribution is permitted as a current account remittance only after the bank reviews audited financial statements, withholding tax payment receipts, and the original FDI registration record. Export proceeds are not subject to compulsory conversion into KRW; the entity may retain hard currency in foreign-currency deposit accounts. The principal compliance friction arises from ensuring that every inward and outward remittance is mapped to an approved purpose code and supported by documentation consistent with the international operation and the AI LLM API revenue model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Enforcement Decree, exporters of goods or digital services, including AI/LLM API services, must procure the export proceeds within six months from the date of export declaration or service completion. There is no mandatory surrender ratio requiring conversion of earned hard currency into KRW; proceeds may be withheld in foreign currency accounts held at a domestic designated foreign exchange bank. If the commercial settlement terms require a longer collection period, the exporter must submit an extension request to its designated foreign exchange bank before the original deadline expires, accompanied by the underlying master service agreement, billing milestone schedule, and a foreign receivable aging certificate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) responsible for exchange control policy and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) providing the enforcement framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; medium only as an ancillary foreign-currency receivable collector. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business, or Stripe Treasury do not possess a Korean banking license under the Banking Act and cannot issue KRW current accounts or access the Korean won clearing system. Using an EMI as the principal bank account for a Korean corporation would violate the Electronic Financial Transactions Act because the EMI is not an authorized domestic financial institution. Such EMIs may be used for selective offshore foreign-currency deposit and cross-border payout niche collections, but funds managed through an EMI must eventually be repatriated to a designated Korean bank via a documented SWIFT transfer and reported under the applicable FETA purpose code. Korean underwriting banks generally do not accept EMI transaction statements as conclusive evidence of export proceeds or paid-in capital; therefore, the Chusik Hoesa must maintain its statutory treasury account at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions; Master File and Local File must be submitted to NTS within 9 months after the fiscal year-end, with an annual transfer pricing report attached to the corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulations exist, but Korea applies the OECD BEPS beneficial-ownership and substance-over-form rule; a recipient of treaty-reduced withholding tax must exercise Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) functions—contract negotiation, pricing, AI-model development control, server capacity risk and credit management—in Korea, supported by payroll, office, board minutes and business records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Offenses Act Art. 3: imprisonment up to 10 years and/or a fine up to 3 times the evaded taxes; under Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 47-3: 40% fraudulent-filing surcharge; criminal referral mandatory if the amount exceeds the tax criminal threshold.

Director personal liability: On concealment or disclosure failure, the director is jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties if the corporation lacks liquidity. The criminal court may impose a personal imprisonment sentence without corporate immunity; NTS can blacklist the company and the signing director, trigger travel-ban orders, seize personal foreign accounts, and require restitution of misappropriated proceeds before winding-up.
Transfer Pricing Noncompliance and Document Fraud

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Art. 5: re-determination of taxable income and adjustment of the arm's length price; additional surcharge on income adjustment of 20%; deliberate documentation absence triggers a 40% fraud surcharge plus OCR penalties, and criminal prosecution for concealment if submitted transfer pricing documents are fabricated.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return and knowingly submit false transfer pricing documentation are treated as joint abettors; they can be ordered to compensate the firm and third-party shareholders under the Korean Civil Code Art. 750 (tort) and be disqualified from other board positions; travel restrictions and personal asset seizure are usually ordered to secure unpaid surcharges.
Shell Company / AML Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Specified Financial Information Act require full beneficial ownership reporting; false declarations supply fines up to KRW 100 million, deregistration of foreign investment status, and denial of all treaty relief. AML surveillance suspends payments and referrals to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) trigger criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: The signer or substantial owner may be personally liable for false beneficial ownership statements: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to KRW 50 million under financial laws. The court may pierce the corporate veil and freeze personal assets, apply a travel ban (blacklisting), impose civil restitution to the tax state and third-party creditors, and order criminal imprisonment after conviction.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

South Korea's Commercial Act and Commercial Registration Rules require a definite and identifiable registered office (본점) for tax registration and service of process. A so-called virtual office or commercial mailbox address may be accepted at the registration stage if the address belongs to an approved office-sharing/registered-agent facility and a valid lease is submitted. However, Korean banks (especially for corporate account opening) and the National Tax Service (NTS) will typically reject or heavily scrutinise a pure virtual mailbox without physical office equipment, employees or actual business activity; the NTS may impose a business-site verification that requires a physical meeting at the registered address. For a MarTech SaaS company with no office staff in Korea, a virtual address is permissible only if there is a compliant local agent, a real sub-lease and adequate substance obligations are assumed, otherwise account application and tax registration are likely to be denied.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr); customs clearance portal: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (https://unipass.customs.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a domestically incorporated Chusik Hoesa, legal incorporation is effected with the Korean court registry, after which the company must file a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office via HomeTax or in person. On successful filing, NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the unified corporate tax identification number and VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity that has no domestic place of business but must register for Korean corporate income tax or VAT activity must appoint a Korean tax representative and file the Business Registration Application at the district tax office with jurisdiction over the tax representative's address, attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing the tax representative, power of attorney, and any required due diligence certificates. NTS then issues a foreign corporation business registration number which is used for all corporate tax, withholding tax, VAT, and e-invoice reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a general hybrid entity classification election. A Chusik Hoesa is treated as a domestic corporation under the Commercial Act and is subject to entity-level corporate taxation; no pass-through eligibility is available for equity shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold. Any business operator engaged in taxable supplies in Korea must complete VAT business registration before commencing activities, irrespective of forecast turnover. A simplified taxpayer category applies to certain individual businesses with annual VAT-exclusive revenue not exceeding 80,000,000 KRW, but simplified status does not remove the mandatory registration obligation or the obligation to file periodic VAT returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Korean VAT Act applies to exported goods which are physically shipped from Korea to a foreign territory and accompanied by customs export clearance. It also applies to qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents, including cross-border digital content, software, SEO analytics, and similar electronically supplied services where the recipient is a foreign purchaser and the service is deemed supplied outside Korea under the statutory foreign-payment and foreign-consumption criteria. To sustain 0% VAT, the Korean supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous proof of actual delivery or service performance, full commercial documentation, and documentary evidence that the export consideration is paid from abroad.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Customs Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods out of Korea (not applicable for offshore digital services)
  • Commercial invoice and signed export/offshore service contract
  • Foreign exchange purchase/remittance certificate or inward remittance certificate (payment receipt certificate) proving that the consideration was received in a foreign currency from the overseas customer
  • Service performance documentation for digital services, including server or usage logs and customer acceptance records where available

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system for qualifying supplies, and the issuer must transmit the e-invoice to NTS no later than the day following the supply. System-to-system transmission via the HomeTax open API is supported for ERP integration. The NTS maintains third-party reconciliation and risk-matching analytics on e-invoice data; there is no physical fiscal stamp, but the e-invoice is validated and stored by NTS. Electronic tax invoices must be retained for a minimum of five years and reconciled with VAT return declarations; failure to transmit or systematic under-reporting triggers administrative penalties and audit action.

NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly review export zero-rating claims, refund applications, and electronic tax invoice records. If the supporting export or offshore service evidence is missing, forged, non-contemporaneous, or inconsistent, the NTS may deny the 0% rate and assess undeclared VAT at the standard 10% rate together with interest and penalties. Fraudulent or abusive refund claims may attract an additional penalty surcharge of up to 40% of the refunded or underpaid VAT amount, and deliberate tax-fraud cases may be referred to the prosecutor's office under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Additionally, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days or materially inconsistent foreign exchange documentation can trigger both VAT clawback and foreign exchange regulatory sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly convertible foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border SaaS subscription fees, royalties, and service charges, provided each settlement is handled through a licensed foreign exchange business bank and the underlying economic purpose is documented. The capital account is partially controlled: foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa, the acquisition of shares, and the remittance of paid-in capital require the mandatory filing of a foreign exchange transaction report through the receiving bank, and certain large or unusually structured capital flows may require ex-ante regulatory review. Exporters are no longer subject to a hard surrender quota; they may retain foreign currency proceeds in approved foreign currency accounts. For dividend distributions, the bank requires audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring dividends, corporate tax payment or waiver confirmation, and a Form 17-style foreign exchange declaration. In practice, banks also enforce FATF/Korea Financial Intelligence Unit beneficial ownership rules and may independently ask for underlying documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from regulated goods and services must be collected and received into a licensed domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days of the export declaration or the contractual due date for intangible digital services, whichever is applicable; longer collection periods for multi-year or project-type digital service contracts require an approved extension through the bank and the relevant Korean trade insurance authority. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; there is no fixed export surrender obligation to the Korean won, but receipts must be reported to the domestic foreign exchange bank and the company must retain the export service contract, customer invoice, and payment confirmation for at least five years as part of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act compliance file.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injection must be settled by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's licensed bank to the Korean entity's foreign currency demand deposit account at a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank. The wire must identify the beneficiary as the Chusik Hoesa, include the business registration number and the receiving bank SWIFT BIC, and state the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code for acquisition of equity securities in Field 70, with the memorandum Capital Contribution to KR_CHUSIKHOESA. Upon receipt, the bank issues a foreign exchange purchase/capital contribution confirmation; the company must file the inward foreign investment report through the bank within 30 days, and a certified public accountant must verify the paid-in capital before the balance is transferred to the KRW operational account.

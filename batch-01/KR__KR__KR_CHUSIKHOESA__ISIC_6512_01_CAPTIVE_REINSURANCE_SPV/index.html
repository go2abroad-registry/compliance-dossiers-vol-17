Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act for a Chusik Hoesa; there is no statutory minimum paid-in capital and promoters may set the capital amount freely in the Articles. However, before court registration, the full subscription price must be deposited into a bank account opened in the name of the company being formed, and the bank must issue a deposit balance certificate. No blocked escrow account is required for a non-public company at incorporation. For captive reinsurance authorization, the Financial Services Commission may impose a prudential minimum paid-in capital or guaranty deposit requirement under the Insurance Business Act as a separate licensing condition.

Funding & unlock path: The lessor and building manager must confirm that the office premises have the Fire Protection System Completion Certificate under the Fire Services Act and pass the periodic Korea Fire Facility Maintenance inspection. Civil defense special permits are not triggered by an office-only captive reinsurance SPV. For occupational safety, the company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act; if it employs ten or more workers, it should satisfy the workplace safety and health plan obligations of the Ministry of Employment and Labor and undergo a basic workplace safety inspection. No structural load or dangerous-chemical clearance is required because the SPV has no manufacturing, assembly, or warehousing operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea and its HomeTax online portal (www.hometax.go.kr); export and customs clearance matters are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic customs system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa, the Korean entity must first complete incorporation by registration at the Commercial Registration Office. Within 20 days from the opening of the business, the company must apply for a business registration certificate at the competent local NTS district tax office, either in person or through HomeTax, using the statutory business registration application, articles of incorporation, certificate of company registration, lease or ownership evidence for the registered business place, identity documents of shareholders and directors, and, where applicable, foreign investment documents required under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The NTS issues a 10-digit corporate business registration number that serves as both the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the Value Added Tax (VAT) identification number. A non-resident entity without a fixed Korean place of business that needs to file direct or indirect tax returns is required to appoint a domestic tax representative with an address in Korea before the registration application is processed.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch classification election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies. Any person individually or jointly carrying on a business, including a captive reinsurance SPV or holding company, is obliged to register for VAT within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxpayer regime applies to certain businesses with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80 million, but this changes filing and invoice mechanics and does not exempt registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, the export of goods is zero-rated if the goods are actually shipped out of Korea and an export customs declaration is filed with and accepted by the Korea Customs Service. Designated foreign-currency-earning services, including international freight and certain non-resident services, are also zero-rated when supplied to non-resident persons and substantiated with foreign exchange receipts. Offshore digital services supplied by a Korean supplier to a non-resident recipient may qualify for zero-rating if the services are performed or consumed outside Korea and are not classified as VAT-exempt financial, insurance, or reinsurance services. Captive reinsurance and related financial services are generally exempt from Korean VAT rather than zero-rated, which means input VAT recovery on costs directly attributable to such exempt services is restricted unless statutory exceptions apply.

  • Export Customs Declaration approved by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight
  • Foreign exchange receipt confirmation issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank proving inward remittance or export settlement

All VAT invoices issued between registered businesses must be issued as electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system, and the invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the issue date. Korea does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for VAT invoices, but UNI-PASS is mandatory for customs export declarations and provides the underlying proof for export VAT zero-rating. Retail and instalment sales also require mandatory cash receipt issuance under the cash receipt system for final consumers.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export declarations and supporting documents for a period of at least five years, extended to ten years in cases of fraudulent omission. Export zero-rating that is unsupported by a valid UNI-PASS export declaration, transport documents, or foreign exchange settlement proof is retroactively reversed, resulting in additional VAT assessment and deemed input tax reversal. Penalties may include an additional tax of up to 40 percent of the amount involved under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and intentional fraudulent conduct may be criminally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with fines and imprisonment. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the required statutory settlement period may also trigger sanctions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and can disqualify the supply from zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly liberalised current-account convertibility regime for bona fide trade, reinsurance premiums, claims settlement, dividends and royalties, but capital-account transactions remain subject to a restrictive declaration, notification and statistical-reporting framework under FETA. A captive reinsurance SPV structured as a Chusik Hoesa must ensure every cross-border money movement is classified accurately: inward premium cessions and outward claims payments are current-account service transactions, while an equity injection from a non-resident shareholder is a capital-account foreign direct investment requiring advance MOEF/KOTRA foreign investment notification. There is no mandatory surrender of export/foreign currency proceeds into Korean won, but institutions must submit acquisition-and-payment reports to BOK through their handling foreign exchange bank. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted once an audited balance sheet and board resolution demonstrate distributable reserves; the practical hurdle is not convertibility but the bank's right to demand underlying evidence of the reinsurance arrangement, transfer pricing documentation and the tax residence of every payee before releasing the remittance. The treasury blueprint must therefore maintain an evidentiary file for each wire, including treaty references, actuarial calculations, invoices and settlement vouchers, to satisfy both the commercial bank and BOK audits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The FETA and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations impose no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender requirement for South Korean residents receiving foreign currency proceeds; export/foreign currency receipts may be held in a foreign currency account at a licensed foreign exchange business institution. However, the exporter or beneficiary must ensure that all proceeds are settled through an authorised Korean foreign exchange bank, reported in the bank's foreign exchange acquisition/payment reporting system, and collected within the contractual trade payment terms. For exports declared to the Korea Customs Service, payment typically must be received within the permissible trade settlement period, commonly five months from the shipping date unless an extension is approved by BOK or the handling bank; overdue collectibles require explanatory documentation and may attract administrative penalties under the Customs Act and FETA.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the institutional FX policy executor, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), with operational foreign exchange business supervision delegated to BOK and domestic banks authorised as foreign exchange business institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Weak, and legally barred as a primary treasury rail. An offshore digital EMI without a Korean 'foreign exchange business institution' licence cannot open a domestic KRW operating account or perform FETA-reportable foreign exchange transactions for a Korean entity. FETA restricts foreign exchange business to licenced Korean banks and registered money transfer institutions, so an EMI such as Wise or Revolut Business may only act as an auxiliary remittance conduit through a licenced partner bank. For a captive reinsurance SPV requiring capital verification, FX purpose codes, BOK statistical reporting and documentary settlement of reinsurance treaties, the only viable route is direct onboarding with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident investor must complete the foreign direct investment notification with MOEF/KOTRA before moving funds, then route a clean SWIFT MT103 from the investor's overseas bank to the SPV's designated capital account at a Korean Tier-1 bank, using the direct-investment FX purpose code and clearly referencing the foreign investment registration number. Upon receipt, the Korean bank verifies the remittance against the registered capital, issues a foreign exchange bank handling certificate, credits the Capital Escrow Account or Basic Operational Currency Account, and the capital verification is evidenced by an audited balance sheet or certified bank confirmation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

National corporate income tax progressive brackets: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on taxable income over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21% on taxable income over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion. A 10% local corporate income tax surcharge applies to national corporate tax, producing combined effective rates of 9.9%, 20.9%, 23.1%, and 26.4% respectively. A non-resident Chusik Hoesa with a Korean domestic business place is taxed on Korean-source profits at these same rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% of gross dividend amount paid by the Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge. Bilateral DTA relief commonly reduces this rate to 5-15%, with many Korean treaties providing 10% for a direct corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Korean International Tax Adjustment Act and its Enforcement Decree, a taxpayer engaged in foreign-related-party transactions must prepare and, upon NTS request, submit contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation when total foreign-related transactions for the fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 10 billion. International-transaction reports are required at lower thresholds, generally any cross-border related-party transaction of at least KRW 100 million, and a tax-haven counterparty or offshore captive reinsurance SPV can trigger audit scrutiny even below KRW 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the Gulf Cooperation Council type, but the NTS applies substantive anti-abuse doctrines and CFC rules. The captive SPV's underwriting, actuarial, investment-management, risk-absorption, reserve-setting, and claims-approval functions must be genuinely performed by Korean personnel using adequate office, IT systems, and insurance licenses. The Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 substance-over-form doctrine, the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 17 CFC regime, and the OECD MLI principal purpose test permit the NTS to recharacterize the SPV, deny treaty benefits, and impute income to the Korean controlling shareholder if core income-generating activities are performed outside Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed captive insurance and sham reinsurance SPV with corporate-veil piercing

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed insurance business is criminally sanctionable under the Korean Insurance Business Act with imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 200 million, or both. The NTS will deny deductions, apply 30-40% additional tax for intentionally false returns, and disregard the sham contracts under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto directors who approved the sham contracts are jointly and severally liable for damages to the company and third parties under Korean Commercial Act Articles 389(3) and 401. The NTS may pursue director assets where the corporation is a mere conduit, impose overseas travel bans, attach personal assets, and refer directors for criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Aggressive transfer pricing, profit shifting, and false transfer-pricing documentation

Statutory sanction: The NTS may adjust taxable income under the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose documentation penalties for missing TP files, apply 10-30% additional tax on false reporting, and charge daily late-payment surcharges. Criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act include imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to 5 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The CFO, CEO, TP-document preparers, and de facto controllers who participate in intentional evasion are jointly liable for unpaid corporate taxes when the SPV is a shell. Directors may face civil liability under Korean Commercial Act Article 401, personal fines, confiscation of profits transferred abroad, and an NTS departure-prevention order blocking international travel until the tax liabilities are satisfied.
Outbound withholding tax evasion and abusive treaty relief or dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: The payer is liable for the unpaid withholding tax, daily 0.03% delinquency surcharge, and a withholding non-compliance penalty of generally 10% of the unwithheld amount, with aggravated rates for intentional non-withholding. False treaty-benefit claims may trigger reassessment and 30-40% additional tax; willful evasion through a captive SPV is prosecutable under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to 5 times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct the payment without withholding are personally liable for the withheld tax and penalties under the Korean responsible-management liability rule. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order the foreign parent and its appointed Korean directors to compensate the company's unpaid tax. In flagrant cases, directors are blacklisted, prohibited from leaving Korea, and may face imprisonment of up to 5 years, in addition to civil damages under Korean Commercial Act Article 401.

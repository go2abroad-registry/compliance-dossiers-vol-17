Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korean company law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The incorporators may fix any capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation, provided the total issue price of shares subscribed is fully paid in at the time of incorporation. For practical banking and visa purposes, a paid-up capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 72,000) is commonly recommended for foreign-invested companies seeking D-8 visa sponsorship or meaningful banking relationships; however, this is not a statutory requirement. Subscription payments must be deposited into a provisional escrow account in the name of the company or its incorporators, established with a Korean bank, and remain blocked until the registration of incorporation is completed; after registration, the funds are released to the company's operating account.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. The entity operates exclusively as a digital magazine publishing and sponsored media placement portal under ISIC 5813, with no physical manufacturing, chemical, assembly, or warehousing operations. No Civil Defense fire safety certification, structural load inspection, or workplace safety approval is required. However, the entity must maintain ordinary commercial premises that comply with the Building Act, and if it has more than one employee, it must register with the Ministry of Employment and Labor for industrial accident compensation insurance and comply with workplace safety obligations under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, but these do not constitute industrial operating licenses.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); customs and export clearance through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.kr)

E-government filing portal

The Korean Chusik Hoesa must complete incorporation with the Korean court registry, then file an Application for Business Registration with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction or through the HomeTax portal within 20 days from business commencement. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the unified Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean permanent establishment that supplies electronic/digital services to Korean consumers, the NTS requires registration as a non-resident business operator through HomeTax; the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative, submit a notarized power of attorney, foreign business registration or certificate of incorporation, and evidence of the foreign address, and the NTS will then issue a non-resident BRN. If the foreign supplier establishes a permanent establishment or branch in Korea, the branch must be registered with the NTS under its own BRN, and the branch will follow standard corporate tax and VAT registration procedures. No filing fee is charged for BRN issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The resident Chusik Hoesa is a Korean corporation taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; it is not pass-through eligible. A foreign non-resident supplier without a Korean permanent establishment is taxed only on Korean-source income on a territorial basis, and the non-resident entity is not classified as a Korean corporation for ordinary corporate income tax purposes.

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box or equivalent entity classification election. A Korean Chusik Hoesa is compulsorily a taxable corporation. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act may disallow certain deductions arising from hybrid instruments or hybrid entities, but they do not create an elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the Chusik Hoesa, VAT registration is mandatory upon commencement of business; no turnover exemption or threshold applies for a stock corporation. Simplified taxpayer treatment under Korean VAT law is not available to corporations. For a foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services, registration is also mandatory without any turnover threshold when the foreign supplier provides digital content, digital magazine subscriptions, or sponsored media placements to Korean consumers, Korean business recipients, or through a Korean payment intermediary.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to the export of goods and to services supplied to non-residents outside Korea, including digital magazine portal subscriptions, digital-media services, online content streaming/downloads, and sponsored media placements provided to foreign advertisers or foreign users. To qualify, the actual recipient of the service must be a non-resident or foreign company, payment must be received from abroad, and the service must be delivered, consumed, or used outside Korea. For digital services, country-level IP addressing, user-account jurisdiction, payment source, and digital download logs are accepted evidence of foreign usage when retained and supported by a commercial contract.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration record (electronic export clearance, equivalent to the export Bill of Entry/Export Manifest)
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract or service agreement with the foreign buyer or advertiser
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any physicalized deliverable, or digital download/usage logs with IP-country evidence for pure digital supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign exchange purchase/settlement receipt issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing timely receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency

Korea operates the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system. VAT invoices must be issued for all taxable supplies, and electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax API or web interface within one day of issuance for business-to-business transactions. Tax invoices must be retained for at least 5 years. Korea does not operate a Latin-American-style digital fiscal stamp or pre-clearance fiscal server; the anti-fraud mechanism is NTS real-time e-invoice matching, risk-selection analytics, and mandatory periodic reconciliation. Foreign electronic-service suppliers must issue simplified tax invoices or equivalent electronic receipts for B2C supplies and file VAT returns through HomeTax on the semi-annual cycle (January and July filings).

If zero-rating is claimed without the complete documentation bundle or without proof of foreign use, the NTS will reclassify the supply as taxable and assess 10% VAT on the full invoice value, together with statutory additional taxes. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, omission penalties of 20% to 40% of the underreported tax, late-payment penalties, and interest may be imposed. In fraudulent cases, criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may apply, including imprisonment up to 5 years and fines. The NTS may also claw back zero-rated input VAT credits, impose administrative penalties for falsified foreign-exchange settlement certificates, and bar the taxpayer from claiming export zero-rating during the audit period until all deficiencies are cured.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalised yet monitored foreign exchange regime. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, current account payments are fully convertible, and capital account transactions are now largely deregulated, but all cross-border payments and receipts must be reported to the Bank of Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaged in digital magazine publishing and sponsored media placements, the admission of foreign equity capital, subsequent royalty inflows and advertising proceeds denominated in USD or EUR are treated as service-export receipts; these funds can be credited to foreign currency accounts or converted into KRW without requiring prior approval. However, certain procedures apply: foreign direct investment must be reported to the MOEF or a receiving bank, and the investor must obtain a certificate of investment report. Profit remittances, such as dividends, require a tax clearance certificate and must be routed through a designated bank, with automatic withholding of corporate tax and dividend tax. The cornerstones of convertibility are the mandatory declaration of transactions exceeding USD 5,000 and the restriction that speculative offshore KRW trading is prohibited for non-residents without underlying trade exposure. Overall, the KRW is fully convertible for business and trade purposes, but every transfer must be accompanied by an electronic transaction report lodged with the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 17 of the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within one year of the date of export declaration unless prior approval is obtained. There is no statutory obligation to convert currency receipts into KRW; exporters and service providers may maintain proceeds in a foreign currency bank account with a licensed domestic bank. Nevertheless, all receipts exceeding USD 5,000 must be declared to the designated foreign exchange bank, and the underlying export invoice or digital-service contract must be retained for five years. For digital publishing sales routed through Stripe and AdSense, the net service-export amount must be reported under the BOK classification code 621 for other intellectual property royalties or 622 for software and digital content licences.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment capital must be reported to the MOEF through the designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA, using foreign exchange transaction purpose code 700111 for equity investment. The wire must be sent via SWIFT MT103, with a detailed beneficiary reference containing the tokenised FDI registration number. Funds must be deposited into a provisional capital escrow account at a designated Tier-1 bank and then converted into KRW after capital verification by the bank foreign exchange desk. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (External Audit Act) and amendments effective for fiscal years starting after 2021, a Chusik Hoesa in South Korea is mandatorily required to appoint an independent certified CPA auditor if it meets any of the following quantitative criteria: (a) total assets at fiscal-year-end exceed KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9 million); (b) annual revenues exceed KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million); (c) number of employees exceeds 300 full-time-equivalent staff; (d) it is a listed company, a company listed on KOSDAQ (KONEX/KOSDAQ), or a state-owned/designated public-interest entity; (e) it has issued public debentures or listed securities; (f) it qualifies as a large corporation under the Monopoly Regulation and Fair Trade Act with asset threshold of KRW 5 trillion or group-wide criteria; (g) financial industry licensing or registration applies (bank, insurance, investment, or mutual savings company); or (h) the entity is deemed by the Korean Financial Services Commission to require a statutory external audit for investor protection or public-interest reasons.

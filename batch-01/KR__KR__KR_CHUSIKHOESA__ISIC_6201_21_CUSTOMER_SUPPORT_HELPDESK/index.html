Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; incorporators may determine a nominal capital amount. However, the full subscription price must be deposited into a bank-designated incorporation escrow or share subscription deposit account before filing the registration application, and the bank deposit certificate must be attached to the incorporation filing. For a foreign investor seeking foreign direct investment recognition under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the investment must generally meet the KRW 100 million threshold and be reported to the Foreign Investment Ombudsman/KOTRA procedures; otherwise no blocked escrow minimum applies.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable; no Special Civil Defense fire permit is required for pure digital SaaS operations. The company must nevertheless obtain a standard office fire-safety inspection certificate under the Fire Safety Act for its registered office, comply with the Building Management Act and maximum occupancy limits, and, where the office employs five or more regular workers, submit the basic workplace safety and health declaration under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. No additional industrial fire, hazardous-material, or structural civil-defense permit applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative (a person residing in Korea) and file the corporate registration and business registration application with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the representative's place of abode. The filing must include the entity's articles of incorporation, the written appointment of a tax representative, a power of attorney, and evidence of the transaction nexus such as a digital service contract. For VAT purposes, the Business Registration Application for a foreign supplier must be submitted before the supply is made; the NTS typically issues a 10-digit registered business number functioning as the TIN/VAT ID within 2-5 business days. Registration can be executed electronically through HomeTax using a bearer certificate or an appointed agent's certificate; where the entity has no Korean IP address or certificate, the tax representative files physically on behalf of the entity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative (a person residing in Korea) and file the corporate registration and business registration application with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the representative's place of abode. The filing must include the entity's articles of incorporation, the written appointment of a tax representative, a power of attorney, and evidence of the transaction nexus such as a digital service contract. For VAT purposes, the Business Registration Application for a foreign supplier must be submitted before the supply is made; the NTS typically issues a 10-digit registered business number functioning as the TIN/VAT ID within 2-5 business days. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a check-the-box classification election; a Chusik Hoesa is automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax and VAT. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules are applied under the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination and the OECD MLI, but no elective tax classification is available to a non-resident party.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration: annual taxable turnover exceeding KRW 10,000,000; for foreign suppliers of electronic services (B2C digital supplies): cumulative annual revenue exceeding KRW 10,000,000 from Korean consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on the export of goods under Article 21 of the VAT Act and, for services, on services prescribed as export services (foreign exchange earning services), including software and helpdesk services rendered to a non-resident without a Korean place of business, where the supply is used wholly outside Korea and the consideration is paid in foreign currency or in Korean won from a foreign-source account. For a customer-support helpdesk SaaS, the contract must identify the foreign customer, the service usage location, and the remittance of invoices in an approved currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Acknowledgement) filed and processed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Commercial invoice / service contract with the non-resident purchaser specifying the nature and type of software/helpdesk service
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation (Exchange Bank Settlement Certificate) evidencing inward remittance in foreign currency or foreign-source Korean won
  • Service performance evidence such as server logs or jurisdiction-neutral user access records demonstrating that the beneficiary is a foreign entity

All VAT invoices issued by a Korean supplier or foreign registered supplier are required to be electronic e-tax invoices transmitted in real-time to the NTS electronic tax invoice system (HomeTax) on or before the last day of the month following the supply. B2C foreign digital services are filed in quarterly VAT returns (April 1-30, July 1-25, October 1-25, January 1-25). There is no physical fiscal stamp in Korea; the e-invoice transmission itself acts as the clearance, and the NTS performs anti-fraud matching between e-invoices, VAT returns, and customs transaction data. Failure to transmit triggers additional tax of 0.1%-0.5% of supply value.

Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, the NTS may conduct retrospective review for up to 5 years. If zero-rating is rejected due to failure to substantiate the export or foreign exchange receipt, VAT is recomputed at 10% output tax with an additional tax for unfaithful returns (10% of the understated tax), or 40% in cases of fraudulent misreporting or document falsification, plus statutory late-payment interest from the due date to the payment date. The taxpayer is also denied any corresponding input VAT credits on related costs, and a criminal referral can be made for tax evasion exceeding statutory thresholds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalized current-account regime but retains a statutory capital-account control framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Bank of Korea, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, administers the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) through authorized foreign exchange banks. For the customer-support helpdesk SaaS Chusik Hoesa, operating income from exported SaaS subscription services is convertible into KRW on a settlement basis without mandatory surrender; however, each receipt must be reported through an authorized bank. Capital receipts, such as paid-in capital from a non-resident parent, are freely convertible only after a foreign-investment report is filed and funds are deposited into a dedicated foreign-currency account; otherwise the remittance is characterized as a borrowing transaction and subject to external-debt regulations. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted once the foreign-invested enterprise registration is complete and corporate taxes, dividend withholding tax, and the foreign investor's resident tax certificate are settled. Practical hurdles include FEIS transaction-code granularity, a 15-month export-proceeds receipt limit, and the requirement that all payments be cleared through a locally licensed foreign exchange bank, which slows cross-border invoice settlement and complicates outsourcing-custody cash flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exported software services and SaaS subscriptions, export proceeds must be received and settled by the domestic exporter within 15 months from the shipment or service-provision completion date. The mandatory receipt proportion is 100% of the invoice value; no currency surrender is required, meaning the exporter may maintain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert it to KRW at the market rate. Overdue proceeds exceeding 30 days require a delay report to the authorized foreign exchange bank, and unrecovered export amounts after 15 months require Bank of Korea/FEIS approval to avoid administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the implementing authority for the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking rail. Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Banking Act, only institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission as banks are permitted to conduct foreign exchange banking and maintain the FEIS reporting identity required for corporate settlement. Foreign digital EMIs and non-bank fintech providers such as Wise or Revolut do not hold a domestic banking license in the Republic of Korea, and therefore cannot legally service the Chusik Hoesa as its main treasury account or capital escrow vehicle. Stripe and PayPal are payment aggregator rails for collecting subscription income, but they must settle to a domestic tier-1 bank account and cannot replace a licensed Korean bank for FX reporting, trade payments, or foreign-investment compliance. Designating an offshore EMI as the principal account would block capital verification, create unreported cross-border clearing, and be rejected by Korean tax and customs authorities.

All inbound equity or quasi-equity from the foreign parent must be routed through an authorized foreign exchange bank in Korea using SWIFT MT103. The wire should be denominated in USD or KRW, reference the foreign-investment report number, and carry the FEIS transaction code matching foreign direct investment into a domestic corporation. Funds must first credit a dedicated foreign-investor capital escrow account and then be converted to KRW after the foreign-invested enterprise registration is issued. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary banking rail. Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Banking Act, only institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission as banks are permitted to conduct foreign exchange banking and maintain the FEIS reporting identity required for corporate settlement. Foreign digital EMIs and non-bank fintech providers such as Wise or Revolut do not hold a domestic banking license in the Republic of Korea, and therefore cannot legally service the Chusik Hoesa as its main treasury account or capital escrow vehicle. Stripe and PayPal are payment aggregator rails for collecting subscription income, but they must settle to a domestic tier-1 bank account and cannot replace a licensed Korean bank for FX reporting, trade payments, or foreign-investment compliance. Designating an offshore EMI as the principal account would block capital verification, create unreported cross-border clearing, and be rejected by Korean tax and customs authorities.

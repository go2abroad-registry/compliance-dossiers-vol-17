Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the Articles of Incorporation must state the aggregate amount of member contributions and each member's subscription amount. All cash contributions must be fully deposited into a temporary Korean bank account opened in the name of the company in formation before the incorporation registration application is filed, and the bank must issue a valid capital payment certificate or balance confirmation. Contributions in kind must be appraised by an independent appraiser, and the appraisal record must be attached to the registration file. No blocked escrow account is required beyond the statutory restriction that contributed funds may not be withdrawn before the legal personality of the company arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to the U.S. check-the-box election. The Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is statutorily classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act and is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income (subject to territorial limitations for foreign-source income under tax treaties). Available statutory elections include: (i) simplified taxation for SMEs with annual turnover below the statutory threshold (currently KRW 80 million), which affects VAT calculation mechanics but does not change the entity-level income tax classification; (ii) consolidated corporate tax filing for domestic business groups meeting the criteria under the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination; (iii) tax incentive elections under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act for new growth industries; and (iv) hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted under domestic law; however, the Korean tax authorities apply OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations when assessing cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum economic turnover threshold exists. A person or entity supplying taxable goods or services in Korea (including foreign non-resident suppliers) is mandatorily required to register for VAT upon commencement of any taxable business activity. Failure to register subjects the entity to penalty taxation and automatic assessment on deemed supplies.

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the NTS HomeTax system for all B2B supplies. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for every VAT taxpayer, regardless of annual turnover, since 1 January 2014. Each e-tax invoice must contain a digital signature, unique invoice number, and a machine-readable QR/code, and must be transmitted near-real-time (within 1 day after the supply date) to the NTS via the HomeTax API or certified fiscal intermediary. For B2C retail transactions, a cash receipt must be issued when requested or when the transaction value exceeds the statutory threshold; these are cleared through the NTS real-time cash receipt system. Integrated tax clearance requires the use of NTS-certified e-invoice API connectors or HomeTax Mini/Plus for real-time data submission; the system provides an anti-fraud digital stamping feature that verifies the authenticity and integrity of each invoice. Export zero-rated invoices require linkage with the KCS UNI-PASS export declaration system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a fundamentally liberalized but transaction-based foreign exchange control regime. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act applies a negative-list approach: transactions in both the current account and the capital account are permitted unless specifically prohibited, notified, or reported. The Korean won is convertible for legitimate trade and investment, but it is not a freely deliverable offshore currency for speculative corporate flows; non-residents cannot create unrecorded KRW liquidity, and every cross-border payment must be classified and filed through an authorized foreign exchange bank. For the Yuhan Hoesa, current account transactions such as export collections for silicone fidget toys, platform-based marketplace payouts, and import payments for raw silicone are generally unrestricted and can be executed under ordinary trade documents and purpose codes. Capital account transactions, including the initial non-resident shareholder capital injection, intercompany financing, and future dividend remittances, require prior foreign investment reporting or registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and are subject to bank compliance review; however, they are not subject to blanket convertibility prohibitions. Dividend remittance is possible after corporate income tax filings and retained earnings verification, but a 22% withholding tax under domestic tax law and a foreign exchange declaration will apply. The practical convertibility constraint is not the absence of hard currency but the high granularity of reporting: each inbound and outbound leg needs a purpose code, underlying contract, customs or trade evidence, and economic substance. The Bank of Korea aggregates cross-border settlement data through the Foreign Exchange Information Network, so banks will reject transactions without complete dossiers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Trade Regulations, export proceeds from the silicone fidget toy business must be collected through a registered foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of the Korea Customs Service export declaration or the date on which the goods are shipped, whichever is evidenced by the customs record. There is no mandatory surrender to the Bank of Korea and no mandatory conversion ratio; 0% of export proceeds must be converted to KRW. Exporters may hold the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account and later convert at market rates when making KRW payments. If the proceeds are converted, the bank records the foreign exchange purchase in the Foreign Exchange Information Network. If collection cannot be completed within the six-month statutory timeline, the company must submit a written application for extension to the foreign exchange bank, supported by evidence of buyer default, trade disputes, customs delays, or other justifiable reasons. Failure to receive export proceeds within the prescribed period or to file an extension can lead to administrative fines and interruption of the company's foreign exchange bank relationship.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) for central bank oversight and the Foreign Exchange Information Network reporting duty, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to operate domestic KRW accounts, local payment collections, VAT/tax clearing, or statutory foreign exchange reporting in Korea. An offshore EMI cannot legally serve as the Yuhan Hoesa's corporate settlement bank because it cannot interface with the Korean Real Name Financial Transactions Act, cannot file Foreign Exchange Information Network declarations on the company's behalf, and cannot issue FIPA capital verification documents. EMI and platform payment products may be used only as supplementary front-ends for marketplace sales if the ultimate settlement is routed into a Tier-1 Korean bank account; otherwise, they expose the company to unregistered foreign exchange collection and adverse AML ratings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial non-resident capital injection, the shareholder must file a foreign investment report with KOTRA or a designated domestic foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or within a permissible window around the remittance. The capital wire must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 to the Yuhan Hoesa's FIPA foreign currency capital account at the selected Tier-1 Korean bank, using the exact shareholder name as on the foreign investment report, the finance purpose code for foreign direct investment, and the foreign investment confirmation number in the remittance reference field. Upon receipt, the bank verifies that the ordering customer matches the foreign investor, issues a foreign currency deposit receipt, and converts the funds into KRW at the market exchange rate before transfer to the KRW operating account. Capital verification for the Korean LLC is completed through the bank's balance certificate, the FIPA registration certificate, and, if applicable, a statutory auditor's certificate; the bank records the conversion in the Foreign Exchange Information Network. For high-value equipment and tooling imports, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (sight or usance) issued by the Korean bank is the recommended clearing rail, with a confirming or advising bank abroad. Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like BVI/Cayman, but applies a substance-over-form doctrine under corporate tax law and anti-avoidance principles. Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, transactions with no genuine business purpose or with a foreign shell entity may be disregarded. The Korean entity must have substantial employees, premises, and operational control of manufacturing and marketing activity to avoid the NTS attributing income to the foreign parent. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include product design, manufacturing quality control, inventory management, supply-chain decision-making, and financial responsibility. A Yuhan Hoesa that is a mere pass-through without CIGA in Korea will be attacked, its intercompany pricing adjusted, and the entity may be deemed to have a foreign permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Environmental / Product Safety Violation (Food-Grade Silicone Compliance)

Statutory sanction: Under the Children's Product Safety Special Act, a manufacturer or importer failing to obtain safety certification is subject to imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million. If the product causes a safety accident, the fine is up to KRW 100 million or imprisonment up to 5 years. The Ministry of Environment's chemical control law (CCA) imposes fines for use of restricted substances of up to KRW 30 million per violation. The Korea Customs Service may confiscate shipments and impose customs delay penalties.

Director personal liability: Korean courts have pierced the corporate veil where directors were personally involved in the decision to source non-compliant materials. Directors could be held civilly liable for consumer injuries and punitive environmental damage. Criminal liability: a director who intentionally manufactured with non-food-grade silicone faces imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of KRW 50 million. For repeat or gross negligence offenses, the punishment increases by half. The NTS may also recommend denial or revocation of export/import license, and the director faces personal injury tort liability.
Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, omitting more than 30% of total revenue or underreporting net taxable income by more than KRW 50 million constitutes tax evasion, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 100 million. Additionally, the NTS imposes a 40% unfaithful return penalty on the understated tax, plus late payment surcharge of 0.022%/day. If the evasion exceeds KRW 300 million, the criminal penalty is up to 10 years imprisonment and fines up to 3 times the evaded amount. Habitual evasion increases the punishment to up to 10 years, and the corporate entity receives an additional penalty.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a shell or the director used the company to evade tax. The director is jointly liable for the unpaid corporate income tax, penalties, and surcharges. The court may order seizure of personal assets, bank accounts, and real estate of the director. Criminal liability: the director faces a sentence of up to 5 years imprisonment for intentional false reporting. For VAT evasion, similar imprisonment. Under Article 17 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, the director of a company evading corporate tax additionally faces a fine equal to 2 to 5 times the evaded amount. The NTS also requests prejudgment attachment of personal property. Non-resident directors who leave Korea may be subject to travel restriction (출국금지), red notice from Interpol, and extradition.
AML / Structuring / Unregistered Foreign Financial Account

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any Korean law subject orKorean branch receiving foreign currency exceeding USD 5,000 equivalent must report the receipt. Non-reporting is subject to a fine of up to KRW 20 million. Under the Act on Real Name Financial Transactions, false-name transactions attract fines up to KRW 15 million and imprisonment up to 3 years. The AML law (Act on Reporting and Using Information on Financial Transactions) imposes a fine up to KRW 100 million for failure to file suspicious transactions. In double tax avoidance structures, unreported cross-border dividends attract additional 20% WHT and the NTS may impose a 50% negligence penalty on unreported tax. The court can confiscate the foreign undeclared assets under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who control the bank accounts or direct the offshore payment channel are personally liable. Criminal liability for money laundering under the Criminal Act (embezzlement/breach of trust) can result in imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering proceeds from tax evasion. Under the Korean AML Act, a director who reports no suspicious transactions while controlling the flow can be fined up to 30 million and imprisoned up to 3 years. If the director is a foreign resident, the NTS may initiate mutual legal assistance to freeze accounts in the counterparty jurisdiction. Personal liability for unpaid taxes of up to 100% is imposed if the corporate veil is pierced. The director may also receive a travel ban and be banned from re-entry into Korea until the full amount of evaded tax is paid.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa under the current Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total number of shares and a par value of at least 100 Korean Won per share. The full subscription price for all issued shares must be paid into a blocked deposit or escrow account at a Korean licensed bank before the incorporation registration is filed. The bank must issue a Certificate of Deposit of Subscription Price, and the funds may not be withdrawn until the incorporation registration has been completed and the company acquires legal personality.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as an industrial or civil-defense permit. Ordinary office workplace safety under the Occupational Safety and Health Act and building fire safety under the Act on Fire Prevention and Safety of Buildings apply to the registered office, but no specialized Civil Defense fire permit is triggered by a software-as-a-service EMR business operating from a standard commercial office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic online portal: NTS HomeTax (hometax.go.kr) for business registration, tax filings, and electronic tax invoice clearance; Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for export declarations and trade filings.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing or operating as a Chusik Hoesa, the procedure is as follows: (1) Determine the Korean tax residence: a foreign-incorporated entity becomes a Korean domestic corporation if its head office, place of effective management, or registered office is in Korea; a pure foreign corporation without a domestic place of business must nevertheless obtain a Business Registration Number (BRN, the 10-digit TIN used for both corporate income tax and VAT) before making taxable supplies in Korea or claiming input-tax refunds. (2) File a business registration application with the competent NTS district tax office, or remotely through NTS HomeTax using an accredited digital certificate. (3) Attach the statutory dossier: foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of association (apostilled or consular-legalized as applicable), board resolution or power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative, passport or ID copies of the representative, and evidence of the Korean place of business or service activity (for example, lease agreement, client contract, or bank account details). (4) If the foreign entity has no Korean business office, the NTS may require the appointment of a tax representative resident in Korea to receive notices and perform compliance duties. (5) After verification, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) constituting the entity's TIN and VAT registration. (6) The BRN holder must enrol for electronic tax invoice issuance through NTS HomeTax and keep its NTS e-authentication credentials current; a foreign person may delegate HomeTax functions to the Korean tax representative. The application should be lodged before business commencement or within 20 days of commencement to avoid penalty.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box pass-through election; a Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a separate domestic corporation on worldwide income. No hybrid-mismatch check-the-box election exists under the Corporate Tax Act. Separately, a resident parent corporation may elect consolidated corporate income tax filing for 100-percent-owned domestic subsidiaries with NTS approval, but that election does not change the entity-level classification of the Chusik Hoesa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold (0 KRW). Registration is mandatory upon business commencement; the application is due within 20 days of the business-commencement date. The simplified taxpayer threshold applicable to individual sole proprietors is unavailable to a Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, a 0% rate applies to exported goods and to services supplied for use or performance outside Korea, including qualifying offshore digital services. The EMR clinical workflow SaaS supplied by the Korean Chusik Hoesa to foreign medical clinic customers qualifies as a zero-rated export only when the recipient is a non-resident foreign entity, the service is consumed outside Korea, the supply agreement clearly provides for an offshore SaaS export, and the consideration is received in foreign currency through Korean foreign exchange banking channels. The supplier must prove the foreign place of supply and foreign-currency repatriation; a foreign address on the invoice alone is insufficient.

  • Customs Export Declaration or Export Clearance Certificate prepared through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system, containing the export declaration number and HS/statistical code; for pure online SaaS exports, a UNI-PASS electronic export filing should be made where required, otherwise the service-performance evidence bundle below substitutes for customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport or electronic delivery evidence proving physical exportation (for physical media or software carriers); for online-only SaaS, delivery and usage records serve as the export performance evidence
  • Commercial invoice describing the EMR SaaS, supply terms, contract value, and 0% VAT statement
  • International service contract or purchase order executed with the foreign medical clinic customer
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange earning certificate issued by the Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency from the overseas customer
  • Service performance records demonstrating offshore use (for example, go-live certificate, subscription activation log, server access or usage data, or customer acceptance email)
  • Foreign exchange bank record of repatriation within the 180-day statutory deadline, or an approved extension where applicable

Korea operates a mandatory national electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance regime. All B2B VAT tax invoices must be issued electronically through NTS HomeTax or an approved ERP/API-linked e-invoice system and transmitted to the NTS in real time. The NTS validates each e-tax invoice and assigns a unique NTS approval number; there is no physical fiscal-stamping hardware. The tax invoice must be issued no later than the day after the supply date, subject to the limited statutory lump-sum issue schedule. Anti-fraud controls derive from real-time NTS clearance and matching of e-invoices, entity-level digital certificates, and administrative penalties for missing, late, or false e-invoice transmissions.

NTS audit teams cross-check export declarations, FIRCs, service contracts, e-invoice output, and payment receipts. If zero-rating is denied, the Chusik Hoesa becomes liable for the 10% VAT on the full supply value, plus the VAT Act additional tax attributable to unregistered supplies, unfaithful returns, or false e-tax invoices, and late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Intentional or fraudulent claims attract aggravated penalty tax and may be referred for prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to ten years or a fine not exceeding three times the tax evaded. The NTS may reopen returns within five years of the statutory due date, or ten years where the error is fraudulent or tax-avoidance-related, and any refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility and substantial but regulated capital-account convertibility. The national currency, the Korean won (KRW), cannot be used offshore except through licensed FX banks; all resident-to-non-resident and non-resident-to-resident exchanges must be executed by a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported into the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). For a Korean Chusik Hoesa, all software export receipts, including EMR subscription revenues collected via Stripe Billing, must be repatriated as foreign-currency receipts. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is freely transferable provided that the foreign shareholder submits the audited financial statements, a board resolution approving dividends, the corporate registration extract, and the National Tax Service withholding tax clearance. Inbound equity capital requires a proper filing under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) and opens a foreign investment capital deposit account. No fixed surrender ratio is imposed today, but export proceeds must be retained in a domestic FX account or converted by a bank. The regime is generally favourable for a treasury function, but every operation must be documented against the real commercial transaction and must be cleared by the bank's sanctions and AML engine.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Foreign Exchange Business Regulations, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic Korean foreign-currency account within one month of the receipt date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio currently in force; the exporting company is free to hold the foreign currency in its bank account, convert to KRW at market rates, or use it for permitted FX payments. All repatriations must be documented in FEIS with the export invoice and contract, along with shipping/document tracking evidence for physical goods (for software, the SaaS/service contract and electronic delivery records).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Foreign Exchange Administration of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed foreign exchange banks may operate foreign-currency deposit, forward, and cross-border clearing services for resident entities. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer do not hold a Korean FX banking licence and cannot accept deposits or issue bank guarantees for a Korean company. They may be used only as ancillary payment aggregation for international e-commerce/SaaS receivables, but the company must maintain its primary operating and escrow accounts with a licensed Tier-1 domestic bank. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for capital outflow, intercompany loans, or dividend repatriation will be rejected by the customs/forex reporting system and is structurally non-compliant.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Thin corporate substance and non-resident UBO: the Chusik Hoesa is majority-owned by a foreign investor, and the board may have no Korean resident director, making it difficult for the bank to verify operational control and source of wealth.

Root cause: Thin corporate substance and non-resident UBO: the Chusik Hoesa is majority-owned by a foreign investor, and the board may have no Korean resident director, making it difficult for the bank to verify operational control and source of wealth.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from a non-resident shareholder must be filed in advance with a licensed foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The investor obtains a foreign investment notification number; then funds are remitted via SWIFT MT103 with the reference of the capital contribution and the Korean FX purpose code for direct investment (equity). The remittance is received into the Foreign Investment Capital Escrow Account maintained with the Tier-1 bank. Upon receipt, the bank issues a Foreign Capital Deposit Certificate, and after the share issuance is completed, the company files the capital subscription report to obtain a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, which serves as the statutory capital verification evidence. The funds may then be converted to KRW and transferred to the operating account at the market exchange rate, subject to the bank's FX desk quotation. No separate remittance approval is required for qualifying FDI, but the bank will need the articles of association, investment board resolution, and confirmation that the entity is not in a restricted sector.All foreign payments—for cloud hosting, international software licences, vendors, and dividends—must be executed by a licensed foreign-exchange bank. For trade settlements above the bank's reporting threshold, the beneficiary and purpose code must be specified; a standard SWIFT MT103 is used for general service payments. Imports of hardware or medical-adjacent infrastructure are handled through documentary collections or commercial letters of credit, while software costs are cleared as service payments. All outbound transfers must be supported by underlying commercial documentation such as contract, invoice, tax clearance, and must be submitted to the Foreign Exchange Information System through the bank's reporting module. Domestic payroll and operational disbursements in KRW are settled through the BOK-Wire+ / large-value payment system and the ACH/CTS for batch payments.Onshore KRW/USD forward contracts with licensed foreign exchange banks for firm export and import exposures, subject to the real-demand principle and the submission of underlying trade documents.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) concluded with a licensed bank, allowed only for bona-fide hedging of a non-KRW underlying exposure (corporate foreign-exchange risk) and not for speculative positions.Cross-currency swaps (KRW-USD) for maturity-matched debt or equity-related working capital exposure, provided the bank has approved the underlying transaction and the swap is documented under the standard ISDA/KSAPA terms.Currency options only when embedded in a commercial loan or trade-finance structure and fully hedged for underlying trade invoices, with the bank's compliance desk review.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed foreign exchange banks may operate foreign-currency deposit, forward, and cross-border clearing services for resident entities. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer do not hold a Korean FX banking licence and cannot accept deposits or issue bank guarantees for a Korean company. They may be used only as ancillary payment aggregation for international e-commerce/SaaS receivables, but the company must maintain its primary operating and escrow accounts with a licensed Tier-1 domestic bank. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for capital outflow, intercompany loans, or dividend repatriation will be rejected by the customs/forex reporting system and is structurally non-compliant.

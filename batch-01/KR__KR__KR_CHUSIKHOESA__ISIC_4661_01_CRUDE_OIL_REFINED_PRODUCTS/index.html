Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act after the 2012 abolition of the minimum capital floor. However, each subscribed share must be fully paid before incorporation registration. The subscription money must be deposited into a bank account opened in the name of the corporation in the Republic of Korea, not a personal account of an incorporator. Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a non-resident subscriber must file a foreign investment report with a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA and may be required to hold the inward remittance in a dedicated foreign investment escrow-type account to obtain a foreign investment certificate; such certificate is useful for later tax exemptions and license applications.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Fighting Facilities Certificate and Civil Defense Safety Inspection from the local fire station under the National Fire Agency; structural load inspection certificate for tank foundations and pipe racks from a licensed structural engineer; workplace safety and health risk assessment approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act filed with the regional office of the Ministry of Employment and Labor; chemical release and emergency response plan approved for any dangerous substance storage; and certification of fire extinguishing equipment adequacy before the storage or distribution facility may begin operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); export and foreign-trade records are integrated through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a domestic tax representative (certified tax accountant or licensed tax attorney) for the non-resident equity holder and execute a power of attorney to act before the NTS. Step 2: Obtain the mandatory petroleum wholesale licence under the Petroleum and Petroleum Substitute Fuel Business Act and any marine-fuel bunker supplier authorisation, because the NTS business registration file requires the statutory licence number. Step 3: Incorporate the Chusik Hoesa at the Korean court registry and open a Korean bank account to receive export settlements. Step 4: Submit the Business Registration application through HomeTax or directly to the competent district NTS tax office using the company's Korean principal place of business; attach certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, board resolution naming the domestic tax representative and registered office, lease or ownership evidence for the business premise, and foreign parent/investor corporate documents. Step 5: Upon verification, NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions simultaneously as the entity TIN and VAT registration number; a separate VAT ID is not issued. Step 6: Activate the corporate HomeTax electronic certificate, register for the NTS-certified e-invoice API/EDI channel, and obtain a UNI-PASS trader ID for export declarations. There is no minimum capital requirement for tax registration, and 100% foreign ownership does not change the procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a stock corporation: the Chusik Hoesa is required to register for VAT from the first taxable supply. The Korean small-business VAT exemption threshold (KRW 80 million for goods and KRW 30 million for services, as adjusted by statute) is available only to individual entrepreneurs, not to a corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 26 of the Value-Added Tax Act zero-rates goods exported from Korea when an export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service and the goods are physically moved out of Korea. For marine fuel oil, distillates and crude-oil products, zero-rating also applies to bunker supplies delivered to foreign-going vessels engaged in international voyages and to international aircraft, as well as to qualifying export brokerage/commission services supplied to a non-resident counterparty for the underlying physical export trade. The supplier must prove the goods left Korea, the vessel/aircraft was in international trade (for bunkers), and the foreign-currency settlement was received from abroad. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated sales is refundable through the NTS VAT return system.

  • KCS UNI-PASS export declaration acceptance certificate (Export Declaration Certificate) covering the crude oil, distillates or marine fuel cargo
  • Commercial invoice, sale/purchase contract, packing list, and quantity/quality certificate for the cargo
  • Ocean or multimodal Bill of Lading, or KCS-accepted electronic export transport document
  • Bunker Delivery Note and vessel master's signed receipt confirming the vessel or aircraft is employed in international navigation (for bunker zero-rating)
  • Bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing the inward remittance from the foreign buyer, equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate

All corporate VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax repository or an NTS-certified EDI/API integration. The e-tax invoice is transmitted to the NTS at the time of issuance and receives an NTS reference number that is used for anti-fraud matching with customs and bank foreign-exchange data; paper tax invoices are not permitted for a corporate trader. For zero-rated export and cross-border supplies, the electronic invoice must carry the relevant export declaration reference and the 0% zero-rated output VAT amount. Although Korea does not use a pre-approval digital stamping system, the mandatory NTS e-invoice repository and UNI-PASS integration operate as a real-time fiscal clearance layer for export zero-rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea is not a hard surrender-based exchange-control state but a fully-notified managed float regime. Current-account transactions, notably export and import settlements, service payments, dividends, royalties and cross-border trade finance, are generally liberalized under FETA and require only report-based verification through a designated Foreign Exchange Bank. Capital-account transactions are formulated under the capital account liberalization schedule: inbound foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa is permitted after filing a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance/Bank of Korea through a local bank or KOTRA, and funds must be held in a designated foreign currency account until certificate of capital verification. There is no forced surrender of export foreign currency to BOK, but export proceeds must be either converted into KRW via a licensed FX bank or retained in a domestic foreign-currency deposit account before the statutory collection deadline. Profit remittance is administratively workable but not frictionless: dividends require audited financial statements or a certified balance sheet, corporate tax payment verification, board resolution with the corporate seal, and a beneficial-ownership attestation; the remitting bank will also screen for sanctions and verify the non-resident shareholder's ultimate identity. Foreign currency loans and trade financing over threshold volumes are reportable and must contain genuine economic substance. Overall, the KR regime is compliant for an energy commodity trading entity if in-country banking is used and all transactions are recorded with the correct purpose codes and BOK/FIU reporting protocols.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, the default statutory export settlement period is 18 months from the date the export declaration is accepted; an extension may be granted by the designated foreign exchange bank for legitimate delays such as trade disputes, deferred payment terms, or force majeure conditions. There is no fixed mandatory conversion/surrender ratio to KRW. The exporter must choose, within the settlement window, to convert the receipts into KRW through an authorized FX bank or deposit them in a domestic foreign-currency account opened with a licensed bank; every receipt and conversion must be logged to the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System with the related export declaration number and transaction purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System, with policy and supervisory coordination by the Ministry of Economy and Finance; BOK exercises indirect capital-flow management and surveillance rather than a black-market rationing regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low: offshore digital EMI services are legally non-viable as the primary treasury or clearing bank for a South Korean resident legal entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any cross-border settlement, export receipt, import disbursement, or FX conversion must be processed through a licensed Foreign Exchange Bank in Korea. Offshore EMIs do not hold a Korean banking license, cannot open a domestic foreign-currency account for a Chusik Hoesa, and cannot file the mandatory BOK/FIU reports. Use of an offshore EMI for corporate operating payments may trigger AML, tax and foreign-exchange-reporting violations. At best, a non-bank payment system may process small local collections, but it cannot handle crude oil/refined products trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholders' capital is remitted via an international SWIFT wire (MT103/MT202) in USD or KRW to the Chusik Hoesa's designated foreign-currency account held at a licensed Korean Foreign Exchange Bank. The remitter must reference the Foreign Investment Promotion Act report number in the payment narrative, and the beneficiary bank will apply the inbound funds to the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System with an inward foreign exchange purpose code corresponding to equity investment in a domestic corporation. Within the statutory period, the bank issues a foreign currency investment certificate (capital verification certificate) confirming the remitted amount, exchange rate, date, and bank reference, which is then used for registration and corporate capital increases at the court registry. Any KRW conversion must be booked through the same bank and carry the capital-verification FX contract number.For downstream refined-product and crude oil disbursements, the Chusik Hoesa will use SWIFT Oversea Remittance MT103 with back-to-back trade documentation, or an Standby Letter of Credit/Commercial Letter of Credit when the beneficiary requires bank undertaking. A documentary collection is acceptable only for tested, low-sanctions-risk counterparties and requires a signed purchase agreement, verified bill of lading, certificate of quality/quantity, and payment schedule. For KRW-denominated vendor payments inside Korea, clearing is achieved through BOK-Wire+ / domestic KFTC rails. The bank will verify each outward transfer against an underlying trade contract and, when necessary, the exporter's foreign exchange purchased/sold record, ensuring that payments do not exceed documented invoice values and that maritime disbursements are covered by a confirmation of cargo/vessel identity.Onshore deliverable USD/KRW FX forward booked with a licensed domestic Foreign Exchange Bank, subject to the Korean 'real demand' principle and supported by the underlying energy trade contract or receivables/payables schedule.Cross-currency interest-rate or basis swap between USD and KRW used solely to hedge long-term external financing or capital-loan exposure, with prior BOK/FX bank reporting and no speculative net open position.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) can only be used where the trade-leg currency is a non-KRW exotic pair or where the bank approves a back-to-back NDF with a foreign counterparty; a Korean resident energy trader may not run an open NDF position against KRW outside the BOK real-demand framework.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low: offshore digital EMI services are legally non-viable as the primary treasury or clearing bank for a South Korean resident legal entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any cross-border settlement, export receipt, import disbursement, or FX conversion must be processed through a licensed Foreign Exchange Bank in Korea. Offshore EMIs do not hold a Korean banking license, cannot open a domestic foreign-currency account for a Chusik Hoesa, and cannot file the mandatory BOK/FIU reports. Use of an offshore EMI for corporate operating payments may trigger AML, tax and foreign-exchange-reporting violations. At best, a non-bank payment system may process small local collections, but it cannot handle crude oil/refined products trade settlements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

National Corporate Income Tax at progressive marginal rates: 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 20% on taxable income from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 22% on taxable income from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; and 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion. A local income tax surcharge equal to 10% of the national corporate tax is superimposed, yielding effective combined marginal rates of 11%, 22%, 24.2%, and 26.4% respectively. For wholesale energy trading profits above KRW 300 billion, the effective top rate is 26.4%.

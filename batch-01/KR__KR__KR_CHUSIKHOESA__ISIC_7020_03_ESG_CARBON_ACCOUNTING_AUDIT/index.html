Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are generally not accepted for company registration in Korea. The registered office must be a physical address where the company can receive official correspondence and where the company's books and records are kept. The National Tax Service requires a physical presence for tax registration, and banks require a physical office for opening corporate accounts. For the ESG carbon accounting and audit industry, a virtual office is likely to be rejected by the tax authority and banks due to the need for client meetings, data storage, and audit trail. A serviced office or shared office space may be acceptable if it provides a dedicated address and meeting rooms, but a pure virtual mailbox is not permissible.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act. A Chusik Hoesa may be formed with any subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation; there is no blocked escrow account requirement. The subscription price must be fully paid into a Korean bank account before registration, and the bank's capital contribution certificate is submitted to the court. For a nominal issued capital of approximately KRW 10 million, the aggregate official court filing fee and local registration tax is about USD 55, and it scales with the capital declared in the articles.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a Chusik Hoesa (Stock Corporation) with a domestic place of business, the representative or legal counsel must file a Business Registration Application with the local district tax office (or through the HomeTax portal) within 20 days of commencing business, attaching the articles of incorporation, lease contract or proof of fixed business premises, and identification documents of the representative. The NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both the Corporate TIN and VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a domestic fixed place of business but supplying taxable electronic services or other qualifying B2C digital supplies into the Republic of Korea, the foreign entity must appoint a local fiscal representative (Kukse Daeriin) and apply for Registration of Foreign Business Operator via the HomeTax portal. The NTS assigns a separate VAT Business Registration Number with a distinct prefix for foreign operators. The application must include the foreign entity's official registration certificate in the home jurisdiction, details of the local fiscal representative, a description of the supply chain, and the banking account designated for potential VAT refunds. All amendments, closures, or changes to the tax representative must be reported to the NTS within 15 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the manner of a 'check-the-box' election; the Republic of Korea subjects all resident stock corporations to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Tax Act. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General registration is mandatory for any supplier in the course of business within the Republic of Korea, irrespective of a minimum turnover for standard corporations. A small-business simplification threshold applies where annual VAT-exclusive taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 48,000,000, allowing simplified taxpayer status. Foreign non-resident providers of electronic services are required to register as foreign business operators on HomeTax without a volumetric threshold; however, an optional simplified registration and return procedure is available when overseas remote digital supplies to Korea do not exceed KRW 30,000,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act to the export of goods, international services rendered, and qualifying offshore-supplied digital services. For goods, the exporter must physically deliver the goods out of the Republic of Korea, submit a customs export declaration to Korea Customs Service, and retain export documentation supporting tax-exclusive export supply. For services, the recipient must be outside Korea, the service must be consumed or utilized abroad, and the service must not fall under exemption rules. ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage services supplied to overseas clients may qualify for 0% VAT only if the service contract and performance evidence substantiate overseas use and no Korea domestic benefit occurs. The zero-rating claim must be reflected on the relevant VAT return and supported by complete documentary proof; input VAT attributable to exported supplies is then fully creditable and refundable through NTS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) approved by Korea Customs Service
  • Export Tax Invoice (zero-rate VAT invoice) issued under the Value-Added Tax Act
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Foreign Exchange Proceeds Claim (exchange contract/verification certificate) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Service supply contract and performance report for qualifying offshore digital services or international services

The Republic of Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing under the Value-Added Tax Act. All business-to-business VAT invoices must be issued as electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system. Issuers must transmit a copy of each fully accurate e-invoice to the National Tax Service within one day after issuance via the HomeTax platform or a licensed e-invoice service provider API, and are expected to issue the e-invoice no later than the statutory tax-invoice issuance deadline, generally the 10th day of the month following the supply month. There is no real-time fiscal stamping or clearance approval as in certain European jurisdictions; instead, NTS applies a post-supply data analytics and cross-matching model that compares invoice data against VAT returns, customs declarations, and bank remittance records. Delayed or non-transmitted e-invoices attract additional charges and administrative penalties.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service maintain a joint data-matching system over export declarations, VAT e-invoices, foreign exchange receipts, and shipping events. If the export zero-rating claim is unsupported, incomplete, or materially inconsistent, the NTS will retrospectively reverse the 0% rate and assess VAT at 10% on the export supply plus withholding of refunds. Additional charges under the Basic Act for National Taxes can reach up to 40% of the underpaid tax for illegal refund claims or unreported tax liabilities; repeated or documentary-forged offenses can trigger criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, including up to three years of imprisonment or a fine not exceeding KRW 100,000,000. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 180-day deadline carries monetary penalties, criminal sanctions, and possible retrospective customs revocation, which in turn invalidates the export declaration and requires full VAT reassessment at trend rate with interest and penalty surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalized but reportable foreign-exchange regime, not a surrendering convertibility system. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act classifies transactions into current and capital items; current-account settlements are freely permitted, while capital transactions, including foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and securities-related flows, generally require advance filing, declaration, or post-event reporting to the Bank of Korea or an authorized foreign exchange bank. The Korean won is highly convertible for genuine trade and corporate operating transactions, but the authorities maintain granular resident and non-resident identification requirements derived from the Real Name Financial Transactions Act and the Financial Action Task Force framework. There are no surrender quotas forcing the conversion of export proceeds into won; exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-exchange bank accounts, but title to the currency and records must remain booked with a locally licensed bank. Profit remittance and dividend outflows are permitted after corporate income tax, withholding tax, and capital verification have been satisfied, with the bank reviewing the foreign-invested enterprise checklist and supporting board resolutions. This framework means the entity can successfully clear inbound equity and outbound dividend flows, but treasury procedures must include reporting codes and authorized-bank documentation for every capital cross-border movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires export proceeds to be received through a domestic authorized foreign-exchange bank generally within six months from the export declaration date unless an extension is granted for justifiable commercial causes. Mandatory conversion ratio: none. Exporters may retain the foreign exchange in a domestic foreign-currency account, and any conversion into KRW is done at eligible commercial bank rates; the bank records the transaction under its foreign-exchange reporting duties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and coordinating foreign exchange administration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and authorized domestic foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms and international neo-banks are not licensed as banks or foreign exchange dealers in South Korea and cannot directly issue KRW, settle with the Bank of Korea settlement system, or satisfy the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting and tax certificate obligations. The entity may use a regulated foreign EMI only for paying overseas subscription fees in a non-KRW currency when the balance is funded from Korea through a licensed bank, but Korean tax and customs authorities look to the domestic bank trail. Therefore, the main account set must be with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; any fintech account is auxiliary and cannot be used for capital verification or profit repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first file a Foreign Investment Report with a domestic authorized foreign-exchange bank or KOTRA. The capital remittance should then be wired via SWIFT MT103 using the foreign exchange purpose code for direct equity investment, naming the Chusik Hoesa as beneficiary and referencing the Foreign Investment Report number. The funds land in the entity's foreign-currency capital deposit account at the bank, the bank verifies the remitter, reference and payment intent, and issues a foreign currency purchase or receipt confirmation. A certified public accountant then performs statutory capital verification and files the necessary tax registration update. Each outward remittance must be supported by the relevant invoice, contract, tax invoice where applicable, and a foreign exchange payment purpose code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion of cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Master File and Local File; KRW 10 billion-50 billion triggers a simplified report; taxpayers with revenue of less than KRW 100 billion and transactions of less than KRW 5 billion are exempt.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose OECD-style ESR, but the Substance-over-Form doctrine under the Framework Act on Tax and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) requires a Chusik Hoesa to demonstrate real management, staff, assets and decision- making in Korea; the 2019 Multilateral Instrument (MLI) enables denial of treaty benefits if the main purpose is to obtain treaty relief, including a bright-line minimum substance test for principal purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs (Art. 6 and Art. 12) and the Corporate Income Tax Law (Art. 26). Korean tax authority imposes a 30% additional tax on understated income, plus 6.6% annual interest on unpaid tax, and disallows the deduction entirely.

Director personal liability: Civil joint liability: Directors (registered and de facto) become jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax under the National Tax Collection Act Art. 39 when the corporation lacks assets. A criminal prosecution may result under the Aggravated Punishment Act on Specific Economic Crimes (Art. 3-1) with imprisonment up to 2 years plus a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax; international travel ban (blacklisting) under Art. 109-2.
Sham 'Shell' Company without Korean Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service denies treaty benefits under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Art. 26-2 (Principal Purpose Test) and recharacterizes the invoice payments as dividends. A penalty of 20% of the recomputed tax is imposed, plus a KRW 1 million to 10 million civil fine under Tax Offense Prosecution Rules.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Korean Court precedent (Supreme Court 2005Da 1/2) allows creditors and the NTS to collect taxes from directors. Furthermore, the director is personally liable for willful nonpayment under Criminal Act Art. 349; blacklisting and departure restrictions are automatic for the director; imprisonment up to 5 years for fraud.
Fraudulent Carbon Credit Auditing and Offset Brokerage (AML/Environmental)

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Framework Act on Carbon Neutrality (Art. 23) and the Act on Fraud against Consumer Property (Art. 2). The tax office will deny tax credits, impose a 50% fraud penalty on the unpaid/evaded tax, and, under AML rules, combat fraud under Act on Reporting and Using of Certain Financial Transaction Information.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of all fraud proceeds; additional criminal penalties: imprisonment up to 3 years under Art. 39 of the Framework Act, and up to 5 years for VAT and income tax fraud. Directors are blacklisted from government procurement and may be subject to international asset freeze.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, electronic tax portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr); for outbound customs formalities and export declarations the Korea Customs Service operates UNI-PASS (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A Chusik Hoesa organised in Korea files an Application for Business Registration with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over its principal place of business within 20 days after business commencement. The registration is filed on HomeTax or with an NTS-affiliated tax office manager and produces a 10-digit Business Registration Number used simultaneously for corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding tax. A foreign non-resident entity that has no Korean domestic place of business but is required to register for Korean VAT must appoint a domestic tax representative, file the identical business registration application through the representative, and submit the entity's certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, evidence of authority of the representative, and a statement of the Korean place of supply or business activity. If the entity intends to export cargo from Korea, it additionally registers with Korea Customs Service as an exporter in UNI-PASS to obtain a customs ID for export declarations. The NTS issues the statutory Business Registration Certificate after verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No flow-through or check-the-box election is available to a Chusik Hoesa; it is a domestic corporation taxed at entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. The only available statutory group election is the consolidated corporate tax base filing mechanism under the Corporate Tax Act if a resident Korean parent owns 100% of the domestic subsidiary, subject to continued ownership and reporting conditions. Korean anti-hybrid rules under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act apply to any hybrid financial instrument or hybrid entity arrangement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold is provided; every person supplying taxable goods or services in the Republic of Korea is required to register. A simplified taxable person threshold of KRW 80,000,000 in annual turnover applies only to individual entrepreneurs, not to a Chusik Hoesa, and even simplified persons must be registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies when a Korean VAT-registered operator exports goods from Korea to a foreign destination and retains customs-accepted export evidence. Project-cargo logistics services performed directly for exported heavy-haul and out-of-gauge cargo are treated as services directly related to exports and are likewise zero-rated, provided the service invoice identifies the relevant export cargo, the purchaser, and the service charge, and the service is paid for by or settled from foreign currency. Where no physical export occurs, zero-rating may still apply to services performed overseas under the place-of-supply rules if the recipient belongs outside Korea and the Korean supplier substantiates the offshore performance and foreign receipt of funds.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) showing the UNI-PASS electronic declaration number and export item description
  • Original Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the out-of-gauge cargo from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing out-of-gauge dimensions, weights, marks, and project cargo descriptors
  • Freight forwarding or project-cargo service contract demonstrating the direct nexus between the zero-rated service and the export shipment
  • Certificate of Purchase/Sale of Foreign Exchange issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank, or a bank remittance advice showing export proceeds credited in a foreign currency
  • Out-of-gauge transport permits, route survey, or special haulage approval from the competent road or port authority where required to evidence lawful export movement

VAT-registered operators must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice service and transmit invoice data to the NTS by the day following the supply date using the HomeTax web interface, bulk file protocol, or machine-to-machine API. Korea does not impose a pre-clearance fiscal stamping model; instead the NTS clears invoices through real-time database matching of issued and received tax invoice data, VAT returns, customs declarations, and foreign-exchange settlement data. Late, erroneous, or omitted electronic transmission triggers VAT additional tax at prescribed statutory rates.

When NTS or Korea Customs Service disallows the zero-rating evidence, the supply is reclassified as taxable and VAT at 10% is assessed on the full supply value, together with additional tax for an unfaithful or fraudulent return under the Framework Act on National Taxes and late-payment interest. A fraudulent claim of export VAT refund is clawed back with punitive surcharges up to 40% of the underpaying amount, and the responsible directors and tax representatives are exposed to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Offenders Act for deliberate evasion. Customs false declaration penalties and export-restriction sanctions may additionally apply where the export declaration or Bills of Lading are fictitious.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates under a managed floating foreign exchange system. The won is freely convertible for current-account transactions, including trade-related payments, dividends, service fees and interest payments, subject to verification through a domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions are substantially liberalized, but certain remittances require reporting, registration or approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; inbound foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa is recorded through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. No mandatory export surrender ratio is imposed; exporters may retain foreign currency in designated foreign exchange deposit accounts and convert at will. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary: the bank must receive the audited financial statements, corresponding withholding tax payment certificates, minutes of the shareholders' meeting determining the dividend, and evidence of tax clearance before processing the outward dividend remittance. Foreign exchange rates are freely market-determined, and hedging is possible via authorized banks. Speculative foreign exchange transactions by non-financial corporations are restricted, and short-term external borrowing is subject to reporting and monetary policy guidelines. Overall, South Korea offers a highly convertible, institutionally transparent environment suitable for cross-border project cargo heavy haul operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Trade Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 6 months from the date of the export declaration; an extension of a further 6 months may be granted by the foreign exchange bank with supporting reasons. There is no mandatory conversion requirement and no minimum Korean won surrender ratio; the corporation may hold the entire foreign currency amount in a Foreign Exchange Deposit Account with a licensed Korean bank and later convert at market rates or use it directly for foreign currency payments. Export proceeds received in advance must be settled against the related export declaration within the prescribed periods, and any balance remaining after liquidation can be retained or converted. Failure to collect within the statutory window requires an additional report to the Bank of Korea and may affect the exporter's trade compliance standing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of Korea) acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Under the Korean Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only banking institutions licensed by the Financial Services Commission may lawfully accept KRW deposits and provide corporate payment accounts with local clearing capability. Offshore digital EMI providers without a Korean banking license cannot offer a compliant primary operating account to the Chusik Hoesa; any such solution would be an unlicensed deposit-taking arrangement. EMIs may be used only as an ancillary support for multi-currency card expenses, provided they partner with a Korean bank or hold client funds at a licensed institution, but they are not accepted as core treasury rail for capital verification, export settlements or tax remittance. Consequently the treasury must be structured exclusively through Tier-1 domestic commercial banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.0% top marginal national Corporate Tax rate under Article 55(1) of the Korean Corporate Tax Act (tax base above KRW 300 billion), plus a 2.4% local income tax surcharge (10% of national corporate tax) for aggregate top-bracket burden of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22.0% aggregate statutory withholding: 20.0% under Article 98 of the Corporate Tax Act plus a 2.0% local income tax surcharge (10% of the 20.0% national tax) on gross dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to non-resident corporate shareholders. A bilateral DTA can lower the rate (commonly to 5.0%-15.0%, e.g., 10.0% under many OECD-comparable DTAs for substantial shareholdings).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the final corporate tax return must be filed by March 31 of the following year; for other fiscal year-ends, within 3 months after the fiscal year-end. Preliminary corporate tax payments for the first half are due by August 31 and for the second half by November 30 of the taxable year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate international related-party transactions triggers contemporaneous Local File and Master File preparation; a multinational group with consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or more must also prepare a Country-by-Country Report. Entities below the transaction threshold are still required to disclose international transactions on the annual corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations registration system, but the National Tax Service applies a functional Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis and substance-over-form doctrine under Article 3 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and Article 14 of the Framework Act. The Korean Chusik Hoesa managing OOG heavy-haul projects must maintain a Korean board of directors, operate bank accounts and fleet/contract documentation in Korea, employ project engineers and commercial staff locally, and exercise decision-making in Korea; an entity without these functions will be disregarded as a shell and its profits reattributed to the foreign beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Willful failure to withhold and remit non-resident dividend withholding tax

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full 22.0% unpaid withholding tax plus a 40.0% fraudulent-omission penalty tax under Article 66 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus daily late-payment interest; the payor is treated as the solely responsible withholding agent and cannot shift the liability to the foreign shareholder.

Director personal liability: The representative director and board members who approved the distribution are jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax under Article 39 of the Framework Act; unpaid national tax above the serious-arrears threshold triggers travel blacklisting; criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act can result in imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to KRW 100 million.
Transfer-pricing mispricing and intentional omission of contemporaneous documentation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may re-determine the arm's-length consideration under Article 13 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, adjust taxable income, disallow unsubstantiated deductions, and impose penalty tax of 20.0% for negligent understatement or 40.0% for intentional/manipulated understatement, in addition to late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: A director who certifies false transactional documents or ignores a tax accountant's TP warning may be personally assessed for the resulting corporate tax under joint-liability provisions; continued false documentation can support an aggravated tax-evasion prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years and personal asset seizure.
Shell company operation and DTA/treaty abuse (economic substance failure)

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 14 of the Framework Act and Article 3 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the NTS will disregard the corporate existence for tax purposes, deny DTA benefits, reattribute Korean-source project income to the foreign beneficial owner, impose a 40.0% penalty tax on the understated income, and assess dividend withholding tax on the disguised distributions.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil only where the entity is a fraud or sham; directors become personally liable for corporate tax arrears, plus penalties; they also face civil damages to the tax authority, travel bans for unpaid liabilities, and criminal imprisonment terms up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 100 million under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act.

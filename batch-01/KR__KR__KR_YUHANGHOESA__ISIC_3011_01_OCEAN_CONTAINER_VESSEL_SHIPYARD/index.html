Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; the subscribers may fix capital at any amount, including KRW 1. However, for a commercial shipyard operating as ISIC 3011 and to satisfy the facility and financial-capacity examination of the Shipbuilding Industry Promotion Act and prudent foreign lenders, the Company should initially contribute at least KRW 300,000,000 (approximately USD 225,000). Foreign capital brought into Korea must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and may be held temporarily in a foreign-capital deposit account at a licensed foreign-exchange bank; once the company is registered, such funds must be transferred to the Company's corporate operating account and converted to Korean won as required. No government-administered blocked account is required for the Yuhan Hoesa itself, but bank escrow accounts should be established for ship-purchase maritime installment LCs and project-secured escrow payments.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Export declarations are made through Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNI-PASS electronic system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity: (1) determine whether a domestic business place exists in Korea; if none, appoint a statutory tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes. (2) Prepare certified copies of the foreign entity's registration certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of identity of the responsible officer, and a power of attorney naming the Korean tax representative. (3) Submit the application for business registration to the district tax office with jurisdiction over the tax representative or domestic business place, or via the HomeTax portal, including the VAT registration disclosure required for non-resident enterprises. (4) The NTS will issue the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the Korean Tax Identification Number for corporate income tax and VAT purposes. (5) If the foreign entity supplies goods or services in Korea without a fixed place of business, use the tax representative for all HomeTax filing, e-tax invoice issuance, and refund claims.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. All persons supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register as VAT entrepreneurs regardless of turnover. A special small-business simplified taxpayer regime applies to annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 solely for VAT calculation and filing purposes, not as a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies under Korean VAT law to goods exported from Korea, including ocean container vessels and dry cargo deep-sea commercial ships delivered to foreign buyers, provided the export is evidenced by a valid customs export declaration, the goods are physically or constructively delivered outside Korea, and the export sale is settled in foreign currency. Qualifying offshore services and international shipping or shipbuilding support services performed abroad may also be zero-rated where prescribed by the VAT Act and Enforcement Decree.

  • Customs Export Declaration (run through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS and confirming clearance of the vessel for export)
  • Bill of Lading or Vessel Delivery Certificate (evidencing export dispatch or constructive delivery of the vessel to the foreign consignee)
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate / Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing full repatriation of export proceeds

Electronic tax invoice issuance is mandatory for VAT entrepreneurs; e-tax invoices must be issued to the customer and transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice service within the statutory deadlines. Retailers and service providers in prescribed consumer sectors must operate cash receipts through the NTS cash receipt system. There is no country-specific anti-fraud digital stamping regime such as SII or RAF; fiscal control is achieved through mandatory e-reporting and NTS real-time data linkage.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If required export documentation, foreign currency receipt, or genuine export status is missing, the zero-rated output VAT is clawed back, 10% VAT is assessed retroactively on the export supply, and interest and additional penalties are imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act. Intentional submission of false export declarations, forged bills of lading, or fraudulent foreign exchange settlement certificates is punishable under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including criminal fines and imprisonment. Long-term maritime installment LC contracts should be supported by export proceeds collection schedules to avoid statutory penalty triggers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains free current-account convertibility for the KRW under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), but capital-account transactions are operated through a reporting and licensing regime, not through overt exchange restrictions. Current-account payments for trade, freight, insurance, and standard maritime services can be settled in KRW or foreign currency without prior approval if a licensed foreign exchange bank sees the supporting trade contract and customs declaration. Capital-account operations, including capital subscription by foreign shareholders in the shipbuilder, must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank; the Bank of Korea receives the record through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). No mandatory export-surrendering ratio applies to South Korean shipyards, but exported USD receipts must enter the banking system and can be held in domestic foreign-currency accounts before any KRW conversion at a licensed bank. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders requires audited financial statements proving distributable reserves, a corporate tax clearance certificate, and a disclosed FX purpose code; the bank will treat unresolved tax or undisclosed beneficial ownership as a withholding and AML obstacle. The net effect is high operational convertibility for genuine shipbuilding treasury flows, with non-resident, speculative, or anonymous access to KRW strictly curtailed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: six months from the date of export declaration under FETA Enforcement Decree; the exporter may use a licensed foreign exchange bank to extend the period when vessel milestones, classification certificates, or force majeure justify an extension. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; no fixed percentage is surrendered to the central bank. However, proceeds must be received through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank and reported into the FEIS promptly; retention of export proceeds in an unlicensed overseas wallet or EMI account will be treated as a violation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low for standalone treasury use. Korean FETA reserves foreign exchange business for banks licensed as foreign exchange banks. An offshore digital EMI not registered in Korea cannot receive and clear KRW, cannot become a correspondent to BOK-Wire+, and cannot lawfully hold mandatory custody of export proceeds for a domestic Korean LLC. Fintech products may be used only as a user interface over a licensed Korean sponsor bank, and even then the offshore EMI itself must not become a principal to the custody or settlement chain. A direct remittance into a non-Korean EMI before the Korean bank account would also undermine the Foreign Investment Promotion Act report and may be rejected by the Korean receiving bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign equity cash arriving into KR_YUHANGHOESA must be remitted to a licensed foreign exchange bank and to the segregated Capital Escrow Account or the Basic Operational Currency Account as designated in the FIPA report. The sender must issue SWIFT MT103 with the Korean shipyard legal entity name, corporate registration number, the exact purpose code for acquisition of equity in a domestic Korean company, and the beneficiary bank name. The Korean receiving bank completes the Foreign Exchange Information System filing, matches the remittance to the FIPA registration amount, confirms the identity of the foreign investor, and after final AML clearance issues the foreign-investment certificate used for capital verification. The shipyard auditor must prepare the paid-in capital verification, and the funds are released for shipbuilding procurement in KRW or foreign currency only after such verification. The remittance must never be structured into multiple sub-threshold wires, as Korean banks are mandated to report attempted structuring.Procurement of hull steel, engines, marine electronics, subcontract labor, and engineering services from foreign counterparties will be cleared through a licensed Korean bank using the following rails: direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for confirmed invoices under USD 5 million; standby and commercial import LCs (MT700 and MT710) for engine sets, steel slab and plate, and long-lead equipment; and documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) for standardized low-risk marine equipment. The bank will apply a Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code on each disbursement, requiring match between invoice, UNI-PASS declaration, and freight receipt, and will not execute a payment to an offshore EMI or shell correspondent if sanctions screening of the beneficial owner fails. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion for mandatory Master File and Local File on combined international related-party transactions; KRW 5 billion for the simplified Local File in certain defined service, interest, and IP categories; Country-by-Country Report threshold is consolidated group revenue of KRW 1 trillion

Economic substance scope: Korea does not statute the Gulf-regime Economic Substance Regulations or the exact Core Income-Generating Activities label, but its substantive doctrines under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and the Corporate Tax Act require that the shipyard conduct and direct the core business activities - vessel design, procurement, engineering, construction, supervision, and risk assumption - within Korea. A Yuhan Hoesa that merely books profit while its board and all key decision-making occur abroad will be disregarded, with income reattributed to the actual value-creating entity. Equivalent CIGA principles are applied in treaty claims to ensure the recipient is not a conduit, and shell entities without employees, premises, or business risk are denied DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3(1) and Article 8 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, tax evasion by fraudulent or active concealment is punishable by imprisonment for up to ten years or a fine of up to five times the evaded tax; the National Tax Service also imposes a 20-30% penalty surcharge on the understated tax amount. Intentional underreporting above KRW 50 million is automatically referred to the prosecution service.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may disregard the limited liability structure where the company is a sham or the director knowingly organizes a façade; the director becomes jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax and surcharges. The director may be added as a secondary taxpayer under the National Tax Collection Act, blacklisted as an illegal tax evader, subjected to a travel ban restricting departure from South Korea, and criminally sentenced to imprisonment for up to ten years with possible asset confiscation and restitution of the evaded tax.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Shell Conduit

Statutory sanction: Articles 4 and 5 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs authorize the National Tax Service to reconstruct the transaction, impute taxable income to the Korean Yuhan Hoesa, and impose a penalty of 20% (or 30% for reckless disregard) on the corrected tax base; the same facts may trigger denial of treaty benefits and foreign exchange controls.

Director personal liability: The director who executes the intercompany agreements with knowledge of the sham is considered a de facto or shadow director under the Commercial Act, subject to civil damages for the company’s tax losses. If the company lacks assets to pay the additional tax, the director can be held personally liable under the National Tax Collection Act; repeated or serious manipulation leads to criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and an entry ban or travel blacklist.
Withholding Tax Default and Fraudulent DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: The Corporate Tax Act and the Local Income Tax Act assess the unpaid withholding tax, a 30% penalty for willful failure to withhold (50% in the case of fraudulent failure), and a 10% additional charge for false DTA claims. Improper use of a treaty to escape Korean tax is prosecuted as tax evasion, with fines and imprisonment available under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Withholding and remittance is an officer-level fiduciary duty; the representative director is personally liable for any withheld-not-remitted tax and penalties. A director who procures or participates in a fraudulent TRC claim may face asset restitution of the under-withheld amount, a travel ban (blacklisting) on grounds of outstanding tax delinquency, disqualification as a corporate director, and criminal imprisonment where severity warrants.

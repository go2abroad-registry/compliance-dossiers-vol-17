Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the Hometax electronic web portal at hometax.go.kr for registration, electronic tax invoices, and declarations; customs-side export controls are handled by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

        
E-government filing portal
The foreign non-resident founders establishing the Korean Chusik Hoesa must first create a domestic business place in Korea for the entity, then file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean address. The filing set comprises the foreign parent or founder's certificate of incorporation and foreign registration certificate, Korean or English notarized and apostilled translations, the Korean entity's articles of incorporation, director identification, power of attorney for the local fiscal representative, proof of lease or ownership of the domestic place of business, and a bank account certificate for the Korean entity's foreign-currency receipts. Once NTS accepts the application, the entity receives a single ten-digit Business Registration Number that serves simultaneously as the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number, the VAT registration number, and the withholding agent number. Foreign non-resident digital suppliers that have no Korean fixed establishment may, in limited cases, use the streamlined Hometax foreign non-resident registration path; however, for this reverse-logistics operation the ordinary domestic business registration path is mandatory, and any subsequent address or representative changes must be updated through Hometax within the statutory filing deadlines.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The foreign non-resident founders establishing the Korean Chusik Hoesa must first create a domestic business place in Korea for the entity, then file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean address. The filing set comprises the foreign parent or founder's certificate of incorporation and foreign registration certificate, Korean or English notarized and apostilled translations, the Korean entity's articles of incorporation, director identification, power of attorney for the local fiscal representative, proof of lease or ownership of the domestic place of business, and a bank account certificate for the Korean entity's foreign-currency receipts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. A Chusik Hoesa cannot elect pass-through treatment; Korean corporate income tax is levied at the entity level. A qualifying controlling resident corporation may, however, elect consolidated corporate income tax filing with its wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries under the Corporate Tax Act, and affiliated businesses may apply to the NTS for VAT group registration; neither election alters the entity-level classification of this Corporation.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
There is no monetary de minimis threshold for a stock corporation; the Chusik Hoesa is required to be VAT-registered from the commencement of any taxable business activity in Korea. A simplified taxpayer regime covers only certain individually-operated micro-businesses with annual taxable revenue below KRW 80,000,000, and it is not available to ordinary corporations; therefore this entity must obtain full VAT registration.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods supplied by the Korean entity to a foreign buyer are zero-rated when the Korea Customs Service accepts an export declaration and the goods legally leave Korea, provided the exporter receives the foreign-currency sales proceeds within 180 days. Cross-border reverse logistics and returned-goods quality-grading services supplied for a foreign principal or an offshore e-commerce seller are zero-rated as foreign-currency-earning services when the recipient is a non-resident, the services are used outside Korea, the services are separately invoiced to the non-resident counterparty, and the consideration is repatriated to Korea in foreign currency as evidenced by a foreign exchange receipt certificate. In both cases the zero-rating fails if the substantiating documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or involves circular fund flows through affiliated accounts without actual cross-border foreign exchange.

A registered business with VAT taxable turnover exceeding KRW 80,000,000 must issue, through the Hometax e-Tax Invoice System or an NTS-approved electronic data interchange or API service provider, an electronic tax invoice for each taxable supply. The e-invoice data is transmitted to the NTS no later than the day following the tax invoice issue date; the NTS performs real-time format validation and assigns a unique electronic approval number, thereby creating a digital audit trail. No separate anti-fraud fiscal stamp is imposed, but the NTS system automatically compares e-invoice issuance with the filed VAT return and can pre-flag unexplained discrepancies.

      
The NTS may disallow the 0% rate and retroactively assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the unsubstantiated export, together with statutory additional taxes for under-reporting and non-issuance of electronic tax invoices, plus monthly late-payment surcharges. The statutory limitation period for VAT assessment is generally five years from the filing date, extended to ten years for cases involving fraudulent evasion or deliberately false documentation. Fraudulent or grossly negligent zero-rating claims are also subject to the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, which may impose criminal imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of not less than five times the evaded tax amount, in addition to civil penalties, forfeiture of offsetting input VAT, and disqualification of refund claims for the tax year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates a notification-centred rather than permission-based foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible for current account items such as trade settlements, service fees, refunds, and dividends, provided that an authorised foreign exchange bank obtains the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration or certificate evidencing the payment. Capital account transactions, including an inbound capital contribution from a foreign shareholder, are generally permitted but must be reported through FEIS and in some cases require a certificate of foreign investment from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy. Dividend remittance out of audited profits is recognised as a current account transfer and can be processed without a separate exchange licence, but banks will require board minutes, audited financial statements and tax clearance to demonstrate that withholding taxes have been settled. Macroprudential measures such as the leveraged FX derivative ceiling can temporarily tighten daily liquidity, yet they do not delay ordinary reverse logistics refund flows. For export-related receipts in reverse logistics, no mandatory surrender of foreign currency to the won is imposed; exporters may hold proceeds in resident foreign currency accounts at domestic banks.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds for goods must be collected within one year from the actual export date, while service-related proceeds are generally collected within one year from service completion. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a resident foreign currency deposit account at an authorised Korean bank or convert to KRW at the prevailing market rate. If converted, the bank must report the transaction to FEIS using the underlying contract or invoice. Extension of the collection period can be obtained for delayed receivables caused by long-term construction, shipping disputes, overseas claims, or other justification approved by the foreign exchange bank.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) - the central foreign exchange regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, coordinating with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Financial Supervisory Service, and the Korea Customs Service through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Low feasibility as a primary account. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex are not authorised by the Korean Banking Act to hold corporate deposit accounts or act as clearing institutions in South Korea. They are not domestic banks and cannot provide a statutory escrow, trade settlement, or FEIS reporting account. Offshore EMIs may connect to Korean banks through white-labelled local banking partners for execution of cross-border payments, but the operating entity must maintain a Tier-1 Korean bank account as the legal account for capital escrow, KRW operations, FX reporting, and trade settlement. For this reverse logistics Chusik Hoesa, Airwallex can be used only as a supplementary execution layer after the domestic banking relationship is fully established.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Non-resident shareholders should remit equity capital via SWIFT from their corporate bank account to the Chusik Hoesa capital escrow or paid-in capital account at a Tier-1 Korean bank. The remittance message must clearly state the investment nature and the corresponding FEIS transaction purpose code. If the payment is in USD or another foreign currency, the Korean bank will execute the conversion to KRW at the market rate using the share subscription agreement or investment contract. The bank then issues a capital verification certificate which the company files with the court for incorporation or with the registry for a capital increase. If the structure qualifies as foreign investment, a report must be filed with KOTRA or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the subsequent foreign investment registration certificate should be retained in the bank file so that future dividend remittances and foreign exchange conversions are not blocked. All capital and service flows must be supported by the underlying reverse logistics commercial contract or order to avoid false-invoice classification.Domestic merchant refunds and logistics vendor payments should be cleared through the KFTC local ACH rail using the company KRW current account. Cross-border supplier payments, refunds to overseas merchants, and quality-grading fees must be executed through SWIFT MT103 transmissions using the corresponding FEIS trade or service purpose code, supported by credit notes, inspection certificates, original order numbers, and customs declarations when goods physically cross the border. For large return consignments and restocking deliveries, a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection is recommended, with bill of lading, packing list, and inspection report attached. The bank will expect an internally generated reconciliation schedule matching outbound payments to actual reverse logistics events and may require standing instruction limits for repetitive refund disbursements to reduce false-positive screening friction.Underlying-backed deliverable forward contracts (USD/KRW) for recognised trade receivables and payables arising from reverse logistics service fees, freight costs and returned-goods settlements, with maturity aligned to the contractual collection period and subject to bank credit approval and FEIS reporting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only as a hedging instrument where the entity has genuine KRW/USD trade exposure and the bank documents the underlying transaction; speculative NDF positions by a non-financial corporate treasury are not permitted under Korean FX regulations.Foreign exchange swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps can be used only to hedge long-term foreign shareholder loans or investment payables, with a documented debt-service schedule, confirmed drawdown, and FSS reporting; speculative short-term swaps are not permitted.

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies and its Enforcement Decree, a Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily subject to an independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit if its total assets as of the previous fiscal year-end equal or exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9,000,000). Companies whose securities are listed or traded on the Korea Exchange (KOSPI or KOSDAQ) are always subject to external audit regardless of asset size. A company may also be subject to a designated audit or compulsory external audit when ordered by the Financial Services Commission for public interest, credibility of accounting, or investor protection reasons. There is no independent headcount-based audit trigger under Korean law; the primary quantitative trigger is total assets, with secondary FSS designation powers for revenue and corporate-government-risk cases. A company that is not required to have an external audit may still be audited voluntarily, but the audited financial statements will be accepted only if prepared by a licensed accounting firm registered with the FSS.

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the registered head office of a Korean medical device manufacturer and exporter in ISIC 3250. The Korean Commercial Act and corporate registration law require a real, identifiable principal office. A serviced office with dedicated meeting space and signage may be acceptable for administrative functions only, but the declared manufacturing scope requires physical production, storage, quality-control, and related records at the registered address.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs and export controls through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity engaged in taxable supplies in Korea must obtain a Business Registration Number (BRN) for VAT and withholding tax purposes. The procedure: (1) appoint a tax representative in Korea if the entity has no domestic place of business, or establish a domestic place of business with a responsible manager; (2) file an application for business registration (including a signed Power of Attorney for the tax representative) with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the place of business or the representative's address; (3) file through HomeTax (electronic) or in person, attaching the applicant's foreign corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, and proof of the non-resident identity; (4) the NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate with a unique BRN, which serves as the VAT registration number; (5) the registration must be updated in case of changes. No minimum capital. The procedure generally completes within 3-5 business days.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None. South Korea does not provide a 'check-the-box' or hybrid entity election for Yuhan Hoesa (LLC). VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (Zero KRW threshold). Any person, including a non-resident, who supplies taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT irrespective of turnover. Small businesses with annual turnover below KRW 48 million may use the simplified taxation regime, but they are still registered and issue tax invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is applied to: (a) export of goods — title and possession must transfer to a foreign buyer, and the goods must leave Korea; (b) export of services — services rendered wholly outside Korea or to a foreign customer under conditions defining overseas supply; (c) other qualifying transactions such as international transport. The exporter must substantiate that the buyer is a non-resident, that the goods/services are physically or contractually exported, and that foreign currency payment is received unless exempted.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate issued by KCS via UNI-PASS)
  • Commercial Invoice detailing the medical diagnostic consoles and value
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Airway Waybill) evidencing physical export
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certification or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean bank for the export proceeds
  • Copy of the export contract or purchase order, and Packing List

Mandatory Electronic Tax Invoices (e-invoices). All VAT-registered businesses must issue e-invoices through HomeTax or approved ASPs and transmit them to the NTS by the day following the issue date. The system supports automatic data matching and anti-fraud analytics. There is no separate real-time physical 'fiscal stamp'; however, the NTS conducts real-time monitoring of e-invoice issuance and uses digital risk algorithms to flag non-filing. Cash receipts are also mandatory for all credit/debit card retail transactions. Failure to transmit e-invoices in time triggers penalties of 2% of the invoice value.

The NTS and KCS cross-verify export declarations against e-invoices, bank settlement records, and customs clearance data. Zero-rating claims must be fully documented; absence of proof leads to immediate VAT assessment at 10% plus additional tax. Penalties include: (a) 40% of the fraudulent or unjustly refunded amount under the Framework Act on National Taxes for unlawful refund claims; (b) negligence penalty of 10% for insufficient documentation; (c) criminal liability for export fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 50 million. If export proceeds are not received within the mandatory 18-month repatriation period, the initial zero-rated treatment is retrospectively clawed back with interest for the intervening period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Trade-related settlements for medical imaging equipment exports and production imports are freely convertible through any licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions, including cross-border equity injections, shareholder loans, and dividend remittances, are subject to reporting with MOEF/BOK via the settlement bank; a Foreign Investment Report is required for foreign direct investment before an inward remittance is made. Export proceeds must be collected no later than 18 months from the shipment date, unless an extension is obtained under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korea no longer imposes a fixed mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency earnings; proceeds may be retained in eligible foreign currency accounts at a licensed foreign exchange bank or converted to KRW at the market rate. For profit remittance, the Yuhan Hoesa must first complete statutory book-closing, tax returns, and any required external audit, then submit to the bank a board resolution, share register, dividend distribution statement, and withheld tax payment certificate. Dividends are remittable once the corporate income tax and withholding tax certificates are issued, but banks will reject remittance unless the original FDI notification, capital verification trail, and shareholding documentation are intact. The principal hurdles are documentation completeness, shareholder identity verification, and tax certificate delays rather than legally fixed conversion quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated no later than 18 months from the date of shipment, unless extension is approved by the competent authority. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may either convert the foreign currency into Korean won through a licensed foreign exchange bank or hold the proceeds in an approved foreign currency account. The settlement bank must receive the export customs declaration, invoice, and proof of receipt to close the transaction under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reporting record.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the prudential central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) which administers the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and delegated FX regulations; the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) exercises AML surveillance authority over the banking corridor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers without a Korean banking license cannot engage in foreign exchange brokerage for Korean residents under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They cannot open local KRW settlement accounts, issue Korean export LC advices, complete Customs BOP declarations, or act as the capital-verification bank for an FDI report. Acceptable use is limited to ancillary non-Korean operating payments only after funds are lawfully remitted through a domestic licensed bank; they are not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail and are not recommended for this medical device exporting entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File; KRW 100 billion together with qualifying group revenue triggers Master File and Country-by-Country Report requirements under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs

Economic substance scope: Korea applies general anti-avoidance and substance-over-form doctrines, not a formal Economic Substance Regulations return. For a medical-device manufacturer, Core Income-Generating Activities include product design, component sourcing, manufacturing, quality control, risk assumption, and platform sales functions. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa with only a registered office, no factory floor staff, no inventory, and no board meetings can have its transactions disregarded and income reattributed to the real controlling non-resident or resident beneficial owner under LCITA and Corporate Tax Act anti-abuse provisions; import/export customs valuation and VAT rules similarly require physical substance to support zero-rated export claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 - imprisonment up to 2 years and/or fine up to twice the evaded tax; Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3 imposes 40% additional tax on fraudulent omission; NTS may also apply discharge of the corporate veil, deny NOL carryforward, and reassess all open years.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers are personally civilly liable for unpaid corporate tax and WHT under National Tax Collection Act Article 39 if corporate assets were dissipated; they face criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for large-scale patterned evasion, NTS travel ban, asset preservation order, and public taxpayer blacklisting.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act GAAR permits the NTS to disregard the shell and tax the economic owner; DTA benefits are refused on beneficial ownership grounds; the local registration may be challenged under the Commercial Act; administrative sanctions include cancellation of VAT zero-rating and imposition of 10% additional tax for False Certificate of Export.

Director personal liability: Directors who formed or operated the sham entity are jointly and severally liable for all corporate taxes, penalties, and unpaid WHT; courts have pierced the veil where creditors or tax authorities were misled; directors face criminal liability for aiding and abetting tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 5 years, and may be named in INTERPOL red notices or Korean departure bans when under prosecution.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: AML Act Article 15/16 imposes imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 50 million for non-reporting of suspicious transactions; KoFIU can impose administrative fines of up to KRW 100 million; export of medical devices without statutory license can trigger the Medical Devices Act sanctions, including business suspension.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal imprisonment, fines, confiscation of proceeds, and civil restitution for fraud toward counterparties; the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit and tax authorities share intelligence, so undeclared offshore receipts quickly trigger VAT/corporate tax assessments, joint personal liability under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act, and travel bans pending criminal trial.

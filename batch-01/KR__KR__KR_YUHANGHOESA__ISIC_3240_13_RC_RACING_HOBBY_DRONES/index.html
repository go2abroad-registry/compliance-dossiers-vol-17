Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Registration Division of the competent District Court (Beopwon-dung-gi-gwa). For a registered office in Seoul, the Seoul Central District Court Registration Office; for other locations, the District Court Registration Office of the local court having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000 as extensively amended), specifically Articles 287-2 to 287-74 governing the Limited Liability Company (Yuhan Hoesa); Article 289 (contents of articles of incorporation); Article 292 (notarization of articles); Article 293 (paid-in capital); Articles 542-2 to 542-13 (external audit thresholds for certain balance-sheet sizes); and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act for non-resident investors. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The incorporators may fix paid-in capital at any lawful amount, and no escrow or blocked account is mandated by statute. However, the full subscribed capital must be deposited into a domestic bank account held in the name of the company or the incorporators before the incorporation registration, and a certificate of capital deposit certified by the bank must be submitted to the court. For a carbon-fiber milling and FPV racing drone assembly company, paid-up capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 75,000) is commercially recommended to satisfy banking due diligence, supplier credit limits, factory lease underwriting, and foreign-invested enterprise registration with Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA), if applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Fighting Systems Installation Completion Certificate and periodic Fire Safety Inspection Certificate issued by the National Fire Agency; Civil Defense emergency preparedness and shelter assignment approval from the local civil defense authority; structural load inspection certificate by a licensed Korean architect for CNC milling machinery vibration and roof-mounted dust extraction equipment; and Occupational Safety and Health Act workplace safety approval covering dust explosion prevention, local exhaust ventilation, emergency exits, lithium battery fire suppression, and annual risk assessments. These clearances are statutory pre-operation conditions and must be maintained and renewed according to the applicable inspection intervals.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered through the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr). Export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa (Korean LLC), the registration procedure begins with judicial/company registration under the Korean Commercial Act at the competent court registry. After registration and before commencing taxable commercial activity, the company must file the statutory Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its registered domestic place of business. The application must be accompanied by the certified corporate registry extract, articles of incorporation, shareholder and beneficial owner identification, proof of the domestic place of business, representative identification, and, where applicable, a foreign-investment report filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The NTS then verifies the business establishment and issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the VAT registration number. The company must subsequently register for the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system. For a non-resident foreign entity with Korean VAT obligations but no domestic permanent establishment, a separate non-resident tax registration procedure applies, requiring appointment of a Korean tax representative and registration with the NTS under the relevant provisions of the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or a pass-through entity election for Yuhan Hoesa companies. A Korean LLC is mandatorily treated as a separate entity-level taxpayer. No hybrid-mismatch entity classification election exists under Korean corporate income tax law. The only related elective regime is the consolidated corporate tax return election available to a parent corporation with a wholly-owned domestic subsidiary group, which does not alter the separate taxable status of the LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No corporate turnover threshold applies to a Yuhan Hoesa company. A Korean corporation is a general VAT taxpayer and must register as soon as it commences business, regardless of projected revenue, because the simplified taxable-person regime is available only to certain individual businesses whose annual taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 80,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, zero rating for exports of goods applies when the FPV racing drones are sold to a non-resident buyer, the goods are physically exported from Korea under a customs export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, ownership and risk pass to the foreign buyer, and the export proceeds are settled in foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Qualifying offshore services, including certain digital supplies delivered and used outside Korea, may also be zero rated where the Korean VAT place-of-supply rules place the supply abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, including the export declaration number and export clearance certificate
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing shipment to the non-resident buyer and foreign destination port
  • Commercial Invoice containing the zero-rated export notation and product description for FPV drones
  • Packing List
  • Export sales contract or irrevocable purchase order from the non-resident buyer evidencing the foreign trade transaction
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), or functional equivalent issued under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act evidencing actual repatriation of export proceeds
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through the NTS HomeTax system with the export declaration reference recorded in the prescribed zero-rating field

All VAT invoices must be issued as electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or through NTS-certified third-party software integrated via the official HomeTax API/EDI interface with an NTS-issued digital authentication certificate. Invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the date of supply. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance fiscal-stamping model; instead, anti-fraud controls rely on centralized electronic invoice matching, mandatory linkage between export declarations, VAT returns, and customs clearance data, plus NTS risk-based real-time audit selection.

The NTS systematically cross-matches electronic tax invoice data, Korea Customs Service export declarations, transportation records, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If any mandatory document is missing or inconsistent, the claimed zero rating is rejected and the supply is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply, resulting in assessment of VAT at 10% plus statutory interest and surtaxes. Deliberate or dishonest export zero-rating claims are subject to additional penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, including surcharges up to 40% of the understated tax for fraudulent reporting, mandatory clawback of any refunded VAT with interest, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act for serious cases of false refund fraud. The ordinary statutory statute of limitations for VAT assessment is generally five years from the relevant filing deadline, with extended time limits for fraudulent or non-filed returns.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalised current account while maintaining a controlled yet investment-friendly capital account. The Korean won is freely convertible for underlying trade, dividends, profits, and service-related inflows/outflows; however, all cross-border transfers exceeding USD 50,000 equivalent must be routed through a domestic foreign exchange bank and a supporting statutory declaration (e.g., Form FD-TR) may be required. Capital account convertibility requires prior registration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Bank of Korea for equity investment, loans, and financial derivatives. Non-resident shareholders may remit after-tax profits without incremental approval provided the capital was legally imported and the entity complied with quarterly corporate tax filings. A foreign investor establishing a Yuhan Hoesa must file a 'Foreign Direct Investment Report' with MOEF (via a local bank) before injecting share capital in order to secure the tax benefits and ensure outward remittance rights. In practice, the bank scrutinizes high volumes of same-day in/out flows and can defer conversion if suspicious. Surrender requirements for export proceeds were abolished in 2020, but exporters are required to report inward receipts to an authorised bank within 15 business days and settle records with the Korea Customs Service. Overall, convertibility is high for ordinary trade but subject to documentary evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 6-3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received within six (6) months from the date of export declaration; an extension may be granted by the foreign exchange bank for cause. The exporter has no statutory obligation to convert proceeds into KRW; proceeds may be held in a domestic foreign currency account (FE-account) or sold at the prevailing market rate. However, any receipt must be brought into the Korean banking system within fifteen (15) business days of the export invoice settlement date and recorded in an FX monitoring statement. If the company does not repatriate within the six-month limit, it must file an explanatory report with the designated bank and the Korea Customs Service to avoid a foreign-exchange violation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Low. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

National Corporate Tax Rate (KR): progressive brackets of 9% (up to KRW 200m), 19% (KRW 200m-20bn), 21% (KRW 20bn-30bn), 24% (above KRW 30bn), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax assessed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local resident surtax, resulting in a combined statutory rate of 22% on dividends paid by a Korean Yuhan Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder (Article 98 of the Corporate Tax Act controlling the national withholding aspect; local surtax is imposed under the Local Tax Act).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File requirement: where the Korean entity's applied transactions (purchases, sales, royalties, loans) with foreign related parties exceed KRW 50 billion in the fiscal year; Master File requirement: where the multinational enterprise group has consolidated annual revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion and the Korean entity's foreign related-party transaction volume exceeds KRW 50 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies no separate Economic Substance Regulation for domestic companies, but the body of law combines the place-of-effective-management rule (a foreign company managed in Korea is Korean resident), the Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) regime, and the general principle that transfer pricing arrangements must reflect actual functions, assets, and risks. For an FPV drone trading vehicle, substance is evidenced by board meetings in Korea, operational control of inventory, contract negotiation, marketing decisions, and accountable service cash flows; a paper-only Korean distributor (particularly a holding in a low-tax regime) may be recharacterized as agent or deemed holding company and taxed accordingly under the Law for International Tax Adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and Article 108 of the Corporate Tax Act, the tax authority imposes a fraudulent underreporting penalty equal to 40% of the evaded national tax (plus local surcharge 10%), daily interest surcharge at 0.022%, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine equal to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Korean LLC is a sham: directors become jointly and severally liable for the tax debts and penalties; travel ban (blacklist) is automatically issued during criminal investigation; upon conviction the individual director faces civil restitution to the National Tax Service, disqualification from serving as a corporate officer for a term, and actual imprisonment for up to 3 years (aggravated to up to 10 years under the Special Act for the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes where tax evaded exceeds KRW 300 million).
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company Interposition

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authority will deny treaty benefits based on the beneficial owner and principal purpose tests under the OECD-conforming rules (Law for International Tax Adjustment, Article 2-1 and Article 6). The deficiency becomes the full 22% withholding tax plus a 40% false-claim penalty; interest and charges accrue at 0.022% daily; foreign exchange controls may be applied to cross-border transfers. The penal consequences for the Korean payer include liability for uncollected WHT as withholding agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the intercompany financing, royalty, or dividend resolution are treated as responsible withholding officers; they can be personally assessed for unremitted tax, subjected to mandatory travel ban during prosecution, and prosecuted for aiding and abetting tax evasion, carrying imprisonment of 1-5 years and a fine of up to KRW 200 million (under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 18). Civil liability extends to restoring funds transferred out of the Korean entity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 12 of the Law for International Tax Adjustment, failure to submit transfer pricing documentation within the request deadline triggers a KRW 50 million per-document penalty and allows the tax authority to estimate taxable income using its own benchmark methods; under-assessed tax will bear a 10% gross negligence surcharge (20% for omission without based-on-review justification) plus 0.022% daily delay interest. The National Tax Service may also impose a secondary adjustment and deem additional dividends to the foreign shareholder, subject to 22% withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for accounting and tax management may be held civilly liable for company losses caused by the fine; deliberate destruction or concealment of exchange-control and manufacturing cost records invokes criminal liability under Article 15-2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, leading to imprisonment of up to 3 years, and a personal ban from travel overseas during any pending criminal and extradition procedures.

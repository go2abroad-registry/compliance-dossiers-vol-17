Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Division of the District Court (or Local Court Support) having jurisdiction over the registered office in the Republic of Korea; for a Seoul based entity, the Commercial Registration Division of the Seoul Central District Court.

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, as amended), particularly Articles 288, 289, 291, 295, 312, 317, 318, 337 and 393; together with Articles 1 to 10 of the Framework on Non-Contentious Litigation Procedure for court registration filings; professional legal service practice additionally governed by the Attorney-at-Law Act.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, all subscribed shares must be fully paid in prior to the incorporation registration filing, and the bank's capital payment certificate must be issued from the capital subscription account. There is no blocked escrow account requirement for an ordinary commercial company; after registration, the paid-in capital may be used for corporate purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the company automatically becomes a domestic resident corporation upon incorporation and must complete VAT Business Registration (Value-Added Tax Act) and Corporate Tax registration at the district NTS office having jurisdiction over the registered office. File the Business Registration Application through the HomeTax portal or in person within 20 days of business commencement, together with the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership evidence of the Korean principal place of business, representative identification, and corporate seal registration certificate. The NTS then issues a 10-digit Tax Registration Number / VAT Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID) used for corporate tax, VAT, withholding, and customs declarations. A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean permanent establishment or branch must file a Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration under the Corporate Tax Act and a parallel VAT Business Registration; required supporting documents include the foreign company registration certificate or equivalent, apostilled certificate of incorporation, board resolution authorizing the Korean branch, power of attorney, branch manager identification, and evidence of the Korean fixed place of business. If no Korean permanent establishment or fixed place of business exists, the foreign non-resident entity is generally not required to obtain a Korean VAT ID for outward-bound cross-border services; any Korean-sourced B2B service VAT may be handled by reverse charge by the Korean recipient.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory KRW turnover threshold under the Value-Added Tax Act; any business operator is mandatorily required to register for VAT from the commencement of business. Foreign non-resident entities with no Korean fixed place of business are not required to register for Korean VAT solely for outward cross-border services unless they create a taxable establishment or are otherwise required for specific imported service rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods and qualifying exported services are zero-rated. Goods must be physically exported from the Republic of Korea and supported by a customs export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service. Services, including cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration advisory, and asset tracing services supplied by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a foreign non-resident client, are zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident with no Korean business establishment and the service is properly characterized as an exported service, the consideration is paid in foreign currency, and the service is actually delivered to the offshore client with evidence of foreign exchange proceeds repatriated to Korea. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to foreign non-resident consumers are also zero-rated when the foreign-exchange and non-residency documentation requirements are met, and such zero-rating gives the Korean supplier the right to reclaim related input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Receipt) accepted by Korea Customs Service, required only for physical goods or ancillary physical evidence shipments, filed through the UNI-PASS electronic customs portal
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, where physical goods or ancillary litigation exhibits are exported
  • Signed cross-border legal services agreement / client engagement letter evidencing the foreign non-resident recipient and the offshore nature of the services
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT tax invoice issued under the Value-Added Tax Act, carrying the zero-rated export supply indicator required by NTS electronic tax invoice data standards
  • Foreign Exchange Purchase/Repatriation Certificate or equivalent inward remittance certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, or a bank statement/foreign exchange registration record evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency
  • Copy of the foreign client's certificate of incorporation, permanent establishment non-residency evidence, or equivalent legal existence document
  • Evidence of service performance and offshore beneficial enjoyment, including engagement scope, work files, time records, arbitration or court filings, asset tracing reports, and electronic communications proving delivery outside Korea

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all business-to-business supplies under the Value-Added Tax Act. Issuers must transmit electronic tax invoice data to the National Tax Service through HomeTax or an NTS-approved EDI service provider; transmission must occur no later than the day following issuance. Each transmitted electronic tax invoice receives a unique NTS electronic invoice serial number for anti-fraud matching. There is no pre-clearance fiscal device blocking or physical digital stamping mandate; the real-time NTS electronic invoice database, automated matching with customs and foreign exchange data, and mandatory serial number system supply the anti-fraud control.

The National Tax Service may audit every zero-rated export claim by comparing VAT electronic tax invoice data, Korea Customs Service export declarations (for goods), service contracts, client non-residency evidence, and foreign exchange bank records of inward remittance within the statutory deadline. Where a zero-rated export is not fully substantiated or is found fraudulent, the NTS will reclassify the supply as a domestic taxable supply, assess VAT at 10% on the consideration, deny or claw back any input VAT refunds already released, and impose additional taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act, including penalties for false or dishonest tax returns (up to 40% of the evaded VAT for fraudulent non-reporting or underreporting) and late-payment interest under the National Tax Collection Act. Criminal prosecution is possible under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act for intentional VAT evasion, carrying imprisonment up to five years and fines up to three times the tax evaded; the statutory assessment limitation period is extended to ten years for egregious fraudulent evasion, and retroactive clawback can therefore reach all VAT and penalties for the full ten-year window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a market-determined floating exchange rate with current account convertibility broadly free for trade, services, and ordinary business transactions. Capital account transactions are largely liberalized but remain subject to a declaration and filing regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree. Non-resident investors may freely remit dividends and profits, provided the overseas remittance is supported by tax payment certificates (e.g., corporate tax withholding certificate) and foreign exchange purchase evidence. Surrender requirements are minimal: exporters are permitted to retain foreign currency proceeds in domestic foreign currency accounts without mandatory conversion into KRW. However, banks must verify the source and underlying trade documentation to assign the correct Foreign Exchange Transaction Classification (e.g., code 600200 for trade receipts). Certain cross-border capital flows, especially when exceeding USD 100,000, may trigger reporting obligations to BOK/MOEF or require submission through the Customs declaration system. Overall, the regime is not a restrictive exchange-control system; it is better classified as a liberalized but prudentially monitored framework with emphasis on anti-money laundering, beneficial ownership disclosure, and tax compliance for profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within the payment period stipulated in the trade contract (typically 120 days from the date of shipment, extendable under certain circumstances). There is no mandatory surrender ratio: all foreign currency export earnings may be credited to a domestic foreign currency account and retained indefinitely, or converted into KRW at market rates without requiring a specific conversion percentage. Banks must report each outgoing/incoming receipt in the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) via the Korea Customs Service or the banking network, assigning the applicable transaction code. Failure to repatriate within the contractual terms, or to submit the required export declaration (Cargo Clearance and Export Declaration), may trigger administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act administered in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not formally licensed as banks or electronic financial companies in South Korea under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. While they can offer multi-currency wallets and payment services to Korean entities through partnerships with local licensed banks, they cannot act as the primary settlement account for statutory capital verification, IOLTA-compliant trust accounting, or tax registrations with the National Tax Service. Their viability as a complementary rail is 'Low' for official treasury functions, but acceptable for operational spending where the funds are routed through a domestic partner bank. Korean regulators require every entity to maintain a domestic bank account for corporate seal-based transactions and tax filings; offshore EMIs generally fail this requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or litigation-war chest funds must be wired from the non-resident investor via SWIFT MT103 to the domestic KRW account or foreign currency account at the chosen Korean bank. The remitting bank must be instructed to include the purpose code '400100' (direct investment) or '400200' (indirect investment) as defined by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulation for capital receipts; for client funds held in trust, use code '410500' or appropriate service receipt code. The receiving Korean bank will complete a Korea Customs Service FEIS report within 1 business day and request the underlying certificate of incorporation, board resolution approving the investment, and for investments exceeding KRW 100 million, a notarized share subscription agreement. Capital verification (capital verification for paid-in capital) is performed by the external auditor (Samjung KPMG etc.) and recorded with the registry court; the bank will issue a 'Certificate of Foreign Currency Depository Balance' upon request.Outbound payments for settlement, expert compensation, court filings, or litigation expenses are executed by SWIFT MT103 (international wire), domestic wire (K-Fast) for KRW, or, if the counterparty opens a letter of credit, a Commercial LC will be issued through the bank's trade desk. For recurring fees, an aggregated netting protocol with monthly payment instructions is recommended. Documentary collections are permissible for trade-related disbursements only; legal services usually settle against invoices via MT103 with supporting legal fee invoices and counterparty tax registration numbers. Standard purpose codes for outgoing service remittances are '300900' (legal services) or '300010'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax rate (progressive: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax, producing an aggregate headline rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate withholding when a Korean Chusik Hoesa pays a dividend to a non-resident shareholder: 20% national income tax under Article 125(1) of the Income Tax Act plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% of the national tax); this aggregate may be reduced under an applicable Double Tax Agreement, typically to 15% on portfolio dividends and 10% on dividends received by a treaty-partner corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting capital; no additional Korean corporate tax is levied on the distributing corporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under Article 6-2 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs arises when a Korean domestic corporation has annual gross revenue of KRW 100 billion or more and cross-border transaction values with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year; a fuller Master File obligation applies where the intercompany transaction amount exceeds KRW 50 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is for a multinational group with consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more; for lower-turnover litigation chambers, contemporaneous TP documentation is still strongly recommended where foreign affiliated lawyer bills are deducted, and the NTS can impose penalties of up to 30% of the understated tax for failure to produce documentation within 60 days.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 17-2 contains a strong general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and Korea's tax courts apply 'substance over form' extensively; in cross-border commercial litigation and asset tracing, the Korean Chusik Hoesa must prove that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed in Korea - key attorney decisions, client acquisition and retainership management, arbitration strategy formulation, risk assessment, settlement authority, and international execution of foreign judgments - otherwise the NTS may reattribute profits to a foreign principal, disregard a sham foreign branch, or deny treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test. Expected records include fee earning schedules mapping each foreign collection matter to specific Korean case managers and supporting core activity documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-2 and 47-3 impose penalty taxes of 30% of the evaded amount plus daily delinquency surcharges; Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 punishes tax evasion with imprisonment for up to 10 years and a fine of not less than 2 times and not more than 5 times the amount of tax evaded; the NTS may treat the transaction as a sham and apply the substance-over-form doctrine to disregard the shell intermediary.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly approved the fraudulent return is subject to personal criminal prosecution as a joint principal; Korean courts frequently pierce the corporate veil under the abuse of corporate personality doctrine to hold directors civilly liable for the entity's unpaid taxes and related damages; the NTS can impose a travel ban (blacklisting) on the director under the immigration enforcement rules and seize personal bank accounts, foreign real estate, and trust distributions up to the amount fraudulently removed; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment of up to 10 years plus a fine and disqualification from serving as a director of any Korean company.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Failure to Maintain TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA) Article 8, the NTS may reallocate income by its own arm's-length method and impose an additional tax penalty of up to 30% of the understated corporate tax; Article 13(3) provides a penalty for failure to submit TP documentation of up to 10 million KRW or 5% of the transaction amount; for severe manipulation, the GAAR provisions in the Framework Act may disallow loss deductions and recharacterise the foreign entity as a sham; the statutory daily surcharge continues for each day of non-payment.

Director personal liability: The director who signatory to the non-arm's-length contract becomes a specially liable person; Korean insolvency jurisprudence allows the court to pierce the veil if the director intentionally depleted the corporation to the detriment of the tax authority; the director may be personally ordered to make restitution of the transferred fee amount plus statutory interest; a tax-driven travel ban can be imposed, and if the NTS proves a criminal design the director faces imprisonment of up to 5 years for violation of LCITA, in conjunction with the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.
AML/CFT Failure and Misuse of Client Trust (IOLTA) Accounts

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information (Korea AML Act) Article 12, failure to file a suspicious transaction report is subject to a criminal fine of up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment for up to 5 years; Article 11 imposes imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 30 million on legal practitioners who abet money laundering through professional services; the Financial Information and Analysis (FIU) may impose a corrective order, revocation of the attorney-at-law license, and blacklist the firm from accepting further client transfers; aggravated penalty provisions can double the sanction if the funds are traced to organised crime or foreign bribery.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for knowingly structuring the misuse of the client trust account; Korean criminal courts have pierced the corporate veil to force director-level asset confiscation, and the FIU coordinates with the Prosecutor's Office to obtain travel bans and freezing injunctions on personal and corporate assets; a director convicted of AML facilitation faces imprisonment of up to 5 years, a personal fine, civil disgorgement of all trust funds diverted, and permanent disqualification from practising as a litigation counsel in Korea.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act; however, all subscribed shares must be fully paid into a designated capital deposit escrow account maintained by a licensed commercial bank or securities company before incorporation registration under Article 295. The deposited funds are blocked from withdrawal until the company receives its certificate of incorporation registration. A paid-in capital of at least KRW 10,000,000 is practically recommended for corporate bank account opening, Amazon seller verification, and cross-border payment processing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), Government Complex-Sejong; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs clearance counterpart: Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate from the competent Korean District Tax Office or online through HomeTax by filing a business registration application. Required attachments include: the foreign corporation's certificate of incorporation, articles of association, evidence of a registered Korean place of business or fixed establishment (including an Amazon FBA dedicated point of presence), identity documentation of the legal representative and/or resident tax agent with power of attorney, and a completed non-resident taxpayer registration form. Upon approval the NTS assigns a 10-digit registered business number which serves simultaneously as the Korean Tax Identification Number and VAT Identification Number. The entity must then subscribe to the HomeTax electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) system, open a bank account at a Korean licensed bank for export proceeds, and notify NTS of any changes to business data within 15 days. A Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation for tax purposes and must register regardless of revenue level or foreign ownership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis VAT registration threshold for corporations; a Chusik Hoesa must register before commencing business. Small individual merchants with annual turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 may use simplified taxable status, but this threshold exemption does not apply to companies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Korea to a foreign market, including cross-border e-commerce dispatches from Korean FBA warehouses, provided the export has a valid export declaration and the supplier receives export proceeds from a foreign buyer. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Korea and consumption occurs offshore. Sales delivered and consumed inside Korea via Amazon Korea or domestic channels are standard-rated at 10%.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic Export Declaration (Bill of Export / separate export clearance equivalent to a Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air) or combined transport house waybill proving the goods left Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation from a Korean licensed bank evidencing foreign currency repatriation of export proceeds

All VAT invoices must be issued as electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) and transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system within one day after the taxable supply. Businesses must integrate batch transmission via NTS-approved EDI/API channels; there is no private physical fiscal stamp, but anti-fraud is enforced through mandatory digital certificates, timestamp circulation, automatic e-invoice matching, and cross-validation of export declarations with Korea Customs Service records.

If the 0% rate is claimed but the required customs, logistics, invoice, or foreign-exchange evidence is missing or inconsistent, the NTS reclassifies the supply as domestic at 10% VAT and imposes statutory additional taxes, including unintentional underreporting penalties of 10% of the tax payable, negligent non-filing penalties of 20%, intentional fraud penalties of 40%, plus value-added surtax interest. Issuing or procuring a false export declaration or false e-invoice additionally triggers criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Korea Customs Act, carrying imprisonment not exceeding three years and fines up to five times the evaded amount, with retrospective clawback of all zero-rated input VAT previously credited or refunded and potential suspension of export VAT refund eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but carefully supervised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade settlements, direct operating expenses, and profit remittances, subject only to documentary verification and reporting to a foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, equity injections, and cross-border loans, are not freely convertible without prior or simultaneous reporting and registration. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in foreign exchange accounts with authorised domestic banks. Profit remittances by a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa are permitted once the statutory taxpayer or dividends have been properly documented, but they require filing of a foreign exchange receipt/payment report with the settlement bank and evidence of underlying earnings, tax clearance, and audited financial statements to ensure no undisclosed obligations exist. For Amazon FBA e-commerce operations, typical inflows are USD or EUR received via Amazon Pay and other PSPs, which must be routed through a domestic bank without violating the requirement that all cross-border claims be settled in Korea. The practical hurdle is not convertibility of the won for legitimate trade and investment, but the need for accurate purpose codes, underlying trade documentation, and a transparent corporate treasury trail that satisfies the bank's anti-money laundering and foreign exchange monitoring obligations. In practice, the BOK and MOEF can request flow-of-funds explanations, and any significant deviation between declared e-commerce turnover and bank receipts will trigger enhanced due diligence or sanctions. Overall, the won is convertible for genuine business and investment flows, but every transfer must be traceable and supported by a complete documentation suite.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Enforcement Decree of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds for e-commerce sales such as Amazon FBA must be collected and brought into Korea within 18 months from the export declaration date. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio: 0% of export proceeds must be converted into Korean won, and 100% may remain in a foreign currency account at an authorised foreign exchange bank. The exporter must submit a foreign exchange receipt report to the bank within the settlement period. If collection cannot be completed within the 18-month deadline, an extension must be requested from the relevant foreign exchange bank or MOEF before the deadline expires. For Amazon FBA settlements, the net proceeds from Amazon Pay are normally received by the Korean seller as marketplace remittances; these are classified as export proceeds and must be credited to the corporate foreign currency account and reported as such, with Seller Central statements, customs declarations, and VAT evidence retained for audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) and delegated foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Direct fintech EMI onboarding for a Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa is legally restricted. Airwallex, Stripe, and similar cross-border digital payment providers are not licensed as banks in South Korea and cannot open domestic KRW settlement accounts unless they are registered as electronic financial business operators under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act of the Financial Services Commission. As a practical matter, these EMIs must partner with a domestic licensed bank to provide settlement rails, which introduces layered KYC and may conflict with the bank's requirement that the entity maintain primary operating relationships with the local branch. The viability rating is Low-to-Medium: Airwallex and Stripe can support pay-in and pay-out via partner banks, but the statutory foreign exchange reporting and capital verification obligations still require a domestic banking relationship. The critical legal constraint is that any foreign EMI that processes KRW payments without a domestic banking partner would be operating unlawfully, and Korean banks will refuse to accept settlement from unlicensed overseas PSPs. The safest approach is to use a domestic tier-1 bank as the settlement node, with Amazon Pay and Stripe acting only as collection funnels for foreign currency, never as the primary KRW treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be remitted in foreign currency (typically USD) to the Korea-based capital escrow account of the Chusik Hoesa at a tier-1 foreign exchange bank. The remittance instruction must include the purpose code for foreign direct investment or paid-in capital. The foreign shareholder must first file an FDI report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with KOTRA or the bank, obtain an FDI registration number, and ensure the remitter name on the SWIFT MT103 exactly matches the investor of record. After the bank receives funds, it will issue a Certificate of Foreign Exchange Receipt, which the company uses for capital verification at the registry and for subsequent tax filings. The bank will automatically report the inward remittance to BOK/FEIN; the company must not split the remittance to avoid reporting thresholds. For operational injection that exceeds the registered paid-in capital, the company must treat it as loan or capital increase and follow the applicable reporting rules under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. All Amazon FBA sale proceeds received from overseas marketplaces must also be credited to the designated foreign exchange account and reported, with supporting marketplace payout reports maintained for at least five years.Outbound trade disbursements for Amazon FBA inventory, shipping, and logistics are cleared via SWIFT MT103 messages through the domestic foreign exchange bank, with each payment carrying the correct trade settlement purpose code and underlying documents including customs declarations, commercial invoices, and purchase orders. For larger procurement commitments, commercial LCs (letters of credit) and documentary collections are permissible where the Korean bank confirms the LC, with the bank relying on the shipping documents and bill of lading to release funds. KRW cannot be realistically used for inbound or outbound cross-border balances; all international treasury movements must be conducted in USD, EUR, or other acceptable settleable currencies. Amazon FBA disbursements into the Korean bank account may use Amazon's global payout system, but the bank must be able to identify the settlement source from a single named account, and the company must provide Amazon's periodic settlement reports converted into the required reporting format when asked by the bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Direct fintech EMI onboarding for a Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa is legally restricted. Airwallex, Stripe, and similar cross-border digital payment providers are not licensed as banks in South Korea and cannot open domestic KRW settlement accounts unless they are registered as electronic financial business operators under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act of the Financial Services Commission. As a practical matter, these EMIs must partner with a domestic licensed bank to provide settlement rails, which introduces layered KYC and may conflict with the bank's requirement that the entity maintain primary operating relationships with the local branch. The viability rating is Low-to-Medium: Airwallex and Stripe can support pay-in and pay-out via partner banks, but the statutory foreign exchange reporting and capital verification obligations still require a domestic banking relationship. The critical legal constraint is that any foreign EMI that processes KRW payments without a domestic banking partner would be operating unlawfully, and Korean banks will refuse to accept settlement from unlicensed overseas PSPs. The safest approach is to use a domestic tier-1 bank as the settlement node, with Amazon Pay and Stripe acting only as collection funnels for foreign currency, never as the primary KRW treasury rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national corporate income tax rate on taxable income above KRW 300 billion; lower brackets 10% up to KRW 200 million, 20% for KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion and 21% for KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax liability makes the aggregate headline maximum effective rate 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax under Article 98 of the Corporate Tax Act plus 2% deemed local income tax) on outbound dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder, unless reduced by an applicable Double Tax Treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File is mandatory when consolidated annual revenue of the Korean group and related parties exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country Reports are required for multinational groups meeting statutory global revenue thresholds under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the National Tax Service enforces the substance-over-form principle under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Principal Purpose Test in tax treaties; the Korean Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate real operational substance, local decision-making and Core Income-Generating Activities such as inventory risk, order management, customer contracts and treasury functions in Korea, and a paper entity established solely to obtain tax treaty benefits may be denied treaty access, have its profit re-attributed, or be disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Imposition of an unjustified underreporting additional tax equal to 40% of the evaded tax under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes; criminal liability under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine up to three times the evaded tax; for amounts over KRW 500 million, the prosecution and collection statute of limitations is extended to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The representative director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties and surcharges under Articles 38 and 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes; the National Tax Service can seize personal assets, impose an overseas departure ban, place the director on the high-risk unpaid taxpayer blacklist, and a criminal conviction leads to imprisonment, disqualification from serving as a director, and extradition risk under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping and Shell Entity Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recompute Korean taxable income under Articles 4 and 7 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and make a transfer-pricing adjustment, imposing a negligence penalty of 30% of the understated tax amount for missing TP documentation and up to 10% additional penalty for non-arm's length pricing; the foreign payee may also be deemed to have a Korean permanent establishment, triggering branch tax under Article 98-3 of the Corporate Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the sham arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted corporate tax, branch tax and penalties; where the Korean company is found to be the alter ego of the director or shareholders, the tax authority pierces the corporate veil to recover personal assets; criminal tax fraud for abusive profit stripping may result in imprisonment, civil restitution, travel bans and placement on the National Tax Service blacklist.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Foreign Exchange Repatriation Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, unauthorised or unreported cross-border remittances are punishable by a fine of up to KRW 100 million and imprisonment up to five years; under the Act on Reporting and Using of Certain Financial Transaction Information, failure to report foreign financial accounts or beneficial ownership information attracts a criminal penalty of up to KRW 300 million and imprisonment up to five years, together with asset freezing orders by the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: The director may be ordered to make civil restitution of the repatriated funds and statutory penalties; a criminal conviction triggers confiscation of personally held assets, travel bans, Posting on the financial crime blacklist, disqualification from managing regulated businesses, and imprisonment, while fraudulent concealment may lead to cross-border investigative assistance and asset freezing abroad.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act; the promoters may fix the capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation. Nonetheless, the entire issue price of all subscribed shares must be fully paid into a domestic foreign-exchange bank before incorporation registration, and the bank's certificate of deposit of the subscription price must be produced to the court. No government blocked escrow account is required; the domestic bank capital account serves as a restricted deposit until registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; official electronic portal HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr; administrative filing is made through the local District Tax Office for the entity's registered business address.

E-government filing portal

A Korean Chusik Hoesa obtains its Tax Identification Number by filing a Business Registration Application with the competent NTS District Tax Office within 20 calendar days from commencement of business. Required attachments include the certificate of incorporation and corporate registration, articles of incorporation, proof of the registered office, shareholder and director identification, and Korean bank account details. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that serves as the unified TIN for Corporate Income Tax, VAT, and withholding tax purposes. A foreign entity without a Korean establishment that supplies digital services to Korean users may separately apply for simplified VAT registration through HomeTax and must appoint a domestic tax representative where direct filing is not administratively feasible.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Korean Chusik Hoesa obtains its Tax Identification Number by filing a Business Registration Application with the competent NTS District Tax Office within 20 calendar days from commencement of business. Required attachments include the certificate of incorporation and corporate registration, articles of incorporation, proof of the registered office, shareholder and director identification, and Korean bank account details. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that serves as the unified TIN for Corporate Income Tax, VAT, and withholding tax purposes. A foreign entity without a Korean establishment that supplies digital services to Korean users may separately apply for simplified VAT registration through HomeTax and must appoint a domestic tax representative where direct filing is not administratively feasible.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Korea has no US-style check-the-box election. A Chusik Hoesa is a statutory resident corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through election is available. Eligible domestic corporate groups may elect consolidated corporate income tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules disallow deductions for payments treated as not includible by the counterparty jurisdiction. For VAT, a business with annual turnover below KRW 80,000,000 may be classified as a simplified taxpayer for filing purposes, but this does not alter entity-level corporate income taxation. Foreign tax credit elections for direct and indirect foreign taxes are available under the applicable tax treaty and domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for VAT registration; any person commencing a business activity in Korea must register for VAT within 20 days of commencement. A simplified taxpayer filing regime applies to individual businesses with annual gross turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but the entity remains a registered VAT taxpayer and must charge 10% VAT on taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to goods physically exported from Korea and to services supplied to non-resident customers outside Korea, including digital health and fitness application subscriptions, biometric telemetry, wellness tracking platforms, and hosted analytics delivered to foreign end users. The Korean supplier must hold a valid Business Registration Number, issue an electronic export tax invoice, and maintain evidence that the place of supply is outside Korea. For app-store distribution, sales to Korean resident consumers remain standard-rated at 10% VAT despite the App Store or Google Play commission interface; sales to non-resident foreign consumers qualify for zero-rating when the contract, foreign-currency payment, and offshore consumption evidence are complete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (mandatory for physical goods; for pure digital services, the equivalent controlled record is the e-Tax export invoice and export service register filed with the VAT refund claim)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export movement for goods; for digital fitness app exports, the equivalent is the online platform download/usage log and App Store sales report showing foreign end-user access
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank MT103, or foreign-currency collection confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign purchaser
  • International service, subscription, or licensing agreement identifying the foreign counterparty, product scope, contract value, and delivery terms
  • Country-of-use, IP geolocation, and telemetry usage logs substantiating that the health fitness app services are consumed outside Korea

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all registered corporate taxpayers through the NTS HomeTax system. The e-Tax Invoice must be transmitted to the NTS and communicated to the buyer by no later than the day following the issuance date. Korea does not require physical fiscal stamping; the NTS issues a digital acceptance and validation number upon electronic receipt. NTS-certified e-invoicing providers use API integrations, ERP interfaces, or HomeTax web services for submission. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice triggers a penalty tax of 1% of the supply value, plus general late-payment interest. Cash receipts are mandatory for qualifying final-consumption sales.

Zero-rated export claims are closely audited by the NTS and Korea Customs Service. Auditors compare export declarations, e-Tax invoices, foreign-currency receipts, contract counterparties, and digital consumption logs. If export proceeds are not collected in foreign currency within 180 days, the zero-rating is retroactively reversed, 10% VAT is assessed, and input VAT credits are clawed back. Fraudulent or fictitious export claims, including circular financing, fabricated download logs, or sham foreign buyers, attract an additional tax surcharge of up to 40% of the over-refunded or evaded VAT, daily overdue interest, a 10-year extended statute of limitations, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains liberalized current account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Cross-border receipts from digital subscription services, app store payouts, royalties, and operating revenue settlements are generally freely convertible and remittable, subject to foreign exchange transaction reporting through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are also permitted but are subject to registration or reporting requirements. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is structurally available to non-resident shareholders of a Chusik Hoesa, but the remitting Korean company must demonstrate: (i) distributable profits from carefully prepared financial statements; (ii) a valid shareholders meeting or sole-shareholder distribution resolution; (iii) compliance with Korean dividend withholding tax at the applicable statutory rate, generally 20% plus 2% resident surcharge, or a lower rate under an applicable tax treaty; (iv) settlement of the tax withholding through the Tax Number of the non-resident shareholder; and (v) presentation of the corresponding tax payment certificate and foreign exchange purpose documentation to the Korean foreign exchange bank. There is no general requirement to obtain a separate central bank license or permit for a standard dividend remittance once the underlying tax and foreign exchange reporting obligations are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Korean Foreign Exchange Regulations, export proceeds must be received within the statutory settlement timeline, generally 15 months from the date of export or service performance for goods and digitally delivered services unless the Korean foreign exchange bank approves a clearly documented extension. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: South Korea does not currently require surrender of export receipts, and the Chusik Hoesa may retain 100% of export proceeds in a multi-currency foreign exchange account at a licensed Korean bank. If proceeds are not received by the settlement deadline, the company and the bank must file the required foreign exchange change report to MOEF/BOK, and the unreceived amounts may be reclassified as a capital transaction rather than a current account export collection. For Apple App Store and Google Play subscription revenue, the qualifying evidence consists of the platform financial statements, payout statements, merchant remittance reports, tax invoices supplied by the platform, and a copy of the platform distribution agreement demonstrating the true recipient of the digital service revenue.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) is the central bank authority, acting as the principal foreign exchange information and settlement oversight institution together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF). Routine foreign exchange transactions and corporate banking operations are delegated to licensed foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree and Foreign Exchange Regulations.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national dividend withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate tax returns must be filed within 3 months after the end of each fiscal year. For a December year-end, the statutory deadline is March 31. Interim and preliminary returns are also filed semi-annually by corporations with prior-year taxable income exceeding prescribed thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Total cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion trigger mandatory Local File and Master File submission; a threshold of KRW 100 billion triggers full contemporaneous documentation retention.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Korean Chusik Hoesa entities are subject to domestic and OECD economic substance standards; no-true-substance overseas outsourcing of core revenue functions may be recharacterised and trigger anti-shell-company and substance disallowance penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Inflated Royalty Remittances

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result, addition of 10-40% penalties on underreported income, and a 20% withholding penalty on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the shortfall if the company becomes insolvent, and face criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and disallowance of intercompany deductions; 40% fraud penalties; referral for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in operating the shell may be personally disqualified, prosecuted for tax fraud, and exposed to civil restitution orders.
AML and Unlicensed Payment Handling

Statutory sanction: Penalties, business suspensions, and criminal fines for operating an unlicensed payment aggregation or money transmission business.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined and imprisoned for violations of Korean AML and electronic financial transaction regulations.
Late Filing and Remittance Violations

Statutory sanction: 0.03% daily late payment surcharge; 10% understatement penalty; interest and additional 20% non-filing penalty; suspension of tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the unpaid corporate tax plus penalties when acts are grossly negligent or intentional.

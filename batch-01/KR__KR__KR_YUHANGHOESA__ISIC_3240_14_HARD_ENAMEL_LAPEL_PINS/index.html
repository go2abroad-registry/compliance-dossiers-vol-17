Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under the Korean Commercial Act. The full subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be deposited into a domestic corporate bank account before the incorporation registration is filed. A Certificate of Balance issued by the bank must be submitted to the competent registry authority. For non-resident shareholders, inbound equity investment must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; no blocked escrow account is explicitly required by the Companies Act, but the deposit must remain unavailable until registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain a Fire Facility Installation and Maintenance Certificate and a civil defense fire safety inspection approval for the production and storage areas under the Fire Service Act. The workplace must undergo a structural load inspection for the production line and warehouse mezzanines, pass an electric installation safety inspection, and submit an occupational safety risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act. All workers in polishing, enamel filling, and brass etching areas must receive hazard communication training and be supplied with appropriate personal protective equipment, including chemical-resistant gloves and VOC respirators.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a highly liberalized current account regime with full convertibility for trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, and ordinary remittances. Capital account transactions are conditionally liberalized and require statutory reporting or prior approval depending on the transaction size and nature. For a non-resident-owned limited liability company engaged in the manufacture and export of hard enamel lapel pins, capital importation via a foreign direct investment (FDI) qualifies as a capital account transaction under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The subscription of shares and receipt of the initial capital contribution must be reported ex ante to the MOEF or delegated institution (e.g., a domestic commercial bank) through a foreign exchange purchase and sale, and the proceeds can be credited to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account. Current account operations, including the receipt of export proceeds, are freely convertible but subject to documentary verification and the mandatory repurchase declaration of the export invoice. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is treated as a current account transaction; it is freely transferable after corporate tax withholding is settled and a tax clearance certificate or a tax receipt is presented to the remitting bank. The principal practical hurdle for a foreign shareholder is the need to demonstrate the lawful origin of the initial funds and to satisfy the bank's KYC/UBO diligence before the capital injection; after that, the KRW proceeds can be converted into USD or other hard currencies at the prevailing market rate without central bank quotas. No surrender requirement applies to export proceeds after the statutory collection period, but the exporter must repatriate the proceeds to Korea within the stipulated due date (normally 180 days from the export declaration, extendable upon bank approval) and may retain them in a foreign exchange account or convert them to KRW at will. The capital account remains constrained by the requirement to file a Foreign Exchange Transaction Report for every transaction exceeding USD 5,000 equivalent, and certain derivative transactions are limited to firms with underlying trade exposure. Overall, the regime is best characterized as convertible with prudential oversight, not a restrictive surrender-based system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 18 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Enforcement Decree, export receipts must be collected within the payment due date stated in the export contract, with a statutory default ceiling of 180 days after the export declaration date (extendable by the Minister of Economy and Finance in exceptional circumstances). The exporter must present the export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and bank receipt to the domestic bank through which the proceeds are received. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or use the proceeds directly to settle import payables. However, the exporter is required to obtain a foreign exchange purchase and sale confirmation from the receiving bank and to file an export proceeds collection report within 15 business days of the collection date. Failure to comply triggers administrative penalties and the freezing of the Foreign Exchange Account until the matter is cured.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the sovereign regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally viable as primary banking channels for a Korean resident entity. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act mandates that any entity providing payment services, foreign exchange brokerage, or deposit-taking facilities for Korean corporate clients must hold a license from the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and must be registered as a foreign exchange business operator with the Korea Customs Service or the MOEF. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex do not hold such licenses in Korea and cannot open local KRW clearing accounts on behalf of the entity without partnering with a licensed Korean bank. They may be used peripherally as a supplementary collection aggregator for inbound retail payments from platforms like Etsy or Stripe, but only through a licensed domestic bank as the underlying custodian of the settlement account. For real-time trade payments, tax remittances, and FX hedging, the entity must operate through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. The KYC standards of domestic banks are aligned to the FATF recommendations and typically require a larger documentation bundle than offshore EMIs; however, the legal and reputational risk of relying on an unlicensed EMI is deemed unacceptable for a treasury mandate. Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury, Medium as a sub-processor for e-commerce micro-collections where the EMI has a Korean banking partner and the funds are immediately settled to the domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound FDI capital injection must be routed via the designated Capital Escrow Account opened at a Tier-1 domestic bank. The wiring instructions are a SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the parent's offshore bank to the Korean bank's corresponding USD or KRW account, with the intended beneficiary name registered under the Foreign Investment Report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally viable as primary banking channels for a Korean resident entity. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act mandates that any entity providing payment services, foreign exchange brokerage, or deposit-taking facilities for Korean corporate clients must hold a license from the Financial Services Commission (FSC) and must be registered as a foreign exchange business operator with the Korea Customs Service or the MOEF. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex do not hold such licenses in Korea and cannot open local KRW clearing accounts on behalf of the entity without partnering with a licensed Korean bank. They may be used peripherally as a supplementary collection aggregator for inbound retail payments from platforms like Etsy or Stripe, but only through a licensed domestic bank as the underlying custodian of the settlement account. For real-time trade payments, tax remittances, and FX hedging, the entity must operate through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. The KYC standards of domestic banks are aligned to the FATF recommendations and typically require a larger documentation bundle than offshore EMIs; however, the legal and reputational risk of relying on an unlicensed EMI is deemed unacceptable for a treasury mandate. Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury, Medium as a sub-processor for e-commerce micro-collections where the EMI has a Korean banking partner and the funds are immediately settled to the domestic bank account.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation may lawfully provide for a zero or minimal stated capital. However, if any share subscription proceeds are collected, those funds must be deposited into a dedicated incorporation deposit or escrow account before registration, and the bank subscription certificate must be attached to the registration filing. After the court issues the Certificate of Registration, the block is lifted and funds are released for corporate use. There is no mandatory continuing blocked capital escrow account, but the statutory legal reserve rule in Commercial Act Article 458 applies as retained earnings accumulate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Completion Inspection Certificate issued by the local Fire Department for the building, including automatic fire detection, sprinkler or suppression systems, kitchen hood fire suppression, emergency lighting, exit signage and evacuation routes. Civil Defense Preparedness Clearance requires the submission of a protective action and evacuation plan, participation in municipal civil defense communication protocols and structural load classification for the building. Occupational safety and health clearance is issued through the Ministry of Employment and Labor workplace risk assessment, covering machine guarding, food processing blade safety, hot oil and steam handling, sanitation disease prevention, personal protective equipment, work hours and welfare facility compliance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa, the entity must file an application for Business Registration (사업자등록신청) with the NTS via HomeTax or the local tax office within 20 days of business commencement. The representative director or local tax representative must submit the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign investment registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (if applicable), and documentation evidencing the physical place of business. Foreign entities without a domestic place of business must appoint a tax representative for filing and payment obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Korean tax law. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically taxed as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act. The only relevant elective mechanism is the consolidated corporate taxation regime for wholly-owned subsidiaries under Article 12 of the Corporate Tax Act, available for groups meeting statutory shareholding and filing requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold; every business operator supplying goods or services in the course of a business is mandatorily required to register for VAT with the NTS within 20 days of business commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act and its Enforcement Decree, zero-rating applies to exported goods and certain internationally-connected services. For in-flight catering supplies to airlines, zero-rating is available when the goods are loaded onto an aircraft operating an international route, evidencing that the meals are exported from Korea for consumption outside Korean territorial waters. The service is also zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident airline and the benefit is consumed entirely outside South Korea, provided the proceeds are received in foreign currency and repatriated. The taxpayer must retain documentary proof of the export transaction and foreign exchange receipt.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (수출신고필증 / Export Declaration Confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the international flight and consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange bank receipt showing receipt of the export proceeds in an approved foreign currency
  • Electronic tax invoice (zero-rated) issued to the airline customer
  • Sales contract or purchase order from the foreign airline specifying international route and catering service

All VAT-registered businesses issuing tax invoices for B2B supplies must issue mandatory electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) through the NTS HomeTax system within one day after the invoice issuance date. There is no real-time clearance or fiscal stamping requirement, but the e-invoice data is transmitted digitally to the NTS through the standard HomeTax API or portal interface. Failure to issue an e-invoice or late transmission subjects the supplier to an additional tax penalty of 1% of the invoice value. Additionally, cashier receipts are required for consumer transactions as fraud prevention.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the export declaration, transportation documents, sales contract, or foreign exchange repatriation records are missing, incomplete, or falsified, the claimed zero-rate is retroactively reversed and VAT at 10% is clawed back with statutory interest. False claims constitute unlawful tax evasion, subject to an additional penalty up to 40% of the evaded tax amount, criminal fines up to twice the amount of tax evaded, and imprisonment for up to two years, in accordance with the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including ordinary trade settlement, service fees, and repatriation of legally declared dividends, subject to documentary verification by an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are not freely convertible; they require prior declaration, notification, or reporting to the Bank of Korea through a domestic foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Inward foreign direct investment and outward investment remittances must be filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System, and the financing bank must validate the purpose code and underlying contract. There is no general obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of Korea, but all foreign currency receipts must be received through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank and reported. Profit remittances by a Chusik Hoesa are permitted once audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board approval of dividends are presented, and the remittance must be processed through a domestic bank where the corporate account is maintained. For a foreign-invested inflight catering company, the practical hurdle is not convertibility itself but the KYC and capital verification evidence required for the foreign exchange bank to approve each remittance. Payment for imported food ingredients, aviation galley equipment, and licensing royalties must be supported by relevant trade documentation and declared under the applicable FEIS transaction code. Capital account liberalization is extensive, yet Korea retains administrative screening and anti-money laundering scrutiny over unusual or large cross-border flows, particularly where the beneficial owner is a non-resident corporation or where the intended use deviates from the declared corporate purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For inflight catering supplies classified as exports from Korea under the Customs Act, export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of export declaration. There is no statutory mandatory surrender ratio to the Bank of Korea; however, the exporting Chusik Hoesa must settle the foreign currency receipt into its domestic KRW account or maintain it in a foreign currency account at the same domestic bank, and the receipt must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System. Any extension beyond the one-year collection period requires prior approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance, with documentary support such as airline purchase orders, delivery certificates, and evidence of an unavoidable delay in settlement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with foreign exchange policy authority exercised jointly by the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) reporting framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as the primary banking or clearing rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a corporate entity operating in Korea must maintain its operational and foreign exchange settlement accounts with an authorized domestic financial institution. Offshore EMI platforms such as global digital payment providers do not possess a Korean banking licence and cannot provide a compliant KRW clearing account, foreign exchange reporting, or capital verification services acceptable to the Bank of Korea. A fintech EMI may be used only as a supplementary ancillary tool for travel expenses or low-value procurement, but it cannot replace the domestic tier-one commercial bank relationship, cannot accept foreign direct investment capital, and is legally barred from servicing the company as its clearing bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax (CIT) on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; the effective combined rate with local income tax (10% surtax on CIT) is 26.4%. Progressive national rates apply: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion; 21% between KRW 20 billion and KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final income tax return must be filed within 3 months from the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending December 31, the due date is March 31 of the following year. Interim prepayments (for the first 6 months) are due within 2 months after the end of the first half of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local file and master file are required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion per fiscal year. Additionally, a master file is compulsory if the Korean enterprise is part of a multinational group with consolidated revenue at or above KRW 100 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies where group consolidated revenue is at or above KRW 1 trillion, and reporting must be lodged electronically in accordance with the International Tax Adjustment Act.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies a statutory substance-over-form doctrine under the Corporate Tax Act (Article 9-1) and the Framework Act on National Taxes (Article 17). Tax treaty benefits are denied where a non-resident or a foreign entity lacks beneficial ownership or substantive business activity. For an airline in-flight catering kitchen, the Korean tax authorities (NTS) will expect: an active operational license from the Civil Aviation Authority, physical kitchen and refrigerated storage premises, a team of production and logistics employees, actual delivery contracts with airlines, independent management decision-making, and distributive pools. A mere pass-through invoicing arrangement or EBITDA-stripping corporate shell will trigger anti-avoidance re-characterization and forfeiture of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Intentional Underreporting / False Evidence)

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, criminal penalty: imprisonment up to 5 years (extended to 7 years in aggravated cases) and a fine not exceeding five times the evaded tax. Additionally, the NTS assesses underreported tax penalties of 20% (40% if involving false audited statements) plus daily late-payment surcharges of 0.022% on any overdue tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or knowingly benefited from the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. Several liability also arises under the secondary tax collection rules. Travel bans (prohibition of departure from Korea) are routinely imposed during tax investigations. In egregious cases, the responsible CEO and CFO face personal criminal imprisonment, civil restitution, and lifetime disqualification from corporate directorship.
Empty Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in In-flight Catering Operation

Statutory sanction: NTS will re-characterize the arrangement under substance-over-form principles, deny treaty benefits, subject the full margin to Korean CIT at 24% plus local tax, and exact a 20% deficiency penalty. Civil administrative penalties also include asset freezing. The Foreign Exchange Transaction Act may impose fines for unregistered foreign transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may trigger piercing of the corporate veil if capital is clearly inadequate and the shell is used for fraud. The NTS can hold directors personally liable for the entire tax debt and penalties under Article 14-2 of the Basic Law on Local Taxes (secondary tax liability). Travel restrictions and a ban on leaving Korea are automatic during criminal investigations for tax fraud.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Failure to Maintain TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: NTS assesses the arm's-length price (KPGA) and recalculates the Korean entity's taxable income upward; a penalty of 20% of the additional attributable tax applies. Additionally, non-documentation of TP arrangements carries a penalty of up to KRW 100 million under the International Tax Adjustment Act. Interest on the deficiency accrues at 0.022% per day.

Director personal liability: Where directors are complicit in documenting a fabricated transfer-pricing policy, they are subject to secondary liability for the adjusted tax and penalties. The NTS may seek delegation of criminal referral for document forgery, which carries up to 5 years' imprisonment. Foreign travel bans remain active while criminal matters are pending.
Anti-Money-Laundering / Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Korea's Financial Transactions Reports Act (FTRA), unregistered foreign exchange transactions are punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million. Failure to file cash transaction reports over KRW 10 million triggers an administrative fine of up to KRW 20 million. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) can also suspend the corporate's currency conversion activities.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for laundering criminal proceeds exposed in tax investigations; sanctions merge with tax evasion charges. The Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission imposes travel bans and financial account freezing. Directors can be sentenced to up to 3 years for money laundering and deprivation of the right to operate a regulated business.

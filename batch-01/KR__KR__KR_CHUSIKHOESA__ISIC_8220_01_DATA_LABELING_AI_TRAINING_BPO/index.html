Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korea abolished the statutory minimum capital requirement for a Chusik Hoesa. The only requirement is that all issued shares must be subscribed and the full subscription price must be paid in cash, or a permitted non-cash contribution must be appraised by a court-appointed inspector where required, before the incorporation registration is filed. No blocked escrow account is mandated by statute.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 11 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Presidential Decree Article 13, goods are zero-rated when physically exported from Korea under an accepted export declaration and not returned to the domestic market. Services supplied to non-resident clients can also qualify as zero-rated exported services if the place of supply is outside Korea or the service is performed in Korea but used or consumed wholly outside Korea and the contract is paid in foreign currency. AI data labeling, computer vision annotation, and RLHF data curation supplied under a B2B contract to an overseas non-resident customer may qualify when the exporter retains verifiable evidence consisting of the executed foreign currency contract, the foreign client's non-resident status, date-stamped data delivery records, reproducibility logs, and the inward foreign exchange remittance. Services tied to Korean land, buildings, or tangible goods located in Korea are excluded from zero-rating.

  • Export Declaration (수출신고서) electronically accepted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or air waybill evidencing physical removal of the goods from the Republic of Korea
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or official Foreign Exchange Transaction Record showing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

All VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices; electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) are mandatory and must be transmitted to HomeTax no later than one day after the supply date whenever the counterparty is a VAT-registered business and the supply value including VAT is at least KRW 300,000. The National Tax Service operates a centralized e-tax invoice clearance database; there is no block-chain fiscal stamping, but late, omitted, or false electronic transmission triggers penalty surcharges under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service conduct retrospective audit reviews of zero-rating claims. If the export documentation, foreign exchange receipt, or foreign user evidence is missing, fraudulent, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is revoked retroactively. The tax authority assesses the 10% VAT previously exempted, plus an additional tax of 40% of the underpaid VAT where concealment or intentional fraud is established, and daily delinquency surcharges for late payment. Criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act is available for wilful evasion; the statutory assessment period extends to 10 years for fraudulent cases. Export proceeds not repatriated within the mandatory 180-day foreign exchange deadline may be recharacterized as domestic supplies and lose zero-rating retroactively.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime for the Korean Won (KRW) and a largely open current account, including trade in goods and services, dividends, and interest payments. However, capital account transactions remain subject to a registration and reporting-based control system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Export proceeds from goods and services must be repatriated into domestic bank accounts within a statutory timeframe, generally no later than five business days from the receipt of foreign currency, with a mandatory sale requirement for proceeds exceeding a threshold set by the BOK. Profit remittances for foreign investors are permitted upon submission of a certified tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a remittance application through a designated foreign exchange bank; there is no explicit approval but the bank must verify the underlying economic substance and tax compliance. For AI data-labeling BPO operations, typical clients abroad pay via cross-border wires or global payment platforms; such receipts are treated as export proceeds if contracted offshore. There are no surrender requirements for service export proceeds in excess of the exemption threshold, but banks still enforce conversion into KRW unless the entity holds a valid foreign currency account. The main deterrents to full convertibility include documentary hurdles for capital inflows such as foreign direct investment (FDI) notifications, the requirement to register the entity with the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System (FERP), and potential delays in large remittances due to anti-money laundering screening. Overall, KRW is fully convertible for current account purposes, while capital account flows require compliance with pre- or post-reporting obligations, and the BOK exercises monitoring rather than hard rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts in foreign currency must be received into a domestic designated foreign exchange bank account within five business days of the due settlement date, and the portion exceeding USD 100,000 equivalent (or the threshold set by MOEF/BOK) must be converted to KRW promptly. A full exemption applies to service export earnings where the contract value is below the reporting threshold; however, all receipts must be reported via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FERP) when the annual cumulative amount exceeds the regime limit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Act framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Payoneer cannot act as primary banking institutions for a Korean-registered Chusik Hoesa. Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed domestic financial institutions (commercial banks, brokerage firms) and registered money transfer operators can handle foreign exchange transactions for resident entities. Offshore EMIs are prohibited from providing KRW-denominated accounts or clearing services without a banking partnership with a local bank. Wise may technically support outbound payouts in certain currencies, but inbound settlement into a Korean entity requires local bank routing. Therefore, fintech EMI cross-border viability is Low as a primary treasury rail; it may be used only as a supplementary collection tool if the entity maintains a local bank account, and withdrawals must be routed through a compliant domestic bank. The legal constraints effectively bar unlicensed virtual account providers from maintaining direct settlement rails for KRW invoices.

Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed domestic financial institutions (commercial banks, brokerage firms) and registered money transfer operators can handle foreign exchange transactions for resident entities. Offshore EMIs are prohibited from providing KRW-denominated accounts or clearing services without a banking partnership with a local bank. Wise may technically support outbound payouts in certain currencies, but inbound settlement into a Korean entity requires local bank routing. Therefore, fintech EMI cross-border viability is Low as a primary treasury rail; it may be used only as a supplementary collection tool if the entity maintains a local bank account, and withdrawals must be routed through a compliant domestic bank. The legal constraints effectively bar unlicensed virtual account providers from maintaining direct settlement rails for KRW invoices.

24% top marginal national Corporate Tax rate (progressive national rates: 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 21% over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% over KRW 300 billion), plus 10% Local Income Tax surcharge on the national tax, producing an aggregate headline rate of 26.4% in the top bracket

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion in intercompany transactions with foreign related parties in a fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation comprising a Local File, Master File, and supporting analysis; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) obligations are triggered for Korean ultimate parent or constituent entities when consolidated group revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion, and lower thresholds may apply for certain tax haven transactions

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the substance of a Chusik Hoesa is tested through the Korean GAAR, the international transaction adjustment rules, the principal purpose test under tax treaties, agency-PE risks, and the requirement that profit attribution be substantiated by Core Income-Generating Activities performed in Korea; a company that merely invoices annotation services and routes profits offshore without local personnel, management, servers, data processing, or contracts will be recharacterized and denied treaty and tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through fraudulent corporate return

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Offenders Act, imprisonment for a term of up to 10 years or a fine of up to five times the amount of corporate tax evaded, plus corporate tax additional dues of 40% of the fraudulent understatement and daily late-payment surcharges of approximately 0.022% of the unpaid tax

Director personal liability: A director who ordered, participated in, or intentionally caused the concealment loses the limitation-of-liability shield; he or she is jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties under the Korean Commercial Act tort and director-liability rules, may be placed on the National Tax Service blacklist with a travel ban, and may suffer seizure of personal bank accounts and criminal imprisonment
Shell company and abusive treaty shopping / dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test and the international transaction adjustment rules, recharacterize the diverted income, impose statutory 22% dividend WHT and 16.5% royalty WHT, and levy an underreporting additional tax of 40%, together with interest and negligence penalties

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for intentional violation; directors are personally liable for the resulting tax restitutions and damages to third-party creditors, may be subject to asset forfeiture, and may be criminally prosecuted for tax fraud and foreign exchange evasion with imprisonment; travel bans are routinely imposed on blacklisted directors of conduit entities
AML and foreign-exchange reporting violation

Statutory sanction: The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million (and confiscation/forfeiture of proceeds) for unreported international payment transactions; the Act on Reporting and Use of Specified Financial Transaction Information provides parallel AML penalties for failing to report suspicious transactions or operating without a license, and aggravated sanctions apply when the entity is used for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors who personally control the offshore payment rails are treated as principals rather than protected corporate agents, face joint and several civil liability for restitution to the Republic and injured counterparties, criminal imprisonment for AML and foreign-exchange violations, blacklisting by Korean regulatory authorities, travel restrictions, and in extreme cases extradition or mutual legal assistance proceedings for cross-border financial crimes

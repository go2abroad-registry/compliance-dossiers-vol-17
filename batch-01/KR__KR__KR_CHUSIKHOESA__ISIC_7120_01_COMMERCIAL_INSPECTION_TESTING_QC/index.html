Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - the Commercial Act currently does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The articles of incorporation must state the authorized shares and the issued shares; all issued shares must be fully paid in at incorporation. Subscription money must be deposited in a blocked corporate capital subscription account with a Korean bank; the deposit certificate is filed with the court and the bank releases the funds only after issuance of the court registration certificate. The official filing fee is variable in Korean won and is calculated as registration license tax of 0.4% of the paid-in capital, education surtax equal to 20% of that tax, and court registration fees; because no capital amount is fixed in this dossier, the recorded USD amount in this schema is shown as 0 for not-fixed rather than as a payable state fee.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), via the HomeTax electronic portal at hometax.go.kr, with local filings accepted at the district tax office competent for the registered Chusik Hoesa business address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine home-tax registration category. A foreign-owned or non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa for inspection/testing/QC services must apply for a Business Registration Number (combined TIN and VAT ID) before commencing taxable supplies. If the entity has no permanent local premises, it appoints a Korean tax representative with a domestic address and files a power of attorney with the NTS. Step 2: Prepare the statutory application pack: certified copy of certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign tax residence certificate, passport/ID of the authorised signatory, power of attorney for the local tax representative, proof of business address or lease, and a business description stating ISIC 7120. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold relieves VAT registration; every business operator engaged in taxable supplies in Korea must register. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Article 24 of its Enforcement Decree, zero-rating is available for actual exports of goods and for exported services supplied to a non-resident or foreign enterprise when the service is not consumed in Korea and the consideration is received in foreign exchange from abroad. Pre-shipment factory inspection, quality-control testing, and audit-agency services performed in Korea for a foreign principal on goods destined for export qualify as exported services if the service contract, foreign inward remittance, and, where applicable, the Korean Customs Export Declaration and transport documents establish the cross-border character. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated on the same non-resident-use and foreign-exchange-receipt conditions. The zero-rated electronic tax invoice must be issued in HomeTax before the VAT return filing deadline, and the export substantiation bundle must be retained for the statutory audit period.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration issued through UNI-PASS (customs export clearance certificate) for the underlying goods, where the inspected goods are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing the inspected goods consignment to a foreign destination
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate, issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, showing collection of the full service fee in convertible foreign currency from abroad within the statutory repatriation period
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through HomeTax with the export/zero-rating reason code
  • Export service contract or purchase order naming the non-resident principal and itemising the factory inspection, testing, and QC audit services
  • Proof of the foreign counterparty's non-resident status, such as an overseas certificate of incorporation or tax residence certificate

Since 1 July 2022, all Korean VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices through an NTS-licensed service provider and transmit them to HomeTax. Korea does not operate a pre-issuance fiscal clearance or per-invoice digital stamp; instead, e-tax invoice issuance data is reported continuously to the NTS, normally by the day after issuance. The e-invoice must include the buyer and seller Business Registration Numbers, supply date, supply value, VAT amount or zero-rate reason code, and, for export supplies, the export/remittance reference. NTS analytics cross-match e-invoices with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations and bank foreign-exchange records.

The NTS cross-matches zero-rated electronic tax invoices with UNI-PASS customs export data and foreign-exchange remittance records. If the export price is not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or the documentation bundle is incomplete, the zero-rating is retroactively denied: the VAT refund is clawed back, 10% VAT is reassessed, and statutory late-payment interest plus additional tax apply. Additional tax is scaled by conduct, with up to 40% of the underpaid VAT for fraudulent or intentional misdeclaration. Fabricated customs declarations, false bills of lading, and fictitious inward remittance certificates are treated as tax evasion and also attract criminal liability under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, with imprisonment up to five years and/or fines up to KRW 50 million; the assessment clawback period extends to ten years for intentional concealment or false records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but closely monitored foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance sharing regulatory authority. Current account transactions, including trade settlement, service fees, and ordinary business remittances, are generally free and do not require prior approval, although banks must verify supporting documents to ensure the Report of Decline in Export Proceeds and similar filings are accurate. Capital account transactions, particularly cross-border equity contributions, foreign borrowings, and profit repatriation for a Chusik Hoesa, are subject to mandatory reporting and may require advance declaration when foreign investment exceeds KRW thresholds. There is no forced surrender of export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign exchange accounts, but converting to KRW for operating purposes is routine. The principal hurdles for non-resident shareholders are profit remittance taxes, compliance with foreign-investment registration formalities, and documentary proof of underlying trade or service transactions for every outward transfer. Korean banks enforce strict source-of-funds and purpose-of-payment checks, and any mismatch in the purpose code can delay clearing. Overall, convertibility is high for genuine commercial flows, but the burden is documentary precision rather than discretionary controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within one year from the date of the export shipment unless an extension is approved by the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; 0% of export foreign currency receipts must be surrendered to the central bank. The exporter is required to deposit the proceeds in a domestic foreign exchange bank account and report the receipt through the Foreign Exchange Information System. If not remitted within the statutory timeline, the bank will flag the transaction to the Korea Customs Service and the Bank of Korea for non-compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea / Ministry of Economy and Finance (Foreign Exchange Transactions Act regime)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut do not hold a Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act license for domestic KRW clearing, cannot provide compliant statutory foreign exchange transaction receipts, and are not permitted to service a Korean legal entity as its main inbound/outbound banking counterparty. Such platforms may only be used as a very limited ancillary remittance channel through licensed Korean banking partners, and even then corporate treasury departments should avoid routing material inspection-fee proceeds or trade settlements through them because Korean tax and foreign exchange authorities treat the use of unlicensed intermediaries as a red flag.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital injection must be routed through a Korean domestic foreign exchange bank as a SWIFT MT103 wire with a declared purpose code corresponding to inward foreign direct investment. Before receiving funds, the Chusik Hoesa or its foreign shareholder must file a foreign investment declaration with KOTRA or the foreign exchange bank; after the funds are lodged in the designated Foreign Investor Capital Account, the bank issues a certificate of foreign currency receipt, and the entity registers the foreign investment with the Ministry of Economy and Finance / KOTRA to obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut do not hold a Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act license for domestic KRW clearing, cannot provide compliant statutory foreign exchange transaction receipts, and are not permitted to service a Korean legal entity as its main inbound/outbound banking counterparty. Such platforms may only be used as a very limited ancillary remittance channel through licensed Korean banking partners, and even then corporate treasury departments should avoid routing material inspection-fee proceeds or trade settlements through them because Korean tax and foreign exchange authorities treat the use of unlicensed intermediaries as a red flag.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (highest statutory marginal rate, with lower progressive brackets of 9%, 19%, and 21% on taxable income bands; local income tax surcharge of 2.4% on corporate tax is added)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends to non-residents: 20% income tax plus 10% local surcharge on that income tax (i.e., 2 percentage points), aggregating to 22%. This is imposed on the gross dividend amount at the moment of constructive payment; the rate may be reduced under an applicable DTA, typically to 5% or 10% for portfolio investors and often to 5% for 10% or 25% corporate shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The statutory threshold requiring contemporaneous Local File and Master File preparation is KRW 50 billion in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions with a single foreign related enterprise or group. When the amount of intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceeds this threshold, the Korean corporation must attach the Foreign Related-Party Transaction Schedule to its annual tax return and submit the Local File and Master File within 45 days of the tax filing deadline. For transactions below the threshold, transfer pricing documentation is still required to be prepared and available on call, though not mandatorily filed with the return.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the corporate tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine through the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Law for Coordination of International Tax. A Korean company claiming treaty benefits, or a non-resident intermediary participating in a Korean dividend flow, must perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as executing inspection contracts, managing quality-control personnel, issuing reports, and making management decisions in the declared jurisdiction. If the Korean entity is only a shell, its separate legal personality may be disregarded, all of its income will be attributed to the actual beneficial owner, and the withholding tax rates will be applied at full statutory levels without DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Evasion of Corporate Tax Through Fictitious Testing Fees

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, intentional fraudulent tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to three times the amount of evaded tax. In addition, the National Tax Service imposes a 40% understatement penalty, an 0.075% per-day overdue penalty, and includes the entity in the public disclosure of chronic high-risk abnormal corporate taxpayers.

Director personal liability: When the fraud is authorized or acquiesced in by a director, the corporate veil is pierced under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the director becomes personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and surcharges. The director may be criminally prosecuted for fraud and tax evasion, subject to travel restriction/blacklisting by the Ministry of Justice, and in cases involving embezzlement of corporate funds, criminal asset restitution attaches to the director's personal property.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may deny relief under the tax treaty and re-apply the 22% statutory withholding tax on the entire outbound dividend and royalty amounts. Under Article 6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax, the agency may recompute taxable income at arm's length, levy a 30% erroneous-report penalty, and assess an additional 20% on the resulting tax shortfall; the entity is also blacklisted by the Korean tax registry.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the sham SPV agreement or knowingly operated the shell can be personally assessed for the entire extra tax shortfall plus compound administrative surcharges. Individual civil lawsuits by minority shareholders can compel restitution; criminal charges under the Specialized Crime Act for breach of trust and embezzlement may lead to imprisonment of up to 10 years and the director's personal passport and departure ban under the Immigration Control Act during a fugitive investigation.
AML and Wire Transfer Structuring to Hide Repatriated Profits

Statutory sanction: The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes a fine of up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment of up to two years for unreported cross-border capital transactions. The Framework Act on National Taxes adds a 20% intra-back-tax penalty and the unpaid withholding tax plus a 0.75% monthly surcharge. For money laundering, the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit has the power to freeze the corporate account and refer the director to the Prosecution Service for aggravated punishments under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the company's tax debt when they willfully mismanage the payment rails or conceal revenue. Blacklisting as a high-risk tax debtor is automatic for amounts exceeding statutory thresholds, and the director's travel abroad is restricted pending settlement. Civil liability extends to restitution of the evaded withholding amount, interest, and collection costs, and a criminal conviction triggers disqualification from serving as a director for up to 10 years under Article 9 of the Commercial Act.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Commercial Act. However, all subscription funds must be fully deposited into a blocked capital verification escrow account opened in the name of the company at a licensed domestic bank before the registration application is filed; the bank must issue a certificate of deposit verification confirming full receipt. If any subscriber is a non-resident, the remitted funds must enter Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, and the foreign-invested enterprise registration certificate or capital movement report must be obtained. The escrowed capital may not be released or used until the company is duly registered and the statutory officers provide post-registration instructions to the bank.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, acting through the district tax office with territorial jurisdiction over the domestic place of business, and using the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); for customs/export registration, the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident person establishing or owning a Korean domestic Yuhan Hoesa must first complete incorporation under the Commercial Act at the competent Korean court registry, then file the statutory NTS business registration application, named the business registration (TIN/VAT ID) application under the Value-Added Tax Act, with the competent district tax office within 20 days from the date of business commencement. Documents include the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, domestic lease/office evidence, representative identification, foreign investment report under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act where applicable, and a written appointment of a domestic tax agent if the foreign entity has no residence or place of business in Korea. After review, NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number that simultaneously functions as the corporate income tax ID and VAT ID, requiring no separate VAT number. For export activities, the company must subsequently create its Korea Customs Service account and exporter registration in UNI-PASS and obtain the relevant medical device exporter/notifier registration from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety if not already held.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Korean domestic Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation taxed under the Corporate Tax Act on its worldwide income, with no pass-through classification for Korean tax purposes).

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box or any other pass-through-election for a domestic LLC. Qualifying domestic parent-subsidiary groups may elect the consolidated corporate tax regime under the Corporate Tax Act, but that election is an aggregation method and does not alter the entity-level classification of the Korean LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to companies; every business, including a domestic Yuhan Hoesa, must file business/VAT registration with the NTS from business commencement. Korean law provides a simplified taxpayer regime for certain individual natural-person businesses with annual taxable revenue not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but a corporation cannot use that regime and must complete standard VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods that are physically exported from Korea and services supplied to foreign customers for credit or qualifying offshore use are subject to zero-rated VAT under the zero-rating provisions of the Value-Added Tax Act. The exporter must evidence actual customs clearance, legal exportation from Korea, transfer of title and risk to the overseas counter-party, and the receipt of foreign currency export proceeds. Input VAT incurred in manufacturing titanium orthopedic implants is creditable and refundable once the export zero-rating position is substantiated; additionally, the implants must be manufactured and exported in compliance with the Medical Devices Act.

  • Customs export declaration (KCS UNI-PASS export clearance record, including export bill and manifest data)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or airway bill
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice issued through NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice service
  • Foreign-exchange inward remittance certificate (Korean remittance confirmation or foreign currency purchase certificate) and bank ledger evidencing receipt of export proceeds from abroad

Korea mandates the issuance of electronic tax invoices (ETI) through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system. There is no third-party pre-clearance or fiscal stamping engine; the anti-fraud architecture is based on mandatory NTS electronic transmission, invoice transaction matching, credit-card sales information reporting, cash receipt compulsory issuance in the service sector, and API-based e-Tax Invoice integration from taxpayer ERP systems. Export supplies must be evidenced through zero-rated ETIs and export customs clearance data submitted through KCS UNIPASS into the same NTS audit trail.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service may jointly verify that export declarations, customs clearances, container/air cargo movements, and bank letters of credit or wire remittances are mutually consistent. If export proceeds are not received, or if documents are forged, incomplete, or received late, the permitted zero-rating is reversed and 10% VAT becomes assessable on the full export consideration, together with additional tax, statutory interest, and, in cases of fraudulent or aggravated non-disclosure, criminal referral. The company remains liable to substantiate the export chain for the statutory tax-assessment limitation period, and any refunded input VAT may be reclaimed with penalties where the export zero-rating is disallowed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate with current-account convertibility for genuine trade, services, dividends, and royalties, but capital-account movements are conditional on declarative compliance rather than unrestricted. The KRW is not a fully convertible currency in the sense of unrestricted convertibility for any purpose; the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires that all resident cross-border transactions be supported by an underlying business purpose and be reported through a designated foreign-exchange bank to the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). An inbound direct investment is permitted without an individual central-bank license, but the investor must file a Foreign Investment Notification and the domestic bank must verify the receipt. Profit and dividend remittance is allowed if the foreign-invested company can prove origination from properly notified FDI, has settled corporate tax, and supplies a board resolution, audited financial statements, and a tax clearance certificate. The MOEF can impose prudential regulation, including leverage measures on FX derivatives and temporary convertibility restrictions during a balance-of-payments crisis. In practice, cash flows generated by the orthopedic implant company are freely convertible to KRW for current operating needs, and dividends can be repatriated through the domestic FX bank once the documentary trail is complete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must collect foreign-currency proceeds within 15 months from the date of the export declaration and deposit them into a domestic Foreign-Currency Deposit Account at a designated foreign-exchange bank. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is 0% under normal market conditions: the exporter may retain 100% of the proceeds in foreign currency or sell them for KRW at the market rate. Each receipt must be reported to the FEIS using the export declaration number and the relevant medical-device shipping documents. An extension of the 15-month settlement period may be requested through the designated bank if the exporter demonstrates a genuine collection delay, returned goods, or currency risk. During an emergency balance-of-payments disturbance, the MOEF may temporarily require full or partial surrender, but no emergency surrender regime is currently active.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) is the competent foreign-exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Financial Services Commission (FSC) supervises financial institutions for existing FX and anti-money-laundering compliance obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low; offshore digital EMIs cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for a Korean-resident company because they are neither licensed banks nor electronic financial business operators under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They cannot hold KRW current accounts, access Korea's won settlement systems, or file the FEIS declarations required for capital and trade flows. A foreign EMI may be used only as an ancillary treasury interface for upstream or downstream multi-currency collection, provided the final KRW settlement and statutory reporting occur through a domestic licensed bank. Relying on an offshore EMI for inbound FDI or the collection of export proceeds would invalidate the foreign investment notification, create an unlicensed banking arrangement, and produce an immediate regulatory and AML concern for the domestic bank.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Inflated Costs

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, evasion of corporate tax is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine not exceeding KRW 50 million. Where the evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million, the maximum imprisonment term becomes 10 years. Korean tax authorities will add a 40% additional tax on the fraudulent underpayment and cannot waive the impost.

Director personal liability: The NTS pierces the corporate veil when the LLC is used as a pure device for tax fraud; the director and beneficial shareholder are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Simultaneously, each director who signed the fraudulent tax return is subject to criminal sentencing as a direct co-principal. Once more than KRW 50 million of tax remains unpaid, the NTS publishes the individual's name and places a travel ban (blacklisting) on the director, blocking departure from the Republic of Korea until the debt is settled or a security is posted.
Failure to Withhold Korean Tax on Outbound Royalties / Hidden Profit Remittances

Statutory sanction: Under Article 122-2 of the Corporate Tax Act and Article 47-5 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the withholding agent is liable to pay the under-withheld 22% tax plus a 40% negligent additional tax on the unpaid portion, and daily late-payment penalties of 0.022% per day from the due date. In addition, the Korean tax authority must recalculate the deductible royalty and add back the excess above arm's length as a 22% taxable deemed dividend — destroying the company's loss carryforward claim. Criminal liability attaches under Article 11(2) of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act: failure to withhold is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to KRW 20 million.

Director personal liability: Under Article 45(1) of the Basic Act for National Taxes, the director who is the person required to withhold and remit the tax is personally, jointly, and severally liable with the corporation for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties. The NTS may execute against the director's personal bank accounts, followed by a travel blacklist. If the director actively signed a fraudulent foreign-payment instrument, he will be prosecuted as a principal offender and may serve actual imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Treaty Abuse and Shell Company Conduit for Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 24(1) of the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs denies the treaty benefit and recharacterizes the dividend as Korean source income. The NTS will impose the full 22% withholding tax (20% + 2% local income tax) on the dividend plus a 40% fraud penalty on the under-withheld amount. The company is also subjected to an immediate audit extension for all related party payments. If the arrangement is deemed concealment of taxable income, the director may be prosecuted under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, facing imprisonment up to 10 years when the amount evaded exceeds KRW 300 million.

Director personal liability: The NTS pierces the corporate veil and treats the shell Hong Kong company as the inactive alter ego of the Korean director. The director becomes personally liable for the full corporate tax amount, the 40% additional tax, and interest. The director is also blacklisted: an international travel ban is registered, Korean border-control denies boarding, and all bank accounts and real estate are subject to attachment. In aggravated criminal cases, the director is criminally convicted of tax infidelity and ordered to serve a sentence of 5 to 10 years. This same doctrine applies to any non-resident director who uses the Korean LLC to funnel profits through a third-country conduit to avoid the Korean dividend withholding tax.

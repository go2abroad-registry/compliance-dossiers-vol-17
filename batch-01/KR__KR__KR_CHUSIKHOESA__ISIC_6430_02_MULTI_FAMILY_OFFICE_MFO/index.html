Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a licensed discretionary multi-family office, a virtual office, shared co-working address, or commercial mailbox is rejected by the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service, the National Tax Service, and licensed custodian banks. Under Article 19 of the Commercial Act and Article 12(2) of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the registered office must be the actual place where corporate management is exercised, personnel are employed, compliance records are maintained, and client files are stored. A serviced office may be acceptable only if it is a dedicated leased unit with full-time staff and physical management autonomy, but a mere postal address is never sufficient because the National Tax Service will reclassify the corporate seat for local tax purposes and banks will refuse to open custody and interbank accounts.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-in capital under the Korean Commercial Act for a Chusik Hoesa. The promoters may fix the initial capital in the articles of incorporation, but the full subscription amount for the shares issued at incorporation must be paid in cash and deposited in a Korean bank before the registration application is filed. The bank issues a Capital Deposit Certificate evidencing the payment; the amount is not a blocked escrow in the ordinary sense, but it must remain on deposit and cannot be withdrawn for any purpose other than corporate expenses after registration is completed. If the company applies for a financial investment business license or registration under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act, the Financial Services Commission will require minimum equity capital applicable to the license category, such as discretionary investment management or investment advisory, and a bank verification of paid-in capital must accompany the license dossier.

Funding & unlock path: Before commencing operations, the registered office must obtain a certification of fire prevention from the relevant fire station in the form of an automatic fire suppression system inspection and evacuation plan approval; the building must have a certificate of occupancy issued by the Gu district office. The employer must register with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency for workplace safety compliance, conduct a risk assessment, appoint a safety and health manager, and maintain fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, exit signs, and an emergency evacuation route per the Fire Fighting Facilities Maintenance Act and the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - no turnover threshold triggers registration; any person making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT regardless of volume. The KRW 80,000,000 simplified-taxpayer threshold applies only to certain individual entrepreneurs and does not exempt a Chusik Hoesa from registration or ordinary invoice duties.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Korea qualify for 0% VAT when an export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service, the goods are carried out under international transport documents, and export proceeds are received in foreign currency. Services supplied to a non-resident or foreign corporation qualify for 0% VAT where the service is used or consumed outside Korea; for a Multi-Family Office, discretionary portfolio advisory and asset-administration services to foreign families or offshore funds may be zero-rated only if the recipient is non-resident, the benefit is enjoyed outside Korea, and the fee is collected from abroad. Services used in Korea or supplied to a Korean recipient are taxable at 10%, and financial service activities that are exempt under the Value-Added Tax Act (such as certain custodial or fund services) do not generate input-VAT credits.

  • Export Goods Declaration (export clearance/export permit) filed with Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing international transit
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice issued and transmitted through HomeTax
  • Foreign Exchange Purchase Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank (or equivalent foreign-currency receipt certificate) proving actual collection and repatriation of the export price

Electronic tax invoices are required for suppliers meeting the statutory e-invoice threshold, generally prior-year taxable supplies of KRW 100,000,000 or more; such suppliers must issue and transmit all tax invoices, including zero-rated export invoices, to NTS through HomeTax in structured electronic form, generally by the day following issuance. Large taxpayers and certified e-invoice service providers connect through HomeTax EDI/API interfaces. There is no physical fiscal stamp or governmental pre-clearance approval; anti-fraud control is executed through NTS HomeTax issue-number reconciliation, mandatory cash receipt reporting, payment card sales data, and matching with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly match export declarations, transport documents, tax invoices, and foreign-exchange receipts. A zero-rating claim without complete documentary proof or actual foreign remittance is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply; NTS assesses 10% VAT plus penalty taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act (up to 40% of the unlawful refund or understated tax, plus late-payment interest) and retroactively claws back any erroneous export refund. Deliberately fabricated export documentation is prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for tax evasion, with imprisonment up to five years, fines up to three times the tax evaded, and loss of Customs export privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalised current account regime, allowing unbounded conversion for trade and service transactions subject to documentary verification and post-trade reporting. The capital account, however, while broadly open, remains subject to mandatory advance reporting, registration requirements for foreign direct investment (FDI) under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), and prudential limits on debt-related inbound flows. For a multi-family office (MFO) structured as a Chusik Hoesa, inbound equity injections must be declared to the MOEF via a designated foreign exchange bank to obtain a certificate of foreign investment registration, which then permits KRW conversion at market rates. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders require withholding tax compliance and a foreign exchange bank's confirmation of the underlying audited financial statements; there is no surrender requirement but the resident entity must file monthly foreign exchange transaction reports if an enterprise is classified as a business entity under FETA. Discretionary portfolio services involving cross-border securities or derivatives trigger further licensing under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (FSCMA) and may require the MFO to hold a financial investment business license from the Financial Services Commission, making full capital account convertibility conditional on both regulatory licensing and transactional reporting, rather than on any blanket currency control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 16 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the accompanying Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within 90 days from the export date (extendable to 180 days for general merchandise or longer for high-value goods under prior bank approval). There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; exporters may hold foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign currency accounts or convert them at market rates, but they must be received in a domestic designated foreign exchange bank account and reported to the Bank of Korea within 30 days of collection via the Foreign Exchange Information Network. Failure to collect within the statutory timeline requires an explanation and may result in administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) exercising foreign exchange policy oversight and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) as the core regulatory statute; the Bank of Korea also administers the Foreign Exchange Information Network and delegates day-to-day approval authority to designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility: South Korea does not recognise offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) or virtual account providers as licensed foreign exchange banks under FETA. Only designated domestic financial institutions (banks and certain brokerage houses) are permitted to execute foreign exchange transactions, open foreign currency accounts, and process cross-border settlement on behalf of resident corporate entities. An overseas fintech EMI cannot service the MFO's KRW current account or execute SWIFT interbank transfers with a valid Korean purpose code, and using such a provider would violate foreign exchange reporting obligations, leading to registration cancellation, fines, and potential criminal liability for the directors. Therefore, the MFO must maintain all operational and cross-border accounts with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank, using fintech solutions only for ancillary payroll or reconciliation dashboards that integrate via open banking APIs without touching actual FX clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation) is required where the annual consolidated gross revenue of the Korean taxpayer equals or exceeds KRW 10 billion; Master File is required when such revenue reaches KRW 100 billion. For all intercompany cross-border transactions exceeding KRW 1 billion, a detailed functional and risk analysis, arm's length benchmark, and transfer pricing reports must be available upon request. The National Tax Service may impose documentation penalties up to 30% of the adjusted amount where required documentation is not maintained.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute analogous to Gulf states, but the equivalent doctrines are embedded in the Corporate Tax Act and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The tax authority applies a substance-over-form test and may recharacterize transactions through the 'actual place of management' doctrine. For a multi-family office, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be performed by employees and systems located in Korea: discretionary investment decisions, contractual due diligence, portfolio risk management, trade execution, and custody wire instruction approval. If these CIGA are in fact performed abroad and the Korean entity is merely a contracting shell, the Korean corporate veil may be pierced, and the entity may be denied treaty benefits on its own outbound payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service will re-determine income upward under Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and impose an under-reporting surcharge of 10% of the adjusted tax, plus daily overdue interest at 0.022% per day. If documentation is missing, an additional penalty up to 30% of the adjusted amount may apply. If the adjustment exceeds a material threshold and is willful, the matter is referred for criminal charging under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act with fines of up to three times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized the false transfer prices are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties; the National Tax Service can attach personal bank accounts, real estate, and receivables. Civil restitution orders may pierce the corporate veil, and travel bans (blacklisting as delinquent taxpayers) are automatically imposed for unpaid arrears exceeding a statutory threshold. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 3 years; if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, the recommended minimum imprisonment rises to 2 years and may reach 5 years for organized fraud.
Unlicensed Asset Management, AML, and Custody Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Article 447 of the FSCMA, an unregistered financial investment business is punishable by imprisonment of not more than 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 200 million, or both. For aggravating circumstances involving systemic AML failures, the enforcement authorities may also impose administrative fines under the Financial Transactions Reports Act and orders to terminate business. The Financial Supervisory Service may refer the case to the prosecution with recommendation for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for the illegal profits derived from unlicensed asset management and for any client losses caused by unlawful execution. Shell Company, Treaty Shopping and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 2-3 of the Corporate Tax Act allows the tax authority to disregard a corporate entity that is not established for real business purposes, and Korean tax treaties contain, or incorporate through OECD rules, a limitation-on-benefits or principal purpose test. A sham entity is taxed as a transparent vehicle, and all treaty relief is denied. In addition, a penalty of 50% of the under-withheld tax is imposed for intentional misuse of a treaty exemption or reduction.

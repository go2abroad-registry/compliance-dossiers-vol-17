Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Office (Commercial Registration Division of the Local District Court; in Korean, beop-won deung-gi-gwa / sang-eop-deung-gi-so) having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office, administered by the Courts Administration Office of the Supreme Court of Korea; electronic filing is accepted through the Internet Registration System (IROS / inteonet-deung-gi-so).

Governing statute

Korean Commercial Act (Korea Sangbeop), Act No. 1000, promulgated December 20, 1962, as amended most recently by Act No. 19111 (January 2, 2023); in particular Articles 288 through 371 (formation and governance of a stock corporation / chusik hoesa), Article 289 (matters to be stated in the Articles of Incorporation), Article 295 (incorporators' subscription), Article 301 (deposit of subscription price), Article 305 (the Inaugural General Meeting), Article 317 (registration of incorporation within three weeks of the inaugural meeting), and Articles 386 through 408 (representative director and statutory officers). For non-resident investors the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (Act No. 19036, 2022), Articles 2 and 5, and the Enforcement Decree provisions on foreign direct investment notification also apply.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Incorporation of a Stock Corporation (jusik-hoesa-seollib-deunggi-sincheongseo) filed under Article 317 of the Korean Commercial Act, accompanied by the notarized Articles of Incorporation (jeong-gwan), the Inaugural General Meeting minutes, the Capital Subscription Deposit Certificate, and the written consents to registration of the statutory officers.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None as a strict statutory minimum: the former minimum paid-up capital of KRW 50,000,000 for stock corporations was abolished by Act No. 10267, effective April 15, 2012, and the Korean Commercial Act now imposes no minimum capital floor. Nevertheless, at least one share must be validly issued and the entire subscription price must be deposited, prior to the Inaugural General Meeting, into an escrow-type capital deposit account maintained with a domestic commercial bank or licensed securities brokerage in Korea; the depository must issue a Capital Subscription Deposit Certificate (jabon-geum-nap-ip-bogwan-jeungmyeongseo) evidencing the deposit, and the deposited funds are blocked from withdrawal until the incorporation registration has been completed by the court. For a single-family office the recommended operational substance is approximately KRW 100,000,000 (about USD 74,000) so as to support bank onboarding, enhanced due diligence, and the private banking and securities custody arrangements contemplated by the target payment rails.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial civil-defense fire permits, structural load inspections, or workplace safety certification because the company will operate only as a leasehold administrative office for a family-office investment holding business. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea via the HomeTax electronic portal at https://www.hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance and export declarations are administered separately by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal at https://unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to carry on a taxable business in the Republic of Korea must first appoint a legal tax representative if it has no domestic place of business. The entity or its tax representative must file an Application for Business Registration with the relevant district tax office of the National Tax Service, either in writing or through HomeTax, accompanied by the entity's constitutional documents, evidence of foreign registration, certification of the representative director and address, and proof of the domestic place of business or tax representative appointment. Upon approval, the NTS issues the Business Registration Number, a ten-digit identifier that functions simultaneously as the VAT identification number and the general national tax identification number for corporate tax, VAT, customs declarations, e-invoice exchange and foreign investment reporting. No separate TIN or VAT ID is issued to a non-resident entity without a Korean taxable establishment, but such an entity must still obtain a Business Registration Number if it makes supplies subject to Korean VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity intending to carry on a taxable business in the Republic of Korea must first appoint a legal tax representative if it has no domestic place of business. The entity or its tax representative must file an Application for Business Registration with the relevant district tax office of the National Tax Service, either in writing or through HomeTax, accompanied by the entity's constitutional documents, evidence of foreign registration, certification of the representative director and address, and proof of the domestic place of business or tax representative appointment. Upon approval, the NTS issues the Business Registration Number, a ten-digit identifier that functions simultaneously as the VAT identification number and the general national tax identification number for corporate tax, VAT, customs declarations, e-invoice exchange and foreign investment reporting. No separate TIN or VAT ID is issued to a non-resident entity without a Korean taxable establishment, but such an entity must still obtain a Business Registration Number if it makes supplies subject to Korean VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not operate a check-the-box or elective entity classification regime for domestic corporations. A Chusik Hoesa is uniformly taxable as a resident corporation at the entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules are statutorily applied, but no elective hybrid mismatch classification is available to a Korean-resident company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold is prescribed for VAT registration: any person or entity supplying taxable goods or services in the Republic of Korea is required to register from the date of business commencement, irrespective of annual turnover. A separate simplified taxable person regime applies only to eligible natural persons whose annual taxable revenue is below the statutory threshold currently prescribed by Presidential Decree (approximately KRW 80,000,000, subject to legislative indexation); this does not exempt them from the obligation to obtain a Business Registration Number but only affects reporting and liability computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate applies where legal ownership of goods is transferred to a foreign purchaser and the goods are physically exported from Korea under the customs export procedure, as evidenced by an accepted export declaration. Zero-rating also applies to qualifying services performed outside Korea, services supplied to non-resident recipients and paid in foreign currency in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, international transportation services, and services directly connected with exported goods. The supplier must be able to evidence the actual export from Korea and the receipt of sales proceeds in convertible foreign currency within the statutory repatriation window.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry / Declaration of Export Goods) filed with and returned by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or multimodal transport document evidencing physical exportation from Korea
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Payment Confirmation / Foreign Exchange Transaction Certificate (equivalent to a Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate, FIRC) issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export sales contract, and any invoice-level ledger reconciling the Korean supplier, foreign buyer, zero-rated supply value, and foreign currency remittance

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all corporations, including a Chusik Hoesa. Invoices must be issued through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or through an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice service provider using machine-readable XML/API interfaces; the invoice data is transmitted to the NTS at the time of issuance or within the statutory reporting period, and the NTS assigns a unique tax invoice number for matching in VAT returns. The Republic of Korea does not currently operate a transactional fiscal stamping device or a pre-clearance model requiring government approval before invoice issuance; integrity is maintained through NTS-controlled invoice numbering, cross-matching against VAT filings, and penalty surcharges for non-issued, erroneous, or late-transmitted electronic tax invoices.

The National Tax Service and the Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims by matching export declarations, electronic tax invoices, foreign exchange transaction records, and cargo movements. Where the required customs documentation or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is incomplete or false, the NTS reverses the zero-rating and assesses VAT at 10% on the supply value, together with statutory interest. Additional penalties are imposable under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including negligent underreporting penalties, intentional underreporting and fraud penalties up to 40% of the evaded tax, invoice-related penalties of 1% of the supply value per missing or false electronic tax invoice, and criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act in cases of fraudulent substitution or fabricated export documentation. The ordinary reassessment limitation period is five years, extended to ten years for fraudulent concealment, allowing retrospective clawback of unsubstantiated zero-rated export claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but closely supervised foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for bona fide current account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, dividends, and profit remittances, subject to standard bank verification and supporting documentation. Exporters are not required to surrender hard currency; however, export proceeds must be collected within the statutory collection period, normally six months from the date of export declaration, and any delay must be justified and extended via the reporting foreign exchange bank or BOK. Capital account transactions are more restricted: foreign direct investment, overseas borrowing, external lending, and portfolio investments require notification, registration, or approval depending on size and counterparty. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa structured as a single family office, inbound equity injections and subsequent outbound distributions or loan repayments must be booked through a designated foreign exchange bank using the correct transaction codes and supporting corporate documents. The Government has gradually deregulated since the 1997 and 2008 crises, but macro-prudential limits on currency mismatches, short-term external borrowing, and derivative positions remain. Profit remittance hurdles are low if audited financial statements, tax compliance certificates, and board resolutions are presented; dividends and liquidation proceeds are generally freely remittable after withholding tax obligations. Overall, the won is convertible for ordinary trade and investment flows, but every capital transaction must be traceable, documented, and aligned with BOK reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage conversion or surrender requirement for Korean export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in designated foreign exchange accounts. However, the export receipts must be collected within 6 months from the export declaration date under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related regulations. If collection is delayed beyond 6 months, the exporter must file a delay report with the relevant foreign exchange bank and obtain an extension, typically limited to another 6 months unless exceptional circumstances are proven.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with policy and enforcement cooperation from the Ministry of Economy and Finance through designated foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) have very limited viability in South Korea for corporate treasury operations. Korean law does not recognize a standalone EMI charter equivalent to European e-money institutions; entities are classified as electronic financial businesses under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and are restricted to payment services, not deposit-taking or corporate equity capital escrow. They cannot issue accounts that support capital verification, foreign investor registration, FX conversion, or trade settlement in a legally compliant manner. Consequently, fintech EMIs are not a viable alternative for the Chusik Hoesa single family office; all regulated cross-border liquidity clearing must route through licensed domestic banks with foreign exchange dealing authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Chusik Hoesa must be structured as foreign direct investment equity contributions. The foreign investor must file a foreign investment notification with a designated foreign exchange bank or the Invest KOREA center before remitting funds, obtain a foreign investment registration certificate, open a domestic foreign currency account and a KRW corporate operating account, and wire the funds via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose code for equity investment or capital contribution. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the source of funds, converts foreign currency to KRW through the domestic foreign exchange market at the market or agreed rate, and issues a capital verification certificate confirming the paid-in capital. The transaction must be recorded with BOK/foreign exchange bank reporting; a post-investment report must be filed within 30 days of capital settlement, and the company's commercial registry must reflect the capital increase before any withdrawal is permitted.Outbound trade and operational disbursements are cleared through standard banking rails: SWIFT MT103 direct payments for supplier invoices, service fees, dividends, royalties, and intercompany payments; Commercial Letters of Credit (L/Cs) for purchase contracts of goods requiring documentary security; and Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-risk open-account deliveries or sample transactions. All outbound payments must be accompanied by underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax clearance documents, and must use approved Korean purpose-of-payment codes. Dividend remittances require board approval, audited financial statements, tax payment confirmations, and a remittance resolution. Foreign exchange banks will screen every transaction against the Bank of Korea, FATF, US OFAC, EU, and other applicable sanctions lists before execution.Forward foreign exchange contracts with a documented real economic exposure, such as future export receipts, import payables, or equity repatriation obligationsNon-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts for KRW hedging executed through onshore banks or offshore counterparties in accordance with BOK macro-prudential limitsPlain vanilla Korean won currency options and exchange-traded (or OTC) swaps used solely for hedging the family office's balance-sheet currency risk, with no speculative or naked derivative positioning

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) have very limited viability in South Korea for corporate treasury operations. Korean law does not recognize a standalone EMI charter equivalent to European e-money institutions; entities are classified as electronic financial businesses under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and are restricted to payment services, not deposit-taking or corporate equity capital escrow. They cannot issue accounts that support capital verification, foreign investor registration, FX conversion, or trade settlement in a legally compliant manner. Consequently, fintech EMIs are not a viable alternative for the Chusik Hoesa single family office; all regulated cross-border liquidity clearing must route through licensed domestic banks with foreign exchange dealing authority.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 30 billion; 22% for KRW 20 billion to 30 billion; 20% for KRW 200 million to 20 billion; 10% for first KRW 200 million; plus 10% local income tax surcharge (effective top rate 27.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax on dividends plus 2% local income tax surcharge) under Korean domestic law, unless reduced by a Double Tax Agreement; typical DTA rates range from 5% to 15% depending on ownership and treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 500 million for annual tangible-asset transactions, KRW 50 million for intangible-property or royalty transactions, and KRW 50 million for services, per foreign related party per year; local file mandatory above these thresholds. Master file required if consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a BVI-style Economic Substance Regulation. However, substance-over-form, place-of-effective-management, and CFC rules apply. A Chusik Hoesa acting as a single-family office must maintain real business presence and perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea to retain Korean tax residence and treaty access. A purely passive shell lacking employees, office, or meeting minutes may be disqualified under BEPS Action 5 and the Principal Purpose Test, triggering full Korean taxation and treaty denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 10 and 11 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine not exceeding 3 times the evaded tax (aggravated to 5 years if evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million). Additionally, a 40% negligence penalty under the Basic National Tax Collection Act and public disclosure on the tax-evader blacklist may apply.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil when the company is a sham or the director abused limited liability. Directors can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under the actual-business-operator doctrine; assets may be frozen and overseas travel banned. Criminal conviction leads to imprisonment, disqualification from board service, and civil restitution actions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4-8, the National Tax Service may adjust taxable income to the arm's length price, impose a 10% penalty on the underreported tax amount, and apply daily surcharges of up to 0.022%. Willful violations attract criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing certification or knowingly approve inflated outbound payments may be treated as actual co-taxpayers; personal assets can be attached for the adjusted corporate tax. Travel bans, criminal indictment for aiding and abetting tax evasion, and breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims by family beneficiaries or minority shareholders are realistic consequences.
Willful Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Taxes / Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agents are liable for the unpaid tax plus additional charges of 10% (20% or 40% if intentional) under the Personal Income Tax Act and the Basic National Tax Collection Act. Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment of up to 2 years or a fine of up to 2 times the amount not withheld. Treaty benefits may be denied retroactively, with interest on all underpaid taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors, as statutory withholding agents of the Chusik Hoesa, are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties. Korean courts may issue overseas travel bans and enforce collection against director personal assets. Criminal conviction results in imprisonment, disqualification from corporate directorship, and severe reputational damage; in egregious cases, the corporate veil is pierced to hold directors personally accountable for the entire unpaid tax and criminal fines.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole statutory registered office for a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaging in bulk petrochemical polymer wholesale. The National Tax Service and district registration courts require a verifiable physical place of business under the Direct Control of the company where books and management are kept. A serviced-office address may be accepted only if there is a real leased desk, office signage, structured meeting space, dedicated mail handling, and a resident director or employee; however, most Korean foreign-exchange banks will reject or de-risk a virtual-office corporate client for LC-based petrochemical trading on anti-money-laundering and residence grounds. An actual registered office and warehouse-risk documentation are mandatory for KYC, VAT and corporate tax invoicing, and trade finance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Korean Commercial Act sets no minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the amount of capital is fixed in the Articles of Incorporation and must be fully paid into a temporary incorporation bank account before registration. If the foreign investor remits KRW 100,000,000 or more and holds at least 10 percent voting ownership, an FDI report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act must be filed with a designated bank or KOTRA, which will issue a foreign investment certificate. For smaller remittances, a foreign exchange transaction report is sufficient. There is no blocked escrow account requirement; the subscribed capital is held in the temporary incorporation account and is released only after the incorporation registration certificate and business registration certificate are obtained.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety System Approval and Completion Inspection under the Fire Service Act must be obtained before warehouse occupancy; automatic fire alarm, extinguishers, hydrants and emergency exit lighting are mandatory based on storage floor area. A structural load and racking safety certification prepared by a licensed structural engineer is required for high-bay racking. The company must file an Occupational Safety and Health initiation report with KOSHA, complete workplace risk assessments, provide personal protective equipment and first aid, and conduct fire drills. No separate civil defense shelter approval is required for a general warehouse unless the local ordinance classifies the building as a civil defense facility; if so, a structural civil defense shelter permit must be obtained from the local government before operation.

Paid-in capital rule: The Korean Commercial Act sets no minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the amount of capital is fixed in the Articles of Incorporation and must be fully paid into a temporary incorporation bank account before registration. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains an open but regulated current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations. Ordinary trade- and service-related remittances are freely convertible through authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital-account transactions operate on a positive-list and reporting basis: direct foreign investment, intercompany loans, cross-border guarantees, and derivative transactions are permitted when filed or approved under the FETA. The Korean won is domestically fully usable but cannot be circulated offshore in unrestricted volume; an offshore non-deliverable market exists, while onshore KRW conversion must be executed through banks under the real-name transaction system and reported via the BOK-managed Foreign Exchange Information System. Export proceeds must be collected through a Korean authorized bank and either retained in a foreign-currency account or converted into KRW, creating a transparent audit trail. Profit remittance hurdles for a Chusik Hoesa are present but manageable: the company must maintain an active foreign-invested company registration with KOTRA/Invest Korea, file verified financial statements, settle applicable Korean corporate and withholding taxes, and present board minutes confirming dividends together with the bank's underlying FX settlement report. Each outgoing dividend must pass independent source-of-funds and sanctions screening before remittance. Liquidation proceeds are repatriable after tax clearance and cancellation of the foreign-investor registration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA, export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is approved by MOEF. Once received, the foreign currency must be deposited without undue delay into a foreign-currency account at an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank or sold to that bank. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into KRW; the effective mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, and the exporter may hold the proceeds in foreign currency. However, when conversion is needed, it must be executed through the authorized bank, marked with the appropriate trade settlement purpose code, and the export settlement must be reported in the BOK trade settlement statistics. Failure to meet the statutory receipt deadline creates customs enforcement and documentary penalty exposure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the central bank and foreign exchange oversight authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the policy-setter for FX and capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a principal onshore treasury rail. An offshore fintech EMI is not automatically barred from invoicing or ancillary payments to Korean counterparties, but it cannot open a domestic KRW current account, cannot provide Korean electronic settlement instructions, and is not recognized as a foreign exchange business operator under FETA. Korean Tier-1 banks will not accept EMI accounts for statutory foreign-investment capital verification or FX reporting. For a bulk petrochemical trading Chusik Hoesa, use of a fintech EMI is limited to peripheral multi-currency cash concentration outside Korea; all regulated KRW, trade finance, LC, and FX hedging must pass through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a foreign investor establishing a Korean Chusik Hoesa: (1) open a Foreign Investor Deposit Account with a Tier-1 authorized foreign exchange bank in the name of the foreign investor; (2) remit paid-in capital via SWIFT MT103 using the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code for direct equity investment in a domestic corporation; (3) the bank credits the Foreign Investor Deposit Account and the investor submits a Foreign Investment Report through KOTRA/Invest Korea or the bank before or within the statutory window; (4) after the bank issues the Foreign Investment Confirmation Certificate, the investor incorporates the Chusik Hoesa at the relevant Korean Commercial Court registry; (5) transfer the verified investment funds from the escrow-type deposit account into the corporation's KRW operating account and, for capital verification, retain the deposit certificate, foreign investment certificate, bank receipt, and corporate registration certificate in the treasury records.Use SWIFT-based documentary credit as the primary rail. The Korean corporate bank opens an irrevocable documentary L/C via SWIFT MT700 in favor of the overseas polymer producer for each bulk import lot. Settlement on presentation of UCP600-compliant documents is executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 through the bank's clearing channel. For smaller or repeated contracts, documents against payment (DP) and documents against acceptance (DA) collections are permitted. All outbound disbursements must align with Korea Customs import declarations and be booked against the authorized FX settlement code; any KRW conversion requires a foreign exchange transaction report. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% central headline rate (10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 20% on the next tranche; 22% on the excess), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing a top combined rate of 24.2%

22% (20% corporate-income-tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge), before application of a bilateral DTA

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate cross-border transactions with specially related parties for Master File/Local File preparation; KRW 1 trillion group consolidated revenue for Country-by-Country Report preparation; contemporaneous TP documentation may be required earlier upon examination order.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer a separate ESR filing regime, but the OECD substance standard is implemented through the Act for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and judicial substance-over-form analysis. A Chusik Hoesa must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea through employees, management decisions, office premises, and risk-bearing capacity. If a foreign-owned Korean entity is found to be a shell lacking real decision-making, the NTS may disregard its separate existence and attribute trading income to the actual foreign principal; CFC rules similarly attribute low-taxed passive income to Korean shareholders. This is the functional Korean analogue to ESR and the CIGA analysis.

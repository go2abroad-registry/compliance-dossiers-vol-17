Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected by the National Tax Service, the relevant port authority, and domestic banks for the ISIC 5224 bulk port stevedoring line. The National Tax Service requires a substantive business establishment for business registration under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries port-terminal licensing regime requires an operational berth-side office or a registered office with demonstrable operational capacity. Domestic Korean banks, including KEB Hana, Shinhan, and Woori, require a physical site visit, verification of the lease contract, and confirmation that the registered office is staffed during business hours before corporate account opening. A PO-box-only address is not acceptable for this industry, because stevedoring operations require equipment, labour, insurance, port access, and customs records physically rooted in the terminal office. Virtual-office use would constitute a false representation of place of effective management and may void director liability protections under the Commercial Act.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under Korean law. All shares subscribed at incorporation must be fully paid in cash or in-kind before registration. The paid-in capital must be deposited into a provisional bank account opened in the name of the company; this is not a blocked escrow account, but withdrawal before completion of incorporation is prohibited under the Commercial Act. If incorporation is abandoned, the deposited capital is refundable to the subscribers in accordance with the statutory refund procedures, and in the case of in-kind contributions, the contribution must be inspected by a court-appointed examiner unless exempted or no-par-value rules apply.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Permit under the Act on Fire Prevention, Firefighting and Disaster Management, issued by the local Fire Service Headquarters after inspection of hydrants, extinguishing systems, escape routes, fire doors, and hazardous cargo receiving areas; structural safety and load-capacity inspection of quay cranes, conveyor trestles, silos, and pier decking by a registered structural engineer; Occupational Safety and Health clearance from KOSHA for bulk cargo handling, vessel mooring, confined-space entry, dust exposure, and heavy equipment operations; and, if hazardous quantities of dangerous substances are stored, a Dangerous Materials Storage Permit and separate emergency response approval from the local fire authority.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax. Korea Customs Service administers the UNI-PASS import/export clearance portal for customs and trade records.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean taxable footprint, the statutory procedure is to file a business registration application with the district tax office via HomeTax within 20 days of business commencement. The applicant must submit the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, proof of the Korean place of business, director identification and, for foreign non-resident principals, a domestic tax representative appointment letter. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that simultaneously constitutes the corporate TIN, corporate income tax identifier, and VAT registration number. A foreign entity without a Korean place of business that nevertheless makes VAT-taxable digital or other supplies in Korea must obtain a Korean VAT registration through the HomeTax electronic portal; where no Korean establishment is created, a domestic tax representative is mandatory. The port stevedoring business must first satisfy the applicable Korean maritime port cargo-handling/service provider licensing requirement before tax registration, and the registered domestic business address will be the place of supply and VAT collection posting point.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean taxable footprint, the statutory procedure is to file a business registration application with the district tax office via HomeTax within 20 days of business commencement. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea has no check-the-box regime analogous to the United States and no elective hybrid-mismatch classification mechanism. A Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxable as a resident corporation under the Corporate Tax Act; any consolidated corporate taxation available under Korean law is a statutory group taxation regime, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the simplified taxpayer threshold for small businesses, but it does not excuse a port terminal stevedoring Chusik Hoesa from mandatory VAT registration. A business with a Korean place of business is required to register upon commencement of a taxable business, regardless of turnover; a foreign non-resident supplier with a fixed place of business or any other VAT-nexus-creating supply in Korea is likewise required to register through HomeTax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act, goods physically exported from Korea are zero-rated where the customs export declaration, shipment documentation and foreign exchange receipt substantiate the export. Qualifying services related to international trade, including stevedoring and bulk cargo loading/unloading services provided for goods exported through a Korean port, may be zero-rated if the service is supplied in the course of or incidental to the export of such goods and the consideration is collected in a manner consistent with the VAT Enforcement Decree. Port services connected with domestic cargo or with import clearance of goods into Korea remain subject to VAT at the standard rate.

  • UNI-PASS electronic Export Declaration (export clearance confirmation with export declaration number)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and weight list
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent multimodal transport document showing the cargo destined from a Korean port to a foreign place
  • Certificate of receipt of export proceeds issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing settlement in foreign currency
  • Terminal stevedoring contract, work order and service completion certificate covering the bulk port handling operation
  • Cargo manifest/terminal tally sheet or container record matching the loading operation to the export declaration

Electronic tax invoicing through the NTS HomeTax system is mandatory. Every VAT invoice issued by a registered business to another business must be an electronic tax invoice transmitted to the NTS with the statutory invoice number assigned by the system. For export and cross-border trade flows, the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal operates electronic export/import clearance, requiring data exchange with shipping agents and the NTS. Korea does not maintain a separate digital fiscal stamp, stamp duty, or receipt-stamping system for ordinary VAT; the anti-fraud control is embedded in the mandatory real-time electronic invoice transmission and customs clearance records.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service may audit the linkage between the customs export declaration, bill of lading, terminal service records and foreign exchange remittance evidence. If zero-rating is not substantiated, or invoices or customs documents are false or fictitious, the NTS will claw back the zero rate and assess the 10% VAT plus statutory additional taxes for failure to report, underreporting and late payment, with penalty interest running until payment. Deliberately fraudulent export zero-rating or fabricated stevedoring/customs documentation is subject to criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Customs Act, including imprisonment and fines, and the responsible directors or Korean tax representative may be jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

KRW is a managed floating currency with current account transactions largely liberalised under Article 4 / 5 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Import/export trade settlement and service payments can pass through a licensed foreign exchange bank after submission of underlying contracts, invoices, and customs documents. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI), securities investment, loans, and derivatives, are subject to either advance declaration, approval, or post-settlement reporting to the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). There is no overnight surrender ratio for export proceeds, but foreign currency earnings must be retained in a domestic foreign exchange account or sold to a licensed bank within allowed settlement periods. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are current account transactions if the entity produces audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board dividend resolutions; however the bank may apply a currency conversion screening for excessive cash sweeps or round-tripping. Non-resident won accounts are closely watched and offshore won clearing is disconnected from domestic transfer rails, so convertibility is ultimately conditional on KYC, tax status, and underlying economic substance. For a port stevedoring terminal, tariff receivables from non-resident owners, equipment imports, and dividend payments require an unbroken paper trail and FEIS filing; there is no meaningful unofficial parallel market for KRW.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Statutory timeline: export proceeds must be collected through a Korean foreign exchange bank and reported to FEIS within 180 days of the export declaration date, unless a longer credit period is approved in advance by the Ministry of Economy and Finance or notified via the relevant foreign exchange bank. If not converted to KRW, the full amount must be credited to a domestic foreign currency account at a Korean bank; conversion at the bank is permitted for qualifying import settlements, external debt repayments, or dividend payments. Any failure to bring proceeds into Korea or to report settlement is treated as an unrepatriated export collection and may trigger a foreign exchange audit.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), supported by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) as the regulatory backbone under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs and cross-border payment fintechs are structurally barred from acting as the primary settlement bank for a Korean Chusik Hoesa because only licensed domestic foreign exchange banks may execute KRW settlement, receive foreign investment capital, handle export proceeds, or clear outbound FX payments under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. An EMI could only be used for pre-onboarding expense logistics or as a non-ledger coordination tool, and all actual treasury transactions must be routed through a tier-one Korean bank. Using an EMI without a Korean foreign exchange bank licence would create unlawful foreign exchange dealing, expose the entity to FEIS penalties, and break the capital verification chain required by the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% for taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% for taxable income above KRW 200 million and up to KRW 20 billion; 21% for taxable income above KRW 20 billion and up to KRW 300 billion; 24% for taxable income above KRW 300 billion (exclusive of the 10% local income tax surcharge, making the aggregate headline burden 9.9%, 20.9%, 23.1% and 26.4% respectively).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory rate on outbound dividends to non-resident corporate shareholders: 20% corporate withholding tax under the Corporate Tax Act plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding tax; treaty-reduced rates apply only after administrative relief is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 in annual international related-party transactions for a mandatory Local File; a Master File is required when the multinational enterprise group has consolidated annual revenue of at least KRW 1,000,000,000,000 and the Korean Chusik Hoesa is a participating group entity; a Country-by-Country Report is required at the group level for consolidated revenue above the KRW 1 trillion threshold.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations by that exact name, but the substance over form principle under the Basic National Tax Act and the general anti-abuse rule allow the tax authority to disregard a corporate vehicle that lacks genuine economic operations. A Korea Chusik Hoesa operating a bulk port stevedoring terminal must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea: employment of stevedoring labor, operation of cranes and cargo handling equipment, maintenance of the port terminal concession, execution of service contracts, invoicing from Korean offices, and exercise of board-level management functions in Korea. A foreign-owned shell that merely invoices port services and relocates profits to a zero-tax jurisdiction without Korean employees, assets or decision-making will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and the related-party transaction may be reconstructed as a dividend or capital contribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Basic National Tax Act Articles 47-2 through 47-5, the tax authority imposes a 40% fraudulent underreporting surcharge on the additionally assessed tax, daily late payment surcharge of 0.03% per day, statutory delinquency interest, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3; the offender may receive imprisonment for up to 3 years and a fine of up to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil if the Chusik Hoesa is found to be a facade or conduit; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, and the representative director is blacklisted as a delinquent taxpayer, subject to a travel ban, asset seizure and compulsory examination. Criminal prosecution may result in personal imprisonment and a personal fine, and the director is disqualified from serving on any Korean corporate board for the duration of the criminal sanction.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion on Dividends, Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Tax Act and the Income Tax Act, the withholding agent becomes personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax and is additionally assessed a withholding penalty of 10% of the withheld amount where the failure to withhold is negligent, with daily surcharges and interest; if the non-payment exceeds the statutory threshold, the criminal provisions of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act apply with imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: The representative director and the finance director who authorized the wire remittance to the foreign payee are jointly liable for the withheld tax and penalties; the NTS attaches personal real estate and bank accounts, the director receives a travel ban, and a criminal conviction carries personal imprisonment of up to 3 years, with potential replacement of the corporate charter and revocation of the port terminal license for non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4-5 and 7, the National Tax Service recomputes the arm's length income, disallows the excess related-party payment, adds the amount to the Korean corporate tax base, and imposes transfer pricing penalties, international transactions report penalties of up to 3% of the undisclosed transaction amount, and general underreporting penalties of 40% for a fraudulent return, together with daily late payment surcharges and interest.

Director personal liability: The directors who signed the false transfer pricing documentation and the Korean tax return are personally liable for the additional tax when the legal entity is a sham; the NTS applies the substance-over-form doctrine to deprive the corporate veil, aggregates personal and corporate assets, imposes a travel ban and blacklisting, and files criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for fraudulent tax evasion, carrying a sentence of imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the evaded tax, with the court additionally ordering restitution of the national tax loss.

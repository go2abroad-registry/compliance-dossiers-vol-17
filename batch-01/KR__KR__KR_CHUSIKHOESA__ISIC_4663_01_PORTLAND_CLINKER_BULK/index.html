Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists under the Korean Commercial Act, and no blocked escrow account is required. The only statutory funding requirement is that each subscribed share must be paid in full and the total subscription price must be deposited in a bank account opened in the name of the corporation before registration; a bank-issued capital deposit certificate must be filed. For recognition as a foreign-invested enterprise under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the foreign investor must subscribe at least KRW 100,000,000 (or such lower amount as listed for certain service industries) in issued shares; this is a foreign-investment eligibility threshold, not a corporate capital formation requirement. For a standard registration based on KRW 100,000,000 paid-in capital, the court registration license tax and local education surcharge aggregate approximately KRW 486,800 (approx. USD 361 at 1,350 KRW/USD).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea - HomeTax portal (www.hometax.go.kr) for e-filing, business registration, electronic tax invoices, and VAT returns; Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS) for export clearance validation

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa must first complete court incorporation registration under the Commercial Act (company formation registry). Then, before commencing business, the corporation must apply for business tax registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its domestic place of business, either through HomeTax with a Korean public certificate or in person through its authorized tax representative. The application must submit articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation registration, evidence of foreign investment (foreign investment report or certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act), lease or ownership documents for the domestic place of business, the identification of the representative director, and a notarized power of attorney if a resident tax agent is appointed. The district tax office issues the 10-digit Business Registration Certificate (business registration number), which serves simultaneously as the VAT tax identification number and corporate taxpayer number. A non-resident entity without a domestic place of business but making taxable supplies in Korea must register as a non-resident foreign business through the NTS and obtain a 10-digit business registration number for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Korean tax law; the Chusik Hoesa is irrevocably taxed at the legal entity level under the Korean Corporate Tax Act. Wholly-owned Korean affiliates may elect a consolidated corporate tax filing regime if they meet group requirements, and certain partnership-style special taxation elections are available only to limited companies, not stock corporations; no hybrid mismatch check-the-box-style elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporations: a Chusik Hoesa carrying on any taxable business is mandatorily registered from business commencement. The KRW 80 million simplified taxpayer threshold applies only to qualifying individual sole proprietors, not to stock corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods, including portland cement and clinker, requires the goods to be actually exported from South Korea to a foreign destination under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act; the seller must hold a customs export declaration acceptance certificate, evidence of transportation abroad (ocean bill of lading), a commercial invoice in the buyer's name, and proof that the export proceeds were received through accepted foreign-exchange settlement. The zero rate also applies to the export of goods without inland consumption; supplies of offshore digital services may qualify only if they are received outside Korea and satisfy the place-of-supply rules, which are not applicable to the current cement wholesale scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Acceptance Certificate / Export Bill of Entry) issued by Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Ocean Bill of Lading evidencing shipment of the container or bulk vessel cargo from a Korean port to a foreign port
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (inward remittance certificate) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank showing receipt of the export proceeds (LC negotiation, TT wire, or SWIFT settlement record)
  • Export contract, purchase order, or irrevocable letter of credit evidencing the foreign buyer and trade terms

All corporations must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) through HomeTax for every taxable supply; the e-invoice must be sent to the customer and transmitted to the NTS by the day following the supply date. There is no physical fiscal stamping device; the NTS HomeTax central system acts as the real-time fiscal clearance and anti-fraud repository, validating the invoice sequence and issue history. The e-invoice must contain the issuer's business registration number, buyer's business registration number, supply date, supply value, VAT amount, and digital signature/transmission confirmation.

NTS audit practice for export zero-rating focuses on matching the customs export declaration with the tax invoice, the B/L, and the bank foreign-exchange settlement certificate. If the exporter cannot substantiate actual exportation and foreign-exchange receipt, the zero rate is reversed, 10% VAT is assessed, and statutory additional taxes are imposed for underreporting and late or non-payment. Fictitious or inflated export declarations with fraudulent refund claims expose the entity to criminal tax evasion sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and to retrospective clawback; the ordinary re-assessment period under the National Tax Basic Act is 5 years, extended to 10 years where the return was induced by fraud or other unlawful acts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but compliance-intensive foreign exchange system. Current account transactions, including trade-related receipts, commissions, and royalties, are freely convertible and can be settled through any registered foreign exchange bank without prior regulatory approval. Capital account transactions, however, require form-based declaration to the Bank of Korea or its delegated banks, and certain speculative flows are subject to circuit-breaker thresholds. There is no mandatory surrender law; exporters may hold export proceeds in domestic or offshore foreign currency accounts, but must collect and alienate receivable amounts within 360 days from the export declaration date unless an extension is granted by the handling foreign exchange bank. Profit remittance is permissible for a Chusik Hoesa once audited financial statements, National Tax Service tax clearance, and board resolutions authorizing dividends are submitted through a commercial bank. For a wholesale portland clinker trading entity, all cross-border receipts and payments must be matched against shipping, customs, and trade contracts under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The KRW is fully current-account convertible and benefits from an actively traded offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) market, yet onshore derivatives are reserved for genuine trade risk hedging. Physical KRW is not freely exportable beyond small threshold amounts, and all foreign exchange transactions exceeding prescribed amounts require documentary evidence and periodic regulatory reporting. Foreign-invested companies are afforded equal treatment regarding repatriation, provided the underlying investment was reported and capital verification completed through a domestic foreign exchange bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 14 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received and collected within 360 days from the date of export declaration; extension may be granted by the handling foreign exchange bank in case of documentary non-payment or quality disputes. There is no mandatory conversion ratio because the Republic of Korea abolished forced surrender of foreign currency earnings. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in domestic foreign currency accounts, offshore accounts, or convert to KRW at their discretion. All export receipts above USD 50,000 per contract must be reported on an aggregated monthly basis to the designated bank, and every inward remittance must be traceable to a corresponding customs export declaration (ECDO) and commercial invoice to prevent trade-based money laundering.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) as the designated foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its subordinate Foreign Exchange Regulations; operational surveillance is exercised by the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and the Korea Customs Service, which retains trade settlement records for anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. Digital electronic money institutions (EMIs), including international neobanks and virtual account providers, are legally barred from servicing corporate entities in Korea for domestic KRW settlement or foreign exchange operations unless they obtain a banking license under the Banking Act and are separately registered as a foreign exchange bank by the Financial Supervisory Service. Such EMIs can only act as front-end payment facilitators for individual customers without offering business current accounts or FX clearing. For a wholesale cement/clinker trading company, all treasury operations must be executed through a licensed Korean commercial bank with a physical presence in Seoul or Busan, as the Real Name Financial Transactions Act mandates verified corporate banking relationships and prohibits impersonal umbrella account services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and working capital remittances must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through an approved foreign exchange bank using the Korea Import/Export purpose code for foreign direct investment (typically 10020 for equity subscription or 10022 for additional capital increase). The remitting foreign bank must identify the Korean entity name and the corporate bank account number. Within 30 days of receipt, the bank will verify the debit against the certificate of capital registration registered with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, and issue a Foreign Currency Acquisition Confirmation. An official capital verification (Hand-Off) by an external auditor or judicial scrivener will then be lodged with the registry office, after which the funds are available for operational disbursement.Outbound trade disbursements for imported clinker and vessel-related costs should be cleared using the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) for KRW domestic legs, and via SWIFT MT103 for direct interbank TT wires for foreign payees. For letter of credit structures, use SWIFT MT700 for documentary issuance and MT720 for transfer, together with confirmed DP/DA documentary collection. All disbursements exceeding KRW 5 million require submission of supporting shipping documents, customs declaration, invoice, and when applicable the bill of lading to the foreign exchange bank; the bank will then release the KRW or foreign currency payment within the same operating day, subject to no outstanding suspicious-transaction report.Outright forward foreign exchange contracts with a licensed Korean commercial bank for up to 365 days, limited to the expected amount of clinker trade receivables or payables;Cross-currency interest rate swaps between USD and KRW permitted only with generated underlying trade debt or revenue exposure, requiring an import/export contract as the hedging trigger;Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for KRW/USD executed with offshore branches of foreign banks are permissible solely for non-speculative hedging by the Korean entity or its parent, with prestated underlying exposure and monthly LCR/FX position reporting to the Financial Supervisory Service;Covered currency options (puts and calls with underlying physical settlement) offered by domestic banks for hedging the exchange rate risk on maritime freight invoices and port-side disbursements, provided the notional does not exceed 110% of the projected invoice.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9 percent on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19 percent on taxable income above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21 percent on taxable income above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; 24 percent on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion (effective for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2023, as per the restructured Korean Corporate Income Tax brackets)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20 percent on dividend income paid by a Korean domestic corporation to a non-resident corporate shareholder (including the local surtax of 2 percentage points on top of the 18 percent basic withholding tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act, resulting in a combined statutory rate of 20 percent; however, under the Individual Income Tax Act for individual shareholders the statutory rate is 22 percent).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are required for each business year where the aggregate value of controlled transactions (international related-party transactions) exceeds KRW 10 billion. If the aggregate value of controlled transactions with overseas related parties is between KRW 5 billion and KRW 10 billion, a simplified reporting form (Controlled Foreign Transaction Summary Report) is required. Companies with controlled transactions of at least KRW 10 billion must also submit a Country-by-Country Report if the consolidated group revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion; the disclosure threshold for the transaction summary report is KRW 5 billion for domestic controlled transactions with special related parties.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) specific to shell entities in low-tax jurisdictions; however, the Korean tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine and the 'place of effective management' principles (head office or place of effective management is treated as the domestic place of a corporation). The anti-avoidance rules include the Corporate Tax Act's controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, general anti-abuse clauses (GAAR), and the Principal Purpose Test under tax treaties. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaged in wholesale bulk cement distribution, the company must demonstrate adequate substance in Korea through board meetings, operational decision-making, staffing, and performance of core distribution and logistics functions to avoid recharacterisation of profits and potential penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (jointly with the Corporate Income Tax Act), imprisonment for up to 5 years, or a fine not exceeding the amount of evaded tax. In addition, the National Tax Service imposes a 40 percent penalty surcharge on the understated taxable amount, plus the daily interest surcharge of 0.022 percent per day.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally or negligently participated in the filing of a false tax return are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax deficiency and penalties. Criminal conviction may result in imprisonment and travel bans. If the underreporting is linked to diversion of corporate assets to shareholders or related parties, the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the directors personally liable for all unpaid tax liabilities.
Transfer Pricing Audits and Penalties

Statutory sanction: Penalty for non-submission of transfer pricing documentation up to 30 million KRW, or 0.3 percent of the transaction amount if higher. The National Tax Service may assess a transfer pricing adjustment, apply a 10 percent penalty surcharge on the additional tax assessed, and impose an underreporting penalty of 10-40 percent of the understated tax base. Country-by-Country report non-filing may attract a penalty of up to 50 million KRW.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer pricing manipulation is deemed to be intentional or grossly negligent for the purpose of tax avoidance, directors of the Korean entity can be personally liable for the resulting penalties if they are found to have exercised control or approved the manipulative transaction. In egregious cases, criminal liability for violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act applies, including imprisonment up to 5 years and travel restrictions.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Tax on Dividends/Royalties/Interest

Statutory sanction: The Korean payer is liable for the withheld tax amount plus an additional 10 percent penalty for non-withholding (for failure to withhold) or, if withheld but not paid, the amount withheld is due plus a 5 percent penalty for late payment, and a daily interest surcharge of 0.022 percent. If non-withholding is intentional, the penalty surcharge increases to 20 percent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to withhold on distributions or who structure payments to avoid withholding are personally liable for the tax plus penalties, and may be subject to imprisonment up to 3 years. If the failure results in a fraudulent export of assets from Korea, the National Tax Service may seek a travel ban and asset freeze against the directors.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Violations

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the legal form and recharacterise the Korean entity's transactions, taxing the income as if it accrued to the actual substance owner. Penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes include 40 percent of the underpaid tax for fraud and 10-20 percent for negligence. The company's registration may be cancelled, and the entity may be treated as a permanent establishment of the foreign shareholder, exposing the foreign shareholder to Korean branch filing obligations.

Director personal liability: Where directors use the Chusik Hoesa as a sham to evade taxes, the corporate veil is pierced under general civil law principles. Directors can be personally liable for the entire tax shortfall, and criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (up to 5 years imprisonment) will apply. Travel bans and civil restitution orders are issued to recover tax losses.

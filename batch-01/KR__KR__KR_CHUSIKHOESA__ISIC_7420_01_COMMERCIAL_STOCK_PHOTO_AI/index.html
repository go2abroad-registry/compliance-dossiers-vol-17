Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Commercial Act of Korea. However, each issued share must have a par value of at least KRW 100 (or such lower amount expressly permitted by the Act), and the full subscription price of all issued shares must be deposited in a commercial bank before incorporation registration. No government-held blocked escrow account is required; instead, the deposit must be evidenced by a Capital Deposit Certificate issued by the bank, which cannot be withdrawn until the Company is duly registered.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa, the entity must first complete corporate registration at the competent local District Court, obtaining the Corporate Registration Number. Thereafter, the legal representative or designated local tax representative must apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN; TIN and VAT registration) with the competent district tax office or via the Home Tax Service portal within 20 days of business commencement. The required documents include the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, lease agreement for the local place of business, identification document of the representative, power of attorney and registration evidence for the tax representative (where applicable), and bank certificate evidencing the paid-in capital. The NTS issues the Business Registration Number in real time upon online submission through HTS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Qualifying foreign exchange-earning services, including cross-border licensing of digital stock media and stock photography to foreign users, are zero-rated when the service is deemed supplied to a foreign country, payment is received in convertible and freely transferable foreign currency, and the foreign currency is repatriated into South Korea. The Chusik Hoesa must maintain evidence of foreign counterparty identity, supply and licensing contracts, proof of actual performance or digital delivery, and final receipt of foreign exchange.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration No. / Export Confirmation issued by the Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill/Electronic Transport Document for digital freight or accompanying physical media)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean bank, evidencing the receipt and repatriation of foreign currency

All VAT-registered persons must issue a mandatory electronic tax invoice (Jeonja Sesyeo) through the NTS Home Tax Service within the day following the supply date. The electronic invoice must be transmitted to the NTS using the prescribed API/HTS interface and must contain the transaction certificate number, anti-fraud surrogate key, taxable amount, VAT amount, and the Business Registration Numbers of both parties. Paper tax invoices are permitted only with explicit NTS approval and are subject to strict issuance deadline rules.

If the NTS determines that the zero-rated export was false, unverifiable, or failed to meet the foreign exchange repatriation deadline, it may reassess VAT at the standard 10% rate, impose additional tax penalties of up to 40% of the evaded tax for fraudulent or grossly negligent filing, and apply negligence surcharges under the National Tax Basic Act and the Value-Added Tax Act. The filing of false export documentation can trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment of up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding 20 million KRW, and the clawback of any VAT input tax credits or refunds already claimed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized but declaration-heavy foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The Korean won is convertible for current account trade and service flows, which include stock-photo licensing and subscription fees; these settlements may be carried out freely through a designated foreign exchange bank, with reporting on a post-transaction basis. Capital account transactions, however, require express reporting or approval; the full subscription of shares by non-resident beneficial owners is classified as foreign direct investment and requires filing with the designated bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Export proceeds are subject to a settlement accounting deadline, but Korea does not require compulsory surrender to the central bank. A company may keep receipts in a foreign currency deposit account or convert them into Korean won at the market rate. Profit remittance is permitted once audited retained earnings exist, the dividend distribution is reported, bank-level verification is completed, and Korean withholding tax is paid. The won in practice is settled onshore only, with offshore non-deliverable forwards as the standard synthetic hedge for non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act export settlement regulations, service and digital asset export proceeds must be received within the contractually agreed payment due date, or within 18 months from the export declaration/invoice date when no specific contract date exists; receipt must occur through a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the Chusik Hoesa may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account, or elect to convert to Korean won at the prevailing market rate. All export receipts must be filed against the company's trade settlement ledger, and any receipt routed through third-party wallets without an underlying service invoice will be reported as an FX procedural violation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) exercising supervisory authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Legally barred as the sole corporate treasury spine. Under the Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only licensed banks may receive deposits and provide foreign exchange settlement for Korean entities. Offshore fintech EMIs - including Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Stripe Treasury - can support settlement between the platform and overseas photographers, but they cannot act as the Korean Chusik Hoesa's designated foreign exchange bank for Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting, capital contribution verification, or KRW clearing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

South Korean corporate income tax is progressive: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on the excess up to KRW 20 billion; 21% on the excess up to KRW 300 billion; and 24% on the excess over KRW 300 billion; plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined on dividends distributed to a non-resident shareholder: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge, unless reduced under a DTA.

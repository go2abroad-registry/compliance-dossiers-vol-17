Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Korean law: no statutory floor on paid-in capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa. Shares must be fully subscribed and the subscription price must be fully paid before registration; a bank paid-in capital certificate is required. No blocked escrow account is required by the Commercial Act. Practical capital may be KRW 10,000,000 or more to qualify for venture-company certification, but this is a fiscal policy option, not a legal minimum.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea; online portal Home Tax Service (Hometax) at hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service at customs.go.kr is competent for customs export clearance and export declaration matters; licensed Korean foreign exchange banks administer inward remittance reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System under the Ministry of Economy and Finance

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity commencing Korean VAT-taxable activities must obtain a Korean business registration number, which is the unified taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number. For a foreign corporation with no Korean domestic place of business but supplying electronic services to Korean customers, the NTS provides a simplified registration pathway for foreign electronic service providers under the VAT Act. The procedure is: 1) confirm whether a Korean domestic place of business exists; if none, appoint a Korean tax representative in accordance with the Framework Act on National Taxes; 2) prepare the foreign entity constitutional documents, board resolution, apostilled or notarised corporate existence certificate, and power of attorney for the Korean representative; 3) file the business registration application with the competent NTS district tax office or the designated Hometax registration center for foreign electronic service providers; 4) submit proof of the Korean business address for a physical establishment, or for a purely F-type remote digital supplier, submit evidence of the electronic marketplace activity, the foreign entity legal details, and the tax representative appointment; 5) obtain the NTS-issued 10-digit business registration certificate; 6) for cross-border electronic services, apply for the simplified VAT registration classification and receive the assigned VAT ID; 7) maintain this ID for quarterly VAT returns and electronic tax invoice reporting. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the domestic incorporation process automatically generates the corporate registration file and the initial business registration application is made to the district tax office; the business registration number is used for both corporate tax and VAT compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for domestic Korean businesses: any person carrying on a VAT-taxable business in Korea must register with the NTS before making taxable supplies. Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services, including voice-streaming and app-marketplace services consumed in Korea, also have mandatory Korean VAT registration with no KRW revenue threshold; the zero-threshold rule applies to overseas electronic service providers under the VAT Act and its Presidential Decree.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act where goods are physically exported from Korea and where services are supplied to non-resident persons and actually consumed outside Korea. For the voice-streaming social app scope, the Korean supplier may claim 0 percent VAT on supplies to overseas end users only if it can prove foreign consumption through foreign user IP address or device jurisdiction, foreign app-store listing and billing location, non-Korean payment rails, and no Korean user access in respect of that transaction. Export services under invoice and contract must be in foreign currency and the proceeds must flow back into Korea through a licensed foreign exchange bank or through foreign app-store remittance channels. If the recipient or consumption is in Korea, zero rating is denied and standard 10 percent VAT applies.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Certificate (export clearance certificate) for physical goods exports
  • Commercial invoice and export/service contract evidencing the foreign buyer and delivery term
  • Bill of Lading, seaway bill or airway bill for tangible merchandise; not required for pure digital voice-streaming services but must be retained for mixed goods transactions
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance confirmation or foreign exchange settlement certificate from a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • App-store settlement reports from Apple IAP or Google Play Billing showing gross receipts, commissions, territory of sale and net remittance to the Korean entity
  • Overseas end-user evidence including IP geolocation logs, mobile country code, billing address, payment metadata and usage session logs demonstrating foreign consumption
  • Hometax zero-rated sales schedule and supporting ledger reconciling each zero-rated supply to the foreign exchange evidence

The NTS operates the Hometax electronic tax invoice system. Registered businesses must issue e-Tax Invoices for B2B supplies and transmit them to the NTS within the statutory filing deadlines, with automated cross-matching against the counterparty's VAT returns. There is no separate pre-supply anti-fraud digital stamping or customs clearance authorisation for ordinary VAT invoices; instead, the NTS performs real-time receipt and matching of e-Invoice data using Hometax and its API-based filing channels. Foreign electronic-service providers under simplified VAT registration must file returns and pay through Hometax, and must preserve transaction-level records showing the customer, service description, amount, foreign-currency settlement, IP-geolocation and consumption-location logic. For payments routed through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the Korean taxable entity must additionally retain app-store commission statements, remittance reports, and evidence of the Korean end-user vs foreign end-user split to support VAT treatment and export zero-rating.

If the NTS determines on audit that export zero-rating was claimed without valid documentation or that the service was consumed in Korea, the exported supply is reclassified as taxable at 10 percent and the VAT underpaid is retrospectively assessed with statutory penalty surcharges under the VAT Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Intentional submission of forged export declarations, false remittance certificates or fabricated foreign-user evidence can trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment or a criminal fine, and the tax authority may reopen prior tax periods within the statutory assessment window. Exporters also risk the suspension of export VAT refunds, the denial of future zero-rating claims, customs compliance sanctions from Korea Customs Service, and adverse foreign exchange reporting penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for non-repatriation or late repatriation of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), the Korean won is fully convertible for current account items, including software and digital content export proceeds, without prior central bank approval. The capital account, however, is liberalized on a 'negative list' basis: transactions are permitted unless specifically restricted, but must be channelled through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported indirectly via that bank. Inbound foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa requires a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; the inward remittance is then freely credited to a capital escrow account. Profit repatriation, whether dividends, capital gains or liquidation proceeds, is treated as a current account remittance for the foreign shareholder, subject only to documentary proof of distributable reserves, audited financial statements, withholding tax clearance with the National Tax Service, and a Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting filing. There is no surrender requirement and no quantitative restriction on conversion, but all settlements must be evidenced with a Foreign Exchange Certificate issued by the receiving bank. A de facto practical hurdle is the conservative bank scrutiny and the physical documentation standard rather than any legal convertibility ceiling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory surrender requirement was officially abolished in 1999; Korean exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency export proceeds in a foreign currency account with a licensed foreign exchange bank. For service exports, including digital content and app-store receipts, the exporter must receive the proceeds through an authorized bank within a settlement period of one year from the date the service is rendered, and any extension beyond the period requires a statement of justifiable cause submitted to the designated foreign exchange bank. Upon settlement, the bank issues a foreign exchange receipt certificate used for zero-rated VAT refund and export credit support. While no fixed percentage of conversion to KRW is mandated, the customs and tax authorities require the company to demonstrate actual repatriation of export revenue; a mere contractual right without inward remittance is not recognised as taxable zero-rated export turnover.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority, the Korea Customs Service for trade settlement declarations, and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) for AML reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any person performing foreign exchange business, money transmission, or payment services in Korea must register with the MOEF or operate as a licensed foreign exchange bank. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut and other neobank-style platforms do not hold a Korean foreign exchange business licence and cannot provide a domestic clearing account, KRW IBAN equivalent, or local FX settlement for a Chusik Hoesa. A domestic entity's treasury must therefore be anchored at a licensed bank; an offshore EMI may only act as a supplementary payout aggregator if, and only if, it routes funds through a licensed Korean partner bank, and the bank's compliance committee will treat such aggregators as unapproved correspondent channels for a Medium-Risk Enhanced entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Chusik Hoesa must be reported as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with the foreign investor filing the investment report through the designated bank. The capital is then remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for the cover) with the statutory Korean foreign exchange purpose code for inward direct investment, e.g., establishment of a corporation or acquisition of shares, and wired into the Capital Escrow Account opened in the company's name at the Tier-1 bank. The receiving bank verifies the remitter against the FDI report, issues a Foreign Exchange Certificate, and the law firm completes the capital verification with the Korean Registration Court; the escrowed KRW funds become operational once the paid-in capital is registered. No proceeds may be disbursed until the capital registration certificate is filed.Outbound cash payments for royalties, content licensing, cloud infrastructure and vendor services are executed through SWIFT MT103 with the applicable FETA service-transaction purpose code, supported by the underlying contract, invoice and tax invoice. For physical goods (not expected in this app-only model), commercial letters of credit under UCP600 and documentary collections (DP/DA) would be available through the bank's trade desk, but for a pure voice-streaming operation the bank will clear vendor payments through its corporate electronic banking portal via the local KRW clearing (HOFNET) for domestic vendors and via MT103 for cross-border SaaS and creative partners. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any person performing foreign exchange business, money transmission, or payment services in Korea must register with the MOEF or operate as a licensed foreign exchange bank. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut and other neobank-style platforms do not hold a Korean foreign exchange business licence and cannot provide a domestic clearing account, KRW IBAN equivalent, or local FX settlement for a Chusik Hoesa. A domestic entity's treasury must therefore be anchored at a licensed bank; an offshore EMI may only act as a supplementary payout aggregator if, and only if, it routes funds through a licensed Korean partner bank, and the bank's compliance committee will treat such aggregators as unapproved correspondent channels for a Medium-Risk Enhanced entity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (statutory top marginal rate; progressive brackets of 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national corporate tax withholding, plus 2% local resident surtax (10% surtax on the national tax), aggregating 22% on cash and stock dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, taxpayers must prepare and retain a Local File where the aggregate amount of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds KRW 10 billion (approx. USD 8 million) in a fiscal year. A Master File is additionally required when the multinational enterprise group has consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more and the Korean entity's related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion. A CbC report is required if the group's consolidated revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime modeled on the UAE or BVI. However, the substance-over-form principle and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under the Framework Act on National Taxes, together with beneficial ownership and limitation-on-benefits clauses in Korea's double tax treaties, require companies to demonstrate genuine economic activity. For entities seeking incentives in Free Economic Zones, a CIGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) analysis is applied: the taxpayer must show that key decision-making and core profit-generating functions (e.g., development, marketing, and user support) are located in Korea. Where a Chusik Hoesa acts as a conduit for royalties or dividends without local substance, Korean tax authorities may disregard its separate existence, deny treaty benefits, reclassify income, and impose understatement penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Revenue Diversion

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Article 3-1), intentional evasion of corporate tax by fraud or other wrongful act is punishable by imprisonment for not more than 5 years or a fine not exceeding two times the evaded tax. Where the amount evaded is at least KRW 500 million, the maximum imprisonment is raised to 10 years. In addition, under the Framework Act on National Taxes, an understatement surcharge of 10%-20% of the underreported tax is imposed, and daily late payment surcharges accrue at 0.022% until the deficiency is paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or participated in the revenue diversion may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the principle of piercing the corporate veil when the corporation is a mere alter ego. They may also be blacklisted (subject to departure bans) by the NTS for tax arrears over KRW 100 million. Civilly, the company can recover damages for breach of fiduciary duty, and criminally the directors may be sentenced to imprisonment; the court may order restitution of diverted funds. Repeated offenders face aggravated sentencing in parallel tax fraud prosecutions.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance / Improper Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 98 and 98-5 of the Corporate Income Tax Law and Article 2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, a withholding agent who deliberately fails to withhold tax is liable for the uncollected amount plus a late payment surcharge. In addition, a separate surcharge of 20% of the unreported withholding is imposed. Criminal penalties apply for willful non-withholding under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to twice the unpaid withholding.

Director personal liability: The director responsible for authorizing the dividend distribution without withholding becomes jointly and severally liable with the corporation for the unpaid WHT, penalties, and interest. The NTS may issue a personal tax collection notice, attach personal assets, and impose a departure ban. If the court finds intentional disregard, criminal sentence of imprisonment can be imposed upon the director as an accomplice or principal of the offense.
Transfer Pricing Malpractice / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the tax authority may adjust the taxable income upward and disallow the excessive royalty deduction. An additional tax equal to up to 30% of the understated tax is imposed for failure to submit TP documentation or for transactions not at arm's length. For recurring noncompliance, an additional gross negligence penalty may apply. If the sham transaction forms part of a scheme to defraud taxes, criminal charges can be brought under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the agreements may be held personally liable for the tax deficiency under the alter ego doctrine, particularly where the intermediary has no real Board, office, or employees. Civilly, each director is exposed to a derivative action for the loss caused by the tax adjustment. Directors may be prohibited from leaving Korea, and if convicted of document fraud or tax evasion, face up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines. The NTS may also request cancellation of the company's legal entity registration in cases of repeated shell-company abuse.

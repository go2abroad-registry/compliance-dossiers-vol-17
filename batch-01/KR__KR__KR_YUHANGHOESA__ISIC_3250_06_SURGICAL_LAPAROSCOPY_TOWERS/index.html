Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, online electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) via hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance is separately administered by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean-resident Yuhan Hoesa LLC, a non-resident foreign investor must form the LLC in Korea and then complete business registration before the competent district tax office of the NTS within 20 days of business commencement. The foreign shareholder files the statutory business registration application through Home Tax Service or the district tax office, attaching the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, business license of the foreign shareholder, power of attorney for a domestic tax representative when applicable, lease or proof of the domestic place of business, and, because ISIC 3250 medical device manufacturing requires a statutory license, the license or preliminary trial run documentation from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS). The NTS then assigns an integrated 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID) in the format XXX-XX-XXXXX. This same number constitutes the Corporate Income Tax TIN, the VAT registration number, the electronic tax invoice reference number, and the customs filing reference number. The applicant must begin operating the e-tax invoice account in HTS and register the foreign currency cash settlement account with a licensed Korean Foreign Exchange Bank in order to complete the export compliance loop.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically shipped from Korea to a foreign purchaser are zero-rated under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act. The zero-rating applies if the exported goods are cleared through the Korea Customs Service under an accepted electronic export declaration, ownership and risk pass to an overseas buyer, the goods leave Korea without domestic consumption, and the Korean exporting LLC receives payment from the foreign buyer in foreign currency through a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank (subject to approved inward remittance procedures). For ISIC 3250 ultra-HD 4K endoscopy towers, the manufactured devices must be declared by product code and HS classification at export, and the LLC must retain the complete export transaction trail. Qualifying offshore B2B or digital services, if ever added, are zero-rated only if actually supplied and received outside Korea under the destination principle; the current entity scope covers zero-rated goods exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic export clearance system
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air cargo) showing the Korean loading place and the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice, purchase order/sales contract, and packing list evidencing the overseas counterparty and full equipment configuration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Proceeds Receipt Certificate issued by a licensed Korean Foreign Exchange Bank confirming receipt of the export sales proceeds in foreign currency
  • Electronic tax invoice transmitted to Home Tax Service marked as a zero-rated export supply

The LLC must issue electronic tax invoices for every taxed supply to VAT-registered recipients and transmit them to the NTS through the Home Tax Service e-tax invoice network within the statutory timeframe (normally by the tenth day of the month following the supply month, with earlier transmission required for certain high-risk taxpayers). The electronic invoice must contain the issuer TIN, recipient TIN, supply date, item description, supply value, VAT amount, and a unique e-tax invoice serial number. Transmission is performed through the HTS web portal, B2B EDI, or the NTS certified open API using accredited X.509 digital certificates. Korea operates an invoice-matching audit system rather than pre-clearance stamping; the NTS electronic system cross-references issuer and buyer e-invoices, cash receipts, and customs export declarations to identify missing or under-declared VAT. Anti-fraud protection is embedded through mandatory electronic transmission, unique serial numbering, real-time matching of e-invoices, and the national cash receipt network.

If the LLC applies 0% VAT export zero-rating without the complete statutory evidence trail, the NTS will retroactively assess 10% VAT on the export consideration and will deny or claw back any input VAT refund already claimed on the exported laparoscopy tower components. The assessment can be accompanied by statutory additional VAT penalties for underdeclaration, late payment and understated returns, and the customs false-declaration fines and penalties under the Customs Act. Where false or forged export documents or e-invoices are used, criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may be imposed. Export proceeds not received within the 180-day repatriation deadline, unless an approved extension is granted by the Korean Foreign Exchange Bank, will make the shipment ineligible for zero-rating and subject to the same recapture, plus statutory interest, retrospective for the full VAT returned. Assessments generally remain open for five years, and ten years where the zero-rating was claimed fraudulently.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Korea operates a convertibility regime distinguished by current-account and capital-account treatment. For this entity, the current account is fully convertible: export proceeds from sale of Ultra-HD 4K rigid endoscope towers may be received in USD by a Korean foreign-currency account remitted by foreign buyers, with no requirement to route through government control. The capital account is liberalized but subject to reporting and licensing. Inbound equity capital is permitted under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) and requires an FDI declaration filed with a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA; non-resident ownership is permissible without mandatory government approval, but the remitted funds must be registered and verified by the bank for the Company to obtain a Foreign Invested Company certificate. Profit remittances to a non-resident shareholder are treated as dividends and are allowed once the board approves a dividend reserve and the bank verifies tax withholding and the net-profit basis; the bank must report each remittance under FETA. There is no general surrender requirement today: exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts for up to 12 months after collection and may execute conversion when rates are favorable, though large transactions must be settled to or from KRW only through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported into the BOK/MOEF information system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Delegation Rules, export proceeds must be received within the contractual payment term and, in absence of a customs-approved extension, within 12 months of the export date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in the current liberalized regime; the exporter may deposit receipts into a domestic foreign-currency account or convert them to KRW at a licensed bank. If the receipt fails the 12-month collection window, the bank will require a written overdue-report justification and updated supporting documentation (invoice, bill of lading, non-payment claims) in order to keep the export collection report compliant.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) — delegated exchange administration and monitoring functions alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), which remains the principal standard-setter under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs including Europe-based e-money institutions and Asia-Pacific cross-border neo-banks have no South Korean banking license and cannot provide a local KRW account number, join the Korean payment and clearing system (KFTC, BOK Wire), or act as a designated foreign exchange bank for FDI or capital verification. South Korean law requires a corporation receiving equity capital or making trade settlement in KRW to operate through a domestic licensed financial institution that reports to the Financial Intelligence Unit and BOK. An EMI can at most function as an auxiliary wallet for low-value discount products or global receivables if integrated via a partner Korean licensed bank, but it is legally barred from serving as the primary treasury or liquidity rail for this local LLC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 3,000,000,000 in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File documentation; KRW 10,000,000,000 in annual revenue combined with KRW 5,000,000,000 in intercompany transactions triggers Master File preparation and submission to the National Tax Service under Article 111 of the International Tax Adjustment Act.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 17 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and the OECD BEPS Attribution of Profits Approach are applied by the NTS. Core income-generating activities of the laparoscopy tower manufacturer - research, production, procurement, risk control and senior management - must reside in Korea; a free-zone incentive may be denied if the taxpayer is a shell company with delegate management and no physical staff, despite legal ownership of the intellectual property.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company abuse of free-zone tax holiday

Statutory sanction: As determined under the Corporate Income Tax Act Article 178 and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act Article 86, the tax holiday is retroactively revoked, 100% of the exempted tax is recaptured with interest, and a penalty equal to 10% of the under-reported tax is imposed under Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-2(2).

Director personal liability: The representative director may be jointly and severally liable to the NTS for the unpaid corporate tax and penalty under the Civil Acts standard of tort liability; a travel ban and national tax blacklisting can be issued when overdue tax exceeds KRW 50,000,000; criminal indictment under Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3(1) can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and fine aggregating to 3 times the evaded tax, plus director's personal bank accounts and real property may be attached.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 111 and Article 13(3) authorise the NTS to adjust the taxable income to the arm's length result and impose an additional assessed tax plus a penalty of up to 20% of the under-reported income, with late-payment surcharge accruing at 0.025% each day until paid, and a criminal sanction of up to 2 years imprisonment for intentional non-disclosure under Article 113.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed off on falsified intercompany pricing invoices without economic substance are subject to piercing of the corporate veil by the NTS or creditor action; personal civil restitution is measured by the amount shifted against Korean taxable income. The director's passport may be suspended and a travel ban imposed, and the district prosecutor may pursue criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, leading to imprisonment of up to 5 years if tax evasion is sustained.
Non-resident dividend repatriation evasion through sham loan and non-DTA routing

Statutory sanction: Under the National Tax Collection Act Article 41 and Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-2, the unpaid 22% withholding tax is assessed on the payor, plus an unfaithful filing penalty of 20% of the unpaid withholding and a daily surcharge of 0.022%; the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 115 recharacterizes a transaction lacking commercial substance as a constructive dividend, and the withholding obligation stands.

Director personal liability: The director as the statutory withholding agent bears personal primary liability for the evaded withholding tax; the director's assets may be seized without the need to pierce the company. The NTS may impose a blacklist restriction, cancel a personal foreign travel permit, and refer the case to the prosecution; if convicted under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3, the director faces imprisonment for up to 5 years and a financial penalty of three times the non-remitted tax, while personal civil restitution remains enforceable in separate civil proceedings.

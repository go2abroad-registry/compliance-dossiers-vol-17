Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Court Registry Office (Corporate Registration Division) under the Supreme Court of Korea; territorial jurisdiction lies with the district court registry of the principal registered office, commonly the Seoul Central District Court Registry Office for Seoul-based companies.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, as revised by Act No. 19084 (2022)); incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) is governed by Articles 288 through 331; corporate governance and auditing by Articles 361 through 409; share subscription deposit requirement at Article 294; and corporate registration procedure under the Non-Contentious Case Litigation Procedure Act.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a private Chusik Hoesa under the current Commercial Act. The company may be formed with as little as KRW 1, but the full subscription price of the allotted shares must be deposited into a blocked escrow account at a bank in the name of the company to satisfy Article 294. The bank certificate of deposit must accompany the registration application; escrow is released automatically upon issuance of the registration certificate. For an ocean car-carrier operation, banks and chartering counterparties will generally require materially higher paid-in capital and net worth sufficient for vessel casualty, war risks, and contractual liabilities.

Funding & unlock path: Onshore registered offices, port cabins, and vehicle storage buildings require a Fire Protection Certificate and Civil Defense inspection clearance from the local fire department under the Fire Services Act. Vessels require KOMSA ship surveys for fire safety per SOLAS and the Vessel Safety Act, inspections for load lines and stability, and a Safety Management Certificate under the ISM Code. Occupational workplace safety for shore crews and cargo handlers requires KOSHA risk assessments, lashing and cargo-securing work plans, and incident reporting under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr) for business registration, VAT e-invoices, VAT returns, and entity tax filings; Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNIPASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for export/import declarations and export confirmation certificates.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean-incorporated Chusik Hoesa, the foreign non-resident shareholders first complete the commercial company registration at the competent Korean court under the Commercial Act. The entity must then register for Korean tax at the NTSelectronic HomeTax system by submitting the corporate tax inception registration and the VAT business registration application within 20 days of commencement of business, attaching the certificate of incorporation, court registration certificate, articles of incorporation, evidence of the registered business site, appointment of an authorized representative, and shareholder ownership/beneficial-ownership declarations. A foreign investor is permitted 100% ownership without minimum capital; the court registry does not require Korean nationality for directors. NTS assigns a single 10-digit Business Registration Number that is the unified tax identification number for corporate income tax, VAT withholding, employer withholding, and customs usage. A foreign non-resident entity that has a Korean place of business must separately complete NTS non-resident business registration through HomeTax using apostilled/certified foreign incorporation documents, foreign tax identification number, appointment of a Korean tax representative, and a domestic address for service. A foreign entity without any Korean domestic place of business is not entitled to a general Korean VAT registration number; it is required to register only in the specific non-resident simplified VAT regime if it supplies enumerated digital services to Korean non-taxable consumers, and otherwise it simply files from its foreign tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to a U.S. check-the-box election. Every Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily treated as a separate resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no pass-through or partnership election available. The only material statutory election is the consolidated corporate tax return regime available under the Corporate Tax Act, which a domestic parent and its wholly-owned Korean subsidiaries may elect upon NTS approval. Korea also applies mandatory hybrid-mismatch deduction adjustment rules; these are not elective and do not permit a taxpayer to reclassify the Chusik Hoesa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - no general turnover threshold; every person, resident or non-resident with a domestic place of business, that supplies goods or services in the course of business in Korea is required to register for VAT with NTS. The simplified taxable person regime for certain small merchants does not remove the registration obligation, is subject to an annual revenue threshold, and is unavailable to a corporate deep-sea RoRo ocean carrier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT treatment is available for the export of goods and for international maritime transport, including deep-sea RoRo ocean vehicle transport, under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act. A Korean Chusik Hoesa can invoice international ocean freight at 0% only where the transport service is supplied in connection with carriage by a vessel engaged in international navigation, the voyage involves movement between Korean and foreign ports or between foreign ports, the customer is a foreign person, and the freight revenue is earned in and payable in a foreign currency. The zero rate does not apply to domestic cabotage or domestic port-to-port RoRo movements. The taxable person must hold contemporaneous evidence showing the foreign character of the voyage, the vehicles carried across customs borders or internationally, the issue of a commercial invoice and e-tax invoice to the foreign customer, and eventual settlement in foreign currency.

  • Export Declaration and Export Declaration Certificate issued through Korea Customs Service UNIPASS for the vehicles exported on the RoRo voyage, reflecting the vessel identifier, cargo type, port of loading, foreign port of discharge, shipper, and consignee
  • Vessel's Bill of Lading or sea waybill issued in the carrier's name for the international RoRo voyage, evidencing loading at the origin port and discharge at the foreign destination port
  • Charter party agreement, voyage charter, slot charter, or contract of affreightment for the ocean transport, together with the commercial freight invoice issued to the foreign customer
  • VAT e-Tax Invoice issued to the foreign customer through HomeTax with the zero-rated tax code and a clear description of international sea transportation of vehicles
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate or bank settlement confirmation issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, evidencing receipt of the ocean freight from abroad in a convertible currency
  • Vehicle stowage plan and cargo manifest reconciled to the bills of lading and the customs export declaration, showing vehicle count, identifying numbers, ports, and vessel voyage details

Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing through the NTS HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system. General taxable businesses must generate each VAT tax invoice with prescribed statutory content and transmit it to NTS by the day following the supply date; transmission may occur through HomeTax web, machine-to-machine API, or an NTS-authorized delegated e-invoice service provider. There is no physical fiscal-stamp clearance, but the NTS assigns an electronic integrity value to each transmitted record, and input VAT recovery generally depends on the counterparty retaining a properly transmitted electronic tax invoice. Export and zero-rated international shipping documentation is filed through KCS UNIPASS, which uses electronic EDI/XML messaging and exchanges export-clearance data with NTS. Zero-rated sales must be included in the VAT return under the zero-rated category, and underlying proof must be retained for statutory audit periods.

NTS, together with Korea Customs Service and the Foreign Exchange Information System, audits zero-rating claims by matching export declarations, e-Tax Invoices, bills of lading, cargo manifests, charter parties, and foreign exchange settlement records. If any required document is missing, forged, inconsistent, dated outside the statutory repatriation period, or unsupported by actual foreign-currency repatriation, NTS will disallow the zero rate, retroactively assess 10% VAT on the gross freight, and impose statutory underreporting and non-payment additional tax plus interest. Deliberate issuance of false tax invoices, fraudulent export documentation, or misclassification of domestic carriage as international transport may result in criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including fines and imprisonment. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, the assessment and clawback period is extended to at least 10 years in cases of false or fraudulent returns, and failure to obtain an authorized extension for late repatriation of export proceeds is separately penalized under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with possible loss of export confirmation status and zero-rating entitlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating Korean won and a generally convertible current-account regime regulated by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Inbound and outbound current payments such as trade freight settlement, port disbursements, vessel tonnage fees and dividend remittances are classified as current transactions and may be executed without a license, provided the underlying commercial documentation is submitted to the designated foreign-exchange bank. Capital-account movements, however, are not freely convertible: the initial equity contribution into a Chusik Hoesa must be pre-notified under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), obtain a KOTRA/Invest Korea registration number, and be booked into an FDI escrow account at a licensed bank before the won can be deployed. Profit remittance by a foreign shareholder is legally permitted but operationally conditioned on the payment of Korean corporate tax withholding clearance, submission of audited financial statements, and evidence that the dividend was properly approved by the shareholders general meeting. There are no residual surrender requirements for export earnings; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a designated account, but any conversion to won must be settled at the prevailing market rate through a licensed bank. The practical treasury hurdle is therefore documentary traceability and transaction purpose validation, not exchange rationing, and the company must present a full real-transaction evidence file to move capital across accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export freight receivables and vehicle transport proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of the export declaration or invoice issue; if collection is delayed, the company must file a delay report with the designated foreign-exchange bank. The mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won is 0%; there is no forced surrender to the Bank of Korea. The foreign currency may be credited to a company foreign-currency account. If the treasury elects to convert the proceeds to KRW, the transaction is executed at the interbank market rate and the receipt is reported by the bank to the Korea Customs Service and the Electronic Trade Settlement System on the same business day.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK); FX policy and licensing are co-administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banking is not viable for a Korean Chusik Hoesa under the current legal framework. South Korea does not recognize offshore EMI licenses as equivalent to a banking license for resident corporations, and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act mandates that foreign-exchange business, inbound FDI settlement, and SWIFT settlement involving the Korean entity must pass through a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Offshore EMIs, including Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer and Currencycloud, may not hold the entitys KRW treasury balance, issue an IBAN, or execute MT103 settlement as principal. They can only serve as unlicensed quote-aggregation or payment-notification layers, with no legal capacity to handle the companys Korea-resident current-account payments. Practical feasibility rating: Low, and the treasury blueprint should default to domestic Tier-1 banks for all material cash flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national top marginal rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surcharge resulting in 26.4% aggregate top marginal rate; lower brackets are 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, each subject to a 10% local surtax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national statutory dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends to non-resident shareholders, plus 2% local income tax surtax, producing a 22% aggregate statutory withholding rate; treaty-reduced rates are often 5% for a direct corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the voting capital and 10% for portfolio dividends under many Korean bilateral treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when the total amount of cross-border controlled transactions between the Korean Chusik Hoesa and foreign related parties exceeds KRW 5 billion during the fiscal year; Master File documentation is mandatory when the multinational group's total cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 100 billion. An annual international transaction report is filed on HomeTax with the corporate return once controlled cross-border transactions with any single foreign related party exceed KRW 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer the offshore-centre Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) label, but the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 96-2 of the Framework Act on International Tax Affairs and the limitation-on-benefits provisions of each DTA impose a mandatory Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis. The Korean Chusik Hoesa must own or time-charter the RoRo vessels, employ the master commercial and crewing decision, negotiate bunker and port contracts, control voyage planning and risk, and keep strategic vessel operation management in Korea. If the NTS finds that the foreign shareholder is a shell or that the Korean entity is a letter-box operating only through non-resident decision-makers, the authorities may recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, disallow foreign tax credit, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and apply penalties under domestic anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion by Falsifying RoRo Revenue and Voyage Accounting

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 prescribes imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to 5 times the evaded tax for unlawful evasion. National Tax Collection Act surcharges apply at 40% for fraudulent underreporting plus daily overdue interest at 0.025%; the National Tax Service may publicly disclose the Chusik Hoesa as a high-value tax evader and refer the representative director to the prosecutor's office.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Chusik Hoesa is undercapitalized or directed fraudulently, making the representative director jointly liable for unpaid CIT, surcharges, and interest. The director's personal bank accounts, vessel shares, and real property can be seized without benefit of limitation of liability; a travel ban is imposed by the Korean Immigration Service for unpaid national taxes exceeding the statutory threshold; deliberate tax evasion over the criminal threshold carries imprisonment and confiscation of personally held vessel proceeds.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Non-Resident Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends, Interest, Royalties, and Ship Charters

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act withholding rules render the Chusik Hoesa liable for the unwithheld or un-remitted tax plus an additional 10% collection penalty on the unpaid withholding amount and daily delinquency interest. A false withholding statement is a criminal violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to 5 times the under-withheld tax.

Director personal liability: The representative director is jointly and severally liable with the company for withheld but un-remitted taxes because a withholding agent is a statutory fiduciary of collected taxes; the NTS can seize director assets without seeking judgment against the corporate entity first. The director is subject to a domestic and overseas travel ban, disqualification from government contract privileges, and criminal imprisonment if the failure was intentional or accompanied by a fabricated remittance payment confirmation.
Transfer Pricing, Thin Capitalization, and Shell-Company Substance Infringement

Statutory sanction: The Framework Act on International Tax Affairs authorizes the NTS to compute an arm's length taxable income and imposes an international tax adjustment penalty of 10% of the adjusted amount, increased to 15% in fraud cases; failure to file the international transaction report carries a separate statutory fine; denial of treaty relief converts the outbound dividend/royalty to the full 22% withholding with interest; and the resulting deficiency triggers criminal referral when a false or deceptive TP document was used to conceal Korean-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve abusive intercompany rates or cause the Korean RoRo entity to be no more than a shell face piercing of the corporate veil and personal restitution of the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties. Korean courts and the NTS may impose joint liability on directors for causing the company to be undercapitalized or insolvent at the time of the tax adjustment; the director is blacklisted as a high-risk international tax evader, subjected to an immigration control travel ban, and, for intentional documentation fraud, sentenced to imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act where the concealed revenue or adjusted tax exceeds the statutory criminal threshold.

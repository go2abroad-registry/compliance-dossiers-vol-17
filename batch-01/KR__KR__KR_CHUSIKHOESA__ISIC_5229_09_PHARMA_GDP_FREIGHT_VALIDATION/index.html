Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

As of the current Korean Commercial Act, no statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a general Chusik Hoesa that is not a bank, financial investment business, insurance company, or other regulated financial institution. However, the entire amount of issued share subscription money must be paid in before registration and held in a blocked temporary escrow account in the name of the promoter or the notary until the incorporation registration is completed, after which funds may be released for lawful corporate use. The Articles of Incorporation may impose a higher internal paid-in capital threshold based on the Company's GDP clinical logistics business plan.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Completion Inspection Certificate issued by the local Fire Station under the Fire Services Act, certifying that the fire alarm, extinguishing systems, emergency lighting, evacuation routes, fire doors, and smoke-control systems comply with the fire resistance standards applicable to warehouse and logistics facilities. A structural load inspection certificate for racking systems and refrigerated cold cells must be issued by a licensed structural engineer. Occupational safety and health clearance from the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency is required for forklift traffic, load bearing, electrical safety, worker exposure to cold environments, and emergency response arrangements; the Company shall also register with the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service for industrial accident compensation along with the four mandatory social insurance plans.

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities must apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN) at the local district Tax Office under the NTS. Required documents include: certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of foreign registration, and a domestic address for tax purposes. The application can be submitted online via HTS or in person. Upon approval, the entity receives a 10-digit BRN which serves as both corporate tax ID and VAT registration number. For VAT purposes, registration is mandatory upon commencement of any business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain international services are zero-rated under the VAT Act. For GDP pharma freight, zero-rating applies if the services are directly related to the export of goods (e.g., freight forwarding, customs clearance, and warehousing for export). The service provider must retain evidence of the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) for all VAT-registered businesses. E-invoices must be issued via NTS-approved systems and transmitted to NTS within 1 day after issuance. Real-time clearance and anti-fraud measures include digital signature, validation, and integration with NTS's Home Tax Service.

Penalties for fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating include additional tax of 40% of the claimed amount, criminal charges, and retrospective clawback of zero-rated benefits. The NTS conducts rigorous audits and cross-checks with customs data.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a negative-list foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for legitimate current-account payments such as freight charges, logistics service fees, royalties and other service income, provided the transaction is reported through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank using the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Capital-account operations, including inbound foreign direct investment, equity contributions and shareholder loans, are generally permissible but require preliminary notification/reporting under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act to KOTRA or the Ministry of Economy and Finance and must be booked through a Korean foreign exchange bank. Repatriation of profits, programme revenue and dividends to foreign shareholders is lawful once the Korean entity has satisfied corporate income tax, withholding tax, statutory audit and shareholder-meeting requirements. There is no blanket pre-approval by the Bank of Korea for routine trade-related payments in the goods-distribution and freight sector, but payment execution can be delayed if the bank identifies inconsistencies among the underlying transport contract, tax invoice, customs declaration and FEIS purpose code. In practice, non-financial barriers such as ultimate beneficial owner documentation and cross-border invoice authenticity generate more friction than legal convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not impose a fixed statutory surrender ratio or mandatory conversion of export foreign currency proceeds to Korean won. Export settlement, however, must be made to a domestic foreign exchange bank, and the Korean exporter is required to collect export proceeds within the credit terms specified in the trade contract, subject to a statutory outer limit of 180 days from the export declaration date unless an extension is approved by the Korea Customs Service or the Bank of Korea under FEIS rules. Proceeds may be held in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account or sold at market rate. No central bank surrender percentage applies, but all export proceeds must be traceable through the bank's FEIS report.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank and foreign exchange policy executor, operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Ministry of Economy and Finance's delegated foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally non-viable as a primary corporate clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot lawfully buy or sell Korean won with residents, hold resident corporate operating accounts, or issue FEIS reports to Korean authorities. They may be used by the foreign parent or non-resident supplier side as convenience deposit accounts, but the Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain its revenue, capital escrow, FX purchase/sale and trade settlement through a domestic Tier-1 Korean bank. Relying on an EMI for outbound KRW or trade settlement would create KYC discontinuities and invalid proof-of-payment records for Korean tax and customs clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for a Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed as a direct investor-to-company wire and reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remittance. The foreign investor files a Foreign Investment Report with KOTRA or a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank; only then may the physical remittance be received into a dedicated Foreign Investment Capital Escrow Account. The SWIFT MT103 should state the investor's name and account, the Korean corporation's registered name, the KOTRA or foreign investment notification number, and the FEIS purpose code for foreign direct investment equity acquisition. The bank will apply the exchange rate at value date, credit the KRW amount to the operational account and generate a Capital Payment Certificate. For capital verification, the statutory auditor or Korean certified accountant confirms that the paid-in capital equals the registered shares issued after the company's incorporation filing. No third-party funding, split remittances, or cash depot payment is acceptable.For cross-border freight and logistics disbursements, the Korean entity must pass payments through a domestic foreign exchange bank and a FEIS purpose code corresponding to freight and transportation charges. The clearing rail should be SWIFT MT103 for service payments and ancillary trade costs; a commercial letter of credit is preferred when the transaction involves physical purchase of temperature-controlled packaging, pharma cargo or equipment; documentary collection is acceptable for extended supplier payment terms. The bank requires a three-way match among the commercial invoice, customs declaration or airway bill, and the signed transport service contract. For domestic payments in KRW, Direct ACH using Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute bulk clearing is acceptable for local subcontractors, while SWIFT MT103 remains the foreign settlement rail. All outbound wires must record the underlying export or import declaration number and maintain audit trail connectivity to the FEIS report.Onshore US dollar and Korean won forward exchange contracts executed by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank for hedging legitimate freight receivables and payables, with maturity not exceeding the term of the underlying invoice plus 30 days and no contingent leverage.KRW non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed by an overseas branch or offshore institution solely to hedge non-resident KRW profit or contractual exposure; NDFs are not a treasury tool for routine onshore onward purchases because settlement is in foreign currency and may fail the onshore real-demand test.Cross-currency swaps and plain vanilla FX swaps restricted to converting a shareholder loan or capital account surplus into KRW for operational funding, with an underlying foreign-investment loan or trade-finance facility, counterparty limited to a top-tier domestic Korean bank, and no rollover beyond the loan term.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300,000,000,000; plus 10% local income tax surcharge yields an aggregate top marginal rate of 26.4%. Lower brackets are 9%, 19%, and 21%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax, producing a composite 22% statutory withholding rate on cash dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders that do not obtain treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korea requires contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation where cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10,000,000,000 per relevant transaction category, or where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 and aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions exceed KRW 50,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime, but substance governs treaty access through the beneficial ownership and principal purpose tests. The Korean Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate that board meetings, cold-chain freight operations, personnel, bank accounts, invoices, and core income-generating activities are performed or located in Korea. If the entity is a letter-box company and management is abroad, Korea may disregard the corporate shell, impute profits to the actual owner, and treat remittances as distributable dividends subject to withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fabricated GDP Freight Expenses

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, a person who evades tax by fraudulent or unjust means is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine not exceeding 5 times the evaded tax. The Framework Act on National Taxes additionally imposes a 40% unfaithful-return additional tax. If the false expenses are linked to money laundering, the Proceeds of Crime Act adds asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: The representative director who signs the false return loses the liability shield: he or she is jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax and unpaid withholding tax under Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act. Personal assets may be reached through civil restitution in shareholder derivative actions and creditors' direct suits. The director may be classified as a high-amount abusive tax delinquent, resulting in travel bans, public blacklisting, asset seizures, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 10 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs permits the NTS to re-determine taxable income using the arm's length standard, with a correlative adjustment under Article 14. The unpaid tax is increased by a 20%-40% unfaithful-return penalty under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Knowingly false transfer-pricing documentation may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the false Local File and transfer-pricing declaration may be sued for damages under Article 399 of the Commercial Act. The court may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere instrumentality, leaving directors liable for adjusted tax, interest, and penalties. In serious cases, personal bank accounts and securities are seized, exit from Korea is prohibited until tax clearance, and conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years plus fines.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS applies the principal purpose test under Article 7 of the OECD MLI as incorporated into Korea's tax conventions, denies treaty benefits, and recharacterizes remittances as Korean-source dividends subject to 22% withholding. The Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 12 authorize substance-over-form and treaty-abuse adjustments. A mandatory 40% false-return penalty applies, and failure to withhold and remit triggers independent penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: The nominee director is stripped of the corporate veil because he or she exercised no independent judgment. As the responsible withholding agent, the director is personally liable for the 22% withholding deficiency. Courts may order civil restitution disgorging all fees received for the conduit arrangement, the director's travel is restricted under the National Tax Collection Act blacklist regime, and intentional non-withholding of non-resident tax carries criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years.

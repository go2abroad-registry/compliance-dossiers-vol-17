Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic tax portal: HomeTax system (https://www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service electronic declaration portal: UNI-PASS (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity with a Korean domestic place of business: (1) determine existence of a domestic place of business under Article 94 of the Corporate Tax Act; (2) prepare the foreign corporation business registration application, notarized certificate of incorporation, articles of association, written resolution appointing a Korean domestic tax representative, and power of attorney; (3) file the application through HomeTax or at the NTS district tax office with jurisdiction over the domestic place of business, or if the entity is a newly formed Korean Chusik Hoesa, file the standard business registration application before commencing activities (Korea does not impose minimum capital for a Chusik Hoesa); (4) receive a single 10-digit business registration number from the NTS, which functions as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration ID; (5) use that number for corporate income tax, VAT, electronic tax invoice issuance, and customs transactions. A foreign entity without a domestic place of business is not required to register for Korean VAT unless it undertakes Korean-taxable supplies, in which case registration through a Korean tax representative is required under the Value-Added Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Korean Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation subject to separate entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through or partnership tax treatment.

Available election forms

None: Korea has no check-the-box election that would allow a Chusik Hoesa to be treated as a pass-through entity. A wholly-owned Korean subsidiary may be included in a Korean tax consolidation group under the Corporate Tax Act, and hybrid mismatch deduction-denial rules apply under the Corporate Tax Act for cross-border hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for a Chusik Hoesa: VAT registration is mandatory before commencing any business activity. The small-business VAT exemption in the VAT Act applies only to small individual entrepreneurs and is not available to a corporation such as a Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act to (1) goods physically exported to a foreign destination and cleared by Korea Customs Service; and (2) qualifying services supplied to, performed for, or actually consumed by non-residents outside Korea, including financial data and newswire API syndication services delivered to foreign subscribers. To substantiate the zero rate, the Korean supplier must evidence the cross-border movement of goods or the offshore location/use of the digital service, the non-resident status of the counterparty, and the receipt of payment in convertible foreign currency.

  • Export Customs Declaration Certificate (Export Declaration/Customs Clearance Certificate) issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or international Air Waybill showing export from Korea and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and signed export/sales contract stating the export price, Incoterms, foreign buyer, and payment terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Export Performance Confirmation issued by the foreign exchange bank or Korea Customs Service evidencing completion of export performance
  • For zero-rated digital/API services: executed offshore services agreement, non-resident customer identity evidence, IP/server access logs proving offshore consumption, and the foreign-currency remittance certificate (in lieu of customs declaration and bill of lading)

Electronic tax invoice issuance is mandatory for all corporate VAT taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted to the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system by the day following issuance (normal B2B rule), with digital-certificate authentication and NTS-validated electronic invoice numbers. The HomeTax system performs automated purchase/sales invoice cross-matching for anti-fraud purposes. Korea does not operate an ex ante real-time fiscal clearance stamping system; the anti-fraud control is the mandatory electronic transmission, issue-number assignment, cryptographic authentication, and post-transaction risk analytics.

The NTS may deny an unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating, re-classify the supply as domestic 10% VAT, re-assess the VAT plus interest, and impose additional tax of up to 40% in cases of fraudulent underreporting or refund claims, or 10% for negligent underreporting. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the required 180-day period without a valid extension can invalidate the zero-rating and trigger clawback of the VAT benefit. Intentional falsification of export or foreign-exchange documentation may also result in criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines, under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a highly liberalised current account. The Korean won is fully convertible for trade settlements, service payments, dividends, and remittances of ordinary business profits once the underlying transaction is verified and reported by a foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are also broadly open to foreign investors, but require prior notification, post-transaction reporting, or registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There are no general surrender requirements for export proceeds; mandatory conversion ratios were abolished. Nevertheless, profit remittance hurdles exist in practice: the remitting bank will demand evidence of dividend distribution under the Korean Commercial Act, tax withholding certificates, and a clear statement of the foreign exchange purpose code. Any remittance of a capital-account nature requires reporting via the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and non-resident Korean-won accounts are strictly managed. The won is not internationally deliverable without a domestic banking connection, so offshore NDF pricing is referenced for hedging. Exchange-rate volatility can be managed with licensed hedging products, but all hedging must reference real trade or investment exposure. In short, Korea is not a stringent exchange-controlled jurisdiction, but compliance friction is found in registration, documentation, and purpose-code discipline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Transactions, export proceeds for ordinary goods must be collected within 180 calendar days from the export date, with extension or alteration approval for long-term projects or special overseas payment arrangements; service export receipts are normally required within 90 days unless approved by the receiving bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; the exporter maintains the foreign currency in a licensed domestic foreign-currency account and may elect to convert. The receiving bank must issue an export settlement certificate and report the collection into the Bank of Korea statistics system for surveillance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for any fintech EMI acting as a standalone treasury rail. South Korea restricts the taking of deposits and the conduct of foreign exchange business to institutions licensed under the Banking Act or authorized as foreign exchange business handling institutions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore EMIs such as global digital banking platforms are not currently recognized for Korean corporate won accounts or local clearing. They can assist with upstream conversion outside the country, but the Korean entity must rely on a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for collecting subscription revenue from domestic clients and for executing compliant cross-border wires. Any EMI must partner with a licensed local bank to touch Korean won, and the bank's KYC rather than the EMI's KYC will be relied upon for treasury counterparty risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant sequence is: file the foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before receiving the funds, open a foreign-currency capital escrow account at a Tier-1 bank, receive the SWIFT MT103 with the correct FDI purpose code and exact beneficiary name, convert into Korean won through the bank's licensed FX desk at the prevailing spot rate, obtain the Foreign Investment Report Certificate and the bank-issued capital receipt or verification, and register the investment with the local tax authority if applicable. Any inbound transfer must be tagged with the investor's legal name and the corporate registration number of the Korean entity; the bank will verify the remitter against the approved FDI shareholder list.For monthly syndicated finance, news aggregation, and content licensing fees, SWIFT MT103 is the primary clearing rail, with supporting service contracts and VAT invoices submitted to the bank for outward remittance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For any physical or documentary trade, such as media equipment or office procurement, Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are acceptable rails; the bank will review the underlying commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and tax evidence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for any fintech EMI acting as a standalone treasury rail. Offshore EMIs such as global digital banking platforms are not currently recognized for Korean corporate won accounts or local clearing. They can assist with upstream conversion outside the country, but the Korean entity must rely on a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for collecting subscription revenue from domestic clients and for executing compliant cross-border wires. Any EMI must partner with a licensed local bank to touch Korean won, and the bank's KYC rather than the EMI's KYC will be relied upon for treasury counterparty risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Korean domestic CIT: 9% on taxable base up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion; plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the computed CIT (effective top rate 23.1%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory dividend WHT (20% national dividend withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge). DTA relief can reduce this to 0-15%, typically 10% for a qualified 10% or more corporate shareholder and 15% for portfolio, provided the beneficial owner satisfies the treaty's limitation-of-benefits clause.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (intercompany transaction master and supporting comparability study) is required when the annual remuneration from each foreign related party in international transactions exceeds KRW 5 billion. Master File is required when total revenue is at least KRW 100 billion AND the sum of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds KRW 5 billion. In all cases, an 'International Transactions Report' (Form M/N) must be filed with the annual Corporate Tax Return even below the KRW 5 billion threshold.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but NTS and tax courts enforce a substantive 'substance over form' rule. For a financial newswire API under ISIC 6391, the Core Income-Generating Activities are: sourcing raw financial news, editing/aggregating the feed, software development, API infrastructure and server hosting, contract negotiation, billing/credit risk management, and customer support. These activities must be performed by employees and assets actually located in Korea. If they are outsourced to a related foreign company without profit attribution, the Korean entity's income may be recharacterised, treaty benefits denied, and personal liabilities imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS disregards the shell form under Article 7 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and Article 103 of the Corporate Tax Act, adding the diverted licensing fee to the Korean tax base and applying 30% fraud penalty. The Department of Justice can indict under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for tax evasion; the court may impose imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of not less than 3 times the evaded national tax.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil when the company is a facade. Directors who signed the sham licensing agreement are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax deficit, interest, and penalties under Commercial Act Articles 389(3), 399 and 401; they must restore the extracted corporate funds to the estate. The NTS may request a departure ban (Blacklisting) against the CEO and the controlling shareholders until the delinquency is settled; criminal conviction also triggers civil asset seizure and restitution from the personal bank accounts.
Transfer Pricing Fraud / Fake Arm's-Length Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: The NTS will perform a transfer pricing adjustment under Article 85 of the Corporate Tax Act and Article 7 of the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, recalculating the arm's-length gross margin at 15-25% for a low-functionality distributor, and will assess the difference plus 20% negligence penalty and 3.8% overdue interest. If the pricing documents are fabricated, the business is criminally liable for false tax invoices and fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 10, with imprisonment up to 7 years in aggregation.

Director personal liability: The director who certified the false TP policy is in violation of the duty of care under Commercial Act §399 and is liable to reimburse the company for the entire additional taxes and penalty. If the evasion exceeds KRW 500 million, the NTS names the CFO and CEO in its habitual evader blacklist, triggering travel restrictions, visa revocation, and detention upon departure; the court orders reimbursement from the director's personal assets under civil joint-tort liability (Civil Act Articles 750 and 760).
Withholding Tax, Foreign Exchange Reporting and AML-adjacent False Declarations

Statutory sanction: The Chusik Hoesa, as withholding agent, is personally liable for the under-withheld WHT (KRW 220 million) by operation of Article 76(4) of the Corporate Tax Act and Article 21 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. The NTS imposes a 30% penalty on the under-withheld amount, and the false submission to the NTS constitutes criminal tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3. The Bank of Korea may also apply a delay penalty under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 18 for unreported SWIFT/Stripe transactions.

Director personal liability: The officers who signed the false TRC and routing instructions are jointly liable for the withheld taxes under the 'actual management' doctrine; they cannot avoid liability by resigning. The court will order personal asset restitution of the diverted dividends plus statutory interest, impose a departure ban until the tax is paid, and if the defendant is convicted, impose imprisonment of up to 3 years for the false report and up to 5 years for tax evasion. The foreign recipient is named a non-cooperative jurisdiction risk, which triggers Enhanced Due Diligence by Korean financial institutions for any future Swift Wire transfer.

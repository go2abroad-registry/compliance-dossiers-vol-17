The application requires submission of the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Incorporation, proof of foreign entity registration (e.g., certificate of good standing from home jurisdiction), and appointment of a domestic tax representative (if no permanent establishment). The BRN serves as both the Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the VAT registration number. The application can be filed online via HomeTax System or in person. Upon approval, the entity receives a BRN certificate, which must be used for all tax filings and invoicing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to services supplied to non-residents for use outside South Korea, including maritime crew recruitment services for foreign vessels, provided the services are performed outside Korea or are directly related to export of goods. The service must be supplied to a foreign entity, payment must be received in foreign currency, and the service must not be consumed in Korea.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-determined exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility and a regulated but progressively liberalized capital account. Residents and the Korean entity may make current-account payments if the payment is supported by genuine underlying invoices, contracts, or tax certificates, while capital-account transactions require filing, reporting, or approval through designated foreign exchange banks or the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Export proceeds are not confiscated or forcibly converted; a Korean export service company can retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts and use those funds for legitimate foreign-currency disbursements without surrender. Remittance of corporate profits and dividends is permitted after payment of Korean corporate income tax and withholding tax, provided the underlying foreign investment was duly reported and the bank receives the tax payment certificate, financial statements, and board-approved distribution resolution. For non-residents, the Korean won is not freely traded offshore for speculative purposes; onshore convertibility is supported by banking channels only. Capital inflows must carry a clear transaction purpose and investment declaration, and non-resident loans, derivatives, or direct investments may be subject to reporting thresholds. Overall, the regime balances open current-account flows with strict prudential surveillance on investment and FX hedging activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

General export receipts must be collected through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of export declaration unless an extension is granted under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the mandatory surrender ratio is 0%, allowing 100% of export proceeds to be retained in a foreign-currency deposit account or freely converted into Korean won at the prevailing market rate. For maritime crew recruitment and crew-management services, the corresponding service invoice and signed crewing contract must be presented so the bank can assign the correct foreign-exchange transaction purpose code when the receipt is settled.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of South Korea) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed banks or authorized foreign-exchange dealers under Korean law. The Korean entity cannot maintain a primary KRW operating account or capital verification account with an unlicensed EMI, nor can it execute statutory foreign-exchange reports through such a firm. The Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act restrict payment services to licensed domestic financial institutions. An offshore EMI may be used for low-value non-creditor bookkeeping convenience only in ancillary currency conversion or travel cards, but it can never act as the clearing intermediary for crew payroll, capital injection, or trade settlement. The legal route is a licensed tier-1 Korean bank account supported by the EMI as a secondary information channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first file the required foreign-investment report through a designated Korean foreign exchange bank or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) before the capital wire is transmitted. Once reported, the investor sends a SWIFT MT103 from the original bank account to the Korean bank's capital escrow account, using SWIFT remittance fields to state the investor name, foreign investment declaration number, and foreign-exchange purpose code for direct investment capital. The receiving bank confirms the foreign currency inwards remittance, performs underlying documentation review and sanctions screening, and issues a foreign capital receipt certificate, which becomes the capital verification evidence for the Corporate Tax and FDI registration. A separate KRW current account can be funded by converting the foreign currency at the prevailing market exchange rate after the bank registers the conversion; the KRW conversion must be backed by the investment report and cannot be used for speculative offshore settlement or lending outside the declared business purpose.For seafarer payroll, the clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 per beneficiary or batched multi-currency payroll files through the Korean bank's treasury platform. For payments to non-resident shipowners or crewing principals, use SWIFT MT103, with MT202 cover for bank-to-bank reimbursements where required. Commercial letters of credit are appropriate where a charterparty or crewing service agreement contains payment guarantee conditions; the bank adjudicates the LC against presented shipping documents, crewing confirmation, invoice, and certificate of delivery of services. Documentary collections can be used for lower-value receivables, requiring the same set of original transport/crew-service documents. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Top combined statutory effective rate 27.5% (25% national Corporate Income Tax plus 10% Local Income Tax surtax levied on the national CIT amount); national CIT bracketed rates are 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 20% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 22% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 25% on the excess over KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined rate on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% Local Income Tax surtax (10% of the 20% national withholding). An applicable bilateral income tax treaty may reduce the rate to between 5% and 15%, generally requiring a 10% or higher shareholding for the lower treaty rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Korean taxpayer included in an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of KRW 100 billion or more is generally required to prepare a Master File. A Local File becomes mandatory when cross-border intercompany transactions in goods reach KRW 50 billion, when related-party services or loans reach KRW 10 billion, and when intangible-related transactions reach KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year. Country-by-Country reporting applies when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion. Below these thresholds, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is still recommended for maritime crew-management fee arrangements with overseas shipowners or foreign affiliates.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 and the Corporate Tax Act Article 4 establish a substance-over-form doctrine and a general anti-abuse rule. The NTS and the courts disregard transactions whose only purpose is tax avoidance.

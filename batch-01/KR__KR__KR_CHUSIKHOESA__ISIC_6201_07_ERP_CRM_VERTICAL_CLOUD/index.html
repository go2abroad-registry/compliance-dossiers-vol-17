Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None: Korean law does not currently prescribe a fixed statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. Incorporators may set a nominal share capital in the Articles of Incorporation. However, before filing incorporation registration, the entire subscription price must be deposited into a designated bank subscription account or secured by a bank guarantee in accordance with Article 305 of the Korean Commercial Act. No post-registration blocked escrow account is required; the subscribed funds become corporate assets upon registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; electronic registration and compliance portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr), together with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS for export clearance declarations

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Chusik Hoesa, the statutory tax identification process begins with incorporation in the Korean Commercial Registry. The representative director files the Business Registration application with the competent District Tax Office within 20 days from the commencement of business, using the Korean corporate registration number, articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, corporate seal certificate, evidence of Korean business premises, and director identification documents. The NTS then issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in Korea but is subject to Korean VAT registration for cross-border electronic services, the foreign entity must, electronically via HomeTax or through a properly appointed domestic tax representative, submit a Business Registration application accompanied by the foreign certificate of incorporation, charter or equivalent constitutive document, board resolution, power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative, and any additional evidence that the entity has economic activity triggering Korean tax registration. The NTS will then issue a Korean tax identification number allocated to the non-resident business operator.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration under the Korean VAT Act has no monetary turnover threshold; every taxable business must register within 20 days from commencement. The KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supply threshold identifies eligibility for simplified taxpayer treatment for very small domestic businesses. For foreign non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied digital services to Korean unregistered customers, mandatory Korean VAT registration is triggered when the annual taxable supply to Korean consumers exceeds the statutory overseas e-commerce supplier threshold prescribed under the VAT Act Enforcement Decree, currently set at KRW 50,000,000, or when a designated domestic tax representative or withholding arrangement is otherwise required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods qualify for zero-rated VAT when physical exportation is evidenced and the goods are delivered to a foreign destination. Cross-border qualifying services, including software, ERP, CRM, cloud services, and other electronically supplied services, qualify for zero-rated VAT only when the recipient is a non-resident of Korea, the payment is received from sources outside Korea, title and control pass to a foreign customer, and the service is consumed outside Korea. For vertical cloud software sold both locally and cross-border from Korea, the taxpayer must prove the foreign end-customer location, the export destination of the service data flow, and the influx of foreign currency consideration.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration issued through UNI-PASS (electronic export clearance report evidencing statutory exportation)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the Korean Chusik Hoesa as shipper/exporter and the foreign purchaser as consignee
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the submitted Customs Export Declaration
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice or electronic tax invoice issued under the Korean e-tax invoice system and cross-referenced to the exported transaction

All VAT taxpayers are required to issue Korean tax invoices and transmit electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system. Electronic tax invoices must be submitted to the NTS within the statutory filing timeline after supply, and ERP systems can interface with HomeTax through structured API/ME-based batch transmission channels authorized by the NTS. Korea operates a national centralized e-tax invoice data repository rather than a third-party pre-clearance or fiscal stamping system; the NTS uses real-time data intake for anti-fraud cross-matching of tax invoices, trade transactions, and VAT returns.

The NTS, together with Korea Customs Service and Korean banking foreign exchange records, will cross-check zero-rated export claims against Customs Export Declarations, e-tax invoices, shipping or digital delivery logs, and foreign exchange settlement data. If the taxpayer fails to substantiate exportation, the foreign recipient status, lawful foreign exchange collection, or actual offshore consumption, the NTS will reverse the zero-rating and reassess 10% VAT on the full supply value. Additionally, the NTS will impose statutory penalty tax for negligent or fraudulent under-reporting, incorrect tax invoices, and failure to adhere to e-invoicing rules, with aggravated penalties and criminal referral to the prosecutor's office under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act where forged customs declarations or fabricated remittance evidence are discovered. The clawback extends to any input VAT credits previously claimed against the zero-rated supply, and paid VAT becomes immediately due with statutory interest.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a de jure liberalised current account for trade and service payments; Korean won are fully convertible for legitimate operational flows, and no mandatory surrender of export proceeds is imposed. Capital account transactions including inbound equity investment, intercompany debt, securities and derivatives are subject to real-time reporting and, in certain cases, prior declaration through a licensed foreign exchange bank to the Foreign Exchange Information System. For a vertical ERP/CRM cloud company, the dominant cross-border flows are software licence fees, subscription revenues, subcontractor payments and intercompany royalties; these are treated as current account items when bona fide and underlying economic substance is demonstrated. Profit remittance to a foreign parent is freely permitted after submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the Korean National Tax Service, and compliance with corporate governance documentation. The KRW floats with occasional BOK smoothing intervention; there is no layered approval licence for ordinary commercial transactions; however, practical hurdles include document-heavy validation of beneficial ownership, strict deadlines for reporting foreign receipts, and a legal prohibition on relying on unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs as the primary collection account for Korean revenue. The assessor must also distinguish capital from current account for each FX operation to avoid triggering the post-audit penalty regime under FETA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in South Korea. Exporters must receive all foreign currency due under an export or service agreement through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank and must report receipt of each export value to the bank within 5 business days under FETA. Foreign currency may be held in a resident foreign currency account or converted to KRW voluntarily at market rates, but the underlying customs declaration or service export contract must be matched to the receipt. For software-related services, the documentation includes the signed contract, the SK-505 service export filing where applicable, and corresponding invoice/settlement instruction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), with the Foreign Exchange Information System as the operational reporting gateway

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Korean financial regulation confines deposit-taking and foreign-exchange business to locally licensed banks. Offshore fintech EMIs holding an e-money or payment institution licence in another jurisdiction are legally barred from maintaining won-denominated corporate accounts or executing onshore FX for Korean entities without a local banking joint venture or a licensed bank umbrella. Stripe Billing and Direct Debit can be used as payment gateways, but funds must ultimately settle into a licenced Korean Tier-1 bank account. A global EMI may be used for non-Korean revenue streams, yet any Korea-sourced revenue or intra-Korea treasury needs a bank-account path. The onboarding compliance team should therefore always start with a domestic Tier-1 bank, and a foreign policy EMI only as a supplementary instrument for USD/EUR receipts where the customer is physically located outside Korea.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital from the overseas parent must be wired to a designated Foreign Exchange Bank under the bank's FDI code 'Foreign Direct Investment' (FETA classification 320100). The transferring bank must file a foreign direct investment declaration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the Foreign Investment Support System (FISS) before or upon receipt. The funding channel is standard SWIFT MT103 with the explicit purpose code embedded in the wire narrative. After receipt, the bank issues a 'certificate of foreign direct investment', enabling registration of the Korean entity as a Foreign-Invested Company. Korean won are then released to a domestic operating current account at the spot exchange rate. For intercompany loans, a separate loan facility with a mandated rate and repayment schedule must be filed under FETA to prevent regulatory recharacterisation.Software subscription royalties, partner commissions, infrastructure fees and consulting disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 messages with the appropriate International Service Trade purpose code (e.g., 22600 for software implementation, 20400 for IT services). Each payment must be backed by a signed contract and an itemised invoice archived for at least 5 years under FETA. Commercial letters of credit may be used for large enterprise licence deals, but are uncommon in cloud software; documentary collections are rarely used. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9%-24% progressive national Corporate Income Tax; plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national CIT, giving an effective combined range of 9.9% to 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, giving an aggregate statutory rate of 22%; applicable Korea tax treaties typically reduce the rate to 5% to 15% depending on the shareholder's ownership percentage and treaty-lite clauses

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File documentation is required where the Korean entity has annual revenue of at least KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions of at least KRW 50 billion; Local File documentation is generally required where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions reach KRW 50 billion, with per-foreign-related-party thresholds of KRW 5 billion triggering file preparation; even below the main thresholds, the annual Report on International Related-Party Transactions (Statement of Overseas Related Party Transactions) must be filed with the CIT return and contemporaneous documentation should be kept for transactions above the simplified reporting thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and OECD BEPS Action 6 LOB/PPT principles incorporated into Korea tax treaties; a Korean Chusik Hoesa claiming domestic deductions or treaty benefits must show real core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea, including actual ERP/CRM sales staff, cloud infrastructure decision-making, contract negotiation, software development, risk assumption, and local bank accounts; a pure conduit subsidiary with no economic substance cannot claim DTA relief and may have intercompany payments recharacterized as deemed dividends or Korean-source income subject to the full 22% withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to ten times the evaded tax, with repeat offenders exposed to up to ten years; the Framework Act on National Taxes imposes a 20% negligent payment penalty plus daily late-payment interest; fraudulent VAT or cash-sale suppression can also trigger the VAT Act penal provisions.

Director personal liability: Korean courts and the NTS will look to the de facto controlling director; under Korean Commercial Act Articles 389 and 401, directors are jointly and severally liable for company tax liabilities caused by intentional or grossly negligent conduct; civil asset seizure, criminal imprisonment of responsible directors, travel bans and NTS delinquency blacklisting may be imposed, and the corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a sham for tax evasion.
Transfer pricing manipulation and missing TP documentation

Statutory sanction: The International Tax Adjustment Act permits the NTS to adjust taxable income to the arm's-length amount, deny deductions for lacking substance, apply a 20% penalty on the underreported international transaction tax amount, and extend the statute of limitations; failure to produce TP documentation can result in a separate penalty and adverse evidentiary inference.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or approve fabricated transfer prices can be treated as secondary taxpayers when the company cannot pay, with personal liability for CIT, local surtax, and penalties; criminal prosecution for document forgery or tax avoidance can lead to imprisonment; the foreign parent's excessive payments may be reclassified as constructive dividends subject to 22% WHT, and directors face personal asset recovery and travel restrictions.
Shell company and tax treaty abuse / failure of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The NTS will disregard the shell entity under the substance-over-form GAAR provisions of the Framework Act on National Taxes, deny treaty benefits under LOB/PPT clauses, reclassify all reported service or royalty payments as Korean-source dividends or other income, apply the full 22% WHT plus interest, and impose a 20% unfaithful-return penalty; the tax authority may also cancel tax clearance and report the entity to the financial information disclosure system.

Director personal liability: The directors and shadow directors who control the shell face piercing of the corporate veil, personal assessment for all unpaid taxes, civil restitution of diverted assets, Korean court-ordered travel bans and arrest warrants, and imprisonment for fraudulent misrepresentation; non-resident directors conducting Korean management without a work visa also face immigration bars and forced exit from Korea.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - Korean law does not prescribe a minimum stated capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, if the promoters fix a stated capital in the Articles of Incorporation, the full subscription price must be paid into a designated bank account opened in the company's name before the incorporation registration is filed. The bank must issue a capital payment certificate or deposit certificate confirming the receipt of the full subscription funds. There is no statutory minimum amount, but the capital must be genuinely received and the deposit account is effectively blocked until the registration certificate is issued; funds may be released for lawful business purposes after incorporation.

If a warehouse is introduced, obtain a separate Civil Defense fire safety certificate, racking stability inspection, and emergency evacuation plan approval before operations begin.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS). The official electronic portal is the HomeTax system, which is the online channel for tax registration, electronic tax invoice transmission, VAT filing, and issuance of Business Registration Certificates. Export clearance is handled separately by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation), the statutory route is: (1) notarize the articles of incorporation and complete any shareholder capital subscriptions, noting that no statutory minimum paid-in capital is required; (2) register the incorporation with the competent District Court under the Korean Commercial Act and obtain the Certificate of Corporation Registration; (3) within 20 days of business commencement, file the Business Registration application with the competent District Tax Office or electronically through the NTS HomeTax system, using the procedures prescribed by the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act; (4) submit the supporting dossier, including the articles of incorporation, Certificate of Corporation Registration, proof of the principal office location (lease agreement or building registration), identity evidence of directors, the corporate seal registration certificate, and the board resolution authorizing the Korean entity; (5) upon verification, the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is simultaneously the corporate tax identification number, the VAT registration number, and the number required for all tax invoices. For a foreign non-resident seller that has no Korean place of business but makes taxable Korean-destination supplies, including cross-border e-commerce, digital content, or game distribution, the foreign entity must appoint a Korean tax representative under a notarized power of attorney. The tax representative files the Foreign Business Operator VAT Registration application through the NTS HomeTax foreign-registration channel and submits the foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution, statutes or equivalent constitutional documents, evidence of the Korean payment gateway and local logistics or digital-service use arrangements, and the tax representative's appointment documents. The NTS then issues a Foreign Business Operator VAT identification number, which the foreign seller must quote on any tax invoices and on its Korean VAT filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean national tax law applies mandatory entity-level taxation to a Chusik Hoesa and does not permit US-style check-the-box elections, partnership-style pass-through treatment, or hybrid mismatch entity elections. A Chusik Hoesa cannot be disregarded for Korean corporate income tax or VAT purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold for a Chusik Hoesa: registration is mandatory within 20 days of business commencement regardless of turnover. The simplified taxable person ceiling for certain unincorporated small physical merchants is KRW 80,000,000 of annual taxable turnover, but this simplified status is not available to a stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, a zero rate applies to supplies of goods exported from Korea, to services rendered to non-residents outside Korea where the place of supply and effective use and enjoyment are outside Korea, and to qualifying international transportation services. For digital content and game exports, the Korean supplier must evidence that the recipient is a non-resident customer located abroad, that the service is supplied and consumed outside Korea, and that the consideration is received through foreign payment rails. For physical e-commerce and mail-order exports settled by cash on delivery, zero rating requires the goods to be physically delivered to a foreign customs jurisdiction under a Korea Customs Service export clearance, followed by foreign exchange repatriation to Korea. In a COD cross-border model, the exporter must preserve the complete transactional chain proving the destination of goods, the foreign buyer identity, and the payment collected overseas and remitted inbound.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration cleared through UNI-PASS and containing the export bill number
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or a postal/EMS dispatch record for small-value parcel exports
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Remittance Confirmation under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Export sales contract or overseas buyer purchase order
  • Proof of final overseas delivery, such as foreign customs entry evidence, delivery confirmation, or logistics event trace for the destination country

Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing through the NTS HomeTax system for all VAT-registered businesses. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS at issuance, and the HomeTax platform validates the issuer and assignee tax registration data through an API-connected real-time clearance layer. There is no physical paper stamp or independent fiscal seal, but anti-fraud protection operates through mandatory sequentially referenced electronic tax invoice numbers, automatic NTS matching of input and output VAT, mandatory electronic cash receipt issuance for qualifying B2C transactions, and strict denial of input VAT credits for invoices that were not issued, transmitted, or validated through HomeTax. ERP-to-NTS integration is supported by HomeTax API and since-module connectors. Korea therefore has a mandatory digital fiscal clearance record, but not a pre-transaction stamping model.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating through cross-matching of UNI-PASS declarations, transport and parcel tracking records, payment gateway settlement data, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rated export supplies are found to be fictitious, unsupported, diverted to the domestic Korean market, or not settled through qualified foreign exchange repatriation within the statutory 180-day deadline, the zero rating is retrospectively annulled and the supply is reclassified as taxable domestic supply. The NTS will assess output VAT at 10%, plus negligence surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes, fraudulent understatement penalties of up to 40% of the unpaid VAT, and interest. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act separately imposes penalty surcharges and administrative fines for unrepatriated export proceeds, and confirmed export VAT fraud or issuance of false electronic tax invoices is subject to criminal referral under Korean tax evasion statutes. The statutory assessment period for these clawback adjustments applies for the standard national tax audit window, during which the taxpayer must retain the complete documentation bundle listed above.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-market current account with no mandatory conversion of export earnings to KRW; however, all capital account movements and certain current account settlements must be reported to the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Profit remittance by a foreign-invested Chusik Hoesa is generally permitted after verification of paid-in capital, retained earnings, tax clearance, and statutory audit where required, but remittances above USD 500,000 must be filed with the foreign exchange bank and may require additional supporting documentation. Non-resident shareholders may convert KRW dividends into foreign currency without prior approval provided the underlying investment was registered and the remittance is within the confirmed earnings base. Capital account items, including inbound equity injections and outbound loans, are subject to prior notification or approval thresholds. The Korean won is convertible for trade and ordinary business transactions, but speculative flows are curtailed by real demand documentation, certificate of deposit rules, and one-time registration requirements. Exchange rates are determined under a market average system, and banks may provide local and foreign currency accounts without statutory surrender ratio.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within 18 months from the export performance date; there is no mandatory surrender ratio to local currency, but the proceeds must be deposited with a foreign exchange bank and the conversion to KRW may be made at the bank's announced customer exchange rate. Failure to collect within the statutory deadline requires Bank of Korea approval or an extension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability is low for offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex as a primary account because they are not licensed banks under the Korean Banking Act and cannot hold customer deposits in KRW or open foreign investment registered accounts. They may serve only as ancillary FX collection and disbursement rails if they operate through a licensed domestic bank partnership; domestic regulatory reporting must still be executed by the Korean bank. Therefore, an entity should not rely on a standalone fintech EMI for statutory capital verification or trade settlement reporting.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean resident corporation to a non-resident, plus 10% local surcharge on the WHT, for an effective statutory rate of 22%; most Korean DTAs reduce the rate to 5%-10% for substantial shareholdings and 10%-15% for portfolio investors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate foreign related-party transactions are at least KRW 5 billion for the fiscal year; Master File is mandatory when the aggregate reaches KRW 50 billion; a reduced threshold of KRW 3 billion applies to related parties in designated tax-haven jurisdictions, and documentation must be submitted to the NTS within 30 days of a request.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a separate labeled ESR statute but enforces a functional equivalent through substance-over-form principles, the corporate residence rules of the Corporate Income Tax Act, and CFC and GAAR provisions of the Adjustment of International Taxes Act; a Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain real local premises, employees, books, management decision-making, and control over COD logistics and payment settlement, because if Core Income-Generating Activities are performed in Korea yet attributed to the foreign parent, the NTS may recharacterize the parent as a Korean permanent establishment, reallocate e-commerce profit to Korea, deny treaty benefits, and impose TP corrections and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported Cash-on-Delivery Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 9 and 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, intentional tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to 2-3 times the evaded tax; civil additional charges include a 40% fraudulent-return negligence surcharge, a 20% non-filing surcharge, and daily delinquency interest of about 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: a director who ordered or benefited from the concealment is jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, VAT, surcharges, and interest; the director is blacklisted as a serious delinquent taxpayer, published with the tax deficiency, placed under an overseas travel ban, and faces criminal imprisonment and personal asset restitution to the National Tax Service.
Withholding Tax Failure on Outbound Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The entity as statutory withholding agent is held liable for the full unwithheld amount plus a 30% non-payment additional charge and daily delinquency interest; treaty relief is denied because the TRC and beneficial-ownership protocol was not satisfied, and criminal penalties apply if the transaction is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto representatives who signed the remittance instructions are secondarily liable for the unwithheld tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes; the NTS may attach their personal assets, place them on the departure-restriction blacklist for unpaid tax above the statutory threshold, and pursue criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Korean GAAR and the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, the NTS may deny treaty benefits, reject the Tax Residency Certificate application, recharacterize the arrangement, impose transfer pricing understatement penalties of 15%-30% of the adjusted taxable income, and add daily delinquency interest; the foreign parent can be taxed as a Korean permanent establishment.

Director personal liability: Resident directors are personally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties, with the corporate veil pierced to attach personal bank accounts and real estate; directors are blacklisted and travel-restricted, must restore transferred assets to the tax authority, and face criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, including imprisonment for up to 5 years.

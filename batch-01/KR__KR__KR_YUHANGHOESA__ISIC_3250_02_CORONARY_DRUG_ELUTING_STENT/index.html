Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating Korean won (KRW) with a fully convertible current account for trade settlements, services, and ordinary business remittances. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act adopts a notification-based system rather than a blanket license requirement: current account transactions are generally unrestricted, while capital account transactions such as direct equity investment, overseas borrowing, and portfolio flows require prior filing, approval, or use of a designated foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts and no mandatory conversion to KRW is imposed. Profit remittances such as dividends for a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa are legally permissible after the statutory corporate tax return and withholding tax clearance; however, remittance applications must be supported by audited financial statements, board resolutions, and tax payment certificates. The main hurdles are not convertibility caps but documentary proof of beneficial ownership, filing of the foreign investment notification through an authorized bank, KOTRA, or the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and compliance with FATF-aligned beneficial ownership reporting. In practice, banks apply enhanced due diligence to Class III medical device manufacturers and may reject transfers if the underlying commercial contract, shipping documents, and product certification are not consistent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export settlement must be completed within the agreed contractual payment term, with a standard statutory benchmark of 90 days from the date of export declaration; longer periods require approval from a foreign exchange bank. Korea does not impose a mandatory conversion or surrender ratio on export proceeds; exporters are allowed to maintain the proceeds in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts or convert them at market rates for KRW working capital.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (central bank), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the principal foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and similar offshore digital financial institutions are not viable as primary or legal corporate treasury rails in South Korea. They are not licensed as banks or authorized foreign exchange dealers under the Banking Act, cannot issue or operate a domestic KRW corporate account, and cannot clear inbound FDI or outbound trade settlements under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore EMIs may at most provide ancillary non-KRW expense cards, but their use is severely limited and they are not acceptable for FX exemption, capital verification, or KYC compliance evidence. Viability rating: Low.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax plus a 10% municipal local income tax surcharge (26.4% combined top marginal rate); lower brackets: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate for a non-resident corporation receiving dividends from a Korean payer (20% corporate income withholding under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 plus 2% local income tax surtax under Local Tax Act Article 103).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final corporate tax return must be filed within three months after the close of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year taxpayer the deadline is March 31 of the next year; non-resident corporations may, through a tax representative, obtain an NTS extension of up to seven months after fiscal year-end.

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion in the taxable year, with simplified documentation available above KRW 10 billion for small and medium-sized enterprises; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion and Korean related-party cross-border transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion; Country-by-Country Report filing is mandatory when the group has consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion.

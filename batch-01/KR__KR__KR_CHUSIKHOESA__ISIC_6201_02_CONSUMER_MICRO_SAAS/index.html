Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Korean law, a Chusik Hoesa must have a registered head office within Korea that is determined by the articles of incorporation; the address must be a physically existing place where the company's management functions are actually exercised. A virtual office or commercial mailbox without a lease of physical premises is generally not accepted by the Korean tax authorities (National Tax Service) or domestic banks for the Micro-SaaS industry, especially for obtaining a business registration certificate (사업자등록증) and opening a corporate bank account which requires in-person verification and a physical office visit in certain cases. Although online-only businesses may operate from a home address or serviced office, the National Tax Service expects lease agreements, utility bills, and evidence of actual business substance at the address upon registration and during on-site verification. Pure virtual office arrangements, where no staff is present during business hours and only a mail-forwarding service exists, are rejected by major banks as insufficient for account opening and by the NTS for input tax credit purposes. For a low-risk Micro-SaaS company using payment platforms such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy, a serviced office with a dedicated room and a management company agreement can be acceptable, provided that the address appears as the company's registered address in the corporate registry and NTS filings, and where the company maintains exclusive access to the space, business signage, books and records, and corporate email.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, operating the Home Tax Service (Hometax) electronic portal at hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) in South Korea must register for a Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number (Business Registration Certificate) with the local tax office having jurisdiction over the entity's registered head office address. The incorporation process via the Supreme Court Registry Office automatically notifies the tax authority; the entity must submit the application for business registration (including the corporate application for business registration) to the district tax office (or through Hometax) within the statutory time frame after incorporation. For VAT purposes, the same business registration number serves as the Value-Added Tax registration number, with a unique business type code for VAT. The representative director (foreign or Korean) must provide the corporate seal certificate, articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, and proof of registered address. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean permanent establishment selling cross-border digital services to Korean consumers may be required to register for simplified VAT registration under the Foreign Business Operator system, using the direct registration process via Hometax with a tax representative if required. For a resident Chusik Hoesa, once the corporate income tax registration is complete, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (e.g., 000-00-00000), which is used for corporate tax, VAT withholding, and customs purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for a domestic Chusik Hoesa; it is automatically treated as a corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax. No check-the-box partnership election exists. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration; however, a foreign provider of digital services has no threshold and must register effective from the first day of deemed supply of electronic services to Korean consumers. A domestic Chusik Hoesa engaged in business is mandatorily registered irrespective of turnover, but small businesses under the threshold may apply for simplified VAT registration; the general mandatory registration threshold is KRW 80,000,000 in the preceding calendar year (excluding VAT) for suppliers other than new businesses. Newly started businesses must register within 20 days of business commencement regardless of projected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (0% VAT) under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are delivered to a foreign buyer or exported via a customs export declaration. Services and digital content supplied to non-residents are also zero-rated. For a Micro SaaS subscription business, the supply of cloud software and digital content to non-residents (foreign users without a Korean place of business) qualifies for zero rating as 'overseas service exports' under the VAT Act if the place-of-supply rules treat the service as supplied outside Korea or if the consideration is received in foreign currency and the service is actually supplied for use outside Korea. Foreign exchange earned through exports must be repatriated to Korea unless a foreign currency receipt account is maintained with a Korean foreign exchange bank. The zero rating also applies to merchandising sold through overseas app stores, content platforms, and e-commerce when the place of effective use is outside Korea. Documentary substantiation requires a tax invoice for export, an export invoice, a copy of customs clearance (Export Declaration), and proof of payment in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) as filed with Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system
  • Bill of Lading (AWB / ocean bill / proof of export for intangible services may be substituted by service export invoice and payment evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the foreign exchange bank or a Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate (FIRC) proving receipt of payment from the foreign purchaser in foreign currency

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice / e-Bill) through the NTS Hometax system for all B2B supplies. For B2C transactions, a cash receipt (cash receipt system) must be issued upon request or when the payment amount exceeds KRW 100,000. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS within the day immediately following the end of the tax period (for monthly VAT filers, by the following month-end in some cases). There is no real-time pre-clearance stamping like Latin America; instead South Korea uses a risk-based real-time electronic tax invoice reporting system, e-Tax Invoice issuance and reporting via the NTS siren system, with the National e-Procurement (KONEPS) integration for government contracts. Digital service foreign providers must register for electronic service supply and issue electronic tax invoices through Hometax if transacting with Korean businesses; for B2C digital services, the foreign provider must file VAT returns quarterly and remit VAT on the gross supply at 10%. Anti-fraud digital stamping is not used; however, the NTS operates a robust e-tax invoice system with mandatory digital signatures and real-time audit data exchange.

If the exporter fails to substantiate zero-rated export within the required documentation timeframe or fails to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory deadline, the NTS will reverse the zero rating and assess 10% VAT on the export transaction, plus a 10% additional tax (in addition to the VAT liability) as an uncollected penalty. For a false or fraudulent export zero-rating claim, the importer/exporter may face criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (up to 3 years imprisonment or up to KRW 30,000,000 fine, or both depending on the amount), administrative penalties up to 40% of the evaded VAT, and customs penalties. Moreover, failure to issue and transmit electronic tax invoices can trigger a 2% penalty on the supply value for non-issuance, and a separate penalty of up to 50% of the tax amount for unverified zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalised foreign exchange regime under FETA, but the system is not absolutely free in every category. Current account transactions, including software subscriptions, royalties and service fees, are freely convertible and may be remitted once reporting obligations are fulfilled through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are more heavily administered: foreign investment into a Chusik Hoesa must first be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA), and the foreign investor must obtain a certificate of foreign investment before the capital escrow can be settled. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may hold earned USD/EUR/JPY in foreign currency accounts, but the claim must be received within six months from the export declaration date and reported for balance-of-payments statistics. Profit remittance, including dividends, is permitted after corporate tax withholding, tax clearance, and submission of FIPA registration proof; banks will require strict differentiation between current and capital flows because loan and dividend paths require different dossiers. In practice, KRW is domestically convertible for business operations and current account remittances, while non-resident access to local KRW funding and speculative FX positions remains carefully ring-fenced by bank position limits and real-demand documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and its subordinate regulations, an exporter must receive export proceeds within six months from the date of the export declaration; this period may be extended for another six months with approval of the Minister of Economy and Finance or the head of an authorized foreign exchange bank in delegated cases. There is no mandatory percentage conversion to KRW: proceeds may be credited to a foreign-currency account, voluntarily surrendered, or used to offset export-related obligations. The receiving foreign exchange bank must report the inward collection to the Bank of Korea as an export receipt for balance-of-payments compilation; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory period triggers administrative penalties and may be treated as a foreign exchange violation.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the core foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); BOK is the central bank and monetary authority while MOEF regulates foreign exchange business operators and BOK executes statistical monitoring through authorized foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-negligible as the sole banking rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Airwallex, and Wise are not authorized foreign exchange banks under FETA and therefore cannot legally open KRW settlement accounts or provide the statutory foreign investment capital escrow, tax clearance, and balance-of-payments reporting that a Korean Chusik Hoesa requires. Local Korean fintech platforms, including Toss Bank and KakaoBank, offer consumer-focused banking and do not act as EU-style EMIs; they may be used only as operational complements if they obtain corporate accounts, but the core treasury and FX compliance must sit with tier-1 domestic banks. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a foreign investor injecting equity into a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the foreign investor must file a foreign investment report under FIPA at the designated foreign exchange bank and open a foreign investment capital escrow account at the chosen bank. The overseas remittance should be sent through SWIFT MT103 with the investor's account name and reference; the purpose code should be the balance-of-payments code for a foreign direct investment into a domestic company, typically reported as establishment or acquisition of equity in a Korean corporation in the Foreign Exchange Reporting System. After actual inflow, the receiving bank issues a foreign investment registration certificate, and the paid-in capital is verified by the company's external accountant based on the bank deposit certificate and corporate registry. Because the Chusik Hoesa has no minimum capital requirement, the injection amount can be tailored to the operating plan but must be consistent with the FIPA report.Consumer subscriptions are settled through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record; the aggregators collect presentation fees and applicable taxes and remit net proceeds in USD/EUR/GBP to the Korean entity's foreign-currency account. The bank converts those receipts to KRW using booked FX rates. For B2B and infrastructure payments, the main clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for service fees, dividends, and royalties; commercial import documentary letters of credit for hardware or cloud services purchased from vendors; and documentary collection for high-value transactions where an LC is not cost-effective. Every outbound payment must be accompanied by an invoice, contract, and underlying FX purpose code.Onshore KRW/USD forward exchange contracts with an authorized foreign exchange bank, provided the entity demonstrates a genuine underlying trade or investment exposure and the contract tenor generally does not exceed one year without documented renewal.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for true trade hedge end users and within the bank's position limits established under FETA; Korean domestic corporate hedgers typically migrate to onshore forwards rather than NDFs because onshore forwards are fully legal and settle in KRW, while offshore NDFs carry additional reporting burdens.Cross-currency swaps for long-term foreign-currency loan-to-KRW conversion are allowed where the swap is supported by actual non-resident borrowing and the bank approves the transaction as an enterprise hedge, not speculative trading.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 8 million) for aggregate international related-party transactions in a fiscal year, which triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; CbCR filing threshold is KRW 1 trillion consolidated group revenue for the ultimate parent.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not have an Economic Substance Regulation similar to the UAE, but the NTS applies the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 14 of the Basic Act for National Tax and the OECD BEPS minimum standards. For a Korean micro-SaaS company, core income-generating activities (CIGA) include software development, server maintenance, data security, customer support, and revenue collection. The Korean entity must have real employees, office facilities, and decision-making authority over those activities. A foreign holding company that merely licenses IP into Korea without any substance will be disregarded for treaty relief and profits will be recharacterized as Korean-source through a Korean PE or a sham transaction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fraudulent Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (Articles 3 and 8) and the Corporate Tax Act. The NTS will impose a 40% surcharge on the understated tax, plus a 20% non-filing surcharge and daily overdue interest of 0.025% (annual 9.125%). Fraudulent evasion exceeding material thresholds is a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years or fines up to KRW 100 million, or both.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under Article 13 of the Basic Act for National Tax when the director acts as the de facto taxpayer or uses the company solely as a vehicle for tax avoidance. The director is personally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and interest. The director's assets may be subject to seizure, and the NTS may impose a travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from Korea if unpaid tax exceeds KRW 200 million. Criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and a permanent criminal record.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Sham Intercompany Services

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA), the NTS adjusts related-party transactions to the arm's length price and imposes a 20% unjustified-return penalty on the underpaid corporate tax, plus overdue interest. Failure to submit a Local/Master File when the threshold is met carries an additional 30% documentation-related surcharge and administrative fines up to KRW 20 million. The transaction may be recharacterized and the foreign entity ignored.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false contracts, creates phantom invoices, or issues certificates supporting a sham intercompany arrangement is jointly and severally liable for the additional corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Criminal liability under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for preparing false tax invoices carries imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 50 million. Directors may also be subjected to departure restraint (travel ban) and civil asset claims for breach of fiduciary duty.
Abusive Treaty Relief Claim and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny the treaty benefit under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) or Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clause of the DTA, and under the domestic General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 14 of the Basic Act for National Tax). The Korean payer is assessed the differential withholding tax (22% statutory minus the claimed treaty rate), plus a 40% penalty for willful misrepresentation, and interest. Refund claims made by the conduit are denied with additional penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify or knowingly approve a fraudulent beneficial ownership application are personally liable for the under-withheld tax, as they have breached their statutory duty to verify the recipient's substance. Under Article 11 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, aiding and abetting a tax evasion scheme is punishable by up to 2 years' imprisonment and fines. The NTS may attach the director's personal bank accounts, real estate, and vehicles, and may impose a travel ban on all directors and the registered agent.

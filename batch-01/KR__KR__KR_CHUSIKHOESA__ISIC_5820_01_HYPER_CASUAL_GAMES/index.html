Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A Chusik Hoesa (Stock Corporation) domiciled in South Korea must register with the National Tax Service through the competent district tax office that has jurisdiction over its registered head office or domestic place of business. The registration application must be filed online via HomeTax or by paper submission within one month of the commencement of business. Required documents include the company's court registry certificate (corporate registration certificate), articles of incorporation, proof of foreign capital investment registration where applicable, lease agreement or other evidence of a fixed domestic place of business, identity documents and passports of the statutory directors/representative, and a power of attorney appointing a domestic tax representative if the actual representative resides outside Korea. The NTS validates the filing and issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN/VAT ID) electronically on HomeTax and as a physical Business Registration Certificate. This single number is used for corporate tax withholding, VAT, and customs-identity purposes.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election, and hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognised for indirect tax purposes. Separate statutory consolidation provisions exist for certain domestic corporate tax electives, but those do not alter the entity-level VAT or withholding classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration exemption. Every economic activity involving the supply of goods or services in South Korea triggers mandatory VAT registration with the NTS. A simplified taxpayer compliance status applies to very small businesses with prior-year taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but this status does not waive the mandatory registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Korea upon presentation of a valid customs export declaration. Qualifying offshore digital services, including mobile game software, subscription content, and remote gaming services supplied to non-resident users who consume the service outside Korea, also qualify for the zero rate under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act. The service must be performed or deemed consumed outside Korea, the counterparty must not have a Korean tax nexus, and the consideration must be receivable in a foreign currency. For hyper-casual game export, monetisation through foreign advertising platforms may require the underlying invoice to be issued to a foreign advertiser or holding company to substantiate foreign-service treatment.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) evidencing the actual export of physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or international courier receipt covering the exported consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Commercial invoice matched to the customs declaration and foreign exchange proceeds
  • Foreign-level service agreement or purchase order identifying the non-resident recipient
  • Digital platform payout statements and app-store settlement reports for offshore digital game services and advertising revenues

Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing for all business-to-business VAT transactions. Any VAT-supplying business must transmit electronic tax invoices to the NTS HomeTax central clearing system or via an approved EDI/ERP API gateway. Each transmitted invoice receives a machine-readable anti-fraud key code and digital identifier from the NTS, creating a real-time fiscal clearance trail. The tax invoice must be issued within statutory deadlines, generally no later than the 10th day of the month following the month of taxable supply, and transmitted to the NTS within one business day of issuance. For business-to-consumer sales, cash receipts are required. Non-compliance results in input VAT denial, additional penalties, and administrative fines.

The NTS strictly audits zero-rated claims by cross-referencing export declarations, foreign-currency receipt data, and underlying contracts. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating triggers reversal of the VAT refund, retrospective tax-bearing assessment, punitive interest, under-reporting penalties of up to 20% for negligence, fraud penalties of up to 40% of the undisclosed or unlawfully refunded amount, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act. Clawback is not time-barred when tax evasion is demonstrated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a market-determined, free-floating exchange rate for the Korean won regime in which current-account convertibility is genuinely open: software publishing, ad-monetized digital game services, and associate service fees generated from foreign advertising networks are treated as invisible trade earnings and may be collected freely in foreign currency. However, the regulatory scaffolding remains prescriptive. Every receipt of export proceeds must be routed into a domestic foreign-exchange bank (FX bank) and reported via FEIN with a suitable purpose code; there is no obligation to convert into won, but the original foreign currency must be hosted onshore unless creditable against approved payments. Capital-account operations, including non-resident equity injections, offshore loans, and dividend remittances to foreign shareholders, are more controlled by FETA prior-signal or reporting rules. Profit repatriation is lawful only after audited financial statements are finalized, corporate income tax is settled at 10%-25% statutory rates (treaty relief applied to non-residents), and the FX bank validates the dividend payment under a tax-completion certificate and inward remittance confirmation. Therefore the system is best characterized as current-account liberal, capital-account managed, and surveillance-intensive: the corporation enjoys full earning conversion flexibility, but every cross-border flow must be documentary and bank-intermediated, minimizing the risk of unnoticed offshore treasury leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 15 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Enforcement Decree, proceeds from digital-game service exports must be collected within 6 months from the service performance/invoice date; the counterpart foreign-exchange bank may grant extensions up to 12 months on demonstration of contractual payment terms. No mandatory conversion ratio exists, meaning the Chusik Hoesa may retain up to 100% of foreign-currency ad revenue in an onshore foreign-currency account, but the receipt must be reported through FEIN and matched to the applicable service-export purpose code with supporting e-tax invoices, publisher payout statements, and the export-contract archive.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the statutory foreign-exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), with enforcement oversight by the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and transaction reporting captured through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury counterparty. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business do not hold a Korean banking license under the Banking Act nor a foreign-exchange dealer registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; therefore they cannot issue Korean-account-format KRW settlement facilities or represent a legal clearing rail for a resident Chusik Hoesa on standalone basis. They may be used only as supplementary wallet aggregators to receive USD/EUR AdMob or AppLovin payouts before sweeping into a licensed domestic foreign-exchange bank account, which remains mandatory for FEIN reporting, tax reconciliation, and capital controls. Domestic fintechs such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank offer genuine onshore corporate KRW accounts but their foreign-exchange and trade services are back-to-back delivered by licensed banks, thus providing no treasury advantage over the Tier-1 relationships and banning the EMI shortcut.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject capital by wiring USD, EUR, or JPY into the domestic counterpart foreign-exchange bank’s designated subscription-money account using the Korea Standard Foreign Exchange Transactions purpose classification for acquisition of domestically-issued equity securities; the bank issues a certificate of capital payment (납입증명서) enabling the incorporation filings at the court registry. Within 30 days of share acquisition by a foreign investor, the acquirer must file a report under FETA through the counterpart FX bank to obtain a foreign-investment registration certificate (외국인투자신고필증) when the transaction exceeds the notifiable threshold. All inbound AdMob and AppLovin payouts arrive as SWIFT MT103 credits into a designated USD or EUR foreign-currency account maintained at the lead domestic bank, classified under the BPM6 services purpose code for computer and information services, supported by the dynamic publisher agreements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary treasury counterparty. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business do not hold a Korean banking license under the Banking Act nor a foreign-exchange dealer registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; therefore they cannot issue Korean-account-format KRW settlement facilities or represent a legal clearing rail for a resident Chusik Hoesa on standalone basis. They may be used only as supplementary wallet aggregators to receive USD/EUR AdMob or AppLovin payouts before sweeping into a licensed domestic foreign-exchange bank account, which remains mandatory for FEIN reporting, tax reconciliation, and capital controls. Domestic fintechs such as KakaoBank and Toss Bank offer genuine onshore corporate KRW accounts but their foreign-exchange and trade services are back-to-back delivered by licensed banks, thus providing no treasury advantage over the Tier-1 relationships and banning the EMI shortcut.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, mandatory Local File and Master File documentation is required when the Korean Chusik Hoesa's international related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 5 billion during the fiscal year; the Master File is separately required when annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion. The documentation must be filed within 6 months after the fiscal year-end and contemporaneous with the tax return.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the 'actual place of effective management' test under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and the OECD Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) standard. A hyper-casual game publisher must show Korean-based employees performing game operations, ad monetization strategy, risk management, and strategic decision-making; having only a registered office with nominal share capital and no local activity will cause the Korean tax authorities to recharacterize the Chusik Hoesa as a shell or thin-capitalized intermediary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Revenue Suppression via Offshore Nominee Accounts

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 3 and 8 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, willful tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years (up to 5 years for aggravated repeated violations) or a fine not exceeding the evaded tax; the Korean NTS additionally imposes a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4, plus delinquency surcharges of up to 72% of the unpaid amount.

Director personal liability: Representative directors who sign falsified returns are not shielded by the corporate veil; under Article 399 of the Korean Commercial Act they are jointly and severally liable to compensate the company for tax losses caused by intentional misfeasance, and the NTS can attach directors' personal assets, impose a departure (travel) ban via the National Tax Collection Act, and bring criminal charges leading to imprisonment; directors of the funnel entity are also personally exposed to criminal fines and civil recovery of converted AdMob/AppLovin payouts.
Disguised Dividend Repatriation and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 15-2 of the International Tax Adjustment Act disallows interest deductions on excessive owner loans; the offending payment is recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 22% Korean withholding tax plus understatement penalties and interest; false invoices for royalties or interest may trigger the punitive 40% fraudulent-filing penalty; the tax authority may also apply the anti-avoidance rule to retroactively deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute bogus loan documents or licensing contracts face personal joint liability for the deficient taxes; willful deception can result in asset seizure, travel blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 5 years under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Because the corporate form is pierced where the loan structure has no independent arm's-length economic purpose, the foreign shareholder and Korean directors are jointly responsible for all restored profits and criminal restitution.
Shell Company Economic Substance Failure and Transfer Pricing Misallocation of Intangibles

Statutory sanction: The Korean NTS will invoke Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act to reallocate the profits to the Korean Chusik Hoesa, applying the arm's length principle and assessing the full corporate income tax differential plus a 10% unfaithful-return penalty (40% for fraud), daily delinquency surcharges, and local income tax surcharge; illegal tax avoidance acts may be criminally prosecuted under Article 3-6 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Where the offshore structure fails economic substance, the domestic directors are treated as having actively participated in the artificial disposal of assets; the Korean NTS will pierce the corporate veil to collect unpaid taxes from their personal estates, issue travel bans and asset attachment, and refer the case for prosecution. Directors may receive imprisonment terms of up to 5 years and fines equal to three times the tax evaded, in addition to permanent disqualification from serving as Korean company directors.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea: there is no statutory minimum capital, no court escrow requirement, and no blocked paid-in capital escrow account rule. The Articles of Incorporation must specify the share capital, and the full subscription amount must be paid into a domestic bank provisional account before the court registration application. For this pure-equity-holding SPV, the recommended paid-in capital is KRW 100,000 (approximately USD 75); this amount is deposited into a provisional bank account in the name of the company under formation and is not subject to any statutory lock-up or escrow arrangement beyond the normal bank practice of restricting withdrawal until incorporation registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no civil defense fire safety permit, structural load inspection, or workplace safety approval is triggered in the absence of physical premises, facilities, or employees. If the SPV subsequently establishes a representative office in Korea, the lessor must maintain the building fire-safety certification and the company must register the office with the district government tape; until that time no industrial-occupancy approval is required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 10,000,000 of prior-year taxable supplies ordinarily triggers mandatory registration; however, all corporations and foreign non-resident business operators making VATable supplies in Korea or maintaining a Korean domestic place of business are subject to registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, the 0% export zero rate applies to exported goods, services performed or consumed outside Korea, international transport services, and qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents for use outside Korea. The supplier must prove the actual cross-border movement or cross-border service supply and must settle the foreign currency export proceeds through a Korean foreign-exchange bank in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration filed via UNI-PASS)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill)
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by the Korean foreign-exchange settlement bank
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) showing actual receipt of overseas funds
  • Proof of Export Performance (export performance certificate issued by the settlement bank)
  • Export-related contracts/purchase orders if required by the NTS audit

Korea mandates electronic tax invoices for all business-to-business supplies. Tax invoices must be issued and transmitted to the NTS electronic tax invoice clearing system through the HomeTax portal or a certified ASP/API integration, no later than the day after the supply is made. There is no Latin American-style real-time fiscal pre-clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping; instead, the NTS uses mandatory digital transmission, cash-register-linked cash receipts, and file cross-audit for anti-fraud enforcement.

The NTS and the Korea Customs Service cross-check customs export declarations, shipping evidence, and foreign-exchange repatriation records. If the zero-rate claim is made without actual export, without the statutory document bundle, without repatriation within 180 days, or on the basis of forged or false documents, the NTS will recharacterize the supply at 10% VAT, assess the unpaid tax plus additional tax and interest, claw back any VAT refund or input VAT credit previously allowed, and impose penalties under the VAT Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent zero-rating may additionally result in criminal prosecution and fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but regulated foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The KRW is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlement, service payments, and ordinary dividends, subject to submission of underlying documentation to a foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into Korean entities, purchase of equity interests, and external borrowings, require mandatory declaration or pre-approval through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) or the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) for FDI. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds, but exporters are obligated to receive payment within a statutory collection period, and all proceeds must be repatriated to and deposited with a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank. For a pure equity holding SPV, the primary FX risk arises from dividend outflows, capital reduction proceeds, and intercompany loan repayments, all of which are permitted under the current account or capital account frameworks provided that the payer submits valid tax invoices, board resolutions, and prior notifications. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is not subject to a quota, but the remittance must be supported by audited financial statements, tax withholding certificates, and an authorized foreign exchange bank's due diligence. The MOEF and BOK retain powers to impose temporary restrictions during severe external imbalances, yet the current regime is classified as open for OECD countries with limited administrative approvals. Practically, foreign investors must appoint a Korean bank as a designated foreign exchange business entity to effect conversions and monitor real-name transactions, and non-resident holding companies are expected to maintain a domestic corporate bank account for all funding flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA Article 5-4 and related enforcement decrees, an exporter must collect the full value of exported goods or services within six months from the date of the export declaration or performance of service, unless an extension has been granted by the foreign exchange bank or the MOEF. There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain balances in foreign currency-denominated deposit accounts at authorized banks. The obligation is one of timely inward repatriation to a registered Korean foreign exchange bank account, with documentary evidence of the underlying trade transaction (invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration) kept on file for five years. For services and digital content exports, the 6-month rule applies from the issuance of the tax invoice or completion of service, and the proceeds must be received in an account specified to the tax authorities. Failure to meet the collection deadline triggers a compliance audit and potential administrative penalty of up to 50 million KRW, as well as automatic review of the entity's export license status.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and subordinate regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

The legal viability of offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise, or other independent electronic money institutions) for serving a Korean Chusik Hoesa is Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only domestic banks authorised by the Bank of Korea and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) may carry out foreign exchange brokerage, settlement, and deposit-taking activities with corporate entities located in Korea. Offshore EMIs without a Korean banking license cannot open local bank accounts, cannot provide KRW settlement rails, and are barred from processing dividend remittances or dividend conversions on behalf of Korean-incorporated entities. Any use of a non-licensed EMI for corporate treasury flows would constitute an unregistered foreign exchange business under Article 8 of FETA, exposing the SPV to sanctions, cancellation of its foreign exchange declaration, and criminal liability. Even a fully licensed fintech in another Asian jurisdiction cannot serve as the primary clearing bank for a Korean HoldCo because inward FDI notifications are tied to a specific designated bank, and the Bank of Korea's real-time gross settlement (RTGS) requires a domestic Bank Identification Code. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 Korean commercial banks for account operations, while non-bank fintechs may be used only for ancillary payment tokenisation if integrated through a licensed bank as a technical service provider, never as a custodial or settlement counter-party.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: The legal viability of offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise, or other independent electronic money institutions) for serving a Korean Chusik Hoesa is Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only domestic banks authorised by the Bank of Korea and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) may carry out foreign exchange brokerage, settlement, and deposit-taking activities with corporate entities located in Korea. Offshore EMIs without a Korean banking license cannot open local bank accounts, cannot provide KRW settlement rails, and are barred from processing dividend remittances or dividend conversions on behalf of Korean-incorporated entities. Any use of a non-licensed EMI for corporate treasury flows would constitute an unregistered foreign exchange business under Article 8 of FETA, exposing the SPV to sanctions, cancellation of its foreign exchange declaration, and criminal liability. Even a fully licensed fintech in another Asian jurisdiction cannot serve as the primary clearing bank for a Korean HoldCo because inward FDI notifications are tied to a specific designated bank, and the Bank of Korea's real-time gross settlement (RTGS) requires a domestic Bank Identification Code. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 Korean commercial banks for account operations, while non-bank fintechs may be used only for ancillary payment tokenisation if integrated through a licensed bank as a technical service provider, never as a custodial or settlement counter-party.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% top statutory rate, plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a 27.5% combined top effective Korean corporate tax burden

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Outbound dividends paid by a Korean corporation are subject to a 20% corporate withholding tax plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a 22% effective statutory withholding burden under Corporate Tax Act Article 98(1); an applicable double tax treaty may reduce the total effective dividend withholding burden to 5% for direct qualified owners or 10% to 15% for ordinary portfolio holders.

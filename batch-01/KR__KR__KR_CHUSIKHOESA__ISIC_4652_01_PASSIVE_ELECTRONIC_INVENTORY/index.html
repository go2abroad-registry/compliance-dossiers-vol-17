Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic tax portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); customs clearance and export declaration portal: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr)

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign non-resident entity, the statutory route to a Korean TIN/VAT ID is to apply for a Business Operator Registration Certificate with NTS. If the foreign corporation has a Korean domestic place of business (branch/PE), file the business registration application with the competent District Tax Office, providing: (a) foreign certificate of incorporation and articles; (b) proof of Korean place of business (lease/contract); (c) Power of Attorney designating a Korean tax agent; and (d) bank account details and evidence of the expected Korean taxable activity. If the foreign entity has no fixed place of business but supplies services/goods to Korean customers, it must appoint a VAT tax representative and file the same application through the NTS international/HomeTax channel; NTS then issues a 10-digit Korean Business Registration Number serving as both the TIN and VAT ID. A domestic Chusik Hoesa is registered with the District Tax Office upon incorporation, and no separate non-resident registration is required for the entity itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No turnover threshold for a Chusik Hoesa: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Korea. A KRW 30,000,000 simplified taxable-person threshold applies only to qualifying individual entrepreneurs; foreign non-resident suppliers with a Korean taxable nexus register directly and do not receive a corporation tax registration threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating under Article 20 of the Value-Added Tax Act applies to exported goods physically delivered from Korea to a foreign buyer, and to qualifying services rendered or used outside Korea. A Korean Chusik Hoesa distributing multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors may claim 0% VAT if it can prove actual export, foreign title/risk transfer, and foreign currency consideration.

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all VATable B2B supplies. Taxpayers must issue an electronic tax invoice (ETI) and transmit the ETI data to the NTS HomeTax system by the day following the issue date; the NTS accepts XML/JSON API transmissions and validates the invoice in its clearing database. Korea does not currently require a pre-approval anti-fraud digital stamp on ordinary invoices, but the electronic invoice file must contain the zero-rating indicator for exports, and failure to transmit an ETI is subject to late/non-reporting penalties.

      
NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit zero-rating claims for export transactions. If the required customs declaration, shipping document, or foreign exchange receipt is missing, inconsistent, or fabricated, the transaction is recharacterized as taxable and NTS assesses 10% VAT plus interest and statutory addition taxes. Fraudulent claims are subject to criminal liability and penal additions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with potential imprisonment and fines proportional to the evaded tax, and retroactive clawback of any VAT refund previously paid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates a liberalised but notification-heavy exchange regime. The current account is effectively convertible for bona fide trade and profit remittances; foreign exchange sales and purchases can be executed at market rates through licensed foreign exchange banks without central approval. The capital account is also materially open, particularly for foreign direct investment, but requires ex-ante notification to MOEF through a designated foreign exchange bank, registration of foreign-invested enterprise details, and periodic transaction reporting. A Korean Chusik Hoesa importing or distributing passive electronic inventory must classify all cross-border payments under the correct officially issued purpose code, maintain underlying trade contracts and customs declarations for a minimum of five years, and ensure any KRW settlement with non-residents is routed through a domestic authorised bank. Profit remittance such as dividends is permissible only after corporate tax returns are filed and tax clearance is demonstrated; the remitting bank will require a tax payment certificate or no-objection proof if outstanding tax arrears exist. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds, but all export receipts must be collected through a domestic foreign exchange bank. The KRW is therefore best described as conditionally convertible: current account flows are free, while capital account flows are legal but subject to a web of filing, evidence, and anti-money laundering checks. Any suspicion of trade-based value transfer or round-tripping can trigger a full statutory audit and temporary remittance freeze.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and relevant Declarations on External Transactions, export proceeds from passive electronic component sales must be received through a Korean foreign exchange bank within 6 months from the date of export clearance; deferred payment terms can extend the collection period up to 36 months. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring export receipts to be exchanged into KRW; the exporter may retain funds in a foreign-currency onshore account or convert to KRW at prevailing commercial bank rates. Each receipt, conversion, or onward remittance must be reported to the receiving foreign exchange bank under the correct trade purpose code, with supporting invoices, bills of lading, and customs export declarations retained for compliance inspection.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Operations Committee under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low. Offshore digital EMI services without a Korean banking licence are legally barred from holding KRW current accounts or executing domestic payment instructions. Licensing under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act is required for payment institutions serving Korean entities, while Toss Bank and KakaoBank are domestic internet banks but their corporate cross-border foreign exchange accounts remain limited and typically do not offer dedicated global cash management for a passive-electronics distribution business. For a standard Chusik Hoesa treasury structure, a Tier-1 commercial bank branch in Seoul with an established SWIFT and trade finance desk remains the mandatory primary onboarding route; EMIs can at most serve secondary treasury visibility or reconciliation, not as a substitute for legal clearing rails.

Defense evidence: Full ownership registry of each shareholder, an unbroken beneficial ownership declaration chain naming all ultimate natural persons, recently certified certificates of incorporation for each intermediate entity, audited financial statements for the direct foreign parent, and officially endorsed passports of all natural-person beneficial owners.

      

      
      

        Non-resident directors or remote management layers create uncertainty over control and actual operational substance

Defense evidence: Notarised and apostilled board resolution appointing local authorized signatories, signed minutes of the founding shareholders meeting, detailed Czech-style corporate governance minutes, original certificate of corporate seal registration, and a Korean licensed attorney legal opinion confirming the entity is actively trading in electronic component distribution.

Defense evidence: Source-of-wealth statement from the funding shareholder, three months of bank statements from the remitting account, corporate balance sheet showing availability of funds, signed capital contribution resolution, proof of foreign direct investment notification to MOEF, and a Korean certified public accountant capital verification report.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
24% national corporate income tax rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% resident surcharge (local income tax surtax), producing a combined top marginal rate of 26.4%; the lower progressive brackets are 9% for income up to KRW 200 million, 19% for income from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 21% for income from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
22% on gross dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder (20% national withholding income tax plus 10% resident surtax computed on that 20% withholding tax), unless the applicable bilateral income tax treaty reduces the rate; treaty reduction commonly requires that the non-resident shareholder be the beneficial owner and, for the reduced corporate-shareholder rate, hold at least 5% to 25% of the Korean company's capital according to the particular DTA

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion, being the annual aggregate amount of cross-border related-party transactions that triggers mandatory preparation and retention of the Korean Local File; the Korean Master File is mandatory when the annual aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 50 billion; the Country-by-Country Report applies to a Korean ultimate parent of an MNE group whose consolidated revenue satisfies the statutory threshold (currently approximately KRW 1.5 trillion, the Korean equivalent of the OECD EUR 750 million threshold)

      
Economic substance scope: South Korea does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation or a separate Core Income-Generating Activities filing; however, Korean tax law incorporates OECD BEPS minimum standards and general anti-avoidance rules, including the substance-over-form doctrine, the place-of-effective-management concept, beneficial ownership requirements, and the requirement that economic substance support each cross-border transaction; for the passive electronic components distribution entity, the NTS will test whether the Korean Chusik Hoesa performs purchasing, inventory receiving, warehousing, quality control, sales negotiations, credit risk, customer invoicing, and logistics decision-making; without these core income-generating functions performed by Korean-resident employees, the Korean entity cannot earn the arm's-length distribution profit and may be disregarded as a conduit or recharacterized as paying a constructive dividend

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Sham and Artificial Profit Shifting
        
Statutory sanction: Under Articles 4, 6, and 8 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the NTS will disregard the non-arm's-length prices, re-determine the Korean taxable income by applying the most appropriate arm's-length method, and impose transfer pricing adjustments; the Framework Act on National Taxes adds an underreporting penalty of 10% of the understated tax or 40% where the distortion is fraudulent, plus the 0.022% daily late-payment surcharge; the NTS may also impose an additional 20% additional settlement tax on the adjusted amount, reject all related documentation, and assess tax by estimation if the taxpayer fails to produce its transfer pricing records

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Commercial Act Articles 389 and 401 where the corporation is a mere facade and the transfer-pricing structure is an abuse of limited liability; the representative director and de facto directors are jointly and severally liable without limit for the Korean corporation's tax deficiency and creditors' damages; directors who approve the inflated inventory invoices or signing documents with intent to evade tax are subject to imprisonment of up to 2 years under the Act on Punishment of Tax Evaders, a fine of up to twice the tax evaded, and, if the unpaid tax exceeds the delinquency threshold, an NTS-issued international travel ban and departure block

      

      
      

        Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Dividend, Interest, or Royalty Tax and Fraudulent DTA Relief
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act Articles 156 through 158 and the Framework Act on National Taxes, the withholding agent is personally liable for the unwithheld tax amount, and the NTS will collect from the Korean payer the full national tax and local surcharge together with a 10% to 40% penalty depending on intent and a 0.022% daily late-payment surcharge; fraudulent treaty forms are treated as false documentation under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, attracting a fine of up to twice the evaded tax and criminal prosecution; the reduced treaty withholding may be reversed for all past open fiscal years and the NTS may apply annual reassessment records for up to 10 years

        
Director personal liability: A director who causes or permits the omission of withholding is jointly and severally liable with the corporation under Article 22(1) of the National Tax Collection Act; if the corporate veil is abused solely to strip Korean profits, the Commercial Act Article 401 and Article 401-2 de facto-director doctrine subject the directors' personal assets to restitution and compensatory damages; the criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 2 years, and the unpaid withholding amount can trigger a certificate of unpaid taxes resulting in a travel blacklist, asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment for intentional fraudulent non-repatriation of tax

      

      
      

        Shell Company Abuse and Trade-Based Money Laundering
        
Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny all related expense deductions and treat the disguised funds as constructive dividends subject to 22% withholding; the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes fines and imprisonment for unapproved capital transactions through unauthorized settlement channels; the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information penalizes failure to report suspicious transactions with imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to KRW 50 million; the Commercial Act and the Act on Punishment of Tax Evaders allow the tax authorities to set aside the limited-liability structure and recover the tax directly from the persons in actual control

        
Director personal liability: Because the corporation is the alter ego of its non-resident principals, the court may pierce the corporate veil, making directors personally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, withholding taxes, and creditor claims; directors who sign false invoices, route wire transfers, or open and operate the credit line for concealment are personally blacklisted by the NTS, subjected to a travel prohibition, liable to civil restitution of the disguised dividend amounts, and face criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years together with confiscation of the proceeds derived from the sham electronic-parts trading scheme

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic filing through the HomeTax system (www.hometax.go.kr); cross-border goods clearance is managed separately by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

Because the target is a domestic Korean Chusik Hoesa, the entity is a Korean resident taxpayer rather than a foreign non-resident. The mandatory procedure is: (1) incorporate the Chusik Hoesa with the Korean court registry; (2) register with the district tax office as a business operator, filing the Business Registration Application (Saupja Deungrok Shincheong) via HomeTax or in person within 20 days from the commencement of business, accompanied by the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, evidence of virtual-office mailing address, and applicant identity documents; (3) receive the 10-digit Korean Business Registration Number, which serves as the TIN and VAT registration ID for all Korean tax purposes; (4) obtain the e-tax invoice issuance certification and tax invoice certificate. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business generally does not obtain a Korean Business Registration Number solely for own offshore activities, but a foreign electronic service supplier providing electronic services to Korean consumers is required to complete a simplified VAT registration through HomeTax and report VAT on that basis.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

None. Korea does not have a check-the-box or hybrid entity election mechanism for domestic stock corporations. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 / no general turnover threshold. Every business operator with a Korean domestic place of business must register for VAT before commencing business, regardless of expected turnover. A foreign electronic service supplier without a Korean place of business must also register if it supplies electronic services to Korean residents; a business with only zero-rated export turnover is still required to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are eligible for 0% VAT when they are physically exported from Korea under an export contract, the export customs declaration is accepted by Korea Customs, the goods are delivered to a foreign destination or foreign free trade zone, and the exporter receives the export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory repatriation period. Services, including qualifying digital content and offshore services, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident or used entirely outside Korea; the Korean business must retain the service contract, performance record, customer non-resident confirmation, payment evidence, and a zero-rated tax invoice. The zero-rating is substantiated through a zero-rated VAT invoice and the export documentation bundle rather than a separate refund permit. A precondition for the VAT refund claim is that the corresponding input VAT relates to the zero-rated export supply and the full export documentation set is produced during NTS audit.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Form (UNI-PASS electronic filing)
  • Certificate of Export Declaration issued by Korea Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice marked as zero-rated export
  • Bank Certificate of Receipt of Export Proceeds / Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Packing list where required by the commodity
  • VAT zero-rated tax invoice

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory. The taxpayer must issue a Korean e-tax invoice through the NTS HomeTax system, transmit the invoice data to the NTS through the approved API/web service, and preserve the issue numbers for reconciliation. The NTS performs automated database matching between e-tax invoices, VAT returns, customs export declarations, and foreign-currency remittance records. Korea does not apply a physical fiscal stamping or anti-fraud clearance device for virtual-office and mail-scanning services; the anti-fraud control is achieved through the centralised HomeTax e-invoice repository and audit matching. Failure to issue, transmit, file, or report an e-tax invoice within the statutory deadlines exposes the business operator to surcharges and penalty taxes under the VAT Act and the Framework Act for National Taxes.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service maintain a joint anti-fraud review of zero-rated exports. If an exporter fails to substantiate physical export, fails to repatriate the export proceeds in foreign currency within the 180-day statutory deadline, or submits incomplete or incongruent documentation, the zero-rating is retrospectively withdrawn, the unpaid 10% output VAT is reassessed, interest accrues under the Framework Act for National Taxes, and additional penalty taxes for unfaithful or late filing under the VAT Act are imposed. Fraudulent declarations, including circular trading, false invoices, or fictitious customs declarations, additionally expose the taxpayer to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act and to Foreign Exchange Transactions Act sanctions for non-repatriation of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Korea operates a liberalized but regulated foreign exchange system. Current account transactions are generally free, and there is no general prohibition on the conversion of foreign currency into KRW or on the remittance of trading profits. However, Korea is not a full-liberalization jurisdiction: capital account transactions are subject to reporting, registration, or licensed-bank intermediation under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A foreign shareholder may inject capital into a Chusik Hoesa and receive share-consideration transfers through an authorized foreign exchange bank. That bank must verify the inward remittance, settle it into KRW at a referenced market rate, and issue a foreign investment certificate. Export proceeds are not mandatorily surrendered to the Bank of Korea, but they must be received and deposited through an authorized foreign exchange bank within the statutory collection time; exporters may keep balances in foreign currency accounts. Profit remittances such as dividends are permitted after corporate tax filing and payment: the remitting bank requires audited financial statements, tax payment confirmation, a board dividend resolution, and tax clearance from the National Tax Service. Willful delay or failure to report a capital-account transaction creates penalty risk. Overall, Korea allows full convertibility for genuine business flows but requires bank rails, documentary proof, and timely reporting to satisfy the AML/CFT and prudential framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of the underlying export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no compulsory surrender of proceeds to the Central Bank; exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts. If collection is not achieved within the one-year statutory period, the exporter must submit a written explanation and extension request through the authorized bank. For ISIC 8299 digital administration and mail scanning services, the same one-year collection rule applies; the bank will require either the export declaration (for goods) or a signed service invoice, the cross-border contract, and evidence of service delivery as the collection basis.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority, administers Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Authorized domestic commercial banks are delegated as foreign exchange banks for settlement, verification and reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Cross-border fintech modular banks such as digital EMI providers are not licensed commercial banks in Korea and cannot independently issue KRW-denominated account numbers or originate domestic payments. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires foreign exchange business to be conducted through authorized foreign exchange banks. A Chusik Hoesa may use an EMI only as an ancillary payout wallet for overseas contractors or platform payouts, but the core KRW treasury, card settlement, and FX conversion functions must remain at a Tier-1 Korean bank. Relying on an unlicensed EMI for inbound capital or trade settlement would trigger automatic enhanced due diligence, possible rejection of the remittance, and regulatory reporting failures. Therefore the treasury blueprint should not recommend a fintech EMI as the primary operating account for this virtual-office mail scanning entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must register the investment as a foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before transfer. The investor files a Foreign Investment Report with Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or the designated authorized foreign exchange bank. The inbound wire should be a SWIFT MT103 naming the Chusik Hoesa beneficiary account, with the purpose field clearly stating 'Direct investment – subscription of new shares' and referencing the Foreign Investment Report number. On receipt, the Korean bank credits a foreign currency temporary account, converts to KRW at the bank's customer reference rate, deposits the KRW proceeds into the entity's basic operational account, and issues the Certificate of Foreign Investment. Capital verification is completed through the investment certificate, the SWIFT tracking message, the conversion advice, the issued share certificate in the company's registry, and a capital verification report prepared by an external auditor evidencing that shares were validly issued and fully paid.Clearing rails must match the commercial reality of the ISIC 8299 virtual-office and digital-scanning business, which is primarily a service export rather than a physical goods trade. Commercial letters of credit are acceptable only for back-to-back transactions involving hard-copy scanning hardware or outsourced document-processing supplies. Documentary collections may be used when a Korean entity is paying an overseas vendor after inspection of delivery; however, because the business model is digital and remote, the bank will not expect standard shipping documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Cross-border fintech modular banks such as digital EMI providers are not licensed commercial banks in Korea and cannot independently issue KRW-denominated account numbers or originate domestic payments. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires foreign exchange business to be conducted through authorized foreign exchange banks. A Chusik Hoesa may use an EMI only as an ancillary payout wallet for overseas contractors or platform payouts, but the core KRW treasury, card settlement, and FX conversion functions must remain at a Tier-1 Korean bank. Relying on an unlicensed EMI for inbound capital or trade settlement would trigger automatic enhanced due diligence, possible rejection of the remittance, and regulatory reporting failures. Therefore the treasury blueprint should not recommend a fintech EMI as the primary operating account for this virtual-office mail scanning entity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal statutory corporate tax rate under the progressive Corporate Tax Act rates (9% up to KRW 200M; 19% from KRW 200M to KRW 20B; 21% from KRW 20B to KRW 300B; 24% above KRW 300B). A 10% local income tax surtax is imposed on the corporate tax itself, producing a top combined effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory rate on gross dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder: 20% corporate tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax. Under applicable Double Tax Agreements the rate may be reduced to 15%, 10%, 5%, or 0% depending on ownership percentage, holding period, and beneficial ownership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: aggregate cross-border related-party transactions reaching or exceeding KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year. Master File: aggregate cross-border related-party transactions reaching or exceeding KRW 50 billion in the same fiscal year. Country-by-Country Report: Korean group annual consolidated turnover equal to or greater than KRW 1 trillion, subject to the International Tax Adjustment Act schedules.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate under the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations nomenclature, but the National Tax Service rigorously applies the OECD substance-over-form doctrine, the Principal Purpose Test, and Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Chusik Hoesa acting as a virtual-office and mail-scanning provider must substantiate physical office existence, actual employees carrying out scanning and client services, board meetings, bank accounts, business records, binding client contracts, and real decision-making substance. Absent genuine economic substance, the NTS may disregard the entity, attribute the fee income to the real economic owner, deny treaty benefits, constrain PE exemption claims, and impose transfer-pricing penalties on intercompany mail-scanning charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders and Tax Fraud Act, imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding three times the tax evaded may be imposed. Civil reassessment adds an understatement penalty of 20%-40% of the omitted tax, daily late-payment interest, and possible cancellation of the licensed commercial mail-receiving agency permit.

Director personal liability: The representative director and any director or financial officer who planned or benefited from the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax deficiency, are subject to departure restriction orders and travel bans, face criminal indictment, confiscation of assets traceable to the diverted mail-scanning receipts, and civil restitution to the National Tax Service.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS will recharacterize the corporate vehicle as a sham, attribute all Korean-source income to the entity that actually performs the business functions and economic substance, deny treaty withholding rate reductions under the Principal Purpose Test, bind the taxpayer to the full 22% gross withholding or 26.4% top combined corporate tax rate, and add negligence and fraud penalties plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the sham structure may be personally assessed for the unremitted corporate and withholding tax, subjected to travel bans, barred from leaving Korea until the deficiency is secured, held civilly liable for restitution of the amount diverted under the corporate veil, and criminally prosecuted for aiding tax evasion.
AML and Banking Compliance Infraction

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Financial Transactions Reports Act and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit guidance may be sanctioned by criminal imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to KRW 50,000,000, forfeiture and freezing of the mail-scanning revenue, cancellation of the merchant acquiring agreement, and referral to the prosecution service. Enhanced banking scrutiny attaches to virtual-office CMRA businesses classified as Medium-Risk Enhanced.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer, representative director, and beneficial owner found to have knowingly processed tainted funds are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, personal fines, confiscation of assets, travel bans, disgorgement of laundering profits, and disqualification from operating a licensed mail-receiving agency.
Withholding Tax and Transfer Pricing Failure

Statutory sanction: Korean law makes the paying corporation the primary obligor for unwithheld tax and imposes a 10% withholding negligence penalty plus daily interest. Transfer pricing adjustments are made by the NTS under CUP, resale price, cost-plus, TNMM, or profit split analysis; a transfer pricing surcharge of up to 20% of the adjusted amount may be imposed, and non-submission of documentation attracts separate penalties up to KRW 100,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the officer responsible for tax compliance become personally liable for the unremitted withholding amount, are subject to civil restitution claims, may be denied exit from Korea during NTS collection proceedings, and can be indicted and imprisoned for up to 5 years for intentionally causing the corporation to violate withholding or reporting duties.

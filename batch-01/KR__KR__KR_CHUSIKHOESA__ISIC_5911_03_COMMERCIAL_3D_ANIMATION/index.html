Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None: the Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up share capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, all issued share subscription money must be fully paid into a blocked subscription deposit account at a Korean financial institution before submission of the incorporation registration application. The deposited funds remain blocked until the court registry issues the Certificate of Registered Matters. For non-resident shareholders, the remittance into Korea must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and kept traceable as investment money; the deposit receipt from the bank is a mandatory registry exhibit.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — no Civil Defense fire permit, structural load clearance, or factory workplace safety approval is required because there is no industrial plant or warehouse. An ordinary office lease remains subject to general fire safety compliance under the Fire Services Act, including extinguishers, emergency exits, and fire alarms where required by building floor area, but this is not an industrial civil defense permit. Occupational safety obligations under the Occupational Safety and Health Act apply only to the ordinary office environment and only when hiring employees, with no special operating license triggered for digital production.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: Home Tax Service (HTS) at hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed Korean Chusik Hoesa, the statutory TIN is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN). The representative director must submit a business registration application to the competent district NTS office within 20 days of the commencement of business, attaching the court-registered certificate of incorporation from the commercial registry, the articles of incorporation, identity documents of the directors, and proof of the registered office address. The NTS then issues the BRN certificate. For a foreign non-resident entity that has Korean VAT or withholding obligations but no domestic place of business, the entity must appoint a Korean tax representative (a resident individual or a domestic corporation) and file a simplified non-resident business registration through HTS or the competent tax office. Supporting documents include the home-country certificate of incorporation, apostilled or consular-legalized corporate documents, a board resolution appointing the tax representative, a power of attorney, and a statement describing the Korean-source supplies. For foreign suppliers of electronic services, the simplified e-service registration module of HTS must be used; after verification, the NTS assigns a 10-digit BRN and an electronic tax invoice issuance account. The tax representative is jointly responsible for registration, filing, and payment unless a physical Korean establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Korean Chusik Hoesa is classified as a domestic corporation taxable under the Corporate Tax Act on its worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Korean-source income of a foreign non-resident owner remains separately subject to dividend withholding tax under Korean domestic law and applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

None - Korean tax law does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. A Chusik Hoesa is per se an entity-level taxpayer. The only available relevant elections are (i) group consolidated corporate tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act for a domestic parent and 100-percent-owned domestic subsidiaries, and (ii) tax-neutral reorganization elections under the Corporate Tax Act and the Tax Incentive Limitation Act for qualifying mergers, divisions, and asset transfers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Korea (effectively KRW 0). Foreign non-resident electronic-service suppliers are subject to simplified registration through the Home Tax Service (HTS) portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT zero-rating is available for: (i) goods exported outside Korea and duly cleared by Korea Customs Service; (ii) services rendered or supplies made in a foreign country; (iii) foreign exchange-earning services, including commercial animation/VFX production and licensing of copyrights or characters to an overseas client, when the place of supply is outside Korea and the remuneration is paid in foreign currency; and (iv) services supplied for ships or aircraft in international navigation under the VAT Act. The taxpayer must prove actual delivery or performance outside Korea, collection of export proceeds in foreign currency, and record the transaction in the VAT export return. Qualifying zero-rated supplies generate input VAT credits and refunds through the periodic VAT return.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Certificate (Export Customs Clearance Certificate) for physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and detailed service contract / statement of work describing the animation or VFX deliverables
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for the physical movement of media or assets
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Korean authorized foreign-exchange bank proving foreign-currency receipt of the export purchase price
  • Foreign Currency Revenue Ledger and bank remittance advices evidencing collection within the statutory repatriation deadline
  • Digital delivery records, access logs, transfer logs, or overseas client acceptance/completion certificate for intangible or electronically delivered animation/VFX content

Every VAT-registered business must issue a Korean Electronic Tax Invoice (ETI) through HTS or an NTS-certified e-invoicing solution for taxable supplies. The ETI must be transmitted to HTS within one business day after issuance. The system assigns a unique tax invoice number and performs automated cross-matching with monthly VAT returns, customs declaration data, and cash receipt records; this is an anti-fraud e-invoice clearance and matching regime rather than a fiscal digital-stamping model. Non-issuance, late issuance, or issuance of a false ETI triggers additional tax penalties of up to 2% of the supply value plus statutory surcharges.

Export zero-rating is subject to NTS audit cross-checks against Korea Customs Service export declarations, HTS e-invoice records, foreign-exchange settlement records, and the taxpayer's books. If documentation is missing, the 0% rate is reversed and 10% VAT is assessed on the supply, together with additional tax for unfaithful returns (10% of the underreported or reclaimed tax, and 40% for fraudulent returns), plus monthly overdue interest. Proceeds not received within 180 days of export are treated as uncollected and the zero-rating is clawed back unless a banking extension is approved. Deliberate fake exports or false zero-rating claims may also be criminally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, resulting in imprisonment and fines up to the statutory limits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, South Korea maintains a comprehensively liberalized current account and a predominantly open capital account, yet all cross-border movements of funds must be handled indirectly through authorized domestic foreign exchange banks and disclosed through the Bank of Korea reporting system. The Korean won is freely convertible at onshore market rates for legitimate trade, investment, and financial transactions, but KRW is not a physically deliverable international currency outside approved regulated channels. Export proceeds are not subject to any mandatory surrender or conversion ratio; exporters may retain foreign currency balances in designated foreign exchange accounts or convert them as needed. Profit remittance hurdles for a fully compliant Chusik Hoesa are limited to corporate formalities: payment of corporate tax and withholding taxes where applicable, audited financial statements demonstrating distributable profits, a board of directors resolution declaring dividends, and a routine foreign exchange report filed with the receiving authorized bank. For a foreign-invested entity that completes the Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration, dividend and liquidation proceeds can be remitted freely once the capital verification and periodic tax filings are reconciled with the bank. The key practical obstacle is not convertibility but documentary completeness; banks require the full underlying corporate and transaction trail for every cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current foreign exchange transaction regulations, an exporter must receive export proceeds in foreign currency through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank within the statutory due date, generally within six months from the date of export declaration, unless an extension is approved by the bank or authorities for valid commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender obligation; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a foreign currency current account, convert to Korean won at the prevailing market rate, or utilize the funds for permitted trade-related payments. Completed export proceeds must be reported for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and supporting invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and settlement documents should be preserved for at least five years to satisfy auditing and banking review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), operating with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the responsible policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Negligible direct viability for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. South Korea requires any electronic payment or money transfer service provider to be authorized or registered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and cross-border remittance must be originated or received through a domestic foreign exchange bank. An offshore EMI such as a non-bank fintech cannot operate as a standalone corporate settlement account without a domestic banking license. It may be used only as a supplementary receivable channel where it routes funds through an authorized Korean bank, maintains transparent beneficiary disclosure, and is not the primary treasury rail. Any treasury structure that leaves the Chusik Hoesa reliant on an unlicensed EMI will create immediate AML, FX reporting, and tax audit exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must submit the Foreign Investment Report through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before capital transmission. The remittance should be executed via SWIFT MT103 to the bank-designated foreign investment custody account, clearly stating the Korean entity legal name, business registration number, foreign investment declaration number, and capital purpose code corresponding to direct investment. Upon final collection, the bank converts the foreign currency into Korean won and credits the Chusik Hoesa's operational KRW account. The bank's foreign currency receipt confirmation, the exchange conversion slip, and a capital verification certification signed by an eligible Korean certified public accountant must be retained in the statutory accounting archive. Any shareholder loan or intra-group advance should be separately declared as intra-company financing under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations and supported by a pre-executed loan agreement with market-conforming maturity and interest.For high-value international subcontracting in visual effects and animation, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with full underlying contract reference, because it provides direct, irrevocable, and traceable settlement. Commercial letters of credit are acceptable when the debtor needs bank payment assurance, especially for larger licensing deals or co-production milestones, but require exact compliance with UCP600 documentation. Documentary collections are appropriate for routine supplier payments at lower risk where the creditor accepts delayed settlement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Negligible direct viability for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. South Korea requires any electronic payment or money transfer service provider to be authorized or registered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and cross-border remittance must be originated or received through a domestic foreign exchange bank. An offshore EMI such as a non-bank fintech cannot operate as a standalone corporate settlement account without a domestic banking license. It may be used only as a supplementary receivable channel where it routes funds through an authorized Korean bank, maintains transparent beneficiary disclosure, and is not the primary treasury rail. Any treasury structure that leaves the Chusik Hoesa reliant on an unlicensed EMI will create immediate AML, FX reporting, and tax audit exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate under Corporate Tax Act Article 55: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on KRW 200 million to 20 billion, 21% on KRW 20 billion to 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% Korean national withholding tax plus 10% local surtax on that national tax, resulting in a 22% aggregate statutory withholding rate; DTA may reduce the rate to 5%, 10%, or 15% depending on the recipient and shareholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in annual related-party international transactions triggers the Local File; KRW 100 billion group revenue combined with KRW 5 billion related-party transactions triggers the Master File; consolidated group revenue of KRW 1 trillion triggers Country-by-Country Reporting.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Korean General Anti-Abuse Rule, substance-over-form jurisprudence, and the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test require economic activity and business purpose; NTS will disregard a conduit or letter-box company lacking employees, premises, decision-making functions, and core income-generating activities, and will deny treaty benefits where the structure is artificial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Beneficial Ownership / Treaty Shopping / Letter-Box Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under Article 17 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test; recharacterization of the intermediary as a sham; additional tax of up to 20% of the understated withholding tax plus 0.025% daily late payment surcharge; the payer is jointly held to the unpaid withholding obligation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized sham documentation are personally liable for the corporation's unpaid withholding tax under the National Tax Collection Act; personal assets may be seized; a travel ban and NTS blacklisting apply; criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment for up to five years for intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Fraud and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: NTS redetermines taxable income under the arm's length standard in the International Tax Adjustment Act and disallows excessive interest under thin-capitalization rules; assessment plus an unfaithful-return penalty of up to 20% of omitted tax, daily interest, and separate penalty for non-production of transfer-pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: If the corporation cannot pay, directors who directed false transfer-pricing data are secondarily liable for the assessed tax; personal real estate may be attached; overseas travel restriction is imposed; criminal indictment for false document creation and tax evasion may carry imprisonment for up to five years.
Unreported Offshore Profit Repatriation / Foreign Exchange Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, unauthorized transfer of Korean profits abroad can attract imprisonment for up to five years or a fine up to five times the amount involved; the National Tax Service imposes a 20% understatement penalty and unpaid withholding on unreported dividends, plus statutory interest.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil because the offshore account was used as an alter ego; directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution to creditors and NTS; passport revocation, travel ban, asset freezing, confiscation of hidden funds, and imprisonment for up to five years for tax evasion apply.

Korean tax and banking authorities are generally hostile to virtual offices or commercial mailboxes for an e-commerce retail company selling pet supplies and pet care devices. Although the Korean Commercial Act focuses on a registered address for service of process, the National Tax Service requires an actual workplace, operational supervision, physical business records, tax invoices, delivery logistics, inventory control, and a person with authority present during normal business hours. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online administration via the Home Tax Service (Hometax) portal at www.hometax.go.kr. Customs export declarations are filed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal at unipass.customs.go.kr, with foreign-exchange settlement documented through a registered Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity forming or operating a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the local entity must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number from the competent District Tax Office under the NTS. The Korean representative or an authorised tax agent submits the incorporation registration certificate, articles of incorporation, board resolution appointing the Korean representative, power of attorney, identity documentation, and, for foreign-source documents, apostilled or consular-legalised copies with certified Korean translations. The application is filed through Hometax or at the district tax office, and the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number that simultaneously serves as the Korean TIN and VAT registration identity. Where a non-resident foreign entity has no Korean permanent establishment but is registered under the simplified VAT regime for foreign suppliers of electronic services, the NTS issues the simplified Korean VAT registration number through Hometax for filing of quarterly VAT returns. Any subsequent change of registered business address, trade name, representative, or corporate status must be filed through Hometax within the statutory notification period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime for Chusik Hoesa; resident corporations are automatically subject to entity-level Korean corporate income tax. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary exemption threshold: every person commencing a VAT-taxable business in Korea must register for VAT within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxpayer category applies when annual taxable supplies are not more than KRW 30,000,000, but this does not exempt registration. Foreign suppliers of electronic services to Korean customers must also register under the simplified foreign-supplier VAT registration procedure without an exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Pet supplies and pet care devices sold through the Chusik Hoesa e-commerce channel qualify for the 0% export VAT rate under the Korean VAT Act when the goods are actually exported to a foreign country. The substance criteria require a genuine foreign nonexempt purchaser, physical shipment of the goods from Korea, an accepted Korea Customs Service export declaration, international transport to the overseas destination, and return of foreign currency proceeds to Korea through a permissible foreign-exchange channel. Services not connected with Korean real property or goods and supplied to non-resident customers for use abroad may also be zero-rated as exported services if they satisfy the statutory export-service rules. Digital services consumed in Korea by Korean customers remain subject to VAT and do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Export Declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS and accepted by customs officials as the formal export clearance document
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical despatch to the overseas consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List corresponding to the Incoterms and declared export value
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, or platform order record identifying the foreign buyer and agreed payment terms
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate issued by a registered Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing the collection of export proceeds in foreign currency (the Korean bank equivalent of an FIRC)
  • Any additional customs manifest, international courier declaration, or postal export dispatch record required for low-value e-commerce export shipments

Korea operates a real-time issuer-based electronic tax invoice regime through the NTS Hometax platform. All corporations, including a Chusik Hoesa, must issue e-Tax Invoices for VAT-taxable B2B supplies and transmit the invoice data to the NTS electronically at issuance. The e-invoice can be issued through Hometax directly, NTS-certified accounting software, or machine-to-machine interfaces such as Hometax OpenAPI. There is no pre-clearance approval model or fiscal stamping system in the Latin American sense; anti-fraud integrity is achieved through mandatory electronic issuance, unique e-invoice identifiers, real-time reconciliation with VAT returns, and the statutory Korean Cash Receipt System for consumer cash transactions. For export zero-rating, the e-invoice must be matched with the accepted UNI-PASS export declaration and the foreign currency settlement evidence.

The NTS will disallow export zero-rating and retrospectively reassess VAT at 10% if the export cannot be substantiated by valid customs declarations, genuine foreign sales documentation, or timely foreign-currency repatriation within the statutory 180-day settlement deadline. Under the Framework Act for National Taxes and VAT enforcement rules, this triggers underreporting penalties and late-payment interest, with additional civil penalties for false or unsubstantiated tax invoices. Knowing issuance of fraudulent export invoices or intentional evasion of VAT may result in criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with potential imprisonment and monetary penalties, along with suspension of the business registration and denial of future VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea runs a managed floating exchange rate regime with full current-account convertibility for trade, services, royalties, dividends and ordinary e-commerce settlements. The legal framework, principally the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, does not impose a blanket surrender of export proceeds; exporters can hold foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts or convert into KRW using the market rate. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident investment into a Chusik Hoesa, are free in principle but require filing with an authorized foreign exchange bank or the relevant authority. Inward equity investment must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before the remittance; the bank will check the foreign investment report and capital deposit verification. Dividend and profit remittances require proof of tax clearance, audited financials and a foreign exchange remittance report. The Bank of Korea may impose macroprudential measures, such as FX forward position limits on banks, and the Financial Supervisory Service requires banks to monitor underlying trade documentation for every foreign exchange payment. Thus convertibility is commercially workable for e-commerce treasury, but documentation, purpose codes and regulatory notifications must be exact.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory receipt timeline: export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of export declaration unless an authorized foreign exchange bank approves an extension for justified commercial reasons. Mandatory conversion ratio: none. There is no legal requirement to convert export proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain the full amount in a foreign currency account or sell the currency to the bank at the prevailing market rate. Payment aggregator settlements must be recorded with the underlying parcel and customs export record to satisfy the bank's foreign exchange statistics reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; foreign exchange banking is exercised through authorized foreign exchange banks supervised by the Financial Supervisory Service.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. South Korea does not permit an unlicensed offshore electronic money institution (EMI) to act as the primary banking or FX clearing counterparty for a Chusik Hoesa. Any provider that holds KRW customer balances, processes KRW electronic payment instructions, or performs cross-border FX settlement on a Korean entity's behalf must be licensed or otherwise authorized as an electronic financial business under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and supervised by the Financial Services Commission. Current-generation global EMIs can be used only as auxiliary collection tools and must settle into the Korean entity's local licensed bank account; they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for purposes of paid-in capital verification, export proceeds reporting, FX forward execution or regulatory compliance. Stripe and PayPal are payment gateways rather than licensed Korean banks and do not alter this requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the foreign investor wires equity capital via SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the Chusik Hoesa's Korean foreign-exchange bank. The remittance must state the FX purpose code for foreign direct investment into Korean equity, commonly 401100, and reference the Foreign Investment Notification filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The receiving Korean bank will verify the investor's identity, the corporate registration of the Chusik Hoesa, the board resolution approving the capital increase, and the paid-in capital deposit certificate. Funds may be converted from foreign currency into KRW at the market rate once the bank is satisfied that the statutory reporting has been completed. For any investment below the foreign investment reporting threshold, the bank will record the inward remittance as a capital transaction under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and require a clear economic rationale. No cash deposit or multi-step third-party funding is acceptable; the remitter should be the actual registered shareholder.Outbound payments for inventory and procurement from overseas suppliers should be made from the Chusik Hoesa's foreign currency account or by executing a KRW-to-FX conversion at the dealing bank. The routine settlement rail is SWIFT MT103 for open account and advance payment. For high-value or new-supplier transactions, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit using MT700 and MT707 can be used to secure payment terms, and Documentary Collections using D/P or D/A under URC 522 for mature supplier relationships. The bank requires the import contract, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, customs import declaration and, where applicable, a valuation certificate to support the purpose code and to comply with reporting. Since the final product is e-commerce pet supplies, the company must maintain matching inventory records, local tax invoices and shipment-level reconciliation to prevent mismatched payment descriptions.Forward contracts: permitted for USD/KRW and EUR/KRW on a deliverable basis up to 12 months with an authorized foreign exchange bank, supported by the underlying purchase orders, invoices or contracts evidencing genuine trade exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): allowed only to hedge genuine cross-currency receivables and payables where the underlying settlement is in a non-KRW currency; speculative KRW NDF trades are prohibited for resident entities without a recognized real exposure.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps: permissible for balance-sheet hedging of long-term foreign currency obligations, such as shareholder loans or imported inventory financing, subject to bank credit approval, underlying transaction documentation, and, where required, registration or reporting with the competent authorities.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. South Korea does not permit an unlicensed offshore electronic money institution (EMI) to act as the primary banking or FX clearing counterparty for a Chusik Hoesa. Any provider that holds KRW customer balances, processes KRW electronic payment instructions, or performs cross-border FX settlement on a Korean entity's behalf must be licensed or otherwise authorized as an electronic financial business under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and supervised by the Financial Services Commission. Current-generation global EMIs can be used only as auxiliary collection tools and must settle into the Korean entity's local licensed bank account; they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for purposes of paid-in capital verification, export proceeds reporting, FX forward execution or regulatory compliance. Stripe and PayPal are payment gateways rather than licensed Korean banks and do not alter this requirement.

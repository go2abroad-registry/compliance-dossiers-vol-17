Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic administration through the HomeTax online portal and export clearance through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

The Chusik Hoesa must first complete incorporation and court registration under the Korean Commercial Act, then file a business registration application with the competent district tax office via HomeTax within 20 days from the commencement of business. The NTS assigns the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the unified corporate TIN and VAT registration number. The filing requires the corporate certificate, corporate seal certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of business premises, and appointment of the statutory representative. A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business that supplies goods or electronic services into Korea may separately complete the HomeTax online registration for foreign business operators and obtain a foreign business operator VAT identification number; such an entity must also appoint a domestic tax representative unless a statutory exemption applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration turnover threshold applies to a Chusik Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory within 20 days of starting business. For individual simplified taxpayers, the annual taxable turnover threshold is KRW 80,000,000, but a stock corporation does not qualify for simplified taxpayer treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of portable battery generators and solar panels qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 11 of the Korean VAT Act when the goods are physically exported from Korea under a valid export declaration. The exporter must be a registered VAT payer, the goods must leave Korea within the statutory export period, and the exporter must hold contemporaneous documentation proving the sale, ocean or air shipment, and foreign exchange repatriation of the export proceeds.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) obtained through UNI-PASS and accepted before the goods leave Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt certificate evidencing actual receipt of export consideration from the overseas buyer
  • Original sales contract or purchase order with the foreign counterparty

Tax invoices must be issued as Electronic Tax Invoices (ETIs) through HomeTax or an NTS-approved ETI service provider such as e-CLIP and must be transmitted to the NTS by the day after the supply date. For retail sales to final consumers, credit card sales data is reported automatically through approved payment aggregators, and cash receipts must be issued or logged when required. Korea does not operate a Latin American-style real-time fiscal pre-clearance stamp; instead, the NTS applies a centralised Continuous Transaction Controls architecture with post-issuance integrity matching, automated validation against customs, remittance, and inventory data, and mandatory ETI authenticity verification through the official tax invoice portal.

NTS may reassess export zero-rating if the customs export declaration, shipping documentation, or foreign exchange remittance proof is missing, inconsistent, or fabricated. A denied zero-rating claim results in VAT assessed at 10% on the export consideration, together with penalty taxes for under-declaration, erroneous refunds, and late payment interest. Where forged export declarations, false tax invoices, or fabricated inward remittance certificates are used to obtain a refund, criminal liability may arise under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalised but report-based foreign exchange system. The KRW is fully convertible for most current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, without prior licensing, but all cross-border settlements must be effected through licensed foreign exchange banks and must be supported by underlying transaction evidence. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment into a Chusik Hoesa by non-residents, are permitted but are subject to mandatory prior or simultaneous notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Once the foreign investment is registered and capital is remitted through a designated foreign exchange bank, inward capital is freely credited to the entity's capital escrow account and converted to KRW without further MOEF approval, provided the commercial purpose is evidenced. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is lawful after the completion of the annual statutory corporate tax filing and after withholding tax is remitted to the National Tax Service; the entity must present the audited or reviewed financial statements, the board-approved dividend resolution, and the share registry to the remitting bank. The principal practical hurdle is not convertibility but documentary strictness: banks require exact foreign exchange purpose codes, underlying contracts, customs declarations for trade-linked flows, and matching amounts across bank statements, invoices, and shipping documents to prevent unclassified capital flows. Korea also imposes an anti-slavery style 'purpose code' regime, and all cross-border payments must be logged in the Bank of Korea's statistical reporting system. Overall, for a standard online retailer of solar portable power products, current account and investment convertibility are open, but the entity must operate through a regulated domestic banking channel and cannot treat the KRW as freely usable without transaction-level reporting and compliance evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected through a licensed foreign exchange bank within eighteen months from the date of the export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary merchandise exports such as solar panels or portable battery generators; exporters may hold foreign exchange receipts in domestic foreign-currency accounts and are not required to surrender them to the BOK or to any central bank. However, the export declaration, the commercial invoice, the customs clearance certificate, and the bank's foreign exchange purchase or collection confirmation must be retained and presented for reconciliation if the bank or MOEF requests proof of settlement. If export proceeds are not collected within the statutory eighteen-month period, the exporter must file an uncollected export proceeds application with the managing foreign exchange bank and, depending on circumstances, obtain a period extension or written approval from the authorities; failure to report material non-collection can be treated as a foreign exchange violation. In practical treasury terms, the entity should proactively match each export-related SWIFT MT103 remittance to its corresponding Customs Declaration Number and invoice before converting to KRW, because all proceeds are subject to BOK receipt reporting through the bank even though no mandatory percentage must be converted.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the responsible foreign exchange control authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% top national corporate income tax rate under a progressive scale: 10% up to KRW 200 million, 20% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 22% above KRW 20 billion; an additional 10% local income tax surcharge applies on the assessed national corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on outbound dividends plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 22%. A bilateral double tax treaty may reduce the rate, typically to 15% for portfolio dividends and 10% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 25% of the payor's shares, subject to beneficial ownership and principal purpose test conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year entity, the statutory filing deadline is 31 March of the following year. An extension of up to one month is available when the return is prepared or reviewed by a licensed tax accountant, and foreign-controlled corporations may obtain additional extensions under limited statutory conditions.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Corporate Identification and Filing Basis Legal name, business registration number, fiscal year ending date, representative director, principal place of management, business scope, and ISIC 4791 activity classification for direct online retail of portable battery generators and solar panels. A Chusik Hoesa incorporated in Korea is a domestic corporation even if all shareholders are non-residents; therefore, it must declare worldwide income. Do not mark the return as a non-resident corporation return unless the entity is a foreign-incorporated company operating through a Korean permanent establishment. Disclose the ultimate beneficial owner in the shareholder schedule and ensure the representative director signs under penalty of perjury. Incorrect classification can trigger the general anti-avoidance rule and treaty denial. Failure to file a corporate income tax return on time triggers an additional tax equal to 20% of the final corporate tax liability. Filing a return with an understated taxable income triggers a 10% penalty on the underpaid tax; fraudulent or wrongful underreporting triggers a 40% penalty on the underpaid tax. Daily late-payment surcharges accrue on unpaid national and local income tax until paid. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion is available under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to twice the evaded tax, and aggravated punishment for frequent or large-scale tax evasion.
Box 2 - Reconciliation of Book Income to Taxable Income Reconciliation starts with financial net income under Korean International Financial Reporting Standards, followed by add-backs for non-deductible expenses and subtraction of tax-exempt income and non-taxable reserves. For e-commerce income, all gross sales processed through Stripe, Affirm BNPL, payment gateways and direct bank transfers must be included. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Report of International Transactions must be filed with the annual corporate tax return when the total amount of cross-border related-party transactions reaches at least KRW 100 million in a taxable year. Mandatory Master File and Local File documentation is required where the corporation's annual revenue is at least KRW 100 billion and the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount is at least KRW 50 billion, or where the group meets the Country-by-Country reporting threshold of at least KRW 100 billion in consolidated revenue with operations in multiple jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the Korean tax authorities apply substance-over-form principles and the general anti-avoidance rule under the Corporate Tax Act and the International Tax Adjustment Act. Income attribution must follow actual economic functions, assets, and risks, consistent with the OECD Core Income-Generating Activities analysis. A Korean retailer cannot shift online sales profit to a foreign shell that performs no functions, assumes no risk, and employs no personnel; such an entity will be disregarded and its income re-attributed to the Korean corporation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the evaded corporate income tax, a 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4, late-payment surcharges, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, carrying imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine of up to twice the evaded tax; aggravated penalties apply for habitual or large-scale evasion.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil when the company is used as a mere alter ego for the controlling shareholder or director. The participating director can be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and the 40% penalty under civil tort principles, subject to travel restrictions, public disclosure as a delinquent taxpayer, and criminal conviction leading to personal imprisonment and disqualification from serving as a director.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Arm's-Length Distortion

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authority may adjust the taxable income using the arm's-length price under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and impose the related underreporting penalty taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes; additionally, the failure to file transfer pricing documentation triggers separate per-document penalties, and fraudulent TP documentation may be reclassified as tax evasion with a 40% penalty and criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing reports or direct payments to paper affiliates can be personally liable for the additional corporate tax, interest, and penalties. The corporate veil may be pierced where the related-party entity is merely a fictional intermediary. Personal criminal exposure includes imprisonment, fines, travel bans, and disqualification orders, and the director's personal assets may be seized to satisfy the Korean tax claim.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Payment Concealment

Statutory sanction: Korea may deny treaty benefits under the OECD principal purpose test and the domestic anti-avoidance rules, recharacterize the shell as not the beneficial owner, and assess the full 22% withholding tax on the hidden distribution. The payment is disallowed as a deductible expense, a 40% fraud penalty is imposed, and the criminal tax evasion provisions of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act apply. The Korean withholding agent is secondarily liable for the uncollected tax and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the controlling shareholder who knowingly implement the shell structure may be jointly liable for the Korean tax claim and the untaxed remittances. If the corporation is stripped of assets to avoid payment, a Korean court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the individuals personally liable; continued nonpayment can lead to asset attachment, travel bans, public blacklisting of the delinquent taxpayer, and criminal imprisonment for the directors involved in the fraudulent repatriation scheme.

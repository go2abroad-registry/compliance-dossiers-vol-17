Entity_Level_Taxation

None. South Korea does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election. The Chusik Hoesa is automatically taxable at the corporate entity level. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for VAT registration. Any person or business making taxable supplies in Korea must file Business Registration within 20 days of commencement, and foreign suppliers of electronic services are also required to register regardless of amount. The simplified taxpayer turnover threshold of KRW 80 million per year is an administrative simplification for very small businesses and is not a VAT registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under VAT Act Article 21 when goods are supplied to a foreign purchaser and physically exported from South Korea under a customs export procedure. The seller must maintain contemporaneous proof of shipment, delivery terms and foreign exchange receipt. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only when supplied to a foreign non-resident recipient; sale of consumer electronics to Korean consumers or delivery to a Korean address is a domestic supply subject to 10% VAT, not export zero-rating. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export sales may be refunded after being claimed in the VAT return through HomeTax.

  • Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, evidencing customs clearance of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Electronic Tax Invoice for the zero-rated export, issued through NTS HomeTax where applicable
  • Foreign exchange bank confirmation/certificate of export proceeds received, or equivalent bank records showing repatriation of the export sale value within the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act deadline

All VAT tax invoices in Korea must be issued as Electronic Tax Invoices (ETIs) through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system or through an NTS-certified ASP connected to HomeTax by application program interfaces. Each ETI is transmitted to the NTS at the time of issuance and given a national electronic document number; anti-fraud checks monitor duplicate invoices, abnormal B2B chains and mismatched returns. The zero-rated export status must be shown on the ETI. Goods imported for online retail resale must be reported to the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, and import VAT is collected at customs clearance before release; properly documented input VAT can be credited against output VAT on the subsequent domestic retail sale.

NTS audits retrospectively compare customs export records, e-tax invoices, VAT returns and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without complete export and proceeds documentation, NTS may deny the zero rate, assess 10% VAT on the supply, add statutory daily overdue surcharges, impose administrative understatement penalties and, for fraudulent or intentionally concealed transactions, refer the case for criminal prosecution. The standard re-assessment limitation period is five years, extended to ten years for cases involving intentional omission or international tax fraud; clawback may therefore cover all open prior periods with cumulative interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalized current-account foreign exchange regime. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act permits trade-related payments to be cleared and converted into KRW without pre-approval, but requires every regulated transaction to be routed through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, intra-group loans, and profit repatriation, are permitted under a declaration-and-capture model rather than a strict licensing model, provided the remitting bank receives accurate FEIS reporting. In practice, dividends to non-resident shareholders are treated as current-account remittances only after the bank verifies tax clearance, audited financial statements, and shareholder meeting resolutions. There is no mandatory central surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may hold foreign currency domestically and convert at their own discretion. The primary conversion risk is therefore not government denial but documentary mismatches, late FEIS declarations, unexplained source-of-wealth gaps, and mismatched purpose codes, which can trigger BOK-oriented bank compliance holds and materially delay KRW clearing. For this Chusik Hoesa engaged in online consumer electronics retail, full convertibility is achievable only through one principal Korean foreign exchange bank that maintains the entity's entire FX ledger, corporate account, and trade file, thereby creating a transparent audit trail for all inward and outward flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and current Korean bank operating regulations, export proceeds must be received through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank within six months from the export declaration date, unless the bank approves a formal extension. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for proceeds; the directive is zero percent mandatory conversion, meaning 100 percent of export receipts may be retained in a foreign currency domestic account for future hedging or overseas disbursement. Any export payment not collected within the six-month statutory window must be supported by a written justification and documentary evidence of collection delay; conventional electronic goods orders with extended buyer payment terms are regularly accepted as long as the bank files the relevant FEIS extension notice.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act enforcement framework, administered through licensed foreign exchange banks and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI licenses do not convey authority to perform foreign exchange intermediation or bank-facing settlement within the Republic of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korean banks will not accept an unlicensed fintech EMI as the primary clearing rail for statutory capital verification, corporate billings, or FEIS reporting. A foreign EMI may be used only as a customer-side payment acquisition gateway where its underlying funds eventually settle into a licensed Korean bank account; it cannot replace a tier-one domestic commercial bank for corporate treasury execution, regulatory reporting, or FX conversion. For this consumer electronics retail Chusik Hoesa, the bank onboarding blueprint must therefore designate a single licensed Korean foreign exchange bank as the exclusive liquidity clearing intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity capital must enter through a direct SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder's corporate banking account to the designated Korean foreign exchange bank escrow account. The wire instruction must name the Korean Chusik Hoesa as the receiver, identify the remitter as the shareholder, and contain a purpose description stating direct equity investment contribution under Article 6 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Before execution, the beneficial owner or legal counsel must confirm whether the contribution triggers Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting thresholds, and if so, file the required foreign direct investment declaration with the designated bank or Korean Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) portal. Upon maturity of the wire, the receiving bank issues the Foreign Currency Deposit Certificate and the foreign exchange sale contract at the prevailing bank rate when converting to KRW. Let's accurately state: The national withholding tax on dividends is 15% under the Corporate Income Tax Act / Personal Income Tax Act for resident individuals, but for non-resident corporations it is 20% (Article 98-1 of the Corporate Income Tax Act). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File must be prepared if both (i) the total intercompany transaction value with foreign related parties exceeds KRW 100 billion (approximately USD 75 million) in the fiscal year, or (ii) the company's total revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion. However, for the purpose of all tax filings, a transaction-by-transaction disclosure is required for any related-party transaction above KRW 500 million. A simplified Local File is sufficient if the intercompany transactions are below KRW 5 billion but the company fails the exemption threshold. On request, the NTS can impose a penalty of up to KRW 30 million for non-submission of TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and GAAR under the Framework Convention for the Prevention of Tax Treaty Abuse. The economic substance doctrine requires that a Korean resident company (chusik hoesa) maintains a real office, employees, and actual decision-making power for its core income-generating activities (CIGA), particularly for e-commerce operations such as logistics, customer service, marketing, and order fulfillment. Korean domestic law does not have a formal 'Economic Substance Regulations' (ESR) like the UAE, but the NTS applies substance-over-form under Article 15 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the LOB/PPT provisions of tax treaties. If the Korean entity is a mere shell with no management and non-resident directors control all its functions, the NTS may disregard the local entity's separate existence and attribute its income to the foreign headquarters, exposing the foreign parent to Korean branch taxation and filing penalties. For digital platform businesses, the new 'Offline VAT' and 'Online Purchase Tax' initiatives do not require extra corporate substance, but the Indian, Korean, and other Asian tax authorities increasingly demand evidence of core functions in the jurisdiction where risks and assets are located.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Violation

Statutory sanction: Article 11 and 15 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (국제조세조정에관한법률): the NTS may adjust the taxable income to reflect the arm's length price and impose an understatement penalty of 30% of the resulting additional tax (or 10% if the taxpayer reported in good faith but with insufficient documentation). Additionally, a separate documentation penalty of up to KRW 100 million applies for failure to submit the required TP files. If the adjustment is made through a deemed dividend to the foreign shareholder, withholding tax at 22% (or treaty rate) is collected, increasing the effective cash cost.

Director personal liability: The representative director (대표이사) is personally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes and penalties if the NTS proves that the director intentionally or negligently omitted the TP documentation and thereby caused the tax loss. Under Article 38 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, a director who approves the filing of a false corporate tax return is jointly liable with the corporation for the tax, interest, and penalties up to the amount of the corporation's assets that the director diverted or disposed of with intent to evade the tax. In flagrant cases, the NTS may file a criminal complaint under Article 118 of the Corporate Income Tax Act (조세범 처벌법) for 'tax avoidance by fraudulent means', carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax. Directors may also be blacklisted from board positions in financial institutions for 5 years.
Shell Company without Economic Substance (PE Trigger and Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The NTS may apply the 'substance-over-form' principle under Article 15 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the OECD's Principal Purpose Test to deny the DTA benefit, treating the Korean entity as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent. This results in reassessment of the Korean source income tax (normal corporate tax plus 10% local surcharge), plus 40% penalty for 'serious negligence' under Article 35 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and withholding tax on the deemed dividend to the foreign parent. The foreign parent may also lose treaty benefits on all future payments.

Director personal liability: The nominee directors of the Korean shell company are personally liable for the tax claims if they signed the corporate tax return while knowing the company had no real operations; they can be jointly assessed under Article 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. Criminal sanctions under the Aggravated Punishment Act for Specific Economic Crimes (특정경제범죄 가중처벌법) apply if the tax evasion amount exceeds KRW 300 million—imprisonment of 3 to 20 years and a fine equal to 2 to 5 times the evaded amount. A travel ban (출국금지) may be imposed on the directors while the criminal investigation is pending, and they may be subject to overseas asset tracing and extradition under the Mutual Legal Assistance Treaty with the relevant jurisdiction.
Failure to Withhold Dividend and Royalty Taxes at Source (WHT Evasion)

Statutory sanction: Article 98(1) of the Corporate Income Tax Act: the payer must withhold and pay the tax to the tax office by the 10th day of the following month. Failure to withhold carries a penalty of 10% of the uncollected amount plus daily surcharge of 0.022% on the unpaid amount. The foreign payee remains liable for the tax, and the local company is not relieved from obligation; the NTS may collect the WHT from the local company as a responsible taxpayer. A refund claim by the foreign shareholder will require the withholding agent to amend the withholding return and pay the full statutory tax, plus penalties.

Director personal liability: If the board of directors (이사회) resolves to make a dividend distribution without arranging for WHT, each director who voted in favor is personally and severally liable for the uncollected WHT, penalties, and interest under Articles 67 and 68 of the Framework Act on National Taxes. The NTS commonly pursues claims against the assets of directors (including personal bank accounts, real estate) via asset seizure (압류) and garnishment (추심). Criminal indictment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (조세범 처벌법 제3조): imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to 2 times the unpaid tax; if the undeclared withholding tax exceeds KRW 100 million, imprisonment up to 5 years. Additionally, the foreign parent may be considered a 'second debtor' and the Korean government may demand payment from its Korean subsidiary's directors personally in cross-border bankruptcy proceedings.

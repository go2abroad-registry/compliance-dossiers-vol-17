Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Commercial Act for a Chusik Hoesa, but the company must issue at least one share and each share must have a par value of at least KRW 100. The subscription amount must be paid before registration into a blocked temporary bank account in the company's intended name, and the bank balance certificate must be submitted with the registration application. Funds are released only after the incorporation registration is completed or after an authoritative court instruction cancels the application. The state registry charges registration duty based on paid-in capital, subject to a minimum duty together with education surtax and court filing fee of approximately USD 80.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a local tax representative (tax agent) and file an application for business registration with the competent district tax office. The application requires the entity's certificate of incorporation, proof of foreign ownership, and a domestic address for service. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (TIN) which also serves as the VAT registration number. The process can be completed online via HomeTax using a digital certificate or through a local tax representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a local tax representative (tax agent) and file an application for business registration with the competent district tax office. The application requires the entity's certificate of incorporation, proof of foreign ownership, and a domestic address for service. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (TIN) which also serves as the VAT registration number. The process can be completed online via HomeTax using a digital certificate or through a local tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold; any person making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT. Simplified taxpayer regime applies to small businesses with annual revenue below 80,000,000 KRW, but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying international services are zero-rated under the VAT Act. To claim zero-rating, the exporter must prove that goods were physically exported or services were supplied to a foreign recipient. For goods, the export must be declared to customs and the goods must leave Korea within a specified period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or proof of foreign exchange receipt

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) must be issued and transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system for all B2B supplies. Real-time transmission is required within the day of issuance. There is no separate fiscal clearance API, but the e-invoice system integrates with NTS for anti-fraud monitoring. Cash receipts are also mandatory for B2C transactions.

Fraudulent or unverifiable export zero-rating claims are subject to penalties under the VAT Act and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Penalties include additional VAT of up to 40% of the understated tax, criminal fines, and imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks may apply for up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Bank of Korea's foreign exchange administration framework, the South Korean won is fully convertible for genuine current account transactions; payments for merchandise trade, freight, insurance, royalties, service fees and routine operating expenses may be executed through designated foreign exchange banks on the strength of an underlying invoice and a customs declaration. Capital account movements require more formality: equity subscription, acquisition of a Korean entity, acquisition and disposal of external securities, and foreign-currency loans are subject to prior report or confirmation by a bank through the Foreign Exchange Information System, and in certain cases pre-approval by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no blanket ban on foreign ownership for a Chusik Hoesa; non-resident shareholders may remit subscribed capital in convertible currency, convert into KRW, and lawfully remit profits and dividends after payment of corporate income tax and compliance with the real-name financial transaction system. Export proceeds are not subject to a fixed surrender percentage, but must be collected within the statutory period (generally one year from export date) and adequately reported; any unrepatriated foreign currency creates a legal exposure. Overall, South Korea maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with liberalised current account flows and a transparent negative-list capital account system, making it a relatively light-touch jurisdiction for a digitally operating fast-fashion apparel exporter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect export proceeds within 12 months from the date of shipment (extension available through an MOEF/Bank of Korea application) and must report such collection through a domestic foreign exchange bank. Korea does not impose a fixed statutory conversion ratio; foreign currency may be retained in a foreign currency account, but the balance must be reconciled with the Foreign Exchange Information System and converted to KRW when used for local operating payments. A domestic bank may require conversion of surplus foreign exchange above the entity's projected six-month import and supplier payment pipeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) - the central monetary authority - together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the policy supervisor under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low for a standalone EMI acting as the sole banking counterparty; Medium only as an ancillary software/payment gateway. A non-bank EMI does not hold a foreign exchange bank licence under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and Bank of Korea/Financial Supervisory Service rules require foreign exchange transactions to pass through a designated foreign exchange bank. Foreign e-money institutions such as Revolut Business or Airwallex can offer multi-currency SWIFT/IBAN rails, but they cannot lawfully serve as the receiving bank for Korean foreign direct investment or maintain a Korean settlement account for export proceeds. If used for fast-fashion e-commerce ancillary payments, the EMI must partner with a licensed domestic payment gateway or bank and the Chusik Hoesa remains the merchant of record for FX reporting and source-of-funds verification. The recommended treatment is to use domestic Tier-1 banks for all capital and FX settlement, and restrict EMIs to low-value micro disbursements to overseas suppliers with a full audit trail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

The shareholding chain contains a non-resident parent or ultimate beneficial owner in a jurisdiction with an opaque beneficial-ownership registry and unclear CRS/FATCA reporting result, creating an unexplained mismatch between ownership documentation and trade counterparties.

Root cause: The shareholding chain contains a non-resident parent or ultimate beneficial owner in a jurisdiction with an opaque beneficial-ownership registry and unclear CRS/FATCA reporting result, creating an unexplained mismatch between ownership documentation and trade counterparties.

Defense evidence: Provide (i) a certified corporate registry excerpt and full shareholder ledger for each intermediate company in the chain; (ii) government-issued passport and tax-residence certificate of every UBO holding 10% or more, notarised within 3 months; (iii) CRS/FATCA self-certification forms for all shareholder accounts; (iv) an independent source-of-wealth report explaining the subscription funds from prior fashion-wholesale or e-commerce revenue; (v) a board resolution appointing the Korean resident agent; and (vi) the Foreign Investment Promotion Act report acknowledged by KOTRA or the receiving bank.

Open-account payables to suppliers in China, Vietnam, and Bangladesh with inconsistent payee names or recurring near-threshold amounts create sanctions-screening and transaction-laundering red flags.

Root cause: Open-account payables to suppliers in China, Vietnam, and Bangladesh with inconsistent payee names or recurring near-threshold amounts create sanctions-screening and transaction-laundering red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant channel is to file a foreign-investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remittance, open a dedicated foreign investment escrow account at a Tier-1 designated foreign exchange bank, receive a SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent using the FEIS purpose-code classification applicable to foreign equity subscription, and have the bank issue a certificate of foreign investment upon receipt. The funds must be held in the escrow/collection account until the share subscription is balanced, then converted to KRW through the bank at the market rate and transferred to the operational KRW account only after signing the branch forex contract. Capital verification in Korea is satisfied by the receiving bank's remittance confirmation, the share issuance board minutes, the updated shareholder register, and the foreign-invested enterprise notification submitted to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or the delegated bank. No cryptocurrency, third-party virtual wallets, or unrelated intercompany sweep funding should be used without prior bank and FSC consent.For ordinary open-account payments to overseas apparel and fabric suppliers, use the bank's international division to send SWIFT MT103 messages through the domestic correspondent network; the Korean bank will apply its FX operation and sanctions-screening checks. For large or strategic purchases, a standby or commercial letter of credit (L/C) is recommended at sight or on a usance basis, with the transport documents consisting of bills of lading, invoices, packing lists, and insurance certificates. Documentary collections are acceptable for repeat supplier relationships where a bank-endorsed bill of exchange accompanies the documents and the payment date is contractually fixed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 5 billion per fiscal year trigger mandatory Local File preparation; KRW 100 billion or higher group consolidated revenue, combined with cross-border related-party transactions, generally triggers Master File preparation under the Korean International Tax Adjustment Act and OECD TP Guidelines

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the domestic anti-avoidance rule and the Korean International Tax Adjustment Act require that the Chusik Hoesa demonstrate real substance in Korea; Core Income-Generating Activities such as order acceptance, procurement, inventory holding, payment settlement, marketing, and returns handling must have actual premises, personnel, payroll, and decision-making in Korea, otherwise the entity may be treated as a mere commissionaire or conduit, a Korean permanent establishment may be attributed to the foreign principal, and treaty or CFC benefits may be denied

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act for National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, the NTS will assess the underpaid principal tax plus 10%-40% unfaithful-return penalties and daily delinquency surcharges; a fraudulent evasion may be referred for criminal prosecution, carrying imprisonment for up to five years and a criminal fine of up to five times the tax evaded

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false return are jointly and severally liable for the suppressed corporate tax; the corporate veil is pierced when the company is used as a mere instrument for tax evasion, exposing director bank accounts, personal residences, and other assets to attachment; NTS also imposes an exit/ travel ban and places the director on the delinquent taxpayer blacklist, with potential criminal imprisonment for the representative director.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Intermediary

Statutory sanction: The International Tax Adjustment Act permits the NTS to re-determine the arm's-length price, ignore the intermediary, re-characterise the Korean entity as the beneficial seller, and impose additional tax, interest, and up to a 30% transfer-pricing documentation penalty if the Local File and Master File are not produced or are false; the general anti-avoidance rule may also deny treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Korean courts and the NTS may pierce the corporate arrangement on substance-over-form grounds and collect the adjusted tax plus penalties directly from the directors who designed the artificial chain; directors may be criminally liable for false documentation, and may face travel restriction, asset oter, and blacklisting; if the shell arrangement is used for money laundering, the AML law exposes directors to imprisonment of up to five years and confiscation of proceeds.
Withholding Tax Evasion and AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for unwithheld national tax at 20% and local surtax at 2%, plus late-payment surcharges and penalties; the Korean Act on Reporting and Using Certain Financial Transaction Information imposes criminal liability where the concealment of beneficial owners or laundering of e-commerce receipts is shown, with imprisonment and fines; the foreign exchange and customs authorities may also levy penalties for false payment classification

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for withholding and remitting taxes have joint personal liability for the withholding tax even after the company is dissolved; an intentional failure to remit withheld taxes is treated as misappropriation and can result in criminal imprisonment; the representative director and compliance officer may be blacklisted by the NTS and Financial Intelligence Unit, denied re-entry or travel abroad, and ordered to restore diverted funds from personal assets under the corporate-veil piercing doctrine.

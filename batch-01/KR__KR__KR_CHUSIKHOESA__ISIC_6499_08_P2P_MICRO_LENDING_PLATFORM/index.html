Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Chusik Hoesa operating an ISIC 6499 P2P micro-lending platform in South Korea, a pure virtual office, commercial mailbox, or mail-forwarding address is rejected for regulatory and tax purposes. The Online Investment-Linked Finance business regime under the Financial Services Commission requires an independent physical office capable of storing the original incorporation record, staffed compliance function, server and system controls, and a documented operating infrastructure for customer onboarding and settlement via ACH direct debit and local wallet payout rail. The National Tax Service treats a virtual office as inadequate for business registration and VAT administration, and Korean banks will refuse to open the corporate operating and escrow/wallet settlement accounts at a virtual address. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa, so an ordinary holding company may incorporate with nominal capital. However, because the licensed P2P micro-lending business falls under Article 6 of the Online Investment-Linked Financial Business Act, the operator must prove paid-in capital of at least KRW 500,000,000. All capital subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked corporate formation account held by a licensed commercial bank in the name of the company-to-be-incorporated; the funds may not be withdrawn or disbursed until the District Court Registry issues the certificate of incorporation registration. The court registry also charges registration and education taxes calculated at 0.4 percent of paid-in capital plus 20 percent surtax, resulting in the approximate official filing fee of USD 1800 where KRW 500,000,000 is subscribed.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial or civil-defense-regulated physical facilities. As a pure digital fintech head office, the company must nevertheless obtain a standard fire safety inspection certificate under the Fire Service Act, maintain an emergency evacuation plan, and comply with occupational safety and health training obligations for office workers under the Occupational Safety and Health Act; however, no special industrial fire permit, Civil Defense shelter approval, or hazardous materials license is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (Hometax) at www.hometax.go.kr. Export and customs clearances are handled by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic customs portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign non-resident entity has a Korean domestic place of business for tax purposes. If it does, file the standard business registration application with the competent district tax office through the HomeTax portal within twenty days of commencing business. Step 2: If there is no domestic place of business, appoint a Korean tax representative who is a resident individual or a licensed tax/accounting professional authorized to receive tax documents, communicate with the NTS, and pay taxes on behalf of the foreign entity; the power of attorney must be filed with the application. Step 3: Submit certified copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation or equivalent constitutive documents, foreign business registration certificate, board resolution appointing the representative and opening the Korean operation, and the tax representative's power of attorney; non-English or non-Korean documents must be accompanied by an English or Korean translation. Step 4: The NTS issues the statutory 10-digit business registration number, which serves as both the corporate tax identification number and the VAT identification number; no separate VAT number is issued. Step 5: Foreign providers of electronic services to Korean non-business customers may use simplified registration through HomeTax where the special statutory conditions for foreign electronic-service suppliers are met, and will receive a Korean VAT registration number under that simplified procedure. Step 6: If the entity will export goods, link the business registration number to the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system and obtain the statutory exporter registration before making the first export.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income, with no pass-through attribution of profits or credits to shareholders; territorial scope is therefore worldwide subject to foreign tax credit relief.

Available election forms

None - the Republic of Korea does not operate a check-the-box election regime and a Chusik Hoesa cannot elect pass-through status for national tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, which disallows deductions or requires income inclusion where cross-border arrangements exploit double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold applies - any entity making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT within twenty days of starting business. For a foreign provider of electronic services supplying Korean non-business customers, the Value-Added Tax Act and NTS administrative rules contain special registration thresholds and a simplified registration procedure through HomeTax, which should be confirmed against current NTS guidance because this platform will earn both VAT-exempt loan-related income and potentially taxable separately charged platform fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are actually exported from Korea to a foreign destination, the export is supported by a Korea Customs Service export declaration, the exporter receives the foreign currency proceeds, and the exporter keeps the transactional and shipping evidence. Services qualify for zero-rating only when the service is performed outside Korea, supplied to a non-resident, and the benefit and use occur outside Korea; for digital or offshore platform services, the contract, service description, IP/geolocation evidence, and foreign payment trail must demonstrate foreign consumption. A P2P micro-lending platform must not automatically apply zero-rating to loan interest because financial services are VAT-exempt; separately charged cross-border platform fees can be zero-rated only when the substantive foreign-service criteria are met.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration acceptance and clearance record issued through the UNI-PASS portal
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing physical movement of the goods from Korea to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, parties, Incoterms, and export shipment details
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence in the form of a foreign-exchange bank remittance advice, foreign currency deposit statement, or certificate of export receipt showing collection of the proceeds within the statutory deadline
  • For offshore digital services, substitute customs documents with a dated foreign sales/service contract, a completion or delivery report, location-of-use evidence, and the overseas bank remittance receipt

All VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices for every taxable supply; electronic tax invoices transmitted through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system are mandatory and must be sent to the NTS no later than the day after the tax invoice is issued. The system performs automated data matching and validation against VAT returns, and no physical fiscal-stamping regime exists in Korea; enforcement and anti-fraud integrity are achieved through mandatory electronic transmission, tax invoice linkage to the business registration number, and NTS real-time reconciliation. The platform must also comply with all statutory payment-receipt and cash register requirements for consumer transactions using approved VAN and card network infrastructure.

The NTS has the statutory authority to audit export zero-rating claims within the general national tax assessment period, which is extended in cases of fraud or deliberate concealment. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory deadline and no authorized extension is obtained, the export is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and the NTS will assess 10% VAT on the full invoice amount, plus late-payment interest and statutory additional taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act. Where the zero-rating claim relies on forged customs declarations, backdated bills of lading, fabricated foreign payment records, or other deceptive documentation, the underlying input VAT refund is clawed back, an additional tax of up to 40% of the supply value may be imposed, and the matter may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with potential imprisonment and fines proportional to the evaded tax. The retrospective reassessment and clawback remain available for the full extended statutory period in fraud cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-float, fully convertible current account and a liberalized capital account with mandatory reporting rather than blanket approval. Non-resident and resident entities may freely convert KRW for trade and service transactions, but any cross-border payment exceeding USD 5,000 must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank, and transactions above USD 50,000 require a certificate of declaration issued by the bank under the Foreign Exchange Information Network (KFXNET). Profit remittances such as dividends, loan interest, and liquidation proceeds are generally permitted without prior MOEF approval, provided the underlying investment was registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). However, a non-resident foreign investor must present audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and board resolutions authorizing the remittance. For a P2P micro-lending platform, capital account transactions, including equity injections, foreign debt borrowing, and acquisition of domestic loan receivables, must be supported by a Foreign Exchange Bank's certificate of acquisition of foreign currency and a report of performance of contract under FETA. There is no forced conversion of export proceeds; Korea abolished mandatory surrender requirements in 1998. Businesses may hold foreign currency proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts, but bank settlement must occur within 30 days if the proceeds are sold in the domestic interbank market. The practical hurdle is not approval risk but documentary burden: every inbound and outbound remittance must align with the stated purpose code (e.g., 6007 for loan disbursements, 6008 for loan collection, 6011 for dividends). The Bank of Korea closely monitors suspicious cross-border flows related to crypto-assets or lending platforms, so an unlicensed P2P lender will face severe difficulties in repatriating profits. Therefore, even in a 'liberal' regime, the platform must establish a dedicated corporate banking relationship with a licensed FX bank and maintain full transaction-level audit trails to satisfy market-conducive regulatory expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not require any export proceeds to be physically surrendered or converted into KRW. According to Article 10 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters may hold, retain, or use foreign currency proceeds freely, provided they report the receipt to the designated foreign exchange bank within 30 days from the date of receipt. The reporting obligation is statutorily fulfilled through the electronic trade settlement system. If the exporter wishes to convert the foreign currency into KRW, there is no mandatory ratio; conversion can be executed immediately at the market rate. For a P2P lending platform that may receive foreign-currency loan repayments from cross-border borrowers, such receipts are not technically 'export proceeds' but will be classified under financial service trade and must be reported as foreign currency income from service provision, with the same 30-day declaration window.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) via the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to Medium with strict licensing constraints. South Korea does not recognize generic 'Electronic Money Institutions' (EMIs) as legally chartered credit institutions. Non-bank fintechs cannot accept deposits or issue money instruments unless licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act as a 'Prepaid Electronic Payment Method' issuer or as a 'Commercial P2P Lending Platform' registered with the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Since October 2019, any P2P lending platform is required to register with the FSC and meet minimum capital of KRW 500 million, and it must hold the loan receivables on a trust through a licensed trustee (usually a commercial bank or securities firm). An offshore digital EMI such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot lawfully provide settlement accounts to a Korean-domiciled entity for the purpose of receiving loan repayments or disbursing credit, because those transactions constitute the business of 'receiving funds for the purpose of remittance' which requires a Money Broker license under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Cross-border EMIs are permitted only as payment rails for invoiced trade if they partner with a Korean licensed bank and use the bank's foreign exchange infrastructure; however, they cannot legally hold the principal working capital of a micro-lending business. Hence, the viable blueprint is a full-reserve bank account at a Tier-1 domestic bank, with the domestic P2P trust account managed under a financial investment company as custodian.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a top combined statutory marginal rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident shareholder is 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% of the 20% national tax), aggregating 22%. Under applicable tax treaties, the dividend withholding rate is commonly reduced to 15% generally or 5% to 10% for qualified shareholders with substantial shareholding and satisfaction of limitation-on-benefits provisions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final annual return: within three months after the end of the fiscal year; for a December 31 year-end, due on March 31. Interim prepayment return: within two months after the close of the first six-month period; for a December 31 year-end, due on August 31. A non-resident corporation with a permanent establishment in Korea follows the same statutory deadlines for the Korean-sourced income attributable to that permanent establishment.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as the national corporate income tax return: March 31 for a December 31 year-end; the local income tax return is due on the same date. For non-resident taxpayers, local tax filing is made at the district municipality where the Korean permanent establishment is located.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Korean enterprise or foreign entity engaging in cross-border transactions with foreign related parties is required to prepare and file a Local File when the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceeds KRW 5,000,000,000. The Master File is separately mandatory for a taxable year in which the annual consolidated revenue of the business group exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000. Foreign investors relying on transactional structure should also maintain benchmark studies and a contemporaneous economic substance file for any intragroup loan, algorithm licensing, or centralized fintech service supplied to the Korean platform.

Economic substance scope: Korea has not enacted OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations for domestic entities, but economic substance is enforced through the Korean CFC (Controlled Foreign Corporation) rules, the General Anti-Abuse Rule, and the tax treaty limitation-on-benefits application. A Chusik Hoesa used for P2P micro-lending must have actual management and control in Korea, a physical registered office with an operating function, local employees, board minutes prepared in Korea, and bank accounts through which the lending funds actually flow. The National Tax Service may recharacterize a Korean entity as a conduit if it finds the entity was inserted for treaty shopping, if the non-resident shareholder lacks genuine economic ownership of the income, or if the main beneficiaries of the lending algorithm or loan book are non-resident related parties without arm's-length remuneration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: An unjustified understatement surcharge of 20% of the additional tax for negligence, escalated to 40% for fraud; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment for up to three years or fines of up to five times the evaded tax; daily late-payment interest of 0.022% on unpaid tax; and tax authorities may immediately collect additional taxes based on a corrected assessment.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false tax return or authorizes concealing revenue may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate income tax to the extent of his or her fault; the National Tax Service may issue a personal collection order and asset freeze. Upon indictment, the director is subject to compulsory appearance in court, travel ban, surrender of passport, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authority adjusts taxable income to the arm's-length result and imposes a 10% negligence surcharge on the additional assessed tax where transfer pricing documentation is not contemporaneous or is materially incomplete; where there is concealment, the surcharge increases to 20%; interest at the daily statutory rate accrues from the original due date. In serious cases, the taxpayer is removed from the mutual agreement procedure protection and exposed to adjusted withholding on constructive dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize avoided transfer-pricing adjustments while knowing underlying foreign related-party statements are false may be personally secondarily liable for unpaid corporation tax and the surcharges. Travel bans and forced-denaturalization disqualifications from future boards may be imposed where the non-compliance is systematic.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may apply the Korean GAAR to disregard the intermediary, re-source income to the beneficial owner, impose full 22% withholding on profits repatriated through the conduit, deny tax treaty relief, assess negligence surcharge of up to 40%, and reclassify loan repayments as deemed dividends. The entity itself can lose tax residency status if the place of effective management is found to be outside Korea, exposing the Korean entity to a deemed export and unlimited assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell Chusik Hoesa who permit the entity to distribute phantom dividends or use corporate bank accounts for the principal lenders' liquidity may be personally liable under the Korean Commercial Act for unlawful distribution; criminal AML examinations may lead to travel bans under the Financial Information Act and imprisonment for money-laundering offenses.
AML and Unlicensed P2P Micro-Lending

Statutory sanction: Operation without a license under the Credit Business or Online Investment Lending rules exposes the Chusik Hoesa to administrative penalties, shutdown orders, fines up to KRW 200 million, and criminal prosecution punishable by up to five years' imprisonment. The statute of relative anti-money-laundering fines ranges from KRW 10 million to KRW 100 million per violation, and follow-up corrective orders may be published publicly.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly allow unlicensed deposit-taking or fail to maintain AML transaction reporting are personally liable for unpaid regulatory fines; the prosecution routinely recommends travel bans, freezing of personal assets, and imprisonment for platform founders in the microlending fintech space. Directors cannot be indemnified by the corporate entity to cover AML penalties.

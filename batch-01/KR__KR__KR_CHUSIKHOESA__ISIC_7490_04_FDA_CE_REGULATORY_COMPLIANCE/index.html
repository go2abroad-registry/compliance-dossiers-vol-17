Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total authorized capital, number of shares, par value, and amount paid in. The paid-in capital must be fully deposited with a bank or securities company before the incorporation registration is filed, and the share capital deposit certificate must accompany the registration application. The deposited funds are effectively blocked until the incorporation registration is completed, after which they become available to the company for lawful business use. Where a non-resident shareholder subscribes shares, the foreign investment report procedure under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act should be considered if the investment reaches the statutory threshold and satisfies the voting-share percentage test, but there is no separate state-mandated escrow beyond the deposit certificate requirement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) - Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean tax presence (through a fixed place of business or deriving Korean-source taxable income), the entity must first obtain a Foreigner's Investment Registration or appoint a domestic tax representative before any taxable activity. The entity (or its representative) must file an application for business registration (사업자등록신청서) with the relevant district tax office (세무서) under the NTS jurisdiction. Required documents include: certified copy of incorporation certificate, proof of ownership/lease of the Korean business premise (if any), articles of incorporation, and an apostilled or notarised power of attorney in favour of the domestic tax representative. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (사업자등록번호) which serves as both the TIN and VAT registration ID. For non-resident entities with no Korean establishment but required to withhold Korean taxes, the NTS assigns a withholding tax registration number (원천징수용 고유번호) via the same HomeTax portal. For VAT purposes, all non-resident suppliers making Korean taxable supplies must register for VAT through the simplified registration procedure (간이과세자 등록 or 일반과세자 사업자 등록) regardless of turnover; a zero-rated-only foreign entity may optionally register for VAT refunds.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold. Mandatory registration for any person (including non-residents) engaging in a taxable supply of goods or services in Korea, regardless of volume. A foreign supplier with no Korean-source taxable supplies may voluntarily register solely to claim VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods and services as defined under Articles 21 to 25 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act: (1) export of goods when the customs export declaration has been accepted and the goods depart Korea; (2) provision of services rendered to foreign residents or to overseas clients, supplied for use or consumption outside Korea (including consulting services provided by a MedTech regulatory consultancy to foreign clients, provided the service is performed while the client is abroad and the benefit is realised overseas); (3) export of qualifying offshore digital services; and (4) supply of exports to temporary outbound processing or international transportation services. To substantiate zero-rating, the taxable person must obtain the prescribed export performance documents, prove foreign currency repatriation, and retain records of actual underlying service delivery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (수출신고필증 - KCS form, issued by Korea Customs Service upon export activation)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) showing shipment from Korea to a foreign destination
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation (외환거래증명서 - issued by the designated foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency within the statutory repatriation period)

All VAT registered businesses (both general and simplified) must issue electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) for business-to-business (B2B) transactions through the HomeTax system or an approved EDI/API interface. The electronic invoice must be submitted to the NTS within the day following the supply date (for the supplier) and the immediate next day (for the buyer) as per the VAT Electronic Tax Invoice Act. The system uses real-time transmission to the NTS integrated BIG3 platform without a pre-clearance stamp. For business-to-consumer (B2C) transactions with total invoice value exceeding KRW 100,000 (or for statutory retail establishments), cash receipts (현금영수증) must be electronically issued via the affiliated cash receipt network. While there is no fiscally automated stamping requirement, all issued invoices are matched to customs and banking data in near real-time to prevent fraud.

Misclaimed export zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to a 10% VAT underpayment (dishonest declaration) penalty plus 0.8% monthly delinquency interest. In addition, the NTS may impose an administrative fine of up to 20% of the misdeclared amount, and unverified zero-rated sales are retrospectively reclassified as taxable at the 10% standard VAT rate with full additional penalties. For non-filed or improperly issued electronic tax invoices, additional penalties of up to 2% of the undisclosed transaction amount may apply. The NTS also refers serious cases of cross-border invoice fraud to the Supreme Prosecutors' Office for criminal investigation, with potential imprisonment for value-added tax evasion and fraudulent export certificate usage.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). The Korean Won is fully convertible for current account transactions, including professional services, royalties, and other ordinary trade payments, which may be settled without prior administrative approval. Capital account transactions are largely liberalised but subject to mandatory reporting, verification, and in certain cases prior declaration. For a Chusik Hoesa in the medical device regulatory consulting space, the key compliance boundary is the requirement to file a Foreign Investment Report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance or through a designated foreign exchange bank when receiving cross-border equity or loan injections from non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is permitted after tax clearance and must be wired through a Korean foreign exchange bank utilising a current account purpose code. While Korea does not maintain quantitative foreign exchange restrictions, banks enforce substantive documentary checks to confirm the underlying business reality. Companies must ensure that all export of services is supported by contracts, invoices, and proof of actual service delivery; the lack of a true commercial receipt can result in administrative fines or cancellation of foreign exchange bank transactions. In practice, no offshore fintech or unregistered electronic money institution can legally substitute for a licensed Korean bank in processing KRW settlements or cross-border payments. The convertibility regime is therefore open but heavily evidence-driven, requiring treasury teams to maintain flawless documentation trails for every cross-border flow to avoid de facto freezing of funds during bank compliance reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 15 and related FETA regulations, Korean residents must receive export proceeds for goods or services within the prescribed deadline, which is generally 18 months from the date of the customs export declaration or the service performance date. There is no mandatory conversion obligation for export foreign currency receipts; exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency deposit accounts at domestic foreign exchange banks, provided the proceeds are actually repatriated into Korea and reported to the receiving bank. If the receipt deadline cannot be met, the entity must file a delayed receipt report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through a designated bank, supported by documentary evidence of the cause. Failure to comply may trigger administrative fines and intensified AML scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (implementing authority for the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with policy oversight by the Ministry of Economy and Finance)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Not viable. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or other unlicensed payment platforms cannot act as foreign exchange banks in South Korea. They are neither permitted to hold KRW denominated deposits in the resident company's name nor to process cross-border trade settlements under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Opening corporate treasury through such an EMI would create an unresolved legal gap in FIRE reporting, capital verification, and tax filing, and would expose the entity to severe AML penalties. All KRW clearing and foreign currency conversion must be routed through a licensed domestic bank participating in the BOK-Wire+ and the SWIFT network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders should remit equity investment funds through a foreign exchange bank using the purpose code for foreign direct investment in a Korean corporation. The funds must first enter a designated foreign currency deposit account, followed by the filing of a foreign investment report (FIRE) with the Ministry of Economy and Finance via the bank. After the report is acknowledged and the invested capital is verified against the newly issued shares of the Chusik Hoesa, the foreign currency can be converted into KRW through the bank's FX desk. For non-equity injections, such as shareholder loans, the company must separately file a loan report under FETA with the term, interest rate, and repayment schedule clearly documented. No inbound payment should be consolidated or passed through third-party intermediaries, as Korean banks require the wire originator to match the foreign shareholder named in the FIRE report.For payments made by the Korean entity to overseas clients or suppliers, the clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for service fees and operating expenses, using the standard purpose code for export of consulting services or cross-border commission. For procurement of medical device prototypes, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are legitimate rails, but are usually applicable only when the transaction involves physical goods and bill of lading documentation. Domestic KRW settlement of the service fee should be handled through BOK-Wire+ with a Korean counterparty, while outbound overseas disbursements require prior bank confirmation that the underlying contract and invoicing are consistent with the FIRE business scope. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national corporate income tax for taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion, plus a 4.5% local income tax surtax on the national tax amount, producing an aggregate nominal burden of 21.945%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax), subject to lower rates under an applicable Double Tax Treaty only if the procedural relief conditions are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared when annual cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 50 billion; a Master File is mandatory when annual cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 100 billion. Transactions below those file thresholds still require the transfer pricing schedule and arm's length substantiation in the corporate income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the National Tax Service applies a strong substantive-taxation doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Substance-Over-Form rule. For treaty relief and transfer pricing defense, the Korean entity must maintain real Core Income-Generating Activities, including substantive personnel, management decisions, office premises, and business risk in Korea. An offshore intermediary inserted into the FDA/CE consulting value chain solely to book margin will be disregarded, with profit re-allocated to the Korean Chusik Hoesa.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Cross-Border Consulting Services

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may reassess taxable income under the arm's length principle of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and impose additional corporate income tax, local income tax surcharges, an understatement penalty of up to 40% of the underreported tax, and a daily late payment surcharge until the full amount is settled.

Director personal liability: The managing director and financial officer who approved the transfer pricing documentation can be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes under the National Tax Collection Act; criminal investigation may follow, leading to the corporate veil being pierced where the company was used as an artifice to evade tax, with travel restrictions and blacklisting by the National Tax Service, and potential imprisonment for fraudulent tax evasion.
Fraudulent R&D and Tax Incentive Claims Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The targeted R&D credits are denied, the tax base is recomputed with a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, and the related national taxes are collected with daily 0.022% late payment surcharges. The act constitutes tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, carrying imprisonment of up to three years and a fine of up to three times the amount of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false corporate tax return cannot hide behind the corporate entity; Korean courts may disregard the corporate form where the entity was a mere instrumentality of tax fraud, exposing directors' personal assets to restitution, and the National Tax Service may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and blacklisting of the representatives while criminal proceedings are pending.
Shell Company and Treaty-Shopping Abuse on Outbound Dividends

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service disregards the conduit for lack of beneficial ownership, applies the statutory 22% withholding on the gross distribution, and imposes the failure-to-withhold penalty equal to 100% of the uncollected withholding tax in aggravated cases, plus the local income tax and daily surcharges. The intermediary's treaty residence claim is rejected under domestic anti-abuse rules and the OECD BEPS nexus doctrine.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly internationalized the dividend flow to escape Korean tax are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax as statutory withholding agents; personal civil restitution of the avoided tax, departure restrictions through the Ministry of Justice, blacklisting as delinquent taxpayers, and criminal imprisonment of up to five years for unlawful evasion through fraudulent structures may be imposed.

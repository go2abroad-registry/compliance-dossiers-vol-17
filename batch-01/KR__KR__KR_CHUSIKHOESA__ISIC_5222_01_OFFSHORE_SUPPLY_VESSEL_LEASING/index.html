Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None; the Korean Commercial Act no longer imposes a minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, local banking practice and the business registration procedure require the full subscription amount, usually KRW 100,000,000 or another stated amount contained in the Articles of Incorporation, to be deposited and retained in a blocked escrow share-subscription account at a licensed Korean bank until the incorporation registration is legally completed. After registration, corporate tax filings and maritime license reviews require proof of capital to support asset acquisition and liability.

Funding & unlock path: All crew members must hold valid STCW certificates and Korean or internationally recognized seafarer identity documents. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, acting through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal (hometax.go.kr). The Foreign Investment Ombudsman and the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) support foreign entities with registration formalities.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that engages in a taxable business activity in Korea must appoint a resident tax representative in accordance with the VAT Act, file a Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration Application through the HomeTax portal with the responsible district tax office, and attach a notarized copy of the foreign corporate registration certificate, a certificate of existence issued by the company registrar, a power of attorney appointing the tax representative, and the foreign company's home-country tax identification number. The district tax office reviews the application and issues a Korean Business Registration Number for VAT and withholding purposes. For a domestic Chusik Hoesa formed in Korea, the company must first complete incorporation registration at the local court registry in accordance with the Commercial Act, then submit the Business Registration Application to the competent district tax office via HomeTax within 20 days of the start of business. The NTS then assigns a Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and Business Registration Certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity that engages in a taxable business activity in Korea must appoint a resident tax representative in accordance with the VAT Act, file a Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration Application through the HomeTax portal with the responsible district tax office, and attach a notarized copy of the foreign corporate registration certificate, a certificate of existence issued by the company registrar, a power of attorney appointing the tax representative, and the foreign company's home-country tax identification number. The district tax office reviews the application and issues a Korean Business Registration Number for VAT and withholding purposes. For a domestic Chusik Hoesa formed in Korea, the company must first complete incorporation registration at the local court registry in accordance with the Commercial Act, then submit the Business Registration Application to the competent district tax office via HomeTax within 20 days of the start of business. The NTS then assigns a Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and Business Registration Certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Chusik Hoesa is categorically under entity-level corporate income taxation with no check-the-box pass-through election. Korea's hybrid mismatch rules are anti-avoidance provisions, not elective regimes. No territorial or tax-exempt-zone election applies to a standard offshore vessel-leasing stock corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover exemption. VAT registration is compulsory for every business operator supplying taxable goods or services within Korea. A domestically incorporated Chusik Hoesa must register from the first day of business. A simplified taxpayer regime with lower compliance obligations is available only to individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable turnover of KRW 80,000,000 or less; corporations are always required to operate under the standard VAT system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying services are zero-rated under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act. For the export of goods, zero-rating is substantiated by the certified customs export declaration confirming that the goods left the territory of Korea, coupled with commercial and shipping documents evidencing the physical movement. International leasing or chartering of vessels used in offshore support services qualifies as a zero-rated export of a service when the vessel is supplied to a foreign purchaser and used entirely outside the customs territory of Korea, or when the charter is for international navigation. For offshore vessel leasing, the statutory criteria require the supply to be rendered under a written charter agreement with a non-resident counterparty, the vessel to be positioned outside Korean territorial waters, and the invoicing to be made in foreign currency. Qualifying supporting service charges directly related to the export charter, such as vessel operating and crewing services provided to the foreign charterer, may also be zero-rated if separately evidenced. Entitlement to 0% VAT is denied if any part of the supply remains taxable in Korea, the customer is resident in Korea, or the goods or vessel are declared for domestic use.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Export/Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean carrier freight evidence
  • Certificate of Foreign Exchange Acquisition issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Remittance Receipt showing payment from the non-resident charterer
  • Written offshore vessel charter agreement
  • Commercial invoice denominated in foreign currency

All VAT-registered supplies between businesses must be documented with an Electronic Tax Invoice transmitted digitally through the National Tax Service HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or via a service provider certified by the NTS under the e-invoice API protocol. Each e-invoice receives a unique document confirmation/report number upon NTS clearance. The adversarial anti-fraud digital stamping is embedded through the HomeTax integrated validation and real-time transmission obligation; VAT-liable businesses must transmit the full invoice content to the NTS within the day after the date of issuance, and failure to issue or transmit is subject to a surcharge of up to 2% of the supply value. Online and foreign companies supplying digital or offshore services to Korean users may incur an additional electronic service registration and digital stamping obligation under the VAT same-service rules.

The National Tax Service scrutinizes export zero-rating through cross-matching of customs export declarations, exchange bank spreadsheets, corporate bank accounts, and the e-tax invoice system. If the export proceeds are not repatriated into Korea within 180 days from the date of the customs export declaration, the zero-rated supply is reclassified as taxable, and the VAT must be assessed retroactively. False, overstated, or unverifiable zero-rated claims trigger the imposition of the unpaid VAT plus additional tax penalties under the Framework Act on Taxes: an administrative penalty of 40% of the dishonest underreported tax liability, or 10% negligence surcharge where there is no fraudulent intent. Where fraudulent means such as forged export declarations or fictitious bills of lading are detected, the Korean PPSs Punishment of Tax Evaders Act applies, allowing criminal penalties of up to three years' imprisonment and fines of up to KRW 50,000,000. The NTS can also impose VAT amounts on subsequent export claims and claw back input VAT credits previously refunded, together with interest at the statutory penalty rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-determined floating won rate under a fundamentally convertible or liberalized foreign exchange system. Current account transactions are largely free from licensing requirements; however, capital account transactions remain subject to the negative list system and require reporting or approval through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Profits and dividends may be remitted overseas after corporate tax clearance and can be converted at domestic banks, but strict evidentiary documentation such as board minutes, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and underlying transaction contracts is demanded. For offshore supply vessel leasing, charter hire flows are treated as current account receipts, which are generally collectible into a foreign currency settlement account without prior approval; yet surrender-related conversion to KRW may trigger transaction proof. Capital inflows for paid-in capital under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act must be notified and reported through the bank and MOEF, and the actual receipt must match the reported amount. The principal obstacles to convertibility are documentary rather than legal, involving purpose codes, real-transaction evidence, and anti-money laundering and sanctions screening. Therefore, treasury planning must integrate Korean bank compliance, full tax clearance, and purpose-code routing to achieve smooth current account and profit remittance convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related MOEF notices, export proceeds from the offshore vessel chartering activity must be received into a resident foreign currency account within 18 months from the date of actual shipment or service completion unless an extension is granted. The statutory timeline is 18 months. Surrender is achieved by conversion to KRW at the market rate through a licensed foreign exchange bank, while holding foreign currency in-country remains permissible for reinvestment or working capital. Export proceeds require a matching export and transaction declaration under the Foreign Exchange Information System and documentary support for bank certification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central banking authority, with foreign exchange administration jointly executed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and monitored by the Korea Customs Service for offshore trade settlement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not structurally viable as primary onshore banking rails in South Korea. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank EMI cannot provide Korean won deposit accounts, perform currency exchange, or intermediate foreign direct investment capital repatriation for a resident Chusik Hoesa. They may serve only a narrow auxiliary role in receipt tracking or non-KRW expense pre-processing, but all KRW-denominated treasury settlements must be executed through a domestic licensed bank. Feasibility rating is Low as principal clearing infrastructure and Medium as a secondary payable management overlay if funds ultimately flow through a licensed bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal corporate income tax rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300,000,000,000, applied through progressive brackets of 9% (up to KRW 200,000,000), 19% (KRW 200,000,000 to KRW 20,000,000,000), 21% (KRW 20,000,000,000 to KRW 300,000,000,000), and 24% (above KRW 300,000,000,000); a 10% local income tax surcharge raises the top effective combined marginal rate to 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under the Corporate Income Tax Act, plus a 2% local income tax surcharge, producing a combined effective rate of 22%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50,000,000,000 (annual aggregate foreign-related transactions) triggers mandatory Local File / comprehensive transfer pricing documentation; KRW 100,000,000,000 (annual consolidated revenue) triggers the Master File; a simplified overview document is required for foreign-related transactions exceeding KRW 100,000,000 with any single related party

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a statutory substance-over-form principle under the Framework Act on National Taxes, disregarding artificial transactions, sham entities, and intermediary vehicles interposed without genuine business purpose. Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules attribute undistributed income of a foreign subsidiary to a Korean shareholder where the foreign effective tax rate is below 15%. The Tonnage Tax regime imposes strict operational substance conditions, including actual vessel ownership, flag registration, commercial management in Korea, crew employment, and real charter operations; a paper company or bare nominee leasing structure without genuine maritime substance will be disqualified and re-subjected to the ordinary corporate income tax regime with penalties

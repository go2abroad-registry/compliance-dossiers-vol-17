For a low-risk online cosmetics and beauty DTC retailer, a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox will not satisfy the Commercial Act requirement that the head office and actual principal management center be located in Korea. The National Tax Service may refuse or later revoke VAT and business registration if the address is an unoccupied mailbox, and Korean banks in their AML/CDD procedures will generally reject an account where no physical operation, staff, lease, or inventory handling can be verified. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the Company must issue at least one share, the par value of each share must be at least KRW 100, and the full amount of the issued and subscribed share capital must be deposited into a dedicated capital contribution escrow account opened with a licensed commercial bank in the Republic of Korea before the incorporation registration will be accepted. The funds are blocked and cannot be withdrawn until the court registry has completed the incorporation registration. If a non-resident subscriber makes a foreign-currency contribution, a report of foreign investment or foreign exchange declaration must be completed with the relevant foreign exchange bank, and the inward remittance must be evidenced before the bank issues the capital deposit custody certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Not required at the formation stage for a pure online retail head office and administrative premises. If the Company later opens a physical beauty counter, warehouse, or distribution center, it will require a building fire safety inspection and Fire Facility Certification under the Firefighting Facilities Installation, Maintenance and Safety Management Act, a certificate of occupancy under the Building Act, and a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act before operation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic administration portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Chusik Hoesa, the representative director must apply for a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, or through HomeTax, within 20 days of the business start date. Required documents include the corporate registration certificate issued by the Korean court registry, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership evidence for the domestic place of business, and the representative director's identification. The BRN simultaneously serves as the corporate tax identification number and VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean place of business that supplies electronic services to Korean consumers or derives Korean-source income must register separately through HomeTax under the foreign business operator registration procedure; such entity must submit evidence of incorporation from its home jurisdiction, provide beneficial owner and representative details, and if required by NTS administrative guidance, appoint a domestic tax representative for tax correspondence and compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; Korea does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime and does not provide domestic elective classification in the manner available in some other jurisdictions. A Korean Chusik Hoesa is automatically a resident corporate taxpayer subject to entity-level tax; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through the International Tax Adjustment Act and related anti-hybrid rules, but no elective mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for corporations; any business operator supplying taxable goods or services in Korea, including a Chusik Hoesa, must register for VAT before commencing business. For individual business operators only, annual taxable supplies below KRW 80 million qualify for the simplified taxable person regime; corporations are always general taxable persons regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported by the Korean seller and cleared through Korea Customs Service, provided the goods are shipped to a foreign buyer and title and possession pass outside Korea. Qualifying online retail export sales of cosmetics may be zero-rated when the seller retains a complete electronic trail from order, payment in foreign currency or settlement, customs export declaration, and transport documentation proving actual removal of the goods from Korea. Services are zero-rated only where the Korean law provides a specific statutory zero-rating rule; for this beauty DTC retail business, goods export zero-rating is the relevant mechanism.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration as recorded in the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system
  • Customs export clearance record or electronic customs receipt issued by Korea Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight
  • Signed proof of delivery or arrival notice confirming delivery to the overseas buyer
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or foreign exchange bank receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

All corporations must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system and transmit the issuance data to the NTS by the day following issuance. Electronic tax invoice data is submitted through NTS-certified electronic invoice service providers integrated with HomeTax; there is no separate physical anti-fraud digital stamp, but the NTS assigns a receiving number to each electronic tax invoice transmission. For B2C cash payments, the online cosmetics retailer must issue and transmit cash receipts through the approved NTS cash receipt system. Failure to enroll, issue, or transmit e-tax invoices within the statutory timeline attracts additional tax penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act.

NTS reconciles export zero-rating claims against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations, electronic tax invoices, bills of lading, and foreign exchange settlement records. If documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, the zero-rated export is reversed and VAT is assessed at the standard 10% rate together with statutory penalty tax, usually 10% of the unpaid tax, and overdue interest. Knowingly issuing or arranging false electronic tax invoices or fraudulent export zero-rating claims may trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and ancillary additional penalties for false tax invoice issuance. Retrospective clawback assessments may be raised within the general five-year statute of limitations for tax evasion and false reporting cases, with any excess VAT refund recovered through error correction and collection procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current account regime for trade, services, and routine business remittances; the Korean won is fully convertible for genuine current account transactions such as import settlement, service fees, and dividend distributions, subject to documentary review by a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, loan borrowings, and investments in local real estate or securities, remain tightly controlled and require a prior or post-reporting obligation under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Non-resident shareholders must complete an inward foreign investment notification before or within 30 days after the capital remittance, and the resulting foreign investment registration certificate serves as the core capital verification document for the domestic Chusik Hoesa. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may maintain their foreign currency earnings in onshore foreign currency accounts at domestic banks, convert freely to KRW, or use those funds for approved purposes. Profit remittance hurdles include demonstrating tax clearance, submitting audited financial statements for the distribution, proving that the dividend is not a thinly capitalized funding return, and obtaining the bank's foreign exchange transaction confirmation. In practice, the bank will require a full KYC refresh of the beneficiary, supporting board resolution for the dividend declaration, and a purpose code consistent with shareholder remuneration. Any suspicious gap between the declared cosmetics e-commerce business and repeated high-volume royalty or management fee outflows triggers enhanced due diligence

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 12 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export, unless an extension is granted by the foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts at domestic Tier-1 banks or converted to US dollars or KRW. All export settlements must be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank using the appropriate export payment method code and reported through the Electronic Export Settlement System

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (Hanguk Eunhaeng), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors must remit funds from their overseas corporate or personal bank account through a SWIFT wire with the explicit foreign exchange purpose code for equity investment. Upon receipt at a Korean foreign exchange bank, the bank verifies the remitting party, counterparty names, and the filed FDI notification. The bank then issues the foreign investment registration certificate for the Chusik Hoesa, which is used for capital verification with the court registrar. Any resulting KRW proceeds must pass through a capital escrow account or a foreign investor deposit account before being deployed into operational accounts. For dividend remittance, the entity must hold a board resolution authorizing the distribution, tax withholding certificates showing final corporate tax and dividend withholding tax paid, audited financial statements, and the foreign investor's certificate of registration; these are submitted to the foreign exchange bank, which assigns the outward remittance purpose code for dividends and secures Bank of Korea reporting where thresholds apply.The primary rail for direct trade settlement is SWIFT MT103 for high-volume, low-documentation purchases, especially for freight, platform licensing, and raw material imports when the transaction value is below Bank of Korea threshold limits and the risk profile supports open account. For larger or strategic contracts, Korean banks advise an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) issued by the Chusik Hoesa's bank in favor of the foreign supplier, with negotiation terms in USD or KRW and payment against presentation of compliant shipping documents. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for established counterparty relationships, especially for cosmetics raw materials from Japan and China. For digital and marketplace receipts, Shopify Payments and PayPal settle through designated partner banks using aggregated SWIFT-cleared channels into the Korean operating account; these aggregated flows must be reconciled to platform-level sales statements to avoid anti-structuring triggers. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% to 24% progressive corporate income tax rate; the top marginal rate of 24% applies to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion (local income tax surtax of 10% on the national corporate tax liability applies, producing a combined headline rate of 26.4% at the top bracket).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding tax on resident entity dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding amount, producing an effective statutory aggregate rate of 22%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate tax return must be filed within three months from the end of the fiscal year (March 31 for a December 31 fiscal year-end); a tax base interim return is due within two months after the end of the first six months of the fiscal year; local income tax surcharge return is due on the same deadline as the national corporate tax return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and Master File are mandatory when the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with foreign related parties exceeds KRW 10 billion in the preceding fiscal year; a Simplified Local File (without Master File) is required when the aggregate amount exceeds KRW 5 billion but is KRW 10 billion or less. A transfer pricing report (Controlled Transaction Summary) is required only when the aggregate amount exceeds KRW 1 billion, subject to the NTS's authority to request full documentation for any amount.

Economic substance scope: The economic substance doctrine is codified under Korean tax law through the substance-over-form principle (National Tax Basic Act Article 14) and the anti-abuse rules in the Framework Act on Foreign Investment and the Corporate Tax Act. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in online retail, the NTS will disregard transactions lacking commercial substance, recharacterize intercompany arrangements, and deny deductions if the principal purpose is tax avoidance. Although South Korea does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE or BVI, the OECD base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) actions and the Korean international tax adjustment rules require the entity to maintain adequate substance (real office, employees with decision-making authority, risk-bearing capacity, and actual operational functions) to avoid the non-recognition of its foreign related-party transactions and potential controlled foreign company (CFC) taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years; fine up to KRW 50 million; additional negligence penalty of 20% of the understated tax; daily overdue surcharges; and disallowance of any tax refunds or carryforwards.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or order tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties; they face criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset seizures, and up to 5 years of imprisonment.
Shell Company Formation

Statutory sanction: The NTS may disregard the entity's separate legal personality under the substance-over-form principle, impose a 40% penalty on the tax amount evaded, and impose a fine of up to KRW 20 million; the entity may be struck off the commercial registry upon administrative order.

Director personal liability: Nominal or actual directors are jointly liable for all corporate taxes and penalties; they face criminal penalty for false registration (up to 3 years imprisonment) and personal liability under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies if financial statements were falsified.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million for violation of AML obligations; failure to file STR can result in a fine up to KRW 30 million; confiscation of criminal proceeds is mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to establish the compliance framework; they can face criminal imprisonment, fines, a ban from corporate management, and civil suits by the Financial Services Commission for damages.
Transfer Pricing Violation

Statutory sanction: The NTS will impose an arm's length adjustment increasing the Korean entity's taxable income and apply a 20% penalty on the understated tax; penalties for failing to furnish the transfer pricing documentation reach 5% of the controlled transaction amount and up to KRW 5 million per unfurnished document; an additional 0.022% per day overdue surcharge applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return with gross negligence are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; they may be held criminally liable for violation of the Act on Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes if the evasion exceeds KRW 500 million, carrying imprisonment up to life.

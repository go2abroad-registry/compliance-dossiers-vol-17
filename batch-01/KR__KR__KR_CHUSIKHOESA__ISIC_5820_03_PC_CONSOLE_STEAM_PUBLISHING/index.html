Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Korean Commercial Act, every stock corporation must have a principal office within the Republic of Korea and register that address with the competent district court registry. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may technically satisfy registration if a valid lease agreement and the right to use the address are documented, and some registered-agent services offer such addresses. However, the National Tax Service (NTS) and most Korean banks reject virtual offices for a profitable digital-publishing company; banks require an in-person visit, a physical signboard, and documentary proof of substantive management. The Korean tax authorities frequently cross-check the registered office address with business activity, lease contracts, and utility bills; a virtual-office address substantially increases the risk of open-ended tax investigations and the denial of corporate bank accounts. Therefore, virtual offices are permissible only as a temporary measure pending physical premises; for the planned Steam and PlayStation publishing activity, a physical office with at least one executive present is strongly recommended.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Commercial Act imposes no fixed statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the promoters may choose any positive aggregate subscription amount, subject to each par-value share being at least KRW 100. The full issue price of every subscribed share must be paid in cash into a bank contribution deposit account opened in the name of the company being formed before the incorporation registration is filed. The bank must issue a certificate of contribution deposit for presentation to the registration court. The deposit is not a government escrow; after registration, the funds become the company's corporate capital and are released to the operating bank account. Foreign investors who subscribe are subject to the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and must file a foreign investment report where the investment reaches KRW 100 million or more, with the report obtainable through the receiving bank or KOTRA's Invest Korea one-stop service. No share withdrawal or operating payment may be made from the contribution account before registration is completed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea; electronic compliance portal: HomeTax Integrated Tax Portal at hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing or owning a Korean Chusik Hoesa must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the local district tax office of the corporation's place of business through the HomeTax electronic filing system. The procedure comprises: (1) incorporation of the Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act and issue of the corporate registration certificate; (2) submission of the business registration application to the National Tax Service within 20 days of business commencement through HomeTax or directly at the competent tax office; (3) attendance of the articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, proof of business establishment, and identification of the statutory representative; (4) issuance by the NTS of a 10-digit Business Registration Number which serves simultaneously as the Tax Identification Number, Corporate Tax number, withholding account number, and Value-Added Tax identification number; and (5) for a foreign-owned Korean entity, appointment of a domestic tax representative if required under the Framework Act on National Taxes for service of process and tax correspondence. For a non-resident foreign corporation without a domestic establishment but supplying electronic services in Korea, the NTS applies the separate non-resident business registration function on HomeTax, assigning a VAT identification number and requiring a domestic tax representative where called for by the Value-Added Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All businesses supplying taxable goods or services in Korea are obliged to register for VAT; there is no general exemption threshold for registered corporations, but simplified taxpayer treatment may apply where annual taxable turnover does not exceed KRW 80,000,000. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services, including digital game publishing and platform-based sales, are required to register for VAT through HomeTax regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating under the Value-Added Tax Act applies to supplies of exported goods, to services supplied to non-resident foreign recipients for use outside Korea, and to qualifying electronic services supplied to non-resident consumers, including downloadable PC/console game content sold through platforms such as Steam and PlayStation Store. To substantiate the zero rate, the Korean vendor must prove that the customer is a foreign resident, the service is treated as consumed abroad, and the export sales proceeds are actually collected in foreign currency and legally repatriated to Korea. The statutory evidence required includes customs export declarations for physical goods and, for digital publishing, platform payout statements and foreign exchange settlement records showing repatriation of the remittances from Valve, Sony Interactive Entertainment, or similar platform operators.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Declaration Certificate issued through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physically exported goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List in international sales format
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Steam or PlayStation Store sales reconciliation and settlement reports showing transactions by country, transaction identifiers, net amounts, and payer details
  • Electronic evidence of the foreign customer's residence, including billing address, payment instrument country, and digital delivery records

Korean VAT law mandates the issuance of an Electronic Tax Invoice for every business-to-business tax invoice transaction. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax e-invoice system by the end of the day following issuance, which operates as a near-real-time fiscal clearance and cross-matching requirement. Taxpayers may transmit via HomeTax directly or through NTS-approved EDI/API interfaces. Korea does not use a cryptographic anti-fraud tax stamp; instead, the NTS enforces the mandate through e-invoice matching, mandatory cash receipt issuance for final consumers, and periodic VAT return verification.

The National Tax Service audits zero-rated export claims by cross-referencing customs export records, foreign exchange settlement data, and digital platform payout records. Where documentary substantiation is missing or inadequate, the NTS assesses VAT at the statutory 10% rate with default additional tax and interest from the original filing period. Fraudulent or misleading claims trigger penalty taxes up to 40% of the avoided VAT, voiding of refund positions, extension of the clawback statute of limitations to 10 years, and potential criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but strictly reporting-based foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, supervised by the Bank of Korea. The Korean won is freely convertible for bona fide current account transactions, including royalties from Steam and PlayStation Store sales, commission payments, and dividend remittances, provided the remitting bank receives an underlying contract and a valid foreign exchange purpose code. Capital account transactions are not freely convertible: any non-resident equity injection, shareholder loan, or acquisition of local securities must be reported through a domestic dealing bank to the Bank of Korea, and transactions above the statutory threshold require prior approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The Bank of Korea does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds; however, all foreign currency receipts must be collected into a Korean foreign exchange bank account within the statutory collection period. Profit repatriation from a Chusik Hoesa is permitted after corporate income tax has been paid, evidenced by a tax clearance certificate and a board resolution declaring the dividend, but the remitting bank must verify the original foreign investment report, the withholding tax payment, and the equivalent capital account reporting before releasing the proceeds to the overseas shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received into a Korean foreign exchange bank account within six months from the export date for goods, or within six months from the completion of services for digital publishing. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio to KRW is 0%, meaning the exporter may hold the foreign currency without any requirement to surrender it to the Bank of Korea or any state-designated bank. The collecting bank must verify the export customs declaration, the platform settlement statement, the SWIFT credit advice, and the commercial invoice. If the six-month window cannot be met, an extension must be requested from the head of the dealing bank, and any extension exceeding the approval authority must be submitted to the Bank of Korea.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), the principal foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs without a Bank of Korea foreign exchange banking license are legally barred from serving as the primary treasury institution for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. A Korean entity's inbound SWIFT payments and outbound trade remittances must clear through a resident dealing bank, and the foreign exchange reporting obligations of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act cannot be fulfilled by a non-bank EMI. Such providers may be considered only as ancillary collection tools, but the full account infrastructure, capital escrow, and FX hedging must remain with domestically licensed tier-1 commercial banks.

The SWIFT wire must include the proper purpose code, for example Investment - Equity Capital, together with the entity's corporate registration number. After the funds are credited to the capital escrow account, the bank issues a foreign direct investment report certificate and the company files the paid-in capital registration with the Korean court. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million) or more in total gross revenue; however, a mandatory Local File must be prepared if the corporation's related party cross-border transactions exceed or are expected to exceed KRW 10 billion in aggregate, and Master File preparation is mandatory when the revenue is KRW 100 billion or more and the intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the economic substance doctrine through Article 15 of the Framework Act on Local Taxation and the corporate income tax anti-avoidance rules in the Corporate Income Tax Act, including the substance-over-form principle. For international tax matters, the OECD BEPS actions and a general anti-abuse rule deny treaty benefits to structures lacking business purpose and economic substance. Additionally, a Korean resident or domestic corporation must report to the Ministry of Economy and Finance any foreign financial account with a balance of KRW 500 million or more, and a shell company or conduit entity without substantial economic activities may be disregarded for treaty relief under the Limitation on Benefits clause or the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of imprisonment up to 10 years, or a fine up to 3 times the evaded tax amount (Optionally up to 50 times the annual tax amount), plus underreporting penalties of 10% to 40% of the unreported tax and a 3.3% annual surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally or negligently participated may be personally jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act and the National Tax Collection Act; the court may impose criminal imprisonment; travel bans may be issued by the Commissioner of the National Tax Service; and the director's personal assets may be subject to collection.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding tax relief, recharacterization of the transaction, 40% penalty on the understated tax amount, and cancellation of the corporate registration. Under the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination, the National Tax Service may impose a 30% penalty on the amount of tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the reconstruction of income and the payment of aggregate penalties; if the shell company is used to launder funds, anti-money laundering sanctions apply with imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible officers; courts may issue asset freezes against the directors and order the disclosure and confiscation of hidden proceeds.
AML and Foreign Account Reporting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 200 million for foreign account reporting violations; under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act, the violation attracts additional sanctions and the proceeds of the crime may be confiscated.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is subject to the criminal fines or imprisonment; the National Tax Service may impose a personal entity tax liability for assets owned by the director if corporate assets are diverted; a travel ban and departure prohibition under the Immigration Act will apply during tax and criminal investigation.
Fraudulent Misrepresentation in Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to KRW 100 million; the false declaration triggers a 40% penalty on the underreported tax, plus 10% of the falsely claimed tax credits; and revocation of beneficial tax treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false filing may be personally liable for the penalty and the additional corporate tax; criminal conviction may bar the director from serving as a registered officer of any company for up to 5 years; civil recovery of the government damages may be pursued in addition to the criminal fines.

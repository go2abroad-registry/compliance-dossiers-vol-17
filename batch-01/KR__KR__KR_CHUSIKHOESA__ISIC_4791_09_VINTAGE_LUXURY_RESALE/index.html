Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Commercial Act, a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) has no statutory minimum paid-in capital. At least one share must be issued, and paid-in capital equals the aggregate par value of the subscribed shares. The total subscription price must be fully deposited with a commercial bank in the Republic of Korea before filing the incorporation registration, and a bank certificate of deposit must be attached. There is no legally mandated blocked escrow account for domestic paid-in capital. A non-resident shareholder must report the investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and furnish the foreign-investment report receipt to the bank; the deposited funds remain usable only after registration and bank account activation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover registration threshold applies to a Chusik Hoesa: any legal entity engaging in taxable business activity in Korea must register for VAT regardless of sales amount. For sole proprietors only, an annual turnover under KRW 80,000,000 may qualify for simplified VAT taxpayer treatment; this status is not available to a Chusik Hoesa stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rated export supply applies to used and vintage luxury goods sold by the Korean Chusik Hoesa to foreign buyers when the goods are physically delivered or transported out of South Korea under Korea Customs Service export clearance, title and risk pass to the foreign purchaser, and settlement is received in convertible foreign currency. Zero-rating is also available when goods are supplied through overseas consignment warehouses, provided the export declaration is executed by the Korean seller and actual export occurs within the statutory period. For online escrow sales, escrow release to the Korean entity must be supported by foreign-currency inbound remittance evidence, not domestic Won settlement alone.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) generated through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Korean port of loading and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and consignment/escrow transaction report
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC/FIRC) or bank statement of covered receipt in foreign currency
  • Foreign buyer identity and order documentation for vintage luxury resale, including brand authentication report and payment authorization

All VAT-registered corporations must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system or an NTS-certified ASP/API integration for every taxable supply of goods or services; the electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the issuance date. The NTS assigns an electronic tax invoice number to prevent duplication and reuse, and the data is automatically matched to the VAT return computation. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice is treated as non-compliance with penalty surcharges.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may re-characterize the supply as domestic-rated if the export documentation bundle, foreign exchange receipt, and escrow settlement trail are incomplete. The taxpayer will be assessed 10% VAT on the supply value plus additional tax, generally 10% of unpaid VAT for non-payment or late reporting, and up to 20%-40% under penalty provisions for negligent or fraudulent underreporting in serious cases, with interest for late payment. Retrospective clawback of input VAT and criminal referral may follow for fraudulent zero-rating claims, and foreign exchange repatriation beyond 180 days without an approved extension triggers additional customs and foreign exchange penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a split and asymmetric convertibility framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, including payments for cross-border trade in vintage luxury goods, consignment fees, freight, insurance and after-sales service, are generally treated as free transactions, but must be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank and are subject to factual reporting through the bank's foreign exchange information system. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity subscriptions into a Chusik Hoesa, shareholder loans and outbound portfolio investments, are not fully free; they require prior report, post-report, or certificate-based validation depending on the economic substance and size. The Korean won is not a fully internationally clearable currency; offshore onshore transfer arrangements are restricted, and any non-resident won account or offshore won repositioning must be justified and reported. Profit remittance to a foreign shareholder is permissible after the audited financial statements are approved at the shareholders' meeting and corporate income tax and dividend withholding obligations are settled. A non-resident shareholder is subject to dividend withholding at up to 22% unless a lower rate is proven under the relevant tax treaty; post-withholding remittance is normally accepted without a further FX licence. There is no mandatory cession of export proceeds to the central bank, but foreign currency receipts must be collected and repatriated to an account in Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank. The practical obstacle to convertibility is not an absolute prohibition but the documentary burden, valuation reconciliation, and the strict enforcement of anti-money-laundering and trade-data matching requirements. Therefore, real-time treasury planning must embed reporting, purpose-code discipline, and physical documentary handling rather than assume frictionless online convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 14 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds for goods shipped from Korea, including vintage luxury items sold online to overseas customers, must be received and repatriated in a recognized form to a domestic foreign exchange bank within 18 months from the date of export declaration. If the underlying trade contract stipulates a longer credit period, the exporter may apply for an exceptional extension before expiry. There is currently no mandatory surrender ratio: an exporter may retain up to 100% of receipts in a foreign-currency account at a licensed bank, or convert the proceeds into Korean won at the going market rate. The statutory surrender ratio is therefore effectively zero percent, while the repatriation and deposit obligation is absolute. Any sale of received foreign currency must be handled through a licensed foreign exchange business operator, and the conversion must be reported in the bank's surveillance system. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window can result in administrative fines, customs penalty reports, and audit flags under the Foreign Trade Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); foreign exchange supervision is administratively delegated by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and operated through licensed foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI viability is very low to non-viable. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only foreign exchange business operators approved or licensed by the Korean authorities can provide accounts and payment-clearing services to a resident entity. A Chusik Hoesa cannot lawfully open an operating account with an unlicensed offshore EMI, and using such an EMI for treasury settlement would breach Korean foreign exchange law and create a serious AML defect. Offshore EMIs may only be used as front-end payment card acquirers or website checkout connectors if the ultimate settlement flows through a licensed tier-1 Korean bank. They cannot serve as the corporate liquidity rail, capital escrow holder, or trade settlement bank. Therefore the treasury blueprint must place all primary cash management, FX clearance, and escrow functions with a top-tier domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed through a designated domestic foreign exchange bank using an international SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire, with the remitter bank exactly matching the foreign shareholder name. The purpose code must reflect an equity contribution to the Korean entity, and the beneficiary account should be a temporary capital escrow account established specifically for the injection. After receipt, the bank verifies the amount and issues a foreign capital receipt; the company then completes the post-report or prior-report process under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Capital verification is evidenced by the bank's currency exchange confirmation, a certificate of foreign investment or capital registration report, and board minutes recording the share issuance. No crypto, third-party intercompany offset or cash-surrender mechanism is permitted.Outbound trade disbursements for vintage luxury inventory procurement, consignment settlement, and operational expenses must be made exclusively from a licensed bank. For standard low-value vendor payments, a SWIFT MT103 with precise remittance information, invoice reference, country of destination, and the applicable foreign exchange reporting purpose code is the primary clearing rail. For high-value procurements from European or Japanese wholesalers, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP600 is preferred, supported by signed commercial invoice, authenticity certificate, packing list, and transport documents. Documentary collections at sight or with tenor are acceptable only for established counterparties with proven trade history. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate (progressive brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% KRW 200M–20B; 21% KRW 20B–300B; 24% over KRW 300B, plus 10% local income tax surcharge making combined top effective rate 26.4%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding of 20% on dividend distributions to non-residents plus a 2% local income tax surcharge, yielding an aggregate 22% withholding tax on outbound dividends (Corporate Tax Act Article 93 and Local Income Tax Act Article 103).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year pursuant to Article 60 of the Corporate Tax Act; a permitted one-month extension is available upon NTS approval, and foreign-invested companies with statutory audits may obtain an additional 1-month extension in limited circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File must be prepared and filed if annual revenue is at least KRW 100 billion AND cross-border related-party transaction value is at least KRW 10 billion; a simplified transfer pricing report (per Korea's Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 11) is required when the total cross-border related-party transaction amount is KRW 3 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply an OECD-style ESR/CIGA regime applicable to zero-tax jurisdictions, but economic substance is assessed through the general anti-abuse rule and controlled foreign company provisions. For an online luxury resale business, substance is evidenced by rigorous physical authentication centers in Korea, local buyer and seller contracts governed by Korean law, actual decision-making of valuation specialists in Korea, and management of the Escrow Pay settlement bank accounts in Korea; absence of such substance triggers treaty denial and CFC taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Article 10(3) of the Tax Evasion Punishment Act: imprisonment up to 10 years or fines up to KRW 100 million, plus NTS penalty of 40% of fraudulently evaded tax and daily interest of 0.03% on the unpaid amount; additionally, the proceeds of the evasion are subject to compulsory collection and asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under Civil Act Article 750 for tortious damages jointly with the company, including restitution of the evaded amount; NTS imposes travel bans (blacklisting) and freezing of domestic and foreign bank accounts; criminal conviction of a directing officer under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act can result in imprisonment up to 10 years, and the director's personal assets are subject to compensatory civil execution.
Transfer Pricing Contravention / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 5 and 6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, NTS may re-determine taxable income at arm's length and impose additional tax equal to 5% of the adjusted income; failure to submit TP documentation gives a penalty of up to 10% of the transaction amount or an additional 30% of the underreported income, plus interest surcharges at the statutory rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or signed false TP documentation can be held personally liable under Commercial Act Article 399 for losses sustained by the company from NTS penalties, including disgorgement of personal bonuses; criminal referral for submission of false reports to a government agency may lead to imprisonment up to 3 years and travel restrictions while under investigation.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information carry criminal fines up to KRW 20 million for natural persons and business sanctions including revocation of the payment affiliate registration; for serious violations, the Financial Intelligence Unit may order suspension of the entity's currency exchange or escrow licenses and impose additional administrative fines up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who were complicit in AML failures face imprisonment up to 3 years under the Act; the company's separate legal personality is pierced for money laundering predicates, and directors' personal assets can be seized for laundering proceeds; directors are subject to cross-border travel bans, addition to the Financial Supervisory Service's blacklist, and disqualification from serving as directors in any financial or e-commerce company.

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally impermissible as the registered seat of a Chusik Hoesa for the consumer survey platform because the Korean Commercial Act requires an actual physical place of business within the jurisdictional court's territorial district where statutory notices, books, and employee contracts can be inspected. The National Tax Service and commercial banks will require a physically signed lease agreement or full ownership title. The tax office will additionally require that all books, invoices, and employment records are accessible at that address on business days.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under Article 329(4) of the Commercial Act there is no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the amount is fixed freely in the Articles of Incorporation. The full subscription price must, however, be deposited in cash at a bank or financial institution before registration under Article 295, and the bank's certificate of subscription payment is mandatory evidence for the registration court. No blocked escrow account is required specifically for incorporation, but funds remitted from abroad by non-resident investors may require a Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before the deposit is accepted by a foreign exchange bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) via the HomeTax electronic tax portal (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS for export clearance

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of a Korean Chusik Hoesa, the entity must register with the National Tax Service and obtain a 10-digit business registration number, which functions as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration number. The statutory procedure is as follows: (1) complete the Korean incorporation and corporate registry process including the articles of incorporation and appointment of a representative director; (2) obtain the official certificate of corporate registry and evidence of the domestic place of business; (3) hold a board or shareholder resolution authorizing the person or firm that will sign and file the tax registration; (4) file the business registration application with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business, in accordance with the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, within 20 days of business commencement; (5) if the foreign owner or director is a foreign non-resident entity, appoint a domestic tax representative to receive service, file returns, pay tax, and receive refunds, and attach the tax representative's written authorization to the registration filing; (6) the NTS will then issue the business registration certificate and assign the business registration number through HomeTax. A foreign non-resident entity that makes a taxable supply in Korea outside the registered Chusik Hoesa, or through a Korean permanent establishment, must independently complete VAT registration using the same domestic tax representative procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not operate a check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for Chusik Hoesa corporations. Corporate income tax is imposed at entity level under the Corporate Income Tax Act, the simplified taxpayer regime under the VAT Act is not available to a Chusik Hoesa, and hybrid mismatch adjustments are applied mandatorily under Korean tax law without any entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to a Chusik Hoesa; VAT registration is mandatory for any profit-seeking business operator from business commencement. For non-corporate individual suppliers only, the VAT Act provides a simplified taxpayer regime when annual taxable turnover is below KRW 80,000,000 for services, but this simplified threshold is unavailable to a Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for zero-rating under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are moved out of Korea under a Korea Customs Service export declaration and the export price is received in foreign currency by a Korean foreign exchange bank. Qualifying offshore digital services, including market research and consumer panel survey SaaS, are zero-rated when the recipient is a foreign non-resident and the place of supply and actual place of use and consumption is outside Korea, evidenced by the recipient's business location, service agreement, and usage records. The zero-rating remains conditional on actual collection of the foreign exchange consideration within the statutory repatriation period; failure to collect within the period causes the supply to be recharacterized as taxable and subject to retrospective VAT assessment.

  • Export Declaration (Export Customs Clearance record on Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and supply contract or purchase order with the foreign customer
  • Packing list and export-related transport documents
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank, equivalent to an inward remittance certificate
  • Electronic tax invoice reflecting the zero-rated export supply

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS by the day following issuance, and the system performs automatic validation of the counterparty's business registration number, invoice fields, supply value, VAT amount, zero-rated supply value, and dates. Integrated ERP/API submission is permitted, and the NTS e-invoice system is the anti-fraud control layer; there is no separate fiscal printer or physical digital stamping regime. Export declarations are processed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS in real-time, and the NTS cross-matches export invoice data with customs clearance and foreign exchange settlement data.

The NTS may deny export zero-rating and claw back the full 10% VAT if the export fact, foreign exchange receipt, or qualifying documentation is not substantiated. Retroactive corrective assessments apply to the original VAT return period, plus daily statutory late-payment interest. Negligent underreporting is subject to an additional tax of 10% of the underpaid VAT, rising to 40% for fraudulent or intentional underreporting. False or forged export declarations and false electronic tax invoices are referred under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying criminal sanctions, fines, confiscation of illegally claimed VAT refunds, disqualification from the export VAT refund system, and joint liability for corporate representatives and related persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized exchange control system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is officially classified as freely convertible in the current account for trade of goods and services; however, actual settlement is administered through authorized foreign exchange banks and subject to document-based reporting to the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance. For capital account movements, the regime shifts from a negative-list notification framework to a prior-approval framework for particularly sensitive operations such as short-term offshore borrowing, Korean won cross-border deposits, and derivative transactions exceeding regulatory de minimis thresholds. Export proceeds are generally credited to foreign currency accounts and must be reported within the statutory declaration window; effectively, funds can be retained in U.S. dollars or other usable currencies for treasury management, but conversion into KRW for operational disbursements requires declaration of the underlying cross-border transaction. Profit remittances by a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa require a remittance report together with audited financials, tax payment certificates, and evidence of the paying commercial purpose; the Bank of Korea concurrently monitors large remittances through the Electronic Foreign Exchange Information System. In practice, current account payment to foreign market-research panelists is openly permissible, while intercompany capital repayment or dividend outflow must meet the substance-plus-documentation test. The practical barrier is not the absence of convertibility but the mandatory declaration and the need for a mainstream domestic bank account as the corridor for KRW clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 23 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the delegated Foreign Exchange Transaction Rules, a Korean resident exporter is required to collect and repatriate export-related foreign currency proceeds within the settlement maturity of the underlying contract, generally not exceeding 180 days from the date of export declaration or service completion, unless an extension is granted by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The current regime imposes a zero mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; export receipts may be held 100% in an authorized domestic foreign currency current account. Once the proceeds are credited, the exporter must notify the foreign exchange bank and complete the settlement reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information Network; if the treasurer chooses to convert the received currency into KRW, conversion is executed at the prevailing spot rate and must be accompanied by the relevant commercial purpose declaration. A failure to meet the 180-day collection timeline without obtaining an approved extension exposes the company to administrative penalties under the FETA enforcement decree.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (with supplementary regulatory oversight and policy formulation by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Foreign Exchange Information Network)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and legally restricted in South Korea. Non-bank fintech EMIs, including global digital EMI brands such as Wise or Revolut Business, may not independently issue Korean won settlement accounts or function as the primary corporate bank for a KR-licensed Chusik Hoesa. The Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act reserve demand deposit-taking and FX inward remittance services for authorized banks. A foreign EMI can only act as a complementary payout channel for lower-value cross-border panelist allowances, typically via a licensed partner-bank sponsored solution; the counterparty bank may require the same CDD and KYC evidence as the domestic tier-1 institution. The onboarding team should therefore plan for a local branch account as the clearing anchor and use the EMI solely for micro-disbursement aggregation. High-risk or unlicensed EMIs that are not registered under the Korean foreign exchange bank framework cannot be legally used for any FX settlement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally restricted in South Korea. Non-bank fintech EMIs, including global digital EMI brands such as Wise or Revolut Business, may not independently issue Korean won settlement accounts or function as the primary corporate bank for a KR-licensed Chusik Hoesa. The Banking Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act reserve demand deposit-taking and FX inward remittance services for authorized banks. A foreign EMI can only act as a complementary payout channel for lower-value cross-border panelist allowances, typically via a licensed partner-bank sponsored solution; the counterparty bank may require the same CDD and KYC evidence as the domestic tier-1 institution. The onboarding team should therefore plan for a local branch account as the clearing anchor and use the EMI solely for micro-disbursement aggregation. High-risk or unlicensed EMIs that are not registered under the Korean foreign exchange bank framework cannot be legally used for any FX settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion per fiscal year in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (Local File and Master File preparation threshold); Country-by-Country Report filing applies when a multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion. This tiered threshold requires contemporaneous records and disclosure of APAs, cost-sharing arrangements, and financial statements.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation, but economic substance remain decisive under the General Anti-Abuse Rule in the National Tax Basic Act, the Korean international tax anti-avoidance framework, and treaty beneficial-ownership jurisprudence. A Korean entity in the cross-border consumer survey SaaS model must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) are actually performed in Korea, such as media content creation, panel recruitment, data analytics, board decision-making, and risk management. If the entity is a mere conduit holding trademark or platform rights without Korean staff or decisive control, Korean tax authorities may disregard the shell, disallow treaty benefits, and reallocate income to the beneficial operator.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Taiwanese Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion, with penalties of imprisonment for up to 5 years or fines up to 10 times the tax evaded, or both. In addition, the National Tax Service imposes an unfaithful filing penalty of 20% of the underpaid tax plus 0.022% daily interest on the outstanding deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct intentionally false returns may be subject to criminal imprisonment. Under Korean Commercial Act Article 401, directors are jointly and severally liable for damages caused to the company by an intentional or negligent violation of the law. Tax authorities can also impose joint liability on directors for taxes when the company is the instrumental tax evasion vehicle, and can issue Personal Tax Clearance certificates or travel blacklists for unpaid national taxes.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Permanent Establishment Peeling

Statutory sanction: Articles 6 and 7 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs authorize the National Tax Service to disregard or recharacterize transactions, make arm's length redeterminations, and impute income based on deemed functions, assets, and risks. Auditor's adjustments can be assessed as additional corporate tax plus penalty surcharges of 10-20% of underreported tax and daily interest, and a 30% assessment against related non-compliance can be imposed under domestic penalty rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement artificial pricing are jointly liable with the company under Commercial Act Article 401. In egregious cases, NTS may hold directors personally for the unpaid deficiency, impose asset-preservation travel bans, and refer executives for criminal prosecutions where a false or fraudulent international tax report was executed.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Dividend Routing

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authorities may deny treaty relief under the General Anti-Abuse Rule or the Principal Purpose Test introduced through BEPS MLI implementation. The consequences are immediate 22% aggregate withholding assessment on dividends, interest, or royalties, imposition of withholding penalty surcharges, and interest accrual from the original payment date. Filing of fraudulent proprietary forms may also initiate criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes untaxed dividend repatriation while shielding the beneficial owner is personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under National Tax Collection Act joint-liability provisions. The director may be blacklisted (travel ban) and unable to perform foreign travel until the deficiency is settled. Where the shell arrangement is designed to commit fraud on the Korean treasury, directors risk up to 5 years' imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets for the evaded tax.

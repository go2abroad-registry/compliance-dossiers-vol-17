Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Commercial Act, the company must designate a physical head-office address in Korea in its articles and register that address with the district court. For an ISIC 6201 B2B enterprise SaaS company, a shared workspace or commercial receptionist address may initially satisfy the registry if it is a real street address in Korea. However, the National Tax Service may require evidence of actual office control before issuing the business registration certificate, and Korean banks under AML and branch operational guidance generally conduct an on-site visit or demand a lease, utility bill, and confirmation that responsible personnel can receive official mail. A pure virtual office, a commercial mailbox, or a P.O. Box is not acceptable for a corporate bank account or for VAT invoice registration in this industry. A home-office registration is permissible only if the resident director or employee actually works at that address and the address is disclosed consistently to the registry, tax office, and bank. For a cross-border funded SaaS entity, substance is strengthened by a dedicated lease, a physical company sign, and at least one locally available compliance or administrative officer.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic tax portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity commencing taxable activity in South Korea, the entity must appoint a domestic tax representative if it does not have a place of business in Korea. The representative then files a business registration application with the competent District Tax Office (or the Seoul Regional Tax Office for cross-jurisdictional cases) using the HomeTax e-filing system. The issued Business Registration Number (BRN) is a 10-digit number serving as both the corporate TIN and VAT registration ID. Required documentation includes the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of foreign registration, board resolution authorizing the Korean registration, power of attorney in favor of the tax representative, and the completed statutory application for business registration. Registration must be completed before commencing taxable supplies in Korea.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold; any person engaging in a taxable business must register with the National Tax Service before commencement of business. For natural persons (individuals only), a simplified VAT taxpayer ceiling of KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover may apply, but a Chusik Hoesa corporation is not eligible for simplified taxpayer status and must obtain full VAT registration from the start of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported from South Korea under applicable customs law. Export-related services, including B2B enterprise SaaS and digital services supplied to non-resident customers, are zero-rated where the place of supply is determined to be outside South Korea, the service is not physically consumed in Korea, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. The supplier must hold contemporaneous documentary evidence demonstrating non-resident status of the recipient, the offshore nature of the service, and the receipt of foreign currency payment on a repatriated basis.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration submitted via UNI-PASS)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed service contract
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Currency Exchange Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Proof of service delivery or usage metrics confirming the foreign recipient's actual receipt of the digital service
  • Certificate or written confirmation from the foreign customer evidencing non-resident business establishment

Electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issuance is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses. Each e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax system by no later than the day following issuance. There is no pre-clearance or real-time fiscal stamping requirement at issuance; however, the NTS operates an anti-fraud central validation and matching system that cross-checks e-tax invoice data, cash receipt statements, credit card transaction summaries, and tax returns. Non-compliance, late transmission, or failure to issue valid e-tax invoices triggers additional VAT penalties.

If the zero-rating claim lacks sufficient statutory documentation, the NTS will deny the 0% rate and reclassify the supply as taxable, assessing 10% VAT retroactively. Underdeclared VAT arising from unjustified zero-rating is subject to statutory interest and additional tax penalties; intentional false claims, fraudulent export invoices, or fictitious customs declarations may lead to criminal prosecution, monetary fines, and confiscation orders. The NTS also retains a five-year statutory assessment period for VAT, enabling retrospective clawback of improper zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won operates under a free-floating exchange-rate regime with separate current and capital account disciplines. Current-account foreign exchange payments, including SaaS subscription royalties, software licence fees, dividends and service remuneration, are freely convertible as long as the remitting corporate client lodges an accurate foreign-exchange purpose code and supporting transaction history with an authorised foreign exchange bank. Korean banks do not impose a surrender tax or convertibility spread for commercial trade; however, the bank must conduct an economic-substance review before every non-trade dividend or profit remittance. Capital-account flows are not fully liberalised: equity injections, external loans, and derivative transactions require declaration, notification, approval, or post-reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Profit remittance from a Chusik Hoesa is operationally possible after the company submits its audited or reviewed financial statements, tax clearance documents, board resolution, and proof that underlying export proceeds have been collected through a resident foreign-exchange bank within the statutory period. A common delayed-exit hurdle is the 'business legitimacy test' applied to foreign UBOs in non-Korean holding structures; the bank will need the offshore chain to be fully visible, with share certificates, source-of-wealth evidence, and prior-year tax filings. The current account is therefore convertible, while capital account convertibility is conditional, monitored, and substantially easier for real FDI than for portfolio instruments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from provided software and services must be physically credited to a resident 'foreign exchange bank' in Korea within 180 calendar days from the date of export/customs declaration or the service completion certificate. This statutory collection period may be extended by an additional 180 days with a documented reason submitted to MOEF/BOK before maturity. There is no blanket percentage-forced conversion quota under the current Korean market-rate system; exporters may retain foreign currency in an onshore foreign currency account, but any conversion to KRW must be settled through the bank at the prevailing market rate and the trade must be continuously reportable to the BOK exchange information system. The 'surrender' requirement is therefore a financial reporting and repatriation requirement rather than a xenocurrency expropriation rule, and every foreign-exchange purchase or sale must be backed by a designated purpose code.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in statutory coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), the Korea Customs Service, and the Foreign Exchange Information Network; MOEF is the primary regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, while BOK operates settlements and reserve policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a legal settlement rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Foreign EMIs (Stripe Billing, Wise B2B) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Korea and cannot legally provide KRW-denominated account numbers, custody, or foreign-exchange clearing services that satisfy the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They may be used as payment-gateway front-ends or as collection agents that compress transaction data, provided every actual fiat settlement is swept onward to a resident Tier-1 bank. The bank's compliance committee should treat EMI statements as secondary evidence and require final MT103/MT202 confirmations on a licensed Korean bank branch. For regulated capital injections or export-proceeds surrender, the EMI structure is not acceptable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial equity capital of the Chusik Hoesa, the foreign investor must remit the Korean won equivalent in foreign currency from an overseas bank via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to a 'Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account' opened at an approved Tier-1 Korean bank branch under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The bank will assign the correct foreign-exchange purpose code (Foreign Direct Investment/Equity); it must secure a 'Declaration of Foreign Investment' before the remittance or use the post-declaration route if no advance report is required for the announced amount. After the funds are received, the bank registers the investment and issues a certificate of foreign-investment reporting; the company then issues shares, deposits the subscribed amount into the operating account, and obtains a capital verification certificate from the foreign-exchange bank and a notarised corporate stamp. The same regulated path will apply for further paid-in capital increases and must not be substituted by EMI fronting.The principal clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 low-value/high-volume SaaS subscription settlement for export invoices; (2) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for treasury transfers between the company's foreign-currency account and any international assembly; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC at sight or usance) where contractual obligations require documentary protection; and (4) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for long-term enterprise licensing arrangements. Software exports do not have customs declarations, so the bank will accept the signed service contract, delivery/access logs, and invoice ledger as the export evidence. All outbound disbursement rails must include the applicable foreign-exchange purpose code and must not escape the six-month repatriation rule.Onshore deliverable KRW/USD and KRW/EUR forward contracts covering documented export receivables or import/expense payables with a maturity aligned to the underlying invoice or royalty collection date.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) with a licensed counterparty for hedging long-term foreign-investment loans, intercompany debt, or subscribed-capital repatriation commitments under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available only through an authorised foreign bank branch for non-resident hedging moments where onshore physical KRW settlement cannot be delivered; domestic Chusik Hoesa treasury desk is normally restricted to onshore deliverable instruments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium as a legal settlement rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Foreign EMIs (Stripe Billing, Wise B2B) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Korea and cannot legally provide KRW-denominated account numbers, custody, or foreign-exchange clearing services that satisfy the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They may be used as payment-gateway front-ends or as collection agents that compress transaction data, provided every actual fiat settlement is swept onward to a resident Tier-1 bank. The bank's compliance committee should treat EMI statements as secondary evidence and require final MT103/MT202 confirmations on a licensed Korean bank branch. For regulated capital injections or export-proceeds surrender, the EMI structure is not acceptable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 aggregate related-party cross-border transactions for full Local File and Master File; KRW 5,000,000,000 simplified documentation for each intercompany goods, service, intangible, or loan category

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but applies economic substance through the substance-over-form principle, the OECD/G20 BEPS anti-avoidance standard, and the general anti-abuse rule in the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA Article 5). A Chusik Hoesa must perform its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea: real employees, substantive B2B SaaS contracts, marketing and technical support, server and development activity, and independent board decisions. A foreign intermediary shareholder must have genuine offices, decision-making, actual employees, and risk-bearing capacity; otherwise the NTS can disregard the intermediary, deny treaty benefits, and reattribute profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Annual Reporting and Electronic Tax Invoices

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 9 and Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-3 and 47-4: additional tax of 20%-40% of the unpaid corporate income tax, daily late-payment penalty, and criminal fine up to three times the evaded tax; fraudulent electronic tax invoices carry separate imprisonment sanctions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors knowingly instigate or conceal the fraud; directors become jointly and severally liable for all unpaid national taxes and penalties, may be blacklisted as habitual tax delinquents with travel and exit restrictions, and face imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Failures in the Related-Party Disclosure Regime

Statutory sanction: Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 5 and Corporate Tax Act Article 52 authorize adjustment to the arm's-length amount; deduction is denied for excess payments; 40% fraudulent understatement penalty and 10%-20% negligent understatement penalty apply; failure to comply with documentation imposes an independent penalty surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return or direct the intercompany pricing are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties if they acted intentionally; Korean authorities can disregard the separate legal personality of a foreign affiliate used solely to avoid tax; senior officers face criminal liability for filing false international transaction statements and travel bans from NTS blacklisting.
Shell Shareholder, Treaty Shopping, and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization under LCITA Article 5 and the Korea-treaty beneficial-ownership requirement; the distribution is re-taxed at the 22% aggregate withholding rate plus penalty surcharges for non-withholding; false treaty exemption filing may be treated as tax evasion with fines up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Korean Chusik Hoesa who signed the treaty-benefit withholding application and knew the shareholder was a shell bear personal civil liability for the deficient withholding tax; criminal exposure includes imprisonment for aiding tax evasion; the representative director's passport and travel may be restricted and assets seized under National Tax Collection procedures.

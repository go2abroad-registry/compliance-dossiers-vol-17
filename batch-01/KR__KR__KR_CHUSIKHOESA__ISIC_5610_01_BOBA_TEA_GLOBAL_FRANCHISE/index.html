Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None by law. The Korean Commercial Act does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The incorporators may declare any positive nominal capital amount, but the declared amount must be fully paid-in before filing for incorporation and deposited into a designated pre-incorporation blocked settlement account. The bank-issued balance certificate must accompany the court registration application. Failure to deposit the full declared amount invalidates the registration and the subscribers may claw back the funds only if the registration is abandoned.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety certificate and annual business fire inspection from the local fire station under the Fire Facilities Installation and Maintenance Act; secure a civil defense fire permit for commercial cooking, heating, and syrup production equipment; complete structural load review and occupancy approval from the local building authority; and register a workplace risk assessment with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, covering employee safety training, emergency exits, first-aid kits, boiler and pressure-vessel inspection, and forklift or pallet-truck certifications where applicable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic tax and VAT filing portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs export clearance is administered by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

Domestic newly formed Chusik Hoesa: complete registration at the Commercial Registry of the relevant Korean district court; file a business registration application with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office or principal place of business; the NTS will verify the identity and location and issue the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration number; the company or its tax attorney must then activate HomeTax e-Tax Invoice access and cash-receipt registration. For a foreign non-resident entity that maintains a Korean domestic place of business or is otherwise required to report Korean VAT, the foreign company must appoint a Korean tax representative and file the same business registration application with the district tax office exercising jurisdiction over the Korean place of business. Required documents include the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, a board resolution appointing the Korean tax representative, an executed power of attorney, proof of the Korean business address such as a lease agreement, and identification documents; foreign documents must be apostilled or certified with Korean translations where required. There is no economic-presence or revenue threshold; registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply in Korea.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Chusik Hoesa incorporated under the Korean Commercial Act is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is subject to corporate income tax at entity level on worldwide income. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment; distributions to shareholders are treated as dividends and are subject to Korean withholding tax or final taxation as applicable.

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide check-the-box elections, partnership pass-through elections, or hybrid mismatch classification elections. Entity status is fixed by the Commercial Act and the Corporate Tax Act; a limited-liability stock company is always an entity-level taxpayer. The consolidated corporate taxation system is a statutory group filing regime for eligible affiliates, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption or monetary threshold in KRW; every business making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea is required to register for VAT before commencing business. The only small-business simplification is a designated simplified taxpayer filing calculation available to certain individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000; this does not eliminate the registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are sold to a foreign buyer, physically exported from Korea under a Korea Customs Service export declaration, and settled in foreign exchange. Qualifying services, including international services and services supplied to and consumed wholly outside Korea by a non-resident, also qualify for zero rating. For the boba tea franchise, exports of syrups and ingredients to overseas franchisees can be zero-rated on a shipment-by-shipment basis if the customs export declaration, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts are maintained. Franchise royalties and brand license fees invoiced to overseas franchisees may be zero-rated where the franchise or licence services are performed for a foreign business outside Korea and are not connected with a Korean place of business; documentation must evidence the foreign recipient's non-resident status, the foreign location of the service, and the actual receipt of royalty proceeds in foreign currency. Offshore digital services are zero-rated only when the supply-place rules treat the digital service as supplied outside Korea and the service is consumed by a non-resident outside Korea. The zero-rating is claimed on the ordinary VAT return, and the input VAT refund will be subject to NTS documentary verification. Domestic franchise royalties from Korean franchisees for stores located in Korea are standard-rated domestic services at 10%.

  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Export Sales Contract

Electronic tax invoice issuance is mandatory for VAT-registered businesses. E-Tax Invoices must be issued on or before the supply date and transmitted to the National Tax Service by the day after issuance through HomeTax, an NTS-certified e-Tax Invoice service provider, or an ERP integration using HomeTax APIs. The NTS operates a central data-matching anti-fraud regime under which e-Tax Invoice data, bank settlement data, cash-receipt data, and export customs declarations are reconciled in near-real-time; there is no physical fiscal stamping, and the HomeTax transmission is the fiscal control step. Late, omitted, or false e-Tax Invoice transmission, and POS consumer sales that should be recorded in the cash-receipt system, carry penalty taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act.

The National Tax Service may audit zero-rated export claims within the statutory assessment period and will cross-check HomeTax e-Tax Invoice filings, UNI-PASS customs declarations, bills of lading or air waybills, and foreign-exchange bank settlement records. If any required document is missing, false, inconsistent, or the foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated by the statutory deadline, the zero-rating is retroactively revoked and the 10% VAT is reassessed from the original return due date, together with interest and penalty taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Intentional fabrication or dishonest omission in export evidence constitutes tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may lead to criminal fines or imprisonment for responsible directors, employees, or tax representatives. Previously granted VAT refunds are clawed back with interest, and if the export financial flows involve related parties, the NTS may also perform a separate transfer-pricing adjustment on franchise royalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal but regulated foreign-exchange system. The Korean won is freely convertible for current-account operations such as export/import trade settlements, royalty fees, dividends, and operating disbursements, provided the underlying economic contract is presented to a registered foreign exchange bank and the transaction is reported through the prescribed surveillance channel. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident equity subscriptions, intercompany loans, and financial investments, may be opened outside Korea if the investor uses an approved or a declaration-based route and executes the necessary foreign investment registration. There is no administrative restriction on the repatriation of earnings from a properly registered Chusik Hoesa; the bank will verify that the dividend source is distributable accumulated profit, that withholding taxes are settled, and that no external debt or excessive trade credit is being serviced. Inward capital movements are subject to source-of-funds diligence, while outward remittances may be paused during emergency stability measures. Overall, KRW is convertible but documentary compliance and reporting are mandatory, making the system administratively stringent rather than punitive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and related Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, Korean exporters must receive settlement for exports within 18 months from the date of export declaration unless a longer deferred-payment term has been approved by the competent foreign exchange authority or delegated bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in an authorized foreign currency deposit account, re-invoiced for operational needs, or converted to Korean won at the prevailing market rate through a licensed foreign exchange bank. The exporter must submit evidence of receipt to the appointed bank and maintain a supporting trade file for the statutory preservation period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), as the central bank, operating jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); day-to-day implementation, reporting collection, and enforcement are executed through authorized foreign exchange banks and Korea Customs Service.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for primary treasury use. Offshore digital electronic money institutions do not hold a Korean banking license and are not permitted to maintain domestic current accounts, receive KRW in electronic money form, or issue Korean account identifiers for entity clearing under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Using an overseas EMI as the main operating account would bypass the legal foreign exchange bank channel, create reporting gaps under FETA, and magnify AML risk. Fintech EMIs may be used only as ancillary channels for non-KRW supplier payments in limited jurisdictions, and even then the treasury legal structure should keep all Korean-source franchise and royalty receipts within an authorized bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound remittance must be sent by SWIFT MT103 through an authorized foreign exchange bank using a foreign-direct-investment-related narrative and purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with the beneficiary legal name as the Chusik Hoesa. The remitter should advise the bank of the KRW conversion and the capital subscription basis; the foreign exchange bank credits the payment to the designated capital collection account, issues a purchase/inward payment receipt, and the entity completes the foreign-invested company registration before the capital verification certificate is issued by a Korean CPA. No cash declarations are needed for wire transfers, but any physical transport of funds exceeds USD 10,000 must be declared to Korea Customs Service. Following registration, the funds can be converted into a basic KRW operating account and applied toward the company's legal capital and working capital needs.For global franchise supply chain settlements, use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 direct credit transfers for lower-risk royalty, administrative, and confirmed supplier payments; use Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) when purchasing syrup, food, and packaging assets where shipment and document control are required; and use Documentary Collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) for repeated, reliable counterparties seeking balanced trade risk. All KRW-denominated domestic operational liquidity movements should clear through KFTC rails, while cross-border US dollar and euro disbursements should clear via correspondent banking using the bank's SWIFT BIC. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for primary treasury use. Offshore digital electronic money institutions do not hold a Korean banking license and are not permitted to maintain domestic current accounts, receive KRW in electronic money form, or issue Korean account identifiers for entity clearing under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Using an overseas EMI as the main operating account would bypass the legal foreign exchange bank channel, create reporting gaps under FETA, and magnify AML risk. Fintech EMIs may be used only as ancillary channels for non-KRW supplier payments in limited jurisdictions, and even then the treasury legal structure should keep all Korean-source franchise and royalty receipts within an authorized bank.

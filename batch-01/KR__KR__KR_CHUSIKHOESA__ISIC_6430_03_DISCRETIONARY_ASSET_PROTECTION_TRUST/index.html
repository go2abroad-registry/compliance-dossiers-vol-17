Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not accepted as a registered place of business for a Chusik Hoesa whose scope is fiduciary administration of trusts or investment vehicles. Although the Commercial Act requires only a registered head office address, the National Tax Service, the Financial Services Commission, and licensed Korean banks require actual physical office capacity, corporate records at the address, and persons authorized to receive tax and legal process. For ISIC 6430 and high-risk fiduciary activity, virtual offices will be rejected by the National Tax Service for value-added tax registration and by the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit for mandatory AML licensing because substance is assessed through business premises and operational presence. The Korean Supreme Court Registry may accept a building number or floor, but not a merely virtual mailbox, because civil procedure rules require service at a real business address. The bank will require lease documentation, evidence of telephone and internet infrastructure, and an employee or director physically present in Korea for authentication. Thus, virtual office is rejected for this industry unless it comprises a serviced office with an exclusive room, physical reception, full-time staff, and the requisite regulatory approval, in which case it must still be disclosed to the registrar.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act; shareholders may establish capital in the Articles. However, under FSCMA Article 12 and its Presidential Decree, the licensed fiduciary administration of irrevocable discretionary asset protection trusts constitutes trust business and requires a trust business license with equity capital of at least KRW 5,000,000,000 (five billion South Korean Won). Capital remitted by subscribers must be deposited into a blocked Korean bank escrow/deposit account in the company's proposed name, remain blocked until the certificate of deposit is issued for incorporation registration, and be released only to the company after registration; the released funds must then be maintained as paid-in capital for trust business prudential requirements.

E-government filing portal

The application requires submission of the articles of incorporation, proof of incorporation, and identification of the representative. For non-resident entities without a domestic place of business, the entity must appoint a domestic tax representative and register for a foreign entity TIN. The BRN is typically issued within 3-5 business days.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT if the goods are physically exported and the exporter retains evidence of export. For services, services provided to non-residents and used outside Korea qualify for 0% VAT if the service is not connected with Korean real estate or Korean goods. For trust administration services, if the trust is offshore and the beneficiaries are non-residents, the service may qualify as zero-rated. The service must be supplied to a non-resident and the consideration must be received from abroad.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised current account but a regulated capital account. The KRW is convertible for trade and service payments and for income and profit remittances, provided the underlying transaction is reported through a licensed bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital account transactions, including external borrowing, intercompany loans and outward remittances for investments, require advance declaration or registration with the Ministry of Economy and Finance via a designated foreign exchange bank. Profit remittances and dividends are freely permitted after corporate tax is paid, but the settlement bank must verify that the foreign direct investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) is complete, audited financial statements are submitted, and a board resolution authorising the dividend is present. Dividends to non-residents are subject to Korean withholding tax at 20% plus 10% local surtax unless reduced under a tax treaty, which must be evidenced by a certificate of residence. KRW is not a reserve currency and offshore delivery of KRW is not permitted; therefore all currency swaps and forward contracts must be intermediated by an onshore licensed foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds are paid into foreign currency accounts without mandatory conversion, but must be received within a statutory timeframe. Remittances above prescribed thresholds require prior consultation between the bank and the remaining foreign exchange authorities, particularly for a high-risk fiduciary entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As per Article 8 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received within six months from the date of the export declaration. The appointed foreign exchange bank may grant one extension of up to two months when justified by the trade contract. All proceeds must be settled through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank; however, there is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW. Export proceeds may remain in a foreign currency account, and exports of less than USD 50,000 may be settled directly in KRW without a conventional foreign currency receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the FX policy authority; enforcement is jointly exercised with Korea Customs Service.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

A dedicated non-bank EMI route is not viable. Legally, only banks and provident foreign exchange businesses licensed under Article 3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act may deal in foreign exchange. Korean fintech platforms such as Kakao Pay and Toss are e-money issuers or sub-scale payment gateways under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; they do not hold a foreign exchange licence and cannot maintain corporate fiduciary funds, issue USD accounts, or process cross-border capital transfers for a trust operator. An offshore digital EMI is also unlawful as the primary account provider because its foreign licence does not extend to Korea, and holding corporate trust funds in an unauthorised non-bank abroad would violate the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and generate an AML red flag. Therefore all cross-border clearing must be executed through a licensed Korean commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal statutory corporate income tax rate for the Republic of Korea, applied to a progressive national schedule of 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 24% above KRW 300 billion; a 10% local income tax surcharge raises the top combined tax incidence to 26.4% before special surtaxes

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory national withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder, plus a 2% local income tax surcharge, producing an aggregate domestic withholding rate of 22%; an applicable double tax treaty may reduce the rate, often to 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for direct dividends when the beneficial owner holds at least 10% of the voting share capital and satisfies the required holding period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 in annual cross-border intercompany transactions with overseas related parties mandates the preparation and submission of a Korean Local File; a multinational group with consolidated revenue of KRW 1,000,000,000,000 or more is also required to prepare a Master File and, where applicable, a Country-by-Country Report for the National Tax Service.

Economic substance scope: While Korea does not operate a classic Economic Substance Regulations regime, the same substance principles are enforced through the place-of-effective-management test, the beneficial ownership requirement in tax treaty claims, controlled foreign company rules, and the general anti-avoidance doctrines in Korea's Framework Act on International Tax Coordination. A Korean fiduciary administering an irrevocable discretionary asset protection trust must demonstrate local Core Income-Generating Activities, including actual local staff, board meetings, investment decisions, trust account control, due diligence on settlors and beneficiaries, and physical management of the trust portfolio; an entity merely holding legal title or a corporate file in Korea without real commercial functions is vulnerable to recharacterization as a shell and to denial of treaty and withholding relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Fraudulent Trust Charade

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, together with the Framework Act on National Taxes additional-tax rules: imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of between two and five times the evaded tax; where the tax evaded exceeds statutory amounts, aggravated punishment applies and the National Tax Service may publicly post the taxpayer name. The entity may also be disqualified from retaining the statutory fiduciary license required for trust administration.

Director personal liability: Under the Korean Commercial Act Article 401, applied with Article 389(3), directors who intentionally or negligently cause the corporation to file fraudulent trust tax returns are jointly and severally liable for damages to the company and tax authorities. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the trust entity is a facade for the personal assets of the settlor, protector, or beneficiary, allowing attachments to directors' personal residences and bank accounts. The National Tax Service can issue a travel ban by placing the director on the restricted-departure blacklist, and a criminal conviction carries real imprisonment, disqualification from corporate officer positions, and restitution of the entire unpaid tax principal and penalties.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Article 4-6 of the Framework Act on International Tax Coordination authorizes the National Tax Service to recharacterize intercompany transactions and reassess the Korean tax base at the arm's-length amount; Article 4-11 permits the denial of treaty benefits where the arrangement lacks a genuine purpose and economic substance. Financial penalties amount to 10% to 20% of the underreported tax amount, plus daily in-arrears interest; in aggravated cases, a sham transaction is prosecuted as tax evasion rather than treated as a mere transfer-pricing adjustment.

Director personal liability: When the corporate form is used as a sham conduit, South Korean courts disregard the separate personality of the Chusik Hoesa and impose personal liability on directors for the additional corporate tax, the unpaid principal, interest, and penalties. Personal civil asset restitution is ordered to recover trust assets from beneficiaries and protectors, and directors may be placed on the National Tax Service departure blacklist pending full payment. A deliberate transfer-pricing arrangement designed to evade Korean tax is a crime under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment, fines, and disqualification from serving on boards of licensed fiduciary institutions.
AML, Beneficial Ownership, and Unlicensed Fiduciary Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information and the Financial Services Commission licensing framework, illegal fiduciary administration invites administrative fines, license revocation, and criminal prosecution; failure to report suspicious transactions can result in imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to KRW 100 million or more, with confiscation of the proceeds of the unlawful trust business.

Director personal liability: Limited liability does not protect directors who knowingly operate an unlicensed asset protection trust or disguise money laundering through trust accounts; the corporate veil is pierced as a matter of public policy, and directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the misappropriated assets to defrauded settlors or creditors. Financial authorities coordinate with the National Tax Service to impose travel bans and bank asset freezes on involved directors. Criminal liability may include imprisonment up to five years for money laundering and, where unreported movement of criminal proceeds is proven, a conviction that permanently bars the director from serving as a fiduciary or licensed financial institution officer.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Seoul Central District Court Registry Division, Corporate Registration Section, Supreme Court of Korea (electronic filing through the Supreme Court Registration System); for an office outside Seoul, the District Court Registry having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, promulgated December 31, 1962, as comprehensively amended), as implemented by the Enforcement Decree of the Commercial Act and the non-contentious registration practice rules of the Supreme Court Registration System. Applicable sections include Articles 288 through 317 (incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa), Article 289 (notarized Articles of Incorporation), Article 295 (payment of subscription price), Article 299 (appointment of first directors and auditor), Article 317 (registration of incorporation with the district court), Article 329 (capital and shares; no statutory minimum paid-up capital), Article 383 (minimum number of directors), Article 389 (representative director), and Article 393 (board authority over major management matters).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa, filed with the notarized Articles of Incorporation and prescribed annexes; upon entry of registration, the court issues a Certificate of Registered Matters and a corporate seal certificate.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa, and no blocked-escrow account is required. Paid-in capital is fixed by the promoters in the Articles and may be any positive amount. Before registration, the full issue price for subscribed shares must be deposited in a temporary subscription account in the company's name, and a bank certificate of deposit must be submitted to the court; the funds are released to the company upon incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered electronically through the HomeTax portal (www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service for export clearance and customs-related verification.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified corporate tax and VAT identifier. The application is filed with the district tax office with jurisdiction over the entity's Korean domestic place of business, or through the NTS HomeTax system where no domestic establishment exists, using an appointed tax representative. The statutory procedure under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act comprises: (1) preparing the foreign certificate of incorporation, articles/statutes, evidence of registration in the home jurisdiction, the valid passport or identity document of the foreign entity's representative, proof of the Korean place of business (lease or ownership document) or power of attorney appointing a Korean tax representative; (2) submitting the Business Registration application to the competent district tax office via HomeTax or in person; (3) NTS validation of the submitted foreign corporate documents and representative's authority; and (4) issuance of the 10-digit Business Registration Number and Business Registration Certificate, which is simultaneously the Company TIN and VAT registration number. If the foreign supplier has no Korean permanent establishment but is required to issue tax invoices or claim a VAT refund, registration is completed through the designated tax representative.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide check-the-box or analogous entity classification elections; a Chusik Hoesa is statutorily classified as a separate corporate taxpayer under the Corporate Tax Act and such classification cannot be disregarded for VAT purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory gross-turnover threshold for general VAT registration; any person carrying on a business that supplies taxable goods or services in South Korea must register with the district tax office. A simplified taxpayer status exists for certain small retail operators, but a Chusik Hoesa engaged in exports and online retail must be registered as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act. For online sales of musical instruments and studio audio gear, the goods must be sold to a foreign customer, physically removed from South Korea and declared to the Korea Customs Service as an export. Qualifying services supplied and consumed outside Korea, and paid in foreign currency that is brought into Korea, may also be zero-rated under the statutory categories set out in the Enforcement Decree. The chusik hoesa must obtain and retain the full statutory evidence package before applying the 0% rate on the VAT return.

  • Export Declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing departure from Korea
  • Foreign currency export proceeds receipt confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Purchase order or sales agreement with the overseas buyer
  • Electronic tax invoice and inventory/shipping records matching the export declaration

Korea requires VAT-registered corporations to issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system. E-invoice data is transmitted to the NTS electronically at the time the invoice is issued, permitting automated cross-matching between e-invoice records, VAT returns and customs export declarations. The NTS offers machine-to-machine/web-service interfaces to licensed accounting and ERP providers. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance digital stamping or front-end fiscal encryption regime; anti-fraud controls are exercised through mandatory electronic tax invoice transmission to the NTS and the NTS data validation framework.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating by cross-checking export declarations, transport documents, VAT returns, electronic tax invoices and foreign-currency repatriation records. If the statutory export evidence is not available or the export is recharacterized as a domestic supply, the NTS retrospectively revokes the 0% rate, assesses the 10% VAT on the export consideration, and imposes VAT surcharges and interest. Where false or fraudulent export documentation is used, the Korean tax and customs authorities may impose criminal penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Customs Act, including fines and imprisonment, within the statutory extended assessment period for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a managed but generally open foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, royalties, and operating remittances, may be settled freely through designated foreign exchange banks after an FX transaction declaration where required. The Korean won is not fully freely convertible in global capital markets; capital account transactions, such as non-resident equity injections, loans, overseas dividend remittances, and acquisition of offshore assets, require prior or post-reporting to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Profit remittance hurdles are reduced when proper board minutes, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and FDI declaration records are presented, but banks may require documentary proof of underlying trade and absence of anti-money laundering concerns. Export receipts are not subject to forced conversion; exporters may maintain foreign currency deposit accounts. Dividend remittances are subject to 22% withholding tax including surtax unless treaty relief applies. Funds from cryptocurrencies, informal third parties, or jurisdictions on FATF grey lists will be rejected by the compliance committees of Korean banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received and settled within 12 months from the export date; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, and exporters may retain foreign currency balances in designated foreign-currency deposit accounts, but must report transaction details through a designated foreign exchange bank and obtain MOEF/Bank of Korea approval if the settlement period requires extension beyond 12 months.

Central bank supervision: Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration, acting with the Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore fintech EMI and digital-only platforms are not licensed as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and are legally barred from opening domestic treasury settlement accounts, executing KRW clearing, or handling cross-border capital and current account flows for a Korean resident export entity. They may only serve as online card-acquiring payment aggregators at the front end, and all sales settlement proceeds must be redirected to an account at a Korean designated foreign exchange bank.

In addition, shareholders holding at least 3% of total issued shares may demand an outside audit even if the statutory threshold is not met. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (national statutory marginal Corporate Income Tax rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; lower progressive bands apply at 9%, 19%, and 21%; a 10% local income tax surtax raises the combined headline burden to 26.4% before treaty and incentive relief).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national dividend withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax) on cash and deemed dividends paid to non-resident shareholders of a Korean Chusik Hoesa; treaty relief may reduce the rate to 5%-15% depending on shareholding percentage and applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The final corporate income tax return must be filed within 3 months from the fiscal year-end; a calendar-year Korean Chusik Hoesa must file by March 31, with a one-month extension available for foreign-parented groups or where foreign business place financial records require verification; interim prepayments are due within 2 months after the first 6-month accounting period.

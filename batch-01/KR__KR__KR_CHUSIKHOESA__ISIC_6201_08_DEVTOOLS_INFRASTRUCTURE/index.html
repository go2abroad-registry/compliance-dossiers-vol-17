Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr), supplemented by Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for export clearances.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes, or takes a holding in, a Korean Chusik Hoesa must ensure the Korean company obtains a Business Registration Certificate by filing the standard business registration application with the district tax office (through HomeTax or in person) within 20 days from the day after business commencement. The company is then issued a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is the corporate TIN and VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity itself with no Korean domestic place of business but with Korean VAT-taxable digital or e-commerce supplies, the steps are: appoint a Korean tax agent with notarized power of attorney; submit through HomeTax the VAT registration application for non-resident business operators; attach apostilled/certified copies of the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Association, foreign trade/business license, and passport of the corporate representative; receive a NTS-issued foreign entity Korean VAT TIN. After approval, the foreign VAT supplier must file periodic VAT returns electronically via HomeTax, either directly or through the tax agent, and, where applicable, issue e-tax invoices.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Korean Chusik Hoesa is a resident stock corporation taxed in Korea on worldwide income at entity level and no U.S.-style pass-through classification exists for this entity form.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold or turnover exemption that postpones registration in Korea; every person supplying VAT-taxable goods or services in Korea, including foreign non-resident digital/e-commerce suppliers, must register within 20 days of commencement. A simplified taxable person regime is available below KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover, but the registration requirement itself remains compulsory and the headline VAT rate remains 10%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, 0% VAT applies to goods exported from Korea after customs export declaration; to services sold to non-resident foreign customers and consumed outside Korea; and to qualifying digital services, including cloud-based DevOps tools, database infrastructure and API monitoring, provided to a non-resident business outside Korea. The taxpayer must retain evidence that the recipient is a foreign non-resident, that payment is collected from abroad, and that the service is not used in Korea. Goods cannot be zero-rated unless they are physically removed from Korea under customs control.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS for goods leaving Korea.
  • Original transport document - Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport evidence showing movement to the overseas buyer.
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales/service contract identifying the foreign buyer, scope of DevOps/cloud service or goods, and destination.
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate/Inward Remittance Receipt evidencing conversion and receipt of foreign currency export proceeds from the overseas customer.

General taxable persons must issue e-tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system, either via the online dashboard or HomeTax API/EDI integration, by the day following each supply. Issued e-tax invoice data is transmitted to the NTS in real time and matched to VAT return inputs and outputs, acting as the statutory fiscal clearance and anti-fraud control mechanism. Registered foreign VAT suppliers satisfy the requirement through their Korean tax agent's HomeTax account or API access; no separate physical fiscal stamp is required.

If a zero-rated export cannot be proved by the required documentation, or foreign currency export proceeds are not repatriated to a Korean foreign exchange bank within the statutory 180-day period without an approved extension, the NTS will disallow the zero-rating and assess 10% VAT on the supply. The understatement penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes are generally 10% to 20% of the unpaid tax, statutory late-payment interest runs from the original due date, and the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act permits criminal sanctions for deliberate evasion or false e-tax invoice issuance. The assessment may also reopen prior returns within the applicable national tax statute-of-limitation period, which can extend up to ten years for fraudulent claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a mostly liberalized current-account regime for software exports and SaaS royalties, but reserves broad supervisory review under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible in the local interbank market at freely negotiated rates, and inbound software licensing fees, cloud subscription income, and management service fees can be credited to a designated foreign exchange bank without requiring case-by-case central bank approval. In practice, every receipt above the roughly USD 5,000 threshold must be supported by a purpose code and by an underlying invoice or contract. Capital account items are more constrained; a non-resident shareholder's equity contribution must be structured as a Foreign Direct Investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the remitted funds cannot be used for prohibited treasury or margin-style purposes. Repatriation of dividends and residual capital is generally permitted, but requires the issuer to produce audited financial statements, corporate income tax and withholding tax clearance, and a certificate confirming that the initial inbound FDI report was registered. There is no fixed surrender quota for ordinary software exporters, and foreign currency receipts may remain routed through a licensed bank's foreign-currency deposit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, Korea applies a six-month collection deadline for export proceeds from the date of export declaration for software and digital services; a bank may grant an extension if supported by a trade contract and delay certificate. No statutory mandatory conversion percentage applies to standard current-account receipts, meaning 0% of the export proceeds must be converted to KRW; the entire 100% may be retained as foreign currency inside a designated foreign exchange bank account. However, if a foreign exchange bank cannot confirm a valid transaction purpose or export declaration, it will block the credit and impose an immediate 100% conversion into a suspended KRW account pending regulatory verification. Export proceeds cannot be credited to an offshore EMI or a third-party collection account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act with policy oversight by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and operational enforcement through designated foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low legal viability for offshore fintech EMIs. EMIs such as Wise or Revolut Business are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission to maintain corporate treasury accounts for resident Korean companies, and the Korean tax authority will not recognize such accounts for export proceeds. Stripe Billing may be used as a front-end payment facilitator to collect SaaS and DevOps tools payments, but settlement must route to a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (top marginal rate; progressive brackets: 10% up to KRW 200M, 20% up to KRW 20B, 22% up to KRW 300B, 25% above KRW 300B)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge (aggregate 22%) on outbound dividends paid by a Korean resident corporation to a non-resident shareholder, unless a Double Tax Treaty (DTA) provides a lower rate (typically 5%-15%, subject to beneficial ownership and shareholding threshold requirements).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 (approx. USD 7.5 million) in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers the mandatory preparation of Local File and Master File. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCr) applies when the multinational group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion. Even below the threshold, contemporary TP documentation is strongly recommended for risk rating.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulation' (ESR) like the UAE, but the substance-over-form principle is embedded in Korean tax law and the OECD BEPS framework. Under Corporate Tax Act Article 52 and International Tax Adjustment Act Article 6, transaction recharacterization is permitted for arrangements lacking business purpose. For IP-intensive DevTools/cloud infrastructure, Korean tax authorities will scrutinize whether the 'Core Income-Generating Activities' (CIGA) — cloud development, database monitoring, API reliability, engineering decisions, and customer service — are actually performed in or outside Korea. If a foreign subsidiary merely holds the IP or invoices royalties without real CIGA, the Korean NTS may recharacterize the income as Korean-source and impose 22% WHT plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Veil Piercing & Tax Evasion through Sham Intercompany Structure

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act (Tax Crime Punishment Act) Article 3, intentional evasion of corporate tax is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax. The NTS will also impose the 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty under Basic National Tax Act Article 47-3, plus 0.025% daily overdue interest and deemed assessment by disregarding the sham structure.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Chusik Hoesa is used as a mere instrument of tax fraud or its assets are mixed with personal assets of directors. Under National Tax Collection Act Article 7, directors can become 'secondary taxpayers' personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they willfully or by neglect disposed of corporate assets for the purpose of evading collection. NTS may blacklist (travel ban) directors who fail to remit large delinquent taxes; criminal trial can result in personal assets seizure, disgorgement of all shifted profits, and actual imprisonment of the representative director.
Withholding Tax Fraud on Cross-Border Dividends & Royalties

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Corporate Income Tax Act and Income Tax Act withholding provisions triggers a 50% additional penalty on the unpaid withholding tax amount for intentional failure, plus 10% penalty for the non-registered withholding agent; under the Tax Crime Punishment Act Article 9, a withholding agent who fails to withhold or remits false returns is liable to imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine up to 100% of the unwithheld amount. The NTS will also deny all expense deductions for the disguised royalty payments, increasing taxable income by 100% of the amount.

Director personal liability: Under Basic National Tax Act Article 39, the director who intentionally failed to withhold and remit tax is jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax undercharge. Korean courts have voided the corporate form where the board is an alter ego; directors' personal bank accounts, personal residences, and foreign assets are subject to civil asset seizure and execution. Blacklisting under the NTS 'Outstanding Taxpayer' scheme prevents directors from leaving Korea until all liabilities are settled, and criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and permanent immigration sanctions.
Shell Company / AML Abusive Beneficial Ownership & Unreported Foreign Bank Account

Statutory sanction: Under Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, unreported payment/receipt transactions between Korean residents and a non-resident can be punished with a fine of up to KRW 100 million and imprisonment up to 3 years. The NTS will impose a 20% penalty on unreported foreign financial account income and will disallow all treaty exemptions because the beneficial owner is non-transparent. Civil sanctions include an instant reassessment of 22% withholding tax on the entire remitted amount, default interest, and criminal referral to the Seoul District Prosecutors' Office.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind the Chusik Hoesa's limited liability when they deliberately failed to conduct adequate AML/KYC on the true shareholder. The court may 'pierce the corporate veil' because the entity was used to conceal a fraudulent tax scheme. Personal liability extends to restitution of all taxes evaded, court-ordered asset freezing, a travel ban under the Minister of Justice, and criminal imprisonment of all consenting directors (including foreign directors if extradited under the Korea-USA/other MLAT). The director's personal D&O insurance will be void for willful misconduct, thereby exposing personal estates to unlimited civil damages.

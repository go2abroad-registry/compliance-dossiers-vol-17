Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Commercial Act, the registered office must be a definite place capable of receiving official notices and identifying the company's central administration. A virtual office address may satisfy registration-only requirements if the service provider physically receives and forwards mail. However, for ISIC 5210 cross-dock/overseas warehouse order fulfillment, the National Tax Service (NTS) generally requires a physical place of business for VAT business registration, tax invoice issuance and customs inventory records. Korean banks also reject virtual-only addresses for corporate account opening because of Financial Services Commission AML CDD rules requiring physical substance verification. Therefore, a virtual office is acceptable only as an interim registered address; a physical commercial lease or warehouse operating site is effectively mandatory for NTS approval and bank onboarding.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa under the amended Korean Commercial Act. However, at the time of incorporation, at least 100 Korean won per issued share must be fully paid in, and subscription payments must be deposited into a blocked account at a Korean licensed financial institution in the name of the proposed company (or deposited as in-kind property with court-appointed inspection if required). The deposit remains frozen until the completion of incorporation registration; there is no separate escrow account requirement, but the blocked deposit fulfils the statutory paid-in capital protection rule.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: Hometax Service (www.hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS, www.unipass.customs.go.kr) for export declarations and customs registration.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act through the Supreme Court Registration Office; the court registry transmits incorporation notice to the competent NTS district tax office for automatic business registration. 2) For a foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business, appoint a statutory domestic tax representative and file the foreign corporation business registration application with the district tax office at the domestic tax manager's location, attaching the home-country certificate of incorporation, certificate of registration or equivalent, board resolution appointing the tax manager, and power of attorney. 3) NTS through Hometax assigns a ten-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that serves as the unified Tax Identification Number / VAT ID for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding obligations, customs clearance, and electronic tax invoice issuance. 4) Register separately with Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS to obtain an exporter code; this code is required for filing export declarations on physical export cross-dock and overseas fulfillment flows. 5) Maintain the original Business Registration Certificate, report changes within the statutory window, and activate the electronic tax invoice issuance certificate in Hometax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) physical goods exported from Korea where the export declaration has been filed and accepted under the Customs Act, title and risk pass to an overseas buyer, and the goods are removed from Korea; (b) services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use outside Korea, including qualifying logistics, warehousing, or cross-dock fulfillment services directly connected to exported goods, provided the services are not treated as domestic taxable supplies; and (c) export proceeds are received in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The taxpayer must report the zero-rated export supplies in the periodic VAT return and retain full documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration acceptance through UNI-PASS)
  • Bill of Lading (master and house, as applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Sale Confirmation (FESC) issued by the Korean dealing bank
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of export transportation and overseas warehouse delivery (Cargo Tracking, Delivery Confirmation, and away-from-port records)

Electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) are mandatory under the Korean VAT Act and must be issued and transmitted through the NTS Hometax electronic tax invoice network. Submission is made via Hometax web portal, EDI, or the Hometax application using an accredited digital certificate. Accepted e-tax invoices receive an NTS reference number; Korea does not operate a private third-party real-time invoice clearance model or physical fiscal stamp. Anti-fraud integrity rests on the taxpayer's certified digital signature, NTS transmission acknowledgment, and the unique e-tax invoice numbering scheme.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit zero-rated exports. If the export evidence bundle is incomplete, export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (unless extension is approved), or the zero-rating is found to be fraudulent or overstated, the tax office will reverse the zero-rating and assess unpaid VAT at 10% on the supply value, plus negligent filing penalty tax and aggravated fraudulent penalty tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent cases may attract criminal referral, monetary fines, and retrospective reassessment. The standard statutory assessment period is five years, extended to ten years for evasion or fraudulent return.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a formally liberalized yet carefully supervised two-tier foreign exchange regime. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, current account transactions -- including dividend remittances, service fees, and ordinary trade settlements -- are generally free and may be processed through licensed foreign exchange banks with only a post-transaction reporting obligation to FEIS. Capital account transactions, such as equity injections, loans, and scriptural investments, require prior or simultaneous notification for statistical balance of payments purposes, but no ex ante authorization is needed for standard foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). The Korean won (KRW) is considered convertible for current account purposes, but the market remains intervened by the BOK under a floating exchange rate system. For the Chusik Hoesa, profit remittance hurdles are non-trivial: the entity must first present documentation proving prior tax withholding, completed foreign exchange bank due diligence, and a completed transaction report through FEIS for amounts above KRW 500 million or USD equivalent. Surplus capital repatriation is likewise permitted under FIPA after a certified capital verification by paid-in capital and actual investment confirmation. In practice, the convertibility is robust for bona fide operating entities, but the friction point is documentary completeness; every cross-border transfer must be supported by the underlying contract, invoice, or tax certificate, and the bank's compliance unit will subject the transfer to a release check against the economic substance of the transaction. There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into KRW, but exporters must report the collection within the statutory timeline and may hold the funds in a foreign currency account. Overall, the regime is business-friendly but demands rigorous real-time reporting and conservative bank audit trails, making an unbanked fintech route impossible for onshore KRW settlement.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (as amended, Act No. 15677), a Korean Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily required to appoint an independent certified CPA auditor if, as of the immediately preceding fiscal year-end, its balance-sheet total assets equal or exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9.0 million). The audit obligation also applies without regard to asset size if the company is: (i) a listed issuer on KOSPI/KOSDAQ; (ii) a financial institution, financial holding company or otherwise separately regulated under financial services law; (iii) designated as a public-interest company by the Financial Services Commission (FSC); or (iv) required to present consolidated financial statements under the External Audit Act. There is no standalone turnover-only or headcount-only statutory audit trigger for ordinary unlisted private companies; turnover and headcount are relevant only where the FSC, exercising designation power, classifies the company as subject to external audit based on public-interest criteria. A company below the KRW 12 billion asset threshold, and not otherwise designated, is not statutorily required to undergo an independent audit, unless the company voluntarily opts for an audit under the Act.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive corporate income tax of 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% over KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% over KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion and 24% over KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the corporate income tax payable (aggregate statutory range 9.9%-26.4%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate withholding imposed on outbound dividends (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax); treaty rates generally range from 0% to 15%, with 10% being common for direct qualified shareholdings of 25% or more, provided the beneficial owner condition is satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within three months after the end of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year entity the deadline is March 31 of the following year. A one-month extension is generally available when audited financial statements are attached, and further extension up to six months for consolidated or specified large taxpayers is possible.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50,000,000,000 (KRW 50 billion) in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the Local File requirement; the Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 100 billion and the Korean entity's foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion. Smaller transactions below KRW 100 million per related counterparty may qualify for simplified documentation, but the overall documentation obligation remains screened by the NTS.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulation statute of the UAE model, but the NTS applies the substance-over-form principle under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 and International Tax Coordination Law. The Korean warehouse entity must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea: physical warehouse management, cross-docking operations, inventory and order processing, freight coordination, credit and collection risk, and strategic logistics decisions through employees, premises and board meetings

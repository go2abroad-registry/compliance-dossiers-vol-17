Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists for a Chusik Hoesa under the amended Korean Commercial Act. However, before filing for incorporation registration, the entire issue price of all shares issued at incorporation must be fully paid in cash to a commercial bank in Korea and evidenced by a certificate of deposit of the subscription price. No separate escrow or blocked account system is required by Korean law, but the deposited amounts may not be withdrawn before registration except in the limited circumstances permitted by the Commercial Act. The bank certificate must accompany the court filing under Articles 301 and 317(2).

No Civil Defense manufacturing fire permit is required for a digital AI avatar video generation software company.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr; the Korea Customs Service at unipass.customs.go.kr handles export clearance for VAT zero-rating evidence.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, the statutory SOP is: (1) confirm subject status for Korean corporate tax and VAT by examining domestic place of business, permanent establishment, or Korean e-services consumer nexus; (2) appoint a statutory tax representative/certified tax accountant in Korea, where no fixed place of business exists; (3) file the foreign corporation registration application and business registration application with the relevant district tax office via HomeTax, attaching the certified home-country incorporation document, Korean translation of charter, foreign business license, power of attorney, and evidence of domestic place of business or tax representative; (4) obtain the Korean business registration number, which is the tax identification number and VAT registration certificate number; (5) if supplying electronic services to Korean non-VAT persons, complete the HomeTax non-resident electronic service supplier VAT registration workflow using the home-country TIN, email address, and payment settlement evidence; (6) for corporate income tax, if a domestic business establishment is created, file the domestic business establishment registration with NTS; if no domestic business establishment, register only through the domestic withholding agent; (7) maintain all documents and submit e-tax invoice issuance configuration before commencing taxable supplies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Korea does not have a turnover threshold that exempts a resident stock corporation or a foreign non-resident electronic service supplier from VAT registration; tax registration is mandatory at the start of business or first taxable supply. An individual with expected annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000 may enter the simplified taxpayer regime, but this is not available to a Chusik Hoesa or to a non-resident corporate supplier of electronic services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimed under the Korean VAT Act when exported goods are transferred out of Korea under a customs export declaration and when services are exported or supplied to a non-resident person outside Korea, including qualifying offshore digital services such as AI avatar video generation SaaS. The supplier must separately identify the zero-rated value in the VAT return and retain evidence proving the overseas destination or recipient, actual use abroad, and the receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident counterparty. For digital services delivered online, the place of supply is treated as the customer's location; supplies to non-residents are zero-rated rather than exempt, thereby preserving the input VAT refund through a VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)

All VAT invoices in Korea must be issued as e-tax invoices under the NTS e-Tax Invoice System and transmitted into the HomeTax real-time fiscal clearance server by the business day following supply; issuance requires an official digital certificate and, for large or registered taxpayers, the HomeTax API or EDI channel. The e-tax invoice data are matched with customs export declarations, cash receipts, and payment gateway records to detect under-declaration. For SaaS supplies to Korean consumers, B2C cash receipts are integrated with the same anti-fraud digital stamping framework, and fraud indicators trigger statutory tax investigations.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service exchange export and VAT data; zero-rating is reversible if the required customs documentation, bill of lading, or foreign exchange settlement certificate is not procured within 180 days. Reversal creates VAT liability, interest, and additional tax penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes, with criminal prosecution for fraudulent refund claims and clawback of refunded input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates under a liberalized foreign exchange system governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree. As of the current regime, the Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions and most capital account transactions are subject to a post-hoc reporting or prior declaration requirement depending on the size and nature. Foreign direct investment, including share acquisition in a Chusik Hoesa, is freely permitted under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, with a simple A to E reporting framework through designated foreign exchange banks. Profit remittances in the form of dividends and royalties are permitted once corporate tax and withholding tax obligations are settled, and the remittance must be processed through a domestic foreign exchange bank using designated purpose codes (e.g., 600201 for dividend remittance). There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may maintain foreign currency accounts with domestic banks, but export proceeds must be received within six months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is approved. Capital account transactions, including trade loans and external borrowing, are subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act notification regime, and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) must be updated. The KRW is not fully offshore-convertible for non-residents without a valid underlying trade or investment purpose; non-residents cannot freely borrow KRW outside Korea. Swap and forward transactions are permitted for real exposure hedging, but speculative trading by non-residents is tightly monitored. Overall, the currency is convertible with a robust compliance ecosystem that requires meticulous documentation of the underlying economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 44 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related notices (Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, Articles 23-5), exporters must receive export proceeds within six months of the customs clearance date unless an approved extension is obtained from the MOEF. The proportion of proceeds required to be converted into KRW is effectively zero; exporters are allowed to retain 100% in a foreign currency held account at an authorized domestic bank, provided the receipt is reported to the bank within 30 days. The bank will issue a certified foreign currency receipt confirmation, which serves as the statutory evidence for tax deductions and customs settlement. Non-compliance triggers penalty surcharges and potential restrictions on the entity's foreign exchange banking privileges.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, M2, InPay) are legally barred from maintaining demand deposit accounts or issuing local IBAN/KRW routing numbers for South Korean entities without a domestic banking license. The FSC and FSS treat unlicensed financial services as criminal offenses under Article 17 of the Financial Business Structure Improvement Act. Any entity wishing to accept KRW must route through a licensed foreign exchange bank or an electronic financial transaction agent commissioned by a bank. Consequently, using an EMI to receive cross-border SaaS subscription revenue is not feasible for statutory treasury purposes; a direct domestic bank account is mandatory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.2% effective top statutory marginal rate (22% national corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the 22% national liability; applicable to taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate outbound dividend withholding tax for non-resident corporate shareholders (20% national Corporate Income Tax as withholding plus 2% local income tax), unless a Double Tax Agreement reduces the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File is required where cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion per fiscal year; Master File is mandatory where total annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and international related-party transaction amounts reach KRW 5 billion or more. Micro AI SaaS exporters below KRW 5 billion benefit from a documentation exemption but still must keep contemporaneous benchmark evidence.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however the NTS denies treaty benefits and PE status to a Korean Chusik Hoesa that lacks Core Income-Generating Activities. The CIGA test, derived from Article 7 of the OECD Model and the Multilateral Instrument provisions on preventing treaty abuse, requires that strategic R&D, risk management and AI model evaluation of the avatar video generation product actually occur in Korea. If legal ownership but operational control lies in Hong Kong or another treaty jurisdiction, the NTS will re-allocate income under the substance-over-form doctrine, disregard the Korean entity as a sham, and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Sham and Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 4 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, intentional under-declaration above KRW 500 million becomes a criminal prosecution: imprisonment of at least 3 years and up to 10 years, plus a fine up to 5 times the evaded tax; the NTS additionally imposes 40% of the underpaid tax as fraud additional tax under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind the corporate veil: under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act, a director is personally liable to creditors for wilful or grossly negligent acts. The NTS will blacklist directors and deny exit permits, confiscate foreign bank accounts and real property subject to asset preservation orders, and the director faces the full criminal imprisonment sentence applicable to the offense.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The NTS adjusts taxable income under Article 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, adds a 10% transfer pricing penalty under Article 12 of that Act, imposes a 20% under-reporting additional tax, and for excessive intercompany interest applies the thin-capitalization disallowance under Article 24. If intent is shown, a separate tax evasion prosecution is added.

Director personal liability: Directors who sanctioned the fake intercompany price have civil restitution exposure under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act; a director who personally received the shifted profit is treated as receiving a deemed dividend and subject to progressive tax plus criminal fraud penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act. NTS blacklisting with transfer pricing criminal indicia can be placed before trial and can cancel business permits and visas of foreign directors.
Failure to File Return and False Withholding on Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Article 73 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, a withholding agent that intentionally fails to withhold incurs a penalty equal to 22% of the amount not withheld, plus daily late filing surcharges of 0.04% and a 20% additional tax for under-reporting. Where evaded tax exceeds KRW 1 billion, the maximum imprisonment is 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for making payments without withholding are jointly and severally liable for all penalties under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act; a travel ban and asset freeze are immediately enforceable by the NTS, and the director faces criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent non-filing. If tax officials were bribed, the director also faces liability under the Special Crimes Act.

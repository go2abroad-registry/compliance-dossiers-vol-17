Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The amended Commercial Act does not impose any mandatory minimum paid-up capital for a general Chusik Hoesa; the founding share capital may be fixed at any positive amount, and no blocked escrow account obligation exists by statute. The only procedural safeguard is the requirement that the aggregate subscription proceeds be fully deposited into a nominal bank account established in the name of the company at a Korean commercial bank or securities depository institution prior to the convening of the incorporation meeting. The subscribed amount must be paid in cash or, if the articles so permit, in kind with an independent inspector's valuation report. Cross-border subscription payments must conform to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and be evidenced by a Korean bank remittance certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Anjeon Jeomgeom) issued by the local District Fire Department under the Fire Services Act is required for the registered office and any storage space, demonstrating operation of detectors, extinguishers, and evacuation signage; a Civil Defense Shelter exemption is assumed unless floors exceed 720 square meters. In addition, the workplace must obtain a general workplace safety clearance through the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency (KOSHA) under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including a manual handling risk assessment for boxes of supplements, emergency exit plans, and electrical safety certification for any battery-operated equipment; the fire and safety inspection certificates must be posted in each workplace of the company and exhibited to the inspecting authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a broadly liberalized convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Current account transactions, including trade payments and ordinary service fees, are freely convertible with no central bank approval; banks merely verify the underlying trade document and prescribe transaction purpose codes for FEIS reporting. Capital account transactions are general-market-convertible but require notification, declaration, or approval depending on event type. For a Chusik Hoesa receiving foreign direct investment, the non-resident investor must file a foreign investment notification with a Korean foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy before advancing equity capital, and the funds must be deposited in a foreign currency deposit account. There is no mandatory exchange surrender for inward investment or export earnings, but every receipt above USD 10,000-equivalent must be traceable to a bank report. Profit remittance via dividend distribution is allowed after corporate tax settlement and is not subject to a separate remittance cap; the bank will verify corporate tax payment certificates and the withholding tax payment record. Transfer pricing, tax clearance and beneficial ownership paperwork are the practical hurdles, not convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed under Korean law; exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign currency, and no surrender to the central bank is required. The statutory requirement is that export proceeds must be collected through a prescribed payment method and physically received within six months from the export declaration date; an extension may be granted only by the MOEF/Bank of Korea for justifiable causes. All export proceeds must be reported to the responsible foreign exchange bank, and any uncollected balance after six months must be explained in writing. If the proceeds are received in a domestic foreign currency deposit account, they can be freely converted to Korean won without tax or penalty as long as the underlying export declaration and Invoice and Packing List are on file.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Financial Services Commission (FSC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Barred for Primary Banking. In South Korea, only commercial banks and licensed internet banks such as Kakaobank and Toss Bank may lawfully accept deposits and hold operating funds of corporations under the Korean Banking Act. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital payment providers such as Airwallex, Payoneer or Currenxie do not have Korean banking licenses, cannot provide a domestic KRW IBAN, and cannot be used as the obligor structure for import/export settlement or capital escrow. They also cannot act as a substitute for a local FX bank account for FEIS reporting or foreign investment verification. The firm may use such an EMI only as an auxiliary offshore collection and disbursement overlay for ecommerce sales in smaller payment corridors, provided the main corporate account is maintained at a licensed Korean bank. FSS regulations treat unlicensed cross-border money transmitters as illegal when they serve local entities without an Electronic Money Transmitter license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% top marginal national corporate income tax rate; lower progressive brackets are 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million and 19% on taxable income over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, with an additional 10% local income tax surcharge on each national tax component.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income surcharge, producing an aggregate 22% statutory dividend withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korean entities with annual gross sales exceeding KRW 100 billion, or with aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion, are required to prepare and submit a Local File and, where the group consolidates, a Master File under the International Tax Adjustment Act. The Country-by-Country Report is mandatory when the multinational consolidated group revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion. High-risk cross-border e-commerce sellers may be required to file transfer-pricing documentation regardless of the general threshold where the NTS issues a documentation request.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a full Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code as in the UAE, but the National Tax Service applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse doctrines under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act. The entity claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate genuine economic presence, personnel, and business decision-making. For a Korean supplement entity, Core Income-Generating Activities such as local order fulfilment, Korean customer support, inventory management, local compliance with food and drug registration, and e-commerce platform risk management are attributes that Korean tax authorities treat as Korean sources of profit. A foreign shell holding company with no CIGA will be denied withholding relief, and its interposed transactions may be recharacterised as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: This violates the Corporate Tax Act and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act. The National Tax Service may impose a 20% unfaithful-return surcharge on the underpaid tax, increase the surcharge to 40% for fraudulent returns, and add a daily delinquency surcharge of 0.022%. Criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to five times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors knowingly conceal revenue or fabricate expenses. Directors are jointly and severally liable to restore the evaded corporate tax from personal assets, may be blacklisted for overseas travel, and may face criminal imprisonment. The NTS may attach personal bank accounts and real estate where the director is found to have controlled the fraudulent reporting.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion Through Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 5 through 9 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, the NTS may adjust the transaction to the arm's-length price, impose a 20% negligence surcharge on the understated tax, and assess local income tax surcharge. Failure to submit the mandatory Local File and Master File is punishable by a documentary penalty of up to KRW 20 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the transfer-pricing return knowing that it lacks a factual or economic basis are personally exposed to civil restitution orders for the underpaid tax, travel bans under NTS blacklisting procedures, and criminal prosecution for abetting tax evasion. Where the structure is a sham, the director may be de facto taxpayer for the adjusted corporate income, and the foreign asset trail may attach personal liability.
Shell Company Abuse, AML UBO Failure, and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny treaty relief under the beneficial-ownership and Limitation on Benefits rules, reapply the 22% statutory withholding, and assess negligence surcharges. The Financial Transaction Reports Act provides for fines of up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment of up to three years for the willful failure to report the ultimate beneficial owner of a high-risk account. Where the criminal proceeds are laundered, the predicate offence can attract imprisonment of up to seven years under Korean anti-money laundering legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate the shell arrangement are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax as withholding agents. The corporate veil is pierced for fraud, permitting creditors and the NTS to execute against director assets. Travel bans and blacklisting are imposed where unpaid national tax exceeds the statutory threshold. Criminal conviction can result in imprisonment and confiscation of assets traceable to the tax fraud and money laundering.

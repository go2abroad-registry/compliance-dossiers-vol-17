Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under Korean law; however, each member's contribution must be fully paid in cash or in-kind before registration. The stated capital must be deposited in a provisional bank account opened in the name of the company prior to incorporation, and the deposit balance certificate must be attached to the registration application. No blocked escrow account beyond this pre-registration deposit is required; the deposit is released to the company's corporate account upon completion of incorporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalized but cautiously supervised convertible currency regime. The Korean won is freely convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related settlements, dividends, royalties, and service payments, subject to declaration through a licensed bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, capital loans, and portfolio investment, require prior or simultaneous reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but are not generally subject to per-transaction approval unless classified as requiring enhanced regulation or exceeding prescribed thresholds. For a South Korean entity owned by non-residents, foreign exchange receipts can be retained in a domestic foreign currency demand deposit account indefinitely or converted into Korean won through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Profit remittances of dividends and liquidation proceeds are lawful after presenting tax clearance, audited financial statements, and the underlying foreign investment documentation. The central operational hurdle is proving the legitimate origin of the inbound FDI and substantiating the payout base, because banks will conduct enhanced due diligence on any dividend or loan repayment that appears inconsistent with revenue, equity, or documented trade volume.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea applies no fixed mandatory foreign currency surrender ratio. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds for goods must be collected within one year from the date of the export declaration unless an extension is approved by the head of the responsible foreign exchange bank, and the full amount must be received into a domestic foreign-currency or won-denominated account. Receipt may be kept in foreign currency or converted to Korean won. Any collection method not recognized by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, or any retention abroad after the permitted term without approval, constitutes a violation and may result in administrative fines and foreign exchange transaction restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange oversight authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Foreign digital banks and EMI-type platforms such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are not licensed banks in South Korea and are legally barred from opening corporate demand deposit accounts, issuing KRW account numbers, or acting as the primary account servicer for a Korean resident limited company. The Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea permit only licensed financial companies, including internet-only banks such as KakaoBank, K Bank, and Toss Bank, to accept deposits and provide foreign exchange services. Foreign EMIs can only support cross-border payment initiation as technical gateways if the underlying transaction is cleared through a licensed domestic bank or a registered money transmitter, and even then the Korean entity must maintain its main operational and capital accounts at a Tier-1 Korean bank. Therefore, the viability of an offshore EMI as the company principal treasury rail is Low; it should be used only as an ancillary collection viewer and never for capital escrow, statutory dividend remittance, or trade settlement with a Korean tax or customs reporting requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI proceeds for the Yuhan Hoesa must first be notified to an authorized foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act before the remittance wiring is executed. The foreign investor must submit a foreign investment report signed by the representative, together with a certified copy of the company business registration certificate, articles of incorporation, and a board resolution approving the investment. Funds are remitted via SWIFT MT103 with the investor name and the FDI capital injection purpose code in the remittance information field, and the receiving Korean bank will post them to a foreign currency deposit account or a won-denominated demand deposit account. The bank issues a foreign currency receipt certificate, which combined with the foreign investment report number is the primary capital verification evidence for the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy and the tax authorities. Companies failing to file the report before payment risk administrative fines and delayed repatriation of dividends; therefore the bank should receive the notification receipt number before the wire is released.For outbound trade disbursements, corporates should use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers, commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 for higher-value settlements, and documentary collections under URC 522. Korean banks clear Korean won payments via BOK-Wire+ and foreign-currency payments via the correspondent SWIFT network. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign digital banks and EMI-type platforms such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are not licensed banks in South Korea and are legally barred from opening corporate demand deposit accounts, issuing KRW account numbers, or acting as the primary account servicer for a Korean resident limited company. The Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea permit only licensed financial companies, including internet-only banks such as KakaoBank, K Bank, and Toss Bank, to accept deposits and provide foreign exchange services. Foreign EMIs can only support cross-border payment initiation as technical gateways if the underlying transaction is cleared through a licensed domestic bank or a registered money transmitter, and even then the Korean entity must maintain its main operational and capital accounts at a Tier-1 Korean bank. Therefore, the viability of an offshore EMI as the company principal treasury rail is Low; it should be used only as an ancillary collection viewer and never for capital escrow, statutory dividend remittance, or trade settlement with a Korean tax or customs reporting requirement.

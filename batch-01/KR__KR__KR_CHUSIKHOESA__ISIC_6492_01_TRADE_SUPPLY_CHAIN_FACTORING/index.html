Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for formation of a Chusik Hoesa. However, all subscription proceeds must be deposited into a blocked capital-deposit account held at a licensed commercial bank or securities company in the Republic of Korea before the registration application is filed; the funds remain frozen and may not be withdrawn or pledged until the Court Registry certifies incorporation. The Financial Services Commission may impose an additional minimum net capital requirement when granting the Credit Specialized Financial Business license for factoring.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to industrial or manufacturing units; the entity must only obtain the standard commercial office fire safety and occupancy approval from the local fire department in the district where the registered office is located, covering fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and electrical safety. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold for a corporation. A Chusik Hoesa is required to register for VAT from the date it commences business activity in Korea, irrespective of annual revenue, because simplified taxpayer status is reserved for individual businesses and is not available to corporations. For reference, an individual whose annual taxable supplies are less than KRW 80,000,000 may qualify as a simplified taxable person, but this status is not available to a Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, VAT at 0% applies to exported goods and to specified services supplied to non-residents for use outside Korea. For goods, the statutory evidence is an accepted Korea Customs Service export declaration filed through UNI-PASS showing the Korean supplier as exporter and physical removal of the goods from Korea. For qualifying offshore digital and cross-border services, the supplier must demonstrate that the recipient is established outside Korea, the service is performed or used outside Korea, the contract is with a foreign customer, and payment is received in convertible foreign currency. Material limitation: the factoring fee and discount income of the Chusik Hoesa is a financial service exempt from VAT under Article 26 of the Value-Added Tax Act; therefore the core accounts-receivable factoring and reverse-factoring commissions are not zero-rated and cannot generate an input VAT refund. Zero-rating can be claimed only for any separately taxable goods or services supplied by the Chusik Hoesa, not for the exempt factoring services.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) showing the Korean supplier as exporter
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment of goods from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List reconciled with the export declaration
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or foreign currency receipt confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act
  • Certificate of Export Performance or other zero-rating export evidence requested by the National Tax Service during audit

The Chusik Hoesa, as a corporation, is mandatorily required to issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) under Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act for all taxable supplies to other businesses. e-Tax Invoices are generated through the HomeTax e-Invoice service via the HomeTax web portal, NTS-certified ERP software, or the NTS API, and must be transmitted to the National Tax Service by no later than the day following issuance. The NTS returns a unique acceptance number upon successful transmission, which functions as the anti-fraud digital validation stamp. Correction invoices are separately transmitted, and any mismatched invoice is subject to penalty taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Korea does not operate a Latin American-style physical fiscal stamp; integrity is achieved through mandatory electronic transmission, acceptance-number tracking, purchaser confirmation on HomeTax, and cross-matching with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations and foreign exchange bank settlement records.

The NTS operates an integrated VAT control system that automatically cross-matches HomeTax e-Tax Invoices with Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declarations and foreign exchange settlement records. If zero-rating is claimed without the required customs and foreign-exchange documentation, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and assessed at 10% VAT, input VAT attributable to that supply is disallowed, and the related refund is clawed back with penalty surcharges under Articles 47-2 and 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, which can reach up to 40% for fraudulent concealment or unfounded refunds. Criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may follow, exposing responsible parties to imprisonment and fines, and the statute of limitations for collection is extended to ten years in cases of fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized but closely supervised foreign exchange system. Current account transactions are convertible without prior authorization, but all cross-border transfers must be routed through a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank and properly documented. Capital account transactions, such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and trade finance cross-border settlements, are generally permitted but trigger mandatory reporting, registration, or approval obligations with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the supervising bank. The Korean won is not a fully internationalized offshore deliverable currency; onshore KRW is freely usable within Korea, but offshore won liquidity and non-deliverable market instruments remain regulated. Export proceeds must be repatriated through a Korean licensed bank within the statutory collection period. Dividend and profit remittances are subject to corporate tax clearance, withholding tax proof, and audited financial statements, but there is no absolute prohibition on profit repatriation. The central bank can impose emergency verification or suspension measures during currency crises, and balance-of-payments and foreign exchange reporting deadlines must be strictly observed. For this factoring entity, convertibility is operationally workable provided that every inbound and outbound remittance is channelled through regulated banks, tagged with the correct Korean FX purpose code, and reconciled with necessary trade documents and tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korean export proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system through a licensed foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the export declaration date, unless an extension of up to six months is pre-approved by the bank due to exceptional trade terms. The full amount of export foreign currency receipts must be collected and credited to a Foreign Exchange Account at a Korean bank or converted into Korean won; there is no partial mandatory surrender ratio, but 100% of export proceeds must ultimately be reported and held inside the authorized Korean banking perimeter.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Standalone fintech EMI viability is legally very low. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act restrict foreign exchange business and deposit-taking intermediation to licensed banks and authorized electronic financial companies. A pure offshore fintech EMI cannot open a corporate KRW settlement account or provide escrow and trade factoring clearing without deep sponsorship by a licensed domestic bank. Offshore EMIs such as Wise or Revolut may act as payment transport layers, but they are not acceptable settlement agents for the underlying factoring receivables and cannot satisfy Korean foreign exchange reporting or capital verification requirements. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on tier-1 commercial banks for all account infrastructure and use fintech products only as ancillary user interfaces connected to a licensed bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into KR_CHUSIKHOESA must be routed through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 transfers from the overseas shareholder's substantive corporate bank account. The transfer must include the correct inward capital FX purpose code as required by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, and the receiving bank will report the transaction to Bank of Korea as part of the balance-of-payments statistics. Funds may be received in USD, EUR, JPY, or CNY and then converted onshore to KRW at the domestic bank's reference rate. The entity must produce the board resolution approving the equity injection, a shareholder investment agreement, source-of-wealth documentation, and the final external audit report confirming paid-in capital; the corporate registry must be amended, and the bank will perform capital verification before funds are available for trade factoring operations. Unlicensed EMIs must not be used for any inbound equity capital because they cannot generate the mandatory BOK reporting proof.Outbound trade disbursements and factoring proceeds should be cleared exclusively through the domestic tier-1 bank's trade settlement infrastructure. Direct supplier payments and factoring settlements should be made through SWIFT MT103 transfers with the full remittance information and Korean foreign exchange purpose codes. For high-value tends to large import/export contracts, a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection arrangement is appropriate to preserve title and enforce payment conditions. All underlying trade documentation must be retained for at least five years and matched to the bank's foreign exchange reports. Factoring payouts to non-resident parties will trigger foreign exchange reporting, outward remittance tax verification, and reconciliation with the overseas borrowing or assignment agreement; the bank may require evidence of the factorized receivable, assignment notice, and confirmation from the corporate borrower before releasing large cross-border payments.Onshore KRW/USD deliverable forward contracts with a licensed domestic bank for documented trade exposureKRW/CNH and KRW/JPY forward contracts where real export or import flows existOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) used only for permitted exposure and executed through the domestic bank's non-deliverable forward reporting windowCross-currency swaps between KRW and settlement currencies for matched factoring assets, subject to real trade exposure documentation and bank/BOK regulatory filing

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Standalone fintech EMI viability is legally very low. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act restrict foreign exchange business and deposit-taking intermediation to licensed banks and authorized electronic financial companies. A pure offshore fintech EMI cannot open a corporate KRW settlement account or provide escrow and trade factoring clearing without deep sponsorship by a licensed domestic bank. Offshore EMIs such as Wise or Revolut may act as payment transport layers, but they are not acceptable settlement agents for the underlying factoring receivables and cannot satisfy Korean foreign exchange reporting or capital verification requirements. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on tier-1 commercial banks for all account infrastructure and use fintech products only as ancillary user interfaces connected to a licensed bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory if the related-party transaction amount (per year, per counterparty) exceeds KRW 10 billion (KRW 10,000,000,000); simplified Local File is required when the aggregate related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 5 billion (KRW 5,000,000,000) irrespective of counterparty. A Transaction Summary Report (TSR) is mandatory for transactions exceeding KRW 100 million (KRW 100,000,000) per related party if total related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime akin to offshore financial centres, but a substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in the Corporate Income Tax Act, the Framework Act on National Taxes (anti-abuse rule Article 17-2), and the International Tax Adjustment Act. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in factoring, the tax authority may recharacterize transactions lacking business purpose, employees, office premises, and real receivable management functions. A factoring entity must demonstrate operational substance through issued factoring contracts, credit evaluation records, collection ledgers, and board resolutions. Shell companies with no Korean economic activity to claim treaty benefits are subject to denial of treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits (LOB) provisions of relevant DTAs and under the domestic general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to 3 times the evaded tax amount under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; additionally, the NTS imposes 40% negligence penalty and 20% underreporting penalty on the evaded tax plus 0.022% daily surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally or negligently participate in tax evasion may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax arrears under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 39. Criminal imprisonment applies to acting directors; travel bans are automatically issued for tax arrears above KRW 50 million. Civil restitution may extend to unjust enrichment received by directors.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, imposition of 20% penalty on the taxable amount adjusted, and refusal of foreign tax credit. Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, a 20% penalty may be imposed on the amount of income derived from a transaction lacking economic substance; the entity may additionally lose its corporate identity for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial interposed structures may be personally assessed for the corporate tax under the actual management liability principle; the NTS may pierce the veil where the entity is used as a fraud instrument. Travel bans and asset seizures are available for outstanding corporate liabilities exceeding KRW 50 million.
AML and Financial Regulatory Violation

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to KRW 10 million for conducting unlicensed financial business; the Financial Services Commission may revoke registration and impose administrative fines up to KRW 100 million. For violation of reporting duties, imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to KRW 20 million for individual violations; additional fines for the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for breach of fiduciary duty to maintain AML compliance; they may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of fraudulently transferred funds. Travel bans are imposed in case of criminal prosecution; imprisonment sentences are served individually.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: NTS adjustment of the taxable income under the arm's length principle; 20% penalty on the adjusted amount and an additional 10% penalty for failure to submit TP documentation; daily surcharge applies from the date the return was due. If foreign tax credit claimed without proper documentation, the credit is denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false transfer pricing filing or who are involved in the preparation of flawed documentation may be personally fined up to KRW 10 million under the Framework Act on National Taxes; civil liability for the unpaid tax difference may be imposed in case of willful neglect.
Under-Withholding on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the unwithheld amount plus an additional 10% negligence penalty, and the recipient remains liable for unpaid tax; daily surcharge applies. In serious cases, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may be initiated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly effect a foreign payment without withholding are jointly liable for the unremitted tax and may be subjected to travel bans and asset freezes; criminal imprisonment up to 2 years may be imposed for intentional violation.
VAT and Indirect Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT penalty of 2% of the falsely issued invoice amount; omitted VAT subject to 40% penalty plus daily surcharge; issuance of false VAT invoice carries criminal imprisonment up to 2 years and fine up to 2 times the VAT evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who oversee invoicing and VAT returns are personally liable for unpaid VAT if they are found to be the actual business operator; for criminal violation, imprisonment is served personally.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permitted only as a supplementary registered-address amenity, not as the sole commercial substance. The Korean Commercial Act and the National Tax Service require a physical place of business in Korea where the statutory books and payroll-equity records can be examined, legal notices served, and tax invoices issued. For an ESOP trust operating with direct payroll wires and equity transfers, banks will normally reject a pure commercial mailbox or a shared virtual office that has no dedicated suite, no on-site responsible person, no original lease, and no utility records. A virtual office can be accepted only if it is a serviced office with a physical dedicated room, full-time reception or management staff, a service-level agreement for records handling, and the ability to host an on-site inspection. Even then, the National Tax Service and the commercial bank may demand documentary proof of substance, including payroll records, payroll agent appointment, registered address signage, and director access.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act abolishes any statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the incorporators are free to determine the subscribed capital in the Articles of Incorporation. However, all subscribed shares must be fully paid in prior to registration, and the proceeds must be deposited in a blocked capital subscription escrow account at a bank licensed in Korea in the name of the promoter or the company. The bank must issue a paid-in capital certificate; the escrowed funds may only be withdrawn and transferred to the company's corporate operating account after the incorporation registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of South Korea, with the official online electronic filing and registration portal being Home Tax Service, operated by the National Tax Service.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that commences a business with a domestic place of business in South Korea must apply for business registration with the competent district tax office of the National Tax Service within 20 days from the date the business begins. The foreign entity must appoint a tax representative if it does not have a fixed place of business in Korea and is required to file tax returns. The application must be filed using the official business registration application form, supported by the foreign certificate of incorporation, relevant constitutional articles or equivalent organizational documents, evidence of a domestic place of business such as a lease agreement or registration of a branch office, appointment of a statutory tax agent or attorney, and proof of identity of the foreign entity's directors and officers. After verifying the documents, the National Tax Service issues a 10-digit business registration number which simultaneously serves as the VAT identification number and the central tax identification number for the Korean tax system. If the foreign non-resident entity has no domestic establishment and makes no domestic VAT-taxable supplies but earns South Korean-source income subject to withholding, it should obtain a foreign taxpayer registration or file withholding tax returns through the Home Tax Service rather than registering for VAT. The entity must subsequently report any changes in business location, ownership, or constitution through the Home Tax Service portal and must authenticate itself for electronic tax invoice issuance.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide check-the-box or comparable hybrid entity classification elections. A Chusik Hoesa is treated as a corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed as a separate tax entity on its worldwide income if it is a domestic corporation. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration exemption applies; all business operators carrying on VAT-taxable business activities must register with the National Tax Service regardless of turnover. A simplified VAT taxpayer regime applies to businesses with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Value-Added Tax Act article governing zero-rate exports. Goods exported outside of South Korea qualify if the exporter files a customs declaration with the Korean Customs Service, the goods physically depart Korea, and the exporter retains the export declaration and customs clearance confirmation. Qualifying overseas services supplied to non-residents, or services performed outside Korea for a foreign counterparty, also qualify for zero-rating only where the entity maintains documentary evidence that the services were performed and paid for in foreign exchange or under a qualifying foreign currency settlement. For an employee stock ownership trust administration service, zero-rating would apply only where the service is supplied to a non-resident recipient and falls within the statutory category of export services; the trust administrator must prove the foreign nature of the recipient, the overseas performance or usage of the service, and the receipt of proceeds in foreign currency. A zero-rated tax invoice must be issued through the electronic tax invoice system at the time of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted through the Korean Customs Service UNI-PASS system, showing the exporter of record, export date, commodity code, quantity and value
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list corresponding to the customs export declaration
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure from South Korea
  • Transaction contract or purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank, evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency or a certificate of payment through a lawful non-resident settlement method

South Korea mandates electronic tax invoice issuance and transmission through the Home Tax Service platform for business-to-business transactions. Taxable suppliers must issue an electronic tax invoice immediately on the taxable transaction date and transmit the written or electronic tax invoice data to the National Tax Service in near-real-time through the Home Tax Service system. The national e-tax invoice system operates as a continuous clearance mechanism for VAT records. For business-to-consumer transactions, the mandatory cash receipt system requires registered merchants to report consumer payment data to the National Tax Service when requested, enabling transaction traceability. There is no separate physical stamping or pre-clearance of invoices; instead, the National Tax Service performs real-time matching, risk assessment, and post-transaction audit using the integrated transaction data obtained through e-tax invoices and cash receipts.

The National Tax Service and the Korean Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, e-tax invoice issuance, foreign exchange receipt records, and the exporter's global transaction data. If documents are found to be fraudulent, incomplete, or inconsistent, or if the foreign exchange proceeds are not collected or properly settled within the statutory 180-day repatriation period or an authorized extension, the zero-rating is retroactively reversed and the export supply becomes taxable at the standard 10% VAT rate. The taxpayer is also liable for additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act, including the unconstitutionally unpaid VAT, additional charges for late filing and late payment, and penalty taxes for false electronic tax invoices. In cases involving intentional falsification or tax evasion, the tax authority may impose criminal penalties, including criminal fines or imprisonment, and may refer the case for prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating won but retains a legal framework of managed convertibility. Current-account payments, including trade settlements, service fees, dividends and normal trust expenses, are generally permitted without prior approval if transacted through a designated foreign exchange bank with the correct BOP/foreign exchange purpose code. Capital-account operations are subject to stricter gates: inward direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa must use the Foreign Investment Promotion Act procedure or, for non-foreign-investment capital calls, a report through the Foreign Exchange Information System; cross-border loans and security dealings require BOK reporting or approval. The won is not freely offered by offshore banks as a settlement currency, and Korean-domiciled accounts are required for resident tax withholding and capital verification. Profit remittances from the ESOP trust or the employer require audited accounts, a withholding tax certificate, and tax-resident declarations. Since 2010 macroprudential measures on banks' FX derivative positions have enforced real-demand hedging, so synthetic currency trades without commercial exposure are discouraged. There is no forced surrender of export receipts; funds can be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted at market rates, or used for approved payments. This converts pure convertibility into a compliance-driven system rather than an absolute guarantee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies: 0% of export proceeds must be exchanged into KRW. Exporters must receive proceeds within 12 months from the date of export declaration unless an extended due date is accepted by the designated foreign exchange bank; proceeds retained in a domestic foreign-currency account must be reported and can later be converted or disbursed under applicable Foreign Exchange Transactions Act rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the foreign exchange administration authority, exercising supervision under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service for banking prudential enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and effectively non-viable as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI providers without a Korean banking license cannot open domestic won settlement accounts, cannot access BOK-Wire+, and cannot legally submit foreign exchange declarations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korean law reserves deposit-taking and payment settlement to licensed domestic banks and authorized electronic financial companies; a foreign EMI may serve only as a supplementary transfer instructing through a bank partner, never as the custody or clearing account for the ESOP trust.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax top marginal rate, plus 10% local income tax surtax, producing a 26.4% combined top statutory rate for taxable income above KRW 30 billion; lower brackets are 9%, 19% and 21%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% for non-resident shareholders (20% corporate or income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax), applied to dividends actually distributed or deemed distributed through the ESOP trust.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 of annual related-party international transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File; related-party transactions equal to or above KRW 5,000,000,000 require a simplified international transaction report attached to the corporate income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the Korean tax courts and tax authorities apply the equivalent substance-over-form and beneficial ownership doctrines. The Chusik Hoesa acting as ESOP trustee must perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea: employees conducting equity plan administration, local payroll records, board decisions, trust accounting, and custody of share registers; otherwise the structure can be recharacterised as a foreign controlled corporation or treaty-shopping conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Falsified Income Statement

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act Article 3, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment ranges from 3 to 10 years for habitual offenders.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and order directors personally to restore evaded tax and civil penalties; a delinquent director will be registered with the National Tax Service high-risk blacklist and may be subject to an immigration departure ban under the Framework Act on National Taxes.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: A withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable for an additional tax equal to 50% of the unwithheld amount under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4; interest accrues until payment and the underlying payment remains taxable in Korea.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the remittance are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, penalties, and interest; the tax authority can seize personal bank accounts and enforce a departure ban while the delinquent amount is pending.
Shell Company - Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied and the amount is recharacterised as a domestic dividend or royalty subject to 22% final withholding under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; a 20% understatement penalty and, in fraud cases, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act may apply.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for lack of substance and for acting solely as a conduit; directors are civilly liable for restitution of treaty benefits obtained, blacklisted as habitual tax evaders, prohibited from leaving Korea, and can be sentenced to up to 5 years imprisonment for intentional submission of false treaty relief documentation.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Commercial Act and court registration practice, a post-office box or mail-forwarding service is not a valid registered office. A virtual office may be conditionally tolerated by regional tax offices for administrative service and VAT-invoice mailing, but it is not accepted as the statutory registered seat by Korean courts. For an ISIC 9000 / NAICS 511130 digital webtoon publishing company receiving Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds, Korean banks will ordinarily reject a virtual-office-only arrangement for corporate account opening because CDD requires a physical business address, lease proof, utility receipts and management access. A serviced co-working space with a private assigned room and physical signage can satisfy the registry and bank requirements only if the service provider allows government and bank inspections, board meetings and mail receipt at that address. Pure mailbox programs are not permissible.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, funds must be held in a corporate banking account and must be sufficient to cover initial operating expenses. No escrow or blocked account is required for incorporation; capital verification is completed via a capital deposit certificate issued by the financial institution where the paid-in capital is deposited.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - the company is an e-publisher with no physical production site, no storage of flammable materials, no heavy machinery, and no workforce above the small-scale threshold that would trigger a special fire safety inspection. The registered office, being a standard commercial lease, must retain a valid 'Fire and Evacuation Plan' (that plan is obtained by the property owner from the local fire station) and shall comply with ordinary building management rules. No separate Civil Defense fire permit is required for a pure digital content operation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea, with the HomeTax electronic tax portal (www.hometax.go.kr) for registration, filing, and e-Tax Invoice reporting; export declarations are processed by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS trade portal (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity cannot directly register as the domestic taxpayer; it must first incorporate or acquire a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act and complete court registration at the local District Court registry. Immediately after incorporation, the Chusik Hoesa must apply for a Business Registration Certificate with the competent NTS District Tax Office through HomeTax using the representative's digital certificate and an authorized foreign power of attorney where directors are outside Korea. Required documentation includes the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of the registered address, lease or ownership documents for the domestic place of business, the statutory seat designation, identification of shareholders and directors, and evidence of the foreign capital contribution under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act where applicable. The tax office will issue the 10-digit corporate registration number, which functions as the Tax Identification Number and is mandatory for issuing e-Tax Invoices. The application must be finalized before the company enters its first VAT-taxable supply chain, and the registration will be updated for any domestic branch, place of business, or warehouse separately if a permanent establishment is later added.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to a Chusik Hoesa; every corporate VAT taxpayer must register at the commencement of business activities. The KRW 80 million simplified-taxpayer threshold applies only to certain individual entrepreneurs and is not available to corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act for exported goods and for services supplied to or used outside Korea, including qualifying offshore digital content syndication. For webtoon chapter-unlocking services, the Chusik Hoesa must prove that the service is supplied to a non-resident purchaser or foreign platform, the digital content is delivered or consumed outside the Korean customs territory, and the consideration is remitted through a Korean foreign exchange bank or another traceable foreign-sourced payment channel. The service export must satisfy the Enforcement Decree test for export services, and the zero-rated supply is recognized only where the place of supply is outside Korea and the contracting counterparty is not a Korean domestic consumer.

  • Korea Customs Service Electronic Export Declaration (customs clearance confirmation issued through UNI-PASS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing shipment to a foreign consignee, where physical goods are involved
  • Commercial Invoice and the executed digital content license or webtoon syndication agreement
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Export Proceeds Receipt Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank as evidence of collection of foreign currency
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice issued through NTS HomeTax for the exported digital service
  • Platform settlement statements from Apple IAP or Google Play Billing showing foreign-origin payments and deduction of app-store commissions

The Chusik Hoesa must issue e-Tax Invoices electronically through NTS HomeTax, EDI, or NTS-approved API integration, and must transmit tax-invoice data to the NTS electronic clearing database without using physical fiscal stamps. E-invoice details must be submitted to the NTS database by the day after issuance. B2C transactions from webtoon distribution made through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing must be substantiated by the platform settlement reports, e-payment transaction records, and cash-receipt system entries where required. The NTS carries out near-real-time matching of e-Tax Invoices, app-store payment data, and cash receipts for anti-fraud clearance.

The National Tax Service may reject zero-rating where any part of the documentation bundle is missing, inconsistent, or untimely. Rejected export supplies are recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies, resulting in assessment of 10% VAT on the full consideration, recovery of any previously claimed input VAT credits, understatement surcharges of up to 40% for fraudulent filings plus daily late-payment interest, and potential civil penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating claims may also be referred for criminal investigation under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the NTS closely audits webtoon syndication platforms by cross-referencing app-store payment data, foreign-exchange remittance records, and Korean consumer access logs to detect fictionally exported digital content.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is a floating, generally freely convertible currency for current account transactions such as export proceeds, service fees, royalties, and digital content syndication payments. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act separates current account from capital account treatment. Current account transactions are liberalized and can be settled through designated foreign exchange banks using identity-based reporting. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, equity subscription, cross-border loans, and issuance of shares to non-residents, are also broadly liberalized but require mandatory pre-reporting or post-reporting to the Bank of Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank. There are no surrender requirements restricting the holding of export proceeds; exporters may maintain foreign currency in local accounts and convert at market rates. Profit remittance is achievable via lawful dividend distribution, but the treasurer must first satisfy corporate income tax settlement, verify distributable retained earnings under Korean Commercial Act rules, and pay the applicable dividend withholding tax for residents or the treaty-reduced rate for non-residents. In a crisis scenario, the Minister of Economy and Finance may impose temporary FX transaction restrictions with IMF notification, but such measures are exceptional and not part of the ordinary treasury process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be collected within one year from the date of export declaration. An extension of up to six months may be granted by the Korean authorities through the designated foreign exchange bank where justifiable commercial grounds are proved. No statutory percentage of export proceeds must be surrendered or converted into Korean won; the former foreign exchange concentration and surrender system has been abolished. Exporters may keep the full amount in a domestic foreign currency deposit account, disburse it for permitted purposes, or convert it into KRW at a market rate. In all cases, the designated bank should record the collection, and any receipt or payment exceeding the regulatory reporting threshold must be filed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service in the administration of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Payoneer, and Wise are not licensed as banks under the Korean Banking Act and are not designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They therefore cannot open a domestic KRW settlement account for a Korean Chusik Hoesa, cannot perform statutory foreign exchange reporting, and cannot accept the capital-injection or dividend-remittance reporting functions that Korean residency requires. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection wallets for non-Korean platform sales, with balances swept through SWIFT MT103 wires into a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. For all regulatory purposes, the Korean entity must maintain its operating capital and FX reporting relationships with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident equity injections must be routed to the Chusik Hoesa foreign currency deposit account at a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. The remitter submits a SWIFT MT103 with full remitter and beneficiary name, address, and beneficiary account number, and clearly indicates the remittance purpose as FDI equity subscription in field 70 using the bank-assigned foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment in a domestic corporation. Under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the investor files an investment report with KOTRA or the designated bank before or within twenty days after the remittance; the bank issues a foreign currency deposit certificate and capital verification confirms the paid-in share capital. The entity then files the share issuance report with the corporate registry and reflects the investment in its balance sheet. For amounts above the regulatory reporting threshold, the bank will separately submit the receipt report under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.For digital webtoon syndication, outbound disbursements are primarily royalties and service fees, best executed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer with total plus charges, referencing the underlying contract and tax invoice. Korean banks settle these service payments through the Foreign Exchange Information Network and will file a payment report when the transaction exceeds the regulatory threshold or when the designated bank requires reporting. For print merchandise or physical publication, the entity may also use a commercial letter of credit as a sight or usance documentary credit in favor of offshore printers, and a documentary collection with documents against payment or documents against acceptance for lower-risk physical trade. However, documentary trade instruments are not necessary for pure digital chapter unlocking; the principal clearing rail for the webtoon business is the bank-to-bank MT103 network supported by service agreements, invoices, and withholding tax certificates.Plain vanilla KRW/USD or KRW/JPY forward purchase and sale contracts permitted only to hedge current trade exposures such as Apple and Google payout receipts and royalty payment commitments, with underlying verification and no speculative open positions.Non-deliverable forwards on KRW are available to non-resident financial institutions and certain permitted resident corporates with verified underlying exposure; for the Chusik Hoesa, an onshore forward is preferable and NDF access will require real-demand substantiation and designated bank approval.Cross-currency swaps and FX options may be used only for hedging capital account exposures such as foreign currency debt, equity, dividends, and large royalty flows, and require the counterparty to be a domestic designated foreign exchange bank or a licensed international bank branch with full documentation of the underlying exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Revolut, Payoneer, and Wise are not licensed as banks under the Korean Banking Act and are not designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They therefore cannot open a domestic KRW settlement account for a Korean Chusik Hoesa, cannot perform statutory foreign exchange reporting, and cannot accept the capital-injection or dividend-remittance reporting functions that Korean residency requires. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection wallets for non-Korean platform sales, with balances swept through SWIFT MT103 wires into a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. For all regulatory purposes, the Korean entity must maintain its operating capital and FX reporting relationships with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 2 billion; 21% from KRW 2 billion to KRW 30 billion; 24% in excess of KRW 30 billion

22% for dividends paid by a Korean resident corporation to a non-resident corporate or individual shareholder, composed of a 20% national withholding tax and a 2% local income tax surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: cross-border related-party transactions equal to or exceeding KRW 5 billion. Master File: annual revenue equal to or exceeding KRW 50 billion and cross-border related-party transactions equal to or exceeding KRW 5 billion. Country-by-Country Report: multinational group with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding KRW 100 billion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies a substance-over-form principle and the general anti-abuse doctrine under the National Tax Basic Act. There is no statutory Economic Substance Regulations regime of the kind used in Gulf Cooperation Council jurisdictions, but the Korean tax authorities may disregard a corporate entity or transaction lacking genuine business purpose, employees, premises, and managerial functions. A webtoon IP holding company without economic substance in Korea will be refused treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as an artificial conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 40% of the underreported tax amount, daily overdue interest of approximately 0.022%, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act with imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine of up to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Representative directors and actual controlling shareholders can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under the National Tax Collection Act. Criminal conviction can result in imprisonment, travel restrictions, asset freezing, and restitution of the evaded amounts.
Shell Company and Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty benefits, recharacterization of payments as taxable Korean-source dividends or royalties, assessment of corporate tax and withholding tax at the 22% statutory rate, and a 40% non-deduction penalty on unsupported related-party payments.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the entity to operate as a mere shell may face secondary tax liability, personal liability for withheld dividend tax, travel bans pending tax collection, and potential criminal liability for intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's length amount, disallowance of the excess royalty deduction, addition of 10% to 20% transfer pricing penalties on the adjusted amount, and interest accrual on the underpaid corporate tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and transfer pricing officers who knowingly execute inflated intercompany contracts may be held personally liable for the tax shortfall under the secondary liability rules, and may be prohibited from leaving the jurisdiction during the tax audit.
AML and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines and imprisonment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Financial Information Analysis Act, including civil penalties up to KRW 200 million and criminal penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment for serious foreign exchange crimes. Unreported foreign income is also subject to tax reassessment and heavy penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal restitution, court-ordered travel bans, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment where the foreign exchange violation was committed with intention or as a systematic corporate practice.

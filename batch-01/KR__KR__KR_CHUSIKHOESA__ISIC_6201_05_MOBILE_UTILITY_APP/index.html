Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; however, all issued shares must be fully paid by the date of incorporation registration. For cash contributions, the paid-in amount must be deposited in a Korean bank account established for the company in formation, and the certificate of deposit must be presented to the district court registry. No government-held escrow or blocked-capital account is mandated. For contributions in kind, a court-appointed inspector must verify the contributed property unless a statutory exemption applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, accessible via the HomeTax online portal (hometax.go.kr), is the competent tax authority for corporate income tax registration, business registration, VAT filing, e-tax invoice clearance and refunds. The Korea Customs Service (KCS), accessible via the UNI-PASS system (unipass.kr), is the competent authority for export customs declarations and export clearance evidence supporting zero-rated VAT.

E-government filing portal

For foreign non-resident entities, the NTS requires the appointment of a tax representative in Korea before VAT registration. Application is filed through HomeTax using the foreign business operator registration process, with a power of attorney, foreign certificate of incorporation, articles of association, foreign corporate registration certificate, board resolution, passport or officer identification, and evidence of the Korean place of business or the tax representative's domicile. NTS verifies the filing and issues the ten-digit Korean Business Registration Number, which constitutes the entity's TIN and VAT ID. If the foreign entity has a Korean domestic place of business (fixed establishment), the registration is with the NTS district office that has jurisdiction over the domestic place; if the foreign entity has no local establishment but supplies digital services through app stores, it may use the simplified foreign electronic service provider registration and must also appoint a Korean tax representative. Registration must be completed within 20 days of commencing Korean taxable business; late registration exposes the taxpayer to registration penalties and loss of input VAT credits before the registration date.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Korean Chusik Hoesa is an independent juridical person and is taxed at the entity level under the Corporate Income Tax Act on worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Foreign entity shareholders are not taxed on the entity's operating profits, only on Korean-source dividends and gains in their own capacity.

Available election forms

None. Korea is not a check-the-box jurisdiction, and there is no general hybrid mismatch election. The only available elections are limited statutory elections such as the consolidated corporate tax election for a parent and its wholly-owned Korean subsidiaries, and M&A tax-neutrality elections for qualifying corporate restructurings. Hybrid mismatch consequences, if any, are controlled by the International Tax Adjustment Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory for any business once it commences taxable business in Korea. Simplified taxpayer treatment is limited to individuals with below KRW 80 million in annual revenue, and is unavailable to corporations and foreign enterprises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies when the goods leave Korea under a valid customs export declaration, ownership risk crosses the border to a foreign customer, and the goods are not regarded as supplied in Korea. For the Mobile Utility App business, zero-rating applies to the supply of digital software services to non-resident end-users via Apple App Store and Google Play only when the place of supply is outside Korea, the end-user is a foreign non-resident, and the transaction is supported by platform settlement reports identifying foreign storefronts, foreign payment currency, and foreign user evidence (IP/device country, store country, SIM if available). The zero-rating is sustainable only when the foreign proceeds are repatriated to Korea within the statutory deadline and traceable through documentary evidence.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration certificate (UNI-PASS export declaration receipt)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill/Master Waybill) for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign exchange settlement confirmation issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rate VAT marking
  • Zero-rated e-tax invoice transmitted to NTS via HomeTax
  • Packing list, export contract and purchase order for physical goods
  • Apple IAP / Google Play Billing settlement statements and transaction-level app store sales reports for digital services
  • Geo-location and storefront evidence demonstrating foreign end-user consumption for mobile app export substantiation

Mandatory e-tax invoice regime. A Chusik Hoesa must issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax for taxable supplies and transmit each e-tax invoice to the NTS by the day after issuance. The e-tax invoice includes the NTS-validated invoice serial number, digital signature, supplier and customer Business Registration Numbers, supply date, item classification, supply price, VAT amount and a zero-rate indicator for exports. NTS automatically matches seller-issued e-tax invoices with buyer-confirmed e-tax invoices, providing de facto real-time fiscal clearing. Foreign app store platforms do not satisfy Korean VAT documentation; the Korean app developer must still issue and transmit the applicable e-tax invoice.

NTS audit and refund verification examines the export declaration, e-tax invoice, bank remittance receipts and platform settlement reports. If the 180-day foreign exchange collection deadline is exceeded without an approved extension, the zero rate is reversed, output VAT is assessed at 10%, the VAT refund is clawed back, and additional tax plus late payment interest is imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fabricated, backdated or unverified export documents expose the taxpayer to customs fines under the Customs Act, foreign exchange fines under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and criminal tax evasion prosecution. The statute of limitations for VAT reassessment is five years generally and ten years in cases of false invoice or transactional fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains an IMF Article VIII-compliant current account system under which Korean won is generally freely convertible for current account transactions such as trade settlements, software licensing fees, app store settlements, and ordinary operating expenses. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa, inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, and outward remittances of dividends or liquidation proceeds, are subject to a declaration-based surveillance regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. In practice, foreign investors may bring capital into Korea through designated foreign exchange banks, deposit funds into an escrowed capital account, and then complete a foreign investment report through the KOTRA or the receiving bank. Profit remittances such as dividends are permissible after corporate tax compliance and require supporting board resolutions, audited financial statements, and a foreign exchange declaration evidencing the remittance as a current transfer; however, Korean banks will require the remittance to be aligned with the declared capital account records and the overseas recipient to be the true beneficial owner. Export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory forced surrender to the central bank, but all inbound foreign currency receipts must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System through a domestic foreign exchange bank. The practical convertibility hurdle is not the absence of convertibility but the documentation burden, because each capital transaction and each profit distribution must be supported by a valid foreign investment declaration, a corporate tax clearance certificate, and a legitimate wire purpose code. Mobile utility app revenue generated through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing will typically arrive as foreign currency trade receipts and must be tracked against the underlying app store settlement reports to satisfy the banks' source-of-funds reviews. Overall, the regime is partially liberalized but remains stringent on reporting, beneficial ownership identification, and reconciliation of capital account entries, so a treasury blueprint should treat every material inbound or outbound cross-border transaction as a declaration event rather than a simple wire transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current Korean foreign exchange regime, export proceeds from foreign currency receivables must be received through a licensed foreign exchange bank and entered into Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System within the statutory declaration timeframe. The collection period for export proceeds is generally required to fall within 180 days from the date of export declaration, with extensions available where the underlying export contract contains deferred payment terms and the foreign exchange bank approves the extension. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent: Korea does not impose a forced surrender of foreign currency export proceeds into Korean won, and the Chusik Hoesa may retain funds in a foreign currency current account or voluntarily convert to KRW based on commercial needs. Any conversion must be transacted through the authorized bank and reported using the prescribed foreign currency receipt reporting code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Financial Services Commission under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information Reporting Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Foreign digital EMI firms and offshore fintech acquirers are not authorized to hold or service local corporate bank accounts in Korea without obtaining a domestic electronic financial business license from the Financial Services Commission or establishing a licensed banking partnership. A cross-border EMI that merely holds an ASPSP or EMD license in the European Union cannot lawfully provide KRW-denominated current accounts or FX clearing services directly to a Korean Chusik Hoesa as a stand-alone bank substitute. Such fintech platforms are therefore viable only as ancillary software layers for payment initiation, expense management, or reconciliation dashboards, not as the primary treasury rail. The core inbound capital escrow account, the KRW operating account, and the main outbound SWIFT clearing account must be held at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank or a licensed foreign bank branch in Korea. Using an unlicensed digital EMI for corporate treasury would expose the entity to bank refusal, FSS administrative sanctions, and a failure of the foreign exchange declaration process. The practical recommendation is to use the domestic Tier-1 bank's API-driven cash management and FX dealing service for 100% of regulated treasury flows, while optionally connecting third-party fintech tools in a read-only overlay capacity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the Chusik Hoesa must be made by wire transfer from the foreign shareholder's bank account to the Korean capital escrow account at the Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 with the foreign investment equity contribution stated clearly in the payment narrative. The remittance should use the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and be pre-linked to the foreign investment report filed with the receiving bank or KOTRA. After the funds are credited and the foreign investment declaration is confirmed, the bank will perform foreign exchange capital verification, record the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System, and permit the capital to be transferred from the escrow account into the operational KRW account. The bank gatekeeper will require the foreign shareholder's corporate documents, the investment resolution, the Chusik Hoesa's corporate register, identification of the investor, and the source-of-funds package described in the AML analysis. Any inbound remittance not declared as a capital transaction risks being classified as an undeclared borrowing or a prohibited current receipt, so the foreign investment filing must precede the inbound wire.Outbound trade disbursements for licensing fees, subcontractor development costs, and international service payments should be settled through SWIFT MT103 after filing a current account foreign exchange declaration with the Tier-1 bank. For goods-related transactions or physically documented software media trade, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections may be used to support title and payment against shipping or delivery documentation. For app store related obligations, such as royalty payments to content licensors or SDK providers, the Chusik Hoesa should remit using a clearly categorized software royalty or service fee purpose code and retain the underlying license agreements, applicable withholding tax certificates, and payment schedules in the bank's records. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign digital EMI firms and offshore fintech acquirers are not authorized to hold or service local corporate bank accounts in Korea without obtaining a domestic electronic financial business license from the Financial Services Commission or establishing a licensed banking partnership. A cross-border EMI that merely holds an ASPSP or EMD license in the European Union cannot lawfully provide KRW-denominated current accounts or FX clearing services directly to a Korean Chusik Hoesa as a stand-alone bank substitute. Such fintech platforms are therefore viable only as ancillary software layers for payment initiation, expense management, or reconciliation dashboards, not as the primary treasury rail. The core inbound capital escrow account, the KRW operating account, and the main outbound SWIFT clearing account must be held at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank or a licensed foreign bank branch in Korea. Using an unlicensed digital EMI for corporate treasury would expose the entity to bank refusal, FSS administrative sanctions, and a failure of the foreign exchange declaration process. The practical recommendation is to use the domestic Tier-1 bank's API-driven cash management and FX dealing service for 100% of regulated treasury flows, while optionally connecting third-party fintech tools in a read-only overlay capacity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10%-24% national progressive corporate income tax (KRW 0-200m at 10%; KRW 200m-20bn at 20%; KRW 20bn-300bn at 22%; over KRW 300bn at 24%), plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing a combined headline range of 11%-26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% of gross dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, composed of 20% national corporate withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax. Bilateral double tax treaties commonly reduce this rate to 15% generally or 5%-10% for substantial shareholdings.

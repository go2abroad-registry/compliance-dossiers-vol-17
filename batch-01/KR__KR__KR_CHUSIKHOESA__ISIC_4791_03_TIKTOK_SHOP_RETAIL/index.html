Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Division of the Local District Court, including the Seoul Central District Court Registry Office, under the National Court Administration of the Supreme Court of the Republic of Korea.

Governing statute

The Commercial Act (Sangbeop), Act No. 1000, promulgated December 29, 1962, as amended; Part III, Chapter II, Stock Companies, Articles 288 through 317 (incorporation and registration), Article 289 (articles of incorporation), Article 295 (payment of subscription price), Article 302 (founders' meeting), Article 317 (registration of incorporation), and Articles 382 through 407 (directors, representative director, and auditor); together with the Commercial Registration Rules and the Registration Fee Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Incorporation of a Stock Company (Commercial Registration Application) submitted under the Non-Contentious Case Litigation Procedure Act; the Articles of Incorporation constitute the primary corporate constitutive instrument.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 7 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the promoters may fix any positive stated capital in the Articles of Incorporation. The full subscription price of each issued share must be paid into a deposit account maintained for the incorporation before registration, and subscription contributions cannot be offset or fictitious. No government-mandated blocked escrow account applies to an ordinary non-public stock company, but the bank-evidenced subscription deposit must remain available until the Registry Office accepts the incorporation registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no physical premises requiring civil defense fire permits, structural load inspections, or workplace safety approval for an online social-commerce retail operation. If the Company later leases an office or fulfillment facility, it must obtain a fire safety completion certificate from the local fire department and comply with the general duties of the Occupational Safety and Health Act, but such clearances are not required at the pure digital retail formation stage.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the promoters may fix any positive stated capital in the Articles of Incorporation. The full subscription price of each issued share must be paid into a deposit account maintained for the incorporation before registration, and subscription contributions cannot be offset or fictitious. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea through its electronic electronic portal HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr, together with the Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS) for export declaration and customs clearance integration.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Chusik Hoesa, the company must file a Business Registration Application with the NTS district tax office having territorial jurisdiction over its registered head office within 20 days of completing incorporation and business commencement. The NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the mandatory 10-digit business registration number, which simultaneously functions as the corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the Value-Added Tax identification number. For a foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in Korea but makes taxable supplies in Korea for which Korean VAT registration is required, the non-resident entity must register as a non-resident business operator through HomeTax by submitting the Business Registration Application, its foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, evidence of a domestic place of business if any, an appointment of a tax representative authorized to receive Korean tax notices, and any foreign business registration certificate. If the foreign entity has no domestic place of business, the NTS will require a mandatory tax representative; the 10-digit registration number is issued after verification and is used as the VAT payer id for filing and electronic invoicing in Korea.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a domestic Chusik Hoesa, the company must file a Business Registration Application with the NTS district tax office having territorial jurisdiction over its registered head office within 20 days of completing incorporation and business commencement. The NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the mandatory 10-digit business registration number, which simultaneously functions as the corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the Value-Added Tax identification number. For a foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in Korea but makes taxable supplies in Korea for which Korean VAT registration is required, the non-resident entity must register as a non-resident business operator through HomeTax by submitting the Business Registration Application, its foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, evidence of a domestic place of business if any, an appointment of a tax representative authorized to receive Korean tax notices, and any foreign business registration certificate. If the foreign entity has no domestic place of business, the NTS will require a mandatory tax representative; the 10-digit registration number is issued after verification and is used as the VAT payer id for filing and electronic invoicing in Korea.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a check-the-box election, pass-through classification election, or hybrid mismatch election comparable to other jurisdictions. A Chusik Hoesa is categorically a domestic corporation taxed at the entity level under the Corporate Tax Act and cannot elect to be treated as a transparent or pass-through entity for Korean tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. Korean VAT law does not provide a turnover-based exemption threshold for a Chusik Hoesa; VAT business registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply of goods or services in Korea. A simplified taxable person regime with an annual supply threshold of KRW 80 million exists only for certain eligible individual entrepreneurs, but it does not exempt a stock corporation from general registration and VAT administration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are eligible for the 0% VAT rate under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act where the goods are actually exported from the Republic of Korea to a foreign destination and the export is evidenced by an accepted Customs Export Declaration in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system. The sale to a foreign purchaser through the TikTok Shop platform is not sufficient by itself; the goods must physically leave Korea, the exporter must be the Korean entity, and export proceeds must be repatriated in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For qualifying offshore digital services or cross-border services, zero-rating applies when the services are supplied for use outside Korea, are paid for entirely in foreign currency from abroad, and meet the documentary requirements of paid foreign proceeds and foreign place of use.

  • Customs Export Declaration acceptance record issued by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, air waybill, or comprehensive transport document evidencing shipment from Korea to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs export declaration in item description, quantity, and value
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence, including a bank remittance or foreign exchange purchase/sale confirmation, or the TikTok Shop payout settlement report coupled with Wise B2B payment records, evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency from the foreign purchaser
  • Foreign purchaser identification evidence such as platform order records and foreign customer shipping addresses demonstrating export outside Korea

Korean registered VAT taxpayers that issue tax invoices to other registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices, known as Jeonja Sesegegyesanseo, through the HomeTax national e-invoice system. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS no later than the day following issuance; the NTS validates the invoice against registered taxpayer files, transaction ledgers, and customs export declarations in UNI-PASS. There is no pre-clearance digital stamping system on each invoice, but the centralized HomeTax database provides near-real-time receipt of invoice data and supports anti-fraud reconciliation. Failure to issue or timely transmit an electronic tax invoice carries the statutory penalty imposed by the Value-Added Tax Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match export VAT returns, UNI-PASS export declarations, and foreign exchange records. If export zero-rating is claimed without a valid customs export declaration, with falsified shipment evidence, or where export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day deadline and no lawful extension is obtained, the NTS may retroactively disallow the zero-rating, assess standard 10% VAT on the disputed supply, add late-payment interest, and impose administrative penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act. Material false invoicing, misdeclaration, or fraudulent export schemes may additionally trigger criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act and penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The taxes, interest, penalties, and any statutory surcharges must be paid before the matter is closed, and an adverse finding will disqualify future zero-rating claims until compliance is restored.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalized yet regulated foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlements and business expenses, with only post-verified reporting obligations. For capital-account transactions, full convertibility is preserved for qualifying foreign direct investment, but payments involving debt, securities, and external derivatives are subject to prior registration or use of a licensed Foreign Exchange Bank. A resident entity must demonstrate real demand for bank-provided derivative hedging and execute all won conversions through domestic banks. Profits and dividends may be remitted abroad without permit, but the bank must verify tax clearance and cumulative investment registration. Export proceeds are not compulsorily surrendered into won; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts. Principal residual friction is documentary: all cross-border transfers require purpose codes and underlying transaction evidence. Non-resident borrowings, advanced payments, and intra-group loans are stringently monitored under the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System, and any failure to report can trigger administrative fines, delayed repatriation, and frozen liquidity channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received through an authorized Foreign Exchange Bank within six months from the date of export or service completion. The statutory collection period may be extended one time for another six months with prior bank approval. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in a domestic foreign-currency account or converted to KRW at the bank; there is no statutory surrender ratio as long as foreign exchange is repatriated into Korea and reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System. Overdue export receivables must be declared by the exporter within five business days after the expiry of the statutory collection period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low primary banking viability. Wise B2B and overseas EMIs do not hold a Korean bank license and cannot provide a resident Chusik Hoesa with a local KRW IBAN or deposit account. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, any entity transmitting funds from Korea in KRW must be a licensed electronic financial business operator or use a bank. Foreign fintechs may offer payment aggregation or card issuance in partnership with a domestic bank, but statutory liquidity, FX declaration, and withheld-tax obligations require a bank-issued account. The EMI route is viable only as an ancillary cross-border payout connector, never as the primary corporate settlement rail.

Inbound equity capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a domestic Foreign Exchange Bank, with the payment detail citing the statutory FDI purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The recipient bank verifies the FDI declaration and issues an FDI registration certificate or capital import verification. KRW conversion is executed at the domestic spot rate in the same bank. Disbursement from the capital escrow account requires matched invoices, tax invoices, or supplier contracts. All outbound corporate disbursements from the Korean Chusik Hoesa must use an authorized Foreign Exchange Bank and require supporting invoices, customs declarations, and purpose codes.

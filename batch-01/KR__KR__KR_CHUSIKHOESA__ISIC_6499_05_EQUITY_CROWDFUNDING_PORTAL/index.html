Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-in capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The incorporators may fix any capital amount in the Articles, but full payment must be made into a blocked capital subscription account at a Korean bank before the registration application is filed. The blocked capital subscription account is released after the court completes the incorporation registration. For the regulated equity crowdfunding business, the company must separately satisfy the paid-in capital threshold prescribed by the FSCMA Enforcement Decree for a small investment brokerage business (currently applied by the FSC/FSS in the relevant capital schedule, with a common floor of KRW 500 million for a dedicated small investment broker, or a higher amount if the portal seeks broader financial investment authorization), and must at all times maintain investor funds in a separate domestic bank escrow account. Client monies received through the crowdfunding portal must not be commingled with corporate operating funds and must be released only upon lawful settlement or withdrawal in accordance with FSCMA rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax at www.hometax.go.kr for VAT and corporate tax registration; Korea Customs Service operates UNI-PASS at www.unipass.customs.go.kr for export clearance and zero-rating evidence.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity obtains its Korean Tax ID (Business Registration Number) by filing the statutory Application for Business Registration with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the Korean business location, or, in the case of a foreign electronic-service supplier without a Korean establishment, by completing Overseas Electronic Service Supplier (OESS) VAT registration on HomeTax. The filing must be accompanied by the certified and apostilled constitutional documents of the foreign entity, a board resolution or power of attorney naming a Korean tax representative, the Financial Services Commission license or authorization for the equity crowdfunding portal, and evidence of the designated Korean business place. For a non-resident that only has Korean-source withholding obligations and no registration duty, NTS will assign a non-resident taxpayer identification number through the International Tax Division; however, a licensed Chusik Hoesa operating a crowdfunding portal is necessarily a domestic VAT corporation and must obtain the BRN before commencing services.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide any check-the-box or elective transparent classification. A Chusik Hoesa is always a taxable domestic corporation; hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory and non-elective. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic Korean businesses have no monetary VAT registration threshold and must register from the commencement of business. Foreign non-resident electronic service suppliers are statutorily required to register for VAT when their annual taxable electronic-service sales to Korean customers exceed KRW 100,000,000. A small-business simplified VAT taxpayer regime applies domestically to entities with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Korea under a customs export declaration and to services supplied for use or benefit wholly outside Korea, including qualifying offshore digital services that are not consumed by a person in Korea. The taxpayer must prove the exported subject matter crossed Korean customs, the counterparty is a non-resident user or buyer, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange. For equity crowdfunding portal services, the fees earned as intermediary compensation may qualify as zero-rated offshore services only where the issuer or investor is non-resident and the service is actually performed for benefit outside Korea; purely Korean-facing crowdfunding services are taxable at standard rate or VAT-exempt financial service, not zero-rated.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) via UNI-PASS with export declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank or other foreign-exchange transaction evidence
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation confirmation or proof of external currency deposit account when export proceeds are retained abroad under permitted FTA transactions

All VAT invoices must be issued through the NTS electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) system, either directly on HomeTax or via NTS-certified ERP or accounting software connected by API or web service. The electronic invoice must be transmitted to the NTS by the invoice issuance date, and the NTS database assigns a digital tax invoice number and status that serves as an anti-fraud validation stamp. For export transactions, the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS declaration must be cleared before goods depart Korean territory, and the export clearance number must be linked to the VAT zero-rated tax invoice.

On audit, NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match the export declaration, electronic tax invoice, bill of lading, and foreign-exchange remittance records. If zero-rating is not sustained, NTS reassesses 10% VAT, applies statutory additional penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act (generally 40% of the under-reported standard tax amount, plus fixed penalties for false electronic invoices), and imposes interest for uncollected tax. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are referred to prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to three years or a fine up to KRW 30 million, or up to five years and KRW 50 million for habitual or gross evasion, with retrospective clawback of refunded VAT and recovery of all penalties from the portal operator or its representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly liberalized yet fully declaratory and surveillance-intensive foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. The Korean won is generally convertible for residents and non-residents on current account items including trade settlement, service remuneration, dividends, royalties, and ordinary transfers. Capital account transactions, including direct equity investment, cross-border loans, acquisition of securities, and foreign currency deposit conversions, are not prohibited but are subject to mandatory reporting via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FIX) or through a designated foreign exchange bank, with post-reporting accepted only where the statute permits. Non-resident shareholders of a Chusik Hoesa may repatriate dividends after corporate resolution, withholding tax settlement, and submission of audited financial statements, without a separate conversion license for profit remittance if the underlying investment was lawfully reported. However, the BOK and MOEF retain emergency powers to restrict convertibility, impose maximum maturities, require prior approval, or mandate surrender during financial market crisis. In practice, all inward and outward FX flows must be substantiated by invoices, contracts, customs declarations, tax filings, and bank forms, because the banking system automatically transmits every FX transaction to the authorities. This means the rate and time of KRW conversion are freely negotiated with the bank, but the underlying economic reality must be completely documented. Retention of export proceeds in foreign currency is allowed, yet all proceeds must enter through a licensed bank account and be traceable to the relevant export declaration, making a fully documentable treasury trail the central risk management requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received into a domestic foreign exchange bank account within the contractually agreed payment term and, in any case, no later than 180 days from the date of export declaration unless the designated bank grants an extended settlement period due to justifiable commercial reasons. There is no general mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain 100% of qualified export proceeds in foreign currency accounts. When conversion to KRW is chosen, the bank executes the transaction at the published market rate and records the settlement in the Foreign Exchange Information Network. Failure to repatriate export proceeds by the deadline without an approved extension triggers the MOEF's non-performance review and may result in administrative penalties or revocation of the exporter's designation as a foreign exchange bank counterparty.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service as supervisory and border controls participants under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% top marginal rate; progressive schedule is 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on taxable income exceeding KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, and 21% on taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory dividend withholding tax applicable to non-resident shareholders: 20% national withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax, unless reduced by an applicable Double Tax Agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in annual foreign-related party transactions creates the mandatory Local File and Master File preparation duty; a consolidated multinational group with annual revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more also files a Country-by-Country Report; even below the threshold, the NTS may demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the Korean entity engages in cross-border transactions with non-resident related parties.

Economic substance scope: Although South Korea does not impose a separate six-box Economic Substance Regulations test, the economic substance doctrine is embedded in treaty abuse rules, transfer pricing analysis and controlled-foreign-company principles; the equity crowdfunding portal, as a regulated financial intermediary, must maintain real decision-making and workforce in Korea, and Core Income-Generating Activities including investor onboarding, issuer due diligence, platform operation, escrow oversight and AML compliance must be performed in Korea; a shell company claiming DTA benefits without such substance will be treated as a conduit and denied treaty protection, and the NTS may re-characterize or disregard intermediary entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, intentional evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to ten years or a fine equal to one to five times the evaded tax; the NTS imposes an unfaithful return surcharge of 20/100 of unreported tax and 10/100 of understated tax under Articles 47-2 and 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus a daily delinquency surcharge of 0.022% from the due date.

Director personal liability: Where directors intentionally cause or wilfully connive at the false filing, the corporate veil is pierced under applicable Korean tax and tort principles; directors are jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid tax, can have personal bank accounts seized, are subject to a travel ban and NTS blacklisting, and may be sentenced to up to ten years of imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The NTS will deny treaty benefits under the Limitation on Benefits clause of the applicable Double Tax Agreement and adjust taxable income to the arm's length price under Article 4 of the International Tax Coordination Act, imposing an adjustment surcharge of 20% of the corrected tax plus daily delinquency surcharges; failure to prepare and submit transfer pricing documentation attracts additional penalties under Article 11 of the International Tax Coordination Act.

Director personal liability: Directors as controlling persons are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; the NTS can issue a departure restriction order or travel ban, blacklist the entity and its owners, and a court can pierce the corporate veil to attach the directors' personal assets in Korea; criminal liability under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act may arise if the directors knowingly received distributions from the treaty-abusive structure.
AML / Financial Crime and Unauthorized Crowdfunding Solicitation

Statutory sanction: A violation of the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act online investment-linked financial business registration requirement is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine of up to KRW 500 million; failure to maintain internal AML controls under the Specific Financial Transaction Information Act attracts administrative fines and criminal imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to KRW 30 million; severe cases lead to license revocation and criminal prosecution by the Financial Services Commission.

Director personal liability: Directors personally face criminal prosecution and imprisonment; courts order forfeiture and recovery of illicit commissions from directors' personal assets; directors are placed on the financial services authority blacklist and receive travel bans preventing departure during criminal investigation; investor restitution claims pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for losses suffered by retail investors.

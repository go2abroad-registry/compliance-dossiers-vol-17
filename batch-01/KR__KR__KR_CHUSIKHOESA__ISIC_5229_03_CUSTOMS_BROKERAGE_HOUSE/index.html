Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Since the 2012 amendment of Article 329(3) of the Commercial Act, no statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa. The paid-up capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation, regardless of amount, must be deposited into a blocked capital subscription account held in the corporation's tentative name at a licensed Korean commercial bank, and funds may not be withdrawn until the notarized Articles of Incorporation and incorporation registration are accepted by the competent court registry.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: Home Tax Service (Hometax) at hometax.go.kr. Customs clearance and brokerage licensing authority: Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity incorporating a Chusik Hoesa, the sole national tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by the National Tax Service. Procedure: (1) complete corporate registration with the competent Korea court registry; the NTS corporate taxpayer file is then activated. (2) File a business registration application through the Hometax electronic portal or the local tax office using the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, foreign ownership/parent documentation, place-of-business lease, and, if the entity has no Korean fixed establishment, a tax representative appointment. (3) NTS issues the 10-digit BRN and Business Registration Certificate; the same BRN functions as the corporate income tax identification number and VAT registration number. (4) Because the entity is a customs clearance brokerage house, it must additionally obtain the statutory customs brokerage license from Korea Customs Service and register in UNI-PASS as a customs declaration filer. No separate VAT ID is issued; the BRN is statutorily conclusive for direct tax, VAT, withholding, and customs reporting.

None in the manner of a U.S. check-the-box election. A Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxable as a Korean domestic stock corporation. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no annual turnover threshold for a Chusik Hoesa; any corporation engaged in taxable business supply in South Korea must obtain a Business Registration Number and register for VAT. The simplified taxpayer turnover threshold of KRW 80 million per year applies only to individual small businesses and does not apply to a stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Korean VAT law, 0% VAT applies to exported goods where physical export is proven by a customs export declaration and the proceeds are earned in foreign exchange. Statutory zero-rating also covers services performed outside Korea, international carriage, and other goods/services prescribed by the Value Added Tax Act and Enforcement Decree as supplied for use or consumption outside the Republic of Korea. A licensed customs brokerage may substantiate zero-rating in respect of qualifying export-linked services only when the statutory evidence demonstrates a clear foreign-bound transaction and foreign-exchange earning.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Bill of Entry equivalent) with clearance number
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (ocean freight) or Air Waybill (airfreight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign exchange settlement confirmation
  • Proof of foreign exchange acquisition or export proceeds report showing receipt of overseas payment
  • Customs broker's export clearance statement when filing is performed by the brokerage house

Registered VAT vendors are required to issue electronic tax invoices and transmit them to the National Tax Information System through Hometax or an approved certified ASP within the statutory deadline; the system is not a continuous real-time fiscal clearance block-stamping model but is a mandatory digital transmission/anti-fraud reporting regime. For exports, the export declaration must be cleared through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS, and the zero-rated e-tax invoice must be linked to the export clearance and reported separately in the periodic VAT return. No analogue paper stamping is required; anti-fraud integrity is achieved by NTS and KCS data cross-matching.

Export zero-rating is accepted only when the mandatory documentation bundle is retained and the export proceeds are repatriated in accordance with Korean foreign exchange rules. If NTS or KCS determines that the zero-rating is unsupported, false, or not related to an actual export, the VAT at 10% is re-assessed with interest and punitive additional tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including enhanced penalties for false tax invoices and fraudulent refund claims. Criminal sanctions, including imprisonment and fines under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, apply to deliberately fraudulent export zero-rating or refund schemes. NTS may retrospectively claw back refunds within the extended statute of limitations applicable to fraud, and the entity's customs brokerage license may be suspended or revoked by Korea Customs Service for repeat non-compliance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a materially liberalized but tightly monitored foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). Current account transactions, including cross-border service fees, customs brokerage commissions, trade settlement amounts, and dividends, are generally convertible freely through authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions, by contrast, require either ex ante reporting, declaration, or in certain cases approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea. A customs brokerage house receiving cross-border commercial payments must ensure that the remittance advice states the proper purpose of payment code to avoid being classified as a capital transaction. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; earnings may be retained in foreign currency accounts, but the underlying export or service contract must be presented to the settlement bank. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible upon submission of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and the foreign investment report. Practical hurdles include strict real-name financial transaction rules, enhanced due diligence for medium-risk licensed entities, and bank compliance departments requiring clear evidence that the broker is operating under its customs brokerage license rather than as an undisclosed payment intermediary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds and deemed export service revenue must be collected within 90 days from the date of the export declaration or the date the service is completed, unless an extension is granted by the authorized foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; 100% of proceeds may be held in a designated foreign currency account. The customs broker or exporter must submit a Foreign Exchange Settlement Report to the settlement bank, and funds must be received in Korea within the statutory timeline to avoid administrative fines and compliance penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal viability for offshore digital EMI is Low. South Korea maintains a traditional bank-centric payments architecture; only domestic banks and narrow-purpose fintech licensees under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act may provide settlement services in Korean won. A non-resident EMI without a domestic banking license cannot directly hold corporate won-denominated accounts or clear customs duty payments through the Korea Customs Service Automated Broker Interface. At best, an EMI could act as an unregulated payment instruction layer via a sponsored bank partnership, but leading Korean banks generally prohibit virtual accounts or umbrella settlement structures for licensed customs brokers. The recommended path is to open Tier-1 domestic bank accounts and use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border receipts rather than relying on EMIs.

Inbound capital injection for equity must be routed through an authorized foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202. The Korean entity must submit a Foreign Exchange Declaration and report foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act using the appropriate purpose code for capital contribution. For shareholder loans, registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act is required. Funds are credited to a Capital Escrow Account and verified against the reported investment amount. After receipt, the bank issues a certificate of foreign exchange purchase or import of foreign currency, and the company registers paid-in capital with the relevant Korean registration court. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5 million) of aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions (including sales of goods, provision of services, loans, royalties, and brokerage commissions) in a fiscal year triggers the simultaneous preparation and submission of the Local File and Master File under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA); simplified documentation may apply to small and medium enterprises with intercompany transactions below KRW 5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the style of the UAE, but the substance-over-form principle is codified in Article 14(3) of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 4(1) of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. The National Tax Service may disregard artificial entities, re-characterise transactions, and deny treaty benefits where the non-resident counterparty lacks core income-generating activities (CIGA) in its own jurisdiction. A licensed customs brokerage house operating in Korea must have local management and decision-making, bank accounts, ACH/automated broker interface systems, personnel, and physical office substance; failure to maintain such substance while declaring dividends or royalties to a tax-haven or low-tax affiliate may result in the assignment of the income to the Korean entity, full 22% withholding, and an understatement penalty of up to 40%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 8(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to 5 times the evaded tax; additional corporate tax of 40% of the understated amount for false or fraudulent return under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3; automatic revocation of the customs brokerage licence under the Customs Act and prohibition from re-registration for 5 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under the Korean Commercial Act Article 389(3) read together with Article 210 where directors have acted in bad faith or with gross negligence; directors who planned, encouraged, or executed the evasion become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and damages. The director may be placed on a travel ban (departure restriction) for tax debts exceeding KRW 100 million, blacklisted by the National Tax Service and Korea Customs, and, upon criminal conviction, imprisoned for up to 5 years and disqualified from serving as a director or customs broker executive for 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Articles 5 and 6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA) authorise the NTS to make an arm's-length adjustment; the additional tax is 20% of the adjusted taxable income, increased to 40% where the return is dishonest or a sham transaction is found; interest surcharge of 0.75% per month on the cash-settled extended tax. In flagrant cases, the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act is invoked for fraudulent profit shifting with criminal sanctions of up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany pricing agreements without conducting an arm's-length analysis or who approve circular financial flows are treated as active participants in the tax offence; they are personally liable for the corrected corporate tax under the doctrine of substance-over-form and can face civil restitution to the company for loss of assets. After the NTS issues a corrective assessment, the unpaid amount creates a director travel ban; if criminal, directors may be jailed for up to 5 years and can be blocked from operating any licensed customs activity.
Abusive Shell Company and AML/CTF Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Breach of Article 27(1) of the Financial Transactions Reports Act; imprisonment of up to 5 years and a criminal fine not exceeding KRW 50 million; violation of customs bonding regulations under the Customs Act Articles 270-274 triggers at least KRW 10 million in fines and revocation of the customs brokerage licence; any profit derived from the implicated brokerage transactions is subject to confiscation; corporate penalties reach 20% of the transaction amounts in additional tax.

Director personal liability: Korean courts will disregard the shell entity and attribute the smuggling, money-laundering, or duty-evading conduct to the natural-person directors because a corporate shell cannot itself perform customs brokerage; directors are jointly liable for customs duties, VAT, penalties, and criminal restitution. The director will be blacklisted by KoFIU and the Korea Customs Service, prohibited from travelling abroad while the criminal case is pending, and, if convicted, sentenced to up to 5 years imprisonment; a second or organized-crime enhanced conviction can double the sentence. The constitutional and commercial law 'limited liability' shield is expressly void where the company is a façade and the director has acted in a self-interested or knowingly fraudulent manner.

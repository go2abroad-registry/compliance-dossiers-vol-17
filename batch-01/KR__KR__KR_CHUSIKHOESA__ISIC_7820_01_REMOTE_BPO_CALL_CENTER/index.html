Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korea no longer maintains a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa (Stock Corporation). However, the authorized issued shares must be fully subscribed and the entire subscription price must be paid into a dedicated blocked capital deposit account at a licensed commercial bank before the incorporation registration is filed. A bank certificate of payment or balance evidencing the deposit must accompany the registration. The deposited capital may not be withdrawn for any purpose until the incorporation registration has been completed and the company has officially come into existence. There is no separate escrow agent requirement, but the bank account is effectively restricted during the formation period.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. No industrial fire defense, structural load inspection, or civil defense operating permit is required for a remote BPO call center because there is no physical manufacturing, chemical, assembly, or warehousing activity. The company nevertheless remains obligated to comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act for general office workplace conditions, including emergency evacuation plans, basic fire extinguisher installation, and ordinary employment safety obligations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr, with Korea Customs Service (KCS) at www.customs.go.kr for export clearance and customs declaration matters

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity forming a Chusik Hoesa in Korea, or a direct non-resident VAT registrant, must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. For a non-resident entity, the procedure is: (1) appoint a tax representative in Korea for NTS notifications and proceedings; (2) prepare the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, certificate of foreign company registration, articles of incorporation, power of attorney identifying the tax representative, and proof of domestic place of business or registered office; (3) file the business registration application through HomeTax or in person at the competent district tax office using the prescribed Business Registration Application form; (4) provide any additional documents requested by NTS to verify the overseas parent or headquarters; (5) receive the BRN certificate and register for the electronic tax invoice system, which is mandatory for VAT. For export/import clearance, the entity must also obtain a Customs Business Code (통관고유부호) from Korea Customs Service, and for services exports must maintain the foreign exchange transaction evidence required by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. In the case of a foreign corporation supplying electronic services to Korean consumers, the non-resident may register directly under the simplified non-resident VAT registration procedure, appoint a local tax representative, and file VAT on a quarterly or semi-annual basis after NTS acceptance.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a Chusik Hoesa; it is a per se domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate tax and VAT. Hybrid mismatch allocation rules under Korean tax law and OECD BEPS-aligned domestic provisions apply automatically where relevant and are not elective. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic business operators, there is no general turnover registration threshold: any person making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register for VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act. A non-resident supplier of electronic services to Korean consumers is required to register and remit Korean VAT when its annual taxable electronic-service sales exceed KRW 300,000,000. The BPO call-center entity itself, if incorporated as a Korean Chusik Hoesa, must register immediately upon commencing business regardless of projected turnover and must issue electronic tax invoices for all taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, services exported to a foreign country are zero-rated. A remote BPO call-center service qualifies for 0% VAT where the recipient is a non-resident business or individual located outside Korea, the service is actually performed for and used outside the Korean tax territory, and the consideration is earned in foreign currency or otherwise received from a foreign counterparty in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The service must be genuinely supplied to the overseas principal; if the call-center operations support Korean domestic customers or the economic benefit is consumed in Korea, zero-rating is denied. The supplier must be able to demonstrate the foreign-resident status of the customer, the cross-border terms of the service contract, and the actual delivery of call-center work such as call logs, staffing records, quality reports, KPIs, and settlement reports.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (수출신고필증) - required only where physical goods are shipped as part of a mixed deliverable; not applicable to pure intellectual BPO service exports
  • Commercial invoice or export invoice describing the BPO services, contract number, billing period, and exact service description
  • Signed BPO/call-center services agreement with the foreign principal, including jurisdiction, payment currency, and service acceptance clauses
  • Service performance evidence such as call detail records, agent roster, ticketing system reports, time-stamped service delivery logs, and monthly performance or SLA reports
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the overseas customer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - required only for any incidental physical shipment of goods or media, alongside the KCS Export Declaration
  • Foreign customer identity evidence including overseas business proof, tax ID, or certificate of incorporation

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) system through the National Tax Service HomeTax portal. All VAT-registered businesses, including Chusik Hoesa entities, must issue e-invoices through the NTS-certified electronic tax invoice service or an interoperable API integrated with HomeTax. There is no per-invoice physical stamping, but each e-invoice is assigned an official digital transaction number and is transmitted to NTS in real time or within the statutory filing period. Anti-fraud measures require the use of the NTS issuance-supply validation service, immediate reconciliation of issued and received e-invoices, and strict prohibition of unregistered third-party billing. A domestic zero-rated export supply must be documented with a zero-rated electronic tax invoice containing the appropriate supply classification, plus underlying export evidence retained by the supplier.

NTS may reclassify a claimed export zero-rating as a domestic taxable supply if the foreign-exchange receipt, contract, or service-performance evidence fails audit. Where a zero-rating claim is unsubstantiated or fraudulent, the unpaid VAT is assessed at 10% with no input-credit benefit, plus statutory penalty surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, typically up to 40% of the misreported tax for fraudulent understatement and additional daily late-payment surcharges for every day after the due date. False issuance or non-issuance of an electronic tax invoice triggers separate penalty taxes under the VAT Act. In serious cases, the Act on the Aggravated Punishment, etc. of Specific Economic Crimes provides for criminal prosecution and imprisonment. Export zero-rating is also retroactively clawed back if the foreign exchange proceeds are not received within the 180-day statutory collection period without prior approval from the Bank of Korea or an authorized foreign-exchange bank, unless permitted extensions apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bank of Korea administers the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance. South Korea applies a largely liberal convertibility regime for both current account and capital account flows, subject to obligatory reporting through authorized foreign exchange banks. Corporate operating receipts, including BPO service exports from Korea, may be denominated in foreign currency and credited to foreign currency accounts without an immediate statutory conversion requirement. However, inbound foreign direct investment and other capital account transactions must be notified or registered with the BOK or a delegated banking institution. Remittance of dividends and profits is permitted once the relevant Korean corporate tax and dividend withholding tax obligations are discharged; banks will require a tax clearance certificate, foreign investment registration certificate, audited financial statements, and a board resolution authorising the remittance. For service exports, the repatriation or collection of foreign exchange proceeds is subject to a general collection deadline of six months from the service performance date, extendable only with BOK approval. In practice, treasury must maintain supporting contracts, invoices, proof of payment, and the bank foreign-exchange transaction report to avoid convertibility frictions and ensure clean repatriation of operating profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and service proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export, shipment, or completion of the BPO service performance. No mandatory percentage surrender to the central bank is currently imposed; the statutory obligation is collection, not forced conversion. Proceeds may be held in foreign currency or converted into KRW through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Any extension of the collection period requires BOK or delegated bank approval supported by documentary justification.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) under the statutory authority of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as primary treasury channel. Offshore EMI providers such as Wise are not licensed as domestic authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for KRW settlement and cannot file foreign-exchange transaction reports with the BOK on behalf of the Korean entity. A Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain its primary operating, payroll, tax, and foreign-direct-investment escrow relationship with a Korean licensed bank. Wise/B2B and similar virtual account services may be used as supplementary non-KRW collection or payment rails for low-value client invoices, provided the Korean entity reports the underlying cross-border receipt through its domestic bank and recognizes the transaction for VAT and corporate tax purposes. Legal risk arises if the EMI is used to circumvent BOK reporting, capital verification, or tax withholding obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act: the foreign investor files an investment declaration through an authorized foreign exchange bank or KOTRA, opens a temporary foreign-investment escrow account in South Korea, wires funds with the appropriate foreign-exchange purpose code for stock acquisition, and the bank issues the foreign exchange handling certificate. After incorporation of the Chusik Hoesa, the company issues shares, the investment is registered with the authorities through the bank, and capital verification is supported by a bank stamp certificate and statutory auditor or CPA confirmation. BPO service-export receivables from overseas clients are routed by SWIFT MT103 with the underlying service contract and invoice; the receiving Korean bank reports the transaction through the Foreign Exchange Information System and credits the KRW or foreign-currency operational account.The primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for operating disbursements such as payroll, office lease, vendor payments, software licensing, commissions, and eventual dividend remittances. For this remote BPO call-center business, settlement is predominantly services-based, so commercial letters of credit are not the standard rail; if physical equipment, hardware resale, or raw materials are involved, irrevocable documentary credit at sight or documentary collections are permissible but should remain secondary. Every outbound payment must carry a truthful foreign-exchange purpose code, be supported by an underlying contract, tax invoice, or board resolution, and be reported by the domestic bank to the BOK. Treasury should centralize disbursements through the Tier-1 Korean bank to maintain a clean audit trail and avoid delays in tax clearance for future profit repatriation.Deliverable KRW/USD, KRW/EUR, and KRW/JPY forward foreign-exchange contracts up to the documented underlying BPO service receivable cycle, typically 3 to 12 months, with no naked speculative positions and proper hedge documentation.Onshore FX swaps in KRW/USD to fund temporary working-capital mismatches between foreign-currency receipts and domestic KRW payroll or operating costs.Cross-currency interest rate swaps in USD/KRW only for long-term external borrowing or capital-expenditure hedges if the board approves a K-IFRS hedge designation and the underlying exposure is clearly evidenced.Non-deliverable forwards are generally offshore instruments used by non-resident counterparties; a Korean resident BPO should execute deliverable forwards onshore, and NDFs should be used only in exceptional cases where the underlying KRW exposure is non-settleable, with an authorized BOK bank and full regulatory reporting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as primary treasury channel. Offshore EMI providers such as Wise are not licensed as domestic authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for KRW settlement and cannot file foreign-exchange transaction reports with the BOK on behalf of the Korean entity. A Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain its primary operating, payroll, tax, and foreign-direct-investment escrow relationship with a Korean licensed bank. Wise/B2B and similar virtual account services may be used as supplementary non-KRW collection or payment rails for low-value client invoices, provided the Korean entity reports the underlying cross-border receipt through its domestic bank and recognizes the transaction for VAT and corporate tax purposes. Legal risk arises if the EMI is used to circumvent BOK reporting, capital verification, or tax withholding obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 (KRW five billion) in annual cross-border related-party transactions for Local File preparation; KRW 1,000,000,000,000 (KRW one trillion) global consolidated group revenue for Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations under the International Tax Adjustment Act and its Enforcement Decree

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a UAE-style Eastman ESR code, but the National Tax Service enforces an economic-substance doctrine through OECD BEPS principles, the substance-over-form rule in Framework Act on National Taxes, place-of-effective-management tests for permanent establishments, and treaty beneficial-ownership jurisprudence. A remote BPO Chusik Hoesa must maintain actual Korean staff, real call-center infrastructure, active management, risk assumption and core income-generating activities in Korea; where the entity is merely a nominee contracting shell, the NTS disregards the entity, attributes its income to the foreign principal and may deny treaty withholding relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Return

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 20% of the unpaid corporate tax (40% where fraudulent concealment is proven) under Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-2 and 47-3; criminal prosecution under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to five times the evaded tax

Director personal liability: If the court pierces the corporate veil, directors and participating shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the deficient corporate tax, interest and penalties; the NTS may seize personal bank accounts, place the director on the tax-delinquent blacklist and impose a departure restriction under the National Tax Collection Act; criminal conviction may result in direct imprisonment of the responsible director for up to 5 years.
Shell Corporate Substance Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity and recharacterization of payments under the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 14 and Framework Act on National Taxes Article 17-2; loss of treaty benefits; withholding shortfall collected at the full 22% statutory rate plus a 10% to 40% penalty and interest

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false substance declarations are personally liable for the unpaid withholding and penalties, exposed to civil restitution by the tax authority, blacklisted and subject to an overseas travel veto; where the arrangement is found criminal, the directors face up to 3 years' imprisonment and individual fines equal to the tax loss.
Anti-Money Laundering and Foreign Exchange Reporting Violation

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties under the Financial Transactions Report Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, generally up to KRW 100 million for administrative violations and imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional evasion involving cross-border funds; additional National Tax Service penalties apply for unexplained foreign remittances treated as taxable income

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for compliance programs or directly controlling wire transfers are personally subject to criminal fines and imprisonment, may be placed on the AML watchlist, their assets subject to civil forfeiture, and repeat offenders face enhanced imprisonment terms up to 5 years together with liquidation of personal assets to satisfy the Korean tax and penalty claims.

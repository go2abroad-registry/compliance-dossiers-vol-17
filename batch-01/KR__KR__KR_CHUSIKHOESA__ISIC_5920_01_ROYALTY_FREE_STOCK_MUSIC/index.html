Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the amended Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation may lawfully provide for zero paid-in capital. If capital is stated, the full subscription amount must be deposited with a Korean commercial bank or trust institution before the incorporation registration is filed. The deposit certificate must identify the company under formation and the court registrar will treat those funds as restricted or escrowed until registration is completed. If zero capital is selected, no blocked deposit is required, but the capital amount and contribution method stated in the articles must be accepted by the registered shareholders.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for digital office operations. If employees are hired, the company must register with the workers' compensation and industrial accident insurance program and perform a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with tax filings and e-invoice services operated through the HomeTax electronic portal at hometax.go.kr; customs verification for export substantiation is handled by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity requiring a Korean TIN/VAT number: (1) appoint a statutory tax representative and file a power of attorney with the competent NTS district tax office; (2) submit the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or business registration, home-country tax identification number, director identity documents, and apostilled/notarized copies (or consular legalization where apostille does not apply), together with the business registration application; (3) designate a domestic tax service address or agent if the foreign entity has no Korean place of business; (4) file online through HomeTax or in person at the district tax office; (5) the NTS will issue a 10-digit business registration number on a business registration certificate, which serves as the unified corporate TIN, VAT registration ID, and e-invoice issuer ID; (6) report any changes to the tax representative, address, or business description within the statutory period. A Korean-incorporated Chusik Hoesa itself follows the same initial registration path: it files the business registration application with the competent NTS district tax office after incorporation and before taxable business commencement, using the articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, and board resolutions, and receives the same 10-digit business registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Korean Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporate tax resident taxed on worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act; it is not a pass-through or transparent entity for VAT or corporate income tax purposes).

Available election forms

None for entity classification; Korea does not have a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections are limited to intra-group consolidated corporate tax filing for eligible wholly owned domestic subsidiaries, tax-neutral corporate restructuring elections, and special small and venture investment business tax elections. Hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily by statute and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (KRW 0 threshold). Every businessperson engaged in taxable goods or services in Korea must obtain a VAT registration regardless of turnover. A simplified taxable person regime for small individual businesses may apply below approximately KRW 80,000,000 in prior-year taxable turnover, but this does not exempt those businesses from VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Korean VAT Act for goods exported out of Korea and for services supplied to non-residents or foreign customers where the consideration is received in a permitted foreign currency and the service is actually used or consumed outside Korea. For royalty-free stock music and audio sample subscription licensing, the digital supply of licenses to a non-Korean customer or platform qualifies as a 0% VAT service export if the licensing/subscription records show foreign consumption, the customer is not a Korean resident, and the proceeds are remitted in foreign currency through a licensed payment channel such as Stripe or PayPal settled to a Korean foreign exchange bank. Input VAT incurred for export activities is refundable to the extent attributable to the zero-rated supply.

  • Export Declaration submitted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic clearance system for physical goods; for pure digital stock-music exports, the service-export evidence route is used instead of a physical customs entry
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment of any tangible media; for digital delivery, the platform download log, IP access/geo-location report, or license server record substantiating offshore supply
  • Commercial invoice and the stock-music license agreement/order record identifying the non-resident customer, licensed scope, and payment terms
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate (bank-issued export proceeds receipt) or a bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing the receipt of export consideration in foreign currency
  • Payment processor (Stripe/PayPal) payout ledger and Korean bank settlement records reconciling the gross export proceeds to the relevant invoices within the 180-day repatriation period

All registered Korean VAT businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice service for taxable supplies where a tax invoice is required, and the e-invoice is transmitted to the NTS upon issuance, with APIs and certified ERP systems providing immediate structured data exchange. Korea does not currently mandate a physical real-time fiscal clearing machine or digital stamping device; anti-fraud control is handled by NTS-side matching, e-invoice transmission rules, cash-receipt reporting, and cross-verification of purchases with counterparty e-invoicing.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match the export declaration, e-tax invoice, license agreement, payment processor payout data, and foreign exchange bank receipts. If zero-rating is not fully substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply, and the NTS assesses VAT at 10% plus National Tax Basic Act penalty taxes, delinquency interest, and retrospective clawback of any input VAT previously refunded. Knowingly false export invoices are treated as tax evasion, attracting criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and an extended national tax assessment period of up to 10 years; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline without prior approval may also trigger foreign exchange penalties and forfeiture of the zero-rating benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating exchange rate regime with broad current account convertibility for trade in goods, services, and ordinary business payments. Cross-border payments and receipts are generally unrestricted but must be effectuated through authorized domestic banks using statutory reporting and purpose codes. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, intercompany loans, securities acquisitions, and derivative contracts, require prior reporting, declaration, or registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts rather than being forcibly converted; conversion to Korean won is conducted at market rates through a domestic bank. Profit remittance hurdles are moderate and manageable: a dividend payment requires board approval, audited financial statements, tax compliance confirmation, and a bank remittance form with the appropriate service or investment income purpose code. The monetary authority monitors cross-border flows through the Foreign Exchange Information System, and any unusual transaction pattern can trigger a suspicious transaction report to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU). For a low-risk digital licensing entity, current account convertibility is efficient, but capital account funding must be carefully documented to avoid administrative penalties and bank onboarding refusal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts in South Korea. Exporters must generally collect export proceeds within 90 days after the date of shipment unless an extended collection period is approved by the relevant authorized bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Received foreign currency may be credited to a domestic foreign currency settlement account and used freely for permitted payments or converted to Korean won at prevailing market rates. Unexpired export receipts must be reported through the bank, and any delayed collection must be justified with supporting trade documentation to avoid Foreign Exchange Transactions Act sanctions.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting as the joint foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is assessed as Low. A foreign digital EMI that does not hold a license under South Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act is legally barred from providing domestic Korean payment accounts, foreign exchange services, or collection rails directly to a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Global platforms such as Stripe and PayPal can be used only as front-end sales collection tools; funds must be settled to a licensed domestic acquiring bank or to the company's Tier 1 commercial bank account. The entity should not route treasury cash through unlicensed offshore EMIs, as such arrangements would violate FX transaction reporting rules and create serious AML compliance gaps with the Financial Services Commission and KoFIU.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for a Korean Chusik Hoesa must follow the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The foreign shareholder must first file a foreign investment notification with a domestic bank or KOTRA, then open an external capital account at the designated bank, and remit the subscribed capital via SWIFT MT103 with the narrative clearly stating 'Foreign Direct Investment / Equity Capital'. The receiving bank will verify the sender and underlying documentation, convert the USD or other foreign currency into KRW at the interbank market rate, and issue a certificate of foreign investment and a capital verification statement. The company must then update the commercial registry with paid-in capital and ensure all shares are recorded in the shareholder register. No physical capital escrow release is permitted without bank confirmation of the completed foreign investment report.Outbound payments for trade and digital content disbursements should be routed through SWIFT MT103 for direct fee remittances, royalty payments, and profit distributions. For physical goods or delivery-based services, Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) and Documentary Collections may also be used to provide trade settlement security. For the stock music licensing model, the standard rail will be MT103 wire transfer or bank payment through the Electronic Banking with a declared service purpose code and supporting contract/invoice. Payment to overseas licensors, artists, or content owners should be made only to verified business or individual bank accounts matching the beneficiary in the underlying agreement. Turnover: Under the Korean Act on External Audit of Stock Companies and its Enforcement Decree, a Chusik Hoesa must engage a registered CPA firm for an independent external audit if it is (1) a listed stock company or a company whose securities are traded publicly; (2) a large non-listed company whose balance sheet total assets at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 12 billion (approx. USD 9.2 million at 1,300 KRW/USD); (3) a company with annual revenue of KRW 10 billion or more that employs 100 or more full-time workers (the turnover/headcount alternative specified in the Decree); (4) a financial, insurance, or private equity company designated by the FSS; or (5) a company that otherwise meets the materiality tests (e.g., owning 20 billion KRW of real estate or having 10 billion KRW of monetary debt) as clarified by the Securities and Futures Commission. Companies below these thresholds are not required to undergo a statutory audit, though shareholders holding at least 3% of issued shares (1% for listed) can demand an audit at the company's expense.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (top marginal rate; progressive brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, 24% above KRW 300 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporation, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the withholding amount, producing a statutory aggregate rate of 22%; bilateral tax treaties normally reduce the dividend WHT rate to 10% or 5% for substantial shareholdings.

Final corporate income tax return must be filed within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year; for a December 31 year-end the statutory deadline is March 31 of the following calendar year; non-calendar fiscal year corporations must file within 3 months of their own fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korea requires contemporaneous Local File and Master File when the aggregate amount of cross-border transactions between a Korean taxpayer and foreign related parties equals or exceeds KRW 50 billion in a fiscal year; a Country-by-Country Report is required when consolidated group revenue is KRW 1 trillion or more over one of the two preceding years.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no broad Economic Substance Regulation like the UAE, but the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and the domestic tax law allow tax authorities to disregard entities or recharacterize transactions that lack a valid commercial purpose and economic substance. For treaty relief, the OECD Principal Purpose Test embedded in Korea's DTAs requires the non-resident entity to demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - including staff, premises, decision-making, and real management regarding the music-licensing business - are performed in its residence jurisdiction; a filing cabinet or mere registry in a low-tax country will not satisfy the substance requirement. Similarly, Korean courts may pierce a paper corporate vehicle used solely to convert business income into treaty-exempt royalty income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion / Underreporting Digital Royalty Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, a fraudulent underdeclaration triggers a penalty of 40% of the underreported tax; additional daily late payment interest applies. The Punishment of Tax Evaders Act provides imprisonment for up to 2 years and a fine of up to twice the amount of evaded taxes if evasion exceeds statutory threshold; tax authorities can also impose a 20% local income tax surcharge.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or causes the filing of a false corporate return may be criminally prosecuted personally; courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is operated as an alter ego and willfully divert Korean revenue to foreign accounts. Directors face civil restitution of the evaded amount, bank account freezes, immigration travel bans (blacklisting by the Korean Ministry of Justice), and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 11 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, the National Tax Service may recharacterize the transaction and adjust taxable income to the arm's-length amount; a penalty of up to 10% of the tax on the adjustment, and an additional administrative fine of up to KRW 100 million for non-submission of TP documents, may be imposed. The failure to maintain TP evidence also increases the assessment period from 5 to 7 years for foreign-related transactions.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing applies where the director personally selected the intercompany royalty rate or shifted profits to an entity with no real employees or function. Directors are jointly liable for corporate tax under the various liability provisions of the Korean Framework Act; they may be subject to asset seizure, freeze of foreign bank accounts, blacklisting as a 'high-risk offshore tax evader,' and imprisonment if criminal intent is shown.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Korean tax authorities may deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, reclassify the royalty income as business income derived through a Korean permanent establishment, and assess corporate income tax at 24% plus withholding tax penalties. Fines under the Framework Act on National Taxes for document falsity and tax evasion can reach 40% of unpaid tax; criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carry imprisonment of up to 2 years, raised to up to 5 years for evasion exceeding KRW 300 million or cases involving international tax crimes.

Director personal liability: Directors who established the foreign shell as a sham can be held personally liable for the full Korean corporate tax, interest, and penalties; civil asset recovery allows the Korean tax authority to trace and seize personal assets held domestically or abroad; the director may be placed on an immigration blacklist, subjected to criminal extradition requests, and sentenced to imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced because the shell corporation is used as an instrument of tax fraud rather than a genuine operating company.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Section of the District Court Registration Office (Court Registry) having territorial jurisdiction over the company's registered office, administered by the Supreme Court of Korea; e-filings are accepted through the I-Company electronic registration portal (www.i-company.go.kr) and Government24.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, as last consolidated), particularly Articles 288 (incorporators), 289-290 (articles of incorporation), 291-296 (share subscription), 298-299 (payment and contribution inspection), 300 (incorporator report), 305 (organizational meeting), 312 (director and auditor appointment), 317 (registration of incorporation), 318 (effect of non-registration), 352 and 356 (share register and share certificates), 383 (director term), 393 (board of directors), 395 (representative director), 396 (board minutes) and 557 (registered office relocation); together with the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 168(1) and Value-Added Tax Act Article 8 for post-incorporation business registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Certificate of Incorporation Registration Application (Company Registration Application form) under Article 317 of the Commercial Act; after approval the registry issues the certified Extract of Corporate Registration (deung-gi sa-hang jeon-bu) and a corporate seal certificate.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Korean Commercial Act; the stated capital may be set at KRW 0 or any positive amount in the Articles of Incorporation. The incorporators must, however, pay in full the amount subscribed by each shareholder before registration and obtain a bank balance certificate from a domestic or foreign exchange bank confirming that amount. No separate statutory blocked escrow account is required for cash subscriptions; the bank balance certificate serves as the proof of paid-in capital. If any contribution is made in kind, a court-appointed examiner's report is required under Article 299 unless the in-kind contribution falls within a statutory exception. Foreign-currency subscriptions must comply with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and any applicable foreign investment reporting.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea - HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); export declarations are processed by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation subject to the Corporate Tax Act and taxed on worldwide income as a separate taxable entity; shareholders do not include entity income as pass-through, and losses are not allocated to shareholders. Foreign corporations without a Korean permanent establishment are taxable only on Korean-source income, and no pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

None. Korean law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or pass-through election for a Chusik Hoesa. The only relevant structural election is Korean consolidated group taxation for wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries electing to file a single corporate tax return under the Corporate Tax Act. Anti-hybrid rules under the Korean Adjustment of International Taxes Act operate automatically; there is no elective hybrid mismatch regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - no general turnover threshold. Every person or entity that begins taxable business operations in Korea must apply for VAT registration within 20 days of commencement. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers are also required to register under the simplified non-resident VAT registration on HomeTax, without any small-supplier threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, a taxable supply of goods qualifies for the 0% rate if the goods are physically exported from Korea and the export is evidenced by a Customs Export Declaration. Services, including remote desktop access, remote screen sharing and unattended machine/IoT telemetry software, qualify for zero rating when supplied to a non-resident and the service is actually used or consumed outside Korea, with payment received in foreign currency. The supplier must prove foreign use through subscriber/customer location data, IP and geo data, usage logs, contracts, payment settlement records and foreign exchange receipts showing repatriation to Korea. Customs export declarations are not required for service exports, but the same evidential and foreign-exchange substantiation standards apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration prepared in UNI-PASS and confirmed by Korea Customs Service as the definitive export clearance record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document) evidencing physical removal of goods from Korea and delivery to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order and export contract matching the Customs Export Declaration
  • Foreign exchange transaction certificate / foreign exchange earning certificate issued by a Korean authorized foreign exchange bank proving that export proceeds were received and repatriated in foreign currency (the Korean statutory substantiation equivalent of an FIRC)

Registered businesses must issue an electronic tax invoice for taxable B2B supplies through HomeTax or an NTS-certified electronic tax invoice service provider, and the e-invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS electronic tax invoice repository. HomeTax provides web filing and API/machine-to-machine EDI interfaces for integrated e-invoice transmission. Korea does not require per-transaction real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or a cryptographic tax code; anti-fraud controls are enforced through mandatory electronic tax invoice issuance, central repository matching, invoice-number sequencing, and penalty taxes. Non-resident simplified VAT registrants serving B2C customers are generally not obliged to issue a domestic VAT invoice, but they must keep detailed electronic transaction records and file VAT returns through HomeTax.

If the NTS or Korea Customs Service cannot verify the export or foreign-use documentation, or if the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the zero rating is reversed and the supply is recharacterised as taxable domestic supply. VAT at 10% is then assessed on the supply, plus penalty tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes - generally 20% of the understated tax, increased to up to 40% for fraudulent or wilful evasion - together with late-payment interest. Any previously refunded zero-rated VAT is clawed back with statutory interest, and if forged export documentation or false foreign-exchange evidence is identified, criminal penalties may be imposed under the Punishment of Tax Evaders, Etc. Act, including imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but notification-based foreign exchange regime. The Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible for most current-account transactions, including trade settlement, software royalties, and dividend remittances, subject to reporting to a licensed foreign exchange bank and the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, inbound equity subscription, and cross-border loans, are permitted after a statutory report or permission under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, and the won floats in an onshore market; offshore access to KRW is restricted, and settlement of offshore contracts may be constrained. In practice, profit remittances such as dividends require proof of retained earnings, foreign investment registration, and completed Korean withholding tax payments; banks will refuse payment if the FEIS report package is incomplete or if the economic substance of the software and IoT licensing revenue is not documented. The regime is therefore open and business-friendly but imposes strict bank-level paper trails, with no legal prohibition against retaining foreign currency in an approved foreign currency account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for software, remote screen share, and IoT telemetry services must be collected within 6 months from the export or service provision date unless a licensed foreign exchange bank approves an extension. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio into KRW, and no forced surrender requirement; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Settlement Account at a licensed Korean bank or convert it freely. Each collection must be recorded with the foreign exchange bank and filed into the BOK FEIS trade settlement record. If collection exceeds the statutory timeline, the exporter must file a delayed-collection or collection-cancellation report supported by the underlying service contract, delivery logs, and evidence of commercial non-performance or force majeure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and foreign exchange information system operator, acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary corporate treasury account. Korean banking law does not recognise a foreign electronic money institution (EMI) wallet as a regulated deposit account for corporate cash management. A foreign EMI without a Korean branch license or a partnership with a Korean licensed bank cannot provide local KRW accounts, clearing rails, or FEIS reporting capability. Stripe and Paddle should be treated only as online payment aggregators for inbound card subscriptions and SaaS revenue, with settlement routed onward to a licensed Tier-1 Korean bank account. The Korean entity must maintain its real operating and foreign currency settlement accounts at a domestic foreign exchange bank, because capital contributions, trade reporting, dividend remittances, and statutory tax remittances require an onshore banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must file a Foreign Investment Promotion Act report through MOTIE or a delegated Tier-1 foreign exchange bank before making the inward remittance. The investor wires the share subscription consideration by SWIFT MT103 to the Korean entity's capital escrow account or foreign currency settlement account at the named Korean bank, with remittance instructions clearly stating foreign direct investment and share subscription purpose. The bank issues a certificate of foreign currency receipt, enters the transaction into the BOK FEIS under the appropriate capital transaction purpose code, and converts the funds to KRW through the licensed foreign exchange market. A Korean statutory auditor or certified public accountant then verifies paid-in capital and the actual issuance of shares; the entity completes registration of the capital increase with the Korean commercial registry and notifies the tax office. Stripe and Paddle receivables are not capital injections and must be processed as service export trade revenue, not as paid-in capital.Outward trade settlements for software procurement, cloud infrastructure, hardware for IoT devices, third-party professional services, and dividend remittances should be made using SWIFT MT103 through the licensed Korean bank, with the bank filing the required FEIS transaction report. All disbursements must be supported by original contracts, electronic tax invoices under the Korean VAT Act, and evidence of withholding tax payment where Korean-source royalties or dividends are remitted. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national Corporate Income Tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion; plus 10% local income tax surcharge on national CIT, giving an aggregate headline burden of 23.1%; lower bracket rates are 9% up to KRW 200 million and 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined for outbound cash or deemed dividends to non-resident persons, composed of 20% national Corporate Tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surcharge on the national withholding tax; DTA-reduced dividend rates of 10% or 15% commonly apply for qualifying corporate beneficial owners with certain shareholding thresholds

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final annual corporate tax return and payment must be filed within 3 months after the end of the fiscal year; for a December 31 fiscal year-end the statutory deadline is March 31 of the following calendar year; foreign corporations may request limited extension under specific statutory conditions

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding taxes on any dividend, interest, royalty, or other outbound payment must be remitted to the NTS by the 10th day of the month following the payment date; annual treaty and withholding summary follows the corporate tax return cycle

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in aggregate intercompany international transactions with foreign related parties triggers mandatory Local File preparation; a Master File is additionally required when the Korean entity is part of a group with annual consolidated revenue of at least KRW 100 billion and the Korean intercompany transaction volume reaches KRW 5 billion; simplified documentation may apply for lower materiality but NTS may request contemporaneous evidence on any audit

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies substance-over-form under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the beneficial ownership and economic substance requirement embedded in all applicable DTA articles; although Korea does not operate an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime, the NTS may deny tax treaty benefits and recharacterise interposed Korean Chusik Hoesa structures when decision-making, risk control, employees, and Core Income-Generating Activities for the remote-screen-share or IoT telemetry business are located outside Korea; CFC rules attribute low-taxed offshore passive income to Korean controlling shareholders if the foreign company lacks real economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1): imprisonment up to 10 years or a fine not exceeding five times the amount of evaded tax; Framework Act on National Taxes fraud additional tax of 40% of the omitted tax plus daily delinquency interest; NTS can impose 20% failure-to-file additional tax on unpaid amounts

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the Chusik Hoesa veil when directors used the corporation as a sham or conduit; directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, face civil asset restitution and seizure of personal bank accounts, may be placed on the NTS departure-restriction blacklist (travel ban) pending tax payment, and can be sentenced to prison for concurrent criminal offences
Abusive shell company and treaty-shopping structure

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 5 permit NTS to recharacterise transactions according to economic substance and impose arm-length corrections; treaty relief is denied under DTA limitation-on-benefits or beneficial ownership clauses; Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 allows disregard of sham entities; 40% fraud additional tax applies to any false treaty claim

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false treaty declarations may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and withholding tax of the shell company; NTS can blacklist beneficial owners and issue departure bans; directors face criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, with imprisonment terms applied under Punishment of Tax Evaders Act where the abuse is wilful
AML, foreign exchange, and unlicenced payment-rail non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information imposes registration, customer due diligence, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations; Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 34 sanctions violations with imprisonment up to 3 years or fines up to KRW 100 million; unreported inbound or outbound transfers can be confiscated together with withholding tax and penalties

Director personal liability: Directors personally face criminal imprisonment and fines; funds routed through unlicensed rails are subject to disgorgement and civil asset forfeiture; Korean tax and financial authorities may issue a departure-control order (blacklisting) against directors and shareholders; the corporate veil is pierced for money-laundering and fraudulent conveyance when the company was used as an instrument of tax evasion

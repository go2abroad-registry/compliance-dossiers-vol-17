Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); customs clearance via Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) must file the Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office within 20 days of business commencement, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered articles of incorporation, and representative identity documentation; the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which also serves as the legal entity VAT identification number. For a foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business, the entity must register with the NTS through a domestically appointed tax representative no later than 20 days after commencing taxable supply, filing the same Business Registration Application together with the power of attorney, constitutional documents, board resolution authorizing the tax representative, and an address of service in Korea; the NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number in the name of the non-resident enterprise. There is no separate VAT ID in Korea; the Business Registration Number is the sole statutory TIN/VAT ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) must file the Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office within 20 days of business commencement, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered articles of incorporation, and representative identity documentation; the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which also serves as the legal entity VAT identification number. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident corporations: mandatory registration from business commencement, no turnover threshold. Non-resident enterprises: mandatory registration within 20 days of commencing taxable supplies in the Republic of Korea, no turnover exemption threshold. Individual simplified taxpayer threshold (KRW 80 million per annum) is not available to corporate entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export of goods: the goods must be physically dispatched from Korea under an export declaration cleared by Korea Customs Service; the purchaser must be a non-resident and ownership and risk must pass offshore. Zero-rated export of qualifying services, including esports platform and tournament services: the services must be supplied to a non-resident party for consumption wholly outside Korea, and if required by statutes, paid in foreign currency and covered by a written export contract. The 0% VAT rate applies only when the prescribed evidentiary documents are retained and filed with the National Tax Service.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic Export Declaration bearing the exporter business registration number and customs clearance number
  • Commercial invoice signed by the exporter referencing the export declaration number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical export movement from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank confirming the inward remittance of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing through the National Tax Service HomeTax system: every VAT-registered business must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) for all B2B taxable supplies and transmit it to HomeTax by the statutory deadline, generally by the 10th day of the month following the supply month. The NTS provides tax invoice transmission APIs and ERP integration; there is no real-time fiscal stamping clearance comparable to Latin American anti-fraud stamping systems, but electronic invoice data is validated and stored in the NTS tax clearance database for continuous tax monitoring.

If the National Tax Service determines that the zero-rated export substantiation is incomplete, false, or that export proceeds were not repatriated within 180 days without a legally recognized excuse, the supply is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply and VAT at 10% is assessed retrospectively. Additional penalty taxes are imposed for non-issuance or wrongful issuance of tax invoices and misdeclaration, typically 10-30% of unpaid VAT and up to 40% for intentional concealment. Criminal prosecution may follow for fraudulent export declaration or fraudulent VAT refunds, with fines and imprisonment under the Value-Added Tax Act, Customs Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the zero-rate benefit is clawed back and the exporter must file a revised tax return with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is highly convertible on current account transactions, including service fees, royalties, payroll, and dividends, subject only to statutorily mandated reporting and genuine underlying transaction documentation. Capital account transactions remain event-driven and require prior or concurrent reporting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in authorized foreign currency deposit accounts, provided proceeds are collected within the prescribed settlement period and reported through FEIS. Profit remittance is legally possible after the foreign investment report has been completed and the capital verification certificate is issued, but the remitting bank will require proof of distributable retained earnings, audited financial statements, a board dividend resolution, tax clearance for the 20 percent (or treaty-reduced) dividend withholding tax, and a true copy of the foreign investment report certificate. Practical hurdles include lengthy documentation review, the need for an in-person banking signatory, and operation of an internal FX transaction monitoring framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, a resident exporter of goods must collect the full invoice value within 18 months from the date of export declaration; for services and digital content (including esports platform subscriptions and tournament management fees), the standard collection period is 6 months from the completion date of the service, extendable by prior approval for justified cases. No fixed mandatory conversion percentage applies; the exporter may retain 100% of foreign currency proceeds in a foreign currency deposit at an authorized foreign exchange bank. However, any transfer or payment in Korean won must be supported by an FX contract at the deposit-taking bank and reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Export proceeds that are not collected or reported on time are treated as unrepatriated export proceeds and can trigger customs, banking, and tax sanctions.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), as monetary authority, acting together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for use as a primary treasury or bank account; Medium only as an auxiliary card-issuing or merchant acquiring rail. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only licensed electronic financial business operators registered with the Financial Services Commission can provide payment services for Korean entities. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex do not have depository licenses in Korea and cannot issue Korean settlement accounts or handle won-denominated deposits. Stripe and PayPal can be used as front-end collection rails for the esports platform after the Korean entity obtains a domestic merchant or payment gateway arrangement, but all settlement funds must eventually clear into a domestic Tier-1 bank account. The treasury should therefore rely on Tier-1 banks for liquidity and treat PayPal and Stripe only as originating channels.

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 14 imposes a substance-over-form doctrine, and the Corporate Tax Act and tax treaties require beneficial ownership and real business substance. The National Tax Service may disregard a Chusik Hoesa or an intercompany transaction that lacks valid commercial purpose, actual employees, premises, board decision-making, and core income-generating activities. The OECD Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is used in transfer pricing and permanent establishment analyses to allocate profit to the jurisdiction where substantive personnel and strategic decisions are located.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 10% to 20% of the unpaid tax under Articles 47-2 and 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, statutory overdue interest, and criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, which allows imprisonment for up to five years and fines of up to five times the evaded tax; aggravated tax evasion above the statutory threshold can result in imprisonment for three to ten years.

Director personal liability: The National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil where the Chusik Hoesa is a sham or the director personally directed the evasion, making the director jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Director personal assets may be seized, the director may be blacklisted as a serious tax offender with public disclosure, a travel ban may be imposed under tax collection procedures, and the director faces criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may adjust the taxable income under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 4 and assess the difference plus additional tax and overdue interest. Failure to produce transfer pricing documentation can result in administrative penalty surcharges, denial of deductions, and a recharacterization of the excessive payments as deemed dividends or other taxable distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the non-arm's length intercompany arrangements can be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiency if the arrangements are found to be artificial and intended to evade tax. Civil asset restitution can be enforced against director personal property, the directors can be placed on the National Tax Service serious-tax-offender blacklist with travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution may follow if false documents or fraudulent transfer pricing records are submitted.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Double Tax Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Article 98 makes the Korean payer a statutory withholding agent; failure to withhold and remit makes the company liable for the uncollected tax, additional tax, and interest. False claims of treaty benefits are treated as fraudulent withholding tax under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and can attract criminal penalties including imprisonment, fine, and forfeiture of the economic benefit obtained from the under-withholding.

Director personal liability: Director officers of the Chusik Hoesa who direct the dividend distribution or approve the treaty relief application are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties. The National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil where the payment structure is a sham, seize the director's personal property, impose an international travel ban, blacklist the director for repeat tax offenses, and pursue criminal imprisonment for intentional evasion of withholding obligations.

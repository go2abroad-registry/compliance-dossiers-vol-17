Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under Korean law. The incorporators may fix any stated capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation, but the full subscription price of issued shares must be paid into a blocked capital-subscription escrow bank account before incorporation registration. The bank-issued certificate of deposit must be filed with the District Court Registry, and the deposited funds may not be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is perfected; thereafter, the funds are released into the operational account of the company. For practical bank account opening, payment gateway onboarding, and e-commerce marketplace approvals, a stated capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 is recommended.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a pure online DTC operation without any physical premises in Korea. If a business office or fulfillment space is later opened, the company must file a Fire Safety Facilities Report under the Fire Services Act, obtain the Civil Defense fire safety inspection certificate, validate structural load compliance for any racking or storage and, if employees are engaged, secure workplace safety clearance and industrial accident compensation insurance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act and the Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory economic turnover exemption threshold for VAT registration; every business operator must register from the commencement of business. A simplified taxpayer status may apply when annual taxable sales are below KRW 80,000,000, but VAT registration remains compulsory and e-tax invoice obligations are not waived.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act, 0% VAT applies to (i) goods physically exported from Korea and cleared by Korean Customs, (ii) certain domestic supplies treated as exports, and (iii) services and intangible property, including digital content and online game/digital-media products, supplied to non-resident persons with use or consumption outside Korea. For the Shopify DTC brand, zero-rating is available for goods exported to foreign consumers and for qualifying digital products delivered to foreign buyers if the seller maintains proof of the foreign non-resident status of the customer, proof that the digital service is supplied to a foreign destination (e.g., IP/geolocation evidence), export/transport documentation for physical goods, and proof of collection of export proceeds in foreign currency. An early VAT refund can be claimed in the NTS VAT return for excess input tax created by zero-rated export sales, but the refund is subject to document-based post-audit verification.

  • Export Declaration (final export declaration submitted through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or comparable carriage document) evidencing physical dispatch from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange collection certificate / inward remittance confirmation for export proceeds
  • For digital services: non-resident verification records (IP logs, customer country data, order history) in addition to the export evidence

Electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) are mandatory for all VAT-taxable supplies to other business operators and must be issued through the National Tax Service HomeTax system using authorized accounting/ERP software or the HomeTax open API. E-tax invoice records are transmitted to the NTS in near-real-time for validation and matching with VAT returns; B2C transactions require the NTS cash-receipt system when cash receipts are requested. Korea does not apply an anti-fraud cryptographic stamp system; fraud controls are based on NTS real-time e-invoice validation, automated cross-checks, and compulsory e-invoice retention.

If the export documentation bundle is incomplete or the export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline and no valid extension is obtained, the NTS will deny the zero rating, reclassify the supply as domestic, and assess 10% VAT on the transaction. Penalties may include additional tax for underreporting/unfiled VAT, late-payment interest on the assessed VAT, and, for fraudulent or intentionally forged export documents, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Any early VAT refund previously triggered by the zero-rated export will be clawed back with the additional taxes. Export transactions remain subject to NTS post-clearance and VAT audit risk for a multi-year prescription period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized but formally monitored convertibility framework. Current-account flows, including DTC brand sales, supplier invoices, and service fees, may be settled freely through licensed foreign exchange banks, subject to documentation and threshold-based reporting requirements. Capital-account flows are open but require notification, registration, or ex-ante reporting before funds can be legally capitalized or remitted. Foreign direct investment, equity injections, and intercompany loans must be declared through the Foreign Exchange Information System via an authorized bank; dividend and profit remittances are lawful but are conditional on tax clearance, audited financial statements, board resolutions, and a credible source-of-funds trail. The Korean won is not freely offered offshore to non-residents without documentary business basis, and non-resident onshore KRW accounts are restricted. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender to the central bank, but foreign currency receipts must be channeled through approved banking routes. The practical challenge for a cross-border DTC operation is therefore not availability of hard currency but the sufficiency and consistency of the underlying transaction evidence required by banks and the Bank of Korea to prevent disguised capital flows and tax avoidance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 13 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected through a domestic foreign exchange bank or an approved foreign financial institution within six months from the date of export declaration, unless a valid extension is obtained from the MOEF/BOK for bona fide reasons. There is no mandatory percentage surrender to the central bank; the exporter may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts, convert to KRW at market rates, or use them for approved offsetting foreign exchange payments. For low-value e-commerce parcel exports, collection must still be traceable to the registered exporter, and consolidated transaction reports must be provided when aggregate activity exceeds BOK reporting thresholds. Failure to realize export proceeds within the statutory deadline without lawful justification can result in administrative penalties, reclassification of the receivable, and enhanced AML reviews on subsequent export collections.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) retaining primary foreign-exchange policy and oversight powers under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its subordinate regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a treasury clearing rail. South Korea's Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act do not recognize foreign electronic-money institutions or remote-only EMIs as licensed deposit-taking banks for domestic corporate account opening, capital receipt, FX reporting, or trade settlement. Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe may be used as consumer-facing collection gateways for DTC e-commerce orders, but they cannot legally serve as the primary liquidity rail for the Korean Chusik Hoesa's foreign currency account, FDI capital injection, trade settlement, or Bank of Korea reporting. Any attempt to rely on an unlicensed offshore EMI for settlement into Korea will trigger automatic AML holds, delayed payment recognition, and potential fines for using unregistered foreign exchange channels. The compliant structure is to keep a Tier-1 Korean bank as the treasury core and route gateway settlements onward through SWIFT or local bank transfers into that core account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity investment into the Chusik Hoesa must be executed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The non-resident shareholder remits foreign currency, normally USD or EUR, by SWIFT MT103 to the Korean company's designated foreign currency temporary account at a Tier-1 Korean bank, using the FX purpose code for non-resident stock acquisition in a domestic company and referencing the corporate registration number. The Korean receiving bank files the foreign direct investment report to the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information System, after which the funds may be converted to KRW and credited to the capital account. The company then obtains a certified public accountant's capital verification certificate, files the capital increase with the relevant court registry, and maintains a complete capital contribution ledger. Funds must come directly from the registered shareholder; third-party advances, cryptocurrency proceeds, or non-resident KRW-account funding are not acceptable for capital verification.Outbound supplier and operational payments should be settled through the corporate bank's licensed foreign exchange channel. For standard purchase obligations, SWIFT MT103 or MT202 remittances are appropriate, with correct FX purpose codes and underlying invoices. For higher-value merchandise procurement requiring conditional assurance, a Commercial Letter of Credit is the recommended clearing rail. Documentary collections, including D/P and D/A, are viable when the trade relationship supports less expensive settlement with transport documents. Every outbound disbursement must be supported by a pro-forma invoice or purchase order, supplier bank details in the destination country, customs HS classification, tax invoice evidence, and the beneficial owner cascade. E-commerce DTC outflows, such as fulfillment center fees, logistics costs, and advertising spend, must be pressed through the same bank account and reported to the Bank of Korea when aggregate transaction value exceeds the applicable reporting threshold.Deliverable KRW/USD forward contracts with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, limited to the volume of actual export receivables, supplier invoices, and documented trade exposure under the real-demand rule.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts on Korean won, permissible only where the hedging counterparty is an authorized bank and the underlying exposure is clearly proven by export orders, payment forecasts, or invested foreign capital; settlement is in USD and reported under the applicable FX purpose code.Cross-currency swaps for external debt refinancing, intercompany funding, or long-term trade hedging, provided that the bank approves the facility and the notional amount is strictly matched to the agreed underlying exposure.Hedge tenors must not exceed the maturity of the underlying commercial contracts, and the entity must maintain a documented hedge policy demonstrating no speculative, naked, or excess positioning beyond forecast cash flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a treasury clearing rail. South Korea's Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act do not recognize foreign electronic-money institutions or remote-only EMIs as licensed deposit-taking banks for domestic corporate account opening, capital receipt, FX reporting, or trade settlement. Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe may be used as consumer-facing collection gateways for DTC e-commerce orders, but they cannot legally serve as the primary liquidity rail for the Korean Chusik Hoesa's foreign currency account, FDI capital injection, trade settlement, or Bank of Korea reporting. Any attempt to rely on an unlicensed offshore EMI for settlement into Korea will trigger automatic AML holds, delayed payment recognition, and potential fines for using unregistered foreign exchange channels. The compliant structure is to keep a Tier-1 Korean bank as the treasury core and route gateway settlements onward through SWIFT or local bank transfers into that core account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Article 36 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, a Korean Chusik Hoesa must prepare a Local File and Master File when the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 10 billion per fiscal year; a simplified documentation requirement applies to smaller transactions where the taxpayer has engaged in intercompany transborder dealings with affiliates.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the UAE-derived statutory term 'Economic Substance Regulations', but applies a substantive economic-substance doctrine through Article 4 of the Corporate Tax Act, Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the place of effective management rules. A foreign shareholder of a Korean Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate that the Korean company executes its core income-generating activities in Korea, maintains director-level substance, keeps board records, has bank accounts, and is not a tax-haven shell. A company incorporated abroad but with effective management in Korea may be recharacterized as a domestic Korean corporation for tax purposes, and treaty benefits can be denied under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule if the shareholder is a mere conduit without local offices, employees, or business operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealment of E-commerce Revenue Shell

Statutory sanction: Articles 9 and 11 of the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act impose imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to three times the evaded tax; aggravated punishment is triggered when the tax evaded exceeds KRW 1 billion. The National Tax Service also adds a 20% to 40% understatement or non-filing penalty and daily delinquency surcharges.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Article 42 of the Korean Commercial Act and Article 39 of the National Tax Collection Act. A director who knowingly participated in the concealment will be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax with personal asset seizure, international travel ban via NTS blacklisting, civil restitution in shareholder derivative litigation, and criminal imprisonment punishable by the underlying evasion offense.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Korean General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 2-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes denies the treaty rate; Article 92-8 of the International Tax Adjustment Act recharacterizes the intermediary as a Korean shareholder and reattributes intangible income back to Korea. A serious understatement penalty of up to 40% of the recharacterized tax base applies, and prosecution may be referred to the Public Prosecutor's Office for abuse of tax convention.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to veil piercing and become personally liable for the aggregate denied-benefit amount, incur NTS travel blacklisting, be named on the customs turnover report and asset seizure orders, have personal assets traced through foreign accounts, and face criminal imprisonment of up to 10 years in aggravated cases involving artificial conduit arrangements.
AML / Unlicensed Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Articles 5 and 6 of the Financial Transaction Reports Act impose imprisonment of up to 5 years or a monetary penalty of up to KRW 100 million for intentional failure to report suspicious transactions; under the Proceeds of Crime Act, any commercial money laundering activity carries forfeiture of assets and imprisonment of up to 14 years for aggravated cases. The Financial Intelligence Unit may also impose administrative fines and revoke the company's foreign exchange banking permissions.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil where a director directed or benefited from the laundering practice, imposing joint liability for launder proceeds, personal asset freezing, travel ban and blacklisting by the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit, lifetime disqualification from serving as a director, and criminal imprisonment reflecting the underlying AML offense.

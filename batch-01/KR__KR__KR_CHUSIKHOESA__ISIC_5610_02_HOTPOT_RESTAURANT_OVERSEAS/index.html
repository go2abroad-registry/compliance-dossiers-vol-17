Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Commercial Act. The paid-in capital is the amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation and must be deposited into a bank account opened in the Company name before the incorporation registration application is filed. There is no blocked escrow account requirement; however, the funds must remain on deposit until the registration certificate is issued and the funds can be used only for Company purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety permit under the Act on Firefighting Facilities Installation and Safety Management, including fire escape routes, extinguishers, gas detection, exhaust duct cleaning, and sprinkler inspection; structural load and occupancy approval from the local building office; civil defense emergency evacuation signage and approval for the restaurant occupancy; and occupational safety and health clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act from the Ministry of Employment and Labor.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs and export clearance portal: Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a Chusik Hoesa (Korean stock corporation), the single statutory identifier is the 10-digit Business Registration Number issued by NTS, which serves as Corporate TIN and VAT ID. A foreign non-resident entity entering Korea must first determine if it has a domestic place of business (fixed establishment). If yes, registration is mandatory under the Value-Added Tax Act; if no fixed establishment, the foreign entity should register through a domestic tax representative for any Korean VAT/tax exposure. Procedure: (1) prepare apostilled certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign business license, evidence of Korean place of business (lease or right-to-use agreement), passport/alien registration details of the resident manager, and notarized power of attorney for the tax representative; (2) file the statutory Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office or via HomeTax; (3) NTS validates the submitted establishment facts and issues the Business Registration Number, generally within three business days; (4) register for electronic tax invoice issuance under HomeTax; (5) if exporting goods, obtain an exporter code from Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS) and register for export declarations. Non-resident entities with no Korean fixed establishment but with Korean-source taxable supplies must nevertheless register, or appoint a Korean tax representative, to file and pay VAT; failure to register exposes the entity to non-deduction of input VAT and penalty surcharges.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea has no check-the-box or pass-through entity election; a Chusik Hoesa is per se taxable as a domestic corporation. Separate statutory elections exist for group tax consolidation of wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries and tax-neutral restructuring (merger/spin-off), but these are not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover exemption threshold. Every business operator, including foreign entities with a Korean domestic place of business, must register from commencement of taxable business. Simplified taxpayer status applies to small businesses with annual taxable turnover under KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Korean supplier may claim 0% VAT on: (i) tangible goods (e.g., hotpot condiments, packaged food ingredients) physically exported from Korea and cleared by Korea Customs Service under an export declaration; (ii) services supplied to non-residents or to foreign jurisdictions where the place of supply and actual use/benefit are outside Korea, including franchising, management, royalty and offshore digital services; (iii) goods and services supplied for international transportation or overseas construction projects, where statutory evidence exists. For the overseas hotpot dining chain, dining services performed in Korea are standard-rated at 10%; fees charged to overseas franchise affiliates are zero-rated only if the supporting contracts and performance evidence demonstrate actual foreign place of supply/use and foreign currency receipts. Exporters with excess input VAT are entitled to accelerated VAT refunds, subject to production on audit of the full substantive evidence bundle.

  • Korea Customs Service approved Export Declaration (UNI-PASS electronic export clearance, equivalent of customs Bill of Entry)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Korea
  • Overseas sales contract or purchase order
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Inward Remittance Certificate and Korean bank certificate showing receipt of foreign currency in Korea
  • Service performance evidence for qualifying offshore service exports (statement of work, delivery/access logs, overseas user evidence)

All business-to-business supplies require issuance of an electronic tax invoice through the NTS HomeTax system or an approved e-invoice service provider. The e-tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day following the supply date. There is no discretionary VAT pre-clearance approval; anti-fraud control is achieved through mandatory electronic transmission, digitally authenticated issuer credentials, unique sequential invoice numbers, and NTS cross-matching of e-invoices with customs export declarations and corporate VAT returns. Large taxpayers must use system-to-system/API or EDI integrations with HomeTax for bulk transmission. Simplified taxpayers are subject to reduced e-invoicing/cash-receipt obligations, but B2B zero-rated exports must always be supported by a zero-rated electronic tax invoice.

NTS reconciles zero-rated sales against customs export declarations and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without verified export, or foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated to Korea within the statutory 180-day period, NTS reverses the zero-rating and assesses 10% VAT on the full supply amount with interest, plus surcharges of up to 20% for negligent under-reporting and up to 40% for intentional concealment or fraudulent false e-tax invoices. Issuing false export tax invoices to obtain zero-rating or input VAT refunds is a criminal tax fraud offence under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act and may trigger imprisonment/fines, retrospective reassessment within the extended 10-year statute of limitations for offshore fraud, refund clawback, and disqualification of export refund privileges. The taxpayer retains the burden of proof through bills of lading, FIRC, contracts, and service-performance evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized, OECD-compliant foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with current account transactions generally treated as freely convertible while capital account transactions are subject to registration, declaration, and reporting obligations. Non-resident and resident investors may transfer dividends, profits, and liquidation proceeds after tax if the original foreign investment was properly notified to BOK or a designated foreign exchange bank. There are no pervasive rationing rules for ordinary operating receipts; however, the foreign exchange bank remains responsible for verifying the underlying transaction, collecting trade proceeds within statutory deadlines, and reporting settlements through BOK's Foreign Exchange Information System. The main friction for a Korean Chusik Hoesa is not technical inconvertibility but documentary compliance: missing BOK transaction codes, delayed declaration of foreign direct investment, incomplete export or import customs confirmation, or inconsistent beneficiary and reference lines can trigger bank holds and regulatory penalties. Profit remittance to overseas shareholders requires board minutes, audited financial statements, tax withholding documentation if dividends are paid, and a foreign exchange bank remittance declaration. In practice, convertibility is high for lawful operating cash flows, but the compliance burden is asymmetric for cross-border equity injections and overseas disbursements, which require more rigorous underlying documentation and purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect export proceeds through a Korean foreign exchange bank within one year from the export declaration date; extensions may be granted by the foreign exchange bank for justified reasons. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio into KRW; export proceeds may be credited to a foreign currency account or converted to KRW at market rates, but every receipt must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System and the export declaration must be settled against the actual collection.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as Korea's central bank and foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, operating alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance's Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, foreign exchange business may be conducted only by banks licensed as foreign exchange banks; overseas digital EMIs without a Korean banking license cannot legally maintain corporate KRW or foreign currency operating accounts for a Chusik Hoesa or hold client funds for FX settlement. They may only be used as supplementary payment gateways for local merchant acquiring or POS processing through licensed acquirers, and they must never be treated as the primary treasury, clearing, or FX rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank as the receiving bank. The overseas shareholder must wire via SWIFT MT103 to the entity's Capital Escrow Account or operating account with the precise shareholder name, investment reference, and appropriate BOK foreign exchange purpose code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 500 million aggregate foreign-related party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory filing of the Foreign Related Party Transaction Statement with the annual corporate tax return. Local File (and Master File if applicable) is mandatory when foreign-related party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion in a year and the total revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; however, to avoid NTS audit risk, contemporaneous documentation is recommended for any intercompany transaction regardless of threshold, especially for trademark royalty flows and condiment supply agreements.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a strong substance-over-form and anti-avoidance framework: under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, transactions structured to artificially avoid tax will be recharacterized. For foreign subsidiaries and branches, the Korean Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules attribute undistributed foreign income to the Korean parent when the foreign entity lacks genuine economic activities. The Korean-foreign hotpot chain must demonstrate that each overseas unit has substantial operational management, staffing, physical premises, and real decision-making for menu development, local licensing, and quality control. A purely paper arrangement with a foreign holding company claiming treaty benefits will be denied under the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test, and the NTS will reclassify the royalty or dividend as domestic-source income, triggering 22% withholding and subject to additional civil penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting of Overseas Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 9(1), evasion by fraud or willful misrepresentation is punishable with imprisonment for up to 2 years, or a fine not exceeding 2 times the evaded tax. If the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, imprisonment may extend to 5 years. The National Tax Service also imposes an additional 40% fraudulent return penalty plus late payment surcharge at 0.025% per day on the entire under-reported amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns or directed the concealment are personally liable for the evaded tax and penalties under the secondary liability rule (Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 39). The NTS may seize personal bank accounts, impose a travel ban (blacklisting as repeating tax evader), and refer the case to the prosecutor, leading to criminal conviction, incarceration, and disbarment from serving as a corporate officer.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Dividend/Royalty Tax & Improper Treaty Application

Statutory sanction: The corporate payor becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act plus an additional 20% (40% if intentional) under the Framework Act Art. 47-2, and a 0.025% daily late payment surcharge. If the payor intentionally omits withholding, the director may face criminal sanctions under Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment up to 2 years. The dishonest treaty relief claim can be voided ab initio, and the entire 22% plus penalties becomes immediately collectible.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed for the deficiency, penalties, and surcharges when the company cannot pay. The NTS will pierce the corporate veil and hold each responsible director jointly and severally liable. In serious cases, the tax authority will file a criminal complaint for tax evasion; the director may be extradited or subject to an Interpol Red Notice if already abroad, and any personal assets (including foreign shares, bank accounts, and real estate) are subject to seizure and auction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation & False Related-Party Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs Art. 5 and Corporate Income Tax Act Art. 76-5-3, the NTS will reassess taxable income at the arm's length price and impose an adjustment penalty of 10% of the adjusted income amount. If the statement of foreign transactions is not submitted or is falsely submitted, an additional KRW 2 million penalty applies. Intentional manipulation with deliberate false documentation constitutes tax evasion under Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years (or 5 years if evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million).

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false statement or who established a haphazard transfer pricing policy are personally liable for the reassessed tax, adjustment penalty, and daily surcharges when the corporate entity is asset-drained. More critically, individual directors can be subjected to a 'personal asset preservation order' by the NTS, seizure of shares and property, and an exit ban preventing them from leaving Korea until the assessed amount is secured. Criminal referral may lead to a prison sentence; if the director is a foreign national, the Korean court may impose a fine and the director may be barred from re-entering Korea.

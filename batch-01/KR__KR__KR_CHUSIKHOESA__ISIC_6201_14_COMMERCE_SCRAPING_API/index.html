Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For this industry, a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox arrangement will be rejected by the Korean tax authorities and by financial institutions. The Commercial Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes require the registered office to be the actual center of management, with operating books and records accessible on site. The National Tax Service will require proof of the physical location at the time of business registration, and banks and virtual asset service providers will reject a mail-drop address as insufficient for AML substance verification. A serviced office with a dedicated lease, independent signage, and a Korean-based operations manager may be acceptable only if the company proves continuous substance, but at minimum a physically verifiable registered office is mandatory for ISIC 6201 and payment rails including Stripe and USDC.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under the 2012 amendment to the Korean Commercial Act, there is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for the incorporation of a Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price of shares issued at incorporation must be deposited into a domestic bank or securities company account opened by the promoters, and the deposited funds must remain blocked until the District Court issues the certificate of incorporation registration. If contributions are made in kind, a court-appointed appraiser's certificate is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, via the HomeTax electronic tax portal (hometax.go.kr); customs and export clearing are handled through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity carrying on a VAT-taxable business in Korea must file a Business Registration Application with the NTS district office having jurisdiction over the Korean place of business, using HomeTax or written submission, within 20 days from the date business activities begin. The application must include the foreign entity's articles of incorporation, a certificate of existence or equivalent corporate record from the home jurisdiction, a copy of the foreign representative passport or identification, proof of the Korean place of business (lease or ownership evidence), and, if there is no physical Korean office, a duly appointed local tax agent (tax manager) authorization. The NTS assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both the corporate Tax Identification Number and the VAT registration number; no separate VAT ID is issued. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to Korean customers are generally required to complete the simplified foreign company VAT registration procedure through a local tax agent. The registration must be updated for subsequent changes of address, representative, or taxable business scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a resident Chusik Hoesa is taxed as a separate legal entity on worldwide income; a foreign non-resident entity is taxed only on Korean-source income under the Corporate Tax Act).

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a US-style check-the-box or tax classification election. Corporate tax residency is determined by the location of a corporation's head office or place of actual management, and no hybrid mismatch election is available under domestic corporate tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold. Any business operator making VAT taxable supplies in Korea must register within 20 days from commencement. A simplified taxpayer regime applies to annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory; for non-resident digital service providers, the collection and remittance rules follow the place-of-supply nexus rather than a minimum revenue threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available for exported goods delivered from Korea to a foreign destination, and for exported services supplied to non-residents in exchange for foreign currency where the service is actually used or economically consumed outside Korea. For digital data-scraping and API gateway services, the taxpayer must evidence a non-resident customer, foreign-bound contract terms, no Korean domestic use or consumption, payment in foreign currency, and remittance to a Korean foreign-exchange bank.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration / outward clearance certificate issued via UNI-PASS
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or electronic transport document evidencing export movement
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract specifying the non-resident buyer and foreign-currency consideration
  • Foreign exchange remittance/earning certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (equivalently, an inward remittance certificate / FIRC)
  • Delivery or service-use evidence, including logs, data handover records, API access reports, or other technical proof of overseas use

VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoice) through HomeTax or a licensed ASP/ERP system connected to NTS by API/EDI. Each e-tax invoice must be transmitted to NTS no later than the day after supply. There is no physical fiscal stamping; the digital anti-fraud architecture consists of NTS real-time e-invoice matching, mandatory cash receipt system reports for B2C sales, bank- and card-transaction data sharing, and export/import clearance data exchange with Korea Customs Service. Non-compliance triggers statutory penalties for non-issuance, delayed issuance, or incomplete transmission.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly scrutinize export zero-rating through document review, bank foreign-exchange records, and cross-border data matching. If an exporter cannot substantiate physical export, foreign service delivery, or foreign-exchange repatriation within the statutory deadline, the zero rate is disapplied and VAT is clawed back at 10%, together with additional tax of up to 40% of the underdeclared VAT, late-payment interest, and administrative fines. Fraudulent or intentionally falsified export documentation may result in criminal penalties under the Framework Act on Tax Administration and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, customs sanctions, and revocation or amendment of the VAT registration record.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized foreign exchange framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, including payments for software subscriptions, cloud services, and API licensing, are generally freely convertible but must be routed through domestic authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions are also substantially liberalized, but certain transfers require reports or notifications to the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service. There is no blanket mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may hold foreign currency in Foreign Currency Accounts or convert to KRW through authorized banks. Profit remittance for a Chusik Hoesa is achievable via current account dividend payment procedures, but each remittance requires evidence of distributable profits, board resolutions, tax withholding certificates, and financial statements. Enhanced scrutiny applies when the counterparty is an offshore entity or where the underlying business model involves crypto assets, as local banks must satisfy Financial Intelligence Unit suspicious transaction reporting rules. Korea has no exchange surrender quota for current account earnings, but banks must verify the underlying trade or service transaction and file the prescribed foreign exchange statistics. In practice, repatriation of dividends from a foreign shareholder is processed within one to three business days once the corporate bank has completed the legal and tax verification, provided the company is fully compliant with corporate registration, external audit, and foreign investment notification requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and brought into Korea within 18 months from the date of export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; however, if the exporter requires KRW liquidity, conversion must be executed through an authorized foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts, subject to applicable reporting thresholds and documentary evidence of the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea; central foreign exchange administration is delegated to the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with operational controls executed by the Korea Customs Service and authorized foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs licensed in jurisdictions such as the UK, Lithuania, or Singapore are not viable primary banking rails for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. They are not licensed under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to service an onshore entity's KRW clearing needs, cannot provide a domestic bank guarantee for capital verification, and would be rejected by Korean tax authorities for capital and dividend remittance proof. They may be used only for ancillary non-KRW vendor payments, but domestic banks will treat any mingling of EMI balances with USDC or crypto settlement as a severe money laundering red flag. Overall feasibility rating: Low for domestic KRW clearing; limited for offshore ancillary USDC-denominated commercial payments and therefore not recommended for treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for a Chusik Hoesa should be wired via SWIFT MT103 through an authorized foreign exchange bank in Korea. The sender must include the precise remittance purpose code for inbound equity investment, and the Korean entity must file the post-arrival foreign investment report with the Ministry of Economy and Finance through the authorized bank. A separate Capital Escrow Account is strongly advised for paid-in capital, as it facilitates capital verification by an external auditor and supports the issuance of a Capital Verification Certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Reportable International Transactions require disclosure when intercompany cross-border related party transactions reach KRW 500 million; Local File is mandatory when foreign related party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Master File is mandatory when consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion

Economic substance scope: Korea applies substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance rules; although the explicit Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime is not used in Korea, a foreign corporation owned by a Chusik Hoesa must still demonstrate real management, employees, servers, decision-making, and asset allocation in Korea; failure to do so may attract corporate tax penalties and treaty abuse reclassification

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Crimes Penalty Act Article 3 imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to 5 times the tax evaded; in addition, the unfair underreporting penalty is 20% of the evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the fraudulent return are personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; personal bank accounts can be seized, assets traced from shareholders can be clawed back, and a travel ban (blacklisting) may be imposed to secure collection; conviction exposes directors to criminal imprisonment
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act for International Taxation and unilateral adjustments, the NTS may re-characterize the intermediary as a tax avoidance conduit and impose additional tax; penalty under Article 76-2 of the Corporate Income Tax Act can add up to 40% of the corrected tax base

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced; directors face unlimited personal liability for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties; criminal prosecution for aiding and abetting tax avoidance can lead to imprisonment up to 3 years; in aggravated cases involving cross-border evasion, a blacklist order prevents overseas emigration
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Foreign Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 29 insert: imprisonment not exceeding 5 years or fine not exceeding KRW 500 million; AML violations under the Act on Reporting and Using Specific Financial Information can also result in penalties up to 500 million KRW per offense

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally indicted if they authorized digital asset swapping transactions without compliance sign-off; civil asset restitution to the state, freezing of personal wealth, and a judicial travel ban apply; the court has imposed extraterritorial enforcement actions against Korean nationals managing offshore trusts

For a Contract Research Organization (CRO) engaged in clinical trial management, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible. Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Division of the District Court, Judiciary of the Republic of Korea (Supreme Court registry system KECREG; electronic filing portal for commercial registrations); post-registration business registration administered by the district tax office under the National Tax Service.

Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None statutory minimum: since the 2014 amendment to the Commercial Act, a Chusik Hoesa may be incorporated with any stated capital amount. The full subscribed amount must nonetheless be deposited into a dedicated temporary subscription account (escrowed with a licensed bank under Article 306) and remains blocked until the District Court Registry issues the certificate of incorporation registration; a working capital base of at least KRW 100 million is recommended to demonstrate operational solvency to multinational sponsors, insurance counterparties, and the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in clinical trial sponsorship contexts.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Korean Chusik Hoesa with a domestic place of business must complete court registration of incorporation, obtain its corporate registration number, and then file a business registration application with the competent district tax office within 20 days of business commencement. The NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number that serves simultaneously as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but not fully unregulated convertibility regime. Current-account receipts and payments are freely convertible without prior regulatory approval if routed through a designated foreign exchange bank and supported by underlying commercial documentation. Capital-account transactions are generally liberalized but are classified into three channels: fully liberalized transactions subject only to general permission, reportable transactions, and transactions requiring prior approval. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaged in cross-border CRO services, the practical profit-remittance hurdle is tax and documentation rather than FX approval: dividends may be remitted only after a shareholders' meeting declares distributable profits, the company has satisfied mandatory statutory reserve requirements, Korean withholding taxes have been paid (generally 20% to 22% before applicable tax-treaty reduction), and the foreign-exchange bank has received the board resolution, audited financial statements, and tax clearance evidence. The FX bank then files the required foreign-exchange transaction report with the Bank of Korea; no separate prior BOK approval is required for an ordinary dividend remittance otherwise in compliance with FETA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korean FETA and the related Foreign Exchange Regulations impose a collection-and-surrender obligation on all exporters, including cross-border service providers such as a clinical trial CRO. The statutory rule requires that 100% of invoiced export proceeds be collected in South Korea through a designated foreign-exchange bank within one year from the export or settlement date, depending on the terms stated by the bank. There is no mandatory percentage that must be converted into Korean won because Korea has formally abolished forced foreign-currency surrender; the exporter may lawfully retain collected proceeds in a resident foreign-currency account. However, the proceeds must be credited to the CRO's Korean bank account and the corresponding foreign-exchange transaction report must be filed by the bank. For a CRO, each inbound sponsor payment must be supported by a master services agreement, milestone certificate, tax invoice, and the bank's foreign-exchange declaration to satisfy the export-proceeds verification rule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), exercising delegated foreign exchange administration jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and its Enforcement Decree; routine supervisory and reporting duties are further delegated to designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for primary treasury purposes. Offshore digital electronic money institutions and cross-border fintech platforms are not licensed as designated foreign-exchange banks under the FETA and cannot independently open a Korean won settlement account for a Chusik Hoesa conducting clinical-trial services. Such platforms may be used only as an ancillary collection-engineering tool for small vendor payments or as an FX conversion intermediary outside Korea. The mandatory local regulatory rail is a licensed Korean commercial bank able to file foreign-exchange transaction reports, issue tax support documentation, and provide commercial clearing connectivity. Legal constraints under the FETA and Electronic Financial Transactions Act prevent an unlicensed offshore EMI from acting as the CRO's primary treasury clearing location.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound equity injection into the Korean Chusik Hoesa, the foreign investor must first ensure that the investment is reportable under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and FETA. The remitter wires funds by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own bank account to the designated foreign-exchange bank account of the Korean entity. The wire must clearly use the beneficiary CRO's official English name, its Korean business registration number, and a reference code matching the foreign-investment report number, with the purpose identifier clearly stated as FDI-CAPITAL-INCREASE. Before posting the funds, the Korean bank verifies the remitter name against the foreign-investment report, checks the source-of-funds evidence, validates the relevant Bank of Korea declaration, and only then credits the capital escrow account. The CRO must subsequently issue shares, file the foreign-investment closing report, and complete the Korean court corporate registry update. Loan-based injections must use the intercompany loan purpose indicator and must be supported by a notarized loan agreement, pricing evidence, and repayment schedule.Outbound payments by a Korean CRO for international clinical trial services, investigator grants, laboratory fees, and biotech vendor charges are cleared primarily through bank wire transfers using the SWIFT MT103 system. Each disbursement must be supported by the underlying service agreement, a valid commercial invoice, an itemized milestone statement, and, where applicable, a withholding-tax certificate. The foreign-exchange bank will assign the appropriate Korean foreign-exchange transaction purpose code and automatically file the Bank of Korea declaration. Commercial letters of credit are not the standard clearing rail for intangible CRO services because the service delivery cannot be represented by bills of lading and presentation documents; LCs are used only for procurement of physical clinical-trial materials, medical devices, or pharmaceutical supplies where documentary presentation is commercially meaningful.

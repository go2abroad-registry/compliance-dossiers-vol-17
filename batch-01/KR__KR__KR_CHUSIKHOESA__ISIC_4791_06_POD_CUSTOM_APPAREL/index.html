None. Korean law does not require a minimum statutory paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price of each issued share must be paid in cash into a blocked deposit account at a domestic bank before incorporation registration, and the paid-in funds may not be withdrawn until the court has issued the certificate of incorporation registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax System (http://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax representative in Korea before commencing any taxable supply or business. Submit an application for business registration (business registration certificate) to the competent district tax office through the HomeTax system, attaching the entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of association, board resolution appointing the domestic tax representative, power of attorney, evidence of registered place of business in Korea, and passport or certificate of incorporation of the foreign entity. The NTS issues a 10-digit tax identification number (also used as the VAT registration number) upon approval. If the foreign entity has no physical presence but makes electronic supplies of services or sells goods through a domestic fulfillment arrangement, it must still register through the same non-resident business registration procedure for VAT purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for corporations; every Chusik Hoesa engaging in taxable supplies in Korea is mandatorily required to register for VAT. A simplified taxable person threshold of KRW 30,000,000 per year applies only to individual entrepreneurs, not to a stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the customs territory of Korea qualify for the 0% VAT rate under the Value-Added Tax Act. The statutory criteria require actual exportation of the goods, transfer of title to a foreign buyer, physical shipment of the goods from Korea under customs control, and timely receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency. For print-on-demand retail, the goods must be manufactured and shipped from Korea directly to the overseas customer with documentation proving that the goods left the Korean customs territory and were delivered outside Korea.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Korea
  • Proof of foreign exchange receipt, including the Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate and the equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Order/purchase confirmation from the overseas buyer and marketplace sales records

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issuance and real-time transmission to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax system for all business-to-business and any invoice-issuing supplies. Corporate taxpayers are obliged to issue electronic tax invoices to all registered buyers within the statutory timeframe. The system uses an XML-based API and electronic medium for direct submission, with a one-day transmission window after issuance for normal cases. Cash receipts must also be issued and reported for qualifying business-to-consumer cash sales. There is no separate anti-fraud digital stamping or pre-approval per invoice, but the NTS operates real-time VAT return matching and compulsory e-invoice auditing.

The National Tax Service may disallow export zero-rating if the required customs declaration, transport documents, or foreign exchange receipts are missing, incomplete, or inconsistent. If zero-rating is denied, the unpaid VAT of 10% becomes immediately collectible, plus statutory late-payment surcharges and understatement penalties. Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, negligent underreporting attracts a penalty of up to 10% of the unpaid tax, fraudulent underreporting attracts a penalty of up to 40%, and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may impose criminal sanctions for falsely substantiated export claims. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may also result in loss of the zero-rating benefit and separate penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely open capital and current account under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Korean won (KRW) being fully convertible for current account transactions including trade in goods, services, royalties, and dividends. However, capital account transactions remain subject to a formal reporting and licensing regime, primarily through the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) and designated foreign exchange banks. Exporters are generally required to receive export proceeds within a defined statutory period, but the mandatory surrender requirement has been substantially liberalized; since 1995, export proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts without forced conversion, although a reporting obligation remains for settlement. Profit remittance, dividends, and capital repatriation for foreign investors are permissible but require underlying certification of inward foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and Korean banks mandate a tax clearance certificate and capital verification for outward remittances above specific thresholds. There are no blanket conversion prohibitions, but foreign exchange transactions may be subject to criminal penalty for misrepresentation of purpose codes. Businesses engaged in cross-border trade, such as the target Print-on-Demand custom apparel retail enterprise, must ensure precise classification of payments between current and capital accounts, use designated licensed banks for inward remittance to avoid delays, and be prepared to submit transaction documentation to the bank's compliance division to satisfy the Foreign Exchange Information Network reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected and settled within five years from the date of export declaration, provided the export contract or payment terms do not specify a shorter period. For proceeds received in foreign currency, the exporting entity may retain the funds in a foreign currency account at a domestic licensed bank without mandatory conversion into KRW. If conversion to KRW is performed, it must be executed through a foreign exchange bank, and a Foreign Exchange Settlement Notice is required for amounts exceeding the equivalent of 5,000 USD per transaction. Any diversion, delayed receipt, or non-receipt of export proceeds beyond the statutory timeline must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance with a justification, and failure to do so may result in administrative sanctions or criminal liability.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea) and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are generally not viable as primary banking rails in South Korea. The Korean financial regulatory system requires any entity engaging in domestic payment services, foreign exchange brokerage, or cross-border remittance to obtain a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act or be classified as a 'Foreign Exchange Business Operator' registered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Non-bank EMIs domiciled in jurisdictions such as the UK, Singapore, or Europe are not automatically passportable into Korea and cannot compete with domestic licensed foreign exchange banks for inward investor capital, export proceeds collection, or dividend remittance clearing. They may serve as secondary platforms for merchant acquiring through Stripe or PayPal if those entities partner with domestic banks, but the corporate entity must maintain a primary domestic KRW settlement account at a Tier-1 commercial bank. Any proposal to route capital injections or trade disbursements exclusively through an offshore EMI would trigger strong AML and foreign exchange non-compliance findings during bank onboarding, and is expressly discouraged.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) into the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank via SWIFT MT103 in USD, EUR, or KRW, with the wire designated as 'Capital Injection for Foreigner Investment' and the Inward Investment Code 'FOIC' captured in the SWIFT field 70. The bank will require a Certificate of Foreign Investment Notification issued by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) or the Foreign Exchange Bank after the investment filing. Within 30 days of receipt, a Foreign Exchange Settlement Notice must be submitted if the amount exceeds KRW 5,000,000, and the receiving bank will perform capital verification and issue a Certificate of Capital Injection, which is then used for the registration of the foreign-owned company under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Any inbound remittance not covered by a corresponding investment declaration will be automatically classified as non-declared foreign exchange receipt and may be refused or suspended.For outbound trade settlements involving payment to international garment manufacturers or POD production facilities, the Korean entity should use SWIFT MT103 single-payment transfers for standard supplier invoices, or Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) confirmed by a Korean Tier-1 bank when the counterparty demands documentary security. Documentary Collections are also permissible for lower-value transactions. Each outbound remittance must be accompanied by the Foreign Exchange Purpose Code (e.g., 'A1000' for trade in goods, 'A5230' for business services), the underlying commercial invoice, a transport document (e.g., air waybill, bill of lading), and where applicable, the Customs export declaration. Amounts exceeding USD 50,000 are subject to documentary review, and the bank may require submission of the original export contract or proforma invoice. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion, with mandatory 10% local income tax surcharge producing a combined top effective headline rate of 23.1%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within 3 months of the fiscal year-end (by March 31 for a December 31 year-end). The tax amount must be paid by the same deadline; an extension of up to 1 month is available for qualified conventional requests, and an additional 1-month extension may be granted for international filing groups subject to cross-border disclosure requirements.

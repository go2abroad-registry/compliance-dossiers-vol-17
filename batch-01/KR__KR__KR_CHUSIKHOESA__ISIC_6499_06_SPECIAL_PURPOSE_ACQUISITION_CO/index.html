Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, to qualify as a Special Purpose Acquisition Company, or SPAC, under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act and the Korea Exchange KOSDAQ Listing Rules, the Company must maintain at least KRW 3,000,000,000 in paid-in capital at the time of the public offering, and at least 90 percent of the gross proceeds collected in the initial public offering must be deposited into an independent trust or escrow account maintained with a licensed Korean financial institution. The escrowed proceeds may be released only upon consummation of an approved business combination or redemption event, pursuant to the trust agreement and applicable capital markets regulations.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to the SPAC itself because it does not conduct physical manufacturing, chemical processing, assembly, or warehousing. If the Company maintains a registered office or sponsor office with employees, it must comply with the Fire-Fighting System Installation and Safety Control Act, obtain the applicable fire safety certificate for the premises, satisfy structural load standards under the Building Act, and comply with the Korean Occupational Safety and Health Act administered by the Ministry of Employment and Labor.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered through the HomeTax online electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); export and customs procedures are executed with the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS single-window system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Chusik Hoesa incorporated under the Korean Commercial Act, the statutory TIN procedure is as follows: (1) complete incorporation and court registration; (2) submit the foreign investment notification, if applicable, to KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; (3) within 20 days from business commencement, submit the Application for Business Registration to the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office; (4) attach the certificate of corporate registration, articles of incorporation, proof of domestic business premises, authenticated shareholder identification (apostilled or consular-legalized for foreign shareholders), and the foreign investment notification certificate; and (5) obtain the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the national TIN and, after VAT activation, as the HomeTax VAT ID. A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business that realizes Korean-source income must instead register through the HomeTax portal, obtain a non-resident TIN, and appoint a domestic tax representative with a power of attorney under Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean law does not provide a check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for domestic corporations; a Chusik Hoesa is a domestic resident corporation taxed on worldwide income at the entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. Cross-border hybrid arrangements are instead constrained by the Hybrid Mismatch Arrangement rules in the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs and the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations, which disallow deduction/non-inclusion mismatches and require conformity with the place-of-effective-management tests.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold exists: every person who commences a taxable business must apply for VAT registration with the competent district tax office within 20 days under Article 53 of the Value-Added Tax Act, regardless of turnover. Simplified taxpayers with annual taxable supplies of KRW 80 million or less may apply simplified turnover-based assessment but remain mandatorily registered. A newly formed special purpose acquisition company must therefore register immediately even if its escrow/trust interest income is VAT-exempt financial income.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods qualify for a 0% rate if they are physically removed from Korea under a valid customs export declaration made in UNI-PASS. Cross-border services (services performed for non-residents outside the Korean territory, international transportation and overseas construction/technical services under foreign-exchange earning contracts) also qualify when the customer and place of supply are outside Korea. For a Chusik Hoesa, any cross-border investment-advisory or M&A service will be zero-rated only if it satisfies the foreign service/foreign-exchange earning contract tests and is supported by remittance of proceeds from abroad; domestic escrow interest and domestic listing fees remain VAT-exempt or taxable, not zero-rated.

  • Export Declaration Certificate (export customs clearance certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS single window
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Airway Bill naming the Korean exporter as shipper (or a recognized freight forwarder cargo receipt)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate / inward remittance credit advice from a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • Underlying sale or purchase contract (and, for service exports, a foreign exchange earning contract) showing the foreign counterparty, service description, and payment terms

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing for all B2B supplies. Every registered business must issue electronic tax invoices (ETIs) via the NTS HomeTax national e-invoicing infrastructure and transmit the ETI data to the NTS using the integrated XML/API and G2B/EDI interfaces within the statutory issuance deadline (transaction-based ETIs by the next day following the date of supply; monthly aggregated ETIs no later than the 15th day of the month following the supply month). The NTS validates transmitted invoices in near real time, assigns a national electronic invoice number, performs anti-fraud digital attestation, and may flag abnormal issuers; delayed or omitted transmission triggers automated notices and statutory addition taxes.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-matched by the NTS and Korea Customs Service against UNI-PASS export declarations, cargo manifests, bank foreign exchange settlement records, and HomeTax filings. If any required document is missing, forged, or inconsistent, the NTS retroactively assesses 10% output VAT on the gross supply value, disallows related input VAT credits/refunds, and imposes addition taxes of up to 40% of the unpaid VAT (with aggravated treatment and extended 10-year statute of limitations for fraudulent claims) plus daily late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Willful fraudulent zero-rating also triggers criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment up to 3 years or fines up to KRW 30 million, with aggravated penalties up to 10 years for large-scale evasion), and the NTS may suspend the offending entity's electronic tax invoice transmission privileges and export refund eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized yet regulated foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions, including ordinary trade settlements, service payments, and dividend remittances, are generally freely convertible subject to reporting and supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, such as equity injections, loan borrowings, and proceeds from SPAC initial public offerings, require prior notification or approval under the FETA, with submission through a designated Foreign Exchange Bank. The Korean won does not enjoy offshore convertibility; won must be exchanged domestically. Export proceeds do not face a rigid mandatory surrender ratio under the current regime, but exporters must settle or repatriate receipts within statutory timelines and provide valuation evidence to the receiving bank. Profit remittance hurdles include Corporate Income Tax certificate issuance, audited financial statements, proof of shareholding registry, and confirmation that the remitted amount corresponds to distributable earnings. For a SPAC vehicle, inbound capital earmarked for escrow or IPO proceeds requires a capital verification report certified by an independent licensed CPA and may be subject to MOEF reporting if the transaction exceeds threshold amounts. Cross-border transfers above KRW 500 million require a general report; while those with legal or financial substance above KRW 5 billion might require the approval of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Committee. The overall environment permits legal incorporation and repatriation, but the bank must document each movement using purpose codes and maintain an audit trail for BOK inspection for at least five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive export proceeds within 18 months from the date of the export declaration (export license date). If the exporter maintains a foreign currency deposit account, the proceeds may remain in that account without immediate conversion; however, any conversion into KRW must be executed through a Foreign Exchange Bank. There is no mandatory percentage surrender requirement; the statutory default is full retention in foreign currency or full conversion at the exporter's election, subject to FETA reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and virtual bank challengers are not legally permitted to open or maintain current or escrow accounts for Korean incorporated entities unless they hold a license under the Banking Act or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. A foreign-imposed EMI, such as those chartered in the EEA or Singapore, cannot service Korean domiciled SPAC operations without a domestic branch and FSS authorization. As a result, fintech EMI viability is rated Low for treasury clearing and Medium only as auxiliary payment channels for domestic disbursements if they partner with tier-1 licensed Korean banks. The SPAC's investor-facing escrow, IPO underwriting, and custodial functions must be domiciled at a Korean commercial bank or a licensed trust company to satisfy FSC regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Korean SPAC must be routed through a designated Foreign Exchange Bank using SWIFT MT103 messages with exact Korean Won settlement instructions. The remitter must include a clear reference to the SPAC investment contract and the unique KOLAS business registration number. The bank will escrow funds in a segregated custody account if required by the FSC, and the SPAC must submit the capital verification report from its statutory auditor within 30 days to complete the Foreign Exchange Reporting requirement. The correct FETA purpose code for equity capital is 6010. For acquisition funding to be used after a merger, the routing must also include a copy of the board resolution and the FSC-registered business combination disclosure. As a result, fintech EMI viability is rated Low for treasury clearing and Medium only as auxiliary payment channels for domestic disbursements if they partner with tier-1 licensed Korean banks. The SPAC's investor-facing escrow, IPO underwriting, and custodial functions must be domiciled at a Korean commercial bank or a licensed trust company to satisfy FSC regulations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing an aggregate top marginal burden of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate, comprising 20% national corporate income tax withholding on dividends under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 plus 2% local income tax surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Master File and Local File preparation and submission with or upon request during the tax audit.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies economic substance concepts through the Korean International Tax Adjustment Act general anti-avoidance rule, Controlled Foreign Corporation provisions, tax treaty beneficial-ownership rules, and anti-artificial transaction doctrine. A SPAC must maintain board meetings in Korea, strategic M&A target identification, escrow and trust account management from Korea, actual office facilities, and substantive staff payroll; failure to do so can result in recharacterization as a sham, denial of DTA relief, and CFC income attribution to controlling shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 imposes imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine of up to five times the evaded national and local tax; additional 40% fraud penalty is imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and the corporation may be publicly disclosed as a chronic tax defaulter.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns face piercing of the corporate veil through joint and several civil liability for underpaid tax and interest, National Tax Service blacklisting with travel bans, refusal of tax clearance certificates, and criminal imprisonment for up to five years in aggravated fraudulent evasion cases.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping Abuse Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Korean International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 2 and 17 authorize recharacterization, constructive dividend treatment, and denial of treaty benefits; Framework Act on National Taxes adds fraudulent and negligent surcharges, and Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act sanctions apply for false disclosures in a SPAC listing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under Commercial Act Articles 399 and 401 for damages caused to the corporation and third parties, separately assessed for unpaid tax, barred from future SPAC directorships, and subject to imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the shell structure is used to commit tax fraud, together with travel restrictions and asset freezing.
AML / Foreign Exchange and SPAC Registration Violations

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 137 imposes imprisonment for up to 3 years or a fine of up to KRW 100 million; the Act on Reporting and Using Certain Financial Transaction Information imposes sanctions on designated non-financial businesses for failing to identify beneficial owners, including fines of up to KRW 50 million per violation.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and representative directors can be criminally arrested and detained, personal assets may be frozen in civil restitution actions, and immigration blacklisting may be imposed by the authorities, leading to travel bans and disqualification from managing regulated financial entities.

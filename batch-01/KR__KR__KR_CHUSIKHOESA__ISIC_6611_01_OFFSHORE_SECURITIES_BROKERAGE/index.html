Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr); customs and export documentation administered by the Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS, https://unipass.customs.go.kr); foreign exchange and repatriation oversight by the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1 - Precondition: Obtain a financial investment business license from the Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (FSCMA) for securities dealing and brokerage. Step 2 - File the Business Registration Application (sa-eop-deung-rok-shin-cheong-seo) with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, or with the NTS International Tax Division for a foreign corporation without a domestic place of business. Attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, foreign corporate registry extract, FSC license certificate, power of attorney appointing a domestic tax representative (if applicable), and identity documentation of the representative director. Step 3 - The district tax office issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (sa-eop-ja-deung-rok-beon-ho) which functions simultaneously as the corporate TIN and the VAT registration identification number; issuance generally takes 3-7 business days. Step 4 - For a foreign corporation without a domestic establishment, the NTS assigns a foreign taxpayer registration number via the appointed domestic tax representative or withholding agent. Step 5 - The registration automatically activates electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issuance privileges, cash receipt obligations, and local tax registration for the 10% local income tax surcharge.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation - A Chusik Hoesa incorporated in South Korea is a domestic resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income under the Corporate Tax Act (Article 3), with no pass-through attributes. The progressive CIT rates for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2024 are: 9% on taxable income not exceeding KRW 200 million; 19% on the band from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on the band from KRW 20 billion to KRW 30 billion; 24% on taxable income exceeding KRW 30 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national CIT liability. Territorial scope: worldwide taxation for domestic corporations; no special tax-exempt zone status applies to a standard Chusik Hoesa engaged in securities brokerage.

        
Available election forms
Korea does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; classification follows statutory substance tests under the Corporate Tax Act and the International Tax Adjustment Act. Available elections and special mechanisms include: (1) consolidated corporate taxation (yeon-gyeol-nap-se-je-do) election under the Corporate Tax Act for a domestic parent that wholly owns (100%) domestic subsidiaries, allowing intra-group profit/loss offset and elimination of intercompany dividends; (2) indirect foreign tax credit election for dividends received from foreign subsidiaries; (3) foreign corporation election to register as a domestic corporation vs. a foreign corporation with a Korean permanent establishment; (4) hybrid entity and hybrid mismatch election/designation rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act implementing BEPS Action 2, requiring certain foreign transparent entities to be re-characterized; (5) currency translation election for foreign-currency denominated capital and revenue under the Corporate Tax Act; and (6) special small and medium enterprise tax rate elections for qualifying venture businesses.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No general economic turnover threshold exists for standard VAT registration; any person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea, or importing goods, is required to VAT-register. A simplified taxpayer (small business) regime applies to businesses with annual gross revenue not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 (2024 standard), permitting reduced filing and a simplified VAT calculation. Foreign suppliers of electronic services, offshore brokerage, and other services to Korean recipients must complete a simplified foreign VAT registration regardless of turnover, and are required to appoint a domestic tax representative for filing and payment purposes.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating applies to exported goods and to services supplied to non-residents or foreign enterprises that are deemed performed and consumed outside Korea. For offshore securities brokerage (ISIC 6611), zero-rating is available where the brokerage, dealing, or intermediary service is rendered to a foreign counterparty (non-resident individual, foreign corporation, or foreign trust) and the underlying transaction is executed on an offshore market or otherwise used entirely abroad. Additional zero-rating covers services directly related to exported goods (e.g., brokerage of commodities or export-linked securities) and services supplied to foreign persons that are physically or technically performed overseas. The taxpayer must hold the requisite FSC license, maintain transaction-level records identifying the foreign counterparty and the offshore nature of the service, and file the VAT return with the zero-rated supply schedule (zero-rating basis code) through HomeTax. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated supplies is fully creditable, and excess credits are refunded promptly, typically within 30 days of filing.

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice, jeon-ja-se-geum-gye-san-seo) issuance and real-time transmission to the NTS HomeTax platform for all VAT-registered businesses. E-tax invoices must be issued by the end of the day of supply and transmitted to the NTS in real time via approved software (ASSP), direct API integration, or the HomeTax web portal; each invoice is authenticated with a unique NTS-assigned transaction number and encrypted digital signature. The mandatory cash receipt system (hyeon-geum-yeong-su-jeung) applies to all B2C cash transactions regardless of amount. Non-compliance penalties: additional tax of 2% of the supply value for failure to issue an e-tax invoice, 1% of the supply value for late or erroneous transmission, and 0.5% for failure to issue cash receipts, together with potential criminal sanctions for deliberate evasion. There is no physical digital stamping regime; the NTS electronic clearance platform serves as the central real-time fiscal control and matching mechanism.

      
Claims of export zero-rating are subject to stringent NTS audit scrutiny through cross-matching of e-tax invoices, customs declarations, and foreign exchange settlement records. Under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, a fraudulent or incorrect zero-rating claim is re-assessed at 10% VAT together with additional taxes: 10% of the under-reported amount for negligent under-declaration, and up to 40% of the evaded amount for fraudulent or dishonest returns. Retroactive clawback is permitted for five years from the filing due date, extended to ten years for fraudulent or aggravated evasion. Criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carry imprisonment of up to five years or fines of up to three times the tax evaded, plus public disclosure of the offender's identity on the NTS website. Failure to repatriate foreign currency proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline violates the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and may trigger administrative fines, forfeiture of zero-rating eligibility for the transaction, and referral to the FSC for securities broker license sanctions, potentially including revocation of the financial investment business license.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible for current account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. South Korea maintains a liberalized capital account with a post-audit reporting regime rather than a prior-approval regime: inward and outward direct investment, portfolio investment, and profit repatriation do not require individual central bank approval, but they must be declared or reported through a designated foreign exchange bank and FEIS within the prescribed window. The KRW is not a fully deliverable international settlement currency outside Korea; non-residents are generally prohibited from holding on-demand KRW accounts onshore except through a licensed foreign exchange bank, so all treasury operations must be cleared through domestic licensed banks. Profit remittance hurdles are low but not frictionless: dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders require (i) a withholding tax certificate evidencing the statutory 20 percent domestic withholding (or a lower rate under an applicable tax treaty, subject to the treaty claim procedure), (ii) an auditor-confirmed board and shareholder distribution resolution, and (iii) a FEIS advance report on the outward remittance. An offshore securities brokerage must also demonstrate that remitted funds represent audited distributable profits and are not tainted by unlicensed retail solicitation of Korean residents or by commingled client money held in violation of the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act (FSCMA).

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
The subject entity derives revenue from cross-border securities brokerage commissions; under the FETA, such servicing receipts are classified as invisible trade export proceeds. The statutory collection period for service export proceeds is 90 days from the date the service is fully performed or the date the receivable becomes legally due, whichever is earlier, unless the designated foreign exchange bank grants an extension for justifiable settlement delays. There is no mandatory conversion or surrender requirement: the entity may retain collected proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account with a licensed bank or convert them into KRW at the free market rate, provided the receipt is declared to FEIS. However, for a securities brokerage, client funds are not export proceeds: they constitute segregated customer assets and must be maintained in a trust account under FSCMA Article 44, kept strictly separate from the firm's proprietary commission earnings. Only earned commissions, fees, and rebates are subject to the 90-day export receipt declaration; the client corpus must never be netted against the firm's export proceeds for FEIS purposes.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) is the central monetary authority and custodian of foreign exchange reserves, operating in tandem with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal administrator of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). The Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) is the statutory electronic clearinghouse for all cross-border remittances and is jointly operated by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) with delegated oversight from BOK and the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS). For a licensed securities brokerage entity (ISIC 6611), every cross-border capital movement, segregated client wire, foreign currency financing, or treasury conversion must be executed through a designated foreign exchange bank licensed under the FETA and reported into FEIS under the applicable statutory transaction purpose code.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs and non-bank cross-border payment providers in the Korean treasury stack. Under the FETA and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank institution may not clear KRW, maintain onshore settlement accounts, or perform foreign exchange business in South Korea without an FSS-approved electronic financial business registration and foreign exchange bank designation. Offshore EMIs such as digital money transmission platforms can serve only as supplementary collection rails for non-KRW receivables from offshore clients in the United States or Europe; they cannot act as the principal clearing rail for inbound capital injection, client asset segregation, statutory FEIS reporting, or the firm's KRW treasury. Legal constraints are material: unlicensed foreign exchange business is an offense under FETA, the FSCMA Article 44 asset segregation requirement cannot be satisfied through an unlicensed intermediary, and the FATF Travel Rule and the Korean AML supervisory framework require the receiving bank to verify the originator of each client wire, a standard that offshore EMI white-label rails frequently fail to evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All equity capital injection must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's own account to the entity's capital escrow account at a designated foreign exchange bank. The bank files the foreign direct investment (FDI) notification in FEIS under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; the statutory transaction purpose code must reflect direct investment equity participation by way of capital subscription rather than a loan or service settlement. The remittance must carry the company name, the shareholder's tax identification number, the share subscription agreement reference, and the beneficiary account designation of Capital Escrow Account. Required verification before the account is credited as paid-in capital: (i) the FEIS FDI notification receipt number; (ii) a certified share subscription resolution; (iii) payment by a single identified shareholder in its own name, with third-party or aggregated payment prohibited; (iv) receipt in a freely convertible foreign currency such as USD, EUR, JPY, or GBP, with conversion to KRW at the market rate only after credit; and (v) an FSC-aligned declaration that the injected capital is not derived from offshore retail client funds. No central bank pre-approval is mandatory, but the payment must clear the AML source-of-funds review and the FEIS filing must be completed within 30 days of the wire date to avoid administrative penalties.Clearing rails for trade and operational settlements are as follows: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct third-party vendor payments, salary remittances, intercompany transfers, and client settlement payouts, with KRW legs cleared through the Bank of Korea's BOK-Wire system and USD and EUR legs cleared through correspondent nostro accounts; (ii) MT202 COV for back-to-back treasury transfers to correspondent banks; (iii) documentary commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 and ISBP 745 for any physical merchandise trade or asset acquisition; (iv) KFTC interbank rails for domestic KRW disbursements and rapid client refunds; and (v) FSCMA Article 44 compliant client payout instructions that draw exclusively from the segregated trust account, with each MT103 carrying the client's reference, the trade execution receipt, and the statutory client asset segregation code. All outbound remittances exceeding the equivalent of USD 5,000 must be reported to FEIS by the foreign exchange bank. Payments to non-residents must be cleared against withholding tax obligations on Korean-source income, and commission payments to offshore introducing brokers require either a withholding tax clearance certificate or a treaty-based exemption certificate. Merchant card settlement for Visa and Mastercard funding of client onboarding fees is cleared through the local acquiring bank and settled to the operational account on a T+1 basis through KFTC; card-derived funds must be reconciled against the FSS-mandated client asset ledger and must never be drawn directly against the segregated trust account.Deliverable FX forwards with a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank, used exclusively for hedging crystallized brokerage fees receivable and operational payables, limited to the underlying transaction amount and tenor, and reported to FEIS as a forward foreign exchange transaction.FX swaps (spot versus forward) between KRW and USD or EUR for managing temporary treasury liquidity mismatches, executed with an onshore bank and justified under the FETA trade-related derivative allowance.Cross-currency swaps for long-term hedging of the foreign currency equity injection or international acquisition financing, permitted onshore with a bank counterparty subject to FEIS declaration.Premium-purchased currency options on a delivery or non-delivery basis with a domestic bank, exercised strictly against the securities brokerage's operational foreign exchange exposure; zero-target-cost and barrier structures require definitive FSS interpretive guidance because of their potential speculative classification.Non-deliverable forwards in offshore markets are permissible only in narrow exceptions: a domestic financial investment business entity may transact an NDF with a non-resident solely through the intermediation of a local licensed bank to hedge the foreign exchange risk of a legally permitted underlying securities portfolio position, with each contract reported in FEIS and cash-settled in KRW at maturity. Speculative or naked NDF positions are prohibited, and the hedge must terminate once the underlying exposure extinguishes.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs and non-bank cross-border payment providers in the Korean treasury stack. Under the FETA and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank institution may not clear KRW, maintain onshore settlement accounts, or perform foreign exchange business in South Korea without an FSS-approved electronic financial business registration and foreign exchange bank designation. Offshore EMIs such as digital money transmission platforms can serve only as supplementary collection rails for non-KRW receivables from offshore clients in the United States or Europe; they cannot act as the principal clearing rail for inbound capital injection, client asset segregation, statutory FEIS reporting, or the firm's KRW treasury. Legal constraints are material: unlicensed foreign exchange business is an offense under FETA, the FSCMA Article 44 asset segregation requirement cannot be satisfied through an unlicensed intermediary, and the FATF Travel Rule and the Korean AML supervisory framework require the receiving bank to verify the originator of each client wire, a standard that offshore EMI white-label rails frequently fail to evidence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
9% base rate on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% up to KRW 20 billion; 21% up to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion (effective for fiscal years beginning on or after January 1, 2023); the global headline rate is 24% when surtax on excess income applies

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
15% (plus 1.65% local income tax surtax, making a combined effective rate of 16.5%) on dividends paid by a Korean resident corporation to a non-resident; for a foreign corporation's Korean branch, branch profit remittance tax is 20% (plus 2.2% local surtax, combined 22.2%) on non-taxable branch profits remitted abroad

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation applies to transactions with foreign related parties exceeding KRW 50 billion in total transaction amount per business year for the combined Master File and Local File requirement; an aggregate unrelated party documentation threshold of KRW 100 billion applies for the consolidated master file obligation under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; a Schedule (Statement of International Transactions between Related Parties) is mandatory for all companies with related-party cross-border transactions regardless of amount, but the full Local File and Master File must be prepared and submitted upon NTS request when the domestic entity's related party transaction amount exceeds KRW 50 billion or when one of the parties is a foreign related party and the total amount of all international related-party transactions exceeds KRW 50 billion (average of the business year and the immediately preceding business year; per related party threshold is KRW 5 billion for separate local file with penalty relief if no unreasonable pricing)

      
Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a statutory Economic Substance Regulations framework in the same manner as UAE, Bermuda, or Cayman; instead, substance is tested through the permanent establishment definition, the general anti-avoidance rule under the Framework Act on Taxes Section 2-2, and controlled foreign company rules under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs; a Chusik Hoesa carrying on offshore securities brokerage without Korean staff, Korean office equipment, or Korean decision-making functions does not create a Korean PE unless a dependent agent in Korea habitually concludes contracts; such an entity must have real business substance in the foreign licensing jurisdiction and comply with the foreign jurisdiction's own economic substance or management requirements; Korean tax authorities may recharacterize a shell intermediary as a conduit and deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test and the substance-over-form doctrine

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Jose-Talpi)
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine up to 5 times the evaded tax; corporate entity fined up to 5 times the evaded tax; additional civil liability includes the evaded tax amount plus 100% of related wrongful refunds and all corresponding delinquency and negligence penalties

        
Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who knowingly participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and surcharges; they face criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from board service; in aggravated cases involving cross-border concealment, sentences may be extended to life imprisonment where the evaded amount is extreme; Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil upon undercapitalization, intermingling of funds, or fraud in promoting the corporation

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation
        
Statutory sanction: NTS may adjust taxable income using the arm's length price and impose an underreporting penalty of 10% to 40% of the additional assessed tax; an additional 0.022% per day delinquency surcharge applies; failure to submit the Schedule or required TP documents triggers a penalty of up to KRW 300 million; the tax authority may impose a comprehensive adjustment with interest accrued from the original filing due date

        
Director personal liability: Directors who signed false transfer pricing statements can be held personally liable under the Framework Act on Taxes as a secondary taxpayer where assets were fraudulently transferred; participation in cross-border mark-up schemes may trigger criminal proceedings and travel restrictions during tax inspection

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Substance Deficiency
        
Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the tax residence claim and recharacterization as either a Korean resident entity or a Korean PE; all Korean-source income is then subject to corporate income tax plus 22.2% withholding on deemed dividend liquidation; the GAAR permits a 40% evasion penalty on tax resulting from the abusive arrangement and issuance of a collection order against bank accounts; a foreign corporation may lose all treaty benefits under the principal purpose test

        
Director personal liability: Directors of a shell or conduit entity may be liable jointly for the unpaid corporate tax to the extent they received disguised distributions; upon criminal conviction for the use of a paper company for fraud or evasion, directors face imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to 5 times the economic benefit; travel bans and extradition cooperation in cross-border cases apply

      

      
      

        Unlicensed Securities Brokerage and AML Breach
        
Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial investment brokerage is punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years or a fine of up to KRW 200 million (equity securities); CFDs and derivative brokerage carry penalties of up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 200 million; the Financial Services Commission may order business suspension, revocation of license, and disgorge profits; AML failures under the Financial Transaction Reports Act subject the entity to a fine of up to KRW 100 million per violation and criminal penalty of up to 3 years imprisonment; parallel NTS penalties for proceeds generated without license include 100% civil penalty on illegal income

        
Director personal liability: Directors can be named in personal criminal indictments and face imprisonment, fines, and travel bans; the Financial Services Commission may impose an administrative fine on directors personally up to KRW 500 million and prohibit any financial business employment for up to 5 years; corporate veil piercing occurs upon use of the corporation as a sham for illegal brokerage

      

      
      

        Failure to Remit Dividend WHT and Branch Profit Tax
        
Statutory sanction: Withholding agent is personally liable to the NTS for the unwithheld amount plus a 10% negligence penalty and 0.022% daily delinquency charge; the foreign recipient retains a secondary liability; late filing of the withholding return after the statutory due date incurs a 10% non-filing penalty and daily additive charges; corrective assessments may be made within 5 years (10 years in cases of intentional concealment)

        
Director personal liability: Directors who authorized a distribution without the required gross-up and withholding may be jointly liable for the unremitted withheld tax as a secondary taxpayer under Article 39 of the Framework Act on Taxes; where the entity's assets are insufficient and the failure was intentional, directors face criminal penalty for breach of the obligations of the withholding agent and personal travel bans in pending criminal tax proceedings

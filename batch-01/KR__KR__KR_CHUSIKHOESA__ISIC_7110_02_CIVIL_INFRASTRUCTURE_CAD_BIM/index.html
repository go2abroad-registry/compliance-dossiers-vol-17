Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. Each issued share must have a par value of at least KRW 100, and the total paid-in capital is fixed in the Articles of Incorporation. Subscription proceeds must be fully paid before registration and placed into a designated bank subscription deposit account that is restricted or blocked until the registration certificate is issued. There is no central government escrow account; the bank certificate of subscription payment is submitted to the Registry as proof of capital payment.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no civil defense fire safety permit, structural load inspection, or industrial workplace safety approval is required for pure office-based digital engineering services. The landlord must maintain the building's fire evacuation certificates and structural safety obligations, and the entity must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act if it hires employees in Korea, including mandatory accident insurance registration.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa or directly undertaking taxable business activities in South Korea, the entity or its statutory tax representative must first obtain a business registration certificate (Business Registration Number) from the competent district tax office or via the NTS Home Tax Service web portal. The application must be filed using the official business registration application form together with the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, certificates of representative appointment, the tax representative's power of attorney, proof of local place of business (or representative designation agreement), and any organizational documents requested by the NTS. The NTS will issue a ten-digit or twelve-digit registration number which simultaneously functions as the VAT identification number for indirect tax purposes. The tax representative becomes jointly responsible for filing, keeping records, and cooperating with NTS audits during the period of representation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold. Any entrepreneur, including a Chusik Hoesa engaged in taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea, is under a legal duty to register for VAT through the NTS. A simplified VAT regime exists for certain small-scale individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but this does not constitute a registration exemption and does not apply to ordinary corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, zero-rating at 0% applies to exported goods where the goods leave Korea under an export customs declaration, and to qualifying services rendered to non-residents, performed wholly or partly in Korea and supplied for the purpose of facilitating export or providing offshore engineering services such as CAD/BIM structural drafting for non-resident clients. The taxpayer must retain documentary evidence proving the export nature of the transaction, the offshore status of the recipient, delivery or performance abroad, and settlement in foreign currency through a legitimate foreign exchange transaction. Zero-rating is a statutory entitlement once the legal conditions are satisfied; no prior NTS approval is required but substantiation on audit is mandatory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry / Shipping Bill) lodged with the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate / Foreign Exchange Remittance Confirmation demonstrating inward receipt of export proceeds
  • Service Contract or Purchase Order clearly identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore nature of the CAD/BIM drafting services
  • Other bank or SWIFT records proving foreign currency remittance from the overseas buyer

Electronic tax invoices under the NTS Home Tax Service are mandatory for B2B taxable supplies; the electronic tax invoice must contain the standardized invoice ID, digital signature of the issuer, and must be transmitted via NTS-approved EDI/API channels by the last day of the month in which the supply is made. The NTS maintains a centralized digital validation and receipt system for real-time matching between issuers and recipients; cash receipts are also mandatory for certain consumer B2C sales. Businesses meeting statutory turnover thresholds must issue all tax invoices electronically; any manual paper invoices remain allowed only in narrowly prescribed exceptional cases.

Where zero-rating is claimed without supporting export declarations, without valid foreign exchange settlement, or where export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period (unless a lawful extension is obtained from the relevant authorities), the NTS will reverse the zero-rated treatment and assess output VAT at 10%, together with statutory surcharges and interest. Cases of fraud, false invoicing, or wilful non-substantiation may attract penalties up to 40% of the under-declared tax and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Customs and foreign exchange authorities may additionally impose sanctions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and Customs Act for non-repatriation of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly liberalized current-account and capital-account regime under FETA, but Korean won (KRW) is not a globally freely convertible currency outside the onshore banking network. Capital-account transactions require mandatory reporting to a designated Foreign Exchange Bank and, where FETA thresholds are exceeded, prior or ex-post filing with MOEF. Dividends and profits can be remitted abroad only after corporate tax obligations are settled and after the Korean bank verifies the supporting board resolution, audited financial statements, and the standard Foreign Exchange Information System purpose code for profit remittance. Current-account service exports, including engineering and drafting services, are not subject to prior approval, but all receipts and payments must flow through a Korean foreign exchange bank using proper underlying contract documentation. Surrender rules are lenient: export proceeds must be collected within one year from the export declaration date and must be brought into a domestic foreign-currency account or converted at a bank at the prevailing market rate, but there is no mandatory statutory conversion percentage. The KRW market remains protected by position limits and genuine-exposure requirements for hedging; speculative non-underlying derivative activity is tightly restricted. Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) transactions are permitted only through authorized domestic financial institutions and subject to Financial Supervisory Service position limits, not as a free offshore KRW hedging vehicle. Overall, the system is administratively open but documentary intense: a compliant Korean Chusik Hoesa must maintain real contracts, authentic invoices, and accurate Foreign Exchange Information System codes for every cross-border flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and delivered to a Korean foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of export declaration; an extension may be granted by the Minister of Economy and Finance or the designated bank under FETA. No fixed percentage of KRW surrender is imposed on industrial outsourcing receipts; the full foreign-currency amount may be credited to a Foreign Currency Demand Deposit Account at a Tier-1 Korean bank. Any conversion of such foreign exchange into KRW must occur through a domestic foreign exchange bank at market rates and must be supported by the underlying service agreement and standard collection document template.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); Korea Customs Service supervises export settlement mechanics and the Foreign Exchange Information System supported by BOK for transaction reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, and Medium only as a supplementary cross-border collection channel. Offshore fintech EMIs, including Wise, are not licensed Korean banks and cannot legally open an onshore KRW demand deposit account or provide a domestic Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute (KFTC) clearing number. Under South Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only licensed electronic financial institutions and banks under FSS registration may provide payment or fund-transfer services in KRW. Wise therefore acts as a money-transfer intermediary routing through a licensed partner bank and cannot serve as the statutory account held by the Korean Chusik Hoesa for capital contribution, payroll, tax payments, or trade settlement. The entity should maintain all statutory flows through Tier-1 Korean bank accounts; Wise may be used for small collection of overseas milestone fees only if the Korean bank is fully informed and the underlying FX reporting is completed.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: Low as a primary treasury rail, and Medium only as a supplementary cross-border collection channel. Offshore fintech EMIs, including Wise, are not licensed Korean banks and cannot legally open an onshore KRW demand deposit account or provide a domestic Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute (KFTC) clearing number. Under South Korea's Electronic Financial Transactions Act, only licensed electronic financial institutions and banks under FSS registration may provide payment or fund-transfer services in KRW. Wise therefore acts as a money-transfer intermediary routing through a licensed partner bank and cannot serve as the statutory account held by the Korean Chusik Hoesa for capital contribution, payroll, tax payments, or trade settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when the annual value of cross-border intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceeds KRW 5 billion. Master File documentation is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion and the Korean entity has cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 50 billion. A simplified foreign-related transaction disclosure is generally submitted when such transactions exceed KRW 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to Common Reporting Standard offshore hubs. Economic substance is tested through the general anti-avoidance rule, controlled foreign corporation rules, beneficial-ownership requirements in treaty cases, and the permanent establishment profit-attribution rule. A local vehicle that lacks employees, premises, business function, or decision-making authority in the CAD/BIM engineering outsourcing chain will be treated as a conduit, disregarded, and its profits reattributed to the economically active entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / False Filing

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to 20 percent of the unpaid tax, rising to 40 percent for concealment or false invoices; daily delinquency charge of 0.025 percent; criminal liability of up to five years' imprisonment or a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly facilitate the evasion are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and collection costs; they can be subjected to asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: The shell will be disregarded under the substance-over-form doctrine; intercompany deductions will be disallowed, taxable income reconstructed on an arm's length basis, treaty withholding relief denied, and undistributed offshore income may be taxed under CFC rules.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced where the entity is a mere facade or instrument of fraud; directors face civil restitution and joint tax liability, plus court-imposed travel bans where collection is at risk.
Anti-Money Laundering / Undeclared Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to issue electronic tax invoices attracts a surcharge of 2 percent of the supply value; violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act can result in administrative fines, seizure of undeclared funds, and criminal referral; banks may freeze the underlying accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or authorize such payment structures are personally liable for resulting penalties; they can be charged criminally, have assets confiscated, and be prohibited from leaving Korea pending investigation and enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The NTS may recharacterize the transaction and adjust taxable income using its chosen transfer pricing method; a 20 percent additional tax applies to the understated tax, rising to 40 percent in cases of gross negligence or concealment, together with daily delinquency charges.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for filing and record retention may be named as joint assessment targets in willful non-compliance cases; repeated violations constitute an aggravating criminal factor and may trigger executive travel restrictions and prosecution.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an ISIC 5912 post-production, automated subtitle transcription and AI voice-cloning studio, the National Tax Service and Korean banks treat a pure commercial mailbox or virtual mail-forwarding address as insufficient for a Business Registration Certificate and corporate bank account. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Under the Commercial Act, there is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the total subscription price of shares must be fully paid in at the time of incorporation, and the paid-in amount must be deposited into a bank account in the name of the company (or a designated escrow account) until the incorporation registration is completed. The deposit must be released only after the registration is effective.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Korean domestic place of business for a Chusik Hoesa must apply to the competent district tax office for a Business Registration Number (BRN), which also functions as the Korean VAT registration number. The procedure is: (1) prepare the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of association, board resolution authorizing the Korean established place of business, and a certified Korean translation if the original is in a foreign language; (2) appoint a tax representative who has an address in Korea and is authorized to accept tax notices and filings; (3) submit the application for business registration together with evidence of the domestic place of business (registered lease or ownership document), identity documents for the tax representative and director, and any relevant foreign business license; (4) file the application online through HomeTax using the entity's access credentials or file in person at the NTS district office that has jurisdiction over the Korean place of business. NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number upon verification. A foreign entity without a Korean domestic place of business that makes only off-shore supplies is not automatically obliged to register, but it may need a simplified registration and tax representative if it becomes liable for Korean VAT through a taxable import, intra-Korean supply, or claims a Korean VAT refund.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide check-the-box elections or comparable entity classification elections. A Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a separate corporation at entity level. Hybrid mismatch rules are applied in accordance with Korean tax law and OECD BEPS recommendations, but they do not create an elective classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based threshold exists for corporations. VAT registration is mandatory from the commencement of any taxable business activity in Korea. For sole proprietors, a simplified VAT taxpayer status applies where annual gross supplies are below KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory for every supplier making taxable supplies in Korea.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Korea and cleared by Korea Customs Service, and to services supplied to non-residents where the place of supply is deemed to be outside Korea. For a digital post-production studio in scope ISIC 5912 offering automated subtitle transcription and AI voice cloning, zero-rating is available where the recipient is a foreign business or non-resident individual, the service is actually consumed or used outside Korea, and the supply is supported by a foreign currency contract, proof of delivery of the digital output to the foreign buyer, and evidence of receipt of foreign currency from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by Korea Customs Service confirming physical export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the exporter and foreign consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with full goods description
  • Service agreement or order confirmation with the foreign non-resident buyer
  • Foreign exchange settlement confirmation certificate or inward remittance receipt from an authorized Korean bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Proof of the foreign buyer's non-resident status such as foreign business registration certificate or overseas corporate identity document

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all VAT invoices issued by corporations for B2B supplies. Taxable persons must transmit e-invoice data to NTS through the HomeTax system, approved EDI service providers, or NTS-compliant XML APIs. The NTS validates and assigns an electronic management number to each e-invoice in near real time for anti-fraud control, and the issuer must retain the buyer's VAT identification number or foreign business evidence, supply description, amount, and applicable VAT rate. For zero-rated export supplies, the e-invoice must clearly show the zero rate and the supporting customs or foreign transaction reference.

NTS and Korea Customs Service may review export documentation at any time during the statutory assessment period. If zero-rating is not substantiated, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not received within the prescribed 180-day repatriation window, the supply is recharacterized as taxable and the 10% VAT is assessed retrospectively. NTS may add understatement penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, impose delayed payment interest, and refer cases involving forged customs declarations, fabricated foreign exchange certificates, or false tax invoices to criminal enforcement for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is freely convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, cross-border services, royalties, licensing fees, and ordinary profit remittances. These current account settlements are generally allowed without prior approval, provided the transaction is routed through an authorized foreign exchange bank and reported or supported by the appropriate underlying documentation. Capital account transactions, however, remain subject to a more regulated notification-and-reporting framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. For a Chusik Hoesa receiving foreign shareholder equity capital, the inward remittance must be classified as a foreign direct investment, reported to the designated bank, and evidenced by a foreign investment report certificate. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted after corporate tax clearance and verification of distributable reserves; a foreign exchange bank remittance application must be filed, and dividend withholding tax must be paid. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; Korean exporters are allowed to retain foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign currency accounts and convert to won only when needed. The practical constraints for a post-production digital content studio are moderate: routine service export invoices and AI licensing fees can be received freely through domestic banking channels, but every inward receipt must be supportable by a contract, digital delivery evidence, tax invoice, and the correct transaction code to avoid customs or tax authority recharacterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected through an authorized foreign exchange bank within six (6) months from the date of the export declaration; an extension of up to six (6) additional months may be requested and granted by the responsible bank for legitimate commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 0% of export foreign currency receipts are required to be surrendered to the Korean won, unless the exporter has an invoicing arrangement or a specific regulatory instruction to the contrary. Proceeds may be maintained in a foreign currency trade settlement account and converted through a forward contract or spot sale at the company's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe have limited standalone viability in South Korea. They are not licensed domestic banks or authorized foreign exchange banks and cannot open resident KRW transaction accounts or provide domestic clearing connectivity for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, cross-border payments into or out of Korea must be settled through authorized foreign exchange banks, and any unlicensed money transmission service is legally restricted. Stripe and Wise may nevertheless be used as downstream collection or payout rails only if they route funds through a licensed domestic partner bank and comply with Korean payment and FX reporting rules. Their practical viability is Low-to-Medium for inbound client receivables, but they must not replace a local Tier-1 bank account for corporate treasury, statutory capital verification, profit remittance, or tax compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity capital should be routed via an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank using the FDI purpose code for capital subscription. The foreign investor first files the foreign investment report with MOEF through the head office or branch of the designated domestic bank, confirms the investment amount, and obtains a Foreign Investment Report Certificate. The foreign shareholder then initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its own banking relationship, clearly referencing the certificate number, the Korean Chusik Hoesa's bank account number, and the corresponding transaction purpose code. After receipt, the bank credits a temporary capital escrow or foreign currency account, the company's statutory auditor or designated CPA performs a capital verification and files it with the court registry, and the funds may then be converted to KRW or transferred to the operating account upon submission of the capital verification certificate, an account conversion order, and proof of board authorization.Outbound disbursements for trade and service settlements should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 payments for cross-border service fees, royalties, subcontractor payments, and post-production licensing costs. For transactions involving physical deliverables, a commercial letter of credit may be used, or documentary collections when the contract requires presentation of shipping documents. All outbound trade remittances must be accompanied by a bank remittance application, the underlying contract, tax invoices, an appropriate foreign exchange purpose code, and proof of withholding tax where applicable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe have limited standalone viability in South Korea.

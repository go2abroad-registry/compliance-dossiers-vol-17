Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The subscribers must, however, pay in full the aggregate par value of the subscribed shares before the incorporation registration is completed. Each issued share must have a par value of at least KRW 100. The paid-in capital is not subject to an escrow or blocked-account regime; provided, however, the funds must be deposited into a designated capital payment bank account in the Republic of Korea and must remain in that account until the legal existence of the Company is registered and the restriction on withdrawals under Articles 295 and 302 of the Commercial Act is lifted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold: every business operator supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register, including foreign electronic service suppliers, from the first taxable supply. Small traders may be eligible for simplified VAT taxation only after meeting qualitative criteria, but registration remains compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated upon evidenced customs export. Qualified services, including digital game content, in-app virtual currency and social casino software published under ISIC 5820, supplied to customers resident outside Korea and actually used or performed outside Korea may be zero-rated through the foreign exchange earning arrangement. For the present app-store distribution model, eligibility requires non-resident end-user location evidence and foreign currency receipts remitted into Korea, with the economic benefit enjoyed outside Korea.

  • Customs export declaration (export Bill of Entry filed with Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS) - for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing export from Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, or equivalent bank receipt, confirming collection of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Digital-service substantiation bundle for the slots and social-casino supply: Apple IAP and Google Play Billing disbursement reports, storefront country data, non-resident user sale records, and service agreements evidencing offshore performance and consumption

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) for all B2B taxable supplies, issued and transmitted to the NTS e-tax invoice system through the HomeTax portal, bureau-approved EDI/ERP API integrations, or certified digital platforms. The invoice must include the Business Registration Number, supply description, supply date and 10% VAT. Transmission must take place by the statutory deadline; no separate real-time fiscal clearance stamp is issued, but the NTS performs continuous cross-matching and anti-fraud verification. Late, missing or false e-invoices are subject to penalty surcharges under the VAT Act.

If the NTS establishes that export documentation is absent, forged or untimely, or that the foreign-sourced consideration was not repatriated within 180 days or an approved extension, the supply is recharacterized as domestic taxable; the 10% VAT is assessed with statutory underpayment interest (currently 1.2% per month) and late-return penalties. Intentional or grossly negligent false zero-rating claims attract additional tax penalties and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including penal fines up to ten times the tax understated and imprisonment, and the NTS may revoke the taxpayer's business registration and e-tax invoice entitlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

KRW is largely convertible on the current account: royalties, service fees, wages, and profit remittances may be executed without a prior license, but each payment must be supported by an underlying contract, an invoice, a valid purpose code, and a withholding certificate where required. Capital account operations, including inbound equity contributions, share subscriptions, foreign-currency borrowings, and loans, are subject to the report-and-confirm regime of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; they are not prohibited, but they must be channelled through a licensed foreign exchange bank. There is no general forced conversion of foreign currency to KRW; companies may maintain foreign-currency deposit accounts. In practice, non-resident shareholders can remit dividends after audited financial statements are approved, corporate tax is filed, and 20-22% withholding or treaty relief is demonstrated. The principal hurdle is documentary, not exchange-control: banks require the foreign investment registration, board minutes, tax clearance, and a reconciliation of app-store revenue before releasing large outward remittances. Macro-prudential limits mainly bind banks' FX derivative positions, not ordinary commercial repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory rule is a 100% repatriation and settlement requirement: all export-related foreign-currency proceeds from the digital gaming service must be received through a Korean foreign exchange bank and settled within six months of the relevant transaction date or export declaration date, unless the bank agrees to an extension for justifiable trade reasons. The mandatory KRW conversion ratio is 0% because the export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account. However, the entire receipt must enter the Korean banking system and be recorded in the Foreign Exchange Information System. For App Store and Google Play receipts, the Korean publisher must keep the app store settlement statements, Apple/Google payout advices, and bank MT103 confirmation to evidence the export of services and to satisfy the bank's annual KYC review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the central bank of the Republic of Korea) as the monetary and FX infrastructure authority, operating jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the overseer of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the Financial Supervisory Service as the bank supervisory body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMI accounts do not constitute licensed foreign exchange banks in South Korea and cannot lawfully open a settlement account or execute foreign exchange transactions for a Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa. Korean fintech firms holding an electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act may offer payment gateways and e-money settlement, but they may not accept foreign-investment capital, issue the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, or provide foreign exchange banking services under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Corporate treasury must therefore be anchored in one of the licensed domestic tier-1 banks, with fintech payment processors used only as a supplementary collection layer for in-app store settlements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national Corporate Tax before local income tax surcharge; 26.4% combined top marginal rate after adding 10% local income tax surcharge. Progressive bands: 9% below KRW 200,000,000; 19% from KRW 200,000,000 to KRW 20,000,000,000; 21% from KRW 20,000,000,000 to KRW 300,000,000,000; 24% above KRW 300,000,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on non-resident dividends plus 2% local income tax surcharge, aggregating to 22% in the absence of a treaty; many South Korean treaties reduce the rate to 10% or 15% for substantial corporate shareholders and to 5% or 0% for pension funds or public-interest entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 5, the tax authority may redetermine income at arm’s length; Article 9 imposes up to KRW 50,000,000 in monetary penalties for non-submission of transfer pricing documentation; additional tax of 20% or 40% for fraud applies to the understated tax; Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act provides imprisonment up to 2 years, increased to 5 years where the evaded amount exceeds KRW 300,000,000.

Director personal liability: A director who approved false transfer pricing returns may be jointly liable with the corporation where corporate assets are insufficient for unpaid taxes. The director can be added as a secondary taxpayer, placed on the public delinquent list, subjected to travel blacklisting, and convicted of tax evasion with imprisonment and fines of up to twice the evaded amount; civil restitution can extend to personal bank accounts and property transfers made to shield assets.
Withholding Tax Evasion and False DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act provisions impose withholding obligations on the payer; failure to withhold and remit triggers collection from the payer plus additional tax of 10% and daily overdue interest. A false DTA claim is treated as fraudulent underreporting with a 40% penalty, and tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine not exceeding two times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The director as withholding officer is personally liable for withheld but unremitted taxes if the corporation cannot pay. Directors face asset seizure, housing and vehicle liens, public blacklisting, and travel bans. In egregious cases, Korean courts can pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable where the Chusik Hoesa is a mere alter-ego or conduit of the foreign shareholder.

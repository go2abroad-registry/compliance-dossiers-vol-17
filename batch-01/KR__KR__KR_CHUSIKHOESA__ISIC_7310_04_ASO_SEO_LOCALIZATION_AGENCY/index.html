Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The full amount of subscribed capital must be deposited into a domestic bank account opened in the name of the company before registration, and the bank certificate of deposit must be submitted with the incorporation registration application. There is no blocked escrow account requirement under Korean law; however, the deposited capital may not be withdrawn until the registration of incorporation is completed. If non-resident founders subscribe shares and satisfy the Foreign Investment Promotion Act threshold, the foreign investment must be reported to KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank before or immediately after the share subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a pure office-based digital agency; no Civil Defense fire permit or industrial workplace safety approval is required for app store optimization, search engine optimization, or localization consulting. The lessor must supply a valid fire-safety completion certificate for the office building under the Fire Prevention, Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Control Act, and the company must comply with general occupational safety obligations under the Occupational Safety and Health Act concerning electrical safety, emergency exits, and ergonomic office workstations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Chusik Hoesa is automatically a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporation tax on worldwide income under the Corporation Tax Act. There is no check-the-box election in Korean tax law. Korean consolidated tax filing with 100 percent owned domestic subsidiaries is available under the conditions prescribed in the Corporation Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch countermeasures apply under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, but no elective entity classification is available for the Chusik Hoesa structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold. VAT registration is mandatory for all business operators from the commencement of taxable supplies. A simplified taxable person regime exists for small individual enterprises under KRW 80,000,000 of annual taxable turnover, but a Chusik Hoesa stock corporation is outside that simplified regime and is required to register for VAT without any minimum revenue threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated when the goods are actually exported from Korea under a valid customs export declaration. Exported services, including international supply of services, app growth services, ASO, SEO localization, and other foreign currency earning services provided to non-residents, qualify for the 0 percent rate if the Korean service supplier retains contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is not a Korean resident, the service is supplied for use outside Korea, the service is actually rendered to the foreign party, and the foreign currency consideration is repatriated into Korea through a Korean foreign exchange bank or foreign exchange payment account.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Declaration Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and signed supply or service agreement
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or equivalent proof of physical export dispatch for goods
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or bank purchase confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank showing receipt or inward remittance of foreign currency
  • Service completion report and payment evidence evidencing that the overseas recipient directly paid the Korean company for exported services

Mandatory electronic tax invoice issuance and HomeTax transmission. The taxpayer must transmit the electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) to NTS through HomeTax, generally by the day following the invoice date. API and automated machine-to-machine filing is compulsory for larger taxpayers and available to all through the NTS HomeTax integration interface. Korea does not operate pre-issuance central real-time fiscal clearance or an anti-fraud physical stamping system; instead, NTS receives the e-tax invoice data in near real time, automatically validates and reconciles purchaser and supplier ledgers, blocks mismatched business registration numbers, and subjects late, missing, or false invoices to penalty assessment. The electronic tax invoice must contain both parties business registration numbers, supply date, item description, supply value, and VAT amount.

NTS and Korea Customs Service may retrospectively deny the zero rate when the required export documentation and foreign exchange receipt evidence are not timely obtained, retained, and produced during audit. If zero-rating is disallowed, the VAT at the standard 10 percent rate is assessed on the supply value plus statutory interest for unfaithful returns, with additional tax penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes ranging broadly up to 40 percent for unjustified refunds or unfaithful submissions. Deliberately false export declarations or forged evidence may be criminally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the national tax collection period is extended to 10 years for fraudulent or willful omission cases. Failure to satisfy the foreign exchange repatriation requirement also triggers penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and is treated by NTS as conclusive evidence that the transaction did not qualify for zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a quasi-liberalised current-account regime with structural capital-account controls. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, routine service income, trade settlements, and dividends are legally convertible and remittable provided the transaction is routed through a resident Korean foreign-exchange bank and the required foreign-exchange reporting is completed. Korean won is moderately internationalised; current-account convertibility is effectively full, but capital-account transactions such as borrowing, equity investment, offshore deposits, and complex hedging instruments are subject to prudential licensing, transaction ceilings, and reporting limitations. Export receipts must be collected in foreign currency through a domestic foreign-exchange bank; there is no forced surrender to the central bank, but 100% of export proceeds must be brought into the country and credited to a domestic bank account within the statutory collection deadline. Profit repatriation by foreign shareholders of a Chusik Hoesa is permitted under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act once the foreign investment has been properly registered and the remittance is supported by audited accounts, tax clearance, dividend resolution, and bank confirmation. For an SEO/ASO agency, cross-border fee receipts are current-account earnings and do not require capital-account approval, but each SWIFT MT103 must carry a valid remittance purpose code to avoid document-level AML rejection. The Korean authorities maintain a strict documentary surveillance framework for both inbound and outbound settlement, with all foreign-exchange transactions reported electronically to the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received within one year from the export declaration acceptance date, extendable only with prior Bank of Korea or foreign-exchange bank approval. The full amount of service and goods export receipts must be credited to a domestic foreign-exchange bank account or converted into Korean won through a domestic bank. No mandatory conversion ratio to the central bank is imposed, but 100% of export proceeds are subject to the Korean foreign-exchange banking channel and must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, exercising delegated foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of the Republic of Korea, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service on trade settlement and reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-contraindicated as a primary treasury rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Wise B2B and Payoneer are licensed as money transfer or electronic money institutions in certain overseas jurisdictions, but they are not registered in South Korea as licensed foreign-exchange brokers under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and do not hold a domestic electronic financial transaction business authorisation from the Financial Services Commission. A Korean resident corporate entity may not lawfully execute inward capital registration, tax invoice settlement, or annual foreign-exchange statement filings through an unauthorised offshore digital EMI. Such platforms can be used only as supplementary payout channels for non-Korean vendor disbursements where no domestic bank account is required, but they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 bank for corporate treasury, FX conversion, trade settlement, or profit remittance. Any attempt to route core export service revenues through an offshore EMI would likely trigger Korean customs and central bank documentary compliance rejections and create material AML risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions from a foreign parent or investor into the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting corporate bank to a designated foreign-currency corporate account at a Korean Tier-1 bank. The remittance reference must include the foreign-investment registration number issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a clear capital contribution purpose code, and the board resolution approving the investment. The receiving domestic bank must complete the foreign-exchange transaction report with the Bank of Korea through the Foreign Exchange Information System and issue a foreign-exchange certificate confirming the inflow. For paid-in capital verification, the bank must match the received amount to the share subscription certificates, the company registry filing, and the executed share subscription agreement. No more than five business days should elapse between receipt and completion of the capital verification process. Any capital contribution exceeding the registered amount requires prior and post-formation modification of the corporate registry before the bank will release funds for operational use.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade and service disbursements is a secure SWIFT MT103 transfer with automatic document matching, bank-to-bank beneficiary validation, and proper remittance purpose codes. For recurring high-value ad-platform payments, the Korean treasury should utilise SWIFT gpi to provide real-time payment tracking. Commercial letters of credit are permissible only when the Chusik Hoesa acts as principal in the purchase of physical advertising media, software licences, or resale inventory that requires documentary trade terms; ordinary ASO/SEO service subscriptions are not appropriate for LC mechanics. Documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms may be used for channel partners that require the presentation of shipping, delivery, or acceptance documents before payment. All outbound disbursements to foreign vendors, app stores, advertising exchanges, and contractors must be backed by a signed invoice and an underlying agreement, with the local Korean won account used solely for conversion to USD or EUR through a permitted domestic bank transaction.Onshore plain-vanilla foreign-exchange forward contracts with a domestic Korean Tier-1 bank, supported by documentary underlying receivables or payables from the ASO/SEO service export activity.Cross-currency swaps that exactly hedge a long-term foreign-currency liability or multi-year advertising capacity commitment, subject to Bank of Korea transaction reporting and prudential documentation.Currency options structured by a domestic Korean foreign-exchange bank, limited to hedging existing foreign-currency cashflows and not permitted for speculative uncovered positions.Onshore over-the-counter KRW/USD swap contracts used strictly for liquidity conversion and settlement timing alignment with actual export receipts and vendor disbursements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-contraindicated as a primary treasury rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Wise B2B and Payoneer are licensed as money transfer or electronic money institutions in certain overseas jurisdictions, but they are not registered in South Korea as licensed foreign-exchange brokers under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and do not hold a domestic electronic financial transaction business authorisation from the Financial Services Commission. A Korean resident corporate entity may not lawfully execute inward capital registration, tax invoice settlement, or annual foreign-exchange statement filings through an unauthorised offshore digital EMI. Such platforms can be used only as supplementary payout channels for non-Korean vendor disbursements where no domestic bank account is required, but they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 bank for corporate treasury, FX conversion, trade settlement, or profit remittance. Any attempt to route core export service revenues through an offshore EMI would likely trigger Korean customs and central bank documentary compliance rejections and create material AML risk.

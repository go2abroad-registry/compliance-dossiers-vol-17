Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act of Korea does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, all subscribed shares must be fully paid at incorporation, and the subscription funds must be deposited into a blocked incorporation subscription account with a licensed Korean bank or securities company and must remain restricted from withdrawal until registration of incorporation is completed. This blocked deposit rule is derived from Article 295 of the Commercial Act. If a foreign investor subscribes and the investment amount is KRW 100,000,000 or more and the investor holds 10% or more of the shares, the transaction must be processed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act to qualify as foreign investment, with notification made to KOTRA Invest Korea or an authorized foreign exchange bank. The official registry cost is not a flat USD amount: registry fees are an ad valorem 0.4% registration tax on declared capital, plus 20% local education surtax, plus the court registry filing fee.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration threshold. Any person carrying on a taxable business in Korea must obtain VAT/business registration from business commencement irrespective of annual turnover. A simplified taxpayer status exists for small individual traders below the statutory turnover threshold, but it is not a registration exemption and is not applicable to a corporate Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the Korean customs territory qualify for VAT zero-rating when (a) the supply is made by a VAT-registered Korean seller whose registration number appears on the export declaration, (b) the seller has filed an export declaration through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS in its own name, (c) the goods are actually removed from Korea to a foreign destination and the foreign buyer receives control or possession, and (d) the sales contract, commercial invoice, payment terms, bill of lading quantities, grade (Copper Cathode Grade A), and foreign exchange repatriation records are internally consistent. A mere domestic transfer or change of ownership of an LME warehouse warrant without a Korean customs export does not qualify. Cross-border services are zero-rated only when the place of supply is outside Korea or the services are used wholly outside Korea by a foreign business or user; offshore digital services effectively used by Korean consumers remain subject to Korean VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, including the export clearance certificate and export customs number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (original or electronic equivalent) issued by the carrier showing the Korean VAT-registered exporter as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee, with port-to-port details from a Korean port to a non-Korean destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, contract or Letter of Credit, and LME warrant transfer documents for Grade A copper cathodes, matching the declared customs value, weight, marks, and lot numbers
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency in the exporter's Korean bank account

General VAT taxpayers are required to issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system or an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice service provider. The electronic tax invoice data is filed with the NTS electronically and is available to the NTS for automated cross-matching; no physical fiscal stamp or pre-invoice government clearance applies under the Korean VAT system. Export transactions are cleared separately through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS, and NTS cross-matches the e-tax invoice and the UNI-PASS export declaration. Failure to issue or transmit an electronic tax invoice attracts additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act.

NTS and KCS cross-verify each zero-rated export by matching the UNI-PASS export declaration, bill of lading date, e-tax invoice, sale contract, and the foreign exchange settlement record. If the exporter cannot produce the complete documentation package or the foreign currency proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, the zero rate is overturned, the supply is reclassified as domestic taxable, output VAT at 10% is assessed, related input VAT credits are denied, and additional tax plus interest is collected under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Where false invoices or forged export documents are used, the matter is referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment or fines, and the tax assessment period is extended up to 10 years for deceitful or unlawful acts, creating a retrospective clawback exposure on all claimed zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a dual-account system in which the Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, such as trade in goods, trade settlements, and service payments, but remains subject to authorization and reporting requirements for capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and portfolio flows. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all cross-border payments to be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks, and Korea's foreign exchange concentration system had been largely dismantled since the 1990s, but mandatory surrender requirements for export proceeds were eliminated; exporters are permitted to hold foreign currency in designated foreign currency accounts, provided that the underlying export transaction is reported to the designated bank. However, profit remittances by a foreign-invested company, such as dividends to non-resident shareholders, require proof of foreign direct investment (FDI) registration with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE) and a Foreign Exchange Bank, and dividend remittances need to follow the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The Bank of Korea may impose temporary measures restricting capital flows in the event of currency crisis or excessive volatility, and the Korean government can implement 'macroprudential stability charges' on non-deposit foreign currency liabilities, so a copper cathode trading entity must carefully document all underlying physical trade contracts, LME warehouse receipts, and customs declarations to ensure convertibility and remittance on the current account remains unimpeded. In practice, for a wholesale physical commodity trader, routine trade payments and receipts are considered current account operations and are freely convertible, but any equity injection, shareholder loan, or hedging contract exceeding certain thresholds requires prior reporting to the relevant foreign exchange bank and, if the amount exceeds USD 50 million, may require BOK approval. Corporate treasury planning should therefore separate operational trade flows from capital account transactions, with each route maintaining distinct evidence files and reporting codes under the FEIN system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory statutory surrender ratio or statutory time limit requiring conversion of export proceeds into Korean won under the current Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; exporters must receive and settle export proceeds through an authorized foreign exchange bank within 5 business days after the scheduled payment due date under the underlying trade contract, or if no due date is specified, within 5 business days from the date of shipment. The exporter is allowed to retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account without forced conversion, but each receipt must be reported using the standard trade settlement reporting code (e.g., trade payment notification under FEIN) within the bank's reporting window, and any non-export related credit to a foreign currency account is strictly prohibited.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) reporting system

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. South Korean banking law does not recognize digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) charters from foreign jurisdictions as authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Fintech EMIs that hold e-money licenses in the EEA or UK are legally barred from servicing a Korean-resident corporate entity for inbound KRW settlement, trade documentation, capital verification, or FX conversion purposes because the Bank of Korea and the Financial Supervisory Service require all cross-border payments to be cleared through licensed banks in Korea. A foreign EMI may provide ancillary services such as multi-currency virtual accounts or payment aggregation, but these cannot replace the bank account required for the entity's registered capital escrow, customs-duty payments, or VAT refund processing. The only remote-digital route is through a domestic Korean internet-only bank offering corporate accounts, such as K bank or Toss Bank, provided the entity has a Korean tax ID and statutory director identity verification, but legacy tier-1 banks remain the sole viable counterparties for documentary LC transactions involving LME copper cathode warrants.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. South Korean banking law does not recognize digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) charters from foreign jurisdictions as authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Fintech EMIs that hold e-money licenses in the EEA or UK are legally barred from servicing a Korean-resident corporate entity for inbound KRW settlement, trade documentation, capital verification, or FX conversion purposes because the Bank of Korea and the Financial Supervisory Service require all cross-border payments to be cleared through licensed banks in Korea. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 annual aggregate of specified international related-party transactions for Local File preparation and the Integrated International Transaction Report; Master File documentation is mandatory when the worldwide consolidated group's international transactions exceed KRW 100,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not adopt an UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the substance-over-form, actual business management, Limitation on Benefits and Central Management and Control doctrines are applied by the National Tax Service. A Chusik Hoesa engaged in LME copper cathode wholesale arbitrage must carry on Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, including negotiating master purchase/sale contracts, conducting physical inventory and warehouse controls, managing price and FX risk, issuing LME warrants, taking commodity risk, and settling documentary LCs. A brass-plate invoice middleman lacks CIGA and will lose treaty benefits, be treated as a pass-through, and have income imputed to the beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, Articles 9 and 10, fraudulent tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment up to 2 years and, for aggravated evasion exceeding KRW 300 million, up to 5 years, with fines up to five times the evaded tax; the NTS additionally imposes a 40% fraudulent understatement surcharge under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: A director who approved the false account books can be personally assessed under the National Tax Collection Act secondary taxpayer doctrine; personal assets can be seized after disregard of the corporate entity; the director faces a departure ban and blacklisting for unpaid tax exceeding KRW 50 million and can be sentenced to imprisonment, thereby piercing the corporate veil.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Hollow Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, failure to report or submitting false intercompany transaction disclosures triggers a 10% surcharge on the undisclosed transaction value, increased to 20% for false information; transfer pricing adjustments carry statutory overdue interest and can be treated as undisclosed foreign income, with additional surcharges up to 40% of the tax understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepared fraudulent TP files can be held jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax, interest and penalties; the tax court can recharacterize the arrangement and pierce the company if the Korean entity lacks CIGA; directors also risk criminal charges for obstruction of tax administration, travel blacklisting, and civil restitution of diverted profits.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Reporting and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, failure to report suspicious transactions is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 30 million, with confiscation of proceeds; aggravated trade-based money laundering involving more than KRW 1 billion can exceed the statutory fine range and trigger freezing orders and liquidation remedies.

Director personal liability: The representative director and AML compliance director are individually prosecutable; civil asset restitution is available to defrauded LC counterparties; courts may pierce the corporate veil when the Chusik Hoesa is operated as a mere façade, and a travel ban can be imposed during the criminal investigation.

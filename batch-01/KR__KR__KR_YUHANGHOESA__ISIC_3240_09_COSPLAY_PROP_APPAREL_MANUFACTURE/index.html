Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic tax portal: Home Tax Service (HomeTax) at www.hometax.go.kr.

        
E-government filing portal
For a domestic Korean LLC, the registration is completed at the district tax office after incorporation, receiving a Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the TIN and VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity, the process is: (1) assess whether the entity has a fixed place of business (domestic place of business) or makes taxable digital supplies to Korean consumers; (2) appoint a Korean tax representative if no domestic place of business exists; (3) prepare certified copies of the foreign registration certificate, articles of incorporation and relevant resolutions, a notarized power of attorney addressed to the tax representative, and documents proving the business premise (lease agreement or equivalent); (4) file the prescribed business registration application form (Business Registration Application) with the competent district tax office in person or via the HomeTax portal; (5) the NTS verifies the documents and issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number; (6) the tax representative then becomes the joint addressee for all tax notices and must submit an e-tax invoice issuance bond or guarantee if required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box classification election is available under the Korean Corporate Tax Act; a Korean LLC is automatically treated as a resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax. None of the OECD hybrid mismatch or US-style pass-through elections apply. A domestic group may elect consolidated corporate tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act if all conditions for a wholly-owned group are met.

Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No monetary threshold for regular VAT registration; every person supplying taxable goods or services in the course of business must register. Simplified taxpayer threshold: annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are exported outside Korea (Korean customs territory) and the export sale is evidenced by a customs export declaration and foreign currency settlement. Any goods exported under the Manufacturing Drawback or Free Trade Zone regime also qualify. Zero-rating additionally applies to services supplied to non-residents if the services are performed overseas or are not connected with Korean real estate or moveable property, and the consideration is settled in foreign currency.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a heavily liberalised yet administratively supervised foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The won is convertible for current account purposes, including trade-related payments and profit remittance, once the underlying documentation and reporting are completed through a registered foreign exchange bank. Capital account convertibility is conditional: inflows such as foreign direct investment, equity injections into a Korean limited liability company, and loans must be reported or approved depending on notifiable thresholds. There are no general surrender requirements for export proceeds, but a resident exporter must receive the contracted proceeds within the statutory collection period, normally one year from the export date, and any unreceived balance must be reported. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted after corporate tax reconciliation, with remission evidenced by audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and foreign exchange purpose codes for overseas remittance. The Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Bank of Korea operate a negative-list supervision framework; certain transactions involving cross-border guarantees, derivatives, or foreign exchange risk exceeding thresholds still require registration through a designated foreign exchange bank.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Statutory collection period is one year from the date of export declaration or final loading, with a possible extension of up to six months approved by the responsible foreign exchange bank for documented force majeure or receivables prolongation. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Export proceeds may be retained in a foreign currency account, provided the exporter maintains the bank-in record and submits the export credit collection report to the customs clearance system. Failure to collect within the period triggers a report to the Bank of Korea and may expose the entity to administrative penalty and adverse FX transaction history.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and subordinate Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Rating: 10/100. Not viable as a primary corporate banking rail. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not licensed in South Korea to open domestic corporate operating accounts or process Korean won settlements for this resident entity. Korean law requires resident legal entities to maintain accounts with institutions registered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and authorized as foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. An unlicensed offshore EMI cannot be used as the main clearing rail because it may violate foreign exchange financial transactions reporting and may not support the mandatory customer identification requirements. Such EMIs can only serve as auxiliary receiving channels where settlement is ultimately routed to a Korean licensed bank account; even then, each inbound remittance must be assigned the correct FX purpose code and disclosed if the aggregate amount crosses the reporting threshold.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Route equity subscription proceeds by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own bank account to a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, citing the foreign exchange purpose code for equity investment in a domestic company. The receiving bank files the foreign exchange receipt and use declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; if the amount equals or exceeds the statistical reporting threshold, it is reported automatically to the Bank of Korea. After remittance, a certified public accountant issues the capital verification report, the bank updates the shareholder paid-in capital record, and the entity registers the contribution in the corporate registry at the applicable market exchange rate. Any side-loan or convertible instrument must be separately documented to avoid misclassification as undisclosed debt.Use SWIFT MT103 for routine trade invoices and royalty payments below the bank's documentary threshold. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025: 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 20% on the portion from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 22% on the portion from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; and 25% on the portion exceeding KRW 300 billion. A separately imposed local corporate income tax generally adds 10% of the national corporate income tax, producing combined marginal rates of 11%, 22%, 24.2%, and 27.5%, respectively. Apply the rates and transitional rules in force for the relevant tax year.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
For dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident beneficial owner, the domestic withholding rate is generally 20% national tax plus 2% local income tax, for a combined 22%, before treaty relief. A tax treaty may reduce the rate, commonly subject to ownership, beneficial ownership, residence, limitation-on-benefits, and anti-abuse conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: For mandatory local file and master file reporting, the general thresholds are annual cross-border related-party transaction amounts of at least KRW 50 billion and annual sales of at least KRW 100 billion, subject to statutory definitions, aggregation rules, exemptions, and applicable year rules. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to a qualifying multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion. Separate international transaction information disclosures may apply below these documentation thresholds.

      
Economic substance scope: South Korea does not generally apply a UAE-style stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Korean tax authorities may nevertheless apply substance-over-form principles, transfer pricing rules, beneficial ownership analysis, controlled foreign corporation rules, anti-treaty-shopping provisions, and general anti-avoidance rules. Contracts, personnel, decision-making, functions, assets, risks, and actual conduct should support the reported allocation of income.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax evasion and false tax reporting
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, ordinary or aggravated underreporting penalties, late-payment additional tax, and criminal prosecution. Aggravated tax evasion can result in imprisonment and fines under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act and, where applicable, the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes. The precise penalty depends on amount, intent, duration, and statutory thresholds.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal criminal liability for their own participation, instruction, or knowing approval. Tax debts do not automatically pierce the corporate veil; separate statutory secondary liability, fraudulent-transfer, or criminal rules may apply. Travel restrictions may be imposed only where statutory collection or criminal procedure conditions are met.

      

      
      

        Transfer pricing manipulation and deficient documentation
        
Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, additional corporate tax, interest or late-payment additional tax, and penalties for failure to file or inaccurate reporting. Failure to submit required transfer pricing documentation can attract statutory penalties, including fixed or transaction-based amounts, depending on the report and applicable law.

        
Director personal liability: Corporate exposure is primary. Officers may face personal administrative or criminal consequences where they knowingly falsify records, obstruct an audit, or participate in tax offenses. Maintain contemporaneous benchmarking and transaction-level support.

      

      
      

        Withholding tax and dividend repatriation failure
        
Statutory sanction: The Korean withholding agent may be assessed the unpaid withholding tax, late-payment additional tax, and applicable penalties. Treaty relief claimed without adequate residence, beneficial ownership, or anti-abuse support may be denied and recovered.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible officers may incur personal exposure for intentional tax evasion, false certification, or other statutory offenses. The company should retain residence certificates, relief applications, contracts, payment records, and beneficial-owner analysis.

      

      
      

        Payroll, employment, and social insurance noncompliance
        
Statutory sanction: Back taxes, social insurance contributions, late charges, administrative penalties, and potential criminal sanctions for deliberate evasion or falsification. Classification depends on the actual working relationship, not the contract label.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible officers may face personal liability for deliberate violations or offenses under applicable tax and employment statutes. Maintain payroll, work-status, withholding, and contribution records.

      

      
      

        Shell-company, sham-substance, and treaty-shopping arrangements
        
Statutory sanction: Treaty relief may be denied, income may be recharacterized, and tax, interest, and penalties may be assessed under transfer pricing and anti-avoidance rules. Sham transactions or fabricated records may also expose participants to criminal prosecution.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face personal consequences for knowingly implementing sham arrangements or making false declarations. There is no automatic veil piercing solely because an entity has limited substance; liability depends on statutory grounds and individual conduct.

      

      
      

        Anti-money laundering and suspicious payment activity
        
Statutory sanction: Financial institutions may file suspicious transaction reports, restrict or terminate accounts, and require enhanced due diligence. Criminal and administrative sanctions may arise under applicable anti-money laundering, proceeds-of-crime, foreign exchange, and related statutes depending on the conduct.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly participate in laundering, concealment, or false reporting. A travel ban or imprisonment is not an automatic tax consequence and requires an applicable statutory or judicial basis.

      

      
      

        Corporate records, audit obstruction, and electronic filing failures
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, adverse tax adjustments, recordkeeping penalties, additional tax, and potential criminal liability for intentional falsification or obstruction.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be personally liable for their own unlawful acts or statutory offenses. Preserve books, invoices, customs records, intercompany agreements, tax returns, and electronic communications for the legally required retention period.

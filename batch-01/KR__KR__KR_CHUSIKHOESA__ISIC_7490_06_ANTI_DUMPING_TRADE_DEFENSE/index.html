No VAT registration exemption threshold exists in Korea; every business operator must register with the district tax office. The KRW 80,000,000 threshold applies only to simplified taxable individual taxpayers, not to a Chusik Hoesa, which is always a general taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and certain exported services are zero-rated under the Value-Added Tax Act. For goods, title and physical movement must pass from Korea to a foreign destination, evidenced by an accepted export declaration. For services such as anti-dumping trade defense consulting, zero rating applies when the service is supplied to a non-resident for use or benefit outside Korea and the consideration is received in foreign currency from a foreign source; the service must not be effectively consumed in Korea.

Mandatory NTS e-Tax Invoice system for business-to-business supplies, using Hometax or the NTS Open API; e-tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the supply date. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely free-floating managed currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. The Bank of Korea acts as the central bank responsible for exchange-rate policy, foreign reserves management, and FX transaction monitoring, while the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains delegated authority for capital-transaction licensing and reporting. Korean won convertibility is liberalized for current-account transactions; foreign-currency receipts such as export proceeds may be credited into foreign-currency accounts of domestic foreign-exchange banks without mandatory forced conversion, though incoming remittances require bank reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System. For capital-account transactions, including equity investment in a Chusik Hoesa, cross-border remittances are generally permitted if reportable, but the investor or entity must file a foreign-exchange declaration through a designated foreign-exchange bank before or within a prescribed grace period. Dividends and profit remittances are subject to withholding tax under Korean tax law and cross-border tax treaties; banks will require tax payment certificates, certified financial statements, and proof of no unresolved regulatory issues. The key practical hurdle is not formal inconvertibility but documentary strictness: banks demand real-name identification, purpose-code accuracy, and a contamination-free capital path before releasing foreign currency into won.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received in foreign currency and credited to a domestic foreign-exchange bank account, either kept in foreign currency or converted into Korean won, within the statutory export collection period of six months from the date of shipment or export declaration. The exporter must file receipt evidence through the Foreign Exchange Information System, and although there is no mandated percentage of hard surrender, the full remittance must be returned into the Korean banking system. Extensions require approval from the Korea Trade Insurance Corporation or the relevant foreign-exchange bank under exceptional documentary evidence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Bank of Korea, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, with Ministry of Economy and Finance policy authority)

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; plus 10% local income tax surtax resulting in a 26.4% combined top marginal rate. Lower marginal brackets are 9%, 19%, and 21%.

22% statutory dividend withholding tax on gross distributions to non-resident individual and corporate shareholders, composed of 20% national income tax plus 2% local income tax surtax, unless reduced under an applicable DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when cross-border transactions with foreign related parties in a fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 5 billion. Master File documentation is required when the Korean entity belongs to an international business group with consolidated group revenue of at least KRW 100 billion and international related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion. Country-by-Country Report filing is triggered at the same KRW 100 billion consolidated group revenue threshold, subject to the filing and notification requirements under Korea's Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

Economic substance scope: Korea has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE ESR, but the Korean tax authorities and courts apply a robust substance-over-form doctrine, the general anti-abuse rule, the business-purpose doctrine, and the principal-purpose test under DTAs. A Chusik Hoesa engaged in ISIC 7490 anti-dumping trade-defense consulting must maintain real employees, physical premises, board minutes, contracts, invoices, bank accounts, commercial risk-bearing, and actual decision-making functions. A shell entity with no substantive economic activity will be disregarded, recharacterized, or treated as a conduit, and treaty benefits and transfer pricing positions will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Immediate assessment of the deficient corporate tax plus dishonesty penalties of 30% to 40%, underreporting penalties of 10% to 20%, and a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.022%. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carries imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to three times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and representatives may face personal criminal imprisonment, personal monetary fines, civil restitution orders, travel bans under the National Tax Collection Act, and secondary collection against personal assets for taxes withheld from employees or suppliers and not remitted.
Shell Company Abuse and Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: Disregard or recharacterization of the entity and its transactions, denial of DTA benefits, disallowance of all related deductions, add-back of expenses to the taxable base, imposition of 20% to 40% unfaithful-return penalties, and an order to cease the illegal trade practice in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere instrumentality; directors and beneficial owners are jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax, are subject to departure restraint, and face criminal sanction if the sham was used to evade taxes.
Transfer Pricing Price Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS will make an arm's-length price adjustment, apply secondary adjustments, impose a 10% to 20% transfer pricing penalty, and add daily late-payment surcharges. Failure to file mandatory documentation triggers additional fixed penalties and may deprive the taxpayer of the statutory burden-of-proof benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false TP documentation are personally liable for civil damages to the company and to the tax authority, jointly responsible for penalties where gross negligence or willfulness is shown, and subject to criminal prosecution and travel bans in aggravated cases.
Disguised Profit Repatriation and Dividend Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The payment is recharacterized as a dividend; the net taxable income is increased through disallowance of the disguised expense; the 22% withholding tax is collected from the distributing entity together with a 20% unreported penalty, daily 0.022% late-payment surcharges, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax as statutory withholding agents; civil restitution may be imposed; criminal penalties of imprisonment up to three years or fines apply, and the NTS may issue travel bans and freeze director and shareholder assets.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unlicensed Trade Defense Activity

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information carry imprisonment of up to three years or fines up to KRW 30 million, plus confiscation of proceeds. Unlicensed practice of trade defense law triggers administrative suspension, revocation of licenses, and separate fines under the relevant legal-services regulation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for AML compliance and licensing supervision; they may be personally fined, criminally detained, banned from leaving Korea, and held civilly liable for losses sustained by clients or the tax authority in connection with unreported suspicious wire transfers.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, the total subscription price of each issued share must be fully paid in before the incorporation registration (Article 555), and the full amount of the capital contribution must be deposited into a corporate-named provisional capital subscription receipt account at a commercial bank; no blocked escrow account is required by Korean law. For non-resident participants, the inbound contribution is settled through a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and the funds must be reported within twenty days of the share acquisition. The elected paid-in capital for this entity is KRW 100,000,000, represented by 200,000 shares of KRW 500 par value each.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (http://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa in South Korea must first complete court registration under the Korean Commercial Act at the local district court registry, then file an Application for Business Registration with the competent NTS district tax office (or through HomeTax) within 20 days from the commencement of business. Supporting documents include the certificate of incorporation, registered articles of incorporation, registered corporate seal or authenticated signature of the representative, place-of-business lease or ownership documentation, and, if applicable, the foreign investment report certificate issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The NTS then issues the entity's 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the national Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the Value-Added Tax (VAT) identification number for the enterprise. For a foreign corporation with no domestic place of business, the entity must appoint a domestic tax representative and complete non-resident business registration with the NTS through HomeTax; the NTS will assign a separate non-resident taxpayer identification reference for Korean tax return, withholding, and VAT compliance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or general pass-through classification for a Yuhan Hoesa. A domestic group that satisfies the statutory wholly-owned domestic subsidiary conditions may elect consolidated (group) corporation tax treatment under the Corporate Tax Act; however, hybrid mismatch election mechanisms are not available and anti-hybrid rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act apply to disallow inappropriate deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold exists. Every business operator that supplies goods or services in Korea is mandatorily required to register for VAT from the first taxable supply, i.e., effective threshold KRW 0. A KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover threshold applies only for classification as a simplified taxable person and the associated simplified VAT calculation; it does not exempt a business from registration under the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated when the goods are physically moved out of the Republic of Korea and the export is evidenced by an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. A business may also zero-rate qualifying offshore services supplied to a non-resident for use outside Korea, provided the service contract, recipient's place of residence or establishment, and actual place of use are objectively documented. For the manufacture and export of UHT oat milk, the exporter must maintain full evidence that the goods were exported, the proceeds were collected in foreign currency or as agreed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the commercial benefit was received by a non-resident overseas buyer.

  • Customs export declaration accepted through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic clearance system
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or air waybill evidencing export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Korean foreign exchange receipt confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Purchase order or underlying export sales contract with the overseas buyer

All VAT-registered businesses, including corporations, must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) through the NTS HomeTax system or an NTS-approved e-invoice ASP service provider, and the invoice data is transmitted to the NTS in real time upon issuance. Mandatory e-invoice data fields include the issuer and recipient business registration numbers, supply date, item description, taxable amount, VAT amount, and unique invoice serial number. The NTS performs real-time schema validation, applies anti-fraud digital verification and automated cross-checks between purchase and sales ledgers, and pre-fills VAT return data for taxpayers. A paper tax invoice generally cannot support an input VAT credit for a business-to-business purchase that is subject to mandatory e-invoicing; the electronic record must be retrievable in the HomeTax system.

The National Tax Service and the Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export zero-rating claims and require production of all export and foreign-exchange documentation. If the statutory fulfillment evidence is inadequate or the export is found to be fabricated, the zero-rating is reversed and the NTS reassesses the 10% VAT on the unsubstantiated export supply, plus interest and additional penalty tax. Fraudulent or intentional underpayment claims and false export declarations may expose the entity to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment or fines, and to customs-related penalties under the Customs Act. The export proceeds must be settled and repatriated within 180 days of the export declaration; non-repatriation without an approved extension may cause the NTS or the Bank of Korea to treat the export as unjustified, with retrospective VAT clawback and exchange-control penalties. The general statute of limitations for national tax reassessment is five years, but it extends to ten years where the taxpayer filed fraudulently or omitted the transaction through false or fake documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalised current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account settlements, including trade payments, service fees and dividend disbursements, are generally permitted without prior approval when executed via a registered foreign exchange bank using prescribed settlement methods. Capital account movements are more controlled: inward foreign direct investment requires a notification through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and subsequent capital transactions must be reported to the Bank of Korea through the designated foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory percentage surrender obligation; however, they must be received within the statutory receipt term, typically one year from the export declaration date, and must be settled through a Korean foreign exchange bank or maintained in a domestic foreign currency account. Profit remittance is structurally permitted for a foreign-invested entity once corporate taxes are filed and paid, but the bank will require evidence of the foreign investment declaration, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates and a board resolution approving the dividend. Practical hurdles include prolonged documentary verification, harmonised transaction code reporting, and anti-money laundering review of the ultimate beneficial owner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds must be received by the Korean exporter via a domestic foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of export declaration, with extension possible only under narrowly defined circumstances reported to the Bank of Korea. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the entity may retain export receipts in a foreign currency demand deposit account or convert them to Korean won at market rates, but the bank must confirm the export customs declaration, invoice, shipping documents and packing list before settlement.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. An offshore digital EMI is not a permitted substitute for a Korean foreign exchange bank. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act require any provider of corporate payment and clearing services in Korean won to hold a valid domestic licence. No overseas EMI can lawfully open a settlement account, generate Korean won clearing references, or submit Bank of Korea BOP reporting. Therefore, the Yuhan Hoesa treasury structure must pivot on a domestic Tier 1 commercial bank, with any offshore EMI used only as a non-routing ancillary tool, if at all.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be routed through a Korean foreign exchange bank using a Foreign Investment Promotion Act declaration filed prior to remittance. The wire should be an SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank in cleared funds, clearly referencing the foreign investment notification registration number and the Bank of Korea assigned direct investment purpose code for capital contribution. After receipt, the bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase and sale confirmation and capital verification; the company must then complete the foreign-invested enterprise registration through KOTRA or the local Integrated Support Center. Do not commingle loan proceeds, personal funds or third-party payments into the capital injection wire.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlement of raw oat imports, packaging films, UHT packaging cartons and other input purchases. Commercial letters of credit, both sight and usance, are preferred for high-value equipment imports and are to be issued by the domestic Tier 1 bank with standard ICC UCP 600 terms. Documentary collections may be used for low-risk, low-value shipments, but the bank must verify the underlying export or import declaration and maintain accurate balance-of-payments reporting for each outward remittance.Forward outright contracts are permissible for matched export and import trade cash flows when executed with a designated Korean foreign exchange bank and supported by documented underlying trade exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are available to Korean corporates only for hedging non-trade emerging-market currency exposure and require Bank of Korea reporting where the Korean won leg is settled separately; pure speculative Korean won NDFs are restricted.Cross-currency swaps are permitted for balance-sheet hedging of EUR, USD and KRW exposures, limited to the underlying current or capital account transaction, subject to bank credit approval and a board-approved risk policy.European-style FX options are permissible as premium-covered hedging tools when the maturity aligns with documented trade cash flows and the notional does not exceed the underlying receivable or payable exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. An offshore digital EMI is not a permitted substitute for a Korean foreign exchange bank. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act require any provider of corporate payment and clearing services in Korean won to hold a valid domestic licence. No overseas EMI can lawfully open a settlement account, generate Korean won clearing references, or submit Bank of Korea BOP reporting. Therefore, the Yuhan Hoesa treasury structure must pivot on a domestic Tier 1 commercial bank, with any offshore EMI used only as a non-routing ancillary tool, if at all.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border intercompany transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion in the fiscal year trigger mandatory Local File preparation and KRW 50 billion for the Master File; in all cases a simplified intercompany transaction disclosure schedule must be attached to the corporate tax return, including arm's length range and selection methodology

Economic substance scope: Korean tax law and the OECD BEPS-aligned International Tax Adjustment Act apply a substance-over-form doctrine: the UHT plant must demonstrate physical premises, qualified staffing, purchase and production operational control (CIGA), risk incidence, and decision-making in Korea. An offshore shell or pass-through headquarters with no Korean management board, payroll, or warehouse has no economic substance and the entity can be disregarded for Korean corporate income tax and treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes and Corporate Tax Act, a fraudulent under-declaration triggers an additional 40% unfaithful-return penalty, daily non-payment surcharge, immediate assessment of evaded tax plus local surcharge, and criminal reference to the public prosecutor's office; the penalty tax can equal or exceed the original liability.

Director personal liability: Korean courts and NTS pierce the corporate veil if the LLC was used as a sham or instrument of tax fraud: directors, including foreign shadow directors, become jointly and severally liable for the shortfall, property is subject to attachment without reserve, personal bank accounts are frozen, departure from Korea is banned (travel blacklisting), and criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years may be imposed for each fraudulent return.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The NTS may re-audit, redetermine the arm's length income under the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose a 20% underreporting penalty on the adjusted excessive expense, apply a 0.75% monthly interest on the deficiency, and deny the deduction; if the adjustment is intentional, the penalty rises to 40% and the CFC/anti-avoidance provisions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the intercompany pricing policy and instructed the excessive remittance face joint and several civil liability for the adjusted corporation tax, interest and penalties, are personally disqualified from serving in board roles, have personal assets attached in the NTS collection process, and can be prosecuted for fiscal fraud with imprisonment from 1 to 5 years.
Shell Company Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Tax Act and International Tax Adjustment Act, tax authorities disregard the shell entity, deny treaty benefits under the domestic GAAR and principal-purpose test, join the parent and the LLC as a single taxpayer, reassess all outbound distributions as Korean-source constructive dividends, and impose a 40% fraud penalty plus local surcharge and daily overdue interest.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell are jointly liable for the reassessed corporate tax and penalties because the corporate form was a sham, personal travel is restricted by the NTS blacklist, the director's personal property is civilly seized to satisfy the corporate debt, and the misconduct is classified under embezzlement and tax fraud rules with imprisonment terms up to 5 years and/or criminal fines of up to KRW 50 million.

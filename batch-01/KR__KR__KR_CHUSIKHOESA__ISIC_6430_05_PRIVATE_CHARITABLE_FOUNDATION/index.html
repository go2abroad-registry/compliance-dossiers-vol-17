Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic administration and filing through the HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr), and business registration administered through the NTS district tax offices and the Korea Trade Facilitation System for associated export registrations.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa in South Korea, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by the competent district tax office under the Framework Act on National Taxes. The non-resident incorporator must appoint a local tax representative if it has no domestic place of business, but for a domestic Chusik Hoesa the entity itself is registered as a domestic corporation. Procedure: (1) file articles of incorporation with the Commercial Registration Authority; (2) register the corporation with the court registry; (3) file an application for business registration with the National Tax Service through HomeTax or by visiting the district tax office, using the prescribed application form for corporate business registration and attaching the certificate of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, lease agreement or proof of domestic place of business, and identification documents of the representative and directors; (4) the NTS reviews and issues the BRN, which is the combined corporate TIN and VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity that does not form a domestic Chusik Hoesa but has Korean-source income subject to tax or a VAT registration obligation, the NTS additionally assigns a foreign-invested company or non-resident TIN through the same business registration process; where no domestic business place exists, a non-resident tax representative registration is made and the foreign entity receives a non-resident registration number. The Chusik Hoesa must also register for social security and local tax (local income tax) identification through the integrated local tax system, but the primary TIN remains the NTS BRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for general entity classification. The Chusik Hoesa is a domestic corporation taxable at the entity level under the Corporate Income Tax Act and is not eligible for a check-the-box partnership classification. However, if the Chusik Hoesa qualifies as a non-profit corporation or public-interest corporation under Article 24 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and is engaged in philanthropic endowment management and scientific grants, it may elect or apply for tax-exempt status on a restricted scope of income, and may separately apply for approval as a public-interest corporation under the Act on Establishment and Management of Public-Interest Corporations. Such elections are statutory status elections, not hybrid mismatch elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold: any person supplying taxable goods or services in the course of business, including corporations, is mandatorily required to register for VAT, regardless of revenue amount. A simplified VAT registration threshold of KRW 80 million annual turnover applies to small businesses for simplified taxation, but a Chusik Hoesa engaged in endowment management, grants, and digital content operations is generally not eligible for simplified taxation if it performs taxable activities. For non-resident electronic service suppliers and cross-border digital services, a special simplified registration is required when annual Korean-source e-service supplies exceed KRW 10 million (approximately USD 7,500).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act of Korea, a zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to (1) the supply of exported goods, defined as goods delivered to a foreign country or to a free trade zone; (2) the supply of services rendered to non-residents or services rendered in a foreign country, including intermediate services related to exported goods; and (3) digital services or other electronically supplied services provided to non-resident consumers if the place of supply is treated as outside Korea. For the Chusik Hoesa, zero-rating is available for the export of qualifying strategic content, digital assets, research outputs, and certain offshore services, provided the actual benefit occurs outside Korea and the recipient is a non-resident. Grant distributions themselves are not a taxable supply and are outside the VAT system; endowment management services rendered to foreign charitable counterparties can qualify as zero-rated export services if the service is performed or consumed outside Korea and documented as a cross-border supply.

South Korea mandates full electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issuance and real-time transmission to the NTS through HomeTax for all VAT-registered businesses. Since December 31, 2012, all tax invoices must be issued electronically through the HomeTax electronic invoicing system or approved third-party e-invoicing APIs integrated with HomeTax. Each e-tax invoice is automatically assigned a digital approval number by the NTS in real time, and the supply transaction is immediately recorded in the national anti-fraud database. A cash receipt must be issued for all sales to non-business final consumers, with mandatory issuance through the cash receipt system for any transaction occurring in the course of business. For cross-border services and exports, the export invoice or electronic export tax invoice must be filed with supporting customs export declarations in the UNI-PASS system. Failure to issue or transmit e-tax invoices triggers 2% penalties on the supply value and additional administrative penalties.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict post-export audit by the National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service. The exporter must substantiate zero-rating with the full statutory documentation bundle above, and foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated and received in Korea within 180 days of the export date unless an extension is approved by the Commissioner of the NTS or the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. If the inward remittance is not received within the 180-day deadline or documented evidence is incomplete, the NTS shall deny zero-rating and assess 10% VAT retroactively, impose daily interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and deny input VAT credits related to the export. For fraudulent export claims, fictitious export invoices, or false lost-in-transit certificates, penalty taxes of up to 40% of the unpaid VAT are imposed, together with the additional 10% VAT amount. Criminal referral to the prosecution can result in tax evasion charges punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years or fines up to 3 times the evaded tax. The NTS and Korea Customs Service also jointly verify customs declarations, shipping manifests, foreign exchange receipts, and invoices through centralized cross-agency data matching; any mismatch triggers a full tax audit and immediate clawback of zero-rated refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a free-floating Korean won with full current-account convertibility under Article 9 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account transactions are generally liberalized but remain subject to declaration and in certain situations prior approval. For a private charitable foundation receiving offshore endowment, the KRW is freely convertible into USD, EUR, and other major currencies within designated foreign exchange banks. Inward donations are treated as current transfers provided the underlying charitable purpose is documented. There is no surrender requirement for endowment or investment income. However, every inward remittance above the statutory threshold must be reported through the receiving foreign exchange bank, and the foundation must produce the donation agreement, the donor identification package, and evidence of the public-interest purpose. Outbound grant distributions to foreign beneficiaries are lawful current transfers, but the Korean bank must verify the ultimate beneficiary, verify the grant contract, screen sanctioned parties, and record the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code. The Bank of Korea may ask for the foundation's internal control and treasury investment policy to ensure that the structure is not a disguised for-profit treasury or a conduit for money laundering. In practice, convertibility is constrained by strict FATF-aligned Know Your Customer and Foreign Transactions Reporting obligations rather than by direct quantitative limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to a non-exporting private charitable foundation. Under the general Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act framework, export proceeds must be collected through a designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of the export declaration, or within the contractually approved payment term where an extension has been recorded. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; the exporter may hold foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account, convert freely, or net against approved foreign liabilities. Overdue export collections require a written explanation to the foreign exchange bank and may be referred to the Bank of Korea for a compliance review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (the central bank) together with the Foreign Exchange Policy Division of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the statutory foreign exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very Low and legally insufficient as a primary banking rail. A foreign digital EMI or electronic money institution is not a licensed bank under the Korean Banking Act and cannot lawfully propose KRW current accounts or direct foreign exchange conversion services to a Korean legal entity. A Korean-licensed fintech can only act as a payment facilitator or supplementary payout rail when operating through a designated commercial bank settlement account. For a statutory private charitable foundation requiring capital escrow, grant distribution, and strict AML traceability, the mandatory primary account must be held at a domestic tier-1 commercial bank; the EMI may only be used as an optional downstream disbursement channel for grantees where permitted by the underlying grant contract and the governing bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Progressive national corporate income tax: 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200,000,000; 20% on KRW 200,000,001 to KRW 20,000,000,000; 22% on KRW 20,000,000,001 to KRW 300,000,000,000; 24% on taxable income above KRW 300,000,000,000. A 10% local income tax surcharge is imposed on the national corporate tax, producing aggregate headline exposure ranging from 11.0% to 26.4% depending on the taxable income bracket.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory withholding tax rate on dividend distributions by a Korean domestic corporation to a non-resident shareholder is 20% national income tax, plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 22%. Treaty relief may reduce the national tax without eliminating the local surcharge in most Korean DTAs, so the aggregate effective rate after treaty relief is usually the applicable treaty rate multiplied by 110%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Korean Chusik Hoesa must prepare and attach a Local File when its international transactions with related parties during the fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 5,000,000,000. A Master File is mandatorily required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue reaches KRW 100,000,000,000 and the Korean entity's aggregate international related-party transaction amount exceeds KRW 10,000,000,000. The annual 'List of International Related-Party Transactions' schedule must be submitted with the corporate tax return even when transaction amounts fall below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies a substance-over-form doctrine through the National Tax Basic Law, the force of attraction principle, the beneficial ownership test, and controlled foreign corporation rules. There is no standalone ESR filing regime comparable to the UAE or BVI economic substance regulations. Instead, a private charitable foundation acting as an endowment vehicle must demonstrate sufficient Korean commercial substance through board meetings, managerial control, physical office presence, actual grant decision-making, and qualified local staff. Absent such substance, the Korean tax authorities may disregard the legal form, treat the foundation as a sham, deny treaty benefits, or require consolidation into a foreign parent's Korean taxable presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty tax of 40% to 60% of the evaded national corporate tax, with an additional 10% local income tax surcharge and daily overdue interest. Criminal conviction under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act carries imprisonment of up to three years or a fine equal to up to five times the amount of tax evaded. The court may also prohibit the offender from operating, suspend corporate licences, and confiscate diverted assets.

Director personal liability: Executive and non-executive directors who directed or acquiesced in concealment may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes to the extent of the corporation's inability to settle. Personal exposure includes travel bans, asset freezing orders, restitution to the charitable purpose, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of the treaty claim and requiring full 22% withholding; addition of a 40% unfaithful-return penalty and a 20% aggravated assessment under the Framework Act on International Tax Adjustment; reclassification of grants as constructive dividends; possible administrative taxation on deemed Korean source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute the artificial chain may be held personally liable for the unreported tax as constructive trustees of the foundation's assets. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment for up to three years and fines; civil recovery measures allow tax authorities to pierce the corporate veil when the foundation exists only as a sham.
Money Laundering and Charitable Fraud

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Proceeds of Crime Act and the Financial Transaction Reports Act, including fines of up to KRW 500,000,000, confiscation of the proceeds of the violation, suspension from acting as a grant-making institution, and revocation of registration as a charitable or non-profit entity. Individuals involved face imprisonment of up to five years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML and charity compliance may be disqualified from serving as trustees, personally fined, and ordered to return misappropriated donor funds. Repeated or large-scale money laundering triggers asset seizure, international travel bans, extradition exposure, and imprisonment.
False Documentation and Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Rejection of the deducted management fees or royalties, application of an upward arm's-length adjustment, imposition of the 20% unfaithful-return penalty on the recomputed tax, accrual of daily unpaid interest, and correction of foreign tax credit claims. The National Tax Service may also order civil surcharges of 10% of the disallowed amount and criminal fine exposure of up to KRW 50,000,000.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false tax return and in doing so knowingly transmits false information to the tax office is personally liable for the resulting penalty where the corporation becomes insolvent. The director faces disqualification, travel bans, personal restitution for the lost tax revenue, and a term of imprisonment of up to one year for filing false declarations.

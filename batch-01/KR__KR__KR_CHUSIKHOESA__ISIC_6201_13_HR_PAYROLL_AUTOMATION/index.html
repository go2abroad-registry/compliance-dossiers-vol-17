Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under South Korean law; the incorporators may fix any stated capital in the Articles of Incorporation. The full subscription price must be deposited into a blocked or purpose-limited Korean-won capital subscription account at a licensed domestic bank and may be released only after the company's incorporation registration has been completed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: NTS HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For the declared Korean Chusik Hoesa, the entity will obtain its 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN, which functions as the TIN and VAT registration ID) by filing the prescribed business registration application with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the registered head office; filing is performed electronically on HomeTax after incorporation with the Commercial Act registry. For a foreign non-resident entity with a Korean establishment or a Korean-source activity, the procedure requires: (1) appointing a tax representative if no fixed place of business exists in Korea; (2) completing the HomeTax business registration application, including the foreign entity registration certificate, constitutional documents, proof of the Korean place of business, and the power of attorney for the tax representative; (3) submitting the application to the local district tax office; (4) receiving the BRN and VAT registration certificate. Registration is governed by Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold applies; every person, including a foreign non-resident entity carrying on a business in Korea, is required to file for business/VAT registration under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act within 20 days from the business commencement date regardless of turnover, with simplified taxpayer treatment available only to qualifying small service/retail businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act to exported goods and services supplied to overseas non-residents where the place of supply is outside Korea, including qualifying offshore SaaS, software, payroll platform, and digital-services subscriptions supplied from Korea to foreign business customers. To sustain the zero rate the supplier must create and retain written contracts, invoices, customs evidence, and bank payment records capable of demonstrating that title/control passed outside Korea, the beneficial user is a foreign business/government, payment is collected from abroad, and foreign currency is received in Korea through a Foreign Exchange Bank.

  • Customs export declaration acceptance/export declaration number issued by Korea Customs Service
  • Commercial invoice and underlying contract evidencing the supply to the overseas buyer
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other shipping/electronic-lading evidence of international movement
  • Foreign Exchange Bank inward payment certificate or foreign-currency repatriation evidence proving actual collection of export/SaaS consideration

Korea operates a centralized real-time e-invoice regime: all VAT-registered taxpayers must issue an electronic tax invoice (digital tax invoice) for each taxable supply and transmit it to the NTS HomeTax e-invoice platform via the approved API/ERP integration on the date of supply or no later than the next business day, as prescribed by the Value-Added Tax Act. There is no advance transaction-level fiscal approval or physical digital stamp, but the NTS applies automated matching, e-invoice serial-number controls, and risk-based detection of paper-to-digital manipulation; late or failed transmission triggers per-invoice penalties.

If the NTS determines that the zero-rating is not substantiated by the required contemporaneous documentation or that the export proceeds are not repatriated to Korea by the 180th day, it may retroactively reclassify the supply as taxable at 10%, issue a revised assessment with late-payment interest, impose statutory penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes (including up to 40% of the unpaid tax for fraudulent under-declaration and 10-20% negligence penalties), and pursue criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for willful evasion or false e-invoice issuance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but fully monitored foreign exchange environment under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Enforcement Decree on Foreign Exchange Transactions. Current-account transactions, including payroll licensing, software subscription revenues, consulting fees and trade payments, are generally freely convertible in KRW, provided the underlying income is supported by commercial contracts, tax invoices and payment purpose declarations filed through the Foreign Exchange Information System FEPIS. Capital-account transactions are subject to pre-reporting or post-reporting requirements; foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa is permissible but must be routed through a registered foreign exchange bank and registered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds; Korean companies may maintain foreign-currency deposit accounts and retain export earnings in foreign currency. The main convertibility hurdles are documentary rather than exchange-control-imposed: inward remittances require the foreign investor to file a capital transaction report, and outward dividends, principal repayments or cross-border technology royalties require a validated foreign exchange rate, withholding tax verification from the National Tax Service and a filing with the Bank of Korea. In practice, a foreign-owned payroll automation company must maintain a robust transfer-pricing file, audited accounts, software licensing contracts and payroll service agreements to demonstrate commercial substance before profit repatriation or large cross-border disbursements are approved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds for goods must be received in the Republic of Korea within 18 months of the export customs declaration, unless a foreign exchange bank grants an extension, normally up to one additional year, for documented trade delays or settlement disputes. Services export proceeds, including software and payroll-processing revenues, must be reported to the foreign exchange bank in the applicable monthly foreign exchange information statement. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; Korean exporting entities may hold all export receipts in foreign currency deposit accounts, and the effective surrender ratio for proceeds is zero percent.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, acting as the central bank and principal operator of the Foreign Exchange Information System FEPIS, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange policy authority and licensed foreign exchange banks as delegated executing intermediaries

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury banker. South Korea does not grant full banking licences to offshore fintech EMI providers, and an overseas electronic money institution cannot lawfully hold a Chusik Hoesa local KRW settlement account or execute direct debit payroll rails. A fintech EMI may only transact through a Korean licensed bank sponsor, creating a nested account arrangement that Korean anti-money laundering compliance committees typically reject for a medium-risk SaaS treasury due to third-party counter-party risk. A fintech EMI may be acceptable only as a supplementary outbound cross-border beneficiary interface, never as the regulated domestic account structure.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Mandatory external audit is triggered by the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, etc. The principal test is the balance-sheet asset threshold: external audit is required where total assets exceed KRW 12 billion (approx. USD 9.2 million) as of the close of the immediately preceding fiscal year. There is no Korean legal headcount or turnover threshold that, by itself, imposes an audit on a private stock corporation; however, a company must also submit to an independent audit if it is a listed/KOSPI/KOSDAQ/KONEX issuer, is designated as a public-interest company under the Capital Markets Act, is in a regulated financial sector subject to sectoral audit rules, or is otherwise ordered by the Financial Supervisory Service on application of shareholders or creditors. Payroll/HR SaaS platforms are not licensed financial companies, so for a non-listed private Chusik Hoesa the asset test is the operative threshold.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% statutory top marginal rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; progressive CIT brackets of 10% up to KRW 200 million, 20% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 22% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion, and 25% above KRW 300 billion; plus a 10% local corporate income surtax producing an aggregate top effective marginal rate of 27.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory withholding on outbound dividends, composed of 20% national corporate income tax withheld at source and 2% local corporate income tax surtax; this is reduced only when a bilateral Korea tax treaty provides a lower rate to a beneficial owner meeting documentary and holding-period conditions.

Within three months after the closing day of the accounting period under Article 60(1) of the Corporate Tax Act; a December 31 fiscal year-end is due by March 31 of the following year, and if the due date falls on a non-business day, the next business day. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Local File must be prepared when cross-border related-party transaction amounts exceed KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year; a Master File is required when the multinational group's related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion or the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion, and specific categories of services, intangibles, loans, and goods have separate lower reporting thresholds requiring an annual International Transaction Report where services or intangibles exceed KRW 50 million or goods exceed KRW 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate the Gulf Cooperation Council Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the substance-over-form principle in Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the Business Restructuring rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act, and the OECD principal purposes test require the Korean payroll SaaS entity to demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea. The CIGA of payroll automation include proprietary payroll engine development, HR regulatory updates, salary calculation algorithms, data storage, employee compliance monitoring, and client management; an entity that merely executes contracts and invoices while the foreign affiliate performs all real functions and bears no substantive risk may be disregarded, its intercompany prices adjusted, and its treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Suppressed Payroll SaaS Revenue and Digital Payment Diversion

Statutory sanction: Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and Article 60 of the Corporate Tax Act; under Articles 47-2 and 47-4 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the civil deficiency can be computed as 100% of the omitted corporate tax plus 40% understatement penalty for fraudulent act plus 0.022% daily late-payment interest. Criminal exposure is imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine up to twice the amount of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: The National Tax Service may pierce the corporate veil under Supreme Court substance-over-form precedent and FANT Article 14 when the Chusik Hoesa is a shell used for tax evasion; the representative director and CFO who signed the false return or directed the hidden digital revenue are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties from personal assets, may face a departure ban or immigration blacklist for tax delinquency, and may be criminally imprisoned; under Commercial Act Article 401 the directors also owe civil damages to creditors for gross negligence in payroll SaaS revenue diversion.
Wilful Non-Withholding on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Articles 98, 98-5 and 98-6 of the Corporate Tax Act and Articles 47-4 and 47-5 of the Framework Act on National Taxes: the Korean payer is liable to collect and remit the full 22% withholding tax, is denied any deduction for the gross-up absent withholding, and is subject to a 20% under-withholding penalty and 0.022% daily late-payment surcharge. If the non-withholding was fraudulent, the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act applies with imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine up to twice the unwithheld tax.

Director personal liability: The director or finance officer who deliberately failed to withhold may be personally liable for the unwithheld amount under FANT Article 12, since the company is merely a fiduciary withholding agent; the NTS may collect the unwithheld amount from the director's personal bank accounts and request a prohibition on departure from Korea until the under-withheld tax is settled. Criminal conviction also triggers disqualification of the representative director and civil restitution claims by the Korean treasury.
Sham Shell IP Holding and Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Articles 4, 4-2 and 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act permit the NTS to re-determine the arm's length royalty and constructive dividend, to deny the related-party royalty deduction, and to impose additional tax under Article 19 of that Act; unreported or understated international transactions may incur 10% to 30% penalties and the 40% fraud penalty under FANT Article 47-4; the statute of limitations for assessment is extended for fraudulent omission. The arrangement may be recharacterized as an abusive tax avoidance scheme under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: The NTS will disregard the foreign subsidiary and attribute its CIGA and income to the Korean entity, and if the director causes the limited company to be used for a sham IP tax shelter, the corporate veil may be pierced; the director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, can be blacklisted from international travel, and under Article 9 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can face imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine equal to twice the understated corporate tax deficiency, while Commercial Act Article 401 exposes the director to shareholder and creditor damage claims for the sham structure.

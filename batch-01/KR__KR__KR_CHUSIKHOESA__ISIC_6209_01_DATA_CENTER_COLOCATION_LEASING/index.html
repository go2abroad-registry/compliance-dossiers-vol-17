Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Commercial Act requires the Korean Chusik Hoesa to state a registered head office (dae-pyo-jeom) in Korea, but it does not mandate the ownership of physical premises at the moment of incorporation. However, for a data-center colocation and bare-metal server leasing business under ISIC 6209, the National Tax Service and commercial banks reject a purely postal mailbox or agency-only virtual office. In practice, tax authorities treat a registered office as the business place for VAT, corporate tax filing, and invoice issuance; a virtual office is accepted only if there is a real administrative presence, a permanent signboard, staff, the actual lease is in the company's name, and mail can be physically received. Leading Korean banks and KoFIU-CDD procedures require the business address to match the registered address and demand a lease agreement and utility bill in the company's name. Recommendation: rent serviced office space for at least six months and maintain an onsite local administrator for this industry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that commences a Korean taxable business, including data-center colocation leasing in Korea, must obtain a Korean Business Registration Certificate (BRC), which is the combined TIN/VAT ID. Step 1: Determine the Korean VAT taxable establishment or fixed local place of business; a lease agreement for the Korean data-center facility should be in place. Step 2: Appoint a Korean tax representative if the foreign entity has no habitual domicile or local physical establishment, as required by the Framework Act on National Taxes. Step 3: Prepare the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution authorizing the Korean operation, passport of the authorized representative, power of attorney, and proof of the local Korean business address. Step 4: File Form for Business Registration (Business Operator Registration) with the NTS through the HomeTax portal using the foreign-business registration channel, or in person with the district tax office that has jurisdiction over the Korean place of business. Step 5: receive the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is used for corporate income tax, VAT withholding, e-tax invoice issuance, and customs-related indirect tax purposes. The Korean TIN and VAT ID are the same number; no separate VAT ID is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None equivalent to a check-the-box election under Korean law. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically classified as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax and VAT. Statutory elective mechanisms are limited to Korean consolidated corporate tax filing for 100% domestic subsidiaries and voluntary VAT registration for non-resident suppliers before the first taxable supply. No hybrid mismatch election or fiscal-transparent entity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - VAT registration is mandatory from the moment a taxable business commences in Korea, regardless of turnover. There is no turnover threshold postponing registration for corporations. For foreign non-resident suppliers, registration is required before the first taxable supply unless a reverse-charge or direct import mechanism applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT treatment is available under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act Article 21 for (i) exported goods where the goods are physically removed from Korea and the export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service; and (ii) qualifying exported/foreign-earning services supplied to non-residents, including services that are actually consumed outside Korea, international transport services, and services directly related to export trade. For data-center colocation leasing of rack space, power, cooling, and bare-metal hosting located in Korea, the place of supply is deemed to be Korea and the standard 10% VAT rate applies. The service is not eligible for zero-rating merely because the customer is a foreign resident. Only separately identifiable cross-border digital/cloud services that are supplied from Korea and actually used or consumed outside Korea could potentially qualify for zero-rating, subject to stringent documentary proof of foreign use and foreign-currency receipt.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS Export Declaration Acceptance (export declaration with customs clearance confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical delivery outside Korea
  • Foreign exchange settlement record / inward remittance certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing actual offshore payment receipt
  • Export/offshore service contract and, where applicable, documentary evidence that services were actually used or consumed outside Korea

All taxable business persons in Korea must issue VAT invoices for taxable supplies, and corporations are required to issue electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices; e-segye-gyesanseo) through HomeTax or a certified AGT (ASP) integrator. For B2B supplies, the e-tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the tax invoice issuance date. There is no separate real-time cryptographic clearance or anti-fraud stamping regime; however, the NTS performs near-real-time data matching, cross-referencing e-tax invoice filings, cash receipts, and VAT returns, and mandates API/Hometax electronic transmission for automated filing. Failure to issue and report electronic tax invoices triggers penalty surcharges.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service audit zero-rated export claims by matching export declarations, electronic tax invoices, foreign exchange receipts, and corporate VAT returns. If an exporter fails to collect export consideration within 180 days or submits false or insufficient documentation, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied, and the NTS assesses output VAT at 10% on the suppressed supply. Penalty surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes are imposed, generally 10% of the unpaid VAT for negligent under-declaration and up to 40% for fraudulent under-declaration, in addition to daily interest on the delayed payment. Willful fraud may also trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions under Article 3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including trade settlements, service fees, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions, however, are governed by a negative list system requiring prior declaration or approval from the designated foreign exchange bank when the transaction falls outside the pre-authorized categories. Korean entities receiving foreign currency export proceeds are generally mandated to convert such proceeds into won within the statutory deadline or hold them in a foreign currency account, but the latter requires justification. For a data center colocation leasing entity, cross-border monthly lease fees from overseas customers would be current account receipts (service export under ISIC 6209), which are freely convertible subject to verification of the underlying invoice. Profit remittance in the form of dividends to non-resident shareholders is legal, but requires a Foreign Exchange Bank confirmation of the entity's audited financial statements, withholding tax clearance from the National Tax Service (NTS), and a certificate of foreign investment registration if the shareholding is foreign-direct-investment based. In practice, the Bank of Korea's annual Foreign Exchange Reform Plans have progressively liberalized controls, but the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations still require the submission of a purchase/sale confirmation and a customs declaration for amounts exceeding 50,000 USD, effectively creating friction for large lump-sum data center equipment imports. Surrender requirements were substantially relaxed in 2017, but repatriation of export proceeds must be completed within a prescribed period to avoid penalties. The Ministry of Economy and Finance periodically reviews the basis for restricting speculative FX transactions, and any mass remittance pattern from the Korean entity without corresponding service contracts would raise Bank of Korea inspection flags. Realistically, for recurring colocation lease income, the main hurdle is not currency conversion itself but the requirement to establish a clear underlying services contract with accurate HS/ECC codes and an annual settlement report to the bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For exports of services under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, Korean exporters must receive their foreign currency proceeds through an authorized foreign exchange bank and, for goods exports, the proceeds must be settled within 18 months from the shipment date (recently extended to 24 months for certain SME categories); for service exports such as data center colocation, receipts are generally required to be converted into won when the counterparty is a non-resident, unless the entity operates a designated Foreign Currency Account with the bank's prior approval. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0% after the 2017 deregulation, as the surrender requirement for export proceeds was eliminated, but the exporter must report the receipt via the Foreign Exchange Information System within 30 business days. If the Korean entity chooses to retain foreign currency in a domestic bank account, it must file an acquisition report and apply the proceeds for approved purposes only.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Bank of Korea administers foreign exchange oversight with the Korea Customs Service as operational monitoring agent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Non-viable for storing corporate treasury balances: South Korea does not license standalone digital EMI entities; foreign fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer cannot provide local IBANs or operate as regulated deposit-taking institutions for KRW. They may be used peripherally as a payment collection gateway for international customers denominated in USD, but funds must ultimately be swept into a South Korean licensed bank account or a properly registered foreign exchange broker under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Legal constraint: only banks licensed under the Korean Banking Act can open won-denominated business accounts; any remittance via an unlicensed EMI triggers Bank of Korea foreign exchange reporting and could be treated as unauthorized foreign exchange business. Therefore, the treasury blueprint mandates a domestic tier-1 bank as the primary clearing node, with EMIs relegated to a non-core ancillary invoicing tool if they hold an overseas MSB/fintech license and are used solely for receivables conversion.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal Korean corporate income tax rate (progressive brackets: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion; 21% over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion; 24% over KRW 300 billion). A 10% local income tax surtax is imposed on assessed corporate tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory aggregate: 20% Korean non-resident corporate income tax withheld under the Income Tax Act, plus a 10% local surtax (2 percentage points), bringing the gross statutory WHT to 22%. Most Korean bilateral tax treaties reduce this rate to 5-15% for portfolio or substantial-holding dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion or more of aggregate related-party cross-border transactions in the taxable year triggers mandatory Local File and related-party transaction schedules; consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion with cross-border intercompany transactions of KRW 5 billion or more triggers the Master File. A combined report and TP documentation must be submitted within 45 days of NTS request; failure creates a presumption in favor of the tax authority's arm's length adjustment under Article 5-6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies the economic-substance doctrine through FANT Article 14, the substance-over-form principle in corporate taxation, and LCITA's international tax adjustment and CFC rules. For a data-center colocation lessor, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) are: acquiring and leasing the data-center site, owning and maintaining servers and rack hardware, employing technical engineers to manage power, cooling, and network uptime, negotiating and signing customer agreements, and bearing residual operating and currency risk. Treaty and reduced-rate benefits are denied if those CIGA are performed by a foreign subsidiary while the Korean entity is reduced to a pass-through contract manufacturer with no local people, assets, or functions.

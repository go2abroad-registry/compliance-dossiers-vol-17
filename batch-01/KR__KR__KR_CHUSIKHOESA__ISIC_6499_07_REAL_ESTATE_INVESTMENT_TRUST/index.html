Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act: after the 2012 amendment, no minimum paid-in capital is required to incorporate a Chusik Hoesa. However, for registration as a Real Estate Investment Company under the Real Estate Investment Company Act and its Enforcement Decree, the company must satisfy the statutory paid-in capital, net asset, and equity composition requirements in force at the time of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport application. There is no statutory blocked escrow account rule under the Commercial Act, but the full subscription price must be paid into a domestic bank account and cannot be withdrawn before the Registry confirms incorporation; the bank certificate of deposit is filed with the Registry. The registration tax is 0.4 percent of declared paid-in capital plus a 20 percent education surcharge plus a fixed court fee; the 400 USD field entry is an approximate total for a KRW 100,000,000 paid-in capital case.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the electronic tax administration portal HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Customs and export clearance functions relevant to export zero-rating are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNI-PASS electronic system. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT) issues the statutory license or approval required for a Real Estate Investment Trust under the Real Estate Investment Company Act before the NTS business registration becomes fully effective.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act through court registration at the local registry office and obtain the corporate registration certificate. If foreign capital is involved, complete the foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through a designated foreign exchange bank or KOTRA to obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate. Step 2: After MOLIT REIT approval or license issuance, the statutory representative or an appointed tax agent files a Business Registration Application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business within 20 days from business commencement, in accordance with Article 5-4 of the Value Added Tax Act and Article 51 of the Corporate Income Tax Act. Step 3: The NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit business registration number that functions as the single national tax identification number, the VAT registration number, and the e-invoice identifier for the entity. Step 4: A foreign entity that does not yet have a domestic place of business must appoint a domestic tax representative in accordance with Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes to apply for a TIN or temporary VAT refund registration. Step 5: The REIT must also complete VAT payer registration as a general taxpayer; the simplified taxpayer regime is not available to corporations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (default): a resident Chusik Hoesa is treated as a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income, with a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax; there is no group or consolidated filing regime. Pass_Through_Eligible (REIT special): a qualifying listed REIT may deduct dividends actually paid out of the current fiscal year distributable income under Article 51-2 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, effectively achieving a pass-through tax result for distributed rental income and capital gains, subject to continued listing and payment of at least 90% of distributable income. Territorial_Scope: not the default for a domestic corporation, because resident entities are taxed on worldwide income. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification election exists in Korea; tax classification follows the legal form under the Commercial Act. Election 1 (REIT dividend deduction): a listed REIT may elect to deduct dividends distributed within the statutory period, provided it distributes at least 90% of distributable income and satisfies the listing, debt-to-equity, and asset composition requirements under Article 51-2 of the Corporate Income Tax Act. Election 2 (special tax reductions on acquisitions): the REIT may elect to claim a 50% reduction of acquisition tax and registration tax on qualifying real estate acquisitions made under the statutory REIT management period and resale restrictions. Election 3 (deferral of capital gains on contributed assets): an electing REIT may defer corporate tax on gains realized when shareholders contribute real estate in exchange for REIT shares under the Special Tax Treatment Control Act, subject to holding-period conditions. Election 4 (international tax elections): Korea applies hybrid mismatch adjustment rules under Article 27 of the Law on Coordination of International Tax Affairs, which disallow deductions in Korea where the same payment is deductible in the counterparty jurisdiction; this operates as a conformity rule rather than a discretionary taxpayer election, and requires annual documentation of cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory economic turnover threshold exists for a Chusik Hoesa or a REIT; every business operator must register with the competent district tax office within 20 days of business commencement regardless of revenue. The simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 of annual turnover is available only to individuals and is unavailable to corporations. The REIT must therefore always maintain general taxpayer status, issue VAT tax invoices, and file quarterly VAT returns even when all rental income is derived from residential leases subject to transitional taxation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies to goods exported from the Korean customs territory upon filing and clearance of an export declaration with the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS. Services: zero-rating applies to (i) services supplied to a non-resident or a foreign corporation where the place of supply and place of actual use are outside Korea, (ii) international transportation services, and (iii) foreign-currency-earning service categories prescribed by Presidential Decree, provided payment is collected in foreign currency or in Korean currency with an approved bank record. For the Korean REIT, rental and disposal of real estate located in Korea remains subject to 10% VAT even if the counter-party is a foreign resident; however, real estate located outside Korea is outside the scope of Korean VAT, and asset-management or advisory services rendered to a foreign fund or foreign asset owner qualify as zero-rated supplies where the place of use is overseas and the consideration is received from abroad. The zero-rated export or offshore supply must be substantiated by evidence retained for at least five years from the date of supply.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) issued through the KCS UNI-PASS system with the electronic export report number
  • Commercial invoice and itemized packing list matched to the export declaration
  • Transport documents: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), cargo manifest, or courier delivery receipt evidencing departure to the overseas destination
  • Foreign exchange evidence: Certificate of Purchase of Foreign Exchange or the bank foreign exchange transaction record showing collection of the export proceeds within the statutory 18-month collection period
  • Sales contract or purchase order together with, for service exports, a service performance report, statement of the place of supply, and the foreign counterparty identification records
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice containing the zero-rate code and the export reference remarks
  • REIT-specific transaction documentation: asset management agreements, custodian records, foreign fund subscription documents, and repatriation instructions for cross-border property transactions

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all B2B supplies and all taxpayers since July 1, 2022, pursuant to Article 32 of the Value Added Tax Act. Every e-invoice must be transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system through the HomeTax portal itself, the HomeTax Open Application Programming Interface (API), or a certified e-invoice service provider, no later than the day following the supply date. Korea does not operate a Latin American style pre-clearance fiscal stamping regime; instead, the NTS performs near real-time reconciliation of e-invoices, cash receipts, and quarterly VAT returns, assigning a unique acceptance reference number to each e-invoice. For B2C receipts, business operators must issue cash receipts for cash payments over KRW 100,000, which applies to rental collections from individual tenants. The REIT must transmit monthly rental e-invoices and register new tenant supplies in the HomeTax system before the statutory issue deadline.

The NTS audits export zero-rating and refund claims by systematically cross-matching the KCS export declaration database, bank foreign exchange records, and HomeTax e-invoice data. If export proceeds are not collected within the statutory 18-month collection period, or within an approved extension granted by the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, the zero-rating is retroactively revoked and the supply is reclassified as taxable at 10% VAT, with the tax collected together with overdue interest at the statutory national tax interest rate of 3.5% per annum and general negligent nonpayment penalties. Where documentation is false or fabricated, the Framework Act on National Taxes imposes understatement penalties of 20% or 40% of the underpaid VAT depending on the degree of intention, in addition to negligent reporting penalties; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment for up to two years or a fine of up to three times the tax evaded, increased to five years and a fivefold fine for habitual offenders. The Korea Customs Service may also impose customs fines for false export declarations and suspend or revoke the exporter registration. Bank guarantees, refund freezing, and personal joint and several liability of the REIT directors and its tax representative may be ordered under Article 38 of the Framework Act on National Taxes where fraudulent refunds or zero-rating claims are detected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 3: current transactions including trade settlements, services, interest, dividends, royalties, and normal short-term banking credits are freely convertible without prior exchange-control approval, provided the remittance is executed through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank and reported to the Bank of Korea through FEIS. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into the REIT, acquisition of Korean real estate or securities by non-residents, cross-border loans, guarantees, and return-of-capital distributions, are subject to prior reporting, approval, or registration requirements. Profit remittance hurdles for a Chusik Hoesa REIT are manageable: dividends from the REIT qualify as investment income in the current account only where the foreign investor is a registered shareholder, the distribution is validly declared out of distributable profits, corporate income tax is settled, and withholding tax at the domestic 22 percent rate or treaty-reduced rate is documented. The remitting bank will require the shareholder register extract, dividend resolution, tax number, beneficial-ownership statement, and the applicable FEIS purpose code. There is no fixed KRW convertibility quota, but banks apply single-transaction thresholds, BOP statistical coding, and anti-money-laundering suspicious-transaction screening; a large or structured remittance may be held pending internal compliance clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The REIT is not an exporter and has no statutory export-proceeds collection burden. For any trading or group companies operating an export book, Korea no longer imposes a mandatory conversion ratio or a surrender-to-central-bank obligation; resident exporters may retain export earnings in foreign-currency deposit accounts. The statutory collection timeline is that export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of the export declaration under Article 6-7 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. BOK and MOEF may grant an extension for justified commercial disputes, insolvency, or receivable restructuring. Failure to collect within the deadline must be reported through the relevant foreign exchange bank and may trigger administrative fines. In practice, foreign-currency receipts should be repatriated to a domestic foreign-currency account promptly and disclosed in the periodic foreign exchange transaction report; conversion into KRW is made through the bank's market rate and is an eligible current-account conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, with Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) policy oversight, delegated FX-bank execution, and reporting through the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). The Korea Customs Service enforces border-side collection and suspicious cross-border trade settlement controls.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 million per related-party cross-border transaction for Local File/Master File requirements; KRW 50 million for intercompany services and technology transfers; KRW 100 million for intercompany financing transactions; country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue at or above KRW 100 billion for the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Yes, the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) doctrine applies in the context of the Korean modified ESR concept under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA); while Korea is not a zero-tax jurisdiction and does not impose the UAE-style ESR regime, Korean tax law requires economic substance for tax treaty benefits as a core element of the beneficial ownership analysis and general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) adopted under the OECD BEPS initiative; a Korean chusik hoesa (REIT or ordinary corporation) must demonstrate real business purpose, local employees, office, and decision-making to avoid treaty shopping and substance-based penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the amount evaded exceeds KRW 100 million), or a fine up to KRW 150 million; additional cartel surcharges increase tax up to 40%; criminal investigation referral by the NTS with public disclosure on the NTS website.

Director personal liability: Directors, representatives, and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for underpaid corporate tax, penalties, and unpaid payroll withholdings; civil restitution of evaded amounts, personal travel bans, departure restrictions, seizure of personal assets, and, upon conviction, imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for aggravated evasion).
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Loss of preferential REIT taxation (including distribution deduction), full retroactive corporate income tax recapture, denial of treaty benefits, administrative fine up to 200% of the tax benefit, business registration cancellation, and mandatory delisting from the Korea Exchange; the NCIS-PRIS or KFTC may also impose financial sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally responsible for the entity's liabilities under the Court-ordered clause if corporate formalities are ignored; the national tax collection law imposes joint liability on directors for taxes payable and penalties; criminal prosecution for violation of the REITs Act or Fraud Act and civil damages; travel bans and asset freezes in aggravated shell corporate conduct.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of 3 to 5 years; fines up to KRW 100 million; administrative penalty 20% of the value of the transaction; suspension of the entity's operational license under the Real Estate Investment Company Act; KoFIU can impose corrective action and hold management accountable; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate or conceal AML breaches are personally liable and may be imprisoned; the court can impose criminal fines and disgorgement of profits; travel restrictions during an investigation, asset seizure, and prohibition from holding licensed positions such as asset manager, compliance officer or representative director under the Financial Investment Services and Capital Markets Act.
Cross-Border Repatriation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax on gross payment recaptured at the 24.2% statutory rate, plus 25% deficiency penalty, plus a daily surcharge of 0.025%; a penalty for omission of withholding equal to 5% of the delinquent amount; additional penalties for negligence; treaty benefits can be denied and the foreign payee placed on the NTS non-compliance list.

Director personal liability: Representative directors and financial officers who signed remittance instructions are personally liable for unwithheld amounts and penalties as withholding agents; civil restitution for cross-border loss of tax revenue; travel bans, criminal prosecution for a failure to withhold and remit under the tax code; if the offshore benefit is hidden in personal accounts, personal asset forfeiture follows.

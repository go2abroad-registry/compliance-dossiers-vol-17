For a limited liability company whose industrial activity is high-precision plastic injection molding (ISIC 3240), a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No paid-up capital floor. The Korean Commercial Act does not require a minimum capital for a Yuhan Hoesa, and no national escrow account is mandated. If the articles set a stated capital, the capital must be fully subscribed and effectively paid before the court registration. Non-resident founders generally pay through a foreign-investment account, and the Korean bank issues a contribution receipt; banks may require AML source-of-funds documentation and may hold the funds in a restricted transactional account until business registration is complete.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal HomeTax at hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS single-window portal at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa is organized as a Korean domestic corporation and must register as a business operator with the NTS before commencing taxable activity. The non-resident shareholders appoint a domestic tax representative if no Korean office is open. Within 20 days of commencing business, the company files an Application for Business Registration and the corporate facts-of-establishment form with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the company's Korean head office, attaching: certificate of incorporation, Korean commercial registry certificate, articles of association, board resolution on foreign investment, financial institution account opening confirmation, and the tax representative's power of attorney. After review, the NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) which functions as the VAT ID and corporate income tax TIN. A foreign non-resident supplier with no Korean place of business but Korean VAT liability uses the HomeTax simplified registration procedure for foreign suppliers of electronic services and obtains a BRN for VAT purposes; the same BRN is used for VAT returns and zero-rating claims.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea has no check-the-box or hybrid entity election. A Yuhan Hoesa is automatically classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed on its worldwide income at the entity level; no pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold. Registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply by any business operator in Korea. A simplified taxable person regime for eligible small businesses with prior-calendar-year turnover below KRW 80 million reduces VAT administration but does not postpone or waive registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the export of goods manufactured by the Korean entity is zero-rated if the goods are the subject of an accepted customs export declaration with the Korean company as exporter of record, the goods physically depart the Republic of Korea, the export sale is reported as zero-rated on the NTS VAT return, and the exporter retains the documentary trail: accepted UNI-PASS export declaration, shipping documents, invoice/packing list, and proof of receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident buyer. Offshore digital services supplied to a non-resident for use outside Korea are also zero-rated if the place-of-supply rules and NTS non-resident evidence requirements are satisfied.

  • Accepted Export Declaration (Export Permit) issued through KCS UNI-PASS
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Korea
  • Foreign exchange bank Export Proceeds Receipt Certificate or Foreign Exchange Transaction Certificate (the Korean FIRC equivalent), supported by SWIFT credit advice/PayPal settlement report identifying the non-resident remitter and the credit to the Korean entity's bank account
  • Zero-rated e-tax invoice record (if an invoice is issued for the export transaction)
  • Finished-goods inventory and purchase/input VAT dockets for input credit substantiation

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for VAT-registered businesses and must be transmitted to the NTS through HomeTax or an NTS-approved e-document provider by the day after issuance; Korean tax invoices issued to B2B users require an NTS issuance record. There is no separate pre-clearance physical fiscal stamp; the anti-fraud control is the NTS e-tax invoice database, mandatory UNI-PASS customs declaration, bank foreign-exchange settlement matching, and automated cross-referencing of export zero-rating evidence.

The NTS may deny zero-rating and claw back any refunded input VAT if any export document is missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent. Disallowed zero-rating results in output VAT at 10% being assessed on the supply value, plus statutory interest and VAT additional taxes for incorrect reporting or false tax invoices (generally 2% of the supply value for omitted/falsely entered sales). Aggravated cases are referred under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with criminal fines/imprisonment possible. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without a permitted extension also exposes the exporter to administrative penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and may invalidate the zero-rate claim for that shipment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercising prudential oversight through the Foreign Exchange Information System. For export and import commercial settlements, payments and receipts clear without individual central bank approval, but banking channels require a transaction declaration for each remittance exceeding the statutory basket, especially for capital-account flows. Capital account transactions have been progressively liberalized and are generally handled through notification or reporting to the designated foreign exchange bank rather than requiring central bank prior approval. Inbound foreign direct investment must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or immediately after the capital is wired, and the proceeds must be parked in a named foreign-investment deposit account until capital verification is completed. Profit remittances such as dividends and retained-earning distributions are current-account payments; they are technically freely convertible, but the paying bank will demand evidence of the dividend approval, audited financial statements, tax clearance, and the investor's shareholding record. Export receipts do not have to be compulsorily sold to the central bank; the exporter can hold foreign currency in a domestic foreign-exchange bank account, but settlement and collection deadlines must be met and the inflow must be reported through FEIS. Any conversion to KRW is executed at the prevailing market exchange rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export claims must be collected and settled within the payment term stated in the underlying export sales contract, generally no later than 180 days from the date of export unless the responsible foreign exchange bank extends the deadline for documented open-account, deferred-payment or overseas consignment arrangements. There is no mandatory KRW conversion ratio for export proceeds; the effective surrender ratio is 0%, meaning the exporter may retain all foreign currency receipts in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account with a licensed foreign exchange bank or sell them to that bank at the market rate. The proceeds must be credited to a domestic foreign exchange account held by the Korean entity and the receipt must be reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System, cross-matching the exporter's customs declaration, invoice, and bank credit. Failure to settle within the approved timeline exposes the entity to fines, collection orders, and administrative impediments to future trade payments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act framework; the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercises overall foreign exchange policy supervision, and all licensed foreign exchange banks file reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low viability and materially restricted. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed banks or authorized electronic financial companies in South Korea and cannot hold KRW corporate deposits or provide local clearing membership. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, an unlicensed non-bank entity accepting Korean corporate funds would expose the treasury relationship to adverse regulatory findings. A cross-border EMI may be used only indirectly, if at all, as a payment hook for overseas e-commerce receipts through licensed money transmitter partnerships, but it cannot serve as the primary liquidity or clearing rail. The Yuhan Hoesa should open all corporate clearing and escrow relationships with a domestic tier-1 licensed commercial bank and maintain PayPal/Swift wire as receivables channels only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent or shareholder must remit via SWIFT MT103 to the bank account opened in the Yuhan Hoesa's name, using a purpose classification in the FEIS declaration that covers direct investment equity. The remitter field must match the registered foreign investor, and the payment detail field should carry the investor's foreign investment notification or registration number so the Korean bank can verify the FDI entry. Funds should be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank to the designated Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account, where the bank performs capital verification before the sums are permitted to be transferred into the operational KRW account or used for permitted business expenses. The bank will record the inward remittance, convert at the applicable market rate if a KRW injection is required, and file the transaction with FEIS under the Korean balance-of-payments classification. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.2% combined effective top marginal corporate rate (22% national Corporate Income Tax plus 10% local income tax surtax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax on nonresident dividends under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act plus 10% local income tax surtax), reduced where an applicable Korea income tax treaty provides for 5%, 10%, or 15% dividend withholding.

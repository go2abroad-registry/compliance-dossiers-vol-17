Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa; incorporators may set any capital amount in the Articles. However, the entire subscribed capital must be fully paid or fully delivered in-kind before the incorporation registration is accepted. Korean banking practice normally requires subscription funds to be deposited in a domestic provisional corporate account in the name of the company being formed, which functions as a guarded escrow arrangement; the funds may be withdrawn or transferred to the operating corporate account only after the court issues the certificate of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Final fire safety inspection and completion certificate from the district fire department under the Fire Services Act, covering extinguishers, fire alarms, sprinklers or equivalent suppression systems, evacuation routes, and storage area separation; structural load inspection and building occupancy approval under the Building Act, including elevated aseptic tanks, retort baskets, and pallet racking systems; workplace safety and health approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including KOSHA inspection for pressure vessels, boilers, conveyor line guards, and confined-space entry procedures.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Export customs declaration processing: Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity carrying on taxable business in South Korea must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number. The statutory procedure requires: (1) determining whether the foreign entity has a domestic business place through a fixed place of business, branch, office, or permanent establishment; in the absence of any domestic business place, the simplified foreign electronic-service supplier VAT registration route applies. (2) Appointing a fiscal representative/tax manager in Korea, where required, for tax service and method of administration under the Framework Act on National Taxes. (3) Preparing the foreign entity's constitutive documents (certificate of incorporation, articles of association, director identification, board resolution) with Korean translation and apostille or consular legalisation, plus evidence of any Korean domestic place of business. (4) Filing the foreign corporation domestic-place-of-business registration application (Business Registration Application) with the competent district tax office of the NTS, or electronically through HomeTax using pre-registered credentials. (5) Receiving the NTS-issued 10-digit Business Registration Number (format 000-00-00000) and Business Registration Certificate; the BRN is used for VAT/e-Tax invoice identification and all NTS filings. (6) For non-resident electronic-service suppliers, the NTS issues a simplified VAT registration identifier; reporting is through HomeTax electronic service.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold triggers registration in South Korea. Every supplier of taxable goods or services is required to register for a Business Registration Number from the commencement of a business, generally within 20 days of business commencement. A reduced 'simplified taxpayer' filing regime applies to eligible individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable supplies below KRW 80,000,000, but it does not exempt registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero percent VAT rate applies to exports of goods supplied to a foreign buyer where the goods are actually dispatched from South Korea and export-customs-cleared under the Customs Act, evidenced by the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration and a valid sea or air transport document. Services and electronically supplied digital services are zero-rated where the recipient is a foreign non-resident, the place of supply is outside South Korea, or the service is supplied for direct use or consumption outside South Korea; typical qualifying items include international transport, overseas software/services, intellectual property transferred abroad, and offshore electronic/digital services. Claiming 0% also requires the export-related foreign-exchange proceeds to be received and credited through a dealing bank in Korea. The zero-rated supplier must issue a 0% e-Tax Invoice, record the transaction in the VAT return, and may claim refund of attributable input VAT where the exporting entity is in a refund position.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) issued through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial Invoice (indicating 0% VAT) and Packing List
  • Original export contract, purchase order, or proforma invoice
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate (Foreign Currency Purchase Certificate / Exchange Receipt Confirmation) issued by the Korean dealing foreign-exchange bank
  • Zero-rated e-Tax Invoice transmitted through NTS HomeTax

VAT-registered suppliers must issue an electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) through the NTS HomeTax system. The e-Tax Invoice must be transmitted electronically to the NTS within one day after issuance; taxpayer ERP systems may integrate using HomeTax-approved APIs and open interfaces. Korea does not require a physical digital fiscal stamp on invoices; anti-fraud enforcement is achieved through mandatory e-Tax Invoice issuance, sequential invoice control numbers, HomeTax pre-filing data matching, and the electronic CASH RECEIPT system for certain B2C sales.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service will scrutinise each export zero-rating claim through documentary review, e-Tax Invoice matching, customs export data cross-checks, and bank foreign-exchange receipt verification. If the required export declaration, transport documents, or foreign-exchange receipt certificates are not substantiated, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the exporter forfeits the 0% VAT rate and is reassessed at the standard 10% VAT on that supply. The reassessment carries penalty tax at the statutory rates prescribed by the Framework Act on National Taxes, including aggravated penalty tax for fraudulent refund claims and refund clawbacks with national-tax interest. Any falsified export declaration, forged foreign-exchange certificate, or counterfeit e-Tax Invoice may expose the entity and its responsible officers to criminal penalties under the Customs Act, the Value-Added Tax Act, and the Framework Act on National Taxes, including imprisonment, fines, and debarment from simplified export compliance procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised current-account convertibility framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree. The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for genuine current-account transactions such as import settlements, service fees, royalties, freight, and ordinary business operating remittances, provided the transaction is supported by an underlying real contract and is reported electronically through a licensed foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Information and Statistics Reporting System. Capital-account transactions are not automatically free: inbound foreign direct investment must be declared under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before remittance is made, and the funds must be routed into the Korean entity through a designated foreign-exchange bank for capital verification. Outbound loans, guarantees, securities investment, and certain debt transactions are subject to ex-ante or ex-post reporting thresholds. Profit remittances by a foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa are generally permitted as non-restricted current outward remittances, but the entity must prove that the original foreign investment was validly reported, that capital verification was completed, that dividends were properly declared by a board resolution, that corporate tax withholding obligations were satisfied, and in some cases that a National Tax Service tax clearance certificate is available. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds, but the banking channel must record each collection and ensure the export is closed within the statutory settlement period.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and standard foreign-exchange bank conditions, export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of the export declaration, unless the exporter obtains a documented extension from its foreign-exchange bank due to genuine overseas collection delays. There is no mandatory fixed-percentage conversion or surrender obligation; the exporter may keep the foreign currency in a licensed domestic foreign-currency account or convert it into Korean won at market rates. Each receipt must be matched to the underlying export customs declaration and reported through the bank for foreign-exchange statistics. Failure to collect export proceeds within the statutory period without a valid extension may trigger fines, administrative penalties, and enhanced regulatory scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank and monetary authority; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) administers the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and sets foreign-exchange policy, while licensed domestic foreign-exchange banks execute and report transactions on behalf of corporate clients.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as a primary banking, treasury, or clearing solution for a Korean-resident corporate entity. Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only a financial institution licensed in South Korea can open a KRW demand-deposit account, handle foreign-exchange intermediation, submit statutory foreign-exchange reports, and provide trade settlement instruments such as letters of credit. An overseas digital EMI does not hold a Korean banking licence, cannot access the Korean financial clearing network, cannot hold corporate deposits as a deposit-taking institution, and cannot legally service the entity's domestic payroll, tax payments, VAT settlements, or KRW liquidity needs. Cross-border fintechs may be considered only as ancillary receivable collection tools when they are operated through a licensed Korean partner bank, while the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its governing liquidity accounts and all FX compliance at a domestic tier-1 bank. Viability rating: legally restricted and practically unsuitable as a primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Yuhan Hoesa must be injected through a licensed domestic foreign-exchange bank. The foreign shareholders must first complete the Foreign Investment Report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; the bank will register the investment and issue a foreign investor identification number. The remittance is initiated from the foreign shareholder's bank account through SWIFT into the entity's temporary foreign-investment capital verification account in KRW or a freely convertible currency such as USD or EUR, with the remittance narrative clearly labelled as direct equity investment and including the Foreign Investment Report number. The receiving Korean bank verifies the incoming drawing against the domestic and offshore records, performs capital verification, and issues the Foreign Investment Certificate. Only after capital verification may the funds be converted to KRW and transferred into the operating current account. All transfers must use direct bank-to-bank routing without shell accounts or third-party intermediaries, and the foreign-exchange purpose code must be recorded as direct investment equity participation in a domestic enterprise.Outbound trade payments for raw materials such as cassava starch, sugar, packaging, and production machinery should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers, or through documentary collections and irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the Korean bank under UCP600 for larger or transactional counterparties. Each outbound remittance must be supported by the underlying import contract, pro-forma invoice, and customs declaration, and the bank will file the required foreign-exchange statistics report. Export receivables from overseas boba distributors should be collected by SWIFT MT103 into the entity's foreign-currency account, matched with the corresponding Korean customs export declarations, and either held in foreign currency for permitted trade payments or converted to KRW discretely. The banking infrastructure permits cross-currency settlement through the domestic FX bank, and large recurring export flows can be supported with export bill negotiation, export bills purchased, or post-shipment trade finance instruments. All settlement rails must remain inside the licensed banking system; direct netting outside Korea is not permissible for KRW liquidity purposes.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with a domestic Korean bank, permitted only when the Yuhan Hoesa has an underlying current-account exposure from confirmed trade receivables or payables, with the forward notional and tenor matched to the documented invoice and collection cycle.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts, permitted for hedging genuine KRW exposure where a deliverable forward is unavailable or impractical, subject to the Korean bank's real-demand documentation policy and its internal NDF limit; speculative NDF positions by a domestic exporter are not permitted.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps, permitted only when the company has an underlying long-term foreign-currency debt, investment, or dividend remittance exposure; the swap must have a physical underlying exposure, must not embed speculative leverage, and must be reported through the foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.No leveraged option structures or binary FX products are acceptable for a low-risk manufacturing treasury; plain-vanilla puts and calls are allowed only when maturities, premiums, and strike prices reflect genuine hedging of the boba manufacturing export and import payment cycle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as a primary banking, treasury, or clearing solution for a Korean-resident corporate entity. Under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only a financial institution licensed in South Korea can open a KRW demand-deposit account, handle foreign-exchange intermediation, submit statutory foreign-exchange reports, and provide trade settlement instruments such as letters of credit. An overseas digital EMI does not hold a Korean banking licence, cannot access the Korean financial clearing network, cannot hold corporate deposits as a deposit-taking institution, and cannot legally service the entity's domestic payroll, tax payments, VAT settlements, or KRW liquidity needs. Cross-border fintechs may be considered only as ancillary receivable collection tools when they are operated through a licensed Korean partner bank, while the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its governing liquidity accounts and all FX compliance at a domestic tier-1 bank. Viability rating: legally restricted and practically unsuitable as a primary clearing rail.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods actually delivered out of Korea, overseas services (including digital content where the place of supply is outside Korea), international transport services, and other supplies expressly enumerated under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act; the supplier must substantiate the export with Customs Service declaration, shipping documentation, and proof that consideration was received from an offshore customer in foreign currency through Korean foreign exchange banks; offshore B2B digital content and video match services qualify when the destination and actual consumption occur outside Korea, evidenced by customer IP/geo-location logs, subscriber country of residence, and payment origin.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Declaration Confirmation/Bill of Entry equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) evidencing physical or deemed cross-border shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate) issued by the Korean designated foreign exchange bank evidencing foreign currency repatriation

Mandatory real-time electronic tax invoice issuance under Article 32 of the Korean VAT Act: billers must issue e-tax invoices through HomeTax or an NTS-approved ERP service provider, transmit them to the NTS no later than the next business day after the tax supply date, include the NTS-issued digital signature and verification code, and cooperate with NTS automated risk assessment; there is no fiscal stamping, but the transmission itself constitutes fiscal clearance for VAT deduction claims.

Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims are disallowed and recaptured along with interest, and additional penalties under Articles 25-2 and 29-3 of the VAT Act impose 40% of the evaded VAT for false returns; reckless or negligent misdeclaration triggers 20% penalties; criminal prosecution may follow under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for willful tax fraud, and retrospective NTS reassessment can extend to 10 years for tax evasion cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalised current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. KRW is freely convertible for legitimate trade, service, remittance and dividend payment once a foreign exchange declaration and standard purpose code are captured by a licensed foreign exchange bank. Capital-account transactions, however, are not fully deregulated: foreign direct investment into a Korean stock corporation requires a foreign investment report and bank-issued certificate of registration; intra-group loans, cross-border bond issuance and overseas securities transactions must be reported to the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Bank of Korea with a demonstrable underlying economic purpose. Profit remittance is generally permitted after the company completes corporate tax filing and presents audited or internally reviewed financial statements, but the settlement bank will independently check outstanding local taxes, transfer-pricing substance and the existence of the operating revenue streams. For a digital social-app entity dependent on Apple IAP and Paymentwall, cross-border inflows must be classified as service export receipts, not capital remittances, to prevent the erroneous application of capital-market restrictions. The foreign exchange bank statement should match the corporate revenue ledger, and each payout should be traceable to a signed merchant settlement report.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not impose a mandatory percentage conversion of export proceeds into KRW. However, exporters must repatriate export foreign currency receipts to a foreign exchange account held with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank within six months of the shipment date for goods or the service completion date for services, unless an extension has been granted by the Bank of Korea for documented business reasons. Once repatriated, the company may retain, convert or sell those funds freely through the licensed bank. For the video-matching service, the executed Apple Developer Agreement and Paymentwall Merchant Agreement, together with monthly payout statements and settlement reconciliations, must be retained as export evidence. Each USD credit to the corporate foreign currency account must be matched against the corresponding payout report and booked under the applicable service-receivables purpose code for foreign exchange reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI or neobank platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business or similar are not viable as the primary settlement banking layer for a Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa. Operating electronic money services, KRW IBAN-equivalent accounts and foreign exchange mediation within South Korea requires licensing under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and registration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. None of the prominent foreign fintech EMIs possess such a licence to service domestic entities as a full-KRW current account provider. They may only be used as an auxiliary disbursement gateway if the licensed Korean Tier-1 bank enables the onward payment through its own approved international payment network. Unlicensed collection of KRW cross-border payments for a Korean video-based social app could be characterised as illegal foreign exchange brokerage and expose the treasury workflow to regulatory reversal and criminal prosecution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders will inject equity through a registered foreign investment declaration filed with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. The SWIFT MT103 or MT202 instruction must carry the prescribed purpose code for foreign direct investment capital acquisition and identify the Korean entity as beneficiary. Funds are initially credited to a dedicated capital escrow account and cannot be commingled with operating revenue. After the bank verifies the foreign investment report, the certificate of foreign investment registration is issued and the funds may be converted into KRW at the current market rate without any mandatory surrender. The company must then complete capital verification through the bank and register the paid-in capital with the Korean corporate registry. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate international related-party transactions for Local File documentation; KRW 100 billion consolidated group revenue for Master File and CbC Report threshold.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies substance-over-form under Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, permitting recharacterization or disregard of transactions lacking commercial rationale and economic substance. For treaty benefits, Korea applies a principal purpose test and requires core income-generating activities to be performed in Korea, including employees, premises, and decision-making functions. Although Korea does not have the Gulf ESR regime, a paper Chusik Hoesa with no domestic CIGA will be denied DTA relief and treated as a transparent or sham conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 and Corporate Income Tax Act Article 76-4: imprisonment up to 2 years (up to 7 years for aggravated evasion with cross-border concealment) and fines up to three times the evaded tax; administrative 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3; overdue interest of 0.022% per day.

Director personal liability: If corporate assets are insufficient, NTS pierces the veil under National Tax Collection Act Article 72 and holds directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties. Directors may be issued no-departure orders, blacklisted as responsible officers, have personal bank accounts seized, and be imprisoned for willful participation in the evasion scheme.
Shell Entity and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 4 permits NTS to disregard the shell, attribute income to the actual non-resident enterprise, and deny treaty benefits; the company is reassessed at full statutory 22% WHT plus 20% underreporting penalty, and the foreign shareholder is treated as maintaining a Korean PE without appropriate registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed corporate taxes and unwithheld amounts. They may be additionally charged under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for false declarations, subjected to travel bans, and face civil restitution of all distributed profits and compensation received from the shell.
AML Non-Compliance and Money Laundering Through Video Chat App

Statutory sanction: Act on Reporting and Using Specified Financial Transaction Information (FTRA) Article 50-5: fine up to KRW 50 million per violation and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful AML evasion; criminal proceeds are subject to asset forfeiture and confiscation under Criminal Act Article 48.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual knowledge or willful blindness are personally arrested, criminally indicted, and may face asset seizure and confiscation. The corporate veil is pierced for restitution obligations, including disgorgement of dividends, salary, and profits obtained through the tainted payment flows; directors are also subject to international travel bans and extradition cooperation restrictions.

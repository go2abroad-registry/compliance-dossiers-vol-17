Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None (no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa in Korea). The obligation is simply that the consideration for issued shares be actually and fully paid or contributed before incorporation, typically by depositing the subscribed amount into a designated bank account in the name of the promoter(s) or a special incorporation account. The paid-up amount may be as low as KRW 1 per share and need not be blocked after incorporation; however, amounts deposited before incorporation are released only upon presentation of the registration certificate or a certificate of deposit, and the company may freely use the funds once registered. Foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act must file a foreign-investment report with KOTRA before payment if the investment exceeds KRW 100 million or involves foreign exchange acquisition.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — no factory building, warehouse or industrial workplace, so no Civil Defense fire safety certificate for a factory, no structural load approval, and no workplace permit under the Occupational Safety and Health Act is mandated. Should the company open an office of greater than 1,000 square meters or bring a multi-story building under the Act for software work, it would obtain a fire resistance rating and workplace safety report from the National Fire Agency (소방청) and the relevant local fire station; but under the present pure digital/consulting classification, all physical industrial clearance items are Not Applicable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; primary electronic portal is HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); customs and export clearance are administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa, file corporate business commencement registration with the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over the registered business address within 20 days from commencement. Access HomeTax using a corporate certificate and submit the business registration application, accompanied by the representative director's identification, articles of incorporation, lease or ownership proof of the place of business, and board resolution if required. The NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) that functions as the TIN, VAT registration number, and corporate withholding tax account. Foreign non-resident entities with a domestic place of business must file a foreign corporation business registration with the same district tax office to obtain a BRN. Foreign non-resident electronic service suppliers without a domestic place of business must register through the NTS simplified foreign e-service supplier registration procedure via HomeTax, appoint a Korean tax representative, and obtain a separate foreign operator VAT registration number. Export-active companies must additionally obtain a customs corporate code from the Korea Customs Service.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 (no monetary threshold): every person making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT upon business commencement. Individual small-business suppliers with annual revenue below KRW 80,000,000 may elect simplified taxpayer treatment, but a Chusik Hoesa corporation is ineligible and must register as a general VAT taxpayer. Foreign electronic service suppliers (including MMORPG platform and in-app purchase sellers) must VAT-register in Korea regardless of turnover under the NTS simplified foreign electronic service supplier registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated when physical export is evidenced by a Korea Customs Service export declaration. Services and intangible digital content, including MMORPG global access and in-app purchases delivered through Apple App Store and Google Play distributions, qualify for zero-rating when the recipient is a non-resident, the service is actually used outside Korea, the consideration is paid in foreign currency, and the foreign-exchange proceeds are received and repatriated to Korea. The foreign-exchange earning condition is substantiated by app-store payout statements, bank remittance records, and the corporate accounts showing export consideration. Input VAT paid on costs attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable and refundable if it generates an excess VAT credit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the Korean export clearance certificate / export bill of entry equivalent) issued by the Korea Customs Service for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation evidencing the arrival of export proceeds in Korea
  • Commercial invoice and export contract identifying the foreign purchaser, product scope, and payment terms
  • Apple App Store and Google Play payout/settlement reports together with user geolocation and transaction logs for digital zero-rated supplies
  • Foreign exchange transaction report from the remitting bank or the Foreign Exchange Information System confirming timely repatriation

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for B2B supplies and must be transmitted to the National Tax Service through HomeTax no later than the day after issuance. The NTS operates open APIs for machine-to-machine transmission, and accepts e-invoices after cryptographically validating the Korean business registration number, supply value, VAT amount, and digital signature; an accepted e-invoice carries an NTS validation number that acts as the anti-fraud stamp. Korea does not impose pre-clearance of each invoice before issuance, but the HomeTax system functions as the central fiscal repository and enables real-time risk matching and B2C cash-receipt verification. Failure to issue or transmit an e-invoice in time triggers an additional tax amount equal to 2% of the untaxed supply value.

Zero-rating is closely audited by the NTS through cross-matching with Korea Customs Service export declarations, bank remittance data, foreign exchange transaction reports, and app-store settlement files. If the required documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or missing, the 0% rate is retrospectively clawed back and VAT at 10% is assessed on the supply base, plus additional tax for underreporting and for failure to issue/transmit e-invoices. For fraudulent or intentionally unverifiable export zero-rating, the NTS may impose a penalty up to 40% of the VAT evaded, apply the 10-year retrospective assessment period for fraud under the Framework Act on National Taxes, initiate criminal prosecution for tax evasion, and the exporter may receive parallel administrative sanctions under the Customs Act and the Foreign Trade Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal current-account regime but a materially disciplined capital-account framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Presidential Decree. The Korean won is not a freely traded offshore currency; inward and outward capital flows require the involvement of a licensed foreign-exchange bank and, for transactions above certain thresholds, a report to the Bank of Korea or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Current-account payments for royalties, gaming platform commissions and services are generally allowed with documentation, yet the bank is obligated to screen the economic substance of the invoice. Capital-account operations such as equity contributions, loans and acquisitions require a more granular declaration path; non-resident shareholders may inject capital without prior approval in many cases but must route settlement through a domestic local bank and maintain the relevant certificate. Profit remittance to the foreign owner is treated as dividend payment: it is permissible after corporate tax is settled and the foreign-exchange certificate of inward capital is presented, with withholding taxes levied where a treaty rate applies. There is no surrender requirement that forces conversion of export proceeds into KRW; exporters may keep foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts, but settlement of exports and all receipts must be recorded for Bank of Korea statistics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within 15 months from the export declaration date, unless the exporter obtains a permitted extension or maintains a foreign-currency account with a Korean bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; however, if foreign currency is withdrawn or converted, the transaction must be reported to the main foreign-exchange bank and the proceeds are required to be repatriated into a domestic foreign-currency account rather than held indefinitely overseas.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with Korean Customs Service for cross-border declaration monitoring

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms cannot lawfully act as the principal operating bank for a Korean entity; they are not permitted to provide KRW deposit accounts or direct settlement under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Such providers may merely facilitate instructions through a licensed Korean bank, and the bank must still perform full KYC on the Chusik Hoesa. Therefore, for core treasury operations, offshore EMIs are not substitutable for a tier-1 Korean commercial bank and their cross-border viability is Low.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation are mandatory when intercompany cross-border transactions with a foreign related party exceed KRW 10,000,000,000 per business year; a Master File is required for the consolidated business group with total group revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion; Country-by-Country Report is due for top-tier Korean group entities when the consolidated revenue of the ultimate parent group equals or exceeds KRW 1 trillion (and is exchanged automatically under BEPS Action 13)

Economic substance scope: Korea has no separate Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the Cayman regime, but the OECD CIGA analysis is applied in transfer-pricing adjustments and in denying treaty benefits where the recipient has no activation, management, or founders executing development and publishing decisions. The Korean International Tax Adjustment Act and the OECD/UN commentary recognise substance-over-form: shell instructional entities that ring-fence MMORPG IP would not obtain DTA benefits and may be deemed to lack beneficial ownership. The Code of Practice for International Tax Adjustment Act additionally applies the 'place of effective management' test to determine corporate residence and to attack artificially arranged CIGA distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal Fraudulent Filing)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 11-2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, defrauding the tax authorities is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine of up to 3 times the tax evaded; in addition, the corrected tax is increased by 40% of the understated net amount as an erroneous-report penalty (Article 48 of the Framework Act on National Taxes), plus daily late-payment interest

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the director personally signed falsified quarterly reports or used the corporate bank account to transmit evaded profits; civil liability extends to restitution of the tax shortfall and interest from the director's personal assets; criminal conviction produces public blacklisting, a travel ban with involvement of the Immigration Service, and removal from board positions; a sentence of 5 years or more triggers mandatory deportation risk for non-citizen directors
Transfer-Pricing Erosion / Sham Intercompany Transactions (Aggressive Tax Avoidance)

Statutory sanction: Article 4 of the International Tax Adjustment Act authorises the National Tax Service to re-determine the taxpayer's income and tax base based on the arm's-length principle; a penalty of 20% of the under-reported net tax (or 30% if the evasion is deemed intentional bet-the-company) applies under the Framework Act provisions; additionally a foreign recipient is treated as a permanent establishment in Korea on arbitrary basis when the substance tests cannot be rebutted

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham pricing may be jointly liable for the tax and penalties if the company has been stripped of distributable assets; the court may apply the 'beneficial owner' denial to disallow the reduced treaty rate, exposing the director to an assessment for failure to withhold (WHT plus officer penalty); in extreme cases, intentional use of fabricated documents in an audit leads to criminal charges of obstruction of taxation
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse (Anti-Money Laundering and DTA Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The Korean Taxing Authority applies the Principal Purpose Test under Article 7 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (implementing MLI Article 29) to deny treaty benefits, resulting in full 22% withholding plus a 20% erroneous-reporting penalty on the under-withheld amount; the Financial Services Commission's AML sanctions under the Financial Transactions Reports Act impose fines up to KRW 500 million for failure to identify and report beneficial-owner information; the non-resident entity may be blacklisted by the National Tax Service for real ineffective compliance

Director personal liability: The director who styled the shell as a beneficial owner is a 'de facto director' of the Korean business if the offshore entity has no staff and the director manages all affairs from Seoul; civil isolation and recovery of the tax debt from the director's assets applies under Article 42 of the Framework Act. The framers can also face criminal liability for submitting a false certificate of residence and for aiding tax evasion - punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment; a travel ban persists until the tax liability and criminal warrant are resolved

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Chusik Hoesa carrying on long-haul bonded trucking under TIR, the Korean National Tax Service and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport require a verifiable physical registered office. A virtual office or commercial mailbox will be rejected because (1) the customs broker license for TIR requires site inspection and customs sealing control at a physical address; (2) the Regional Tax Office demands documentary evidence of an actual office (lease contract, electricity bill, telephone line) for VAT registration; and (3) financial institutions for SWIFT wire and fuel card clearing mandate local branch contact and physical inspection before opening business accounts. Hence virtual office permissibility is very low and should be treated as impermissible.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic filing through the NTS Home Tax Service (Hometax) online portal at hometax.go.kr; customs and TIR matters are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For the Korean Chusik Hoesa, the legal representative must submit a business registration application to the competent district tax office of the NTS, or electronically through the NTS Home Tax Service, within 20 days of business commencement. The application must be accompanied by the Commercial Court corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of the Korean place of business, shareholders and director details, and, where a non-resident shareholder is present, the foreign entity certificate of incorporation with apostille and the appointment of a local tax representative. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the unified corporate income tax and VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity not forming a Korean domestic corporation but carrying on a taxable business through a Korean place of business must complete the Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration procedure at the relevant NTS district office before starting business and obtain the same unified 10-digit TIN/VAT identifier. A foreign non-resident entity with no Korean place of business but making taxable digital or offshore supplies to Korean customers may obtain a simplified VAT registration identification through the NTS Home Tax Service; this electronic registration is used only for VAT filing and does not create domestic income tax nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Chusik Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation liable to entity-level Korean corporate income tax; there is no pass-through or check-the-box election under Korean tax law. A wholly-owned domestic subsidiary group may elect consolidated corporate tax filing under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, but this does not alter the entity-level VAT or corporate tax identity. Hybrid mismatch recharacterization is mandatory under the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs; it is not an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration turnover threshold applies. A Chusik Hoesa engaged in taxable transport services must register for VAT before making its first taxable supply, even if turnover is below any simplified taxpayer numerical threshold. Simplified taxpayer relief in the VAT Act is limited to certain individual traders and does not apply to a corporation in the long-haul bonded freight sector.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21(1) of the Value-Added Tax Act for exported goods and services performed in a foreign country. For the TIR cross-border trucking scope, the Korean Chusik Hoesa may apply 0% VAT on international road freight of goods that are exported from a Korean customs office to a foreign destination, or imported from a foreign customs office into Korea, provided the carriage is performed under the TIR Convention, the goods are covered by a KCS electronic export declaration, the commercial contract identifies the international route and non-resident customer, and the freight foreign-exchange proceeds are collected and evidenced. The domestic leg of the transport can be zero-rated only when it is supplied as one integrated international carriage contract; separately billed domestic repositioning or feeder mileage remains standard-rated at 10%. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied by the entity to non-residents are also zero-rated when performed outside Korea, but that category is outside the trucking scope.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export declaration (export clearance certificate, functionally equivalent to an export bill of entry) with the assigned export declaration number
  • TIR Carnet with the Korean customs office of departure stamp, border crossing stamps, and destination customs termination voucher
  • CMR consignment note, the road-transport equivalent of the bill of lading, signed by consignor, carrier, and consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the exported goods
  • International road freight contract between the Korean Chusik Hoesa and the non-resident consignor, consignee, or freight forwarder clearly identifying the cross-border route and freight consideration
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt, SWIFT credit advice, or Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (FIRC) evidencing collection of the export freight proceeds

All VAT invoices must be issued as electronic tax invoices and transmitted to the NTS through the Home Tax Service electronic invoice system by no later than the day after issuance. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamp or pre-clearance invoice authorisation for road freight; integrity is enforced through NTS e-invoice matching, compulsory electronic tax invoice data transmission, and KCS UNI-PASS export and import clearance records. TIR Carnet movements are electronically monitored by the Korea Customs Service.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service will cross-check the UNI-PASS export declaration, TIR Carnet stamps, CMR waybill, transport contract, and foreign-exchange remittance evidence before accepting 0% VAT treatment. If zero-rating is claimed without valid export clearance, TIR movement proof, or FX repatriation within the prescribed period, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply; the NTS will assess 10% VAT on the full freight consideration and collect additional penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act, which can reach up to 40% of the unreported or underreported tax in cases of fraudulent non-reporting, plus late-payment interest and retroactive recharacterization of VAT positions. False or fraudulent entries on the UNI-PASS declaration or TIR Carnet also expose the entity to penal provisions of the Customs Duty Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including fines, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment of responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a highly liberalised current-account and progressively open capital-account convertibility regime, but significant administrative controls remain for cross-border capital movements. The KRW is fully convertible for trade settlement and service payments, provided each transaction is routed through a resident authorised foreign exchange bank and correctly coded under the Foreign Exchange Information System. Export proceeds are subject to a streamlined surrender declaration, requiring collection within statutory settlement deadlines and sale or deposit through domestic foreign exchange banks. Profit remittances by foreign-invested Korean corporations are generally permitted once audited reserves and tax clearances are demonstrated, but dividend remittances require the parent beneficial owner to maintain a valid Foreign Investment Notification with MOEF, supported by board minutes, audited retained earnings, and bank evidence of paid-in capital. An entity engaged in TIR bonded cross-border trucking will routinely move invoices in KRW, USD, and EUR, making it important to classify every wire with the appropriate standard transaction codes so that customs, trade, and FX reporting remain consistent. Overall, Korea is not a frontier controlled-economy regime, yet treasury operations must obey strict reporting, prior-declaration, and bank-intermediary routing obligations to avoid inadvertently triggering anti-money-laundering penalties or foreign exchange administrative fines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receivables must be collected and settled through a domestic authorised foreign exchange bank within the contractual settlement period, generally not later than six months from the export declaration or shipment date. Resulting foreign currency must be sold or converted into KRW unless a specific exemption is granted for hedging, offsetting, reinvestment, or approved foreign-exchange operating expenses; effectively, a 100% sale application applies to undistributed export earnings absent an expressly permitted foreign-currency retention licence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Financial Services Commission (FSC) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, non-bank foreign EMIs may not independently operate multi-currency settlement accounts for a Korean-resident corporate entity without obtaining a foreign exchange business registration from the Financial Services Commission and establishing local bank sponsorship. Fintech platforms such as general card-only cross-border providers are legally barred from becoming the primary clearing conduit for export or import proceeds of a licensed TIR carrier. Their acceptable role is limited to back-office automation, fuel-card data reconciliation, expense control, and API-based payment initiation that ultimately settles through an authorised Korean commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed via a Korean authorised foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Notification to MOEF/KOTRA. The investor opens a foreign-currency corporate account and a domestic KRW account for the FDI proceeds. Wires must carry the correct Foreign Exchange Information System purpose code, with the bank certifying capital impact and issuing a Certificate of Foreign Investment Notification. Capital verification should be performed after each injection by reconciling the Bank of Korea electronic declaration, bank credit advice, and statutory books. Any shareholder loan to the Korean entity must additionally be reported as an external debt transaction under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.For diesel, fuel, tolls, subcarrier payments, customs duties, and transit guarantees, the recommended clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 high-value wires for time-critical operational disbursements, documentary collections for aligning payment with transport documents, and standby or performance letters of credit when the TIR carrier must secure a customs bond. For regular transit partners, an accounts-payable automation layer may be routed behind the SWIFT rail, but it must not replace the bank-mandated settlement channel. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, non-bank foreign EMIs may not independently operate multi-currency settlement accounts for a Korean-resident corporate entity without obtaining a foreign exchange business registration from the Financial Services Commission and establishing local bank sponsorship. Fintech platforms such as general card-only cross-border providers are legally barred from becoming the primary clearing conduit for export or import proceeds of a licensed TIR carrier. Their acceptable role is limited to back-office automation, fuel-card data reconciliation, expense control, and API-based payment initiation that ultimately settles through an authorised Korean commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national Corporate Income Tax (CIT) plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national CIT, producing a combined aggregate top marginal rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national corporate withholding tax plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national withholding tax, giving an aggregate statutory withholding burden of 22% on dividend distributions made by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder. The rate under most Korean double tax treaties is reduced to 10-15%, with 5% frequently available for qualified substantial corporate shareholders (for example, a 10% or greater capital interest under the Korea-United States or Korea-United Kingdom treaties).

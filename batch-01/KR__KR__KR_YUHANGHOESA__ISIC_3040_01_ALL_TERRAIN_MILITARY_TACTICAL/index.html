Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; however, because the entity will seek designation as a defense contractor under the Defense Acquisition Program Act, DAPA requires proof of financial soundness, a three-year business plan, and a bank balance certificate. No blocked escrow account is statutorily mandated for general incorporation, but subscriber contributions must be placed in a provisional capital subscription account until the court issues the certificate of registered matters, after which the funds are released to the company’s operating account.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must obtain a Fire Safety Certification issued by the National Fire Agency after inspection of automatic fire suppression systems, hydrants, extinguishers, and smoke exhaust ventilation, together with a Civil Defense shelter approval if the factory employs more than 300 workers. Structural load inspections by a licensed structural engineering firm must certify that floors can support concentrated armored plate storage, welding fixtures, and ballistic impact test rigs. Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency (KOSHA) clearance is required under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, confirming permissible exposure limits for welding fumes, organic solvents, and noise. If any explosives or ammunition are handled, a separate permit from the local police chief must be obtained under the Safety Control of Firearms, Ammunition and Explosives Act.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; however, because the entity will seek designation as a defense contractor under the Defense Acquisition Program Act, DAPA requires proof of financial soundness, a three-year business plan, and a bank balance certificate. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general minimum turnover threshold triggers registration; every person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT before commencing business. A Simplified Taxpayer regime may apply for businesses with annual revenue below KRW 80,000,000, but VAT registration remains obligatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT where (1) the goods are sold to a foreign buyer and physically exported from Korea; (2) a lawful export declaration is filed and accepted by Korea Customs Service; (3) the exporter retains proof of shipment, customs clearance, and foreign exchange repatriation; and (4) overseas payment is received in the contracted currency through a foreign exchange bank or diplomatic letter of credit. Qualifying exported services, including certain offshore digital/B2B services supplied to non-resident users for use outside Korea, may also be zero-rated if supported by invoicing and foreign exchange evidence demonstrating the overseas supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / export clearance certificate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods out of Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign exchange settlement receipt proving receipt of export proceeds

Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) issuance is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses. Issuers must transmit e-Tax Invoice data electronically to the NTS HomeTax system by the day following the supply date. No physical fiscal stamps or pre-clearance approval API is used; however, NTS applies real-time digital risk scoring and post-filing cross-validation. Cash transactions to unregistered consumers are also recorded through the mandatory Cash Receipt System.

If zero-rated export treatment is challenged and the exporter fails to substantiate customs clearance or foreign exchange repatriation, the NTS will reassess VAT at 10% on the export supply. Penalties include an additional tax of 10% of the underpaid VAT for simple underreporting, 20% for gross negligence, and up to 40% for intentional fraudulent declarations, plus a delinquency charge of 0.03% per day on unpaid amounts. Korea Customs Service may also impose penalties for false export declarations. Criminal sanctions may be brought under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for fraudulent export zero-rating, including retroactive clawback of any VAT credits or refunds received.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won is generally convertible for current-account transactions including trade in goods, services, and routine profit remittances, subject to documentary verification and reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System. For capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections, loans, and cross-border guarantees, Korea maintains a nominal free-float regime but applies a prior-declaration or approval framework for certain categories under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. Export proceeds in foreign currency must be collected, deposited, and either surrendered or converted into KRW within a statutory period, typically within 180 days from the export date, with mandatory conversion obligations depending on the currency and settlement terms; if the export proceeds are held in a foreign currency account at a domestic bank, the bank must verify the underlying export documentation and report the transaction. Capital contributions by non-residents require filing of an acquisition of securities report with the Bank of Korea or a designated foreign exchange bank, and the foreign currency must be converted into KRW through a domestic bank before the capital verification certificate is issued. Profit remittances abroad require evidence of retained earnings audited by a registered accounting firm, payment of corporate/withholding taxes, and a remittance report; stringent AML/KYC review by the receiving bank may further delay execution. Surrender of export proceeds and repatriation rules are treated strictly, and any deviation can trigger foreign exchange violation penalties, customs reconciliation audits, and tax investigation. Overall, the regime is functional and transparent for compliant entities, but defense-sector receipts from foreign government clients will attract extra scrutiny regarding end-use certification, sanctioned-party screening, and dual-use or military goods export licensing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within 180 days from the date of exportation. The mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds is effectively 100% if the exporter intends to use KRW domestically, but the exporter may maintain foreign currency proceeds in a domestic Foreign Currency Deposit Account; however, for those proceeds to be used in domestic payments or to satisfy capital verification, they must be sold to a domestic bank at the market rate and reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System within the prescribed timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority; the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance's Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act govern the approval, declaration, and reporting framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or similar offshore digital banks have low to negligible viability as a primary treasury rail for this Korean defense-tactical manufacturing entity because Korea restricts foreign exchange banking services to domestic licensed financial institutions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. A non-resident digital EMI cannot legally open a won-denominated account directly in the name of the Korean LLC without passing through a domestic bank partnership; moreover, defense-related ISIC 3040 cargo-vehicle manufacturing triggers Defense Acquisition Program Administration licensing, and the banking AML risk tier is High_Risk_Special_License, which requires physical presence of authorized signatories and submission of original certificates of incorporation, statutory director ID, and business license. Digital EMI rails can only be used as ancillary receivables-collection or supplier-payment gateways in limited low-risk jurisdictions outside Korea, but they are not automatically compliant for the mandatory FEIS declarations that domestic treasury operations require.

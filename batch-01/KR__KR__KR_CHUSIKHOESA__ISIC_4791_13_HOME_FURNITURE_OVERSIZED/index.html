If the target market is also the United States or EU, an additional brick-and-mortar office is not required in Korea, but the Korean registered office must meet the 'place of actual management' standard.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, all shares subscribed at incorporation must be fully paid before registration. The subscription funds must be deposited into a dedicated blocked capital subscription escrow account maintained with a licensed financial institution in Korea in the name of the corporation under formation, and the depositary bank must issue a capital payment certificate and deposit balance confirmation that will be attached to the registration application submitted to the court registry.

Funding & unlock path: The warehouse and any retail distribution premise must obtain a fire safety completion inspection and, where applicable, a fire protection civil defense permit under the Fire Prevention, Installation, Maintenance and Safety Management Act and National Fire Agency regulations. The facility must be equipped with automatic fire detection and alarm systems, sprinklers where required by building area and storage height, fire doors, emergency lighting, exit signage and portable fire extinguishers, and the operator must appoint a fire safety manager when the gross floor area or employee count exceeds the statutory threshold. A civil defense shelter-use approval must be obtained if the building or site is designated in the local civil defense plan. The structural integrity of pallet racks and mezzanine storage used for oversized furniture must be certified by a licensed structural engineer, and the workplace must pass the occupational safety certification imposed by the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including manual lifting control measures, powered forklift inspection and licensing, rack load limitation signage, safety training, emergency response drills and prevention of musculoskeletal disorders.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the electronic Home Tax Service (Hometax) portal at www.hometax.go.kr; import VAT and customs duties are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) in South Korea: (1) incorporate the company with the Seoul District Court or competent local court and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation and corporate seal certificate; (2) apply for Business Registration with the local District Tax Office by submitting the articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, proof of Korean business premises, power of attorney, and certified foreign incorporation documents with apostille or consular legalization; (3) obtain the statutory 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as both the corporate TIN and VAT registration number; (4) activate Hometax e-Tax Invoice access and, if exports are intended, register with Korea Customs Service for export trader status under UNI-PASS. If the foreign entity instead has no Korean fixed place of business but supplies electronic services or goods through a Korean marketplace, NTS requires non-resident simplified VAT registration through Hometax with a Korean tax representative, except where a marketplace withholding or platform VAT rule shifts the obligation; this is completed online with foreign incorporation evidence and the representative appointment deed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies to a Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa; any person making taxable supplies of goods or services must register with the competent District Tax Office before commencing business, irrespective of turnover. A foreign non-resident supplier without a Korean fixed establishment selling electronic services to Korean consumers triggers simplified VAT registration when annual supplies exceed KRW 300,000,000; if the foreign supplier has a Korean fixed establishment or maintains local inventory, registration is unconditionally mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 20 of the Korean Value Added Tax Act, zero-rate relief applies to (i) tangible goods exported from the Korean customs territory under a valid export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service to a foreign customer or overseas consignee; (ii) services supplied from Korea to a place outside Korea or consumed overseas under the place-of-supply rules; and (iii) qualifying digital or information services supplied to non-resident customers, provided the Korean supplier is the exporter, receives the consideration in foreign exchange, and separately records the supplies as export zero-rated sales in the VAT return with supporting documentation retained.

  • Export Declaration Confirmation issued by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system, constituting the governing Korean export shipment declaration
  • Bill of Lading (full ocean set), Air Waybill, or international cargo receipt showing goods loaded and destined to the foreign port or foreign consignee
  • Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, or a certificate of purchase of export bills evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Korean exporter to the foreign buyer
  • Packing list, sales contract or purchase order, and any related insurance certificates corroborating the cross-border sale

The National Tax Service mandates nationwide e-invoicing through the Hometax Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) system. Every VAT-registered supplier issuing a tax invoice must transmit the invoice data to NTS no later than the same business day of issuance using the Hometax web service or API. B2C card transactions are automatically reported by acquirers, and cash sales to private individuals must be issued through the electronic cash receipt system when requested. The anti-fraud architecture uses centralised real-time invoice matching, reconciliation of customs declarations with VAT returns, and the national e-invoice network; no digital stamping or QR fiscal clearance is required for standard VAT invoices.

NTS and KCS jointly conduct post-clearance audits of export transactions, electronically matching export declarations against VAT zero-rate returns, export invoices and bank foreign exchange receipts. For VAT zero-rating, export proceeds should be collected within a 180-day benchmark; if the proceeds are not repatriated in time without a bank-approved extension, the tax office may require additional evidence, reassess 10% VAT with interest, and claw back any refund already paid. Fraudulent or materially false export documentation (including fictitious exports, counterfeit foreign exchange certificates, undervaluation or sham contracts) triggers VAT reassessment, a surcharge of up to 40% of the unpaid tax, or up to 100% for intentional concealment, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment up to three years or fine), and retroactive withdrawal of refunds with statutory interest from the refund date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalized yet strictly reported foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions, including export proceeds, overseas service fees, royalties, and remitted dividends from registered foreign-invested entities, are generally freely convertible into Korean won or foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank, provided the underlying trade or business transaction is evidenced and reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, equity subscription, cross-border borrowings, and securities-related flows, are open but require prior filing or post-transaction reporting under FETA and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). The Korean won is not a freely deliverable offshore currency; non-resident access is restricted to authorized settlement channels and onshore deliverable markets. Profit remittance is permitted for a Chusik Hoesa with valid foreign investment registration, but the bank will require corporate tax clearance, audited or certified financial statements, proof that the underlying equity was fully paid, and a statement confirming no unresolved foreign exchange violation. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion to Korean won, but they must be received domestically through a designated foreign exchange bank within statutory timelines. Overall convertibility is high for genuine current-account business flows, while capital-flow transactions are subject to substantive compliance review and documentary verification rather than hard currency rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be collected and remitted to South Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of export declaration or the date of service completion; an extension of up to six months may be applied for through the designated bank in cases of currency controls in the destination country, litigation, or documented trade disputes. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent: no statutory requirement exists to sell export proceeds to the Bank of Korea or to convert foreign currency receipts into Korean won. Retained proceeds may be held in the exporter's foreign currency account. However, for each receipt, the exporter must file a receipt-of-export-proceeds report through the bank, and if the full proceeds are not received by the deadline, a non-receipt or deferred-receipt report must be submitted. Any conversion into KRW is executed at the prevailing market exchange rate and does not require central bank approval, but it must be recorded with the correct foreign exchange transaction purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), as central bank and foreign-exchange information supervisor, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) which holds policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); designated foreign exchange banks execute and report all regulated transactions under BOK oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital e-money institutions and non-bank EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury or settlement rail for a South Korean-domiciled Chusik Hoesa. South Korean banking law and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act reserve deposit-taking and foreign-exchange settlement functions to licensed banks and registered electronic financial business operators. A foreign EMI cannot legally open a native Korean account in the entity's name or issue an IBAN for inward foreign direct investment, export collection, or trade settlement. Fintech players such as Stripe and Affirm may be used only as upstream customer-facing payment gateways and point-of-sale credit providers, with settlement funds swept into a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank operating account. Any offshore EMI claim to act as a settlement bank for Korean trade flows is legally barred; all regulated cross-border transaction reporting must be performed by a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Any offshore EMI claim to act as a settlement bank for Korean trade flows is legally barred; all regulated cross-border transaction reporting must be performed by a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

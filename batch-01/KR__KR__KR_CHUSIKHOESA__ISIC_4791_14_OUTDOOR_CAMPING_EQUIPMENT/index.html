Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act. A Chusik Hoesa may be formed with zero or nominal paid-in capital, and no statutory blocked escrow account rule applies. However, paid-in capital must be actually deposited in a Korean bank account opened for the company in the course of incorporation and evidenced by a certificate of deposit or paid-in capital certificate; withdrawals after registration are subject to the capital maintenance and creditor protection rules of the Commercial Act. If foreign-invested enterprise status is sought under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the foreign investor must hold at least 10% voting ownership and invest at least KRW 100,000,000, and the funds must be remitted into Korea through a licensed foreign exchange channel. The USD fee shown is an indicative conversion of the KRW-denominated registration license tax for a KRW 100,000,000 paid-in capital, including the 20% education surtax and court filing fee.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the electronic filing and registration portal HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance is executed through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS portal (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A domestic Chusik Hoesa must apply to the competent district tax office for a 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN) within 20 days of commencing business, using the HomeTax business registration application and supplying the articles of incorporation, business premises documentation, foreign shareholder proof if applicable, and identification of the legal representative; the district tax office issues the Business Registration Certificate, which serves as the single Taxpayer Identification Number for Corporate Tax, Value-Added Tax, and withholding purposes. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean place of business that is nevertheless required to charge Korean VAT, the entity must complete non-resident business operator registration on HomeTax before the first taxable supply, appoint a domestic tax representative under Article 82 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, and obtain a 10-digit registration number in the same format; no minimum capital or Korean incorporation is required for that VAT registration. If the non-resident entity later establishes a fixed Korean place of business, it converts to the ordinary district tax office registration procedure. Foreign ownership of a Chusik Hoesa does not create any separate foreign shareholder TIN requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Korean tax law. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically a non-transparent domestic corporation taxable at entity level under the Corporate Tax Act. Qualifying Korean resident controlled groups may elect consolidated corporation tax for wholly-owned subsidiaries, but this election does not create pass-through treatment; no hybrid mismatch or US-style check-the-box election is available as a voluntary classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 of annual taxable turnover for classification as an ordinary taxable person under the Value-Added Tax Act; however, VAT registration with the National Tax Service is mandatory from business commencement for any business making taxable supplies, so the KRW 80 million threshold affects simplified-taxpayer status rather than the existence of a VAT registration obligation. A foreign non-resident e-commerce seller with no Korean establishment is required to register for Korean VAT in respect of continuing cross-border taxable supplies made to Korean recipients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, goods exported to a foreign place are zero-rated if: (i) the goods are shipped from Korea under an export declaration filed with Korea Customs Service; (ii) the KCS export declaration identifies the Chusik Hoesa as the exporter; (iii) the export price is actually received in foreign currency within the statutory settlement period; and (iv) the transaction is substantiated by the underlying sale, shipment, and payment documentation. For internet retail of outdoor camping equipment sold through Stripe or PayPal, each parcel must be cleared by KCS as an export and the underlying payment must be evidenced as foreign-currency export earnings. Input VAT attributable to the exported goods is creditable, and any excess refund is settled through the VAT return. Digital services or services incidental to exported goods are zero-rated only to the extent they are performed wholly outside Korea and received by a non-resident recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Shipment Report accepted by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, including the Certificate of Export Clearance
  • Commercial Invoice and export purchase order or contract
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international courier waybill forming the transport evidence for the exported parcel
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued by a designated foreign exchange bank, evidencing the inward remittance and sale/purchase of the export proceeds
  • Stripe/PayPal settlement reports linked to the foreign buyer and confirming collection of net export proceeds in foreign currency

Korea does not operate a Latin American-style pre-clearance invoice authorization model; its statutory real-time fiscal control is the Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) transmitted to NTS HomeTax. B2B e-Tax Invoices and all tax invoices issued by corporations must be transmitted to NTS by the day following the supply date through HomeTax, an approved ASP/VAN service, or the HomeTax open API. Each e-Tax Invoice carries a government-serialised invoice number and digital metadata (business registration numbers, supply value, and VAT) used for anti-fraud matching. For B2C internet retail, credit card and cash-receipt transaction data are transmitted to NTS directly by payment card acquirers and registered cash receipt agents, functioning as the Korean consumer-phase anti-fraud stamping mechanism.

Zero-rating is scrutinised against the KCS export declaration, the transport evidence, and the foreign exchange settlement record. If the export declaration is absent, false, cancelled, or if the export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 18-month period, NTS will claw back the 0% rate, assess output VAT of 10% on the net supply value, and add non-report or underreport additional tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including an enhanced additional tax of 40% for fraudulent or grossly negligent presentation. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment of up to three years or a fine of up to three times the tax evaded. Korea Customs Service and Bank of Korea/Foreign Exchange Bank penalty rules may also apply for false export declarations and late settlement of export proceeds. The tax assessment period may be reopened for up to five years for ordinary cases and up to ten years for fraudulent or intentionally concealed export zero-rating claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border trade settlements, service fees, and profit remittances, subject to mandatory reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) administered by the Bank of Korea. Capital account transactions are largely liberalized but require filing or declaration depending on the type and amount, particularly for foreign direct investment, borrowing, and securities transactions. There is no general surrender requirement compelling exporters to convert all foreign currency receipts into KRW; however, exporters must repatriate proceeds within a statutory period and report the receipt to a designated foreign exchange bank. For a Chusik Hoesa engaged in internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment, inbound equity injections from non-resident shareholders, import payments for inventory, and outbound profit distributions are permissible without prior approval, but must be supported by underlying transaction documentation and routed through a Korean Won or foreign currency account at a domestic licensed bank. Practical hurdles include bank-level compliance reviews for large remittances, Korea Customs Service reconciliation for import/export settlements, and potential transfer-pricing scrutiny on cross-border payments to related parties. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) oversees licensing, and the Bank of Korea requires that any foreign exchange transaction exceeding USD 5,000 be reported, while transactions above USD 50,000 may require a certificate of receipt or a foreign exchange purchase/sale contract. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned entity is not subject to withholding on dividends beyond the standard 22% corporate-level tax and 20-22% dividend withholding tax if a tax treaty rate does not apply, but the remitting bank will require evidence of tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a board resolution declaring the dividend. Overall, the regime is business-friendly but documentation-heavy, and treasury operations must be built on traceable, bank-verified rails rather than unregulated fintech intermediaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; however, under Article 16 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related BOK regulations, exporters must repatriate foreign currency receipts within 18 months from the date of export declaration. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts at domestic banks, but the export proceeds must be reported to a designated foreign exchange bank within 30 days of receipt using the Electronic Import/Export Transaction Report. For goods sold through internet retailing, the e-commerce operator may receive settlement through payment aggregators such as Stripe or PayPal; these funds must ultimately be wired to a Korean domestic foreign currency account as a trade settlement, with the underlying shipping documents, customs declarations, and payment gateway settlement statements preserved for bank verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; operational oversight by the Korea Customs Service for trade settlement reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary treasury operations. South Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all cross-border payments and receipts to be conducted through a licensed foreign exchange bank operating in Korea. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, or multi-currency virtual IBAN providers are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission (FSC) to hold KRW or provide domestic Korean won accounts. They cannot issue local KRW account numbers that are connected to the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) Won Clearing System, nor can they authenticate FEIS filings. Foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa entities must therefore maintain a primary corporate account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. A global EMI may be used as an auxiliary collection wallet for foreign card payments, but settlement from Stripe, PayPal, or Shopify Payments must be swept into the Korean bank account within mandated reporting windows; legal exposure arises if the EMI is used to circumvent BOK reporting or to park export proceeds beyond the 18-month repatriation window. The recommended architecture is domestic KRW account plus one foreign currency account at the same bank, with Stripe/PayPal settlement via SWIFT to that foreign currency account, then a bank-brokered conversion to KRW with FEIS reporting by the bank.

24% top national marginal rate plus 10% local income tax surtax on the tax payable, producing a combined top marginal rate of 26.4%; progressive brackets of 9%, 19%, 21%, and 24%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border transactions with foreign related parties in a fiscal year exceeds KRW 5,000,000,000; Master File is further required where the multinational group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Report filing applies to groups whose consolidated annual revenue reaches KRW 100,000,000,000; documentation must be delivered to the National Tax Service within 30 days following a formal request (and no later than 45 days from the corporate tax return filing deadline).

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the NTS applies strict substance-over-form doctrine, GAAR under CIT Act Article 52, and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) provision in bilateral tax treaties; an e-commerce Chusik Hoesa relying on treaty benefits for cross-border royalties or dividends must evidence real economic operations including substantive board decision-making, employees with managerial authority in Korea, physical office/trading premises, and proportionate profit-generating functions, failing which the tax authority may recharacterize the entity as a conduit and deny treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and monetary fine up to 5 times the evaded tax under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act; civil penalty taxes of 40% of the concealed amount plus late-payment surcharge of 0.022% per day; NTS seizure and public delinquency listing.

Director personal liability: Representative director and responsible accounting director are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; criminal conviction results in imprisonment, travel ban, disqualification as director, and restitution of evaded taxes through asset tracing.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment of the Korean taxable income; additional tax penalty up to 30% of the adjusted tax differential under the Law on International Tax Coordination, plus interest; denial of related-party expense deductions; the NTS may also assess the foreign related party's deemed Korean income.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally subject to civil restitution where transfer pricing manipulation is held to constitute gross negligence; criminal referral for fraudulent transfer pricing attracts imprisonment up to 5 years and travel bans.
Shell Company / Sham Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization and disregarding of the entity under CIT Act Article 52 GAAR and the substance-over-form doctrine; retrocrative re-assessment of Korean-source income at full statutory rates; denial of treaty reductions under the Principal Purpose Test; disgorgement of preferential tax credits.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the shell is used for tax evasion, rendering the incorporating shareholders and directors personally liable for all assessed taxes, penalties, and interest; criminal sanctions include imprisonment and travel/prohibition orders.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information, failure to report suspicious transactions: imprisonment up to 3 years and criminal fine up to KRW 30 million; administrative fines up to KRW 50 million and revocation of electronic payment processing licenses.

Director personal liability: The director responsible for AML governance and compliance officers face personal criminal prosecution, monetary fines, and travel restrictions during pending investigations; repeated violations trigger permanent disqualification from financial services management.
Failure to Disclose Foreign Financial Accounts

Statutory sanction: Penalty tax of 20% of the unreported balance under Article 8 of the Law on International Tax Coordination; where unreported amounts exceed KRW 5,000,000,000, criminal referral with imprisonment of up to 2 years and fines; overlapping addition of 0.022% daily surcharge where tax was underpaid.

Director personal liability: The controlling shareholder, representative director, and any signatory on the undisclosed foreign account are jointly personally liable; NTS enforcement includes travel bans, property attachment, and criminal imprisonment for willful non-disclosure.

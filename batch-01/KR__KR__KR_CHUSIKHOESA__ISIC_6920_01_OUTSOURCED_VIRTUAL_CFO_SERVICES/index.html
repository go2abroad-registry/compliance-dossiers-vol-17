Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act; there is no statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, all subscribed shares must be fully paid up, and the paid-in capital must be deposited in a temporary incorporation deposit account opened in the name of the company at a licensed commercial bank in Korea. That account is effectively blocked and cannot be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is completed, after which the funds are released to the operating account of the company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea via the HomeTax electronic tax portal at hometax.go.kr; export declarations and customs supervision are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS single-window system.

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities may obtain a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN), which simultaneously serves as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number, before commencing taxable activities in Korea. For a foreign entity with a domestic place of business, registration is submitted to the district tax office having jurisdiction over that domestic business location using the HomeTax foreign-corporation business-registration service, accompanied by: certificate of incorporation or foreign registration certificate, constitutional documents, a board resolution documenting commencement of Korean business, proof of the domestic place of business, and a tax representative appointment where the entity does not maintain a principal office in Korea. A non-resident entity without any Korean establishment that supplies remote electronic services to Korean consumers may apply through the simplified foreign VAT registration route on HomeTax, including a foreign-user ID and password, the foreign entity registration certificate, and ratification of a Korean tax representative or VAT-dealing agent. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number after validation. Registration must be completed within 20 days from the start of Korean business, and no VAT invoice may be issued before the BRN is obtained. Any changes to the registered particulars must be reported within 15 days, and closure must be reported when Korean taxable activity ceases. Once registered, the entity files quarterly VAT returns and annual corporate income tax returns through HomeTax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None of the U.S. check-the-box pass-through elections are available under Korean tax law; a Chusik Hoesa is a per-se entity-level taxpayer. Korean tax law permits qualifying wholly-owned domestic corporations to elect consolidated group taxation under the Corporate Tax Act when the statutory shareholding and business-preservation conditions are met. A foreign-incorporated entity whose head office or place of effective management is in Korea is statutorily classified as a domestic corporation, and hybrid-mismatch adjustments apply under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act and the Framework Act on International Taxation; these are not elective subclassification mechanisms. For this vehicle, available elections are therefore limited to consolidated filing eligibility and tax-year alignment, not entity pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies from the first taxable supply, without an economic de minimis for ordinary businesses, and business registration must be filed within 20 days of commencement. The Value-Added Tax Act provides a low-turnover exemption where prior-year taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 10,000,000; for foreign remote-digital-services suppliers, registration is required from the first deemed taxable Korean supply, and a simplified foreign VAT registration pathway is available for suppliers without a Korean place of business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT export rate applies to goods physically exported from Korea after an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, with export proceeds repatriated to Korea in convertible currency. Services, including B2B virtual CFO advisory, fractional CFO services, multicurrency bookkeeping, and qualifying offshore digital services, are zero-rated where the supply is made to a non-resident business or foreign consumer, the service is wholly or effectively used and consumed outside Korea, and the service is not connected to any Korean permanent establishment of the supplier. The zero-rating must be supported by contemporaneous evidence such as the export declaration, transport documents, service agreement, invoices, foreign payment receipts, and any record demonstrating offshore consumption; the burden of proof rests on the taxpayer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry equivalent) filed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS and endorsed with an Export Declaration Acceptance Number including HS code, quantity, FOB value, exporter and importer identifiers
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document showing the goods were consigned to the foreign buyer and physically removed from Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Korean Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by a licensed bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract or purchase order, and internal collection schedule reconciled to the export declaration and transport document

Korea mandates electronic tax invoicing through the National Tax Service HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system for all VAT-registered businesses. Tax invoices for B2B taxable supplies must be issued electronically and transmitted to the NTS via HomeTax or an authorised API integration, with the NTS-assigned electronic invoice serial number. B2C taxable sales are generally recorded through the electronic cash-receipt system, which transmits transaction data to the NTS database in near real time. Korea does not operate a transaction-level pre-clearance or physical digital-stamping fiscal server; anti-fraud control is implemented through mandatory electronic issuance, unique NTS invoice coding, real-time cash-receipt reporting, cross-matching of input and output VAT, and statutory penalties for false, delayed, or omitted invoices. Third-party billing and ERP providers may integrate with the NTS using the taxpayer's electronic certificate and API credentials.

If zero-rating is claimed without complete substantiation or where documentation is found to be false, the NTS may reopen the relevant tax period, disallow the zero rating, assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the supply value, deny or claw back input VAT credits already refunded, and impose statutory understatement penalty tax and interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act. Where fictitious export declarations, false invoices, or unverified foreign remittances are used, the Korea Customs Service and NTS may also impose customs penalties, initiate criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and seek fines or imprisonment. The clawback statute of limitations generally extends to ten years for fraudulent or unfiled returns and five years for understatements, and the taxpayer retains the full burden to prove actual export, foreign receipt, and offshore consumption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a fundamentally liberal but surveillance-heavy foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions, including dividend remittances, royalties, and service fee payments, are fully convertible without licensing, provided that supporting documents and bank-issued tax/filing receipts are presented to a domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions are subject to a registration or notification system: inbound equity investments of a non-resident into a Chusik Hoesa must be reported via the foreign exchange bank to the Minister of Economy and Finance for cross-border capital receipt purposes. The Korean won is freely convertible onshore for residents, but non-residents face restrictions on the external settlement of KRW without underlying economic activity. Profit remittance requires a certified tax clearance (corporate income tax return, local tax payment certificates) plus audited financial statements. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds, but every foreign currency receipt must be credited to a domestic foreign currency deposit account or immediately converted to KRW, and all receipts are reported electronically to the customs service through the foreign exchange information system. Administrative penalties apply for unreported inward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect proceeds within six months from the export date, extendable upon application to the head of the relevant domestic bank, and must either deposit the foreign currency in an authorized foreign currency deposit account or convert to KRW at a domestic foreign exchange bank; no fixed statutory conversion ratio exists, but full repatriation and electronic notification to the Korea Customs Service (Foreign Exchange Information System) are mandatory for each transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the supervisory framework of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as 'electronic financial business operators' for the purpose of operating domestic won-denominated virtual accounts. A Korean-incorporated Chusik Hoesa must route all inbound FX receipts through a domestically registered foreign exchange bank and file electronic reports via the Foreign Exchange Information System. An offshore EMI can be used only as a supplementary payment acceptance terminal (for example, Stripe Billing as a SaaS billing processor) provided that all settlement amounts are ultimately swept to a Korean foreign exchange bank account in the name of the same legal entity; however, the EMI will not be recognized as a clearing bank for statutory capital verification, profit remittance, or tax clearance purposes.

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as 'electronic financial business operators' for the purpose of operating domestic won-denominated virtual accounts. A Korean-incorporated Chusik Hoesa must route all inbound FX receipts through a domestically registered foreign exchange bank and file electronic reports via the Foreign Exchange Information System. An offshore EMI can be used only as a supplementary payment acceptance terminal (for example, Stripe Billing as a SaaS billing processor) provided that all settlement amounts are ultimately swept to a Korean foreign exchange bank account in the name of the same legal entity; however, the EMI will not be recognized as a clearing bank for statutory capital verification, profit remittance, or tax clearance purposes.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% (highest bracket for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion) plus 10% local income tax surcharge, yielding a combined top effective rate of 26.4%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax on gross dividend paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% of the national tax), for an effective statutory aggregate rate of 22%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File under Article 13-1 of the International Tax Adjustment Act apply when cross-border transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 10 billion in the fiscal year; a lower KRW 5 billion threshold triggers the annual transfer-pricing summary report requirement.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the substance-over-form principle under the International Tax Adjustment Act GAAR and Korean Civil/Commercial law requires real business premises, employees, decision-making, and core services in Korea. A shell Chusik Hoesa lacking economic substance may be disregarded for tax purposes, denied treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, or recharacterized by the NTS under anti-avoidance provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine equal to up to 3 times the evaded tax; additional corporate income tax of 30% of the unreported amount plus daily late payment interest (0.022%); local income tax surcharge of 20%–30%.

Director personal liability: Directors who concealed revenue or directed the use of foreign payment rails may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax penalty; the NTS can order a travel ban, initiate criminal indictment without prior administrative collection, and request seizure of personal assets; a court may pierce the corporate veil if the legal entity is found to be a mere instrument of the non-resident owner.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of the Korean GAAR under Article 14 of the International Tax Adjustment Act to recharacterize the Korean entity as a flow-through; denial of treaty benefits and reclassification of all foreign-source income as Korean taxable income; additional tax of up to 40% of the underreported corporate tax and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors appointed solely to mark compliance but without actual authority face personal civil restitution for any resulting tax shortfall; serious cases trigger criminal fraud charges, disqualification from serving as directors, and travel bans pending criminal or administrative proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 5% of the aggregate transaction value for each missing required document; the NTS may disregard the submitted price, adjust taxable income to the arm's length result, and impose up to 30% additional tax on intentionally incorrect reporting; unpaid corporate tax also accrues daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false transfer-pricing records are personally liable for penalties arising from fraud; in aggravated cases, criminal charges for obstruction of tax investigation carry imprisonment, and the court may impose personal recovery orders against company assets and directors' remuneration.
Anti-Money Laundering / Client Due Diligence

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Transaction Reports Act: fines up to KRW 100 million for failure to report suspicious transactions or establish AML controls; the National Tax Service may additionally disallow deductions and impose penalties for unfounded cash receipts; the regulatory body may revoke the statutory accounting-service license.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability if the control failure was deliberate; penalties include imprisonment up to 3 years and fines up to KRW 30 million; the company's civil liability for sanctions may be assigned to directors on grounds of gross managerial negligence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum authorized or paid-in capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price of issued shares must be paid before the incorporation registration is filed. Funds may be deposited into an escrow account or a provisional bank account in the company's formation name, and the depositary bank's certificate of deposit is required at registration. After registration, the company must confirm the capital deposit and raise the escrow status to an ordinary corporate account.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate for Premises under the Fire Services Act: the registered retail office, showroom, and administrative suite must obtain a normal fire inspection and, if the floor area exceeds 200 square meters, a Certificate of Completion of Fire Protection System. Civil Defense Shelter requirements are not triggered for retail-only premises, but the registered office must display the mandatory evacuation notice and emergency contact placard. Occupational Clearance under the Occupational Safety and Health Act: the operator must appoint a workplace safety manager, file the workplace establishment report with the local employment and labor office, and maintain compliance for any warehouse or distribution area with a floor load capacity check, forklift traffic (if used), and electrical installation approval from the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation. The premises must also be inspected for alarm system properly connected to the local 119 emergency center.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea; online electronic portal is the HomeTax System (https://hometax.go.kr), supplemented by the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal for export clearance.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity establishing a Chusik Hoesa in Korea for retailing smart IoT hardware must obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Number from the District Tax Office having jurisdiction over its domestic place of business. The non-resident corporate process requires: (1) filing the prescribed Business Registration Application through HomeTax or with the Director of the competent District Tax Office; (2) attaching the certified foreign incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, power of attorney to a statutory tax representative in Korea, domestic office lease proof, trade license evidence, and the passport/foreign ID of the representative; (3) passing an NTS physical presence and anti-abuse review; and (4) receiving the Business Registration Certificate. The same registration number is used as the VAT identification number, e-tax invoice issuer ID, and customs exporter code. A foreign corporation without a Korean place of business but with VAT taxable activity in Korea may register directly through a tax representative; the representative accepts NTS assessments and e-invoice obligations. If the foreign entity has no domestic permanent establishment, it must designate an authorized tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes for service of process and tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

South Korea applies a unitary national corporate tax system and does not permit check-the-box entity classification elections. A Chusik Hoesa is automatically treated as a domestic corporation taxable on worldwide income at entity level. The only relevant statutory election is consolidated corporate group taxation under the Corporate Income Tax Act for qualified wholly-owned domestic subsidiaries; hybrid mismatch rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act apply on a mandatory, non-elective basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no KRW-denominated VAT registration threshold for incorporated businesses; a Chusik Hoesa must register with the Director of the competent District Tax Office before commencing taxable supplies of smart home IoT hardware and related installation services. Simplified taxpayer status exists only for individuals with annual turnover below KRW 80 million; corporations are ineligible.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available when the Chusik Hoesa sells smart IoT hardware to a non-resident buyer and the goods physically leave Korea under a customs export declaration accepted by the head of the customs office. The statutory criteria are: (1) the export sale is stated on an e-tax invoice; (2) an export declaration is made in the name of the Korean exporter under the Customs Act; (3) the goods are accompanied by shipping documents proving departure from Korea to the foreign country; (4) the foreign buyer is a non-resident; and (5) the export proceeds are settled in authorized foreign exchange through a foreign exchange bank. For direct parcel exports, a consolidated electronic export declaration via UNI-PASS is sufficient when the parcel is transported to an overseas destination. Cross-border digital services incidental to hardware maintenance are zero-rated when the recipient is entitled to supply from a place outside Korea and the NTS holds the relevant foreign exchange settlement certificate.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (electronic export clearance / Bill of Entry) accepted through UNI-PASS
  • e-Tax Invoice issued via HomeTax and transmitted to the NTS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the smart home IoT hardware items, quantities, and values
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of goods from Korea to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by the foreign exchange bank proving receipt of export proceeds

The NTS mandates the e-Tax Invoice System through HomeTax for all VAT-registered businesses. Every taxable supplier must issue an electronic tax invoice containing the buyer's registration number, transaction date, supply value, and VAT amount, and transmit it to the NTS within one business day after issuance using authorized HomeTax API, EDI, or the mobile e-certificate channel. For transactions between VAT-registered persons, e-invoice issuance is mandatory without a lower monetary threshold except for a limited cash-sale exemption below KRW 100,000 where a cash receipt must be issued instead. The NTS clearance model is post-transaction automated risk filtering rather than pre-clearance stamping; each e-tax invoice carries an NTS cryptographic certification code enabling anti-fraud integrity checks and real-time reconciliation with customs export data.

The NTS systematically reconciles export zero-rating claims against UNI-PASS customs declarations, HomeTax e-invoice issuance, and foreign exchange bank settlement records. If the export declaration, shipping document, e-invoice, or overseas settlement certificate is missing, falsified, or incorrectly dated, the NTS revokes the zero-rating and assesses VAT at 10% on the transaction plus statutory surcharge under the Value-Added Tax Act penalty provisions, typically 20% of the unpaid tax for undisclosed inaccuracy and up to 40% for fraudulent claims. Submission of a false e-tax invoice constitutes criminal tax fraud under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, subject to criminal fines and imprisonment of responsible directors. Export proceeds not repatriated to Korea within 180 days without an approved extension under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act will trigger clawback of the zero-rated VAT treatment and retroactive assessment from the date of export, with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalised current account regime for trade in goods, services, and ordinary remittances; Korean won is freely usable within the domestic banking system and convertible at market rates for bona fide current account transactions. However, capital account transactions such as foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, and offshore borrowing, while generally permitted, are subject to reporting, verification, or prior declaration procedures enforced by MOEF and administered through foreign exchange banks. For a Korean Chusik Hoesa engaged in online retailing of smart IoT hardware, cross-border fund movements will be monitored under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Real Name Financial Transactions Act. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted once the financial statements are audited and tax clearance is issued, but each remittance must be effected through a local foreign exchange bank using the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code and supporting evidence. Surrender of export proceeds is not mandatory by ratio, but export proceeds must be received within an 18-month statutory period; the company may hold the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert it to won. Non-resident investors are allowed to own 100% of the entity, and there is no minimum capital. Tax treaties and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act make Korea a stable but documentation-heavy jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 15 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Korea Customs Service enforcement regulations, the exporter must collect export proceeds within 18 months from the date of the export declaration or customs clearance. The bank may grant an extension of up to an additional 18 months for justifiable commercial reasons, and any overdue export must be reported to the foreign exchange bank or the Korea Customs Service. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio, meaning the exporter may keep the foreign currency in a resident foreign-currency account with an authorised Korean commercial bank, or sell it to the bank at the market exchange rate. For exports of digital goods or small parcels, simplified receipt procedures apply to IoT hardware sales through e-commerce platforms.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the national foreign exchange administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Legal viability rating: Low as a primary treasury clearing rail, but Medium as a supplementary card-acquiring gateway. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex, Wise, or Revolut cannot open domestic Korean settlement accounts or provide a local KRW IBAN-like account for a Chusik Hoesa unless they operate through a licensed Korean branch or a domestic bank partner authorised under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Non-bank EMIs are not eligible for a foreign exchange banking licence; they are barred from acting as the licensed entity for capital injections, profit remittance, or FX hedging. For e-commerce card collections, a foreign EMI can aggregate settlement via an authorised domestic payment gateway and then route funds into the Korean bank account, but the entity must maintain a tier-1 domestic bank account as the clearing anchor. The use of a pure offshore EMI as the operating account would violate the Real Name Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and would be rejected by the FIU in an AML review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment into the Korean Chusik Hoesa should be reported before the remittance is made. The non-resident investor instructs its overseas bank to send a SWIFT MT103 transfer to the entity's capital escrow account at a tier-1 Korean bank, using the purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act corresponding to foreign direct investment equity subscription. The Korean bank will verify the foreign investment report, credit the escrow account, and provide a certificate of capital contribution after the funds are settled in KRW. The bank then converts the foreign currency into KRW and transfers the amount to the basic operational account. If the investment exceeds the prior-report threshold, the MOEF or KOTRA report must be completed at least 20 days prior to remittance. No legal minimum capital applies, but the subscribed capital amount must match the entity's registered articles of incorporation and shareholder register and be physically transferred from the shareholder's account.For the purchase of IoT hardware inventory from overseas suppliers, the Korean entity may use direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account trade settlements, or may request the Korean bank to issue an import letter of credit on its behalf. Documentary collections, including documents against payment or documents against acceptance, are permissible for established trade counterparties with signed invoice, packing list, bill of lading and customs clearance records. All payments must be annotated with the correct foreign exchange purpose codes and the commercial invoice must be lodged with the bank to prove that the payment is for current-account trade transactions. For recurring purchases of smart-home devices from Chinese, Vietnamese, or US assemblers, a master supply contract accompanied by per-order purchase orders and e-commerce settlement reports will enable the bank to expedite wire processing; amounts above the bank's thresholds may require pre-approval by the foreign exchange bank under MOEF rules.Onshore forward FX contracts in USD/KRW, CNY/KRW, or EUR/KRW permitted for trade-backed exposures with maximum maturity of one year and a net open position limit approved by the Korean commercial bank.Cross-currency interest rate swaps and FX swaps allowed when the company has an actual underlying asset or liability in foreign currency relating to the import or export of IoT hardware.Offshore non-deliverable forward contracts may be used by non-resident hedging counterparties but a Korean domestic entity is discouraged from using NDFs unless the corresponding trade receivable is in a restricted currency; the bank will not permit speculative NDF positions without documentary evidence of a current-account transaction.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal viability rating: Low as a primary treasury clearing rail, but Medium as a supplementary card-acquiring gateway. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex, Wise, or Revolut cannot open domestic Korean settlement accounts or provide a local KRW IBAN-like account for a Chusik Hoesa unless they operate through a licensed Korean branch or a domestic bank partner authorised under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Non-bank EMIs are not eligible for a foreign exchange banking licence; they are barred from acting as the licensed entity for capital injections, profit remittance, or FX hedging. For e-commerce card collections, a foreign EMI can aggregate settlement via an authorised domestic payment gateway and then route funds into the Korean bank account, but the entity must maintain a tier-1 domestic bank account as the clearing anchor. The use of a pure offshore EMI as the operating account would violate the Real Name Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and would be rejected by the FIU in an AML review.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national corporate income tax rate under the progressive schedule (9% to 24%), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing a combined top effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% gross basis (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident corporate shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the mandatory transfer pricing adjustment report attached to the corporate tax return. KRW 10 billion in cross-border related-party transactions triggers the obligation to prepare and, upon National Tax Service request, produce the Local File and Master File within 45 days. A Country-by-Country Report is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in the form used in certain offshore centers, but Korean tax law applies substance-over-form doctrine, general anti-avoidance rules, beneficial ownership requirements, and controlled foreign corporation rules. A Chusik Hoesa carrying on IoT hardware retail must demonstrate actual functions, assets, employees, inventory management, and decision-making in Korea; artificial invoicing or shell arrangements may be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent underreporting triggers an additional tax of 40% of the underpaid national tax, plus late-payment interest. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act can lead to imprisonment of up to two years or a fine of up to three times the amount of tax evaded.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes or participates in false returns faces criminal imprisonment, fines, and potential personal secondary tax liability where corporate assets have been dissipated to evade collection.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the shell entity, reattribute income to the substantive foreign principal, deny treaty benefits, impose a 10% underreporting penalty or a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, and apply controlled foreign corporation rules to the foreign shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally accountable for unpaid corporate taxes under the secondary tax liability rules; travel bans and exit restrictions may be imposed for significant unpaid tax liabilities.
Cross-Border Withholding Default

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the withheld amount, charged with an additional tax of 10% of the uncollected withholding tax, and subject to daily overdue interest. The expense may also be denied as a deductible business cost.

Director personal liability: The responsible director or financial officer who knowingly fails to withhold is jointly liable for the shortfall and may face criminal penalties if the act is considered intentional tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment, impose arm's length interest, add a 10% underreporting penalty, and deny related deductions. Failure to produce documentation on request can support a conservative audit adjustment and an obstruction-of-audit penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve inflated intercompany payments knowing they are artificial may be subject to criminal tax sanctions and personal liability for unpaid corporate tax if assets were shifted to related parties.
AML / False Tax Invoices

Statutory sanction: False tax invoice penalties can reach up to 2% of the supply value. Anti-money laundering breaches under the Financial Transaction Reports Act carry administrative fines and imprisonment of up to five years, together with confiscation of illicit proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate falsified invoicing or payment concealment face imprisonment, criminal fines, personal confiscation orders, and travel restrictions; the corporate veil may be disregarded where fraud is established.

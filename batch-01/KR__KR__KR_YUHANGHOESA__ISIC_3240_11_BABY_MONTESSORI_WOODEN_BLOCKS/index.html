Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, before registration is completed, every issued share must be fully paid in cash into a blocked escrow account or a temporary corporate subscription account at a Korean commercial bank. If foreign currency is subscribed, conversion must be carried out with a declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or elective pass-through classification. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary VAT registration exemption threshold exists. Any person or entity commencing taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register with the NTS under Article 8 of the Value-Added Tax Act. The simplified taxpayer relief under Korean VAT law applies only to qualifying individuals with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000; a corporate Yuhan Hoesa is always an ordinary taxable person and must be VAT-registered from business commencement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act to tangible goods exported from the Republic of Korea when title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer and the goods are physically removed from Korea under KCS customs supervision. Qualifying exported goods include baby Montessori wooden blocks and sensory toy products manufactured in Korea and delivered to overseas distributors or cross-border e-commerce end customers. The zero rate also applies to certain Korean-supplied services rendered entirely outside Korea or to non-residents in the course of foreign-exchange-earning transactions, provided the service is not treated as domestic VAT-taxable supply. Substantiation requires independent customs and banking evidence proving actual export movement, proper declaration under the correct commodity code (ISIC 3240 / HS chapters of wooden toys), and repatriation of foreign currency proceeds into Korea through a domestic foreign exchange bank.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) showing Korean port/airport of loading and foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange banking settlement certificate issued by a Korean authorized foreign exchange bank
  • HomeTax VAT zero-rating return substantiation schedule and electronic tax invoice copies
  • Purchase and production records evidencing Korean origin, including procurement invoices, factory delivery records, and weighted-average or unit costing evidence

All VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices at or before the time of supply. Electronic tax invoices are mandatory and must be transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax Electronic Tax Invoice system under a registered digital certificate. The Korean model is a near-real-time electronic clearance and anti-fraud reconciliation system: e-tax invoice data is transmitted to NTS on the prescribed statutory schedule, and the NTS automatically reconciles issued electronic tax invoices, filed VAT returns, customs export declarations, and business-to-business transaction data. There is no per-invoice fiscal stamping mechanism, but the integrated HomeTax electronic tax invoice regime functions as the digital anti-fraud mandate for VAT compliance and audit verification.

Korean export zero-rating is strictly audited by the NTS. The exporter bears the burden of proof to substantiate the zero rate with the customs export declaration, transport documents, sales contracts, and foreign exchange settlement records. If the mandatory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline is missed or settlement is made in an unauthorized manner, the NTS may reclassify the export as a domestic taxable supply and impose VAT at the standard 10% on the full gross supply value. Statutory additional tax, neglect penalties, and unfaithful-report penalties of up to 40% of the underdeclared VAT can be assessed, and late-payment interest is applied from the original VAT return due date. Fabricated export declarations, copied or false Inward Remittance Certificates, circular financing schemes, or collusive reimbursement structures are treated as tax evasion; they may trigger criminal referrals, monetary fines, imprisonment sanctions for responsible directors, retrospective clawback of input VAT credits claimed on zero-rated exports, and cancellation of the entity's electronic tax invoice issuance approval. The NTS may extend the audit window to overseas banking records and related-party distribution data under applicable tax treaties and mutual administrative assistance arrangements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility fully liberalised and capital-account convertibility subject to selective declaration, registration, or approval mechanisms. The KRW is freely convertible for trade-related and service payments, dividend remittances, loan repayments, and approved capital movements. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; entities may retain foreign currency earnings in licensed foreign exchange bank accounts, but must deposit and settle such proceeds through a designated 'foreign exchange bank' within the statutory timeframe. For profit repatriation, a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) can distribute dividends outward after corporate income tax and a standard withholding tax applied to non-resident shareholders, provided the remittance is supported by audited financial statements, board minutes, and a foreign exchange purchase/sale report filed via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Specifichurdles include mandatory documentary-verification by the handling bank for distributions exceeding a certain threshold, and the requirement that any external borrowing or equity injection beyond USD 5 million must be reported to the MOEF/BOK within 30 days. Capital account transactions such as acquisition of offshore securities, derivatives, or real estate require additional licensing or post-transaction reporting. Overall, the regime is regarded as open with robust monitoring procedures, requiring precise purpose codes and complete compliance records rather than rationing of hard currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive export proceeds within 6 months from the date of export declaration for standard goods, extendable to 12 months upon prior approval from the designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; export receipts may be maintained as foreign-currency deposit balances in a licensed foreign exchange bank. However, the full amount of the export proceeds must be settled into an onshore foreign exchange bank account and reported through FEIS within the prescribed deadline. Failure to collect or settle within the statutory period triggers penalty interest, fines, and potential revocation of the exporter's trade license.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital financial institutions such as Wise, Revolut, or neobank EMIs are not legally permissible as primary settlement accounts for a Korean-resident corporate entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any entity providing foreign exchange services or operating as an onshore settlement institution must hold a Korean banking license or be registered as a foreign exchange bank. Offshore EMI rails cannot connect to the Bank of Korea's BX-Wire settlement system nor issue domestic account numbers for the collection of export proceeds. They may be used only as ancillary payment channels for smaller vendor expenses if they operate through a licensed Korean partner bank, but the primary treasury and clearing architecture must rely on domestic Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital financial institutions such as Wise, Revolut, or neobank EMIs are not legally permissible as primary settlement accounts for a Korean-resident corporate entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any entity providing foreign exchange services or operating as an onshore settlement institution must hold a Korean banking license or be registered as a foreign exchange bank. Offshore EMI rails cannot connect to the Bank of Korea's BX-Wire settlement system nor issue domestic account numbers for the collection of export proceeds. They may be used only as ancillary payment channels for smaller vendor expenses if they operate through a licensed Korean partner bank, but the primary treasury and clearing architecture must rely on domestic Tier-1 commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: KRW 5 billion or more in annual international connected transactions; Master File: KRW 100 billion in annual international connected transactions and KRW 100 billion in total revenue; Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion (approx. USD 800 million) with KRW 5 billion minimum local threshold for identification.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies an economic substance doctrine (substance-over-form) under Article 14 of the Corporate Tax Act and the GAAR in Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act. The taxpayer must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Korea through employees, premises, decision-making, and risk tolerance. An entity that merely nominally owns minority equity or invoices service fees without local substance will be re-characterised, with expenses denied and profits reallocated. Korean courts require the CIGA to be real, not documentary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (특정범죄 가중처벌 등에 관한 법률) Article 3, aggravated evasion penalty of a fine up to 3 times the evaded amount and forfeiture of property; under Article 66 of the Corporate Tax Act, additional 40% surcharge on the underreported tax (50% if fraudulent); Korean tax authorities may also deny the deduction entirely and impose an additional 20% capital gain adjustment.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: director is jointly and severally liable for the tax, surcharge, and all penalties (Article 7, Official Tax Collection Act – “nominee or materially participating director doctrine”). Civil restitution may be ordered in a shareholder derivative suit. Executive is blacklisted for international travel by the National Tax Service and may face criminal imprisonment of 3-5 years, with elevation to not less than 7 years if the evaded amount exceeds KRW 500 million.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax & DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violation of the withholding obligation under Article 98 of the Corporate Tax Act triggers (i) payment of the withheld tax by the company as withholding agent, (ii) a penalty of 20% of the uncollected withholding tax, (iii) an additional surcharge of 0.75% per month for late non-remittance (maximum 45%), and (iv) if treaty abuse is found, an increased penalty of 40% of the under-withheld amount and disallowance of the underlying expense in full. Consequences under the Korean Exchange Transactions and Foreign Investment Act may also include a fine of KRW 20-50 million.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the withholding declaration are personally liable for the unpaid tax amount under Articles 113 and 114 of the Corporate Tax Act. Criminal sanctions: diversion of sufficient funds is wilful non-withholding, punishable under Article 355 of the Criminal Act (abuse of trust or embezzlement) with up to 10 years’ imprisonment; additionally, a director may be barred from departing Korea (travel ban) until the tax liability is executed. The NTS blacklist commonly applies to ‘serious tax offender’ designations.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, an arm's-length adjustment reallocates the income to the Korean entity, disallows the management fee deduction (Article 19 and 20, Corporate Tax Act), and imposes a 20% surcharge for unreported income; court precedent also denies the tax residence status of the foreign affiliate if it lacks registry and real oversight. Additionally, the failure to prepare the Local File (threshold KRW 5 billion) adds a penalty of KRW 10 million and an extra 15% adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under the Official Tax Collection Act Article 7 for any tax deficiency caused by their gross negligence in supervising the shell arrangement. Civil liability to creditors also arises under the Korean Commercial Act Article 399 for injury to the company. The criminal consequence for making false declarations in a tax audit may reach 2 years’ imprisonment; if the shell structure also imported foreign capital without reporting, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes a fine of up to KRW 50 million and confiscation of unlawfully transferred assets.

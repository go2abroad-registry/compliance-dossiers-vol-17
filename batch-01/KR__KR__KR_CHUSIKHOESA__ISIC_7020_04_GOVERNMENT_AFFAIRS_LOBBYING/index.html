Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None: The Commercial Act sets no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the paid-in capital must be at least KRW 1 (approximately USD 0.00075) and full payment or contribution of the par value of subscribed shares must be made through a designated bank account before incorporation; capital may remain in the corporate operating account upon registration, but if structured as a foreign-invested entity under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a certificate of foreign-currency inward remittance (entry of foreign capital) must be deposited with a foreign exchange bank and reported, without requiring a statutory escrow lock-up beyond preservation of capital funds until incorporation registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification (Green Card) issued by the municipal fire department and the Korea Fire Industry Technical Institute for the leased Class II office premises, including a biennial fire safety inspection of exits, extinguishers, and sprinklers, a structural load safety certificate for the office fixtures if the tenant fit-out exceeds 1,500 kg/m2 floor loading, and an Occupational Safety and Health General Workplace Registration under the Occupational Safety and Health Act if the company employs five or more workers, including emergency exit drills, a safety manager appointment, and a workplace risk assessment every six months; if the registered office is a suburban business center with no kitchen and fewer than five employees, only the fire-safety inspection certificate and the building management's civil defense pass are required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal is the HomeTax system (www.hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that commences business in Korea, establishes a permanent establishment, or otherwise derives Korean-sourced income must register for a Business Registration Number (BRN), which simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number, Corporate Income Tax number, and withholding tax account number. The foreign corporation must appoint a resident tax representative or domestic agent where required by the National Tax Basic Act. The application is filed with the competent District Tax Office through the NTS HomeTax portal or in-person by an authorized agent. Required evidence includes the foreign entity's articles of incorporation, a certificate of foreign registration or equivalent existence document, a board resolution appointing the Korean agent and authorizing registration, a power of attorney in favor of the domestic representative, documentary evidence of the Korean place of business, and the statutory Korean business registration application completed in the Korean language. The NTS reviews the submission and issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number within a statutory processing period. Registration must be completed within 20 days from the date of business commencement, opening of a Korean blue-chip commercial presence, or the date on which the non-resident becomes liable for VAT or Corporate Tax in Korea.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not maintain a check-the-box regime. A Chusik Hoesa incorporated in Korea is automatically treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax on its worldwide income. Foreign entities are classified as domestic or foreign corporations strictly under the Korean Corporate Tax Act based on statutory headquarters and place of effective management. No hybrid mismatch election is available; anti-hybrid rules prescribed under the Korean Tax Act and OECD-aligned implementation will apply to deny unintended deductible outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold applies to corporations. Every taxable person, including a Chusik Hoesa, is mandatorily required to register for VAT through the Business Registration Number before or within 20 days after commencing taxable business activity, irrespective of projected revenue. Simplified taxpayer classification is available only to certain individually owned service businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 48 million, and this does not apply to a joint-stock corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under the Korean VAT Act for exported goods and qualifying cross-border services. For goods, the exporter must complete a Korean Customs Service export declaration using the UNI-PASS system, the goods must physically depart Korea and be delivered to a foreign buyer, the export declaration must be in the name of the taxable entity, and the consideration must be paid in foreign currency by a non-resident. For services, including government affairs lobbying and cross-border trade policy advisory services supplied by a Chusik Hoesa to a foreign principal, zero rating applies only where the service recipient is a foreign non-resident and the service is actually used or consumed outside Korea. Domestic lobbying performed on behalf of a foreign client to influence Korean government action, or services used by a permanent establishment in Korea, remain taxable at 10% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued via Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Korea
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export sales contract signed by the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation (Inward Remittance Certificate) from a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Proof of payment received in foreign currency corresponding to the export invoice

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) regime under the NTS HomeTax system. All VAT invoices issued by a Chusik Hoesa must be prepared in electronic form through the official HomeTax e-invoice service, a licensed accounting program, or an NTS-integrated API channel, unless a narrow statutory paper invoice exemption applies. Every e-invoice is transmitted to NTS in real time and must receive an automatic internet transmission confirmation number before it is treated as legally issued. The NTS captures invoice data through API, applies digital signature and electronic stamping requirements, and reconciles the transmitted data against the VAT return pre-filing system. Failure to transmit an e-invoice through the system is treated as failure to issue a valid tax invoice and attracts the statutory additional tax under the VAT Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly verify export zero-rating claims by matching UNI-PASS export declarations, the e-invoice transmission database, and foreign exchange purchase/settlement records reported by Korean banks through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Any zero-rated supply lacking a valid export declaration, missing foreign exchange receipt within the 180-day repatriation deadline (unless a lawful extension is obtained), or otherwise failing to satisfy statutory zero-rating conditions will be reclassified as taxable, and the VAT is immediately clawed back with interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, false customs declarations, and fictitious export documentation additionally incur an evader additional tax commonly equal to 40% of the underlying VAT amount, late payment surcharges, and criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. For serious export fraud, the responsible directors and tax agents may be jointly and severally liable, and the statutory collection period is extended to a maximum of ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a dualist foreign exchange control regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current account transactions are substantially liberalized: trade settlements, service fees, dividends, and salaries can be remitted freely after a licensed foreign exchange bank performs an underlying transaction review to verify the business reality and the accuracy of the designated foreign exchange purpose code. Capital account transactions, including equity subscriptions, loans, and securities investments, are subject to a report-and-monitor system rather than a prior licensing regime for most direct foreign investment, but directional restrictions and post-transaction reporting obligations may apply under the Capital Transactions Regulations. The Korean won is convertible for current purposes but is not freely tradable offshore; cross-border KRW settlement is restricted to residents and designated settlement accounts, compelling non-residents to fund their capital injections in USD, EUR, JPY, or CNY. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are approvable once audited financial statements, tax withholding certificates, and proof of a properly registered foreign investment are presented; a bank will reject the remittance if the company has not completed its statutory external audit, has unresolved tax arrears, or cannot demonstrate a legitimate capital base. There is no central-bank rationing system, but the FSS enforces strict underlying transaction and suspicious-transaction checks, and the Bank of Korea operates the large-value payment and settlement infrastructure. On balance, the Korean regime is administratively monitored and transparent rather than stringently controlled; no mandatory currency surrender or conversion quota applies to current receipts, but counterparty banks have full discretion to demand documentary proof.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be repatriated and settled within six months from the export declaration or shipment date, unless a bank-approved extension is granted for deferred-payment or long-cycle exports. Proceeds must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank and credited to either a Korean won account or a foreign currency deposit account. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may retain foreign currency freely in a domestic foreign currency deposit account, but the receipt must be reported and confirmed through the Foreign Exchange Information System and by the bank, and the Korea Customs Service may require a proof of receipt when verifying trade settlement eligibility. Any failure to meet the six-month collection timeline without a valid extension creates a legal block to future export financing and VAT export incentives.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); foreign exchange supervisory authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act is primarily the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), with monitoring and enforcement delegated to the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) and designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low to not viable as a primary treasury rail. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a non-bank electronic money institution is not authorized to open a Korean won deposit account with the full legal attributes of a corporate demand deposit account or to execute the statutory foreign investment registration and capital verification procedures required for a Chusik Hoesa. Offshore fintech EMIs such as global business account providers are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as foreign exchange banks, so they cannot submit the mandatory Foreign Exchange Information System reports, issue foreign investment deposit certificates, or perform the underlying transaction verification required for Korean corporate banking. An EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment channel for small service-related outflows if the actual funds are ultimately settled through a domestically licensed designated bank; however, inbound capital injection, dividend remittance, and regulatory capital verification must be handled with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Chusik Hoesa must follow the Foreign Investment Promotion Act route. The non-resident investor should first file a foreign investment notification with a designated domestic bank or KOTRA and obtain a foreign investment registration signal, then transfer the equity subscription amount from an offshore shareholder account via SWIFT MT103 into the Foreign Currency Investment Deposit Account opened at the chosen Tier-1 domestic bank. The payment message must carry the appropriate Foreign Exchange Information System purpose code for direct equity investment and the corporate beneficiary name exactly matching the Korean business registration. Upon receipt, the bank issues a certificate of foreign investment, which is then used to obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate and to satisfy the capital verification requirement through the certified external auditor. For an already-incorporated company, the capital increase must also comply with the Korean Commercial Act by amending the articles, filing the registry, and issuing shares only after the subscription price is fully paid into the bank. Funds must not be split into multiple unnamed deposits, as the full remittance trail must support source-of-funds review and the later dividend remittance approval.Outbound retainer payments for cross-border trade policy and lobbying services should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 from the company's Korean won or foreign currency settlement account, with a purpose code matching current account service transactions and underlying advisory agreements retained for five years. For merchandise trade, the entity may use commercial letters of credit issued by the domestic bank in favour of foreign sellers, with the bill of lading and shipping documents negotiated under a complying presentation, or documentary collections on D/P and D/A terms where the bank acts as collecting agent. All outbound disbursements, whether fees or trade proceeds, are subject to the bank's underlying transaction review, and the company must present invoices, contracts, customs declarations where applicable, and beneficiary due-diligence information before the bank executes the transfer.Onshore forward exchange contracts with a designated foreign exchange bank for hedging identified KRW/USD, KRW/EUR, KRW/JPY, or KRW/CNY trade and investment exposure up to 12 months, provided the underlying receivables or payables are documented and the transaction is reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with licensed Korean banks or through an approved international NDF market only when the corporate has genuine forecast foreign exchange exposure and the position is demonstratively non-speculative; pure carry or directional trades are prohibited for domestic Korean corporates.Cross-currency swaps and interest rate swaps concluded with a domestic bank as hedge for foreign-currency denominated debt, equity valuation exposure, or long-term service contracts, subject to prior bank approval, board authorisation, and capital transaction reporting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low to not viable as a primary treasury rail. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a non-bank electronic money institution is not authorized to open a Korean won deposit account with the full legal attributes of a corporate demand deposit account or to execute the statutory foreign investment registration and capital verification procedures required for a Chusik Hoesa. Offshore fintech EMIs such as global business account providers are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as foreign exchange banks, so they cannot submit the mandatory Foreign Exchange Information System reports, issue foreign investment deposit certificates, or perform the underlying transaction verification required for Korean corporate banking. An EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment channel for small service-related outflows if the actual funds are ultimately settled through a domestically licensed designated bank; however, inbound capital injection, dividend remittance, and regulatory capital verification must be handled with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

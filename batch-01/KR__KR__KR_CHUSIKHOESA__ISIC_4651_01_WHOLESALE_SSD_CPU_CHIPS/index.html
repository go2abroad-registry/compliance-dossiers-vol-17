Applicable AML statute: South Korea's AML/CFT legal framework is principally the Act on Reporting and Using Specific Financial Transaction Information (Act No. 6516, as last amended, often called the Financial Transaction Reports Act), its Presidential Enforcement Decree, and the Real Name Financial Transactions Act. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Chusik Hoesa. All subscribed shares must be fully paid before registration, and the paid-in capital must be deposited in a domestic Korean bank in the Company's name or in a subscription-custody account. The bank issues the paid-in capital certificate required for registration. No formal blocked escrow-account rule exists under the Commercial Act; however, foreign investors must comply with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and maintain inbound telegraphic-transfer or foreign-exchange purchase records for the paid-in capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; official electronic portal: HomeTax (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr. Korea Customs Service (KCS) operates the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes a Korean place of business, permanent establishment, or VAT-registerable supply activity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (business registration number) from the district tax office with jurisdiction over the Korean place of business. On approval, the 10-digit Business Registration Number serves concurrently as the Corporate Tax TIN and the VAT ID. The registration application may be submitted in person by the foreign corporation's local tax representative or certified tax accountant, or electronically through the NTS HomeTax portal using the corporation's digital certificate. Required evidentiary documents generally include: home-country articles of incorporation and certificate of incorporation/good standing (apostilled or legalized as required), board resolution appointing a Korean tax/agent representative, power of attorney, passport/ID copies of the signing local representative, evidence of the Korean business premises (lease agreement or ownership certificate), and, if a branch is registered, the Korean court-stamped branch registration certificate. The same registration is used for VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding obligations; the taxpayer must obtain a tax representative for service of process under the Framework Act on National Taxes if no Korean resident is appointed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - compulsory VAT registration on commencement of business; the Korean VAT Act does not provide a general monetary exemption threshold for VAT registration. A simplified taxable-person filing regime exists for certain small individual entrepreneurs whose prior-year annual taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 80,000,000, but this does not eliminate the registration requirement or apply to a stock corporation acting as a wholesale exporter.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT zero-rating applies under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act to goods actually exported from Korea. The statutory conditions require: (1) the seller is a VAT-registered Korean exporter; (2) the goods are sold to a foreign non-resident buyer or foreign purchaser; (3) an export declaration is lodged and accepted through Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS); (4) the goods are physically shipped outside the Korean customs territory; (5) a zero-rated tax invoice is issued to the foreign buyer; and (6) the export proceeds are collected through a permitted foreign exchange transaction and the payment evidence is retained. The sale must be an arm's-length commercial sale, and the exporter must preserve sufficient customs and foreign-exchange records to substantiate the physical export and value. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where consumed outside Korea and paid in foreign currency; however, under the ISIC 4651 wholesale of SSD and CPU chips, goods exports are the controlling transaction type.

  • Export Cargo Clearance Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS export declaration acceptance record)
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading for ocean shipments or Master Air Waybill for air shipments
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate / Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) issued to the overseas buyer

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-invoice) system through the NTS HomeTax platform. All VAT-registered businesses that issue tax invoices are required to issue, transmit, and store e-invoices electronically through HomeTax, an authorized e-document service provider, or an ERP integration using NTS-approved APIs; paper tax invoices remain only in permitted exceptional circumstances. There is no pre-clearance or real-time fiscal stamping regime like those used in certain other jurisdictions. Anti-fraud controls rely on unique e-invoice numbers, digital certification, cross-matching of e-invoices with VAT returns and customs export records, and prescribed statutory transmission deadlines (generally aligned with quarterly VAT return periods).

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service use the export declaration and bank foreign-exchange receipt records as the primary audit trail for export zero-rating. If the exporter fails to produce the mandatory documentation bundle, or if the export is shown to be fictitious, the proceeds are not received within the prescribed repatriation term (generally 180 days from export clearance unless a bank-approved extension is obtained), or the payment is received through a non-qualifying channel, the 0% VAT rate is retroactively disallowed. NTS will assess the unpaid VAT at 10%, add statutory additional tax for late/improper filing, impose interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and may reassess corporate income tax on the unreported foreign-exchange income. Refunds already paid to the exporter are clawed back with the statutory extra tax and interest. For fraudulent documentation, criminal sanctions are available under the Value-Added Tax Act and related customs and foreign-exchange statutes, and the statutory assessment limitation period extends to ten years for fraudulent or undisclosed transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalised but administratively supervised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. Current-account payments, including trade settlements, service charges, and remittances of distributable profits, are generally free of quantitative restriction; settlement must be routed through a registered foreign exchange bank or licensed foreign financial institution. Capital-account transactions, notably inward direct investment, acquisition of equity, loans, and external debt, remain subject to a post-acceptance risk management framework: an investor or domestic company must file a foreign-exchange declaration or obtain prior approval, depending on the transaction, and transfers against the resulting foreign-currency holding must preserve its traceable nature through the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System. Export proceeds need not be converted into KRW unless the exporter uses a domestic account; however, the export receipt must be realised within the statutory collection period under the relevant export report, and any discrepancy between invoice value and credited amount triggers an adverse-information review. Profit repatriation by a Korean entity requires a valid settlement certificate, audited distributable profits, and proof of payment of corporate income tax, plus a bank confirmation that the dividend distribution was duly filed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. In practice, banks act as gatekeepers, demanding underlying contracts, customs declarations, and shareholder resolutions before executing dividend or capital-account wires. Cross-border KRW clearing is available through domestic foreign exchange banks and the BOK-Wire+ system, but non-resident KRW accounts are subject to use restrictions, and synthetic offshore KRW products are not treated as permitted settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds for ordinary shipments of SSD/CPU goods must be received within 18 months from the date of the export declaration, unless the exporter obtains an extension from the designated foreign exchange bank before the deadline. There is no statutory percentage surrender requirement; the exporter may keep the full 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency, but the receipt must be recorded through a registered foreign exchange bank and driven into the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System. Any amount exceeding the invoice or retained beyond the deadline without extension is regarded as an unrecovered export reported item and may block future bank settlements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of the Republic of Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Banking Act restrict the execution of foreign exchange business and cross-border payment settlement to banks licensed by the MOEF and to registrants expressly permitted to conduct foreign exchange operations. Domestic simple payment institutions and offshore EMIs such as digital-only money transmitters cannot lawfully open KRW corporate operating accounts, perform export-proceeds surrender, or issue capital verification certificates for a Chusik Hoesa. Offshore EMIs are therefore viable only as invoicing or e-commerce payment acceptance tools in the counterparty's jurisdiction; they must never be used as the Korean treasury or settlement rail because their incoming transfers do not satisfy Korean customs, capital verification, and VAT export evidence rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward equity capital injection for the Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the beneficiary KRW/foreign currency settlement account at a Korea foreign exchange bank. The remitting bank must state the purpose code for inward direct investment under the Foreign Exchange Information System transaction classification table, and the Korean entity must file the foreign-exchange declaration with the receiving bank either pre-transaction or within the statutory grace period for non-trade capital receipts. The bank will allocate the incoming funds to a capital contribution ledger and issue a settlement certificate; an independent Korean certified public accountant must then verify the receipt, board resolution, share issuance details, and paid-in capital requirement to complete the statutory capital verification report for the registry.Outbound trade payments for wholesale SSD and CPU purchases should primarily settle through SWIFT MT103 for open-account transactions in USD or KRW where the commercial invoice is verified against the import declaration and the exporter's banking instructions. For first-time or high-value counterparty exposure, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) opened by the Korean bank and advised through the seller's confirming bank offers protection; Documentary Collections (DP/DA) are less recommended because document mismatch disputes can leave settlement stranded. Each disbursement must reference the underlying contract and customs identifier so the bank can map the payment to the beneficiary's export transaction and maintain a clean audit trail for the MOEF reporting system.Onshore deliverable USD-KRW forward contracts with a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, limited to the firm's documented underlying import/export exposure.KRW/USD cross-currency swaps for long-term trade-related financing or non-resident receivables, subject to exchange bank limits and the establishment of eligible underlying debt.FX options (European-style) with a licensed bank for hedging forecast trade cash flows where premium and strike are determined in the onshore KRW market.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) hedges are narrowly permitted only for entities with genuine trade exposure and are blocked for speculative or purely financial postures; offshore synthetic KRW products are not permissible settlement substitutes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korean Won (KRW): Local File must be prepared when total cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion (approx. USD 7.4M); Master File required when such transactions exceed KRW 50 billion (approx. USD 37M); Country-by-Country Report required when combined group revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion (approx. USD 740M). Documentation must be submitted within 30 days of an official request.

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in the Framework Act for National Taxes (FANT) permit the NTS to recharacterise transactions lacking economic substance. A Korean company must have a fixed place of business, active management, sufficient employees, and undertake Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea to satisfy the permanent establishment threshold and to claim treaty benefits. Failure to demonstrate substance can lead to denial of treaty benefits and the imputation of income to the controlling foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Willful Misreporting)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9(1) of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, evasion of corporate income tax is punishable by imprisonment of up to two years or a fine not exceeding twice the amount evaded. Additionally, the National Tax Basic Act imposes an unpaid tax surcharge of 3% per annum (up to 27 months) and a negligence penalty of 20%–40% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the evasion may be held personally liable for the corporate tax debt (joint and several), under Art. 16(1) of the Corporate Tax Act, if they consumed or concealed corporate assets. Criminal prosecution can lead to travel bans (blacklisted by the NTS), asset freezing, and imprisonment up to 2 years. Offshore accounts must be disclosed under the Foreign Exchange Act, failure to do so adds 1 year imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance (Non-Resident Payments)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, the payer becomes liable for the un-withheld tax plus a 10% negligence penalty (or 20% in cases of deliberate non-withholding), and daily surcharges of 0.0225%. Additionally, Article 16 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act imposes a fine of up to KRW 30 million or imprisonment up to 2 years for willful failure to withhold.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign off on payments without a valid treaty relief application are personally responsible for the withheld amount; the NTS may issue a personal collection notice and a ban on travel (blacklisting). If the director uses cross-border accounts to disguise payments, money-laundering charges under the Specific Financial Information Act may attach, leading to asset confiscation and up to 5 years imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 10 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (ITA Act), the tax authority may recompute taxable income at arm's-length and impose an additional tax of up to 30% of the understated taxable income. Failure to produce TP reports upon request results in a civil penalty of up to KRW 100 million and denial of offsetting adjustments for the foreign counterparty. Thin capitalisation interest disallowance is 30% of the excess interest paid.

Director personal liability: The 'supervising director' may be personally fined up to KRW 10 million for gross negligence in maintaining adequate TP documentation. Criminal charges may be filed if the manipulation is intentional, with imprisonment up to 2 years. In severe cases, the NTS can seek a court order to freeze personal assets of the directors and revoke the corporate business licence.

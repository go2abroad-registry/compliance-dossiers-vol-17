Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the amended Commercial Act. The subscribed shares must be fully paid in before incorporation registration, and the deposit certificate of a Korean financial institution must evidence the paid-in amount. The minimum par value is KRW 100 per share, so the theoretical minimum is KRW 100 if only one share is issued, but Korean banks and registry practice generally expect a de minimis operating capital deposit of at least KRW 1,000,000. The official state filing fee is not fixed; the court registry will assess registration tax based on actual capital and provide a bill at filing.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a passive paper SPV occupying only a leased office. If the entity uses physical office space, the building must maintain fire safety completion certificates, and the lessee must ensure compliance with the Fire Services Act and the Occupational Safety and Health Act to obtain civil defense and workplace safety clearance from the local fire department and the Ministry of Employment and Labor.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: NTS HomeTax system at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Chusik Hoesa, complete commercial registration at the competent district court or the Supreme Court electronic registration system under the Commercial Act to obtain the corporate registration number. Then, within 20 days after business commencement, file a Business Registration Application through the NTS HomeTax portal with the competent district tax office. The application must include the articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, lease contract proving the domestic place of business, identity documents of the representative, and, for foreign investors, the foreign investment report certificate and investment registration certificate issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Upon approval, the NTS assigns the statutory 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the VAT and corporate income tax identifier and must be used on all tax invoices and filings. A foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business is not obliged to obtain a local BRN solely for offshore activities; however, if it becomes liable to Korean VAT or wishes to claim treaty benefits or input VAT refunds, it must register via HomeTax as a foreign corporation through a Korean domestic tax representative, submitting the foreign entity's registration certificate, articles or equivalent constitutional documents, a board resolution, and the domestic tax representative appointment form. The entity must also open a local corporate bank account, obtain access to the HomeTax e-invoice system, and, if physical goods are to be exported, obtain a Korea Customs Service exporter code through an export declaration EDI application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for a corporation. Every business operator making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT; the KRW 80 million simplified taxpayer turnover threshold applies only to individual entrepreneurs and does not apply to a Chusik Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Articles 21 and 22 of the Value Added Tax Act for goods physically exported from Korea with a valid Korea Customs Service export declaration, goods deemed exported under statutory bonded transactions, and qualifying services supplied to non-residents or performed entirely outside Korea where the benefit is not enjoyed in Korea and the consideration is settled in foreign exchange or an approved Foreign Exchange Transactions Act method. For this private equity buyout SPV, syndicated loan arrangement, transaction structuring, and buyout advisory fees invoiced to a non-resident target, fund, or investor can qualify for 0% VAT if the recipient is a non-resident, the service is consumed offshore, and the foreign exchange is repatriated to Korea within the mandatory collection window; dividends, interest, and pure equity financing returns remain exempt financial supplies rather than zero-rated supplies.

  • Korea Customs Service export declaration acceptance notice (EDI export clearance report) evidencing actual export or deemed export status
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export or syndicated service contract
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or postal dispatch certificate for goods; for services, a certificate of completion and offshore location-of-benefit analysis
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank receipt issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Overseas buyer's certificate of business existence, non-residency, or foreign tax residency certificate
  • Statement of foreign currency settlement and reconciliation of the export declaration to the bank remittance advice

All Korean VAT registered businesses must issue an NTS-approved electronic tax invoice through the HomeTax platform or a certified EDI service provider no later than the day following the supply date. The NTS assigns a unique approval number and performs electronic cross-matching between issuer and recipient; this e-invoice submission to HomeTax functions as the national fiscal clearance requirement. There is no separate physical digital stamping device; the NTS e-tax invoice approval number and the mandatory real-time electronic transmission through HomeTax Open API or certified EDI constitute the anti-fraud fiscal validation mechanism. Retail cash sales must additionally be recorded through the Cash Receipt System.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit the physical export movement, invoice chain, contract authenticity, and foreign exchange repatriation. Failure to substantiate the zero-rating documentation will cause the NTS to reverse the 0% rate, assess 10% output VAT, and impose statutory additional taxes of 10% for negligent reporting or 20% for fraudulent reporting, together with tax invoice penalties for missing or invalid e-tax invoices. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the mandatory 180-day deadline triggers Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalties including fines and criminal sanctions, denial of input VAT credit related to the export, retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds previously granted, and extension of the statute of limitations for intentional evasion or disguised transactions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is classified as a freely convertible currency on the current account for trade in goods and services, with no mandatory surrender to the central bank for ordinary export proceeds; however, capital account transactions are subject to a notification or license regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For a private equity buyout SPV, inbound equity injections from non-resident investors require a Foreign Exchange Declaration at a designated foreign exchange bank, and the proceeds must be credited to a Foreign Investor Investment Account (외국인투자기업 전용계좌) for capital verification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is permissible once audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates are presented, but remittances exceeding certain thresholds require supporting transactional evidence to prevent disguised capital flight. The won's offshore convertibility is limited; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are traded offshore, while onshore forward contracts require underlying real demand and are restricted to hedgers with verifiable commercial exposure. Surrender of export proceeds is effectively automatic through banking channels, but exporters must report receipts within a legally prescribed period, and any retention of funds abroad beyond the limit requires prior approval. Overall, the regime is transparent and liberal compared to Asian peers, yet compliance with the Korean customs and foreign exchange reporting system remains rigorous, especially for leveraged acquisitions where debt-to-equity ratios are scrutinized under thin capitalization rules. The SPV must maintain meticulous transfer pricing and fund-flow documentation to avoid adverse administrative penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received and settled through a domestic foreign exchange bank within 6 months from the date of export declaration unless extended approval is granted by the MOEF. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into KRW for current account receipts; exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic accounts, but must file a Foreign Exchange Receipt Report within 30 days of collection. For the SPV, any incidental export trade or service income would adopt the same timeline, and failure to repatriate within the six-month window triggers a penalty and a forced conversion order.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for non-bank offshore fintech EMIs due to the Korean Financial Services Commission's regulatory stance that only licensed financial institutions may offer multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services to resident entities. EMIs without domestic banking licenses cannot execute the mandatory Foreign Exchange Declaration or hold the Foreign Investor Investment Account required for capital verification. Even as settlement agents, they would lack clearing access to the local Korean Won interbank system and would require a commercial bank partner, negating the benefit. The legal constraint is absolute under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and any unlicensed operation would constitute illegal money transmission.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital for the PE buyout SPV must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas fund account to the SPV's Foreign Investor Capital Escrow Account at a Korean Tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must include a Foreign Exchange Declaration purpose code '921000 - Investment in stocks of a Korean corporation' for equity contributions, or '921300 - Long-term loan borrowings' for shareholder loans. On receipt, the bank verifies the declaration and issues a Negotiation Confirmation, then transfers the funds to the SPV's operating account after capital verification with the Korean Securities Depository or the local investment authority. For amounts exceeding KRW 5 billion, prior notification to the MOEF is required. No cash conversion is mandatory; the SPV may hold USD/EUR in escrow until the acquisition closing.Trade settlements from the SPV to overseas sellers are executed via SWIFT MT103 for standard purchase payments, with irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) used for gross goods acquisitions. For service-based or digital content payments, MT103 with purpose code '323000 - Professional services' is preferred. Documentary collections are acceptable for low-value transactions under USD 200,000, but the SPV's internal policy defaults to L/C for any disbursement over USD 1 million to maintain an audit trail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for non-bank offshore fintech EMIs due to the Korean Financial Services Commission's regulatory stance that only licensed financial institutions may offer multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services to resident entities. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 (10 billion won) in aggregate transactions with foreign related parties in a business year triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation and submission with the corporate income tax return; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the Korean ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue is KRW 100,000,000,000 (100 billion won) or more.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations registration, but the National Tax Basic Act Article 14 gives tax authorities a substance-over-form power, and the LCITA and OECD BEPS anti-abuse provisions require a Chusik Hoesa SPV to demonstrate real economic activity through its core income-generating activities (CIGA): holding and financing decisions, covenant monitoring, board meetings, treasury, risk management, and employment. A passive private-equity SPV with nominal capital, no employees, no bank account substance, and no decision-making in Korea is vulnerable to a finding of corporate fiction; treaty benefits are denied, dividends are recharacterized, related-party interest and fees are disallowed, and its separate legal personality may be pierced by the courts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 and Article 8, the responsible company and director face criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 10 years for habitual or aggravated evasion and fines up to five times the tax evaded; the National Tax Service will additionally assess an unfair-return penalty of 40% of the underreported tax, plus 0.03% daily late payment interest, and may reopen settled years under the 10-year fraud assessment period.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: under Korean Commercial Act Articles 399/401, the director is jointly and severally liable to the company and third parties for false tax filings and fraudulent conduct. Civil asset restitution may be collected from the director's personal bank accounts, shares, and real estate; the director may be subjected to a departure ban/blacklisting under the Immigration Act for unpaid national tax delinquencies exceeding KRW 50 million, and criminal imprisonment for up to 10 years in aggravated tax-evasion cases.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and Economic-Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may invoke substance-over-form under National Tax Basic Act Article 14 and the general anti-avoidance rules in LCITA Article 17 to deny DTA benefits, recharacterize the arrangement, disallow intercompany deductions, impose the 22% non-resident withholding tax on deemed dividends/royalties/interest, and assess a 40% misuse-of-law tax penalty together with interest. Criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may apply if a false DTA application or forged TRC is used.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign a treaty-relief package for a shell SPV can be personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties. Korean courts may disregard the separate legal personality of the Chusik Hoesa, treating the SPV's assets and income as the foreign shareholder's or parent's assets, exposing directors to civil restitution claims, attachment of personal property, prohibition on giving testimony under privilege, and criminal sanctions for aiding and abetting tax evasion.
AML / Foreign Exchange / Cross-Border Financing Infractions

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its enforcement decrees are subject to imprisonment up to 3 years or fines up to KRW 100 million, plus confiscation of the proceeds of unlicensed foreign-exchange transactions; tax authorities may disallow deductions for unreported cross-border expenses and impose an additional 20%-40% documentary-penalty surcharge on the related corporate tax under the Basic Act for National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind the SPV: the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalizes the actual person who executes the unregistered payment, and the Korean Commercial Act makes directors jointly liable for losses caused by reckless financial handling. Directors may face travel bans, passport revocation, and exit blacklisting while investigations are pending; if the unreported remittances are tied to money laundering, anti-money-laundering laws subject directors to criminal forfeiture and imprisonment, and the director's declaration of personal bankruptcy does not discharge tax-related public law liabilities.

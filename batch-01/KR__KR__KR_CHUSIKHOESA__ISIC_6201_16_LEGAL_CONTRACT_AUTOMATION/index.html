Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; one share may have any par value and the company may be registered with nominal capital such as KRW 10,000. The full subscribed capital must be paid into a domestic bank deposit account before the incorporation application is filed, and the bank balance certificate must be attached to the court filing. The deposit account is functionally blocked because funds may only be transferred to the company's operating account after the court registration is completed. There is no separate government escrow beyond this statutory bank deposit rule.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax representative (with a power of attorney) and file an application for business registration (사업자등록) with the local district tax office having jurisdiction over the representative's address. The application must include the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of foreign registration, and a statement of domestic place of business (if any). The NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (TIN) within 3-5 business days. For VAT purposes, the same number serves as the VAT registration number. The tax representative is jointly liable for tax compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated if the goods are shipped out of Korea and the export declaration is filed with Korea Customs Service. Qualifying offshore digital services (including SaaS, e-signature, and cloud document automation) are zero-rated if the recipient is a non-resident, the service is consumed outside Korea, and payment is received in foreign currency. The service must be classified as an 'export of services' under the VAT Act and must not be a domestic supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - Form 30
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation of Receipt

Mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) issuance for all B2B transactions, submitted to the NTS via HomeTax API within the day following the supply. Real-time clearance is required for e-Tax Invoices; anti-fraud digital stamping is not used, but the system includes automated cross-checking of invoices against VAT returns. Cash receipts are mandatory for B2C transactions.

The NTS may conduct post-export audits to verify the authenticity of zero-rated supplies. If documentation is incomplete or fraudulent, the zero-rating is reversed, and the taxpayer is liable for the 10% VAT plus an additional 40% penalty on the unpaid tax. Criminal prosecution may apply for deliberate fraud, with fines up to 5 times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a comprehensively regulated but substantially convertible foreign exchange regime. The Bank of Korea supervises the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS), while the Ministry of Economy and Finance establishes policy for current and capital account transactions. Current account items for a legal technology SaaS company, such as subscription fees, software licensing, e-signature authentication fees, and associated service imports/exports, are freely convertible through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank after the required FEIS declaration and supporting contract/invoice are provided. There is no surrender obligation for export proceeds and no tax on the act of conversion, although the remitter must evidence the commercial underlying. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity capital, cross-border loans, and dividend repatriation, are reportable and somewhat controlled; inbound direct investment is accepted through temporary FDI deposit accounts and notification to KOTRA or the delegated bank. Profit remittance is lawful after audited financials, tax clearance, and proof of original FDI notification are produced. Non-resident KRW trading remains restricted, offshore NDF activity is monitored, and all onshore FX settlements must be executed through a licensed domestic Korean bank. The overall control regime is therefore a strict documentary convertibility system rather than fully unrestricted or fully prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts from software and digital services must be collected and received through a domestic Korean foreign exchange bank within six months from the date the relevant export declaration or foreign exchange contract is filed, unless the bank or the Bank of Korea grants an extension for justified commercial delay. There is currently no mandatory surrender or forced conversion ratio; the exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency receipt in a domestic foreign currency account or convert it to Korean won at its discretion. The receipt must nonetheless be reported on the FEIS and the company must preserve the export contracts, e-signature transaction logs, invoices, and payment processor settlement statements for potential AML/CDD and tax audit inspection.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and licensed foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs generally cannot operate as the primary treasury settlement layer for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. A foreign EMI that lacks registration under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act is not authorized to open a domestic won account, receive KRW on behalf of the company, or issue electronic payment instruments in Korea. Platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut Business can be used only as a non-resident collection gateway for foreign clients and for initiating an inbound SWIFT wire into a licensed Korean bank account. Domestic statutory FX reporting, capital verification, withholding-tax and trade settlement obligations must remain with a tier-1 Korean commercial bank. The practical viability rating for full EMI treasury management is Low; the acceptable viability for EMI-assisted collections is Medium if the EMI acts solely as a channel and the funds ultimately land in the Korean bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a two-phase FDI deposit process. The foreign shareholder wires capital to a temporary Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account opened in the name of the Chusik Hoesa at a tier-1 Korean bank, using a SWIFT MT103 payment order coded for inward direct investment under FIPA. On confirmation of the proceeds, the bank files the foreign exchange receipt declaration through FEIS, and the company or the bank simultaneously notifies KOTRA or the delegated foreign exchange bank; the investor then completes the foreign investment registration. After the capital contribution is recorded and the accountant issues a capital verification or paid-in capital certificate, the funds are transferred from the temporary account to the domestic KRW operating account at the prevailing bank conversion rate. For subsequent share subscriptions, share issuance records and director resolutions are required. All supporting documents are retained in the bank as evidence of legalized inward capital.For a LegalTech SaaS entity, recurring licence, subscription, API usage, and contractual automation fees should be settled through open-account SWIFT MT103 remittances from the Korean operating account to overseas counterparties, with the bank completing the outgoing payment declaration on FEIS. Services exports and imports should be accompanied by an intercompany or third-party SaaS agreement, a withholding-tax invoice, and a delivery performance report. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) and documentary collections are legally available but commercially inefficient for intangible digital services; they should be reserved for consignment hardware, security modules or physical signature-device sales, with the corporate treasury using MT103 for ordinary operating payments and sight drafts or standby letters of credit only when the counterparty demands a bank settlement undertaking.Onshore deliverable Korean won FX forwards with an expressly documented underlying service-export receivable, intercompany licensing payable or capital-account exposure, executed with a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank and reported under FEIS.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts for hedging Korean won cash-flow risk in jurisdictions where physical KRW settlement is unavailable, provided the transaction is concluded through a licensed Korean bank or its authorized offshore subsidiary and is tied to a real commercial exposure.Cross-currency interest rate swaps and plain-vanilla FX swaps restricted to hedging actual foreign-currency loans, dividend repatriation timelines or material intercompany settlements, with no speculative leverage.Zero-cost collars and participating forwards may be entertained by Korean banks only when the underlying transaction is transparent and fully documented; barrier options, binary options and speculative FX trading are not permissible treasury instruments.

The practical viability rating for full EMI treasury management is Low; the acceptable viability for EMI-assisted collections is Medium if the EMI acts solely as a channel and the funds ultimately land in the Korean bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national headline schedule (9% up to KRW 200,000,000; 19% from KRW 200,000,000 to KRW 20,000,000,000; 21% from KRW 20,000,000,000 to KRW 300,000,000,000; 24% above KRW 300,000,000,000), plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax, producing a combined top effective rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory withholding on outbound dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% national corporate income tax plus 2% local income tax, unless reduced under an applicable bilateral income tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5,000,000,000 in a fiscal year. Master File preparation is required when the Korean covered entity's annual revenue exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 and the global group satisfies the applicable Korean threshold; Country-by-Country reporting follows the OECD threshold of EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue, approximated at KRW 1,000,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the UAE-style ESR label, but has an equivalent substance-over-form rule under Corporate Tax Act Article 14 and the general anti-avoidance rule in the Framework Act on National Taxes. For cross-border LegalTech and SaaS structures, the OECD Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is decisive: a Korean entity with local developers, contract-automation employees, data servers, and final contracting authority cannot be reduced to a zero-risk service provider. An offshore shell with no CIGA in the invoice jurisdiction will be collapsed and reassessed to the Korean operating company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company / economic substance evasion

Statutory sanction: The Korean tax authority may deny the intercompany royalty and service-fee deductions, recharacterize the transactions under Corporate Tax Act Article 14 and the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose a 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty on the reassessed corporate tax, and add daily delinquency interest of 0.022% from the original due date. The non-resident may also lose DTA relief for substantive treaty abuse.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Korean entity is a mere alter ego of the non-resident parent. Directors and shadow directors can be subject to secondary tax liability under the Framework Act on National Taxes, civil restitution of transferred assets, and exit bans or blacklisting under National Tax Collection procedures for unpaid assessments. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may lead to imprisonment of up to 2 years, or up to 5 years where the tax evaded exceeds KRW 300,000,000, plus a fine of up to 3 times the tax evaded.
Cross-border profit shifting through fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may re-determine the Korean taxable income under the Corporate Tax Act and assess a 40% additional tax for fraudulent underreporting, a 20% additional tax for failure to file, and withholding tax additions on undeclared deemed dividends or under withheld royalty payments. Interest on arrears and asset attachment follow automatically.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in or knowingly benefited from the false invoicing scheme face personal civil liability for traceable transferred assets, travel bans and tax blacklisting, and criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment up to 5 years in aggravated circumstances plus a fine equal to 3 times the tax evaded; they may also be prohibited from serving as directors under Korean commercial regulation.
Transfer pricing documentation and reporting failure

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act and its enforcement decree, failure to submit transfer-pricing documentation triggers a penalty of 30% of the tax adjustment made by the tax authority, capped by an additional fixed penalty of up to KRW 100,000,000, and the authority may apply an estimated arm's-length price without accepting the taxpayer's unsupported CUP.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax declaration and knowingly fail to maintain or disclose TP documentation can be held personally liable as secondary taxpayers for penalty surcharges; the National Tax Service may impose exit bans, blacklist the company and its directors, and pursue criminal action under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the false or missing documentation conceals assessable Korean-source income, with prison sentences up to 5 years and fines up to three times the underpaid tax.

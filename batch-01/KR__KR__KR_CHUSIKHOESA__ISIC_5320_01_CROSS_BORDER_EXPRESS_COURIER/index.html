Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the registered office for a Chusik Hoesa operating an international time-definite parcel courier business in Korea. The Commercial Act requires the registered address to be an actual physical place within Korea at which the company can be legally summoned and at which statutory records and books are maintained. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport and municipal tax authorities require licensed express courier operators to have a permanent physical place of business, including a loading/delivery depot, vehicle parking area, and employee supervision office. For the cross-border express courier license under ISIC 5320, the competent licensing authority will physically inspect the address before issuing the statutory operating license. In addition, the Korean Financial Intelligence Unit and Korean commercial banks uniformly reject virtual office addresses for merchant acquirer registration under Credit_Card_POS and ACH_Direct payment rails, because such addresses do not permit on-site AML/KYC verification and increase the risk of unexplained beneficial ownership. Thus, neither tax authorities nor banks will recognize a P.O. Box or virtual suite as the principal place of business in this industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None under the Commercial Act. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. The promoters must specify the par value and the number of issued shares in the Articles of Incorporation. All subscription amounts must be fully paid into a domestic bank subscription account or a financial institution escrow account before incorporation registration; such funds remain restricted and may not be released or distributed until the company has been validly registered. If registration fails or is rejected, the deposited subscription amounts must be returned to each subscriber in accordance with the Commercial Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr). Export and customs clearance processes are administered through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic clearance portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes a Korean operating presence, whether through a Chusik Hoesa (Korean stock corporation) or a branch, must file a business registration application with the district tax office or through the NTS HomeTax System before commencing taxable activity. For a foreign corporation without a domestic place of business, a tax representative must be appointed under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and that representative must submit the business registration application, constitutive documents of the foreign entity, board resolution or power of attorney, evidence of foreign investment registration, and details of the domestic place of business. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which simultaneously serves as the corporate tax identification number and the VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident exporter with no Korean fixed place of business may alternatively obtain a customs identification code from the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS solely for export declaration purposes, but it must complete full VAT business registration through the NTS if it makes any taxable supply in Korea. All registrations, amendments, and electronic tax invoice submissions are processed through the NTS HomeTax System.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - Korea does not provide check-the-box entity classification or hybrid mismatch elections. A Chusik Hoesa is inherently a resident corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income. It may elect a fiscal year, and eligible domestic affiliated corporations may elect consolidated group filing under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are counteracted under the International Tax Adjustment Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold is prescribed for corporate entities; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Korea. Small individual merchants with annual turnover not exceeding KRW 48 million may qualify for simplified taxable treatment, but a Chusik Hoesa is a general taxpayer and must register regardless of turnover. A non-resident foreign supplier making taxable supplies in Korea is also required to register without any de minimis exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically removed from Korea under an export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service and are sold to a non-resident purchaser. Cross-border express courier services are zero-rated when the courier services relate to the export transport of such goods, are supplied to foreign persons, and are paid in foreign currency. The zero rate applies to the international transport and delivery element; domestic pickup, sorting, and final delivery services within Korea remain taxable at 10% unless statutorily zero-rated as part of the export supply.

  • Korean Customs Service export declaration acceptance notice (UNI-PASS electronic export declaration)
  • Commercial invoice for the exported goods and courier service charges
  • Air waybill or bill of lading evidencing removal of goods from Korea
  • Packing list and consolidated courier shipment manifest with item-level export declarations
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange settlement certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Consignee and non-resident purchaser documentation supporting the foreign destination of the goods

All VAT taxpayers are required to issue tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system (e-Tax Invoice). Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS no later than the day following issuance, and each invoice receives an NTS-assigned electronic invoice number and transmission timestamp. The NTS performs near-real-time intake, automatic matching of output and input tax invoices, and digital anomaly detection on the HomeTax platform. Paper tax invoices are not permitted for general taxpayers except in limited statutory exceptions approved by the NTS. The system serves as Korea's anti-fraud fiscal clearance mechanism; there is no separate physical stamping requirement.

The National Tax Service may reverse export zero-rating and assess the unpaid 10% VAT where the required customs documentation, transport evidence, or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is missing or inconsistent. Clawback assessments include the understated VAT, an additional tax of 10% of the understated tax, monthly delinquency additions of 0.75% per month up to 36 months, and a false-invoice penalty of up to 30% of the input tax reclaimed or 40% of the tax base. Fictitious export documentation or deliberately fabricated zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may lead to criminal fines and imprisonment, with enhanced penalties where the tax amount exceeds KRW 300 million.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed, current-account-liberalised foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Legal-tender won is convertible for most current account transactions: cross-border trade settlements, service fees, royalties, dividends, and salary payments may be made through a licensed foreign exchange bank without prior monetary approval, so long as the supporting commercial documentation is available. The capital account is substantially open but remains subject to reporting and declaration requirements; foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa must be notified under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and external borrowings, loans, or securities issuance triggers separate BOK/MOEF declarations. There is no subsidiary-level mandatory surrender of export proceeds; however, all export settlements must be collected within the statutory payment period and reported through the bank. Profit remittances are freely payable after a legal dividend declaration, but practical hurdles include satisfying a certified dividend resolution, producing audited/un-audited financial statements, and providing a tax withholding certificate showing settlement of corporate income tax and remittance tax. Large one-off transfers above USD 10,000 equivalent are subject to bank beneficial-ownership and source-of-wealth verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 23 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK/MOEF enforcement rules, export proceeds must be collected through a licensed foreign exchange bank within the statutory collection window; for general service exports and routine express courier receivables, the standard recognised collection period is 18 months from the date of export declaration, with prior-reported extensions available for capital goods, construction, and long-maturity contracts. Korea does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio on general export earnings: 0% of the gross export invoice must be sold into Korean won, and the exporter may lawfully retain 100% of the funds in a KRW operating account or a foreign-currency current account at a local foreign exchange bank. The entire invoice amount must be collected; if collection fails by the deadline, the exporter must file a non-collection report with the competent customs office and the relevant bank within five business days, or alternatively apply for an extension with evidence of force majeure or commercial dispute. All settlement instructions must reference the export declaration number, invoice, and payment purpose code so the bank can satisfy BOK/MOEF reporting.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting as the statutory foreign exchange regulator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System jointly administered by BOK and MOEF; Korea Customs Service enforces cross-border trade reporting at the border.

Under the Corporate Tax Act, the annual CIT return must be filed within 3 months after the close of the fiscal year; for a December 31 fiscal year-end, the deadline is March 31 of the following year, and quarterly prepayments are due by the end of the first, second, and third quarters after the fiscal year begins.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korea requires Local File and Master File documentation when the Korean entity has controlled intercompany transactions with foreign related parties in aggregate exceeding KRW 50 billion and annual revenue equal to or exceeding KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion. Even below these thresholds, contemporaneous records are required for every controlled transaction to justify the pricing method and to avoid penalty risk.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is enforced through permanent establishment attribution, beneficial ownership requirements, the substance-over-form doctrine, General Anti-Abuse Rule, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test. A cross-border express courier entity must manage and control the Korean business in Korea, employ actual operational staff such as drivers and sorting personnel, maintain material assets such as vehicles and warehouses, and negotiate contracts directly with e-commerce clients; otherwise inbound profits may be reattributed to a foreign PE or foreign shell, and treaty benefits for dividends, interest, and royalties may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-border tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Unfaithful return penalties of 10%-20% of the underreported tax, daily interest arrears, and disallowance of all undocumented deductions under the Corporate Tax Act; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act carries imprisonment for up to 7 years and a fine, and the tax authority may seize assets of the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false return can have the corporate veil pierced, are jointly and severally liable under the Commercial Act for losses suffered by the company, and may be made secondarily liable for unpaid corporate taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes when the company's assets were concealed or improperly distributed. Courts may order civil asset restitution, impose a departure ban, place the director on the National Tax Service high-value delinquent blacklist, and impose consecutive imprisonment terms if multiple false returns were filed.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and artificial intercompany pricing

Statutory sanction: The Korean NTS may recompute taxable income at the arm's length price under the International Tax Adjustment Act, impose an underreporting penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted tax, and assess a documentation penalty for failure to provide required transfer pricing records. False or forged transfer pricing documentation can be criminalized as tax evasion with penalties of up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines for the responsible parties.

Director personal liability: The representative director and the finance director who sign the TP schedules and tax returns may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid adjusted tax and penalties when the court or tax authority finds that the corporation was used as a sham or that assets were transferred out to avoid collection. Directors face personal seizure of domestic and overseas bank accounts, travel blacklisting during the TP audit, and imprisonment where deliberate mispricing is proven; foreign directors are placed on the immigration watchlist and may be barred from re-entry until the tax liability is settled.
Withholding tax evasion, shell company abuse, and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: The NTS will recharacterize the payments as constructive dividends, assess the unpaid 22% withholding tax, impose a 10%-20% failure-to-withhold negligence penalty, add daily late-payment interest, and deny treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test. The Korean Chusik Hoesa as withholding agent is jointly liable for the unpaid WHT, and the shell entity is treated as fiscally transparent for Korean tax purposes.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs the sham service agreement or directs the non-payment of withholding tax is personally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties under the secondary liability regime, and the limited liability of the entity can be pierced where the corporate form is indispensable to committing the fraud. Directors may face civil restitution to the National Treasury, blacklisting on the immigration and tax enforcement watchlist, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment for up to 7 years; if the director is a foreign national, Korea may coordinate removal or extradition within the limits of mutual legal assistance treaties.

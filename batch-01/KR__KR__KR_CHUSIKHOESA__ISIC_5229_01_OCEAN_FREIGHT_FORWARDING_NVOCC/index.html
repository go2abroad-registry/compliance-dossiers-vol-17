Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered head office of a Korean Chusik Hoesa, and is expressly rejected for the maritime freight-forwarding business. National Tax Service business registration guidelines require a fixed address that is physically possessible and verifiable; the Maritime Transportation Act and its enforcement decree require a place in the Republic of Korea equipped to retain shipping records, bills of lading, tariff schedules and accounting documents. Korean banks that must issue the NVOCC financial guarantee will refuse to open a transaction account for a company with only a virtual office address. A virtual office may be registered only as an auxiliary business office, provided the corporate headquarters is physically staffed and a lease agreement and facility inspection report are produced.

Competent corporate registry

Registration Division of the District Court having jurisdiction over the registered office, with online filing through the Supreme Court of Korea Internet Registration Office (i-RO); the Seoul Central District Court Civil Affairs Division is the default registry for a Seoul registered office.

Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital under the amended Commercial Act for an ordinary Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price for all issued shares must be paid before filing and held in a blocked capital subscription deposit account at a bank or securities company under Article 290 of the Commercial Act; the bank-issued deposit certificate must accompany the registration application and funds may not be withdrawn until incorporation registration is completed. For the NVOCC activity within ISIC 5229, the specialized regulator additionally requires proof of financial soundness, typically paid-in capital of at least KRW 100,000,000 or an equivalent guarantee insurance policy, compliance with the NVOCC financial responsibility requirement, and a registered office in Korea. There is no separate escrow blocking beyond the statutory capital subscription deposit, except as demanded by the licensing agency at the time of the NVOCC registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a building occupancy certificate and a partial-use certificate for the leased office floor, install fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, exit signs, and automatic fire detection equipment, and file the firefighting facilities maintenance certificate with the local fire department under the Fire Services Act. Because the operations are administrative only, no structural load approval or process-safety review is required; however, under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, the company must designate a workplace safety and health manager when employing five or more workers and must complete a general hazard assessment and post the required safety notices.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of Korea, acting through the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered place of business, and the mandatory electronic filing portal is HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr) for business registration, electronic tax invoices, and VAT returns.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident shareholder forming a Korean Chusik Hoesa must ensure that the company, acting through its domestic representative director or place-of-business manager, files an Application for Business Registration with the competent NTS district tax office within 20 days from the commencement of business. The application must include the certificate of incorporation and articles of incorporation of the Chusik Hoesa, a foreign investor certificate or proof of directors and shareholding, a power of attorney appointing a tax representative if the representative is non-resident, a lease or proof of the domestic registered office, and a completed registration form. Upon approval the NTS issues the 10-digit corporate Business Registration Number (Taxpayer Identification Number for corporate tax and VAT) and a Business Registration Certificate. This number serves as the single TIN/VAT ID for all domestic tax filings. If the foreign corporate shareholder itself has no Korean place of business and only receives dividends or share proceeds, it does not register separately for VAT or corporate tax in Korea, but may need a withholding TIN for dividend income tax under the relevant income tax treaty.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident shareholder forming a Korean Chusik Hoesa must ensure that the company, acting through its domestic representative director or place-of-business manager, files an Application for Business Registration with the competent NTS district tax office within 20 days from the commencement of business. The application must include the certificate of incorporation and articles of incorporation of the Chusik Hoesa, a foreign investor certificate or proof of directors and shareholding, a power of attorney appointing a tax representative if the representative is non-resident, a lease or proof of the domestic registered office, and a completed registration form. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not apply U.S.-style check-the-box elections; the Chusik Hoesa is per se a resident corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Law and is a separate taxable person for Value-Added Tax purposes. No pass-through election or hybrid-minded entity classification exists under Korean domestic tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold for corporations. Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, a Chusik Hoesa carrying on any VAT-taxable business activity, including NVOCC ocean freight forwarding brokerage, is required to register for VAT with the NTS within 20 days of starting business regardless of revenue volume. Simplified taxpayer treatment is restricted to natural person sole proprietors with annual taxable supplies under the statutory threshold and is not available to a corporate entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act's zero-rating provisions, the supply of goods exported to a foreign country, the international transportation of goods or passengers, and services rendered by a domestic supplier to a non-resident under an export contract are eligible for the 0% VAT rate. For an NVOCC and ocean freight forwarder, zero-rating applies when the service relates to international ocean carriage of goods, or when the freight forwarding and brokerage service is supplied directly to a non-resident customer with consideration sourced from foreign currency. The taxpayer must maintain documentary evidence that the beneficiary is a non-resident, that the transportation involves international movement of cargo, and that the foreign currency consideration is received in Korea or repatriated into an authorized foreign exchange bank account.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Certificate (Export Clearance Certificate) for the underlying cargo when zero-rating is based on exported goods
  • Valid Bill of Lading (master or NVOCC house bill) showing the port of loading, port of discharge, consignee, and container or cargo details
  • Signed international freight forwarding service contract identifying the non-resident customer, the freight scope, and the foreign-currency consideration
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Chusik Hoesa clearly stating a 0% VAT rate and the export-service description
  • Foreign Exchange receipt confirmation from an authorized foreign exchange bank evidencing that the freight and brokerage payment has been received or repatriated within the statutory deadline
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation issued by the bank showing the matched remittance for the specific export service invoice

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory under Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Every registered business taxpayer, including a Chusik Hoesa NVOCC, must issue an electronic tax invoice through the HomeTax portal and transmit the invoice to the NTS by no later than the day after issuance. There is no standalone hardware fiscal stamp device in Korea; the clearance function is performed through centralized NTS HomeTax real-time receipt of e-tax invoice data, with an NTS-issued electronic invoice number and anti-fraud validation at the point of submission. Failure to issue or transmit the e-tax invoice triggers fixed and proportional penal taxes under the VAT Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service operate cross-matching checks among electronic tax invoices, export declarations, bills of lading, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient statutory documentation or the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the NTS will retroactively reassess the unpaid 10% VAT, add additional tax, and impose penal taxes under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent or grossly negligent export zero-rating claims can trigger the unjustified-benefit penalty tax of up to 40% of the tax amount, criminal prosecution with a fine, and imprisonment for tax evasion. The exporter must retain the complete substantiation bundle for at least five years from the filing due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The KRW is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including maritime freight services, port handling fees, operating expenses, and dividend remittances, subject to the real-demand principle and mandatory reporting. Capital-account transactions require FEIS registration, notification, or approval through a designated foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea. Profit remittance by a Chusik Hoesa is lawful at fiscal year-end after audited financial statements and corporate tax clearance; remittance under the intercountry tax treaty requires filing of the relevant withholding exemption or reduction certificate with the remittance bank. There is no multiple-tier exchange rate and no separate FX quota system for treaty-qualified foreign shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No fixed statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export service proceeds. Freight forwarding receivables must be collected within the statutory period prescribed by Presidential Decree, broadly 18 months from the date of performance or invoice, extendable upon the foreign exchange bank's approval for documented trade disputes. All export receipts must be repatriated and either deposited in a domestic foreign exchange bank account or converted into KRW, with FEIS settlement reporting completed through the foreign exchange bank. Monthly consolidated reports of export receipts are due to the responsible foreign exchange bank by the tenth business day of the following month.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Enforcement Decree of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) regulations; foreign exchange business is administered through authorized domestic commercial banks as foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. South Korea does not provide an EMI licensing regime for offshore digital banks or payment electronic money institutions. An offshore fintech EMI cannot lawfully hold deposits or operate local KRW clearing without obtaining a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Act on Regulation of Specified Financial Institutions. Foreign EMIs may only provide card issuance support or mobile collection rails through a licensed Korean bank as the sponsor institution; they are not acceptable substitutes for commercial bank settlement accounts, capital escrow, or FEIS reporting channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan remittances must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a designated Korean foreign exchange bank with precise remittance fields naming the non-resident investor, the Chusik Hoesa, and the FEIS purpose category for foreign direct investment or shareholder loan. The receiving bank must complete FEIS notification to the Bank of Korea before or concurrently with crediting the capital account. Equity capital requires prior filing with the foreign exchange bank or Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) under the Foreign Direct Investment notification procedure, while subordinated shareholder loans require a separate loan report. Treasurer must obtain a Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate and should never split a capital injection into multiple wires to avoid reporting thresholds; credit proceeds must be traceable directly to the offshore investing entity.Disbursements to non-resident shipping lines, feeder operators, port terminals, container depots, and overseas agency partners should be executed via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers or MT202 bank-to-bank cover payments. Commercial letters of credit related to NVOCC cargo transactions are issued and received via SWIFT MT700, with discrepancy-free documents comprising the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate, and certificate of origin; settlement follows the negotiating bank's MT103 proceeds claim after conformity check. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. South Korea does not provide an EMI licensing regime for offshore digital banks or payment electronic money institutions. An offshore fintech EMI cannot lawfully hold deposits or operate local KRW clearing without obtaining a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Act on Regulation of Specified Financial Institutions. Foreign EMIs may only provide card issuance support or mobile collection rails through a licensed Korean bank as the sponsor institution; they are not acceptable substitutes for commercial bank settlement accounts, capital escrow, or FEIS reporting channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 5,000,000,000 in a fiscal year; Master File threshold: total revenue equal or exceeding KRW 100,000,000,000 with cross-border related-party transactions above the Local File threshold; Country-by-Country Report threshold: consolidated group revenue equal or exceeding KRW 2,000,000,000,000

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but economic substance is a condition of treaty benefits through the principal purpose test, limitation-of-benefits clauses, controlled foreign corporation rules, and the general anti-avoidance rule. An NVOCC entity must demonstrate actual staff, office premises, commercial control over freight contracts, bill of lading issuance, and assumption of risk; a mere invoice-passthrough structure will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to three times the evaded corporate tax, imprisonment of up to five years for aggravated evasion, a 20% unfaithful return penalty, and a 0.022% per-day late payment surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or approve false accounting are personally subject to travel bans, civil restitution of evaded tax, and imprisonment; corporate limited liability is pierced where the corporate form is used as a sham for tax evasion.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the substance-over-form doctrine, CFC income inclusion, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, and an additional tax assessment with a penalty of up to 30% for gross negligence

Director personal liability: Directors of the controlling Korean Chusik Hoesa may be held personally liable for undistributed offshore earnings where the shell is deliberately structured to evade Korean tax; the directors may also face departure prohibitions and criminal indictment for false declarations.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, suspension or revocation of the NVOCC license, disgorgement of laundered proceeds, and criminal prosecution under Korean AML legislation; aggravated cases carry imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors face personal travel bans, license ineligibility, personal fines, and seizure of assets traceable to freight-payment money laundering; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud where directors knowingly process illicit cargo payments.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to the arm's-length price, denial of deductions where documentation is not contemporaneous, underreporting penalties of 10-30%, interest, and enhanced audit referral to the NTS international tax office

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation are jointly liable for civil damages under Korean corporate law, may be prohibited from departing Korea, and may face criminal indictment for intentional underreporting in aggravated cases.
Unlicensed NVOCC Operations

Statutory sanction: Business closure order, forfeiture of illegal freight profits, administrative fines, and imprisonment of up to three years for unregistered operation

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally fined or imprisoned; cargo claimants and shippers may pursue civil damages directly against directors, and travel bans will be imposed while the criminal investigation is pending.

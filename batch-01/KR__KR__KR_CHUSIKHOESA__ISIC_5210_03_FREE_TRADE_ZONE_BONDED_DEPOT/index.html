Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail-drop address is rejected for a Chusik Hoesa that will hold a bonded-depot license under ISIC 5210. The National Tax Service requires a fixed place of business under the Value-Added Tax Act for tax invoice issuance and VAT registration; a commercial bank will not open a corporate account without evidence of an actual leased office or warehouse in the Republic of Korea. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Korean Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the full subscription price must be actually deposited into a blocked bank subscription account before the registration application is filed, and the bank must issue a Subscription Deposit Certificate or capital verification certificate. For a free trade zone bonded depot operator, the customs director may require proof of financial capacity, leasehold interest, or a performance bond covering warehousing liabilities. Until incorporation registration is completed, the deposited capital is escrowed or blocked in substance and cannot be withdrawn by subscribers or promoters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies under the statutory zero-rated supply provisions of the Korean VAT Act to exported goods, services provided to non-residents, international transport, and goods delivered into a customs free trade zone or bonded depot for export. A Chusik Hoesa operating a bonded warehouse may claim 0% VAT on exported goods if it can substantiate: (1) physical export evidenced by a Korea Customs Service export declaration; (2) customer as a non-resident or receipt of goods outside Korea; (3) transaction value expressed and settled in foreign currency; (4) export proceeds received through the banking system within the statutory repatriation deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration clearance notice from Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill/CMR) evidencing delivery outside Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange purchase/remittance confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

Mandatory issuance of electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the NTS HomeTax portal for all VAT-registered businesses except limited small-scale suppliers. Businesses must transmit sales and purchase invoice data in real time or within the statutory VAT return filing cycle. The HomeTax e-tax invoice system functions as the official anti-fraud clearance and digital stamping mechanism through cross-matching of tax invoices. For bonded depot operations, goods movement and export clearances must be communicated via Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic data interchange.

Export zero-rating is subject to NTS and Korea Customs Service audit verification. False export declarations, inadequate documentation, or failure to timely repatriate export proceeds may trigger additional VAT assessment, wrongful refund clawbacks, civil fraud penalties up to 40% of unpaid tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, criminal liability for tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, and retrospective reassessment extending the statutory assessment period for fraudulent claims. Bonded-license misuse may additionally result in recovery of customs duties, administrative fines, or revocation of the bonded warehouse operator license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a broadly liberalised current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Bona fide trade payments, trade-related commissions, royalties, service fees and dividend remittances may be settled in Korean Won or freely convertible foreign currency through an authorised foreign exchange bank without prior case-by-case approval. The capital account is also substantially liberalised on a negative-list basis, but each inbound equity contribution, foreign-currency borrowing, security conversion or profit repatriation must be reported to the relevant authorities or, where the transaction falls outside a permitted category, the MOEF or BOK may require pre-notification, approval or enhanced documentary verification. Korean Won is not a fully offshore-deliverable currency in the same way as reserve currencies; onshore convertibility operates through authorised banks under BOK statistical reporting, and offshore markets price Korean Won through non-deliverable contracts. There is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds, so a bonded-warehouse entity may retain its full foreign-currency export receipts in a foreign-exchange account until such time as those funds are converted for local operating expenses or used for overseas supplier settlements. Profit remittance by a Chusik Hoesa requires distributable reserves shown in audited financial statements, a board resolution, payment of withholding tax on dividends and evidence that the original invested capital was registered as foreign direct investment; the remitting bank must file the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System and may request a certificate of overdue tax liabilities. These layered reporting, licensing and FIU requirements mean that although Korean Won is convertible for normal treasury operations, the convertibility path is document-intensive and must be managed through a properly licensed Korean commercial bank rather than an offshore digital money institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Enforcement Decree to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds from goods shipped from Korea, including supplies withdrawn from a bonded depot in a Free Trade Zone, must be collected through an authorised foreign exchange bank within 15 months from the date of export declaration unless an extension is granted by the delegated bank for unavoidable causes. Korea applies a mandatory surrender ratio of zero percent: there is no requirement to convert foreign currency export receipts into Korean Won, and the exporter may retain one hundred percent of proceeds in a foreign-exchange deposit account, use the balances directly for foreign-currency payables, or convert to KRW for domestic disbursements. All collections must be documented and transmitted to the Foreign Exchange Information System through the collecting bank. If export proceeds are not collected within the statutory collection period, or if the exporter cannot provide a lawful justification for the shortfall, the delegated foreign exchange bank must request a non-settlement report, and continued failures can result in administrative fines and adverse classification of the entity's foreign-exchange dealings.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), acting as the Foreign Exchange Authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, jointly administer Korean foreign exchange controls; daily settlement supervision and data reporting are delegated to designated foreign exchange banks and the Foreign Exchange Information System operated with the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-zero viability for the Korean treasury structure. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only authorised foreign exchange banks and registered money-changing businesses may lawfully settle customer foreign-currency and KRW transactions; an offshore digital EMI licence does not confer any Korean foreign-exchange business authorisation. A Chusik Hoesa operating a bonded depot cannot use an EMI as a legal settlement institution for trade proceeds, inbound FDI capital or supplier disbursements because Korean regulators require domestic bank-level KYC, suspicious transaction reporting, foreign exchange statistics reporting and preservation of settlement records. The only acceptable cross-border rails are a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank or a licensed overseas branch of such a bank; an EMI may be used solely for ancillary expense processing outside the regulatory FX perimeter, never for the bonded-depot treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Chusik Hoesa must be injected as a foreign direct investment. Before the remittance, the investor or the Korean entity should file a foreign direct investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with the MOEF or the delegated authorised foreign exchange bank; any subsequent share subscription or capital increase must be filed in the same system. The inbound wire should be sent through SWIFT MT103 to the entity's foreign-exchange capital escrow account at the Tier-1 Korean bank, and the payment narrative must clearly state the purpose as equity participation in a Korean corporation, quoting the FDI report reference and the Board resolution. After receipt, the bank will issue a certified Foreign Exchange Transaction Record and deposit confirmation; the entity must then complete share issuance and share allotment, obtain the foreign-investment registration certificate and arrange independent audit verification of paid-in capital. The capital verification file should combine the MT103, bank confirmation, share register, articles of incorporation, board minutes and the FDI report certificate before any operational treasury conversion into Korean Won.Outbound trade disbursements should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier remittances, commercial letters of credit for high-value or documented import purchases, and documentary collections for lower-risk trade flows where D/P or D/A terms are acceptable. Each outbound disbursement must be supported by the underlying commercial contract, pro-forma or final invoice, warehouse withdrawal or customs entry documentation, transport documents and the entity's official foreign-exchange purpose classification. The authorized Korean bank will debit the relevant foreign-currency or KRW operational account and, where required, convert through its internal FX desk. Local KRW payments should settle through BOK-Wire+ or the bank's domestic clearing system, while USD and EUR payments are cleared through the bank's international correspondent network. All individual transactions above the statutory reporting threshold must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System under the applicable foreign-exchange statistics code before the bank releases funds.Onshore deliverable KRW/USD forward contracts backed by evidenced trade receivables, supplier payables or bonded-warehouse operational cash-flow forecasts.KRW/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging cross-border collection mismatches where the entity holds genuine non-KRW exposure and the NDF tenor does not exceed the underlying trade cycle.Cross-currency swaps for converting long-term foreign-currency direct investment borrowings into Korean Won with matching principal amortisation and complete documentation of the underlying debt obligation.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars used solely to hedge confirmed currency exposures on the bonded-depot trade portfolio, with explicit prohibition on uncovered or speculative option positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-zero viability for the Korean treasury structure. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only authorised foreign exchange banks and registered money-changing businesses may lawfully settle customer foreign-currency and KRW transactions; an offshore digital EMI licence does not confer any Korean foreign-exchange business authorisation. A Chusik Hoesa operating a bonded depot cannot use an EMI as a legal settlement institution for trade proceeds, inbound FDI capital or supplier disbursements because Korean regulators require domestic bank-level KYC, suspicious transaction reporting, foreign exchange statistics reporting and preservation of settlement records. The only acceptable cross-border rails are a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank or a licensed overseas branch of such a bank; an EMI may be used solely for ancillary expense processing outside the regulatory FX perimeter, never for the bonded-depot treasury.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top national marginal CIT rate (9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 24% above KRW 20 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge producing a 26.4% top aggregate rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory withholding (20% national tax plus 2% local surcharge) on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, subject to applicable DTA reduction for the beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion aggregate annual intercompany transactions with foreign related parties triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation; lesser transaction volumes may still be audited and require simpler comparability documentation if the NTS issues a request.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but the NTS applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership analysis, the general anti-abuse rule, and the BEPS Action 6 Limitation-on-Benefits approach. A bonded warehouse operator must conduct its core income-generating activities in Korea, including physical inventory management, customs record keeping, staff and decision-making, to preserve treaty benefits and avoid being recharacterized as a shell or letterbox company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion through false documentation

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Korean Punishment of Tax Evasion Act; intentional evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and fines of up to 3 times the evaded tax, plus civil additional tax of 40% of the underreported amount and statutory daily late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and the representative who signed or caused the false return may have the corporate veil pierced under the Korean Commercial Act, become jointly and severally liable to the tax authority, and face blacklisting as unfaithful high-risk taxpayers, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and personal criminal imprisonment.
Customs bonded warehouse and export control fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs Act smuggling and false declaration provisions authorize forfeiture of goods, revocation of the bonded depot license, customs additional duties, and criminal imprisonment of up to 10 years or fines of up to 10 times the evaded customs value.

Director personal liability: Officers who authorize or benefit from the unlawful removal of bonded goods face personal civil liability for unpaid customs duties and VAT, personal criminal conviction, travel restrictions, release of personal guarantees, and loss of the commercial operating license.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and shell-company profit stripping

Statutory sanction: Korea's International Tax Adjustment Act authorizes the NTS to reassess income at the arm's length price, deny related-party expense deductions, apply additional tax of up to 20% of the underreported foreign-related adjustment, and impose documentation penalties; treaty benefits are denied where beneficial ownership fails.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced against directors who knowingly sign false intercompany agreements or control sham entities, exposing them to restitution of diverted profits, joint liability for assessed tax, blacklisting and travel bans by the NTS, and criminal charges where cross-border fraudulent intent is established.

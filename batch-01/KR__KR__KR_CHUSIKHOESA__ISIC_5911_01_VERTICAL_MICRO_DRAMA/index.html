Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Commercial Act; no minimum paid-in capital is prescribed for a Chusik Hoesa, and one Korean won per share is permissible. However, the aggregate issue price of all subscribed shares must be deposited into a dedicated capital subscription account in the name of the company-in-formation at a licensed Korean bank, and withdrawals are blocked until the certified certificate of incorporation registration is issued. Registration tax of 0.4% of paid-in capital, a 20% local education surcharge, and court fees are payable, with the estimated minimum state fee being approximately USD 40 for a nominal capital company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered electronically through the HomeTax portal at www.hometax.go.kr; import and export clearance is handled by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system at unipass.customs.go.kr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: register the Chusik Hoesa with the Korean Commercial Court registry upon incorporation. Step 2: within 20 days of business commencement, file the business registration application with the NTS district office that has jurisdiction over the registered head office, using the HomeTax portal; attach the Articles of Incorporation, certified registry excerpt, lease agreement or title proof for the registered address, and the representative director's identification. Step 3: receive the Business Registration Certificate carrying the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which is the unified corporate TIN and VAT ID. For a foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business that supplies taxable services or digital content in Korea, the entity must appoint a Korean tax representative and file the HomeTax foreigner registration application with a power of attorney, the foreign certificate of incorporation, the tax representative's identity details, and a statement of expected Korean-source revenue; the NTS then issues a dedicated VAT registration number for indirect tax and withholding compliance. No invoice may be issued and no input VAT deduction may be claimed until the registration is approved.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Korean tax law has no US-style check-the-box or entity-classification election. The elections relevant to a Chusik Hoesa are: (i) the consolidated corporate tax return election for a wholly owned domestic group under the Corporate Tax Act; (ii) the elective hybrid-mismatch self-adjustment mechanisms under the International Tax Adjustment Act for reverse hybrid, double-deduction, or deduction-without-inclusion arrangements; and (iii) the annual choice between foreign tax credit and treaty exemption. No election can convert the Chusik Hoesa into a pass-through or territorial-only taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold exists before mandatory VAT registration; every person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register. The only small-business accommodation is the simplified taxpayer regime, which requires annual turnover below KRW 80 million and applies to small sole proprietors only. A Chusik Hoesa is always a general taxpayer and is subject to full VAT registration, invoicing, and return obligations regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act for (i) goods exported and cleared by the Korea Customs Service; (ii) services supplied to non-residents or foreign corporations when the place of supply is outside Korea; and (iii) digital content services, including vertical micro-drama streaming, monetized through Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, or Stripe for overseas end users, provided the actual viewing or exploitation occurs outside Korea and consideration is received in foreign currency. The Korean supplier must prove the foreign place of supply through contract terms, platform payout reports, and geolocation or device metadata; if the proof is absent, the revenue is reclassified as domestic taxable supply at 10% VAT.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration and Export Clearance Receipt, together with the Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical shipments.
  • Signed international service, licensing, and streaming-distribution agreement with the foreign counterparty defining the licensed territory and remuneration basis.
  • App-platform settlement and payout statements from Apple App Store Connect, Google Play Console, and Stripe, supported by commercial invoices and subscriber/geolocation logs showing foreign end users.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Exchange Earned Certificate issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank in Korea evidencing the repatriation and conversion of the export proceeds.

Korea mandates an electronic tax invoice for every B2B supply. The supplier must issue the e-tax invoice through an NTS-certified e-invoicing provider or the HomeTax EDI/API and transmit it to the NTS by the day following issuance. Every e-tax invoice carries an accredited digital certificate signature; an invalid signature renders the invoice ineffective for input VAT deduction. For B2C digital supply, the platform must issue and report cash receipts in real time through the NTS cash-receipt system. Anti-fraud enforcement relies on real-time cross-matching of issued e-tax invoices against VAT returns, customs export declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, and app-store payout data. Korea does not operate a Latin-American style fiscal stamping clearance, but the electronic signature and next-day filing obligations function as the equivalent digital clearance mandate.

The NTS reconciles every zero-rated export invoice against the customs export declaration, the underlying contract, the platform payout statements, and the foreign-exchange inward remittance. For digital services, NTS auditors may also inspect IP geolocation logs, device metadata, and app-store country reports. If foreign-currency proceeds are not received within 180 days of the export date (or service performance date) without an approved NTS extension, or if the documentation bundle is incomplete, the zero rating is revoked retroactively and the supply is assessed at 10% VAT. The assessment is increased by underreporting penalties of 10% for simple error, 20% for gross negligence, 30% for intentional underreporting, and 40% for fraudulent false invoices under the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus a daily late-payment interest charge currently set at approximately 0.022% per day. Knowingly creating false export declarations or zero-rating evidence triggers criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, carrying imprisonment or monetary fines up to 3 to 5 times the tax evaded. The ordinary statutory assessment period is 5 years, 7 years where no return was filed, and 10 years for fraudulent evasion. Genuine zero-rated exporters can claim refunds of excess input VAT through the semi-annual VAT return, but the refund is held until the same documentation bundle passes the NTS pre-refund review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a market-determined managed float for the Korean won. Current account transactions, including trade, royalties, content licensing, subscriptions, dividends and interest, are broadly liberalized and require no prior permission, although a resident corporation must retain underlying contracts and the bank must report the transaction to the foreign exchange information system. Capital account transactions are also significantly open, but Korean won remains primarily convertible through onshore authorized foreign exchange banks and offshore non-deliverable trading is an offshore market not available to the Korean resident entity. Inward direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa is routed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act: the foreign investor files a pre-investment report, remits equity funds into a foreign-currency undertaking account in a domestic bank, and provides a certificate of capital payment for registration of the foreign-invested enterprise. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is treated as a current transaction and can flow out after corporate tax clearance, withholding tax payment, distributable-reserve verification, and board approval; there is no blanket dividend lock, but the bank will require the balance sheet, audit report where statutory, and tax certificate plus a foreign exchange bank report. The Korean won is not an international settlement currency outside formal banking rails, and certain non-resident won account transfers remain subject to reporting. Overall, Korea is a high-convertibility but high-documentation jurisdiction rather than a hard surrender-currency regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory conversion ratio exists in South Korea; a resident exporter may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency account at an authorized foreign exchange bank and decide the timing of conversion to Korean won. Export proceeds must be collected in accordance with FETA timelines, generally within 90 days of shipment or performance date unless an extension is approved by the dealing bank; overdue collection is reported to the bank and BOK/MOEF through the reporting system. The synthetic surrender requirement is effectively zero under the liberalized current-account framework, and conversion, when elected, is effected at the spot market rate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); authorized Korean foreign exchange banks perform delegated control and reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very low as a primary treasury banking solution. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not banks under the Korean Banking Act and cannot legally provide a domestic KRW checking account with access to the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute rails. A foreign EMI without an FSC license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act is restricted from performing money transmission in Korea, and even a licensed electronic money provider is not a substitute for a Chusik Hoesa corporate bank account because it cannot complete FIPA capital registration, hold foreign currency deposit accounts, issue foreign exchange certificates, verify inbound direct investment, or process corporate tax payments. EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection tool for limited non-KRW customer payments, not as the cross-border liquidity clearing rail. The only compliant route for the Stage 4 treasury function is a domestic tier-1 bank or licensed domestic branch of a foreign bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must remit equity subscription funds from the investor's own bank account to the Chusik Hoesa's foreign investment undertaking account at an authorized Korean foreign exchange bank. A SWIFT MT103 order message should include in field 70 the FIPA declaration or registration number, the company identifier KR_CHUSIKHOESA, and the label FDI_EQUITY_SUBSCRIPTION as accepted by the dealing bank. The receiving bank credits the foreign currency deposit account, certifies receipt of the foreign direct investment capital, and carries out the post-investment foreign-invested enterprise registration via the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy/KOTRA and MOEF/BOK reporting channels. The KRW conversion is executed as a spot FX trade at the prevailing market rate. Capital verification is completed by an independent accountant using the bank-issued foreign currency receipt certificate, the share subscription agreement, and the board resolution approving issuance of shares. All underlying corporate documents must be retained for the bank's foreign exchange audit and capital verification file.For operating expenses, local production costs, cloud hosting, contractor payments and content marketing, the treasury desk should instruct SWIFT MT103 from the Korean won or foreign currency operational account through the bank's online corporate treasury portal, retaining invoices, contracts and tax receipts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very low as a primary treasury banking solution. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not banks under the Korean Banking Act and cannot legally provide a domestic KRW checking account with access to the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute rails. A foreign EMI without an FSC license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act is restricted from performing money transmission in Korea, and even a licensed electronic money provider is not a substitute for a Chusik Hoesa corporate bank account because it cannot complete FIPA capital registration, hold foreign currency deposit accounts, issue foreign exchange certificates, verify inbound direct investment, or process corporate tax payments. EMIs may be used only as a supplementary collection tool for limited non-KRW customer payments, not as the cross-border liquidity clearing rail. The only compliant route for the Stage 4 treasury function is a domestic tier-1 bank or licensed domestic branch of a foreign bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% headline marginal rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, with a progressive structure of 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 20% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 22% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, and 25% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% corporate income tax withholding on gross dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder, plus a 2% local income tax surcharge equal to 10% of the corporate income tax withheld, yielding an aggregate statutory rate of 22%. Under an applicable Double Tax Treaty the dividend withholding rate may be reduced to 5%-15%, generally conditioned on the beneficial owner holding a minimum equity stake of 10%-25% for 365 continuous days.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and its Enforcement Decree, a Korean corporation with international related-party transactions exceeding KRW 5 billion in the prior fiscal year must prepare and retain a Local File and file the related-party transaction report. A Master File is required when the consolidated worldwide group revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion. The National Tax Service may request contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation below these thresholds during an audit, so a prudent threshold for internal preparation is KRW 1 billion in cross-border related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE ESR. Economic substance is enforced through the substance-over-form principle under the Corporate Income Tax Law, thin-capitalization rules, controlled foreign corporation rules, and treaty anti-abuse doctrine. A Korean Chusik Hoesa operating a vertical micro-drama app must perform core income-generating activities through resident employees: content commissioning, IP licensing, platform pricing, server administration, user acquisition, and marketing. If all decision-making and risk-taking occurs outside Korea and the Korean entity is only a cash conduit, profits may be reattributed to the beneficial owner and the entity treated as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent understatement of digital-content revenue

Statutory sanction: Penalty under Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-3 and 47-4: 20% of the unpaid tax for an unfaithful return and 40% for a dishonest fraudulent return. Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, the corporation may be fined up to 10 times the evaded tax and the responsible officer may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to 2 years, escalating to 5 years when the evaded amount exceeds KRW 300 million or the falsification is especially serious.

Director personal liability: A director may lose limited liability through judicial piercing of the corporate veil if the company is found to be an alter ego or a shell. The National Tax Service may hold the director jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties. Personal asset seizure, an overseas travel ban, and exit-hold orders are available when unpaid tax exceeds KRW 100 million, and criminal conviction can result in imprisonment and disqualification from corporate management.
Failure to withhold and remit statutory withholding tax on non-resident payments

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the unpaid withholding amount plus an underreporting penalty under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2. The National Tax Service can assess the full 22% statutory rate retroactively, deny treaty relief, and impose an additional daily interest charge. Where the payment is a disguised dividend, the recipient and the Korean entity face a corporate tax adjustment on the profit distribution.

Director personal liability: The representative director and senior financial officer are jointly and severally liable when the company fails to withhold or remit and the director participated in the payment decision. Willful failure to remit withheld taxes can be prosecuted as embezzlement or breach of trust under the Criminal Act, carrying imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine up to KRW 50 million. Directors who sign false withholding filings also face criminal tax fraud charges and personal restitution orders.
Shell company abuse and sham core income-generating activities

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may disregard the sham contracts under the substance-over-form rule in Article 14 of the Corporate Income Tax Law, recharacterize purportedly deductible fees as non-deductible distributions, and assess the full 22% withholding on the recharacterized dividends. The adjustment is subject to 20% or 40% penalty rates depending on culpability. Treaty benefits are denied, and the Korean entity may be sanctioned as a shell with revoked business registration and a criminal referral for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement or benefit from the shell structure are stripped of the limited-liability shield. They are jointly and severally liable for the avoided taxes, penalties, and interest, may face criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years, and may suffer an international travel ban, asset forfeiture, and personal bankruptcy risk if the corporation is found to be the alter ego of a non-resident director or beneficial owner.

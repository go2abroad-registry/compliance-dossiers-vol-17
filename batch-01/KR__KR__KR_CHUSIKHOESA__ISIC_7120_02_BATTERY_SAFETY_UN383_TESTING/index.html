Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr).

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity forming a Chusik Hoesa (stock corporation) in Korea must register with the National Tax Service through HomeTax. The entity first incorporates under the Korean Commercial Act at a local court, appoints a statutory representative, and establishes a domestic place of business. It then files a business registration application with the competent local tax office, submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, proof of registered address, representative identification, and evidence of overseas incorporation for non-resident corporate shareholders. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number that also serves as the VAT identification number. VAT registration is bundled with the business registration; no separate VAT registration procedure is required. The entity should also open a corporate bank account and register with the Korea Customs Service as an exporter if it will ship physical samples or lab materials abroad.

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under the Korean Value Added Tax Act, exports of goods are zero-rated when the goods leave Korea under export customs clearance and the exporter holds evidence of export and foreign exchange receipt. Qualifying offshore services (laboratory testing, technical analysis, and digital services) may also be zero-rated where the place of supply is treated as outside Korea, the service is actually consumed overseas, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. For UN38.3 battery safety drop testing and related lab services, the entity must evidence both export of physical test samples (if applicable) and the direct supply of testing services to an overseas client.

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing (e-tax invoice) is in force. VAT-registered businesses must issue e-invoices through the National Tax Service HomeTax system; e-invoices must be transmitted to NTS by the day after issuance. No separate real-time fiscal clearance API or digital stamp is used, but the HomeTax system performs automated validation, matching and risk analysis. Non-compliance with e-invoice obligations triggers penalty surcharges under the VAT Act and may block VAT refunds.

      
If export zero-rating is claimed without proper export documentation or timely foreign exchange repatriation, the NTS will retroactively disallow the zero rate, assess output VAT at 10% on the export transaction, and impose statutory penalty surcharges plus interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value Added Tax Act. Intentional fabrication of export records may expose the entity and executives to criminal charges for tax evasion, cancellation of export registration, and clawback of all input VAT previously refunded on export-related purchases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Republic of Korea maintains a largely liberalized current account regime under Article 3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Korean won (KRW) fully convertible for bona fide trade in goods, services, royalties, and testing fees. Capital account transactions, however, are subject to a prior declaration or reporting requirement to the designated foreign exchange bank, with certain transactions requiring advance notification to the Minister of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Information System. For a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa engaged in battery safety testing and CE-RED electromagnetic laboratory services, the monthly aggregation of export proceeds and cross-border service fees must be declared under the relevant reporting code, but there is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Central Bank. The practical convertibility hurdle is documentation-driven: the domestic bank must receive the underlying contract, commercial invoice, and customs or electronic evidence of service completion to classify the inflow as a current account receipt rather than a capital contribution or loan. Profit remittance by a foreign shareholder is permitted as dividend repatriation under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, subject to the company's audited retained earnings, a withholding tax clearance certificate (typically 22% on dividends unless reduced by treaty), and the submission of foreign exchange purchase confirmation forms to the designated dealing branch. Rigorous source-of-funds and tax-residency due diligence is applied to the ultimate beneficial owner because testing laboratories can be used to move sensitive R&D payments; therefore, the bank will require the underlying service agreement, technical scope statements, and proof that the ISIC 7120 testing activity is not disguised IP leasing. There is no general exchange control approval for routine current account payments, but outbound payments exceeding USD 10,000 are captured by the Foreign Exchange Information System and must match the declared business purpose, so any mismatch between invoice descriptions and the KRX customs or laboratory accreditation documentation will trigger a temporary suspension and a request for clarification from the bank's compliance team.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export foreign currency receipts to Korean won. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, Korean entities are free to hold and use export proceeds in foreign currency, but they must report the receipt to the designated foreign exchange bank and the Korea Customs Service within the prescribed time frame, generally within 30 days of the receipt for the purpose of external debt and balance-of-payments statistics. For service export proceeds derived from UN38.3 battery testing and CE-RED electromagnetic testing, the company may retain USD or EUR in a foreign currency account, subject to the bank's internal policy requiring conversion to KRW within 5 business days for aggregate receipts exceeding KRW 50 million unless a legitimate operational need or future payment obligation is documented.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Information System operated by the Korea Federation of Banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Low-to-prohibited for full domestic banking services. The Korean Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea require any entity receiving deposits or performing foreign exchange intermediary activities in Korea to be licensed as a banking institution or registered as a specialized foreign exchange business under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex do not hold a domestic banking license in Korea and cannot provide KRW IBANs, domestic clearing access, or a Foreign Exchange Information System reporting gateway. They may be used only as ancillary settlement rails for receiving small inbound remittances from overseas clients where the Chusik Hoesa already maintains a domestic KRW account as the principal treasury anchor; in that case, the offshore EMI remittance into the domestic account is treated as an inbound current account receipt requiring the standard declaration. The EMI must never be used to hold client funds on behalf of Korean residents, issue payroll in KRW, or execute outbound trade disbursements without a licensed domestic bank counterparty. The practical onboarding recommendation is to rely exclusively on a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank for all clearing and FX operations and to engage Wise or Airwallex only for the initial collection of foreign testing fees from clients in jurisdictions where inbound SWIFT is expensive or slow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The investor should execute an equity or capital increase contribution through a designated Korean foreign exchange bank, with the remittance purpose coded as 'Foreign Direct Investment into a Korean company' under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The overseas parent must wire the funds in USD/EUR to the bank's intermediary corresponding account with the final credit into the Chusik Hoesa's capital escrow account. The bank will issue a Certificate of Foreign Currency Purchase and the company must submit the Foreign Investment Report to MOEF within 30 days to complete the capital verification. For operating revenues from overseas battery testing clients, the inward wire must contain the payment purpose code '251' or '252' (testing/technical certification services) and should be accompanied by an advance invoice, a signed testing work order, and the KOLAS accreditation number. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for smaller recurring lab consumables where the counterparty credit history is positive and the transaction value is below KRW 100,000,000. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-prohibited for full domestic banking services. The Korean Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea require any entity receiving deposits or performing foreign exchange intermediary activities in Korea to be licensed as a banking institution or registered as a specialized foreign exchange business under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Airwallex do not hold a domestic banking license in Korea and cannot provide KRW IBANs, domestic clearing access, or a Foreign Exchange Information System reporting gateway. They may be used only as ancillary settlement rails for receiving small inbound remittances from overseas clients where the Chusik Hoesa already maintains a domestic KRW account as the principal treasury anchor; in that case, the offshore EMI remittance into the domestic account is treated as an inbound current account receipt requiring the standard declaration. The EMI must never be used to hold client funds on behalf of Korean residents, issue payroll in KRW, or execute outbound trade disbursements without a licensed domestic bank counterparty. The practical onboarding recommendation is to rely exclusively on a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank for all clearing and FX operations and to engage Wise or Airwallex only for the initial collection of foreign testing fees from clients in jurisdictions where inbound SWIFT is expensive or slow.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50,000,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File; a Country-by-Country Report is required if the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1,000,000,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Korea is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction like the UAE, but applies the identical substance-over-form principle under Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 14 and the International Tax Adjustment Act Art. 16. For a foreign-owned Korean testing subsidiary, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as laboratory personnel, equipment, risk assumption and technical decision-making must be physically present in Korea; otherwise the entity may be recharacterized as a sham or conduit and tax assessments will be sustained.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion through False Corporate Income Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: Tax Crimes Punishment Act Art. 10 imposes imprisonment up to 3 years (up to 5 years for habitual evasion) and a fine up to five times the evaded tax; Corporate Tax Act Art. 76 adds a 40% fraud penalty on the evaded corporate tax plus local income tax surcharge.

        
Director personal liability: Commercial Act Art. 401 pierces the corporate veil to hold directors jointly and severally liable for damages caused by intentional tax violation. Criminal conviction triggers asset seizure, personal travel ban and blacklisting under the Immigration Act, and restitution orders against personal property and bank accounts.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Art. 7 and Art. 9 authorize the tax authority to recompute taxable income and impose administrative fines for incomplete documentation; Corporate Tax Act Art. 76 imposes a 20% additional tax on underpaid corporate tax and daily overdue interest. Willful concealment attracts criminal prosecution under Tax Crimes Punishment Act Art. 10.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing certificates are personally liable for the tax adjustments, interest and penalties; they may be criminally indicted under the Act on Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes and subjected to an overseas travel ban during the tax audit and criminal proceedings.

      

      
      

        Shell Company Dividend Sheltering and Abusive DTA Relief
        
Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Art. 16 and Art. 32 recharacterize the payment as a constructive dividend; the Korean tax authority applies 22% withholding tax, 40% fraud penalty and interest. Under Tax Crimes Punishment Act Art. 10, the controlling director risks imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to five times the evaded tax.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under the Korean Supreme Court sham-entity doctrine and Civil Act Art. 750; directors are personally liable for all unpaid taxes and creditors' claims, face seizure of personal assets and equity, are blacklisted and travel-banned, and may be criminally prosecuted for tax fraud with prison sentence of up to 5 years.

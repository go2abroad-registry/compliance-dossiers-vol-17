Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Chusik Hoesa after the 2012 amendment to the Korean Commercial Act, and zero-won incorporation is legally available. However, Korean banking and trade finance practice, as well as the planned SWIFT LC and TT operations for imported containerized bitumen, require a fully deposited and verifiable paid-up capital. The promoters shall deposit at least KRW 100,000,000 (approximately USD 74,000) into a dedicated securities subscription escrow account (Jugeum Napseum Yesan) maintained with a licensed commercial bank. The escrowed funds must remain blocked until the court issues the Certificate of Incorporation Registration; any reimbursement to promoters before completion of registration constitutes a criminal offense under Article 628 of the Korean Commercial Act, and the bank-issued deposit certificate must be attached to the registration application as conclusive evidence of payment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal (home.tax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for customs and export clearance data.

E-government filing portal

A Korean Chusik Hoesa must file a business registration application with the competent District Tax Office within 20 days from the commencement of business, submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, lease/ownership evidence for the place of business, and the foreign passport/ID of representatives as applicable. The application is accepted through the HomeTax portal or at the District Tax Office, which issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number serving as both the corporate TIN and VAT registration ID. A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business is registered by the NTS only where it has Korean VAT registration obligations (e.g., supply of electronic services or other taxable supplies to Korea) or a Korean permanent establishment; the foreign entity must submit certified copies of its foreign incorporation documents, articles/powers of attorney, and either a local place of business contract or appointment of a Korean tax representative. Registration is completed through HomeTax and the assigned business registration number must be used in all customs declarations, tax invoices, and VAT returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of containerized drummed/bagged penetration grade bitumen qualify for zero-rated VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are physically exported from Korea under a valid Korea Customs Service export declaration, the risk and title in the goods pass to the overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are received through a foreign exchange bank within the statutory repatriation deadline. Qualifying offshore digital services are not part of this physical goods mandate and are not relied upon for the bitumen wholesale scope.

  • Customs export declaration confirmation issued through the UNI-PASS electronic customs clearance system (Korea Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading (or Sea / Multimodal Transport Document) for the containerized bitumen showing the Korean seller as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee/notify party
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate of export proceeds receipt or bank remittance advice evidencing receipt in freely convertible currency from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and, where applicable, inspection certificate/cargo manifests matching the export declaration and Bill of Lading

A registered business operator must issue all VAT tax invoices electronically and transmit them to the NTS through the HomeTax e-tax invoice system or an NTS-approved Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) service provider no later than the day after issuance. There is no Latin-American style central digital stamp or real-time clearance; NTS operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice repository that continuously cross-checks invoice data against VAT returns and customs export declarations.

NTS will cross-check zero-rated export invoices against KCS UNI-PASS export declarations, Bills of Lading, and foreign exchange bank records. If the zero-rating is not fully substantiated, NTS retroactively assesses unpaid 10% VAT, imposes statutory additional taxes for unissued or false tax invoices (penalty of up to 3% of supply value for false e-tax invoices plus interest), and may refer the matter for criminal sanctions under the Punishment for Tax Evasion Act if fraud or intentional false invoicing is identified. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day foreign exchange deadline can also trigger foreign exchange law penalties and removal of the zero-rate claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions under the OECD code of liberalisation, meaning payments for goods, services, and ordinary profit remittances can be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks without central bank prior approval, subject to submission of underlying transaction documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, are regulated under a prior declaration or license system. Non-resident shareholders may freely remit dividends and disposal proceeds if they have satisfied the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act declaration requirements via a domestic bank. However, all export proceeds from the sale of bitumen and asphalt products must be collected through authorized domestic financial institutions and may be subject to prescribed surrender and collection timelines. Profit remittances by foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa entities require a tax clearance certificate from the National Tax Service, confirming that corporate income tax, value-added tax, and withholding obligations have been discharged. There is no surrender requirement in the sense of forced conversion into KRW at a fixed rate; exporters may keep foreign currency proceeds in a designated foreign currency account, but they must be received within regulatory timeframes and reported to FEIN. Practical friction arises when the underlying purpose code is unclear or when capital contributions are made in-kind rather than in cash, as the bank will demand a verified valuation report and corporate registry amendment filing. Overall, KRW is convertible but operationally controlled, and a Chusik Hoesa engaged in bitumen wholesale must maintain meticulous documentary files to avoid freezing of trade payments, capital inflows, or dividend outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 12 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Detailed Regulations on Foreign Exchange Transactions, export proceeds for goods shipped from the Republic of Korea must be collected within six months from the date of the export declaration or the date of shipment, whichever is applicable for the relevant trade method. Exporters may obtain an extension of up to two additional months from the authorized foreign exchange bank upon submission of a valid justification, such as documentary collection period delays or letters of credit negotiation timing. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring the export proceeds to be exchanged into KRW; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account or convert at commercial bank rates. The entire invoice value must be received unless a justified discount, quality deduction, or open account offset has been properly reported with supporting trade documents and bank approval. Proceeds must be brought into Korea through an authorized Korean bank and reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System within the applicable collection deadline; failure to collect or report triggers administrative fines and potential customs export blockages.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), operating in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN), as the primary monetary and financial stability authority. All cross-border foreign exchange transactions in the Republic of Korea are governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its attendant Enforcement Decree and Detailed Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer are effectively barred from serving as the primary transactional banking rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa because they do not hold a Korean banking license and cannot provide KRW-denominated resident business settlement accounts or Korean Financial Services Commission authorizations for foreign exchange services. They may be used only peripherally for cross-border expense reimbursements or low-volume non-KRW treasury flows, but all substantive export collection, LC negotiation, tax payments, and capital verification must be conducted through an authorized Korean domestic bank. The legal viability rating for such EMIs is Low for this jurisdiction because the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires authorized foreign exchange bank handling, and virtual account issuance by unlicensed non-banks is prohibited. The treasury structure should therefore entirely depend on a tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a dedicated relationship manager for commodity wholesale trade.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into a Korean Chusik Hoesa must be routed through an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202. The remitter must reference the Foreign Exchange Declaration code under the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System, typically code 380101 for 'Foreign direct investment in a newly incorporated domestic company'. The remitter must ensure the capital contribution is in the same currency as stated in the foreign direct investment notification filed with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or the designated bank. The bank will issue a Certificate of Foreign Direct Investment Registration (외국인투자기업 등록증 유사 기능 in substance) but in English, the 'Certificate of Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration' after verification of the inward remittance and the corporate registry amendment. Capital verification requires the registered capital to be fully deposited in a temporary capital escrow account; funds cannot be used for operating expenses until the court-affiliated registry has updated the company's paid-in capital and the certified capital verification report has been issued by the appointed external auditor or a certified public accountant. The injector's identity must match the corporate registry shareholder records; any third-party wire is prohibited unless accompanied by a notarized subrogation agreement.Outbound trade disbursements for bitumen procurement, freight payment, and supplier settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 transfers, irrevocable letters of credit (LC at sight or usance) opened by the Korean bank in favour of overseas suppliers, or documentary collections (D/P and D/A) where the transaction size is moderate and trust is established. Every outbound payment must include the appropriate Foreign Exchange purpose code, such as 310101 for general trade payments under import of goods, and must be supported by the underlying import contract, proforma invoice, and bill of lading. The clearing rail for LC operations is SWIFT MT700/MT701, with reimbursement occurring through SWIFT MT740/MT750; the merchant bank must effect payment through SWIFT MT202 COV. For containerized bitumen shipments, the bank will also require ISPM 15 wood package compliance certificates and the customs export declaration in the buyer's jurisdiction to ensure the LC documents are compliant. Korean banks typically settle KRW transactions through the Bank of Korea's electronic settlement system (BOK-Wire) and foreign currency transactions through the respective currency clearing system (CHIPS, TARGET2, or CIPS for CNY). Only onshore licensed foreign exchange banks may provide these rails, and all outbound trades are monitored by KoFIU for trade-based money laundering typologies.Forward foreign exchange contracts (plain vanilla KRW/USD, KRW/EUR, KRW/CNY) with an underlying real trade exposure, permitted for the bitumen wholesale entity for hedging forecasted export or import cash flows within 12 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on KRW conducted offshore with global banks for hedging currency exposure when the entity does not hold sufficient onshore export contracts to justify a physical deliverable forward.Onshore participation in liquid mainstream currency swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps for balance sheet hedging of foreign-currency borrowings, provided the swap transaction is specifically approved by the bank's treasury products desk and the maturity does not exceed the underlying debt duration.Buying or selling of foreign exchange options with premium payment in KRW, restricted to hedging known commitments, and only when the notional amount does not exceed 2 times the actual underlying trade flow to remain compliant with Korean 'real demand' principles.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer are effectively barred from serving as the primary transactional banking rail for a Korean Chusik Hoesa because they do not hold a Korean banking license and cannot provide KRW-denominated resident business settlement accounts or Korean Financial Services Commission authorizations for foreign exchange services. They may be used only peripherally for cross-border expense reimbursements or low-volume non-KRW treasury flows, but all substantive export collection, LC negotiation, tax payments, and capital verification must be conducted through an authorized Korean domestic bank. The legal viability rating for such EMIs is Low for this jurisdiction because the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires authorized foreign exchange bank handling, and virtual account issuance by unlicensed non-banks is prohibited. The treasury structure should therefore entirely depend on a tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a dedicated relationship manager for commodity wholesale trade.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act after the 2012 amendment. The Articles must state authorized capital and issued shares. Funds are released only after completion of registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic administration through the HomeTax portal (https://www.hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance and export declaration validation are handled electronically through the Korea Customs Service's UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

For the Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa: (1) complete the Korean court registry incorporation and corporate registration; (2) apply for business registration through NTS HomeTax before commencing any taxable supply, selecting the principal domestic place of business in Korea; (3) submit the application together with the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, representative passport or national ID, and lease or ownership evidence of the Korean place of business; (4) receive the 10-digit registered business number from NTS, which serves as both the corporate TIN and VAT registration number; (5) obtain the business registration certificate. For a foreign non-resident entity with no Korean domestic place of business that nevertheless makes supplies taxable in Korea, the entity must appoint a Korean tax representative and file the same HomeTax business registration application using the foreign registry certificate, a notarized power of attorney to the tax representative, and the foreign entity's own TIN; NTS will issue a VAT registration number for that foreign entity. The Chusik Hoesa's Korean business number is also used for customs, e-tax invoices, and foreign exchange receipt reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Chusik Hoesa is a per-se stock corporation taxed at corporate entity level under the Korean Corporate Tax Act; no pass-through or disregarded-entity status is possible for a Korean incorporated Chusik Hoesa)

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election for domestically incorporated entities, and no hybrid-mismatch or partnership classification elections are available; the Chusik Hoesa remains a stand-alone corporate taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold: a person becomes a VAT-registered business operator from the first taxable supply of goods or services in Korea. The simplified taxable person regime for small individual enterprises with annual revenue below KRW 80,000,000 does not remove the registration obligation; it only changes the calculation and reporting burden.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate applies when used construction excavators, hydraulic shovels, and rollers are physically exported from Korea to a foreign buyer and the Korean-resident Chusik Hoesa is the seller and declarant in its own name. To substantiate the 0% VAT rate, the exporter must hold a valid export customs declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service, issue a zero-rated electronic tax invoice through HomeTax, transport the goods out of Korea, and collect the export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory export-proceeds collection period. Domestic sale in Korea or deemed export to a non-exporting domestic purchaser is subject to 10% VAT, unless the transaction is restructured as an actual zero-rated export followed by physical exportation.

  • Export declaration confirmation (outbound shipment) obtained from the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export clearance system, showing the Chusik Hoesa as seller and exporter and the used construction machinery being cleared for export
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with the used equipment details, serial numbers, condition, and HS classification for hydraulic excavators and rollers
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the used machinery from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Certificate of Export Proceeds Receipt (Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, evidencing payment collection in foreign currency
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice transmitted to NTS HomeTax, linked to the export customs declaration reference number

All B2B VAT supply transactions must be evidenced by tax invoices, and all registered business operators subject to e-invoicing must transmit the details as an electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) to NTS through the HomeTax system no later than the day following the date of the tax invoice. Integration is made through NTS-certified ERP platforms via HomeTax APIs, with data digitally signed by the taxpayer's certificate. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping model; instead, NTS performs automatic post-issuance cross-matching of e-tax invoices against customs export declarations and foreign exchange receipt data to detect false zero-rating.

NTS will verify each zero-rated export by matching the e-tax invoice to the customs export declaration and the foreign exchange receipt within the 180-day repatriation window. Failure to export, failure to collect proceeds on time, or defective documentation will reverse the zero rating and cause NTS to assess 10% VAT on the full invoice amount, plus interest and VAT additional tax penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Where the exporter knowingly files false export declarations, forges customs or foreign exchange receipts, or colludes in the issuance of fictitious e-tax invoices, criminal liability can be asserted under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act against the representative and related persons, together with confiscation and collection of the evaded VAT, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current-account foreign exchange regime. Trade-related payments, dividends, interest, and royalties can be converted and remitted through authorized foreign exchange banks upon submission of supporting documentation. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity investment, acquisition of foreign assets, and loans, are liberalized but subject to mandatory reporting and, where applicable, prior registration with a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in foreign-currency accounts or convert to Korean won at a market rate. The practical hurdle for profit remittance from a Chusik Hoesa is the need to evidence accrued and distributable profits through audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance certificates, and a board resolution, together with a foreign investor registration certificate and a valid foreign exchange bank account linked to the foreign investment report. Inflows for used excavator trading require precise declaration of the purpose code and underlying sale-purchase contracts to avoid classification as a suspicious outbound remittance. Domestic banks perform real-time transaction monitoring and may require invoices to be validated by customs declarations. While the Korean won participates in the CLS settlement system only indirectly and is not freely tradable offshore outside controlled interbank channels, current-account convertibility is practically unencumbered for tax-compliant entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory export proceeds collection period under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act is 15 months from the date of export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may hold the proceeds in an authorized foreign currency account or sell them to a foreign exchange bank at the spot rate. Bank-issued foreign exchange transaction records must be kept for five years and be presented for tax and customs audits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and coordinated with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Customs Service for enforcement and delegated authorization of foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - Legally non-viable as primary banking rail. Non-bank fintech EMIs and offshore digital wallets do not hold a domestic banking license and are not permitted to open corporate accounts or facilitate foreign exchange transactions in South Korea. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires all payments, conversions, and collection of export proceeds to be handled through a local authorized foreign exchange bank. Such EMIs may only provide supplementary expense management interfaces if they are linked to an underlying licensed bank account; they cannot replace the corporate treasury structure.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% top marginal (progressive: 10% up to KRW 200M; 20% from KRW 200M to KRW 20B; 22% from KRW 20B to KRW 300B; 25% above KRW 300B) plus 10% local income tax surtax on the national corporate tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge = 22% statutory dividend withholding tax on cash or deemed dividends paid to non-resident corporations and individuals; treaty rates (typically 5% for 10%+ shareholders and 15% otherwise) may reduce this where the applicable DTA is claimed

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.5M) in aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory preparation and submission of Transfer Pricing Master File and Local File; simpler 'Simplified TP Document' is required when related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion with any single foreign related party, even when the aggregate threshold is not met; tax authorities may request contemporaneous evidence within 30 days regardless of threshold if audit risk is identified.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation filing regime but applies the substance-over-form doctrine codified in Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the beneficial ownership rules of the International Tax Adjustment Act. A Korean Chusik Hoesa acting as a mere letterbox for a non-resident principal will be disregarded: the non-resident is deemed to have a Korean permanent establishment if core income-generating activities (negotiation, storage of used excavator inventory, inspection, assumption of risk, and title transfer) occur in the Korean jurisdiction. For treaty relief, CIGA must be demonstrable through physical offices, locally employed managers, actual purchasing/licensing contracts, and bank accounts. The absence of such substance exposes the entity to foreign-controlled domestic corporation recharacterization, disallowance of expenses, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion via Falsified Import Cost Base

Statutory sanction: Article 3(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to 2x evaded tax); Article 47-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes (fraudulent understatement penalty of 40% of the underpaid tax); Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (arm's length adjustment plus 10% of the adjusted income as additional penalty); Customs Law penalties for false declaration of import value (fine up to 5x the evaded duty and criminal liability for customs fraud).

Director personal liability: Korean appellate courts pierce the corporate veil when the company is used as a fraud instrument or the director acts in a grossly negligent manner. The representative director may be jointly and severally liable for the entire underpaid corporate tax under Article 39 of the Framework Act; personal assets can be attached; a departure ban is issued under Article 33 of the National Tax Collection Act; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to five years; the company and director are blacklisted from government procurement and foreign trade license renewal.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 6 (arm's length adjustment); Article 7 (secondary adjustment on deemed dividend or loan if profits are artificially retained abroad); Article 13 (non-submission of documentation: fine up to KRW 200 million); Article 34 (successive penalty charge of 30% of the understated taxable income attributable to undisclosed related-party transactions); Framework Act Article 47-3 for non-filing.

Director personal liability: The tax authority may assert a collection against the director as a responsible manager where the director signed the TP documentation or knowingly approved the non-arm's-length price. Court doctrine permits veil-piercing in egregious cases, exposing rent house and personal bank accounts. A criminal charge under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may be filed, with imprisonment up to five years; the director faces personal civil-liability restitution and disqualification from serving as a director of any Korean corporation.
Treaty Shopping Through Non-Beneficial Ownership and Shell Shareholder

Statutory sanction: Article 17-3 of the International Tax Adjustment Act (denial of treaty relief where the recipient is not beneficial owner); Article 24 of the same Act (disallowance of tax avoidance arrangements); the tax authority imposes domestic 22% withholding on the gross dividend plus 0.022% daily overdue interest; Framework Act Article 47-2 imposes 40% false-report penalty on the withholding agent; the foreign shareholder may be sanctioned with a fine equal to the under-withheld amount and interest. If the scheme includes forged TRC, criminal sanction under Article 3-2 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act applies (imprisonment up to five years).

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally structured the conduit arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the differential withholding tax; the corporate veil is pierced on grounds of abuse of legal personality; the Korean director and the foreign beneficial owner haves their assets frozen, travel bans issued, and are extraditable under the relevant tax mutual assistance treaty; imprisonment up to five years is available, with additional civil restitution for tax revenue loss; the foreign shareholder is also barred from future dividend remittances until the unpaid withholding is fully settled.

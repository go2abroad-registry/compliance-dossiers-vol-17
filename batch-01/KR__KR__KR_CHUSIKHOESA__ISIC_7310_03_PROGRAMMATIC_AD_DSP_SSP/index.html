Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Customs export clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (www.unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Chusik Hoesa: register with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the head office and obtain a 10-digit Business Registration Certificate (BRC) number, the statutory Korean TIN/VAT ID, before business commencement through HomeTax's online business registration function or in-person filing; supporting materials include articles of incorporation, registered office proof, director identification, capital contribution records, and (where applicable) corporate seal attestation. For a foreign non-resident entity with a Korean fixed establishment/branch: file the corporate business registration application with the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the branch within 20 days of commencement, under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Corporate Tax Act, and receive a 10-digit Korean foreign-business/BRC tax number for VAT, corporate income tax and electronic tax invoice purposes. For a non-resident entity with no Korean establishment that supplies electronic services (e.g., programmatic ad/SSP services) to Korean consumers: register via the NTS HomeTax 'overseas business operator registration' channel; the dossier must include the foreign certificate of incorporation, foreign tax residence certificate, board resolution or power of attorney appointing a Korean tax agent, evidence of the electronic service and Korean user base, and expected Korean turnover. NTS will issue a Korean VAT registration number (Foreign Business Operator Number). If the entity will export goods, an exporter identifier is separately registered with KCS in UNI-PASS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Chusik Hoesa is a Korean resident corporation under the Corporate Tax Act, taxed on worldwide income at entity level; no pass-through or transparent status is available.

Available election forms

None; Korea does not operate a check-the-box or similar entity-classification election. A Chusik Hoesa's classification is statutory. Eligible 100% owned Korean corporate groups may elect group consolidated taxation; taxpayers may elect foreign tax credit rather than deduction for foreign taxes. Hybrid-mismatch rules under the Corporate Tax Act are mandatory anti-avoidance provisions and cannot be elected out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No numerical KRW threshold for a Korean-resident corporation; VAT registration is mandatory within 20 days of business commencement. For foreign non-resident electronic-service suppliers, NTS requires registration where taxable supplies to Korean consumers commence; any administrative threshold should be assessed against NTS guidance, but the Korean entity itself has no threshold exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of the VAT Act for (i) export of goods, (ii) services supplied for use outside Korea by a non-resident/non-Korean customer, including digital and programmatic advertising service exports, (iii) international transportation, and (iv) prescribed deemed exports. The Korean AdTech entity must prove the recipient is foreign, the service is actually performed/delivered for foreign consumption, and the proceeds are receivable from abroad; acceptable evidence includes a written contract with the foreign advertiser/publisher/platform, DSP/SSP delivery and impression logs showing foreign geo-locations/IP addresses, campaign performance data, foreign-currency invoice and settlement records, and (if goods are bundled) export clearance documents. Therefore, for 0% VAT rate to be sustained, the substance of the cross-border service and the foreign-exchange settlement must be fully documented; early VAT refunds are possible but subject to later NTS substantiation.

  • KCS Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) filed through UNI-PASS for tangible goods exported from Korea
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the Korean exporting entity as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee/notify party
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank, the Korean functional equivalent of an FIRC, evidencing collection from the foreign buyer
  • Export contract and itemized commercial invoice including destination country, incoterms, and service/goods description
  • For programmatic ad and DSP/SSP service exports: service agreement, campaign delivery and geo-targeting logs, ad-server/SSP transaction records, and a foreign-currency settlement statement demonstrating foreign consumption

All registered VAT businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system, or an NTS-integrated EDI/open-API channel, for taxable B2B supplies; the e-tax invoice data is transmitted into NTS's central fiscal database on issuance and is used in near-real-time matching of the issuer's and recipient's VAT ledgers. The mandatory fields include supplier and recipient business registration numbers, supply description, supply date, supply value, VAT amount, and unique invoice sequence. The anti-fraud solution is the state-run HomeTax digital validation and clearance mechanism; there is no separate private stamp, and once accepted by NTS the e-invoice cannot be altered without a correction/credit note process. Late or non-transmission, and false e-invoices, trigger penalty taxes under the VAT Act and Framework Act for National Taxes.

NTS may audit the underlying export evidence; if zero-rating is not substantiated, 10% VAT is assessed on the full export invoice amount, with late-payment surcharges and penalty taxes. Under the Framework Act for National Taxes, non-fraudulent under-reporting triggers penalty tax up to 20% of the evaded tax and fraudulent under-reporting up to 40%; intentional false export declarations and false e-tax invoices are additionally punishable under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act, including criminal penalties and fines, and KCS may impose separate customs penalties for false declarations. Any previously refunded input VAT is clawed back with interest; the assessment period is generally 5 years, extending to 10 years for international tax evasion, and repeated/adverse findings can trigger stricter audit review and suspension of early refund rights.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a fully liberalised current account and a registration-based capital account. As an IMF Article VIII member, Korea permits unrestricted current-account payments and transfers as long as they are supported by genuine trade or service documentation and processed through a licensed domestic foreign-exchange bank. Legal tender is the South Korean won (KRW). Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), all foreign-exchange transactions are allowed unless specifically restricted by a negative list or by delegated regulations, which makes the regime investor-friendly but operationally documentary-heavy. Capital-account transactions, including inbound direct investment, foreign loans, issuance of financial instruments, and derivative contracts, generally require either a prior declaration or a report to a foreign-exchange bank, and the underlying transaction must reflect a real economic purpose. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds; however, exporters must repatriate and collect foreign-currency receipts within the statutory period, normally 18 months from the export declaration date unless an extension is approved by a foreign-exchange bank, and 100% of the proceeds may be held in domestic or approved offshore foreign-currency accounts. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned Chusik Hoesa is possible after declaring dividends, producing audited financial statements, completing corporate tax settlement, and submitting the foreign-investment registration certificate; dividend remittal is subject to a relevant tax-treaty withholding rate and post-transaction AML reporting. The main practical hurdles are not convertibility but reconciliation and documentation: a bank can suspend a remittance if invoice, tax invoice, or exchange purpose codes are inconsistent with the underlying business. Overall, Korea is not a stringent exchange-control jurisdiction, but its treasury compliance culture requires full record-keeping, bank-level attestation, and strict anti-money-laundering evidence for every visible flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a Korean resident exporter must receive export proceeds within the permitted collection period, generally 18 months from the date of export declaration for goods and a shorter period for services, with extensions available from the foreign-exchange bank upon valid justifications. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; an exporter is not required to surrender foreign currency to the central bank and may retain 100% of export receipts in a domestic foreign-currency account at a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Any conversion of those receipts into KRW must be made at the prevailing market rate through the same bank and must be identifiable in the Bank of Korea's foreign-exchange information system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign-exchange policy authority and the Korea Customs Service as the FX transaction reconciliation authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Primary feasibility: Low for any offshore digital EMI or non-bank fintech platform holding KRW accounts for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Offshore EMIs such as multi-country business banking platforms and virtual-account providers are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as domestic deposit-taking institutions under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and they are legally barred from providing domestic won-denominated clearing and settlement services to onshore companies. They may have ancillary viability for non-KRW custody or collections only if they independently accept a Korean registered entity and if the company's actual operating account remains held at a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank. For any treasury function that touches the local economy, collects KRW advertising revenue, pays domestic VAT, or requires Korean trade/foreign-exchange declarative controls, a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is mandatory. The recommended blueprint is therefore to use offshore EMIs for peripheral USD/EUR/GBP collections and payouts, but to anchor all statutory capital, FX compliance, and KRW liquidity at a licensed domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Chusik Hoesa must route through the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) mechanism. The foreign shareholder files a Foreign Investment Report at a designated foreign-exchange bank before or at the time of remittance. The bank then opens a restricted Foreign Capital Deposit Account into which the parent executes a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credit transfer with the message identifying the report number and the underlying purpose code for inflow direct investment into a Korean stock corporation. Upon arrival, the bank verifies the remittance instruction against the report, converts the foreign currency to KRW at the prevailing bank market rate, and issues a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate once the Chusik Hoesa is incorporated and the investment is registered. Capital verification requires the authorised director to submit the share subscription agreement, certificate of paid-in capital, and bank-issued foreign-currency deposit confirmation; for higher-value injections, Korean statutory auditors or a certified accounting firm may independently verify the bank's capital confirmation and the foreign-investment entry in the Korea Trade Information and Investment Promotion Agency or the relevant bank's FIPA system.For outbound payments to publishers, data partners, and overseas suppliers in the programmatic ad and SSP business, SWIFT MT103 is the base rail for direct discretionary service fees; MT202 is used for bank-to-bank treasury funding when separating client instructions from funding data. KRW-denominated payments to domestic counterparties are cleared through the domestic HOFINETACH system initiated via the bank's corporate cash-management platform. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are legally available but structurally inefficient for programmatic advertising because the deliverable is often occasional digital services rather than physical goods; they remain permissible only for confirmed procurement of physical media or licensed content assets where a set of documents is required. Every outbound disbursement must be supported by a grounds-for-payment declaration, the corresponding commercial contract or invoice, and a Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code; recurring publisher payouts should be pre-notified and linked to an approved request list to prevent typology-driven blockages.Onshore outward FX forwards with a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank, limited to hedging identifiable current-account cash flows such as USD-denominated ad revenue or publisher settlement exposure, with a maturity aligned to the commercial contract and documented under a master forward agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for KRW are permissible only when there is a real underlying transaction and the hedge is structured through or booked at a domestic licensed FX bank; resident companies cannot speculate on the won through NDFs or synthetic KRW exposure.Cross-currency swaps are lawful for Chusik Hoesa only as an incidental hedge of a capital-account transaction such as an inward foreign shareholder loan or repatriation planning, provided the swap has a natural linkage to the underlying outflow and the transaction is properly reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; speculative or structurally leveraged swaps are not available.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Primary feasibility: Low for any offshore digital EMI or non-bank fintech platform holding KRW accounts for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Offshore EMIs such as multi-country business banking platforms and virtual-account providers are not licensed by the Financial Services Commission as domestic deposit-taking institutions under the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and they are legally barred from providing domestic won-denominated clearing and settlement services to onshore companies. They may have ancillary viability for non-KRW custody or collections only if they independently accept a Korean registered entity and if the company's actual operating account remains held at a licensed Korean foreign-exchange bank. For any treasury function that touches the local economy, collects KRW advertising revenue, pays domestic VAT, or requires Korean trade/foreign-exchange declarative controls, a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank is mandatory. The recommended blueprint is therefore to use offshore EMIs for peripheral USD/EUR/GBP collections and payouts, but to anchor all statutory capital, FX compliance, and KRW liquidity at a licensed domestic bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9%, 19%, 21%, and 24% progressive national Corporate Income Tax (CIT) brackets, plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national CIT, producing combined effective rates of 9.9%, 20.9%, 23.1%, and 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on gross dividends paid by a Korean Chusik Hoesa to a non-resident corporate shareholder, composed of 20% national CIT withholding under the Corporate Tax Act plus 2% local income tax withholding. Treaty rates commonly reduce the rate to 5% for substantial holdings and 15% for portfolio holdings, depending on the specific bilateral treaty.

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100,000,000 in cross-border controlled transactions triggers the annual International Related Party Transaction Report; KRW 10,000,000,000 in cross-border controlled transactions triggers mandatory preparation of Local File and Master File; and a consolidated group with annual revenue of at least KRW 1,000,000,000,000 and multi-country related-party operations must file Country-by-Country Report in Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion through false reporting and concealed AdTech revenue

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 40% of the fraudulently underreported tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus daily delinquency charges. Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced where the Chusik Hoesa is used as a sham or conduit for tax evasion. Transfer pricing non-compliance and artificial profit shifting to offshore ad-tech affiliates

Statutory sanction: The Korean NTS may make an arm's-length transfer pricing adjustment under the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs and impose additional tax on the adjusted income, plus penalties for non-reporting or late submission of interrelated transaction statements.

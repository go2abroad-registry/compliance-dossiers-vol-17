Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. Where initial capital is expressed in the Articles of Incorporation, the full subscription amount must be deposited in a restricted corporate bank account in the Republic of Korea before the incorporation registration is submitted, and the deposited funds remain blocked until the registry has issued the certificate of incorporation. In-kind contributions require an independent appraiser valuation report. If no capital is declared, no deposit requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Completion Inspection and Fire Services Act certificate shall be obtained from the local fire and disaster prevention authority before commencement of production; if the factory floor area reaches 500 square meters or more, a civil defense shelter or evacuation plan must be approved by the local defense authority. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities seeking a Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN) must register for the Business Registration Certificate with the competent district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean fixed place of business or the place of the tax representative. The procedure is: (1) appoint a Korean tax representative (Gukse-Gwalli-in) who is jointly liable; (2) file the Non-Resident Business Registration Application (Form 60-A) with the district NTS office or via HomeTax using an external representative; (3) submit supporting documents including the certificate of incorporation (apostilled or consularized), a resolution appointing the tax representative, and evidence of the Korean fixed business address; (4) obtain the 10-digit Business Registration Number (TIN), which simultaneously functions as the VAT ID; (5) register for e-Tax Invoice issuance through HomeTax. No minimum capital is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act, and its global income is subject to corporate income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) is a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act, and its global income is subject to corporate income tax. There is no transparent pass-through regime for a domestic LLC.

Available election forms

None. The Korea Corporate Tax Act does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election. Entity classification is statutory. Hybrid mismatch rules are addressed via specific anti-abuse provisions, but no elective tax classification is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supply turnover. However, non-residents with a Korean place of business or making any taxable supply in Korea must register from the date of business commencement regardless of turnover; persons without a fixed place of business may also be required to register through an authorized VAT agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 21(1) of the VAT Act grants a 0% rate to the supply of goods that are exported, including goods placed into bonded areas, or if the goods are supplied to a non-resident and shipped from a bonded factory. For the AI pet robot hardware, the exporter must demonstrate: (a) the contract is with a non-resident; (b) the consideration is in foreign currency or a non-resident's local currency; (c) the goods are physically exported from Korea and a customs export declaration is filed; (d) after loading, the exporter receives the export price in the agreed currency. The goods must leave Korea generally within 6 months from date of export declaration, subject to extensions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange bank transaction certificate

Since July 1, 2021, e-tax invoices are mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses. Issuers must transmit invoice details to the HomeTax system within the day after issuance. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime akin to Latin American customs; instead, electronic sales reports must be filed semi-annually, and the NTS uses an integrated audit trail system with pre-approval of invoice formats. General rule: same-day transmission to the NTS.

If the export is found not to have occurred or the proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (without a valid extension from the Ministry of Strategy and Finance), the zero-rating is reversed, and the exporter must pay VAT at 10% on the export value, plus an additional tax of 10% of the evaded tax, and up to 40% under the fraudulent claim provisions of the Framework Act on National Taxes (Article 47-3), computed on the underpaid tax. Late filing and non-transmission of e-invoice penalties also apply. Claims may be audited retrospectively for five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalised but regulated convertibility regime. The won is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlements, service payments, and profit remittances or royalties, subject to mandatory reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FERIS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account transactions are broadly open, but certain inward foreign direct investment, securities dealing, and borrowing transactions require prior notification or application to the Bank of Korea or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Foreign-invested companies are entitled to dividend remittances after settlement of corporate income tax and withholding tax; a tax withholding certificate and audited financial statements are normally required. The old physical surrender requirement for export proceeds is no longer in force; however, all export earnings must be settled and reported through a registered foreign exchange bank. Practical convertibility hurdles are procedural rather than structural and include purpose-code classification, submission of underlying evidence, and the mandate to complete settlement through a licensed domestic bank. Non-resident access to onshore KRW liquidity and forward markets is limited to firms with demonstrable, documented underlying exposure. No parallel-market subsidy or central-planning allocation exists, and the official onshore rate is market-determined. Overall credit and convertibility risk is low, but treasury operations must maintain strict FERIS reporting discipline to avoid administrative penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory collection period for export proceeds is five years from the date of export, unless extended with Bank of Korea approval. There is no mandatory ratio requiring conversion of foreign currency export receipts into KRW. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert to KRW at a registered foreign exchange bank, but every receipt must be captured in FERIS under the applicable export settlement transaction purpose code. Delayed collection, unusual partial receipts, or retention beyond the permissible cycle can trigger Korea Customs Service and Korea Financial Intelligence Unit review.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (Hanguk Eunhaeng), in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore financial-technology EMI platforms do not hold a Korean Banking Act license or Electronic Financial Transactions Act registration and are legally barred from providing onshore corporate operating accounts, receiving export proceeds, or acting as the bank of record for capital verification in Korea. A foreign EMI may only be used indirectly for e-commerce card acceptance through a licensed Korean payment gateway such as KG Inicis or Toss Payments, but it cannot serve as a treasury settlement rail for the Korean LLC. Therefore all statutory capital injections, operational cash-pooling, trade settlements, and FX transactions must be routed through a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. Relying on an offshore EMI as a primary treasury channel would create a regulatory violation and expose the entity to anti-money-laundering chargebacks and frozen-funds risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital shall be routed by the non-resident shareholder via SWIFT MT103 to the dedicated foreign exchange bank account opened under the Foreign Investment Report filed with the Bank of Korea and Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The wire must be categorised on the flow-out side as direct investment capital subscription or capital increase under the FERIS statutory foreign exchange transaction purpose code for foreign direct investment. Upon receipt, the bank will convert to KRW at the prevailing market rate, issue a Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate of Inward Remittance and Capital Verification, and the company must complete the statutory paid-in capital registration in the Korean corporate registry. Dividends, loan repayments, and technical royalties can only be remitted outbound after clearance of the corporate income tax return, documentary evidence of withholding tax payment, and submission of audited financial statements to the remitting bank.Routine component supplier payments will be executed through SWIFT MT103 with full invoice, packing list, and purchase order references, using the FERIS trade settlement purpose code for goods import. For higher-value assembly component procurement, use irrevocable documentary letters of credit opened through the Korean issuing bank, with negotiation against compliant documents at the foreign supplier's bank. For trusted medium-term counterparties, documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms are acceptable. Every outbound payment must be matched to a Korean customs import declaration prior to wire release.

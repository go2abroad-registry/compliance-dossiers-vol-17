Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital for a Chusik Hoesa; the founders are free to determine the paid-in capital in the Articles of Incorporation. 10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For domestic businesses: mandatory VAT registration from the commencement of taxable business; general taxpayers with annual supplies exceeding KRW 80,000,000 are subject to full VAT filing and electronic tax invoice obligations. For foreign non-resident electronic service suppliers without a Korean domestic place of business: no turnover threshold applies, and registration with NTS via HomeTax is mandatory before supplying electronic services to customers in Korea.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Export of film copyright licensing and distribution services to non-resident clients qualifies for 0% VAT under the zero-rating provisions of the Korean VAT Act and its Enforcement Decree when the service is supplied to a foreign person, is actually used or consumed outside Korea, and the Korean supplier receives payment in foreign currency. A purely intangible copyright license does not require a customs declaration; zero-rating is substantiated by the licensing agreement, cross-border service description, record of foreign use, and foreign securities. If accompanying physical master media are exported, the goods element also qualifies for 0% VAT upon completion of export customs clearance.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Korean won (KRW) is broadly convertible on current-account items such as film licensing royalties, distribution fees, dividends, and trade payments, which generally settle through domestic foreign-exchange banks under a simplified FETA declaration rather than requiring a separate permission. Capital-account items, including inbound equity subscription by a foreign shareholder, require an advance declaration through the receiving domestic bank and issuance of a foreign investment registration confirmation before the funds are credited to the entity's escrow account. Although Korea's regime is fundamentally liberal, the bank remains legally responsible for checking the reality of the transaction and for ensuring that the stated royalty or capital remittance matches the underlying contract and corporate documentation. In practice, profit remittance to a foreign shareholder or licensor will be blocked if the company fails to produce a valid commercial contract, withholding tax evidence, foreign investor registration certificate, or audited accounting proof of the distributable reserve. Capital-account repatriation can also be deferred by the bank if the entity has unresolved tax investigations, unpaid corporate income tax, or missing CRS/FATCA declarations.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under FETA Article 20 and the Bank of Korea Regulation on Foreign Exchange Transactions, export and film-licensing receipts must be collected through a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank within 12 months of the customs base date or invoice due date, with extension of up to 6 months available on bank approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may hold the receipt in a registered foreign currency account, but the receipt must be reported to the bank and all settlements into KRW must be made through that domestically located licensed bank. Netting against imports or overseas expenses requires prior declaration and can only be done with bank confirmation.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank and foreign exchange management authority, with Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as policy supervisor under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), and Korea Customs Service / Foreign Exchange Information and Monitoring Center (FEMIC) as the trade-related FX reporting and monitoring repository.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low and legally unsuitable as a primary treasury rail in Korea. Under the Banking Act and FETA, only a licensed domestic bank can open KRW current/settlement accounts and act as a foreign exchange bank for a Chusik Hoesa. Offshore digital EMIs generally do not hold a Korean banking licence, cannot provide won-denominated IBANs, and cannot legally clear film-licensing payments on behalf of a Korean resident entity. They may be used only as an ancillary payment aggregator or card issuer, and even then only after the KR entity's main operating account is held at a licensed Tier-1 bank. Relying on an unregulated EMI for subscription capital or royalty collection would create a serious AML blocker and may constitute unauthorized foreign exchange business in Korea.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Equity capital from a foreign shareholder must follow the FDI route: the shareholder and the Chusik Hoesa submit a foreign investment declaration with the receiving domestic licensed bank, obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Confirmation, open a dedicated escrow/capital settlement account designated for that inward remittance, and transfer via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code classified under Direct Investment - Equity Capital and the FDI registration reference noted in field 70. The bank issues a capital-receipt certificate, and the company must secure a certified accountant or auditor capital-verification report before amending the Commercial Registry. Once escrow proceeds are received and verified, funds may be converted to KRW at the bank's market rate for working capital or film-right acquisition; the registration confirmation must be retained as a mandatory precondition for any later dividend or capital repatriation.Film distribution royalties, license fees, and profit participations are current-account payments. After tax-office withholding and corporate banking compliance checks are satisfied, remittances are routed through a Korean licensed bank using SWIFT MT103 with underlying invoices and contracts. For content license distribution where payment is tied to release milestones, a Commercial Letter of Credit with clean terms or a Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A) is accepted. For international digital sales, the bank requires a valid internal FX purpose code and the FEMIC declaration.

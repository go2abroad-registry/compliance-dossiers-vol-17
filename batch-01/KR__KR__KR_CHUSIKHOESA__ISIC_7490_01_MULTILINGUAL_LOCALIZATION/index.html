Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registration Office of the District Court with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office in the Republic of Korea, operated under the Supreme Court of Korea; online filings are accepted through the Supreme Court Internet Registration Office.

Governing statute

Korean Commercial Act, Act No. 1000, enacted December 20, 1962, as amended, particularly Articles 288 through 317 governing incorporation and registration of a stock corporation (Chusik Hoesa): Article 288 (incorporator requirements), Article 289 (execution of articles of incorporation), Article 292 (notarization of articles), Article 295 (subscription of shares), Article 312 (payment of subscription price), Article 313 (appointment of initial directors and statutory auditor), and Article 317 (registration of incorporation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Establishment of a Stock Corporation filed under Article 317 of the Korean Commercial Act and Article 66 of the Rules on Registration of Non-Contentious Matters.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 9 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Korean Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa. However, the total subscription price of all shares issued at incorporation must be fully paid before the incorporation registration is granted. The payment must be made by depositing the funds into a bank or licensed securities company account opened in the name of the proposed company, and the deposit confirmation certificate must be submitted to the court registry. The deposited funds must remain blocked until after registration; after registration, the depositary institution must verify the corporation registration certificate before releasing the funds. In-kind contributions must be appraised by a court-appointed appraiser and the relevant assets must be fully transferred before the registration deadline.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business in Korea; if not, it must appoint a Korean tax representative. The entity then files a business registration application with the competent district tax office through HomeTax, attaching its certificate of incorporation, overseas business registration certificate, board resolution, and power of attorney for the tax representative. The NTS issues the Korean business registration number, which functions as the statutory TIN and VAT ID. For foreign suppliers of digital services or localization services to Korean consumers, a simplified VAT registration may be filed through HomeTax under the foreigner business registration procedure. After issuance, the entity must register its tax invoice issuing credentials and e-tax invoice program with NTS.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business in Korea; if not, it must appoint a Korean tax representative. The entity then files a business registration application with the competent district tax office through HomeTax, attaching its certificate of incorporation, overseas business registration certificate, board resolution, and power of attorney for the tax representative. The NTS issues the Korean business registration number, which functions as the statutory TIN and VAT ID. For foreign suppliers of digital services or localization services to Korean consumers, a simplified VAT registration may be filed through HomeTax under the foreigner business registration procedure. After issuance, the entity must register its tax invoice issuing credentials and e-tax invoice program with NTS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election for a Chusik Hoesa; entity-level taxation is mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover de minimis for VAT registration; registration is required from the commencement of taxable supplies. The simplified taxpayer regime applies to eligible small vendors with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000. Foreign non-resident suppliers with Korean place of supply are also required to register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods must be physically removed from Korea and shipped to a foreign purchaser under a Korean Customs Service export declaration. Localization, translation, media dubbing, and related services qualify for zero rating when supplied to a non-resident business or non-resident customer for use wholly outside Korea, evidencing by written contract, invoice, and foreign currency settlement. International transport and directly related supporting services also qualify.

  • Korean Customs Service electronic export declaration via UNI-PASS (equivalent to an export bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Commercial invoice and localization/dubbing service contract
  • Bank foreign exchange transaction certificate or inward remittance certificate equivalent showing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Overseas client documentation confirming non-resident status and use of delivered digital content or localization outputs outside Korea

Electronic tax invoice issuance is mandatory for all VAT-taxable business-to-business supplies. Invoices must be issued through the HomeTax portal or a certified electronic tax invoice system and transmitted to NTS no later than the day following issuance. NTS applies near-real-time validation through API-based e-invoice submission, and retail sales to individuals are generally evidenced by cash receipts or credit card sales slips transmitted to authorized payment clearing houses. There is no separate real-time anti-fraud digital stamping model; the electronic tax invoice clearance is achieved through mandatory digital transmission to NTS.

NTS and the Korean Customs Service jointly cross-check export declarations, customs loading records, transport documents, and bank remittance trails. If the zero-rate claim is not substantiated by the required customs and foreign exchange documentation, or if export proceeds are not repatriated by the 180-day deadline, the zero rate is reversed and VAT at 10% is assessed on the supply, together with statutory VAT surcharges ranging from 10% to 40% of the deficient tax. Willful evasion or issuance of false tax invoices additionally gives rise to criminal penalties, customs penalties, and a retrospective clawback of any input VAT credits previously recovered on zero-rated export inputs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised current-account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Current-account receipts and payments for goods, services and royalties are generally free and require ex-post reporting rather than licensing. The Korean won is not globally fully convertible due to capital-account controls; all cross-border capital transactions require either prior declaration, registration, or reporting to the licensed foreign-exchange bank or BOK. Equity capital contributions from non-residents must be reported through the reinvestment and foreign-investment notification framework under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act if the investment meets the statutory thresholds. Surplus funds cannot simply be remitted as dividends without corporate tax filing and a BOK foreign-exchange declaration; profit remittance is permitted after audited financial statements and dividend approval, subject to withholding tax and tax clearance evidence. Export proceeds must be received within 18 months from the date of export, may be held in foreign-currency bank accounts, and are subject to deposit reporting; there is no forced conversion of export proceeds to KRW, but when conversion occurs it must be executed at a market rate through a licensed foreign-exchange bank with the appropriate transaction purpose code. The practical constraint for a localization agency is not convertibility fears but the need to document every inbound and outbound cross-border movement and maintain transaction-level files for BOK and tax audits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and BOK notifications, export earnings must be collected and deposited within 18 months from the date of export, with extensions available under BOK approval; there is no mandatory fixed percentage of Korean won conversion, meaning the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%, exporters may hold 100% of proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at domestic licensed banks, and any voluntary conversion must be at market rates through a licensed foreign-exchange bank and reported using the applicable BOK transaction-purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Korea Customs Service act as co-applying authorities under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary entity accounts. Wise and Payoneer can be used only as ancillary payout or collection rails outside Korea where they hold regulated licenses, but they are not chartered banks in the Republic of Korea and are not recognised under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act as substitutes for a statutory corporate bank account. A Chusik Hoesa is expected to maintain its domestic operating account, capital escrow facility and FX settlement at a Korean commercial bank. Domestic fintech providers must be licensed as electronic financial business operators; foreign EMIs therefore cannot be lawfully relied upon as the primary treasury, capital-verification or cross-border settlement account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for a Chusik Hoesa must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's account to the beneficiary's won or foreign-currency capital escrow account designated at a licensed Korean bank. The remittance instruction must include the Korean bank's SWIFT address, beneficiary legal name exactly as registered with the bank, investor legal name, and the BOK transaction-purpose code for inward remittance or foreign investment capital. The receiving bank must issue a Certificate of Inward Remittance before the funds are moved from escrow into operating liquidity. If the injection qualifies as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the company or investor must submit a foreign-investment report to the designated foreign-exchange bank or BOK before or within the statutory period after receipt, using the capital/equity classification rather than a current-transfer classification. The capital verification procedure requires the bank certificate, the revised shareholder register, board minutes and the registered entity's statutory seal acknowledgement.Outbound disbursements for the localization agency should be executed through the domestic bank's corporate FX platform using SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for service and settlement invoices. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are intended for physical trade in goods and are not the correct clearing rail for professional multilingual translation or media dubbing services. For each outbound service payment, the bank will require the underlying service agreement, an itemised invoice, proof of the recipient bank, and the BOK service-transaction purpose code. Where the payment crosses with-a-holding-tax jurisdictions, the bank may require evidence of withholding tax filing or the applicable tax treaty before remittance.Deliverable forward contracts with an onshore licensed Korean FX bank, permitted where the notional amount does not exceed the documented underlying receivable or payable arising from localization service contracts or foreign-currency capital exposure, with the forward tenor aligned to the actual expected settlement date.Foreign-exchange swaps between KRW and USD are permissible for matching funding gaps, provided each leg corresponds to a real transaction such as an upcoming service settlement or a capital remittance, and the swap is not used to build speculative open positions.Cross-currency swaps are permitted only when the Chusik Hoesa has an actual foreign-currency debt or investment position with notional and repricing terms matching the base transaction, and where BOK notification or registration is completed in advance if the underlying transaction crosses the regulatory threshold.KRW non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not an acceptable onshore hedging tool for the Korean-resident operating entity; NDFs are primarily offshore instruments and may be treated as speculative or circumventing Korean FX rules. Onshore hedging must be settled through a domestic licensed bank and continuously supported by underlying transaction evidence.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies and the Enforcement Decree of the External Audit Act, an external CPA audit is compulsory for: (a) every listed company; (b) any unlisted Chusik Hoesa whose total assets as of the previous fiscal year end exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9,000,000); and (c) any company specifically designated by the Financial Services Commission due to public interest, high external borrowing, or dominance in the securities market. For a non-listed localization agency, the decisive quantitative test is the balance-sheet asset threshold of KRW 120 billion? Correcting to KRW 120 hundred million? Actually the threshold is KRW 12,000,000,000 (twelve billion Korean won, i.e., approximately USD 9,000,000). If total assets exceed this level, a registered CPA audit firm must audit the financial statements regardless of turnover or headcount. There is no separate turnover or headcount trigger for general non-listed corporations under the current external audit rules, but the Financial Services Commission may lower the threshold to KRW 10,000,000,000 for certain high-risk industries. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate; progressive Korean corporate income tax rates are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% over KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 21% over KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, and 24% over KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% effective statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, composed of 20% national corporate withholding tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax; the rate may be reduced by an applicable bilateral tax treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions generally triggers the Local File and Master File documentation requirement, with lower category-specific thresholds for service and financial transactions; all cross-border related-party dealings must also be reported through the annual corporate tax return international transaction summary

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Core Income-Generating Activities concept is enforced through substance-over-form judicial doctrine, controlled foreign company rules, and the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test; the Chusik Hoesa must maintain real employees, office space, board decisions, client contracts, invoicing, and quality control for localization and dubbing services in Korea, otherwise the tax authority may disregard the entity as a conduit, deny treaty benefits, and recharacterize income as Korean-source business income

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through unreported overseas payment rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes, fraudulent non-filing or underreporting attracts additional tax of 40% of the evaded corporate tax, plus late-payment surcharges of about 0.03% per day; the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act authorizes imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to three times the evaded tax

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the Chusik Hoesa is an alter ego of its shareholders; the representative director and signatory officers are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax under the Korean Commercial Act, and the NTS may attach personal assets, impose travel bans, and blacklist the directors during criminal tax investigation
Shell company lacking economic substance and abusing treaty benefits

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recharacterize the arrangement under anti-avoidance rules and the OECD MLI principal purpose test, deny the DTA benefits, impute the full Korean-source profit to the Chusik Hoesa, and impose a 40% additional tax for dishonest underreporting together with penalty interest; the foreign shareholder may become secondarily liable for the unpaid assessment

Director personal liability: Any director who signs false substance declarations, boards resolutions, or DTA benefit forms can be treated as a co-principal in tax evasion; civil joint liability follows under Commercial Act Article 401, with criminal exposure of imprisonment up to 5 years, fines, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a Korean corporate director
Transfer pricing mispricing and disguised profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The NTS may reassess the taxpayer under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs using an approved arm's length method, deny interest deductions, apply the 10% or 40% understatement penalty depending on fraudulent intent, and impose daily late-payment surcharges; failure to produce transfer pricing documentation allows the NTS to estimate taxable income by default methodology

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false intercompany agreements and intentionally ignore transfer pricing rules are personally liable for the tax shortfall when the corporate form is disregarded; in egregious cases, the prosecutor can indict the directors under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the court may impose personal asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment up to 5 years

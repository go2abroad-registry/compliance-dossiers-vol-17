Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Chusik Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; a company may be incorporated with nominal capital. However, market practice for a B2B cross-border platform requires at least KRW 10,000,000 (approximately USD 7,500) of paid-in capital to satisfy bank misconduct due diligence and payment service onboarding. The full subscription amount must be deposited into a temporary bank subscription account in the Republic of Korea before incorporation registration, and the bank must issue a certificate of payment for the shares. No blocked escrow account is mandated by the Commercial Act. If the marketplace will hold customer or merchant settlement funds, a trust or escrow arrangement may be triggered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the FSC regulatory guidance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online registration and filing portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity (including a B2B marketplace operator) that commences a business or establishes a fixed place of business in Korea must apply for a Business Registration Certificate at the competent district NTS office, or electronically through HomeTax, within 20 days of commencement of business. Required submissions include the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, evidence of a Korean fixed place of business, an appointed Korean tax representative's power of attorney and identification, a board resolution appointing the representative, and, where available, an apostilled or certified company extraction from the home registry. The filing generates a 10-digit Korean Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which simultaneously serves as the VAT registration number. No separate VAT registration is required beyond this business registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for corporate entities. Any corporation engaged in business in Korea, regardless of turnover, must complete business registration and VAT registration within 20 days of business commencement. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies where goods are sold to a nonresident purchaser, the goods are physically exported outside Korea, and the export is evidenced by an export declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to nonresident business customers are zero-rated when the service is consumed or used outside Korea and is declared on the VAT return with supporting service contracts and foreign exchange receipt proof. For a B2B cross-border marketplace operator, each underlying transaction must satisfy the legal export definition and be documented per Korean VAT and Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (UNI-PASS entry)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for airfreight)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Banking proof of offshore payment: Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation (Overseas Remittance Receipt Confirmation) issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank in Korea in the exporter's name
  • Evidence of the underlying B2B order or contract between the Korean supplier and the nonresident purchaser

Korean VAT law requires every registered business operator supplying goods or services to issue a tax invoice. For general taxpayers, the tax invoice must be issued as an electronic tax invoice transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice platform (e-Scro) no later than the day after the supply date. The NTS assigns and validates electronic signatures and unique invoice numbers; no physical fiscal stamp is required. Although Korea does not operate a full real-time fiscal clearance system comparable to Latin American models, the e-tax invoice transmission constitutes immediate near-real-time tax administration control and supports anti-fraud digital stamping.

If the export documentation is missing, incomplete, or the foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated through recognized Korean banking channels within 180 calendar days from the export declaration date, the NTS may revoke zero-rating, assess 10% VAT on the transaction value, and impose overdue interest. Under the VAT Act, failure to report or underreporting can attract additional tax penalties of up to 20% for negligent errors and 40% for fraudulent or hidden transactions. The statute of limitations on NTS collection is five years from the relevant tax payment date; this extends to ten years in cases of intentional tax evasion or false accounting. Gross misrepresentation may also trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberal but tightly supervised foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), collectively administered by the Bank of Korea, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and authorized foreign exchange banks. Current-account transactions, including cross-border B2B marketplace payments, service fees, royalties, and ordinary operating settlements, are freely convertible upon submission of a transaction confirmation to the designated bank; prior approval is not required. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a Chusik Hoesa, inbound loans, and equity contributions, are subject to a report/declaration framework, with the FDI report filed through a foreign exchange bank before funds are remitted and a foreign investment registration certificate issued. Profit remittance is permitted offshore via a bank after corporate income tax is paid and the board declares a dividend subject to a 20 or 22 percent withholding tax plus local surtax, with treaty relief available where applicable. Actual treasury hurdles include documentary evidence of audited retained earnings, tax payment certificates, bank confirmation of the underlying FDI report, and strict matching between the remittance purpose code and the underlying transaction. KRW is not freely usable overseas and must be converted via onshore banks, but no mandatory surrender of export earnings is imposed. On balance, convertibility is robust for genuine trade and investment flows, with the primary friction being documentation and beneficial ownership verification rather than convertibility denial.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and the Foreign Trade Act, export proceeds received for goods or digital B2B services must be collected through a Korean foreign exchange bank within six months from the export invoice date or service completion date unless an extension is approved by the Korea Customs Service or the Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in the current regime; exporters may retain these receipts in foreign-currency deposit accounts, convert them to KRW at market rates, or use them for eligible payments. However, the bank must receive proof of the underlying export transaction and the exporter unique identification number, and the proceeds must flow through a payment rail that enables reconciliation with Korea Customs Service trade statistics. Overdue and unreported export proceeds are reportable as foreign exchange violations and may attract penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, acting as the central bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange policy authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs are legally barred from issuing domestic Korean corporate IBANs or opening local KRW settlement accounts under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They are also not authorized to act as a designated foreign exchange bank for receiving export proceeds. Their viable role is limited to downstream collection or payment aggregation for marketplace buyers, with funds ultimately routed through a licensed Korean bank. Treasury must therefore treat SWIFT MT103 through a domestic tier-1 bank as the only compliant primary clearing rail. Overall feasibility rating: LOW for primary corporate banking; MEDIUM only as an ancillary payment interface if the EMI partners with a licensed Korean bank, and that bank remains the depository institution of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route FDI capital through a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea. First file the foreign direct investment notification with the bank or Invest Korea under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and obtain a registration certificate. Then open a Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account in KRW or foreign currency with the same bank. Arrange the inbound remittance as an SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident investor, using the applicable Bank of Korea foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment equity contribution and clearly identifying the beneficiary Korean bank and the Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account number. After the funds are credited, the bank verifies the inward remittance and automatically reports it to the Bank of Korea. Capital may be deployed into the operational account only after the foreign investment registration certificate indicates completed capital verification. For established trade partners with acceptable sovereign risk, use Documentary Collections, whether documents against payment or documents against acceptance, supported by a fixed bill of lading or electronic delivery proof. Overall feasibility rating: LOW for primary corporate banking; MEDIUM only as an ancillary payment interface if the EMI partners with a licensed Korean bank, and that bank remains the depository institution of record.

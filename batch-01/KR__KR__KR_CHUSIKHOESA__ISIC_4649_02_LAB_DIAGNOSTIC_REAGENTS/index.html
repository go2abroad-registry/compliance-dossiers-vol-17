Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

The Korean entity (Chusik Hoesa) must apply to the District Tax Office of the National Tax Service for a Business Registration Number within 20 days from the commencement of business under Article 5 of the VAT Act, through HomeTax or by direct filing. For a foreign non-resident shareholder or parent, the Korean subsidiary first completes incorporation at the Court Registry under the Commercial Act; the registry transmits incorporation data to NTS and the BRN is issued after the District Tax Office confirms the head office address, legal representative, and business purpose. The standard submission bundle includes: (i) certificate of incorporation or equivalent foreign registration certificate of the non-resident parent, (ii) notarized articles of incorporation and shareholder resolutions approving the Korean establishment, (iii) lease agreement or ownership evidence for the Korean head office, (iv) the business registration application Form through HomeTax, (v) a power of attorney and identification for the appointed domestic tax representative when no Korean-resident officer exists under Article 96 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, and (vi) the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate if the formation qualifies under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The BRN then functions as the unified corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding account identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory local-currency turnover threshold is prescribed. Any person carrying on a business and supplying taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT with NTS within 20 days of business commencement; a wholesale distributor of IVD diagnostic reagents is therefore mandatorily registered from the initial supply, regardless of annual revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 11 of the VAT Act and Article 24 of the Enforcement Decree, exports of tangible goods such as clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and IVD reagents are zero-rated where the goods are physically delivered to a foreign destination under a Korean Customs Service export declaration, the exporter is the Korean supplier, and the export price is received in foreign exchange from the non-resident buyer. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents and consumed outside Korea are zero-rated when paid in foreign currency, but for physical wholesale distribution the zero rate is substantiated primarily by the customs export documentation and foreign exchange receipt trail.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (certified duplicate) filed through the UNI-PASS system showing KR_CHUSIKHOESA as exporter and the goods as leaving Korea
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing international carriage to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in a convertible currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with HS classification, quantities, unit prices, country of origin, and destination details
  • Sales Contract or Purchase Order executed with the foreign buyer before shipment
  • Certificate of Origin (Korea-EU FTA, RCEP, KORUS, or applicable bilateral agreement) where required, and any IVD-related export authorization issued by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety for regulated reagents

Mandatory electronic tax invoices. Under Article 32-2 of the VAT Act, B2B VAT invoices must be issued through the HomeTax e-tax invoice system or an NTS-certified system connected via the HomeTax Open API; the issuer must transmit the invoice data to NTS within one day of issuance. NTS applies real-time electronic matching between issued invoice data, recipient input VAT claims, and VAT returns; mismatched or unreported invoices are automatically excluded from input VAT credit until corrected. Paper tax invoices are not permitted for ordinary B2B supplies except small cash transactions below KRW 100,000. For anti-fraud integrity, each electronic tax invoice carries an NTS-verified QR/barcode and digital signature generated via HomeTax.

If NTS determines that the export zero-rating is unsubstantiated or that foreign exchange repatriation was not completed within 180 days from export, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 10% VAT and previously credited input VAT is clawed back with additional tax for false or deficient returns at statutory rates ranging from 20% to 40% of the unpaid tax, plus per-day overdue surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes. In cases of fraudulent export invoicing or deliberately fabricated customs declarations, additional penalties include criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and retrospective assessments within the extended adjustment periods for concealed transactions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized capital and current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for trade and service-related current account transactions, and most capital account movements are permitted on a notification or license basis. For a wholesale IVD reagent distribution entity, export proceeds and import payments for qualifying goods and services are freely settled through designated foreign exchange banks without mandatory surrender of foreign currency receipts; however, the exporter must file a settlement report and maintain supporting documents such as invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations to substantiate the underlying trade. For profit remittances to overseas shareholders, dividends may be repatriated freely after tax and after full payment of the proportional capital verification, but the remitting bank will require proof of the board resolution, financial statements, tax clearance, and the official corporate registry extract. There is a real-demand underlying requirement for FX hedging; speculative positions are prohibited. Capital contributions by non-resident shareholders are eligible for registration and subsequent capital verification by a certified public accountant, and the resulting won-denominated equity deposits are freely usable for working capital and operational disbursements. Surrender requirements are essentially absent; instead, exporters must repatriate and convert proceeds into KRW through an authorized FX bank within the statutory reporting timeframe following the month of receipt. Practical hurdles in profit remittance primarily arise from banking KYC reviews around source-of-funds documentation, UBO transparency, and MFDS licensing in the IVD sector, but the legal convertibility framework itself poses no material restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korea does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exports must be settled through an authorized foreign exchange bank, and the exporter is required to submit a foreign exchange receipt/payment report within the reporting month for the settlement; foreign currency may be retained in an overseas or domestic foreign currency account, but the origin must be tracked and the proceeds must be used in compliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the bank's internal trade verification policies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting jointly under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; actual enforcement for trade and capital account filings is delegated to designated foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary legal account holder. South Korea does not recognize foreign fintech EMI licenses as banking charters, and a Chusik Hoesa conducting wholesale IVD reagent distribution must maintain its principal treasury and trade settlement accounts with a licensed domestic foreign exchange bank. Cross-border EMIs can provide ancillary payment initiation, card issuance, or multicurrency wallets only if they partner with a licensed Korean bank, obtain FIU registration, and comply with the Electronic Financial Transactions Act; however, they are not viable for capital verification, trade settlement, or regulatory reporting. Domestic fintech firms such as Toss Bank and Kakaobank are online banks but they generally service retail/consumer use cases and are not optimal for corporate cross-border trade treasury with FX documentation requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must wire the equity contribution to a KRW capital escrow account at a designated Korean foreign exchange bank, using the overseas remitter bank's SWIFT MT103 with purpose code specifically assigned to foreign direct investment capital acquisition. The receiving Korean bank generates a foreign exchange receipt and payment report under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the Korean entity must file the inward FDI reporting. Once the funds are credited and the foreign exchange report is accepted, a Korean CPA completes capital verification to record the paid-in capital in the corporate registry; no physical movement of cash is permitted. The wiring must carry accurate remitter name, the company's registration number, and reference to the equity subscription agreement in the remittance details to avoid rejection by compliance screening.Outbound trade settlements for diagnostic reagent procurement and distribution will use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for standard pre-shipment payments and open account arrangements; commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) issued by the Korean bank are recommended for low-and-burden distribution to established suppliers requiring documentary certainty; documentary collections are acceptable for lower-risk trade flows but require precise invoice, packing list, and bill of lading matching. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary legal account holder. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are required for controlled transactions with foreign related parties when the transaction value exceeds KRW 10 billion annually or aggregate domestic transaction value exceeds KRW 30 billion; Master File is required where group revenue exceeds KRW 50 billion; the annual report of international business transactions must be filed even below thresholds if cross-border transactions exceed KRW 100 million.

Economic substance scope: South Korea applies substance-over-form and abuse-of-rights doctrines under Article 15 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Corporate Tax Act. A Korean distributor or holding vehicle with no real office, employees, warehouse capacity, inventory management, and decision-making functions may be disregarded and its income attributed to the foreign principal. Transfer pricing documentation must include a full functional and risk analysis. Economic substance is equally relevant for treaty entitlement under the OECD Multilateral Instrument and domestic anti-abuse rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to 3 times the evaded tax under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; additional fraudulent underreporting penalty of 40% of underreported income; public disclosure of the taxpayer's identity by the National Tax Service.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in, ordered, or ratified the tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax and penalties; personal travel bans, asset freezing, and criminal custodial sentences may be imposed.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the legal form and reattribute taxable income to the controlling foreign entity; reassessment at the full corporate rate plus penalties; denial of treaty benefits; registration of the entity may be cancelled for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts under the Commercial Act where the corporate veil is pierced; criminal penalties for false notarial registration and foreign exchange violation sanctions including travel bans may apply.
Transfer Pricing Penalty / Improper TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to arm's length; penalty of up to 30% of undocumented international related-party transactions; additional corporate tax assessment on adjusted income; daily interest surcharge of 1.02% per month on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Representative directors and CFOs who signed false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly failed to disclose related-party transactions can face personal liability for the resulting tax assessments and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.
AML / Foreign Exchange Control Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to KRW 50 million for unreported foreign exchange transactions; customs penalties up to five times the value of undeclared goods; potential criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to three years for serious violations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure or execute unreported cross-border payments are subject to personal criminal liability, travel bans, and asset seizure; individual fines and imprisonment may be imposed independently of corporate penalties.

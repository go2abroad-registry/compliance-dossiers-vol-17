Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Chusik Hoesa under the Commercial Act after the 2012 amendment. However, the promoters and subscribers must pay in full the aggregate issue price of all subscribed shares into a licensed bank account before the incorporation registration is filed, and the paid-in funds may not be withdrawn until the incorporation registration is lawfully completed. There is no separate government escrow account requirement beyond this statutory payment restriction.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) through the HomeTax electronic tax portal (hometax.go.kr), with export customs verification through the Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS system (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to supply taxable goods or services in South Korea must appoint a Korean tax representative and execute a power of attorney in HomeTax using the NTS tax-representative management function. The representative files an Application for Business Registration (Sa-eopja-deungrok-shincheongseo) with the competent district NTS office, attaching evidence of foreign incorporation, constitutional documents and board resolution appointing the local agent, proof of Korean business site or agent location, and, for electronic service suppliers, a simplified registration declaration as a foreign electronic service provider. Chusik Hoesa corporations formed in Korea apply within 20 days from business commencement. NTS issues the unified Korean Business Registration Number (BRN), a 10-digit identifier formatted 000-00-00000, used simultaneously for corporate income tax withholding, employment and indirect tax obligations, and VAT registration. For foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers, VAT registration through a tax representative is compulsory before or within 20 days after supply commencement, even in the absence of a domestic physical place of business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - no monetary registration threshold is imposed for a domestic Chusik Hoesa or for a foreign non-resident supplier of electronic services. VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable business activity. The simplified taxpayer regime is limited to small individual businesses and does not apply to incorporated entities. A foreign electronic service supplier must register through a tax representative irrespective of turnover, with no statutory de-minimis exemption for corporations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act to physically exported goods that are cleared through Korean Customs on UNI-PASS and to services exported to foreign customers where the place of supply is outside Korea, the service is used outside Korea, and payment is received in foreign currency. For a no-code website builder, cross-border digital platform services and web templates supplied to non-resident business customers qualify as zero-rated export services only if the supplier keeps contemporaneous evidence of the customer's offshore establishment, the non-Korean place of consumption, and foreign-currency receipt. Supplies made to Korean consumers or to Korean business customers for use in Korea are domestic supplies subject to 10% VAT.

  • Export Declaration approved by the Korea Customs Service on UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and export/supply contract identifying the foreign purchaser and exact service/goods scope
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure of goods from Korea, where physical goods are involved
  • Packing list and export customs documentation matching the UNI-PASS declaration, where physical goods are involved
  • Foreign-exchange receipt certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing collection of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Electronic zero-rated export tax invoice submitted through HomeTax
  • Service records proving offshore place of supply and foreign beneficial use for digital no-code builder and website-template exports

Korea does not mandate real-time continuous clearance or digital stamping. Domestic suppliers subject to electronic tax invoicing must issue tax invoices through HomeTax e-tax-invoice or an NTS-certified EDI/API connection and transmit them to the NTS by the statutory due date, normally by the day following issuance. B2B supply is documented by a Korean tax invoice; B2C cash sales may be documented through the cash receipt system, and credit card sales slips serve as VAT vouchers. Foreign nonresident electronic service suppliers use the simplified NTS electronic-services return system and must retain transaction-level records, including customer identity and place of supply, while issuing a Korean e-tax invoice only for B2B purchasers that request one.

The NTS and KCS jointly verify zero-rated export claims by cross-matching UNI-PASS declarations, tax invoices, shipping documents, and the exporter's foreign-exchange bank receipts. If documentation is absent, inconsistent, or proceeds are not repatriated within the required period, the zero rate is denied, the 10% VAT is recomputed and assessed, and statutory VAT additional tax for non-reporting, under-reporting or fraudulent reporting is imposed. Refunds already received are clawed back with interest-equivalent additional charges. Intentional false export declarations can trigger criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and referral to the Korea Customs Service for wrongdoing. The extended limitation period for fraudulent tax evasion and undeclared foreign exchange receipts is up to ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a heavily liberalized but regulated foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions such as service fees, royalties, and ordinary trade settlements are effectively fully convertible and require only a direct report to a designated foreign exchange bank, not prior approval. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Chusik Hoesa, are free but subject to mandatory notification and reporting under the FETA and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). Foreign capital can be freely injected via a Foreign Investment Capital Deposit Account, but the initial notification must be filed prior or contemporaneous with the deposit. Profit remittances, dividends, and proceeds from the sale of equity are permissible without conversion restrictions provided that the originating bank confirms the source funds and the tax status (i.e., dividend withholding under the Korea–foreign tax treaty). A significant hurdle is the requirement to provide a bank certificate of the inward remittance and a tax clearance certificate from the Korean tax office before repatriating large sums. Additionally, the KRW is not fully convertible outside Korea; offshore trading of the won is heavily curbed, and all physical or financial exchange of KRW must clear through a domestic authorized bank. Thus, the system is not an Article VIII hard-CAPEX surrender regime but rather a transparent current-account free, capital-account managed model with strict documentary trails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current FETA and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Guidance (directives 8-2 and 8-3), there is no mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account at a designated foreign exchange bank. However, export proceeds must effectively be repatriated to Korea within the statutory settlement period, generally 6 months (180 days) from the date of the export declaration or the completion of the service, unless an extension is granted by the foreign exchange bank or the MOEF for justifiable trade credit reasons. Once repatriated, the exporter may choose to convert the receipts into KRW using market rates or hold them in foreign currency for future disbursements, subject to normal transaction reporting thresholds. For services rendered by a no-code website builder (ISIC 6201), the service export receipt must be settled through a Korean foreign exchange bank with a declaration under the appropriate service revenue code (e.g., 'Computer and Information Services' or 'Software Development Royalties'), and the bank will verify the underlying contract to classify the receipt as a current account item.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting jointly under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA); the BOK supervises designated foreign exchange banks and the MOEF issues foreign exchange policy, risk management guidelines, and capital account transaction regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as a primary treasury or liquidity clearing venue for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under Korean banking law, only institutions designated as foreign exchange banks by the MOEF/BOK are permitted to provide foreign exchange settlement, KRW/FCY conversion, and cross-border remittance services. Payment facilitators such as Stripe and Paddle may be used as inbound sales collection agents for global SaaS subscriptions, but they must either obtain a domestic e-payment pre-approval (Form 1 under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act) or partner with a Korean PG and a licensed bank to settle KRW proceeds into the company's domestic account. In practice, an offshore EMI cannot open a local KRW-based corporate account on behalf of the Chusik Hoesa; it can only debit/credit the funds through an underlying correspondent arrangement with a Korean bank. Therefore, the feasible model is: Stripe/Paddle collect client card payments in foreign currencies, then remit the net settlement to the company's foreign currency account at a Korean bank, or, if configured for local card acquiring, they route via a licensed domestic acquire and deposit KRW. The treasury blueprint should treat EMIs as a payment rail, never as a bank account holder.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as a primary treasury or liquidity clearing venue for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 (5 billion Korean Won) of aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions with foreign related parties in a fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, submission of the international related-party transaction report, and preparation of the Local File; Master File preparation must also be assessed against the global group revenue thresholds under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the substance-over-form doctrine and the anti-abuse and beneficial ownership standards under the Corporate Tax Act and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. A Korean entity is not automatically subject to Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but any Core Income-Generating Activities, strategic decision-making, personnel, physical presence, financial risk, and intangible development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation functions must be adequately performed in the jurisdiction claiming the income; shell structures inserted solely to obtain treaty benefits or to dilute Korean taxable profit will be recharacterized or disregarded.

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Tax Filing

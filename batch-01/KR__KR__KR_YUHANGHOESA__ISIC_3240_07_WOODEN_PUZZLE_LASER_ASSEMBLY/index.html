Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, each member's contribution must be fully paid or the in-kind contribution fully delivered before incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked pre-registration escrow account opened in the provisional name of the company; the bank holds the funds and may release them only against the court-issued certificate of incorporation or on lawful abandonment of the formation. For a typical initial capital of KRW 10,000,000, the total official registry fees, registration tax, local education tax and court fee approximate USD 45 at current exchange rates.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, accessible via the Home Tax Service (HTS) electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a domestic tax representative (Korean tax agent) if it has no fixed place of business in Korea. The representative or the entity must submit an application for business registration (사업자등록신청) to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the place of business or the representative's address. Supporting documents include the certificate of foreign corporate registration or equivalent constitutional documents, articles of incorporation, proof of the domestic place of business or agent appointment, and identification documents of the representative. The NTS issues a Business Registration Number (사업자등록번호), which serves as both the Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the VAT registration number for a business. The registration must be completed before commencing taxable business activities, and modifications to business details must be reported to the district tax office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general mandatory registration threshold exists; any person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea in the course of business, regardless of turnover, must register for VAT. For small businesses meeting simplified taxation requirements, special rules apply, but registration is still mandatory once supplies commence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is applied to the export of goods under Article 21 of the former VAT Act and the current Value-Added Tax Act, where goods are delivered to a foreign buyer and removed from Korea, or supplied to ships/aircraft engaged in international navigation. Qualifying cross-border services include those performed or used outside Korea, certain international transportation services, and other services explicitly classified as exported services. The specific supply of laser-cut 3D wooden puzzles qualifies as a zero-rated export of goods when the goods are physically exported by a Korean seller under an export customs declaration and title passes to a foreign customer. Sales via online marketplaces or payment gateways must still be supported by formal export customs clearance documents unless the goods are supplied through an overseas fulfillment center where the export movement is evidenced by customs reports. Offshore digital services provided by a Korean supplier to a non-resident may also qualify as zero-rated foreign services if the place of supply is outside Korea and the consideration is received from a non-resident, subject to documentary evidence requirements.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (수출신고필증 / Export Declaration Certificate) evidencing the completed customs clearance from Korea
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) showing the Korean seller as shipper and the foreign buyer as consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list in the name of the Korean exporting entity
  • Evidence of consideration received from abroad, including bank remittance documentation, overseas payment settlement statements, or international credit card settlement records
  • Foreign exchange earnings certificate or foreign exchange transaction report issued by a foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, showing receipt of export proceeds within the statutory period
  • Contract or purchase order from the overseas buyer evidencing the commercial terms and destination
  • Shipping manifest, container tracking report, or delivery confirmation for goods moved through an overseas fulfillment center or customs-bonded warehouse

All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) through the NTS Home Tax Service, either via the web portal, a dedicated API (e-invoice integration), or NTS-certified ERP/personalized billing programs. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS by the end of the day following the tax invoice issue date. There is no pre-clearance or real-time fiscal stamping model for standard domestic sales, but the NTS operates real-time electronic transaction reporting (현금영수증 의무 발급) for cash receipts and for transactions with end-consumers; credit card transaction data and cash receipts are reported to the NTS by approved payment intermediaries. Anti-fraud measures rely on mandatory electronic issuance, automated cross-matching of purchase and sales invoices, and e-invoice data validation through the Home Tax Service APIs.

The Korean tax authority may scrutinize export documentation under the framework of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act. If zero-rated export transactions are found to be fictitious, unsupported by physical export proof, or lacking repatriation of foreign exchange within the mandatory period without a valid extension, the NTS will retroactively reverse the zero-rating, impose the standard 10% VAT plus an additional tax of 10% of the unpaid tax for non-reporting, and apply penalty taxes up to 40% of the unpaid amount for unjustified underreporting or fraudulent claims. Officers or entities involved in fraudulent export documentation may be subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment up to three years or a fine not exceeding three times the evaded tax under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act. The NTS may also impose the export bond-related customs enforcement and withholding penalties where foreign exchange obligations are violated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalised current account system but maintains a managed/report-based capital account. For an ordinary trade and manufacturing Yuhan Hoesa, cross-border trade payments, service fees, royalties and routine dividend remittances are freely executable through a licensed foreign exchange bank once the underlying transaction is substantiated, reflecting current-account convertibility for visible and invisible trade. Capital-account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment, external equity injections, inter-company loans and cross-border derivatives, are subject to pre-notification or filing with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Bank of Korea under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The KRW is not fully offshore-convertible; offshore non-deliverable KRW positions are restricted and physical KRW cannot be freely transferred across the border beyond the statutory declaration threshold. For non-resident owners, the main remittance hurdle is evidencing the foreign-investment notification and local tax clearance; once the operation is properly registered, profit remission is permissible after dividend resolution, audit/tax filing and bank verification. No mandatory expatriation of export receipts applies, but statistical reporting and collection-period obligations still apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea does not require exporters to surrender foreign-currency export receipts to the central bank. Exporters may deposit export proceeds in licensed foreign-currency accounts or convert them to KRW at a domestic bank. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received within two years from the date of the export declaration unless an extension is granted; the handling bank must be notified of each receipt and settlement. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio, and the exporter is free to match USD or KRW liquidity needs with forwards or spot conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; domestic foreign exchange banks are delegated the day-to-day verification, reporting and settlement execution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally constrained. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Wise Business and Airwallex are not licensed commercial banks in South Korea and cannot open a domestic KRW account for a Yuhan Hoesa or legally process FX transactions for a Korean resident treasury without violating the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Korean money transfer and payment services must be registered with the Financial Services Commission and related authorities. An EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection rail when settlement is ultimately cleared through a licensed Korean bank, but it cannot serve as the primary account for capital contributions, trade payments, corporate disbursements or FX hedging. The treasury relationship must therefore sit with a tier-1 Korean commercial bank.

Korean money transfer and payment services must be registered with the Financial Services Commission and related authorities. An EMI may be used only as a supplementary collection rail when settlement is ultimately cleared through a licensed Korean bank, but it cannot serve as the primary account for capital contributions, trade payments, corporate disbursements or FX hedging. The treasury relationship must therefore sit with a tier-1 Korean commercial bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal corporate income tax rate under the Corporate Tax Act of Korea (Article 55), applied to taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion. The progressive national CIT schedule for fiscal years beginning 2023 onwards is: 9% on the first KRW 200 million of taxable income; 19% on the tranche from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on the tranche from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; and 24% on income above KRW 300 billion. A local income tax surcharge of 10% is added to the national CIT liability, producing a combined headline marginal rate of 26.4% and a combined rate of 9.9% on the lowest bracket.

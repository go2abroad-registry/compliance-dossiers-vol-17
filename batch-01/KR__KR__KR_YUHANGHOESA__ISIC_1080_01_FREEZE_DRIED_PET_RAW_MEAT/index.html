Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, administered through the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

        
E-government filing portal
For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) owned partly or wholly by a foreign non-resident, registration begins after the company obtains its legal corporate existence under the Korean Commercial Act. The company must file a business registration application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its registered domestic place of business, or through the HomeTax portal, within 20 days of commencing business. The supporting dossier must include: the articles of incorporation, a foreign company registration certificate or notarized equivalent proving the foreign shareholders, the lease or ownership certificate for the Korean business address, a domestic bank account statement, identification and residency certificates for each representative director or resident agent, and a written power of attorney appointing a tax representative if no domestic place of business can be established. NTS then issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the company's TIN and VAT registration number. The company must thereafter register its corporate income tax obligations and VAT obligations; foreign non-resident shareholders receiving dividends or retained remuneration are separately covered by a Korean withholding registration through the same NTS tax number. A foreign corporation without a Korean place of business that is nonetheless carrying on a VAT taxable business in Korea must obtain a foreigner's business registration number through a Korean tax representative and the same HomeTax channel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation. A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic Korean corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Its worldwide income is within Korean taxing jurisdiction, and it is a VAT taxpayer under the Value-Added Tax Act once it engages in taxable supplies. No separate sub-national entity classification distinction applies in a unitary Korean jurisdiction.

        
Available election forms
None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a check-the-box election or an elective hybrid entity classification regime. The company's status as a Korean domestic corporation is fixed by the Korean Commercial Act; no tax election can recharacterize the vehicle as a flow-through or transparent entity for Korean corporate tax and VAT purposes.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
There is no monetary turnover threshold for the obligation to obtain a business registration number; every person supplying taxable goods or services must register with the NTS through HomeTax within 20 days of commencing business. An individual sole proprietor whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed KRW 80,000,000 may elect simplified taxable person status, but the simplified status does not remove the business registration obligation.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, supplies of goods exported from the Republic of Korea to a foreign country are zero-rated at 0% VAT. For freeze-dried pet food and pet raw meat products falling under ISIC 1080, the company must prove that title to the goods passed to a foreign buyer, the goods were physically delivered outside Korea, and a Korea Customs Service export declaration was completed. The export must be evidenced by the KCS UNI-PASS export declaration and a corresponding overseas transport document. Qualifying services that are directly connected with exported goods and paid in foreign currency may also qualify, but the core manufacturing business must rely on goods export evidence. The zero-rated sale must be reported as zero-rated output VAT in the VAT return and on the electronic tax invoice, with the export declaration reference number entered in the HomeTax e-invoice record.

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) system under the Value-Added Tax Act for all general VAT taxpayers. The electronic tax invoice must be transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system by the end of the next day after the invoice is issued, using the approved HomeTax API or an NTS-certified ERP adapter. Korea does not operate a government pre-clearance or digital stamp clearance model comparable to Latin American fiscal control; instead, the NTS uses real-time post-issuance validation, automated matching against VAT returns and corporate income tax filings, and anti-fraud analytics to detect missing invoices. Mandatory cash receipts and credit or debit card payment data reporting provide additional transaction-level visibility.

      
NTS and Korea Customs Service audits may reassess a claimed export zero-rating retroactively if the customs declaration, transport evidence, or foreign exchange receipt is missing, falsified, or not collected within the statutory period. The normal statute of limitations for a VAT assessment is 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraudulent evasion. Where zero-rating is overturned, NTS will assess the 10% VAT on the domestic supply, impose statutory additional tax under the Value-Added Tax Act for underpayment or failure to issue proper tax invoices, charge monthly late-payment interest, and may challenge the export if foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated by the 180-day foreign exchange settlement deadline. If the false zero-rating is accompanied by intentional documentary fraud, the matter is referred under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, which exposes the responsible officers to criminal fines and imprisonment, and the refund claim is clawed back with a 40% unjustifiable refund penalty.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Korean won is fully convertible on current account transactions, including routine trade settlement and service payments, subject to reporting and supporting documentation under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital account transactions are restricted and require prior approval or report to the Bank of Korea via an authorised foreign exchange bank, particularly for foreign direct investment, overseas borrowing, and loans between related parties. Dividend and profit remittances for foreign investors are generally free once audited retained earnings and corporate tax liabilities are verified, but banks require a tax payment certificate, audited financial statements, and BOK FDI registration proof. For an LLC manufacturing freeze-dried pet raw meat, export proceeds from overseas distributors must be collected through a Korean authorised foreign exchange bank and reported to the FEIS; there is no current statutory surrender ratio forcing conversion into KRW, meaning exporters may hold eligible foreign currency in a designated account, but domestic operating expenses are normally paid in KRW so treasury policy should plan for conversion drag. Temporary liquidity constraints can arise if export invoices are matched late or forward invoices do not have confirmed underlying trade documentation, because banks treat unsubstantiated currency conversions as speculative and may reject or delay them. Overall, the regime is liberal on operational trade flows but compliance-intensive for capital injections and shareholder loans, demanding a pre-approved FX compliance framework before funding.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export proceeds for goods must be collected within 15 months of the export declaration date through an authorised foreign exchange bank in Korea, unless a valid extension is granted by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no mandatory percentage surrender to KRW under current regulations; exporters may retain eligible foreign exchange receipts in a designated foreign currency account. The receipt must be reported to the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) with the export declaration number, invoice details, and underlying trade contract, and the exporting entity must provide proof of actual receipt before using the funds for settlement, transfers, or dividend remittance.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), exercising delegated authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) operated in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from providing Korean domestic currency accounts or corporate banking services to a domestic LLC unless they hold a Korean electronic financial business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and public notification to the Financial Supervisory Service. No major licensed cross-border EMI currently offers a viable standalone treasury solution for a manufacturing entity with inbound FDI and outbound trade flows. The entity may use an international payment platform merely as a client-facing collection channel if settlement is ultimately channelled through an authorised Korean foreign exchange bank, but doing so creates reconciliation, VAT, and AML shadow-account risks. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should rely on licensed Tier-1 domestic banks for all primary clearing, with EMI routes rejected as a substitute.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
27% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surcharge on the national CIT, producing a combined 29.7% top marginal rate

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
20% national dividend withholding tax plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the withheld amount, combined 22%, on dividends paid to non-residents

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 annual value of intercompany transactions with foreign related parties triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation and submission through the international transaction statement

      
Economic substance scope: South Korea has no standalone offshore-style ESR regime, but the NTS applies substance-over-form, OECD BEPS principal purpose tests, beneficial ownership standards, CFC rules, and anti-conduit doctrines to deny treaty benefits where the Korean freeze-dried pet food manufacturing entity lacks Core Income-Generating Activities such as real operational employees, production premises, and decision-making autonomy.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion by Underreporting and False Bookkeeping
        
Statutory sanction: Article 109 of the Corporate Tax Act: imprisonment for up to 3 years and fines up to 3 times the evaded tax; Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3 imposes an additional 40% unfair underreporting penalty.

        
Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing under Commercial Act Articles 321 and 401; the NTS may seize personal assets, blacklist the director as a delinquent taxpayer, impose an overseas travel ban, and pursue criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Non-Arm's-Length Cross-Border Payments
        
Statutory sanction: Framework Act on International Tax Coordination Article 4 authorizes arm's-length re-determination; failure to maintain and submit TP files triggers separate monetary fines, plus negligent or intentional underreporting penalties of 10% to 40% of the assessed additional tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false TP disclosures are jointly liable for the reassessed CIT, surcharges and penalties; the NTS may place directors on the travel-ban blacklist and bring criminal fraud charges if the adjustment is part of a deliberate evasion scheme.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Treaty Abuse in Profit Repatriation
        
Statutory sanction: Restriction of Special Taxation Act Article 73 allows denial of treaty benefits; reassessment is made at the statutory 20% national WHT plus 2% local surtax; withholding agents face liability under Corporate Tax Act Article 12 and criminal penalties under Article 109.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and effective owners face veil-piercing civil liability for unpaid withholding taxes, personal asset restitution, tax-delinquent blacklisting with overseas travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 3 years for fraudulent structuring.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr) and the EDI customs-trade linkage systems. Foreign non-resident entities file through the competent district tax office or the Seoul National Tax Service International Tax Affairs Division, using the NTS HomeTax online filing gateway and Tax Call Center 126.

E-government filing portal

For the Chusik Hoesa (Stock Corporation) forming the Offshore Development Center, the domestic corporate TIN/VAT ID is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (Saupja Deungnok Beonho) issued by the district tax office after formal corporation registration with the court registry. The application must be submitted within 20 days of business commencement using the NTS Business Registration Application (Saupja Deungnok Shincheongseo), accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, court registration certificate, office lease documentation, identity documents of the representative, and any applicable foreign investment report. For a non-resident foreign entity that is not a domestic corporation, the foreign enterprise must appoint a tax representative in Korea with a domestic place of business, submit the same Business Registration Application, attach apostilled or consular-legalized foreign registration certificates, a power of attorney and certificate of tax representative appointment, and evidence of a place of business in Korea; upon approval, the NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate which constitutes the unified corporate TIN and VAT registration.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections for a Chusik Hoesa. The entity is mandatorily treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporation tax on worldwide income; no passthrough election is available to a limited stock corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based exemption for legal persons; VAT registration is mandatory upon business commencement within 20 days. A separate simplified VAT filing threshold of KRW 80,000,000 in annual taxable supplies applies to eligible small businesses but does not suspend or eliminate VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the Korean VAT Act and Enforcement Decree for goods physically exported and for services exported in exchange for foreign currency. For the ISIC 6202 offshore software engineering and maintenance hub, zero-rating is substantiated when the software development, support, or maintenance services are supplied to a foreign principal, are actually performed and consumed outside Korea or are confirmed as offshore services falling within the statutory zero-rated foreign-currency-earning service categories, and the foreign currency consideration is received from abroad in Korea. The supplier must be a registered Korean entity, must issue an electronic tax invoice marked as zero-rated export supply, and must maintain contemporaneous evidence that the recipient is a non-resident, the services are rendered offshore, and the proceeds are repatriated to Korea.

  • Electronic Export Customs Declaration (EDI Export Bill of Entry) where physical goods, media, or tangible deliverables are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill if physical goods are shipped from Korea
  • Signed Offshore Software Development Agreement and Statement of Work identifying the foreign contracting party
  • Service completion report, acceptance certificate, or deliverable handover sign-off evidencing offshore performance
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated electronic tax invoice (e-Ssuin) distinctly marked as zero-rated export supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Certificate of Foreign Exchange Receipt issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank showing receipt of the foreign currency consideration

Korean VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Ssuin) through HomeTax and transmit them to the NTS by the day following issuance. The NTS performs near real-time electronic clearance and cross-validation of e-invoicing against VAT returns through EDI/API connectivity for enterprise taxpayers; there is no government-issued physical anti-fraud stamp or pre-clearance stamping requirement, but delayed, omitted, or mismatched e-invoices trigger automated NTS audit flags, penalty assessments, and cases are sent to the risk-analytics system for compliance review.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-check export declarations, e-invoices, service contracts, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If a zero-rated export claim is not supported by the required documentation, NTS reclassifies the supply as taxable domestic supply and assesses the 10% VAT not paid, together with an under-reporting penalty of 10% for negligent underpayment and up to 40% for fraudulent or unjustified omission under the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus daily late-payment surcharges at the statutory rate until payment is made. Any previously claimed VAT refund is clawed back with interest, deliberate fabrication of export-support documents may result in criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the entity may be disqualified from future zero-rating and targeted for intensive export VAT auditing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current account and a largely liberalized capital account with residual administrative screening. The Korean won is freely convertible for underlying trade, services, and dividend remittance once the bank reporting requirements are met. However, capital account inflows and outflows require Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIIN) reporting, statement of purpose, and in specific cases pre-registration or approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted after the completion of external audit and National Tax Service clearance on corporate income tax and withholding tax; the remitting bank will verify the audited financial statements, board resolution on dividends, shareholder register, and the applicable withholding tax payment. There is no mandatory export proceeds surrender ratio; exporters may keep hard currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts or convert to Korean won. The deep interbank spot and forward liquidity of the KRW/USD market, alongside the availability of commercial cross-currency swap lines, makes the Korean won one of the most efficiently routed Asian currencies for a structured offshore software engineering entity. Yet the strict FEIIN declaration timeline and the physical presence expectations for senior officers remain the most frequent treasury onboarding friction points. Therefore the entity should maintain all capital and operational documentation in a continuous compliance file accessible to the Korean bank relationship manager.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export performance (service delivery or software milestone acceptance). No fixed surrender percentage applies currently; proceeds may either be retained in a domestic foreign-currency account or exchanged into Korean won. All receipts need to be routed through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported via the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIIN) by the statutory deadline (typically the 25th day of the following month). Overdue receivables require a written extension application indicating justifiable cause and a proposed collection schedule.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) / Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only Korean-licensed foreign exchange banks can legally complete statutory FX transactions and capital receipts. Offshore FinTech/EMI platforms such as Wise for Business and Payoneer are not licensed as local banks and therefore cannot be used for statutory capital injection, capital verification, or the primary treasury clearing rail for the ODC entity. They may only be used as an auxiliary collection gateway for open-account service invoices from non-Korean clients, and even then the underlying receivable should still be settled into a Korean bank account when timing of tax benefits or transfer pricing is required. For a Payment Institution license, domestic fintechs (for example Tosspayments or Naver Pay) can handle local merchant acquiring but not cross-border treasury settlement for a non-resident owned IT services company without partnering with a Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the overseas parent to the designated Korean Tier-1 bank's foreign currency account opened in the ODC's name. The remittance narrative must show the FEIIN purpose code for corporate capital acquisition or equity injection and the reference to the paid-in capital registration. Upon receipt, the bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase or receipt certificate, convert the funds into KRW when required, and file the FEIIN report. For statutory capital verification, the company must complete the capital registration with the court registry office and deliver the bank-issued certificate of capital reservation together with the board resolution and the foreign investor's identity documents.For operational trade settlements, the entity will use: (1) SWIFT MT103 for monthly open-account service invoices owed by the overseas parent or end-client; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit for hardware or software license purchases requiring a payment undertaking; and (3) documentary collections for milestone-based deliverables with contractual bill-of-lading or acceptance certificate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% statutory top national corporate income tax rate for taxable income above KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surtax on national CIT, producing a 26.4% maximum combined effective corporate tax rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% composite rate on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders: 20% national corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax, unless a bilateral income tax treaty reduces the rate to a lower percentage such as 5%, 10%, or 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The statutory threshold for mandatory Local File preparation and transfer pricing disclosure in Korea is KRW 10 billion of cross-border intercompany transactions per business year; full OECD-aligned Master File and Country-by-Country reporting are required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations by name, but substance-over-form, place-of-effective-management, and beneficial ownership rules apply. A Korean offshore development center must maintain real staff, office premises, engineering contracts, payroll records, board minutes, and day-to-day management in Korea. Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Korea to qualify for treaty benefits and to avoid having the Chusik Hoesa disregarded as a tax shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 imposes imprisonment of up to three years or a fine not exceeding twice the amount of tax evaded; where the evaded corporate tax exceeds KRW 300 million, imprisonment is three to five years with a fine up to three times the tax evaded. LCITA Article 4 authorizes the Korean tax office to re-characterize the transaction and disregard the shell entity.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced. Directors are jointly and severally liable for damages under Commercial Act Article 401. National Tax Collection Act Article 39 permits collection of unpaid corporate tax directly from the director's personal assets where default is intentional. Directors are blacklisted as habitual tax delinquents, placed on overseas travel restriction registries, and may be sentenced to criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under LCITA Article 4, the tax authority adjusts taxable income to the arm's length result. National Tax Basic Act Article 47-2 applies an additional tax of 10% of underpaid tax for unintentional under-reporting and 40% for intentional under-reporting. LCITA Article 11 imposes a documentation failure penalty of up to 2% of the related-party transaction amount, plus statutory daily overdue interest on the deficiency.

Director personal liability: A director who approves or signs false pricing documentation and cost-plus calculations is exposed to criminal prosecution under Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, civil asset restitution under Commercial Act Article 401, and personal collection of additional tax under National Tax Collection Act Article 39. Directors may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving Korea until the tax liability is settled.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Article 76-3 and Local Tax Act Article 103-20 require the payer to remit the unwithheld national and local income tax plus a 10% penalty. LCITA Article 4 denies treaty relief for abusive structures and re-imposes the full 22% statutory rate, with additional tax and penalties for under-reporting. If the false treaty documentation is intentional, criminal sanctions under Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors act as withholding agents and are personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax, local surtax, and penalties. Their personal assets may be seized, they are blacklisted as delinquent taxpayers, travel bans are issued, and they face criminal imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years when the tax evaded exceeds KRW 300 million.

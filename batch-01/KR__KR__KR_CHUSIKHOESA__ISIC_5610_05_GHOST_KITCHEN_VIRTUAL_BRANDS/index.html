A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered office for a delivery-only ghost kitchen or virtual brand operator in South Korea. The National Tax Service requires a physical place of business for business registration and value-added tax purposes, and the local district office and municipal food-safety authorities require an actual licensed kitchen or food-preparation facility for the mandatory food service business license. None. The Republic of Korea imposes no statutory minimum stated capital and no permanent blocked-escrow requirement for a Chusik Hoesa. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Customs export filings are separately processed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Chusik Hoesa, the company's representative or appointed tax agent must apply for a Business Registration Number from the district tax office of the NTS with jurisdiction over the registered head office before commencing business, and no later than 20 days after commencement. The application is filed electronically through HomeTax's 'Business Registration' service using the certificate of incorporation under the Commercial Act, the corporate registration certificate, the head-office lease or ownership evidence, and representative identification. A foreign non-resident entity that has no domestic office but makes taxable supplies in Korea, including electronic services, must appoint a tax representative and obtain a Business Registration Number by filing a non-resident business registration application through HomeTax; the non-resident VAT returns must be filed through that tax representative. The Business Registration Number serves as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Korea; there is no statutory turnover threshold before registration is required. A simplified taxable person regime exists for eligible individual businesses with annual turnover of KRW 80 million or less, but it does not waive registration and is not available to a Chusik Hoesa corporate taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for 0% VAT when the goods are actually exported from Korea under an Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service, title and risk pass to a foreign buyer, and export proceeds are collected in foreign currency or through an NTS- and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act-approved settlement method. Qualifying offshore digital services and other services are zero-rated only when supplied to a non-resident customer, the service is actually used outside Korea, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency or through an approved Korean foreign-exchange settlement account. Domestic food delivery sales in Korea, including orders fulfilled through international platform payment rails, are deemed domestic consumption and remain taxable at 10%.

  • Electronic Export Declaration and export declaration receipt issued by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air)
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate or foreign-currency receipt confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Sales contract or service agreement evidencing the foreign counterparty and place of consumption
  • Documentary evidence of offshore service use or digital-content access outside Korea, where zero-rating is claimed for offshore services

Mandatory electronic tax invoicing through HomeTax. VAT-registered businesses must issue an Electronic Tax Invoice for every B2B supply and transmit the invoice data to the NTS within one day after issuance; approved e-invoice service providers integrate through NTS APIs. There is no additional physical fiscal stamp or continuous real-time transaction clearance, but the NTS performs digital cross-checking and pre-issue validation. For B2C restaurant delivery sales, card settlement data is transmitted to the NTS through approved card acquirers, and cash receipts must be issued through the HomeTax cash receipt system.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service will verify export declarations against customs clearance records, shipping documents, bank receipts, and foreign-exchange settlement records. If zero-rating is applied fraudulently or without complete substantiation, the refunded VAT is clawed back with additional tax and interest under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes; false or fraudulent tax invoices and fake export schemes are subject to enhanced penalty taxes, criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and personal liability of directors. Exporters must retain all supporting documentation for at least five years from the relevant filing deadline and must settle export proceeds within 180 days; failure to repatriate within the statutory deadline may cause the export zero-rating to be revoked.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-float and de jure convertible currency regime for current account transactions, but maintains a regulated capital account framework. The KRW is freely convertible for trade-related payments, service transactions, dividends, and profit remittances, provided the underlying activity is documented and filed with the designated foreign exchange bank. Non-resident equity injections into a Chusik Hoesa are treated as foreign direct investment and require a reporting notice via the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or a delegated foreign exchange bank, after which the capital may be brought in as foreign exchange or KRW. Capital account items such as foreign currency loans, overseas deposits, and portfolio flows are still subject to a registration and reporting regime, and the Bank of Korea can impose temporary stabilization measures during extraordinary market volatility. Profit remittance to overseas shareholders necessitates a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and evidence of the board resolution declaring dividends. There are no mandatory surrender requirements today, as the prior surrender system was abolished; exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in designated foreign exchange accounts. However, all receipts must be properly credited under the correct transaction purpose code and supported by underlying trade or service contracts to avoid anti-money laundering scrutiny. The practical concreteness of this convertibility implies that a treasury operation should pre-clear large transfers with the relationship bank and maintain complete primary documentation for every inward and outward flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the ghost kitchen's online sales or third-party marketplace payouts must be received by the Korean entity within five years from the export declaration acceptance date as per Article 9 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; the foreign currency can be held in an authorized foreign exchange account at a domestic bank. The proceeds must be collected via an authorized foreign exchange bank or a permitted payment settlement institution, and the underlying export declaration must be filed with the Korea Customs Service.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and subordinate Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI providers and virtual account platforms carry Low feasibility for a Korean Chusik Hoesa. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, any institution receiving, transmitting, or settling payment instructions in Korea must be licensed by the Financial Services Commission or operate as a registered electronic financial business. Most neobanking app providers from outside Korea do not hold a domestic e-money license and therefore cannot legally provide a primary account or issuance of KRW-denominated transaction accounts. They may help the entity connect to domestic payment gateways such as Inicis, KG Inicis, or Toss Payments, but these act as merchants' acquiring processors rather than full banking counterparts. The corporate treasury should rely exclusively on a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for the core capital and disbursement accounts, and can use an overseas EMI only for non-Korean side accumulation if such EMI is licensed in the counterparty jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial shareholder capital or later equity injections from the non-resident parent must follow the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) reporting route. The Chusik Hoesa's foreign investor files a pre-funding FDI notification with KOTRA or a delegated domestic bank, and the bank then issues a Certificate of Foreign Direct Investment Registration. After the notification, the inbound wire travels via SWIFT MT103 through a designated foreign exchange bank; the purpose code should identify equity investment in a domestic corporation. The bank will perform a source-of-funds review and verify the investor's identity, board approval, and the issuance of new shares in the company register. Following receipt, the bank issues a certificate of inward remittance for capital verification and the company must file the subsequent post-investment report to KOTRA within 30 days, including the investment amount and share details. All documents must be kept in the corporate treasury file for a minimum of five years.The principal clearing rail for outbound operational disbursements in the ghost kitchen structure is the SWIFT MT103 wire for cross-border payments such as procurement of imported ingredients, software subscriptions, insurance, digital licensing fees, and royalties. For larger equipment acquisitions or packaging supplies, a commercial letter of credit (LC) is advisable, as Korean banks will prioritize an LC when the beneficiary is a Chinese or Southeast Asian supplier requiring payment security upon presentation of transport documents. Documentary collections such as D/P and D/A may be used for low-complexity, repeated disbursements to trusted counterparties; all trade-associated documents must be retained due to Korea's trade declaration obligations. For domestic disbursements and payroll of Korean kitchen staff, the entity will use the KRW current account with local interbank clearing via the Hong Kong Interbank Clearing system analogous, only for Korean institutions through the Bank of Korea's Payment and Settlement System. The treasury should align each outward transfer with the underlying commercial contract and confirm the applicable foreign exchange purpose code on every instruction.

They may help the entity connect to domestic payment gateways such as Inicis, KG Inicis, or Toss Payments, but these act as merchants' acquiring processors rather than full banking counterparts. The corporate treasury should rely exclusively on a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for the core capital and disbursement accounts, and can use an overseas EMI only for non-Korean side accumulation if such EMI is licensed in the counterparty jurisdiction.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal corporate income tax rate (progressive: 9% on tax base up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 24% above KRW 20 billion)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% statutory combined withholding on outbound dividends to non-residents (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge); no withholding is required on distributions to another Korean domestic corporation but the full 22% applies to non-resident shareholders absent a treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File / Master File preparation; additional group-revenue thresholds apply for the Custody-by-Country Report and integrated tax report under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs and its Enforcement Decree.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply an UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the National Tax Service applies a rigorous economic substance and substance-over-form doctrine; a Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea, including actual kitchen staff, delivery-platform contracting, inventory management, order processing, accounting records, and senior management decisions; if the entity before being a bare invoicing shell or conduit, the NTS may re-characterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and attribute the income to the real foreign principal under Korean tax law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement of Ghost-Kitchen Revenue

Statutory sanction: 40% unfair tax penalty under Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, plus daily late-payment interest; criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment up to three years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax amount).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax where corporate assets are insufficient and the insufficiency results from intentional or grossly negligent director conduct; assets may be seized and directors may be placed on a tax-delinquent blacklist with a travel ban preventing overseas departure; a criminal conviction can result in imprisonment, restitution orders, and disqualification from serving as directors.
International Withholding Tax and DTA Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is jointly liable for the un-withheld tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Corporate Tax Act, plus a 10% penalty for unfaithful reporting and 40% for fraudulent non-withholding; interest accrues from the original payment date; the NTS may disallow the deduction for the underlying royalty/interest payment.

Director personal liability: Representative directors who knowingly fail to withhold are personally liable for the full withheld amount, penalties and interest; the director may be blacklisted with a departure restriction for delinquent taxes exceeding statutory thresholds; intentional evasion triggers criminal referral, with imprisonment up to three years and civil asset restitution for dividends improperly distributed.
Shell Corporation and Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance with No Real Substance

Statutory sanction: The NTS may re-characterize the arrangement under economic substance doctrine, adjust taxable income to the arm's length result, impose a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty, and deny treaty benefits; failure to prepare and submit transfer pricing documentation may trigger separate documentation penalties under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

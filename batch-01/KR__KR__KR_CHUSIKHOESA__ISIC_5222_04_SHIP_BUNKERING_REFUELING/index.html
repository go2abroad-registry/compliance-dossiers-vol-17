Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a broadly open current-account regime for trade settlements, but capital and financial account operations remain subject to filing, licensing, or notification requirements. The Korean won is fully convertible for most genuine business transactions within the declared banking system, but all cross-border payments must flow through recognized foreign exchange banks and be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Export proceeds from marine bunkering sales must be receipted within the statutory trade settlement window (normally 90 days from shipment) and may be held in a domestic nominated foreign-currency account or converted to KRW at the onshore commercial bank exchange rate; mandatory surrender was effectively abolished in Korea, so the treasury may retain USD or other settlement currencies for future fueling purchases. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are lawful but subjected to a strict documentary trail: audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, undistributed earnings verification, and the underlying foreign exchange bank's trade settlement report. Because KRW is not fully deliverable offshore, the Chusik Hoesa must keep its payment, hedging, and settlement relationships inside the Korean banking system and should avoid unlicensed digital EMIs that lack the statutory foreign exchange bank designation needed for valid capital and trade settlement reporting.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export proceeds must be received within 90 days from the date of shipment under the BOK trade settlement rules; an extension of up to one additional 90-day period or longer is available only with documented justification and, where required, prior approval from the MOEF/BOK. There is no mandatory minimum conversion ratio currently in force, so proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or converted to KRW at a licensed foreign exchange bank; every receipt must be reported with its export declaration number in FEIS and supported by a matching customs export confirmation.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange policy and regulatory authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; enforcement is delegated to the Korea Customs Service for documentary reporting and to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit for AML surveillance.

For ship bunkering, the Chusik Hoesa must demonstrate local capacity by owning or leasing storage tanks, retaining traders and operations staff, maintaining marine fuel supply licenses from the relevant Korean authorities, and making strategic decisions in Korea; otherwise the NTS can disregard the Korean entity as a conduit and deny treaty benefits under the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test.

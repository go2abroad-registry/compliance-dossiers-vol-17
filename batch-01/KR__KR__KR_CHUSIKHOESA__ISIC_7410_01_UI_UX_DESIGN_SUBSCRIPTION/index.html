Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Chusik Hoesa; therefore no blocked escrow account or mandatory capital deposit guarantee is legally required. However, the entire subscription price for all issued shares must be fully paid in before incorporation registration, either in cash deposited at a designated financial institution or through an approved in-kind contribution valuation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea, via the HomeTax electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service for export declarations and customs clearance data.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity engaged in taxable supplies in Korea must register with the National Tax Service by filing a business registration application through HomeTax or with the competent local tax office, appointing a domestic tax representative if no place of business exists in Korea. The applicant submits a certified copy of the foreign registration certificate (apostilled or consular-legalized as applicable), the entity's articles of incorporation or constitutional documents, a power of attorney evidencing the tax representative, and supporting identification documents. Upon completion, NTS assigns a 10-digit business registration number which also serves as the Korean VAT identification number, and issues the business registration certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity engaged in taxable supplies in Korea must register with the National Tax Service by filing a business registration application through HomeTax or with the competent local tax office, appointing a domestic tax representative if no place of business exists in Korea. The applicant submits a certified copy of the foreign registration certificate (apostilled or consular-legalized as applicable), the entity's articles of incorporation or constitutional documents, a power of attorney evidencing the tax representative, and supporting identification documents. Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not provide a check-the-box election or separate hybrid mismatch entity elections under the Korean Corporate Tax Act or VAT Act. A Chusik Hoesa is inherently treated as a Korean domestic corporation taxed at entity level, and foreign-incorporated entities are classified as foreign corporations unless the place of effective management or head office is in Korea.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general KRW registration threshold for foreign non-resident taxable suppliers; any taxable supply of goods or services in Korea triggers VAT registration. For domestic businesses, annual taxable turnover under KRW 80,000,000 may qualify for simplified taxable person status, but a Chusik Hoesa operating as a legal person is generally classified as a general taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, zero rating applies to exported goods and to services supplied to non-resident enterprises, including cross-border digital design and UI/UX subscription services. Services qualify when the recipient is a foreign non-resident entity, the place of supply is overseas, the service is actually used or reviewed outside Korea, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. A zero-rated electronic tax invoice must be issued and the exporter must retain all export, transport, service delivery, and foreign exchange receipt evidence to substantiate the claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by Korea Customs Service (export declaration/Bill of Entry equivalent for exported services supported by service contract and use certificate for goods zero-rating where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or equivalent transport documentary evidence for physical exports, plus proof of overseas delivery for digital service supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, or a bank-confirmed foreign exchange receipt statement evidencing actual collection of export proceeds in foreign currency

All general taxable persons must issue electronic tax invoices for every taxable supply and transmit them to NTS through HomeTax by the end of the business day following the supply date. Korea does not apply physical fiscal stamps; NTS performs near-real-time clearance and data matching against customs, payment card, and bank transaction records. ERP integration and API-based bulk submission via HomeTax are mandatory standards for high-volume taxpayers, and all tax invoices remain subject to NTS digital audit and reconciliation.

If zero-rating is claimed without actual export or foreign exchange receipt, NTS reverses the zero-rating, claws back any refunded input VAT, and applies additional VAT under the VAT Act: 40% for fraudulent or false invoicing and 20% for negligent under-reporting, plus interest. Criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may be pursued, carrying up to 3 years imprisonment or fines up to KRW 30 million. False or overstated export declarations also expose the entity to Korea Customs Service administrative penalties, revocation of export facilitation status, and retrospective reassessments throughout the statutory audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a legally open but carefully reported foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for virtually all current-account transactions, including trade-related design subscription fees, royalties, operating profits, software service payments and ordinary operating expenses. Such payments pass through designated foreign exchange banks without prior approval, subject to post-trade reporting requirements triggered by thresholds that vary by transaction type and delegated commercial-bank authority. Capital and financial account operations, however, are regulated: inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, loan drawdowns and capital repatriation must have a legal transaction basis, and in practice require either prior declaration to MOEF or the use of a designated foreign exchange bank that files the required Foreign Exchange Information System records. Dividends and profit remittances are fully permitted for a foreign-invested Chusik Hoesa, but the bank will demand certified audited financial statements, tax payment/deduction completion certificates, corporate authorization documents and evidence of the original foreign investment registration. Although the won floats freely and BOK does not impose surrender quotas, practical convertibility is conditional on clean KYC files, accurate purpose codes and full supporting documentation. Offshore investors holding KRW exposure generally find the onshore market liquid for USD, EUR and JPY, but exotic cross-currency corridors require pre-confirmed bank pricing and internal compliance approval. Overall, the KRW regime is considered open on the current account and administratively gatekept on the capital account, meaning high but not unconditional convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related delegated rules, an exporter of goods or services must collect export proceeds through a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea and receive those proceeds into a Korean bank account. The statutory collection period is generally six months from the date the export occurs, and exporters must obtain the bank's confirmation that the export settlement has been made. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under current Korean rules; proceeds may be retained in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account or converted to KRW at the market rate. Late collection without a justifiable reason can trigger penalty procedures and additional documentary scrutiny. Exceptions or extensions may be permitted by the bank where payment is delayed by third-party credit events, disputes, or other commercial grounds demonstrated with supporting evidence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank; Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as foreign-exchange administrative authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) as banking and AML supervisory enforcement agency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury and clearing rail. A non-resident fintech EMI such as Wise is not legally authorized in South Korea to hold corporate client funds as a domestic licensed bank or to provide a local KRW transactional current account to a Chusik Hoesa unless it obtains registration under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and a business relationship with a licensed Korean bank. Foreign EMIs may be used only as a supplementary non-KRW payment or collection channel where the local partner is FSS-registered and where the service does not involve capital escrow, FDI inflows, statutory profits, or corporate bank account opening. Therefore, this blueprint mandates a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all statutory treasury and capital account movements, and treats EMI rails as ancilliary low-value payment options only after separate legal validation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% top marginal rate on taxable income above KRW 300 billion; otherwise 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, and 24% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion under Corporate Tax Act Article 55

22% aggregate statutory rate composed of 20% national withholding tax under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 plus 2% local income tax surcharge; reduced rates may apply under a bilateral DTA

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10,000,000,000 in annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions within the calculation period is the statutory threshold requiring preparation and submission of Master File and Local File documentation under the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs; lower transactional values may nevertheless be audited and adjusted by the Korean National Tax Service

Economic substance scope: Korean domestic tax law does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary Korean limited companies, but the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Abuse Rules are embedded in Korean case law and in Article 121 of the Framework Act on Taxation; for DTA relief and withholding tax reductions, the Korean tax authorities require the non-resident payee to carry on Core Income-Generating Activities in its residence state, having its own office, employees, real decision-making authority, and economic risk; shell companies, letterbox entities, and conduits are disregarded and treated as non-beneficial owners, permitting the Korean authorities to deny treaty benefits and impose the full statutory withholding rate

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3: imprisonment up to 10 years or fine not exceeding five times the amount of evaded national tax; additional 40% fraudulent filing penalty, 20% under-reporting penalty, daily unpaid tax surcharge of 0.022%, and full local income tax surcharge liability

Director personal liability: Where the entity is used as a sham or the directors personally caused the evasion, the Korean tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax, local tax, penalties and surcharges; the directors may be blacklisted as dishonest taxpayers, subjected to travel suspension, have personal assets attached, and face criminal imprisonment up to 10 years
Transfer Pricing And Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Coordination of International Tax Affairs Articles 4 through 6, the Korean National Tax Service may adjust the intercompany transaction to an arm's length amount, disallow the excessive deduction, impose transfer pricing adjustment penalties and interest, and if the arrangement is fraudulent, convert excess payments into constructive dividends subject to 22% withholding tax

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately sign sham related-party agreements and route money through shell entities can be treated as receiving disguised dividends or embezzling corporate assets; Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere instrumentality and make directors personally liable for investor restitution, repatriation of diverted funds, the entire tax deficiency, travel bans, blacklisting, and imprisonment for tax evasion if the scheme is criminalized
Shell Company Abuse, AML And Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: The Real Name Financial Transaction Act requires disclosure of beneficial ownership, and violations may result in administrative fines, criminal fines, and penal consequences; the Korean tax authority can disregard the shell entity, tax the controlling non-resident on the Korean-source design subscription income, and deny all DTA benefits; the Framework Act on Taxation imposes joint liability for unpaid tax on the interventionist non-resident and the nominal Korean directors

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors are both at risk; the corporate veil can be pierced if the company is a façade, leading to personal civil liability for unpaid Korean taxes, restitution to creditors, disgorgement of payments made through payment facilitators, travel restrictions and blacklisting by the National Tax Service, and criminal imprisonment for participation in tax evasion or falsification of the corporate record, with a potential sentence of up to 10 years under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act

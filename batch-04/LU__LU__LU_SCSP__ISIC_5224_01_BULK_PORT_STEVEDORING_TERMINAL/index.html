Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), 14 rue Erasme, L-1468 Luxembourg; electronic filing via lbr.lu and MyGuichet.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 944-1 to 944-27 establishing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) as introduced by the law of 23 July 2016; law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; law of 11 January 2013 creating the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); Grand-Ducal regulation on the electronic filing and publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Formation / constitutional filings

Registration application Form C addressed to the RCS together with the fully executed partnership agreement (contrat de société), followed by the ex officio publication of the statutory extracts in the RESA electronic compendium.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal capital: the SCSp enjoys contractual freedom of capital and no escrow account is legally required. Partnership interests are subscribed and contributed according to the schedule in the partnership agreement; contributions must be made available to the partnership through a bank account opened with an EU/EEA or equivalent credit institution, and full liberation of subscribed interests is a condition for any distribution or repayment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) following inspection of the terminal, storage silos, conveyors and office premises; structural load and stability certification for quay cranes, portal cranes, silos and conveyor gantries issued by an approved inspection bureau (organisme agréé) attesting compliance with Eurocodes and the Luxembourg building regulations; occupational health and safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including registration of all lifting and cargo-handling machinery, appointment of a safety and health coordinator for the site, validation of the emergency-response plan, and issuance of the workplace operation clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum legal capital: the SCSp enjoys contractual freedom of capital and no escrow account is legally required. Partnership interests are subscribed and contributed according to the schedule in the partnership agreement; contributions must be made available to the partnership through a bank account opened with an EU/EEA or equivalent credit institution, and full liberation of subscribed interests is a condition for any distribution or repayment.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) (Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) (Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority) for VAT, registration duties, and indirect tax audits. Official portals: impotsdirects.public.lu, aedt.public.lu, guichet.lu, and lbr.lu (Luxembourg Business Registers).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SCSp partnership agreement with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through the eJustice/guichet.lu platform; RCS issuance of the 11-digit matricule (national business number) creates the base tax reference. Step 2: The AEDT links the matricule to the national identification number (NIN) used for direct tax filings; the fiscally transparent SCSp files annual information declarations only. Step 3: Submit the VAT registration application (form 645VA/TVA) to the AEDT; Luxembourg-resident entities apply directly while non-EU entities with a Luxembourg fixed establishment act through a Luxembourg fiscal representative; the AEDT issues a VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix (LU plus 8 digits) within approximately 15 working days. Step 4: Register with the Customs and Excise Agency (Administration des douanes et accises) for the EORI number and, where applicable, an AEO authorization required for export declaration processing. Step 5: Obtain the statutory port terminal concession (Port de Mertert) and the stevedoring operating licence, both prerequisites for lawful taxable activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Step 1: File the SCSp partnership agreement with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through the eJustice/guichet.lu platform; RCS issuance of the 11-digit matricule (national business number) creates the base tax reference. Step 2: The AEDT links the matricule to the national identification number (NIN) used for direct tax filings; the fiscally transparent SCSp files annual information declarations only. Step 3: Submit the VAT registration application (form 645VA/TVA) to the AEDT; Luxembourg-resident entities apply directly while non-EU entities with a Luxembourg fixed establishment act through a Luxembourg fiscal representative; the AEDT issues a VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix (LU plus 8 digits) within approximately 15 working days. Step 4: Register with the Customs and Excise Agency (Administration des douanes et accises) for the EORI number and, where applicable, an AEO authorization required for export declaration processing. Step 5: Obtain the statutory port terminal concession (Port de Mertert) and the stevedoring operating licence, both prerequisites for lawful taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is automatically fiscally transparent as a contractual partnership without legal personality; no national check-the-box election is required for pass-through treatment. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) an election to exercise a commercial activity, causing partnership profits to be treated as commercial income allocated and taxed at partner level; (ii) the ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch alignment under Art. 168ter LIR permitting symmetrical classification of the SCSp against a foreign partner's jurisdiction; (iii) the VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under the Luxembourg VAT Law to treat the SCSp and its Luxembourg taxable partners as a single taxable person; (iv) the limited-partner allocation rules which permit profit-allocation flexibility in the partnership agreement, subject to general anti-abuse principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-enterprise exemption under Art. 57(1)(c) of the Luxembourg VAT Law (raised from EUR 25,000 as from 1 January 2023). The exemption is unavailable where the taxpayer is a port stevedoring terminal operating under a statutory concession, claims deductible input VAT, performs intra-Community acquisitions, or receives reverse-charge services; foreign stevedores with a Luxembourg fixed establishment must register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: the 0% VAT rate applies to bulk cargo dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU where the terminal operator or exporter holds a validated customs export declaration and proof of physical exit from the EU customs territory. Services: stevedoring and cargo-handling services performed by the bulk terminal operator qualify for the credit-bearing exemption (zero rating) under the export provisions of the Luxembourg VAT Law implementing Art. 146(1)(e) of the VAT Directive only where the services are directly connected with goods being physically exported outside the EU or imported into the EU and placed under a suspensive customs procedure; written evidence of the non-EU destination, the customer's export status, and the direct link between the handling service and the outgoing international transport must be obtained at the time of supply. Intra-EU barge traffic on the Moselle and purely domestic stevedoring remain taxable at the 17% standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) validated and released by the competent Luxembourg/EU export customs office, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (sea waybill, CMR, or Moselle river waybill) evidencing carriage of the bulk cargo from the stevedoring terminal to a destination outside the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency within the 180-day repatriation deadline

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime is currently in force in Luxembourg. Invoices must comply with the VAT invoicing rules, be issued by the 15th day of the month following the supply, and be retained for 10 years. E-invoicing is mandatory for invoices addressed to Luxembourg public authorities via the PEPPOL network. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) framework, Luxembourg will phase in mandatory structured e-invoicing and AEDT digital reporting for intra-EU supplies on a timetable beginning no earlier than 2030.

The AEDT and the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency apply strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Where the export evidence bundle is missing, false, or untimely, the zero rate is retrospectively reversed and the stevedoring supply is re-taxed at 17% VAT; late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month from the original due date, a 10% late-payment surcharge may be imposed, and administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 apply for intentional VAT evasion. Criminal VAT fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years. The VAT assessment period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or omission. Customs offences under the Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013) may additionally trigger penalty assessments and suspension or revocation of the terminal operator's concession and AEO/simplification authorisations; the stevedore and the exporter are jointly and severally liable for VAT on wrongly zero-rated supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; no Luxembourg restrictions. Profit remittance from a transparent SCSp to non-resident partners is free of withholding tax and unrestricted; settlement via SEPA/TARGET2 or SWIFT is permissible without BCL approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timescale applies to export receipts in Luxembourg. Stevedoring revenue in EUR or foreign currency may be freely retained, hedged, or transferred abroad without BCL authorisation.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) – Eurosystem member; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, AML, and payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Luxembourg-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions may support escrow and operational floats, but lack the direct SWIFT membership, correspondent network strength, and high-value clearing capacity required for the primary port stevedoring treasury. An EMI can be used for ancillary payroll, concession fees, or low-value port charges, but the main clearing rails remain with a tier-1 corporate bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: SWIFT MT103/MT202 with reference to the SCSp's RCS number and BOP purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity capital in an unlisted partnership' (BPM6/D.3). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Luxembourg City aggregate headline rate for corporate taxpayers: 24.94% (17.00% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19% + 6.75% municipal business tax). An SCSp is fiscally transparent: no entity-level CIT or municipal business tax is levied on the partnership; partners are taxed on their allocated shares of the bulk port stevedoring profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax on dividend distributions; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% shareholding and ≥12-month holding period); DTA treaties typically provide 0%, 5% or 15% rates depending on shareholding and beneficiary status.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December Y, the Form 560 declaration is due by 31 December Y+1; the ACD may grant an extension on reasoned request. Municipal business tax assessment follows the same declaration cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File applies where the aggregate value of controlled intra-group transactions exceeds €1,000,000 per fiscal year; Master File and CbC reporting are required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least €750,000,000 (Grand-Ducal Regulation of 20 December 2019; ACD Circular LIR n° 164/2).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations code; however, substance is enforced through art. 6 LIR abuse-of-law doctrine, ATAD GAAR, CFC and anti-hybrid rules, the participation-exemption taxable-substrate test, and ACD administrative substance guidelines. An SCSp carrying on bulk port stevedoring must demonstrate real personnel, terminal premises, equipment, operational decision-making and commercial risk in Luxembourg to retain pass-through treatment and access to treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of evaded tax plus monthly late interest at 0.6%; administrative penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax; criminal tax fraud conviction carries a fine of up to €125,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years (LIR arts. 226 et seq.).

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp gérant who sign false returns or knowingly conceal income are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; civil restitution (comblement du passif) in insolvency, asset freezes, travel bans during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment apply for personal culpability.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: ACD invalidation under art. 6 LIR and ATAD: revocation of fiscal transparency or denial of treaty benefits; income re-attributed to the Luxembourg stevedoring activity; retroactive reassessment with statutory interest and applicable administrative surcharges.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced against the general partner and directors where the structure was used to conceal the true beneficiary; criminal exposure under fraud statutes, director disqualification, and secondary liability for the tax shortfall.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: ACD transfer pricing adjustment to an arm's length margin; disallowance of excess deductions; documentation penalties and daily late-filing interest at 0.6% per month; aggravated cases may attract criminal tax sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors approving intra-group pricing without benchmark documentation expose themselves to veil-piercing where the adjustment renders the entity insolvent; co-liability for resulting tax, interest and social security debts may be asserted.
AML / Beneficial Owner Register Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to €250,000 per violation; publication of sanctions; potential manager disqualification; aggravated concealment triggers criminal money-laundering sanctions including imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The gérant and directors are personally answerable for RBE filing obligations; aggravated concealment leads to criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel restrictions and imprisonment.

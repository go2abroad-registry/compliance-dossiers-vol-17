Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS company registration; digital portals: MyGuichet.lu, lbr.lu, aedt.public.lu, and tax.lu

E-government filing portal

1) File the SCSp partnership agreement with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) platform to obtain the 15-digit national business number (matricule); 2) Register for VAT with the AEDT via MyGuichet using the matricule, obtaining an LU + 8-digit VAT number; 3) For direct tax, the fiscally transparent SCSp is not separately registered for CIT — each partner is assessed under its own residence tax number; non-resident partners file Luxembourg income tax declarations through a Luxembourg tax representative where required; 4) A non-EU-resident SCSp or foreign partner making taxable Luxembourg supplies must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before AEDT registration, whereas EU-resident entities register directly; 5) Confirm municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) obligations with the competent commune, which may apply at the SCSp level despite income tax transparency.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) File the SCSp partnership agreement with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) platform to obtain the 15-digit national business number (matricule); 2) Register for VAT with the AEDT via MyGuichet using the matricule, obtaining an LU + 8-digit VAT number; 3) For direct tax, the fiscally transparent SCSp is not separately registered for CIT — each partner is assessed under its own residence tax number; non-resident partners file Luxembourg income tax declarations through a Luxembourg tax representative where required; 4) A non-EU-resident SCSp or foreign partner making taxable Luxembourg supplies must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before AEDT registration, whereas EU-resident entities register directly; 5) Confirm municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) obligations with the competent commune, which may apply at the SCSp level despite income tax transparency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; the SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent for income tax (partners taxed in their own hands). For VAT, the SCSp is treated as an independent taxable person and may be required to register upon making taxable supplies. Anti-hybrid rules (ATAD) override transparency where cross-border hybrid arrangements create double non-taxation or deduction/non-inclusion mismatches. The partnership may structure contractual profit-allocation (targeted allocations, tracking or sub-accounts) to shape partner-level taxable income, but cannot elect entity-level CIT liability.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover small-business exemption for locally established taxable persons; for non-established e-commerce suppliers of digitally supplied services (B2C) the intra-EU distance-sales/OSS registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; B2B services to Luxembourg businesses are not registrable by the supplier because the reverse charge shifts VAT to the Luxembourg customer; non-EU suppliers may opt for the Union OSS to avoid local VAT registration for remote B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to qualifying exports of goods to destinations outside the EU where the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days and transport is evidenced. The AI avatar video generation software and associated SaaS services are electronically supplied services: B2B supplies to non-EU customers are outside Luxembourg VAT (place of supply at customer's establishment); B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside scope (place of supply at consumer's residence); B2B supplies to EU customers are reverse-charged; only B2C supplies to Luxembourg consumers attract Luxembourg VAT at 17%. Export-related services directly linked to exported goods may also qualify for 0% under the VAT Act subject to strict documentary proof.

  • Electronic Single Administrative Document (e-SAD) customs export declaration accepted by Luxembourg customs
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, export contract and purchase order
  • Destination-country customs clearance or import entry documentation (where available)
  • Bank payment evidence or foreign inward remittance records supporting transaction genuineness

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls currently apply in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is compulsory only for suppliers to Luxembourg public contracting authorities (B2G) in Peppol/EN16931 format; B2B e-invoicing remains optional. Luxembourg will implement the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) digital reporting obligations from 2028/2030; all invoices, e-invoices and export documentation must be retained for 6 years.

AEDT and Luxembourg customs conduct post-clearance audits of zero-rated and outside-scope exports; defective documentation triggers reassessment at 17% VAT, statutory default interest, and administrative fines (up to 10% of the evaded tax for negligence and up to double the evaded tax for intentional fraud). Criminal tax fraud (fraude fiscale) is punishable by imprisonment (up to 5 years) and significant fines; the re-assessment limitation period is extended in cases of fraud; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, direct partners remain jointly liable for any tax clawback in proportion to their profit split.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area state with full current and capital account convertibility. The free movement of capital is guaranteed under Articles 63-65 TFEU and Luxembourg implements no general exchange controls or currency equalization restrictions. A Luxembourg Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is fiscally transparent, and profit remittances to foreign partners are not subject to Luxembourg domestic withholding tax at the level of the partnership; all distributions are paid gross, though the tax treatment of the partner depends on the partner's own residence and the applicable double tax treaty framework. No administrative authorization is required for remittance, subject to normal AML/CFT and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Luxembourg. Exporters of software/SaaS services and AI-generated content may retain and freely use foreign currency proceeds without any forced conversion into euro or any BCL reporting deadline. Only standard statistical balance-of-payments reporting thresholds and AML transaction reporting requirements apply, and they do not restrict the timing or convertibility of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High (conditional). An EU/EEA licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may operate as a supplementary collection and payment rail for the SaaS revenue, but it is not a substitute for a regulated credit institution for escrow or capital-commitment purposes. Legal constraints: the SCSp must be the named account holder; e-money safeguarding rules under the Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) apply; the EMI cannot provide lending or escrow-grade settlement; and the bank/non-bank relationship is subject to strict AML/KYC and PM-L/CFT compliance at both the EMI and the underlying payment processor (Stripe/Paddle) level.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing requires the use of SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent ISO 20022 payment) from the investor's own bank account to the SCSp's IBAN at its Luxembourg commercial bank, with a reference clearly stating 'Capital Contribution to LU SCSp' or 'Equity Subscription'. The payment must be accompanied by a capital contribution deed, notarized partnership documentation, and an updated share/sedimentation register. The receiving bank performs beneficiary and source-of-funds checks; amounts above EUR 10,000 may require standard AML verification and, if triggered, enhanced due diligence. The purpose code can be referenced as 'Direct Investment' under the BPM6 balance-of-payments classification; BCL statistical reporting obligations rest on the bank rather than the SCSp.Primary clearing rail for non-EEA trade settlements is SWIFT MT103/MT202 for international wire transfers, with the beneficiary account name, IBAN/SWIFT BIC, and a regulatory reference of 'Trade Settlement' or 'Service Fee'. For intra-EEA euro-denominated disbursements, SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) are the standard low-cost rail. For goods or intellectual-property acquisition with documentary risk, a documentary commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700), followed by amendment via MT707 and honour under UCP600, is available through the Luxembourg commercial bank. The SCSp itself, being fiscally transparent (pass-through), is not subject to CIT at the entity level; its profits are allocated to partners who are taxed at their own applicable rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for payments by an SCSp to its partners, as the SCSp is fiscally transparent and distributions of profits are not regarded as dividends; they represent additional remuneration or returns of capital. If the SCSp were subject to an election to be treated as a taxable corporation, the standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax rate would be 15%, reduced to 0% under the parent-subsidiary regime (≥10% shareholding, 12-month holding) or 5% under certain tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg adheres to the OECD TP Documentation Guidelines and EU Transfer Pricing Directives. Mandatory preparation of a Master File and Local File for the Luxembourg entity if: (1) the international group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; and (2) the Luxembourg entity's controlled transactions exceed EUR 100,000 per category (financing, intangibles, services) in a fiscal period. The local file must be filed electronically with the Luxembourg tax administration within 12 months after the fiscal year-end (but must be available immediately upon request during an audit).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply the UK-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the law requires a certain level of economic substance to obtain and maintain tax residency, to benefit from the IP box regime, and to invoke tax treaty protection. The Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority's Circular L.I.R. n° 14/4 on tax residency requires offices, staff, and decision-making functions in Luxembourg. The ATAD (EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive) including the GAAR (General Anti-Abuse Rule) will disregard artificial structures lacking substance. For an SCSp generating AI video software income, the entity must demonstrate real business activity, such as the presence of contractual personnel, license management, and management of development risks. Failure to meet substance requirements can lead to recharacterization of the structure and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Fixed fine of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 plus a late payment interest of 0.8% per month; if tax evasion is established, the tax administration may reassess income for up to 10 years; the amount of evaded tax is due together with an administrative penalty of 10% of the evaded tax, which can rise to 50% in case of a repeated offence.

Director personal liability: Luxembourg law allows the tax administration to pierce the corporate veil where the SCSp has been used fraudulently. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for corpores tax debts if they have knowingly signed false statements. Criminal penalties include imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed during the criminal investigation.
Artificial Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration can disregard the SCSp for tax purposes (substance over form), deny the applicable treaty or EU directive benefits, and assess tax on the deemed Luxembourg resident beneficiary. Administrative penalties up to EUR 10,000 may be imposed for filing incomplete or false statements on economic substance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell SCSp may be held personally liable for taxes due, interest, and penalties if they are deemed to have exercised actual control over the entity's operations. On sham company findings, directors may face civil liability for the total loss suffered by the tax authority, and the criminal sanction includes disqualification from managing a Luxembourg entity and a custodial sentence up to 3 years.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 (AML) and PPPA 2023, failure to register beneficial ownership results in a fine of EUR 1,000 to EUR 20,000 per month of non-compliance. AML violations trigger administrative penalties from EUR 20,000 to EUR 1,000,000; criminal money laundering sanctions include imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and fines up to EUR 1,250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors (including managers of an SCSp who direct the platform) are personally liable for AML breaches if they knowingly neglect their duty to report suspicious transactions. They may receive EU-wide asset freezing orders, travel prohibitions, and imprisonment up to 8 years for money laundering facilitation. The Luxembourg financial regulator (CSSF) will ban the individual from holding compliance or management positions in regulated entities.
Transfer Pricing Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits to the arm's length standard, assessment of additional CIT, municipal business tax, and solidarity surcharge (aggregate rate up to 24.94%). A penalty of up to 50% of the extra tax may be imposed if the pricing is not supported by contemporaneous benchmarking; late interest applies retrospectively.

Director personal liability: If directors permit a deliberate, undocumented transfer pricing scheme, the Luxembourg tax administration may assert personal liability for the additional tax and penalties. In case of fraudulent mispricing, criminal proceedings for tax evasion could lead to imprisonment of the responsible finance director.

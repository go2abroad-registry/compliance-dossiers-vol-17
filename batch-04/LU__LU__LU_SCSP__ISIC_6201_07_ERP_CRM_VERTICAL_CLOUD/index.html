Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de commerce et des sociétés - RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the authority of the Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg (Luxembourg District Court).

Governing statute

Companies Act: Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended), Articles 102-1 to 102-13, governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) as introduced by the Loi du 12 juillet 2013 relative au partenariat; supplemented by the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 November 2014 on the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register and the RESA.

Formation / constitutional filings

Contrat de société (partnership agreement / articles of partnership), executed by private deed (sous seing privé) or notarial deed, filed with the RCS through the e-RCS portal together with the statutory admission declarations; the RCS and RESA publication give legal effect against third parties upon registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for the SCSp (EUR 0); capital amount and contribution terms are freely determined by the partners in the partnership agreement. No escrow or blocked-account certificate is required. In-kind contributions must be described and valued in the partnership agreement, and the contributing partner warrants that valuation towards third parties for five years.

Funding & unlock path: Standard office occupancy clearance applies: municipal conformity certificate for the leased premises, fire-safety compliance verified by the Corps grand-ducal et de secours (CGDIS) for premises open to the public, and occupational-health duties under the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM), including compliant workstations, first-aid equipment and a documented annual risk assessment. No heavy structural-load or civil-defense clearance applies to this intellectual and cloud-based activity.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for the SCSp (EUR 0); capital amount and contribution terms are freely determined by the partners in the partnership agreement. No escrow or blocked-account certificate is required. In-kind contributions must be described and valued in the partnership agreement, and the contributing partner warrants that valuation towards third parties for five years.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 6201 / NACE 62.01 pure software and cloud services. The Ministry of the Economy (Ministère de l'Économie) issues establishment authorizations only for regulated artisan, commercial and industrial professions; vertical ERP/CRM cloud software is an unregulated service activity requiring no such license.

Category: No operating or manufacturing permit required. The business falls under the general freedom of establishment and is exempt from the autorisation d'établissement regime of the Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to independent professions; no industrial operating license is applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority; portal: impotsdirects.public.lu. VAT: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); portal: aedt.public.lu. Electronic corporate life-cycle filing via MyGuichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR, RCS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate/register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) and obtain the 15-digit business registration matricule number. Step 2: The ACD automatically issues the national tax identification number (TIN) for corporate-tax correspondence upon RCS activation. Step 3: File VAT registration with the AEDT to obtain a LU VAT number for taxable supplies (required for B2B cloud subscriptions and intra-EU acquisitions); non-established foreign entities may apply directly to the AEDT and must, if required, designate a Luxembourg VAT agent. Step 4: Register payroll/social accounts if the SCSp hires Luxembourg employees, and obtain a security number from the Centre commun de la securite sociale (CCSS).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp is by default fiscally transparent in Luxembourg; the partnership itself is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax; partners are taxed directly on their allocable shares of SCSp income (character and source preserved), irrespective of distribution, and non-resident partners are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income in accordance with the relevant tax treaty.

Available election forms

Article 175bis LIR election: an SCSp carrying on a business may irrevocably elect entity-level taxation as a capital company (opting/electing partnership), subject to all-partner consent, accounting records requirements, and corporate tax rate alignment. US partners may make a check-the-box election on Form 8832 to classify a disregarded/partnership entity for US federal tax purposes; anti-hybrid rules (ATAD 2, implemented by the law of 21 December 2018) apply to neutralise mismatches where Luxembourg transparency interacts with a partner jurisdiction's entity-level taxation. No general one-stop re-classification outside these statutory channels.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic-turnover threshold: every person carrying on an independent economic activity in Luxembourg, wherever established, must apply for a Luxembourg VAT number. Domestic small-business franchise of EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (Article 159 of the VAT Act) exempts qualifying start-up businesses from charging VAT but is generally not relevant to cross-border vertical cloud B2B supplies. Euro-area intra-Community distance sales/cross-border B2C digital services use the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold and OSS/IOSS schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies where goods are physically transported out of the EU by the supplier, the customer or a carrier, provided transport/exports occur within the statutory evidence window (presumptive 90 days from supply) and proof of departure is documented; intra-EU dispatches qualify for 0% only when the customer supplies a valid EU VAT identification number and the supply is reported in the EC Sales List. Services: vertical cloud/ERP/CRM B2B SaaS supplies are generally outside the Luxembourg VAT charge (Article 58 of the VAT Act - place of supply is the customer's establishment); no zero-rating claim is therefore required for non-resident business customers, but the supplier must hold buyer identification and treat the sale as reverse-charged. Services connected with exported goods (consultancy, ERP customisation for an export project) may qualify for 0% under Article 74 implementing EU Article 146(1)(e) if the foreign recipient is an entrepreneur and the services are directly linked to exported goods.

  • Electronic export customs declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged via PLDA / Customs EDI system) with movement reference number
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (English-language, describing vertical-ERP cloud/hardware deliverables if any)
  • International transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR/freight contract evidencing departure from the EU territory
  • Customs 'exit' confirmation from the Luxembourg Customs Administration (or transit office of exit for non-EU destinations)
  • Customer purchase order / SaaS agreement confirming non-resident business customer status and EU VAT ID (for intra-EU zero-rating)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment confirmation as evidence of consideration received
  • Zero-rated sales ledger extract and export evidence file with audit trail linking invoice, transport and payment

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance (no API/JSON tax-stamping, no digital postmark). E-invoicing is permitted under EU Directive 2014/55/EU in PEPPOL/EN 16931 format; electronic invoices must be issued no later than the 15th day of the month following the supply. Mandatory PEPPOL e-invoicing applies to supplies to Luxembourg public-sector bodies (B2G). A transitional CTC/digital-reporting obligation for intra-EU and, on a longer deferral, domestic B2B transactions is expected under the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age package (2027-2030 phase-in), at which point transaction-level data, e-invoice clearance, and interoperability will replace the current periodic VAT List/EC Sales Listing framework.

Luxembourg VAT law imposes an additional levy of 10% (minimum EUR 750) on any corrected VAT under-declared, rising to 100% where evasion is intentional; late VAT bears statutory interest from the due date of the relevant return. Customs law penalties (fines up to EUR 6,000, plus forfeiture remedies) apply for false export declarations. Zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back, with reversal plus the 10%/100% surcharges and interest, where the exporter cannot produce the transport/customs/payment evidence bundle or where funds are not received in the documented payment chain; criminal prosecution before Luxembourg criminal courts is possible for fraudulent export schemes (VAT carousel or sham cloud-invoicing). The VAT officer and Customs 'EVA' files remain subject to 6-10 year audit windows in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates under a fully liberalized capital and current account convertibility framework consistent with EU treaties. There are no exchange controls restricting inward or outward funds movement, and profit remittances from an SCSp are not subject to prior authorisation. However, cross-border transfers must carry accurate EU Balance of Payments (BoP) purpose codes and must be substantiated by the underlying partnership agreement and commercial or investment rationale to satisfy AML/CFT and tax substance reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios. Export earnings in EUR or foreign currency may be retained in local or foreign bank accounts without forced conversion, and may be freely reinvested or repatriated. Obligations are limited to statistical reporting to the BCL under the national balance of payments survey framework when transactions exceed the applicable reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) designated as the market conduct, AML/CFT, and payment/e-money institution supervisor for all regulated financial actors in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs that hold no EU/EEA passport or Luxembourg licence are not viable for opening a corporate payment account in Luxembourg. A regulated EU/EEA EMI operating through the PSD2 passporting regime into Luxembourg may serve as a supplementary rail for low-risk collections, but core treasury banking will require a domestic credit institution account. Feasibility rating: Medium conditional on the EMI having a legitimate Luxembourg presence, as the SCSp remains subject to CSSF anti-money laundering expectations and a licensed domiciliation agent.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17.00% CIT on taxable income above EUR 175,000; 15.00% below EUR 175,000; plus 7.00% solidarity surcharge (maximum combined 18.19%); municipal business tax variable, e.g. 6.75% in Luxembourg City. A SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not subject to entity-level CIT when treated as pass-through.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15.00% domestic dividend withholding tax for opaque Luxembourg companies; 0.00% for qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or Luxembourg participation exemption. SCSp distributions are generally not subject to entity-level WHT because the partnership is fiscally transparent; tax may arise at partner level, and underlying Luxembourg company distributions retain source character.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate related-party revenue threshold for mandatory Local File; Master File required where group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Report threshold matches the OECD at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg recognises OECD-aligned arm's length and anti-hybrid rules. It has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations for non-EU zero-tax jurisdictions, but ATAD, DAC-6, the General Anti-Abuse Rule, and Luxembourg's substance circulars require real economic activity for SCSp entities to access treaty benefits and prevent constructive shell classification. Genuine business decisions, employees, office premises, accounting records, and bank accounts in Luxembourg are essential.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of one to five years; additional administrative surcharge of 10%-25%; default interest at 3% per annum; confiscation of proceeds and complete reassessment of five prior fiscal years.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who sign false returns may have the corporate veil pierced; personal civil liability for evaded tax, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment for up to five years.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of the SCSp as opaque, addition of withholding tax on deemed distributions, CFC income inclusion in shareholders' tax base, penalty of 100% of tax understated, and mandatory substance documentation failure penalties under ATAD/GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered personally to pay the tax differential, restitution of treaty benefits with interest, management ban up to ten years, and criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; regulatory sanctions against the RBE processing entity; imprisonment of senior officers up to five years for deliberate money laundering facilitation; publication of penal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and senior managers face personal non-capped civil liability for laundering damages, travel bans pending criminal investigation, freezing of bank accounts, and disqualification from directorship for ten years.
Transfer Pricing / Controlled-Foreign-Corporation Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Assessment at arm's length with upward profit adjustment, additional tax up to 25%-50% of understatement, interest, late filing penalty up to EUR 2,500, exclusion of loss compensation, and reclassification of royalty payments as constructive dividends subject to 15% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers may be jointly and severally liable for the re-assessed tax and penalties if they knowingly failed to prepare TP documentation; may be subject to civil restitution, travel restriction orders, and criminal sentences for tax evasion up to five years.

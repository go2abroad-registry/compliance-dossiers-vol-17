Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT. Official digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (eTVA, eCDF, and online RCS filings) and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via lbr.lu for commercial registry filings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SCSp partnership agreement (private deed is sufficient absent contributions of registered real estate; a notarial instrument is commonly used for Luxembourg registered-office formalities). Step 2: File the registration dossier with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the single point of contact on MyGuichet.lu; obtain the RCS registration number, the STATEC national business number (numéro de matricule national), and the commercial register excerpt. Step 3: Publish the required notice in the electronic official gazette (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations, RESA) and file the beneficial-owner declaration with the Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs (RBE) within one month of registration. Step 4: Register for VAT with the AEDT using the VAT registration form (Formulaire 640) submitted via MyGuichet.lu; obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits), which is synchronised with the RCS identification; registration must be completed before the first taxable transaction is performed or invoiced. Step 5: Maintain the statutory corporate and tax files at the registered office, file the annual RCS confirmation (confirmation annuelle), and keep current the RBE register.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory rule for Luxembourg direct taxes (income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax are not levied at the partnership level); each general or limited partner is taxed directly on its allocated share of SaaS profits as if the profits had been realised by that partner, subject to treaty protection in cross-border structures. The partnership remains a taxable person (assujetti) for VAT purposes where it acts in its own name in an independent economic activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory rule for Luxembourg direct taxes (income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax are not levied at the partnership level); each general or limited partner is taxed directly on its allocated share of SaaS profits as if the profits had been realised by that partner, subject to treaty protection in cross-border structures. The partnership remains a taxable person (assujetti) for VAT purposes where it acts in its own name in an independent economic activity. Territorial scope: the SCSp is a Luxembourg-resident pass-through entity with its registered office and place of actual management in Luxembourg; there is no sub-state tax layer in the Grand Duchy.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box system; the transparent status of the SCSp is automatic and cannot be overridden for direct tax purposes. Available elections include: (i) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for a corporate partner holding directly or indirectly at least 95% of the capital of one or more Luxembourg resident companies, with no election available at the SCSp level itself; (ii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT law where the SCSp is established in Luxembourg and has financial, economic, and organisational links with other group members, allowing a single VAT filing group; (iii) the small business exemption option (franchise d'imposition) under Article 57 of the VAT law where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000, which is generally not advisable for an exporting SaaS business because it forfeits input VAT deduction; (iv) no separate hybrid mismatch election exists, but Luxembourg applies the ATAD-compliant hybrid rules (law of 21 December 2018 and subsequent amendments) that deny deductions for payments that are deductible in Luxembourg and not correspondingly taxed or included in income elsewhere.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the domestic small business exemption; however, a B2B SaaS operator is obliged to register for Luxembourg VAT from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg when it is established in Luxembourg or when it intends to perform cross-border B2B services subject to reverse charge, because no general threshold applies for obtaining a VAT number to validate reverse-charge neutrality, file EC Sales Lists, and exercise input VAT deduction; the registration must be completed before invoicing or performance of the external supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

B2B Enterprise SaaS supplied by the Luxembourg SCSp to non-resident business customers qualifies for the territoriality override under Article 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC, under which the place of supply is the customer's establishment rather than Luxembourg. Supplies to EU business customers are outside Luxembourg VAT and subject to reverse charge in the customer's member state; supplies to non-EU business customers are effectively zero-rated in Luxembourg. Eligibility requires (i) a valid customer VAT identification number for EU customers, (ii) documentary evidence of the customer's establishment outside Luxembourg, and (iii) contractual and factual demonstration of the business-to-business character of the SaaS licence. The zero-rated export supplies preserve the pro-rata right of input VAT deduction, so VAT on cloud hosting, software development, legal, and professional fees remains fully reclaimable to the extent attributable to export turnover.

  • Executed B2B SaaS master subscription agreement and order forms evidencing the customer's business identity, establishment country, and licence scope
  • Customer VAT identification number (EU customers) or certificate of tax residence / foreign company registration extract (non-EU customers) retained per customer file
  • Inward remittance evidence: Stripe Billing and Wise B2B settlement reports, bank account credit receipts, or SWIFT/MT103 confirmations covering the invoiced amount
  • Service delivery and usage records (hosting logs, user activity reports, server access records) demonstrating that the SaaS application was made available to the non-resident customer
  • Luxembourg VAT returns and EC Sales Lists (EU customers) showing the value of the export supplies and, where any ancillary goods are supplied with the service, supporting transport or customs evidence

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping regime for B2B electronic invoicing. B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory for public contracts above the relevant thresholds via the PEPPOL network since 1 January 2024. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and PEPPOL/UBL formats are accepted; no AEDT real-time API validation or reporting obligation applies in the B2B channel as of the current legal position. Mandatory B2B e-reporting and e-invoicing are expected only after the transposition of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, currently projected for the 2027-2030 horizon. The SCSp must nonetheless issue compliant invoices through Stripe Billing or Wise B2B, follow statutory invoice dating and content rules, and file Luxembourg VAT returns monthly or quarterly through the eTVA portal.

The AEDT may reassess VAT retroactively for five years (ten years in cases of fraudulent declaration) under Article 76 of the Luxembourg VAT law; if the customer's establishment or the business-to-business substance is not substantiated, the invoiced SaaS fees are recharacterised as taxable Luxembourg supplies and the VAT is clawed back together with default interest at the statutory late payment rate (0.8% per month on unpaid amounts). Misdeclaration and non-compliance penalties range from EUR 250 to EUR 4,000 per offence (Articles 73 and 74 of the VAT law), the tax due may be doubled in cases of deliberate concealment, and criminal conviction for tax fraud may lead to imprisonment of up to five years. Luxembourg tax law does not impose a statutory export FX repatriation deadline, but the 180-day reference period is a customary covenant applied by Luxembourg credit institutions and payment providers (Stripe, Wise) to satisfy foreign-exchange control, sanctions, and AML/CFT expectations; failure to demonstrate repatriation within this window materially increases procedural audit exposure, including risk of reclassification of export turnover and review of related-party financing arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Current and capital account transactions are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Profit remittances and partner distributions by an SCSp to resident or non-resident partners are generally exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax because the SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (subject to substance and no commercial-activity disqualification), and no prior exchange-control approval or conversion permit is required. The only operational requirements are (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for cross-border transactions at or above EUR 12,500 per operation, and (ii) mandatory screening against EU consolidated financial sanctions lists; no quantitative limits or remittance delays apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Luxembourg. SaaS export receipts under ISIC 6201 may be freely retained in EUR or any foreign currency in Luxembourg bank accounts and can be converted or rebilled through any authorised credit institution. Reporting obligations are limited to BCL balance-of-payments declarations (transaction threshold EUR 12,500, aggregate monthly reporting) and standard EU anti-money-laundering declarations; there is no obligation to sell foreign currency to the central bank or to a domestic bank, and no service-sector Intrastat filing applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; monetary and FX policy is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem. There is no dedicated national foreign exchange administration; BCL supervises balance-of-payments statistics, T2 (TARGET services) and the Luxembourg Interbank Payment System (LIPS), while capital-flow rules follow EU Treaty provisions and the EU/UN financial sanctions framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a collection/billing rail, Medium as the sole treasury or clearing account. Legal constraints: Wise (Wise Europe SA, Belgium-licensed EMI) and Stripe Billing (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd, Ireland-licensed EMI) are EEA payment institutions passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2; they are not credit institutions, cannot accept deposits on their own balance sheet, do not participate in the Luxembourg FGDL deposit-guarantee scheme, and must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts held with an EEA credit institution. For an SCSp, using Wise/Stripe for B2B SaaS revenue intake and business payouts is lawful and operationally efficient, but enterprise counterparties and Luxembourg treasury practice require final settlement through a Luxembourg IBAN; the recommended blueprint is to use Stripe and Wise as charge-and-disbursement rails and sweep balances daily to a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a collection/billing rail, Medium as the sole treasury or clearing account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €450,000 (goods or services) or €100,000 for financial transactions; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €50 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for certain activities (e.g., financing, IP holding). For SaaS, general substance requirements apply to avoid being classified as a shell company. The SCSp must demonstrate adequate staff, premises, and decision-making in Luxembourg if it claims treaty benefits or engages in intra-group transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 100% of tax evaded; interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; potential extradition for cross-border fraud.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, imposition of 15% WHT on distributions, and potential liquidation; fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly allowed the company to operate as a shell; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment; freezing of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined and imprisoned for AML violations; travel bans and prohibition from holding directorship may be imposed.

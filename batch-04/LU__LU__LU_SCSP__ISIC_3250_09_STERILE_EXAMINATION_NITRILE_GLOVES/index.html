Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital and no paid-up capital escrow or bank blockage certificate is required for an SCSp; the partnership agreement must only declare the agreed contributions (cash or in-kind), each partner funds its subscription by transfer to the SCSp bank account within the timeline set in the agreement, and the declaration of contributions is repeated in the RCS filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGI) covering the production hall, nitrile latex storage tanks, and packaging lines, including sprinkler conformity, emergency-exit certification, and hot-work permits for the dipping-line ovens; structural load verification certificate from an approved technical control body (Luxcontrol, SOCOTEC, or TÜV Rheinland) confirming the conveyor dipping-line supports, curing ovens, and foundation loads of the tank farm; workplace safety authorization by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering machine-guard conformity on the dipping and stripping lines, chemical exposure monitoring for nitrile latex and vulcanization accelerants, mandatory personal protective equipment programme, and periodic medical surveillance of authorized operators.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital and no paid-up capital escrow or bank blockage certificate is required for an SCSp; the partnership agreement must only declare the agreed contributions (cash or in-kind), each partner funds its subscription by transfer to the SCSp bank account within the timeline set in the agreement, and the declaration of contributions is repeated in the RCS filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) / Luxembourg Inland Revenue Department; VAT registration is managed by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); online portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the eCDF platform for tax filings.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) with non-resident partners, the partnership itself is not a separate taxable entity for income tax purposes; however, it must obtain a Luxembourg national identification number (matricule) via the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and the Luxembourg Business Registers. For VAT purposes, the SCSp must register for VAT with the AEDT by filing the Form 1 (déclaration d'assujettissement) if it performs taxable transactions in Luxembourg without an established business presence, or immediately upon establishment if engaged in taxable activities. The procurement sequence is: (1) file incorporation documents with the RCS via a notary or authorised professional; (2) obtain the 13-digit Luxembourg matricule number; (3) register for direct tax identification if required for withholding obligations (non-resident partners' share of Luxembourg-source income); (4) submit VAT registration application (Formulaire 1) to the AEDT; (5) receive a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits + 2 check digits) issued by the AEDT. For foreign entities without permanent establishment, the VAT registration is handled via a fiscal representative or electronic registration on the AEDT portal, accompanied by proof of economic activity, articles of association, and identification of directors/managers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Luxembourg tax law, an SCSp is by default fiscally transparent (pass-through) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes, unless it has opted for corporate tax classification (tax-opaque status). The option to be treated as a taxable entity (assujettissement à l'impôt sur les sociétés) must be exercised by the partners in the partnership agreement and notified to the Luxembourg tax administration. Luxembourg applies the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules, which may disregard the pass-through status of an SCSp if a foreign jurisdiction treats it as opaque and such mismatch results in a deduction without corresponding income inclusion. The check-the-box-equivalent mechanism is not a unilateral US-style election; rather, the SCSp's classification is determined by its legal form and any express election in its constitutive documents, which must be accepted by the ACD. The Luxembourg tax administration also recognises the possibility of filing a request for a tax ruling (tax clearance ruling) to confirm the classification for cross-border investors, subject to the statutory ruling procedure before the Direct Tax Authority.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full euro-area member with complete liberalization of capital and current account movements under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No exchange control regime, no prior licensing, no quantitative restrictions, and no surrender obligations apply to resident or non-resident parties. Dividend, interest, and profit remittances from the SCSp to non-resident limited partners are fully convertible into any currency and freely transferable, subject only to standard AML/KYC, FATCA, and CRS reporting protocols and the absence of any beneficial owner sanctions exposure. The euro is legal tender; multi-currency accounts in USD, GBP, and CHF are standard market practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation or surrender of export proceeds for receivables collected in EUR, USD, or any other currency. Exporters are at liberty to retain foreign currency balances in bank accounts, convert at market rates through licensed credit institutions, or use the proceeds directly to settle trade payables, hedge obligations, or fund operational disbursements.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); foreign exchange policy and payment system oversight are harmonized within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB), with prudential supervision of credit institutions delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium for payment initiation and receipt of funds but Low for any core treasury or trade-finance role. Luxembourg-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under PSD2 can provide segregated client accounts, multicurrency IBANs, and SEPA/SWIFT rails; however, the SCSp's cross-border merchant and trade settlement profile under ISIC 3250 requires documentary letters of credit, pre-export financing, and large-value TT wires that EMIs generally cannot support. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport must be avoided: they are not supervised by the CSSF/BCL and will cause downstream correspondent bank de-risking and drag on account approval with Tier 1 relationship banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident limited partners inject capital via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for the euro-denominated capital contribution, referencing the SCSp capital call notice and the GP bank mandate. The recommended EU Balance of Payments purpose code is B110 (direct investment in equity capital of a non-financial corporation) or B130 (other capital / partner loan), entered in the remittance field. Funds settle in TARGET2 through the Luxembourg bank's ECB connectivity; the bank will hold the funds on the SCSp's account in the GP's name. The bank requires the capital call certificate, the partnership accounts, and the entry in the SCSp's internal capital accounts; no BCL authorization is required for inward investment.Trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for TT wires to raw-material suppliers (nitrile latex, compounding chemicals, packaging) and via documentary commercial letters of credit (sight or usance, UCP 600) for the export of sterile examination gloves to health-care distributors. Intra-EU supplier payments may be routed via SEPA Credit Transfer. EUR disbursements to other euro-area countries settle through TARGET2; non-euro disbursements are converted at market rate and cleared through the bank's correspondent network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (group consolidated revenue) - exceeds threshold triggers mandatory Luxembourg Local File and Master File for cross-border related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a separate ESR code. The economic substance doctrine is enforced through the abuse-of-law doctrine, GAAR (Art. 6 Steueranpassungsgesetz), the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, and OECD BEPS principles. An SCSp must prove genuine business activity, practical local management, premises, employees, and actual commercial risk to sustain Luxembourg tax residency, claim treaty benefits, and avoid reclassification as an artificial conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of understated tax plus late interest (0.8% per month); fiscal penalty up to 100% of evaded tax; administrative fines up to €250,000; criminal prosecution if the evasion is committed with intent.

Director personal liability: General partner directors and managers may be held jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; criminal courts may impose up to 5 years' imprisonment, travel bans, and director disqualification; veil-piercing is applied where assets are commingled or the SCSp is used as a shield.
Failure to File / Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Forced assessment (taxation d'office) based on available taxable basis; surcharge of 10% to 20% of assessed tax; late-payment interest; administrative fine up to €5,000; derivative penalties for non-disclosure of foreign relevant assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for administrative fines if they were aware of the non-filing and failed to instruct filing; no imprisonment for simple late filing, but persistent intentional omission can escalate to criminal liability.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting / Defective Documentation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing Luxembourg taxable income; denial of interest or royalty deductions; late interest and a penalty of up to 10% on the shortfall; possible corresponding adjustment under the DTA and risk of double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for gross negligence in failing to establish and document arm's length TP policies; personal liability normally requires fault causing tax loss; criminal exposure if the TP policy is a fraudulent sham.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits, EU Directive benefits, and participation exemption; reclassification of income; 20% penalty for abusive arrangements; exit taxation on assets and unpaid capital gains; potential revocation of tax rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be jointly liable for crystallized taxes and penalties; in egregious cases, AML and criminal authorities may impose asset freezes, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced when the SCSp has no separate economic reality.

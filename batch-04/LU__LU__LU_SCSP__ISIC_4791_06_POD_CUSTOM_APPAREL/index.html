Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct tax registration; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration and indirect tax administration; Luxembourg Tax Administration portal: guichet.lu / myguichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or a Luxembourg SCSp acting as a taxable person for VAT must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT on form 010 (Formulaire de demande d'immatriculation à la TVA). The application requires identification of the partnership, its authorized representative, actual economic activity, estimated turnover, and any non-EU entity's local tax representative if established outside the EU/EEA. In the case of an SCSp without legal personality, the registration is made in the name of the partnership itself when the partnership has a fixed establishment in Luxembourg or, if the SCSp has no Luxembourg establishment, in the name of its statutory manager/representative for VAT. The Luxembourg business number (matricule) issued by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) is required before or concurrently with the VAT registration. After validation, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg national identification number for VAT (numéro d'identification à la TVA) bearing the 'LU' prefix. A non-EU principal with no permanent establishment in Luxembourg may appoint an individual tax representative approved by the AEDT, who is jointly and severally liable for the VAT obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. For Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes, the SCSp is generally not itself a taxpayer; its profits and deductions flow through to its general and limited partners in proportion to their entitlement, and each partner is taxed in their own jurisdiction on their allocable share. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Luxembourg SCSp (Société en commandite spéciale) is a special limited partnership without legal personality and is fiscally transparent by default. For Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes, the SCSp is generally not itself a taxpayer; its profits and deductions flow through to its general and limited partners in proportion to their entitlement, and each partner is taxed in their own jurisdiction on their allocable share. For VAT purposes, the SCSp is nevertheless treated as a taxable person where it independently carries out an economic activity, and the partnership itself registers and remits VAT in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

No direct check-the-box election equivalent to the U.S. entity classification election exists under Luxembourg law. The fiscal transparency of an SCSp is the default statutory treatment and is not elective. Certain Luxembourg partnerships may, upon meeting specific legal conditions, opt for corporate income tax treatment only if they are transformed into a corporate vehicle (e.g., a société à responsabilité limitée or société anonyme) by a notarial deed, which is a civil-law conversion, not a tax classification election. Luxembourg has also enacted anti-hybrid rules consistent with ATAD (EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive) that may recharacterize a transparent entity where a foreign jurisdiction treats the same entity as opaque, and hybrid mismatch election mechanisms generally restrict rather than grant preferential pass-through status. No elective transparency regime exists for SCSp beyond the statutory default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover excluding VAT) for small-enterprise exemption from Luxembourg VAT; mandatory VAT registration is required once taxable supplies of goods or services exceed this threshold in the course of an economic activity, subject to special EU distance-sales thresholds and non-established supplier rules. E-commerce marketplace and distance-sales threshold: EUR 10,000 for cross-border intra-Community distance sales of goods to Luxembourg consumers before the EU-wide EUR 10,000 common threshold applies under the OSS (One Stop Shop) regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A POD apparel seller may apply the Luxembourg 0% VAT rate to supplies of goods physically exported by the seller or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union. Zero-rating applies where: (i) the goods are dispatched or transported out of Luxembourg to a non-EU country; (ii) the export is evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration; (iii) the exporter is the owner or has the power of disposal over the goods at the time of dispatch; (iv) the goods leave the EU within the statutory period (generally 90 days from dispatch; extension may apply); and (v) the supplier retains complete documentary proof of export. Zero-rating also applies to directly related services, transport, and freight forwarders associated with exported goods. If the POD apparel is supplied to a customer in another EU Member State and transported by the customer, intra-Community supply exemption (0%) applies only where the customer's VAT ID and a correct EC Sales List are in place. For e-commerce sales of low-value goods to non-EU consumers, Luxembourg zero-rating is available where export transport conditions are met and the supplier keeps the required evidence in its accounting records.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU) accepted by Luxembourg or another EU customs office
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Luxembourg seller or the SCSp to the non-EU buyer
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Proof of dispatch from Luxembourg including carrier tracking and customs movement reference number (MRN)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer/payment processor confirmation evidencing consideration received from the non-EU purchaser
  • Order and contract documentation identifying the buyer, goods, and sale terms (e.g., collection of AMD/other payment records for POD transactions)

No real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) system or mandatory digital fiscal stamping applies to B2B or B2C e-commerce invoicing in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing is permitted and legally equivalent to paper invoices, subject to authenticity of origin and integrity of content via advanced electronic signature, EDI, or reliable audit trail. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only in the B2G (business-to-government) segment: suppliers to Luxembourg public-sector entities must issue electronic invoices through the Peppol network and the Luxembourg national Peppol Authority (SIGI), unless exception applies. For POD apparel e-commerce, no specific API-based fiscal clearance or electronic invoice authorization step is required under Luxembourg domestic law, but records must be retained and made available to the AEDT in case of audit. OSS (One Stop Shop) registration via Luxembourg's portal is mandatory for certain cross-border supplies to consumers to simplify EU VAT compliance.

The AEDT audits export zero-rating claims strictly. If documentation is not produced or proves insufficient, the export is not accepted as zero-rated, and the full Luxembourg VAT becomes due from the seller, with late payment interest and potential penalties. Wrongful zero-rating or fraudulent claims may result in: (i) reassessment of VAT on the supply at 17%; (ii) late-payment interest; (iii) administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 per infringement depending on gravity; (iv) criminal penalties for tax fraud under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, including imprisonment in aggravated cases; and (v) joint and several liability of the legal representative or local tax representative. The statute of limitations for reassessment is generally five years for domestic transactions and up to ten years in case of fraud. Repeated clawbacks also trigger enhanced scrutiny for subsequent export claims, and a POD operator must maintain complete transactional evidence for at least ten years to satisfy Luxembourg and EU record-keeping requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions by virtue of Luxembourg's euro-area membership and Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no Luxembourg domestic exchange controls, no territorial restrictions on the disposition of export proceeds, and no prior authorisation requirements for profit remittance, partner distributions, or dividend-equivalent transfers from an SCSp to non-resident partners. EU restrictive measures (sanctions) and CSSF/BCL AML-CFT screening are the only practical constraints. For a POD retail SCSp settling via Stripe/PayPal, foreign-currency balances (USD, GBP, CHF) may be freely held, converted, or swept to EUR without BCL licences; only ordinary statistical balance-of-payments reporting is collected through resident credit institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in Luxembourg. Export proceeds from non-EU print-on-demand sales need not be repatriated, converted to EUR, or held with a Luxembourg-resident bank. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, no repatriation time limits, and no BCL notification deadlines for individual exporters. Receipts may remain in USD/GBP on Stripe/PayPal accounts or in dedicated multi-currency accounts until operational outflows occur. The receiving Luxembourg bank applies the standard BCL external position statistical classification (direct investment/trade in goods) but imposes no treasury restriction.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem/ESCB framework and in regulatory coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for payment services, AML/CFT supervision, and banking licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium-High for EU/EEA-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg or directly licensed by the CSSF. Target rails are fully viable: PayPal (Luxembourg) S.à r.l. holding a CSSF EMI licence, and Stripe operating in the EU through Stripe Technology Europe Ltd (Central Bank of Ireland licence, passported to Luxembourg under PSD2/EMD2). These EMIs may settle in EUR and provide connected bank accounts through partner credit institutions holding BCL/CSSF licences. Offshore (non-EEA) EMIs are rated Low-to-Non-viable due to the CSSF passporting requirement under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 (AML/CFT) and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services; cross-border acceptance of unauthorised non-EEA EMIs is a regulatory breach. A POD SCSp should maintain a primary EUR account at a tier-1 Luxembourg bank receiving Stripe/PayPal payouts to avoid PSP concentration risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective rate in Luxembourg City (CIT 17% + solidarity/unemployment surcharge of 7% on CIT + Municipal Business Tax 6.75%). The SCSp is fiscally transparent: no CIT at entity level; each partner is taxed on its distributive share (resident corporate partner aggregate about 24.94%; resident individual partner progressive up to 42% plus solidarity surcharge).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions of Luxembourg resident capital companies (SA, Sàrl). An SCSp is fiscally transparent; distributions of profits to partners are capital/current-account withdrawals and attract NO dividend withholding tax at partnership level. The 15% rate can apply where the SCSp or an underlying holding company pays dividends to a partner in a non-participation context.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 group consolidated revenue triggers Luxembourg Master File and Local File obligations under the Law of 18 December 2015 and Circular LIR 56/1 - 57/3 - 164/2; Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000. A standalone SCSp POD retailer normally falls below the thresholds, but contemporaneous TP documentation must still be kept where related-party print or fulfilment services are purchased.

Economic substance scope: Circular LIR n. 14/4 (28 January 2022) denies treaty benefits and tax-residence certification to letterbox entities lacking minimum substance: local premises, personnel, local bank account, management meetings in Luxembourg, and complete accounting records. The SCSp exercising genuine POD activity (online store, merchant accounts, print-supplier contracts, inventory turnover) has inherent substance, but the general partner must evidence governance by holding partner/management meetings in Luxembourg and maintaining statutory books and beneficiary registers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration (Luxembourg Abgabenordnung Arts. 55, 57, 396)

Statutory sanction: 10-15% administrative surcharge plus 0.6% monthly late interest; criminal tax fraud: fine up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years; aggravated or systematic schemes may trigger higher fines and forfeiture of the illicit profit.

Director personal liability: The general partner (gérant) is jointly and severally liable for the SCSp's tax debts where the structure is a sham or assets have been stripped; the ACD can pierce the veil, issue travel bans, attach personal assets, and prosecute de facto managers.
Letterbox Company / Substance Failure (Circular LIR n. 14/4)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the structure; denial of tax-residence certificate and treaty benefits; profit reallocation to the foreign beneficial owner; additional assessment at statutory rates plus penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: General partners and de facto directors face civil restitution for dissipated corporate benefits, joint liability for the tax differential, and potential disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
AML/KYC Failures of the Merchant Entity (Law of 12 November 2004 as amended; FATF standards)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for supervised professionals; freezing of merchant PSP accounts by the FIU; criminal sanctions for laundering the proceeds of tax evasion of up to 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for dissipated stored client funds; travel restrictions and imprisonment for principals in AML-linked fraud; accessory penalties may include prohibition from managing commercial companies.
Cross-Border E-Commerce VAT / OSS Violations (Luxembourg Law of 19 February 2021; EU VAT E-Commerce Package)

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment on all undeclared sales; 10% penalty plus 0.8% monthly default interest; exposure to tax audits in member states of consumption through the EU VAT Exchange; joint liability for VAT of non-EU print suppliers under certain supply-chain flows.

Director personal liability: The gérant is jointly liable for the SCSp's VAT debts where the partnership is left without assets or the declaration is knowingly void; travel bans can be imposed in enforcement proceedings for repeated VAT evasion.

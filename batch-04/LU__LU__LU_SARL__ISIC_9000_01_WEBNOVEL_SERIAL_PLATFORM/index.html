Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct Tax Administration (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for corporate income tax; Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT; electronic filing through MyGuichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) portal.

E-government filing portal

The SARL must first be incorporated and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS); the ACD then issues the corporate Luxembourg tax identification number (TIN). For VAT, a separate application for a VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) must be submitted to the AEDT via MyGuichet, attaching the deed of incorporation, RCS extract, director identification, and a description of intended activities. A non-resident entity with no Luxembourg fixed establishment making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly for Luxembourg VAT before its first taxable transaction and, when required by the AEDT, appoint an approved fiscal representative. Registration triggers periodic VAT return obligations (monthly or quarterly).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Luxembourg SARL is a resident full taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC), and net wealth tax (IF) on worldwide profits; no pass-through classification is available for a SARL; no tax-exempt zone applies to digital publishing or subscription platform activities.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; classification follows statutory legal form (SARL is treated as a corporation). Available elections include: (i) fiscal unity / group taxation for CIT under Article 164bis LIR (95% ownership chain, 5-year irrevocable commitment); (ii) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) applicable since 1 January 2023; (iii) IP box election granting an 80% exemption for qualifying IP income subject to nexus requirements; (iv) R&D super-deduction election for eligible software and content-development expenditure; and (v) the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for declaring EU cross-border B2C digital subscription sales centrally in Luxembourg.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 35,000 annual turnover; non-resident suppliers of digital services: no registration threshold (registration mandatory from the first euro of taxable supplies in Luxembourg); EU-wide cross-border B2C distance-sale threshold for optional supplier-country taxation: EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported to non-EU destinations qualify for zero-rating under Article 41 of the Luxembourg VAT law, provided the export is evidenced to the AEDT. For the webnovel subscription platform: B2C digital services supplied to non-EU consumers fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope because the place of supply is the consumer's country of usual residence; B2B supplies of digital content licensing to non-EU taxable persons are outside Luxembourg VAT scope (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction); B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's country, and the SARL may declare such sales centrally under the Union OSS scheme; where the SARL's intra-EU cross-border B2C turnover does not exceed EUR 10,000, Luxembourg VAT at 17% applies instead.

  • Customs Export Declaration under the EU Union Customs Code (UCC) with export clearance evidence (Export Control System / AES) for any physical goods exported
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR transport document proving physical departure from EU customs territory (for any transiting goods or promotional merchandise)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT statements or acquirer settlement reports evidencing receipt of consideration from the foreign customer
  • Digital service audit trail: consumer geo-location, IP address, billing and cardholder address, App Store / Google Play transaction receipts, and subscription log records
  • Signed subscription terms, platform revenue records, and retained VAT OSS registration validity documentation

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API, or digital stamping regime applies in Luxembourg. Mandatory structured e-invoicing applies only to business-to-government (B2G) supplies via the PEPPOL network (EN 16931). Draft law (Bill 8277) proposes phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing for Luxembourg-established suppliers, with expected commencement in 2025 for entities above EUR 100,000 turnover and in 2026 for all entities, although final enactment remains pending. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and OSS filings are submitted electronically via MyGuichet; taxpayers must retain standardized electronic accounting files (eCDF / SAF-T-compatible) for audit purposes.

The AEDT audits cross-border zero-rating and OSS positions closely. Unsupported export zero-rating or erroneous non-application of Luxembourg VAT results in retrospective VAT assessment at 17% plus late-payment interest, penalty fines (amendes), and, in serious cases, additional surcharges of up to 10% of the tax due. VAT fraud is criminally sanctionable, with extension of the statutory assessment window from 5 to 10 years, clawback of previously refunded VAT, joint and several liability of directors, and potential imprisonment under the Luxembourg General Tax Code and Criminal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full, unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions as a founding Eurozone member and an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction; the euro is legal tender and corporate balances may be freely held in EUR or any convertible currency. No prior exchange-control approval or license is required for payments or remittances. Profit-remittance hurdles arise principally from withholding tax rather than FX controls: dividends distributed by a SARL are subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at the domestic 15% rate unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the domestic Luxembourg participation exemption (minimum 10% shareholding or EUR 1.2 million acquisition value, with a 12-month minimum holding period); no withholding tax applies to royalties or ordinary interest payments to EU/EEA corporate recipients under domestic law. Routine cross-border remittances require only standard bank-level FX documentation and, where requested, balance-of-payments statistical declarations to the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, surrender obligation, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A Luxembourg SARL exporting digital-content subscription services (including net settlements received from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing) may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts indefinitely in any currency at any authorised credit institution, with no statutory deadline or minimum-EUR conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg) within the Eurosystem, with the European Central Bank (ECB) as the supranational monetary authority; capital-movement and foreign-exchange policy is governed at EU level under TFEU Articles 63–66, and Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for Luxembourg/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions operating under the PSD2 passport (e.g., Adyen Luxembourg, Stripe Payments Europe, Payoneer EU entity), which are suitable treasury rails for multi-currency payouts and platform mass disbursements. Rating: Low for offshore/non-EEA EMIs (UK FCA-regulated e-money issuers, US state-licensed money transmitters, Singapore or Hong Kong e-money providers), which cannot participate in Eurosystem T2 clearing or offer SEPA-instant EUR settlement without an EEA license. Legal constraints: under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and e-money and CSSF Regulation 10-02, the SARL may hold funds in an EMI account, but funds held outside a licensed credit institution are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL up to EUR 100,000); the EMI must demonstrate safeguarding of client funds, and the SARL remains subject to CSSF Circular 12-02 AML/CFT obligations when appointing an EMI as payout agent for author royalties.

Legal constraints: under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and e-money and CSSF Regulation 10-02, the SARL may hold funds in an EMI account, but funds held outside a licensed credit institution are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL up to EUR 100,000); the EMI must demonstrate safeguarding of client funds, and the SARL remains subject to CSSF Circular 12-02 AML/CFT obligations when appointing an EMI as payout agent for author royalties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate in Luxembourg City for taxable profits above EUR 175,000 (17% CIT + 7% unemployment fund surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); reduced aggregate of 22.80% for the first EUR 175,000 of taxable profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; 0% where the Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies to a qualifying EU corporate shareholder; treaty reductions typically lower the rate to 0–5%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Model 830 return must be filed by 31 July following the close of the financial year; the ACD routinely grants extensions of up to 6 months on a justified request

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File are required under Luxembourg law (EU TPD implementation) when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; below this threshold the ACD may request contemporaneous documentation for material cross-border related-party transactions, generally where such transactions exceed EUR 500,000 annually in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the offshore style, but permanent residence and treaty benefits depend on substantive local presence: place of effective management in Luxembourg, local board decisions, operational staff, premises, and DEMPE for IP holding. The ACD applies general anti-abuse rules and EU ATAD principles; a SARL functioning as a pure mail-box owner of a webnovel platform will be denied Luxembourg residence and treaty shelter.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Fraud and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg criminal tax law, deliberate filing of false returns is punishable by imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, plus fraudulent assessment surcharges of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax and 10% per annum late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in filing false returns or drowning the SARL in debt are subject to individual criminal liability, joint and several payment of the evaded tax if the company becomes insolvent, and procedures may include travel bans and confiscation of assets under the Luxembourg Code of Criminal Procedure.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard the Luxembourg entity as a sham, reassess profits at the foreign de facto parent level, impose 100% bad-faith penalties, deny DTA benefits and EU Directives, and apply the ATAD / GAAR anti-abuse provisions, resulting in retroactive taxation at a punitive rate.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell SARL can be held civilly liable for the company's tax liabilities under the doctrine of déclaration frauduleuse, may be subjected to personal corporate bans, travel restrictions, and criminal penalties for facilitating tax evasion if the shell is used to hide beneficial ownership.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Luxembourg tax law (Art. 56 LIR) with reallocation of profits to Luxembourg, late-payment interest of 10% per annum, and penalties ranging from 10% to 50% of the additional tax assessed; failure to maintain an adequate TP file may itself attract a separate documentation fine.

Director personal liability: Consultant-level fault (faute lourde) of directors in approving non-arm's length arrangements can make directors personally liable to the tax authorities for the related tax insufficiencies, and criminal sanctions apply if the transfer pricing scheme is used to commit money laundering or intentional tax evasion.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg laws on the RBE register and AML/CFT, failure to register or to keep accurate beneficial ownership information is a criminal offense punishable by fines of up to EUR 25,000 per violation; AML supervision fines for commercial entities can escalate to EUR 2 million or 10% of annual turnover, and the register may publish sanctions.

Director personal liability: Company directors and statutory managers are personally criminally liable for deliberate non-disclosure of the beneficial owner, with imprisonment up to 3 years; they may also be prohibited from exercising management functions and be subjected to travel bans during any money laundering investigation where platform payment flows are considered criminal proceeds.

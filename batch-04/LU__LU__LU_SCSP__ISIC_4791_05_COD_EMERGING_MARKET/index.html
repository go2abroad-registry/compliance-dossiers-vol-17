Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), RCS Luxembourg, 1, boulevard du Prince Henri, L-1724 Luxembourg; e-filing portal: eRegistre (https://eregistre.lbr.lu).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended; specifically Articles 1 through 8, 13-1 through 13-22, and 25-27 relating to the Special Limited Partnership (SCSp); also the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 January 2017 on the electronic publication of companies and associations (RESA).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif / Partnership Agreement of the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp), filed by way of the standard registration application form to the RCS and followed by mandatory extraction and publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

SCSp has no statutory minimum capital and no minimum paid-up capital requirement; no capital escrow account is required. Contributions may consist of cash, kind, or any agreed assets, and the general partner may structure capital contributions freely in the partnership agreement; the limited partners’ liability is limited to their subscribed contributions.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: SCSp has no statutory minimum capital and no minimum paid-up capital requirement; no capital escrow account is required. Contributions may consist of cash, kind, or any agreed assets, and the general partner may structure capital contributions freely in the partnership agreement; the limited partners’ liability is limited to their subscribed contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD – Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified e-portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

Foreign and domestic entities register for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) via form 645 filed electronically with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu after completion of RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) filing. A transparent SCSp is not itself a VAT taxpayer unless it holds a fixed establishment in Luxembourg; the general partner (commandité) or designated operator procures the Luxembourg tax ID and, if non-resident, appoints a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT compliance. The 13-digit national identification number (matricule) is auto-issued upon RCS incorporation and serves as the corporate taxpayer reference.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign and domestic entities register for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) via form 645 filed electronically with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu after completion of RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) filing. A transparent SCSp is not itself a VAT taxpayer unless it holds a fixed establishment in Luxembourg; the general partner (commandité) or designated operator procures the Luxembourg tax ID and, if non-resident, appoints a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT compliance. The 13-digit national identification number (matricule) is auto-issued upon RCS incorporation and serves as the corporate taxpayer reference.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Article 168bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), an SCSp may irrevocably elect to become subject to corporate income tax (opaque treatment), thereby losing fiscal transparency; absent this election, default pass-through transparency applies and investors are taxed as if they earned items directly. No US-style check-the-box regime exists; the Article 168bis option plus partnership-level elections under Luxembourg tax consolidation rules provide the functional equivalent. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD (Articles 168ter–168sexies LIR) override any double-non-taxation resulting from such elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for the small-enterprise exemption under Article 57 LTVL (no VAT registration required); EUR 100,000 EU distance-selling threshold for intra-EU B2C supplies under Article 41 LTVL; no registration threshold applies to a foreign supplier with a Luxembourg fixed establishment, and the SCSp engaged in mail-order sales with local logistics/COD presence typically requires VAT registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export of goods under Article 46(1) LTVL where physical dispatch or transport of goods by the seller or a third party on its behalf leaves Luxembourg and the customs territory of the EU for a non-EU destination (e.g., COD shipments to the DRC/emerging-market customers); title transfer is irrelevant to zero-rating. Ancillary logistics, freight, handling, insurance and COD collection-agent services directly related to the exported goods are equally zero-rated under Article 46(2) LTVL, provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days of supply and supporting evidence is retained.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD – Single Administrative Document, or electronic IE-507 exit summary from the Luxembourg customs system)
  • Transport document evidencing exit from EU territory (CMR consignment note, Air Waybill, or Bill of Lading with customs stamp/tracking)
  • Commercial invoice in the name of the non-EU buyer specifying the shipment terms, Incoterms, and 0% VAT line
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire confirmation from the emerging-market customer matching the invoice amount and FX conversion record
  • Logistics service provider's signed proof of delivery (POD) and cash-on-delivery settlement report evidencing actual collection and repatriation of proceeds

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing via Peppol BIS (structured XML) for all Luxembourg domestic taxable supplies from 1 January 2026, as enacted by the law of 5 July 2024; no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) is currently operational in Luxembourg. Invoices exported to non-EU emerging-market customers must be issued as paper or PDF unless Peppol is contractually agreed; the AEDT retains full audit access to issuance and transmission records.

Under Articles 63–68 LTVL, the AEDT may retroactively assess 17% VAT on exports lacking valid proof of departure, plus default interest of 0.8% per commenced month and administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 per deficiency. Fraudulent zero-rating documentation triggers criminal penalties under Article 67 LTVL (imprisonment of 1–5 years and/or fines up to EUR 25,000), automatic disallowance of related input VAT credits, and joint liability of the SCSp general partner and logistics agent; customs-evasion sanctions and exclusion from simplified export regimes may also apply where the 90-day export proof or 180-day FX repatriation deadline is missed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is part of the euro area and applies unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63–65). No exchange-control approvals are required for profit remittance, partnership distributions, or liquidation proceeds. The practical constraints are AML/CFT verification, EU financial sanctions, and mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Convertibility risk in COD source countries is a commercial treasury risk rather than a Luxembourg legal restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists under Luxembourg or EU law. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or any convertible currency indefinitely. All export receivables must be recognised under Luxembourg GAAP, and foreign-exchange gains or losses are recorded at the transaction date or period-end spot rate. No surrender timeline or fixed conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the conduct and prudential supervisor; foreign-exchange and capital-movement competences are vested in the Eurosystem framework and the European Single Market.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for low-value, high-volume COD collections if the provider is an EU-authorised electronic money institution, such as Airwallex (Luxembourg) S.A. licensed by the CSSF. The SCSp may hold segregated payment accounts and use virtual IBANs, but the EMI will not provide a dedicated capital escrow function. Legal constraints include the general partner's unlimited liability, UBO disclosure for all partners holding 25% or more, concentration risk in emerging-market corridors, and the practical need to maintain a parallel commercial bank relationship for hard-currency settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate for Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); municipal rates vary by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by a tax-transparent SCSp to its partners because the SCSp is not a dividend-paying collective in the CIT sense. The general Luxembourg statutory dividend withholding tax of 15% applies where the payer is an opaque resident capital company; it does not apply to SCSp profit allocations. If the SCSp is not transparent for treaty purposes, no domestic WHT is levied at source in Luxembourg on allocations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers the Luxembourg Master File and Country-by-Country reporting obligation under BEPS Action 13; the Local File is prepared and retained for all material controlled transactions. There is no de minimis threshold for audit; if requested by the Administration des contributions directes, the SCSp must provide the Local File within 15 days. Controlled transactions in the COD e-commerce business—logistics fees, cash collection commissions, Airwallex processing, and trademark royalties—must be documented and benchmarked at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply OFC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, domestic substance-over-form, ATAD GAAR, Luxembourg's abuse-of-law case law, and the OECD 'no PE/letterbox' principle require real substance: the SCSp must maintain its registered office, board/managers' meetings, custody of accounts, strategic decisions, and key COD operational functions in Luxembourg. In mail-order retail with COD settlement, the Luxembourg entity should perform or control order-taking, COD collection reconciliation and risk management; the use of local logistics remittance agents should not be structured to create a PE of the foreign partner. Merely holding an SCSp without functions, employees and office facilities is a fatal red flag.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax increase of up to 100% of evaded corporate income tax and municipal business tax, plus late interest; criminal prosecution can result in fines up to EUR 125,000 and imprisonment up to three years; judgment may be published.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners who knowingly sign false returns or pre-judicially strip assets can be personally assessed to the tax shortfall; the veil is pierced for deliberate fraud, allowing seizure of personal assets, travel bans and custodial sentences.
Letter-box / Shell Company and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may disregard the SCSp, reattribute its income under substance-over-form and impose a 10-100% tax increase; foreign tax authorities may apply CFC rules and deny treaty benefits; the RCS may strike the entity for false declarations.

Director personal liability: A general partner or manager that knowingly lent the SCSp to a foreign conduit arrangement may be jointly and severally liable for the recomputed tax, because the corporate personality of the SCSp is disregarded; this can extend to civil restitution and director disqualification.
AML / CFT / Payment Compliance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of turnover for legal persons under Luxembourg AML legislation; freezing of accounts and revocation of electronic money/payment institution permissions; criminal money laundering sanctions with imprisonment up to five years for the directing mind.

Director personal liability: Managers and AML compliance officers can be held personally liable for AML penalties, prohibited from managing financial institutions, subject to travel alerts during investigation and, on conviction, imprisoned and ordered to confiscate assets equal to the laundered amounts.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to an arm's length taxable profit, with statutory late interest and tax increase of 10-50%; if the adjustment results from gross negligence, the tax increase can reach 100%; double tax and economic double taxation may also arise.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish unrealistic transfer prices or direct the concealment of related-party contracts can be personally liable for the resulting tax assessments; the court may impose civil restitution and enjoin continued foreign travel until the assessment is secured.

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the authority of the Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 318-1 to 318-22 (special limited partnership); Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 January 2003 and Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS).

Formation / constitutional filings

SCSp constitution deed (acte de constitution) executed by private writing or notarial deed, submitted through the RCS e-Registration portal; the RCS registration extract constitutes the operative statutory filing instrument.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; official portals: myguichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors forming a Luxembourg SCSp must first register constitutional documents with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a local notary or legal agent, obtain the national identification number (matricule), then apply for the NIF and VAT number via MyGuichet.lu using Forms 102/845; a non-resident SCSp is also required to register for VAT without delay once it makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg or acquires a taxable nexus. Registration is completed when economic activity commences or when a Luxembourg VAT-obligated transaction is first carried out.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign investors forming a Luxembourg SCSp must first register constitutional documents with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a local notary or legal agent, obtain the national identification number (matricule), then apply for the NIF and VAT number via MyGuichet.lu using Forms 102/845; a non-resident SCSp is also required to register for VAT without delay once it makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg or acquires a taxable nexus. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available; Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent by statute for corporate income tax and net wealth tax. Available elections include: (i) application to form or join a VAT group (assujetti unique) with other Luxembourg taxable persons; (ii) waiver of the small-enterprise VAT exemption; and (iii) election to apply the Ordinary VAT regime for rental/receivables activities. There is no hybrid mismatch election for this partnership unless ATAD/CFC rules are separately implemented at partner level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg-resident small undertakings: €35,000 annual taxable turnover (excl. VAT) is the exemption/registration threshold under the small business scheme; non-resident SCSp has no turnover threshold and must register for VAT as soon as it supplies taxable goods or services in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT if goods are physically transported outside the EU and proved by a customs export declaration; intra-EU supplies can be zero-rated when the customer supplies a valid EU VAT identification number and the EC Sales List is filed; certain lobbying and policy advisory services performed for non-EU or foreign business clients fall outside Luxembourg VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules, making Luxembourg VAT inapplicable and any input VAT recoverable under the supplier's normal business activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API extraction, or digital stamping applies to B2B VAT invoices. Electronic invoicing to Luxembourg public contracting bodies is mandatory via Peppol BIS 3.0/EN16931. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted but optional, and VAT returns are filed separately through the normal AEDT electronic portal.

The Luxembourg VAT administration may require complete export evidence (customs declarations, transport contracts, B/L/AWB, bank transfer and payment advisory) before sustaining a 0% rate; failure to substantiate leads to retrospective VAT clawback at 17%, monthly default interest up to 0.8%, administrative fines for incomplete or inaccurate records, and criminal penalties for falsification or VAT fraud of up to €250,000 and/or five years' imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone Member State; the euro is fully convertible. Both current-account flows and, save for EU/EEA gold-plating measures, capital-account flows are liberalised for cross-border transactions arising from the freedom of movement of capital under EU primary law. Profit remittances from a transparent Luxembourg SCSp are not subject to withholding tax at the partnership level; distributions to non-resident partners are free from exchange-control restrictions and may be made in EUR or any freely convertible currency, subject only to standard bank AML/CTF verification and applicable tax declarations. No prior central-bank approval is required for capital subscriptions, retainer fee repatriation, or liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export receipts or service-policy advisory fees. Foreign-currency accounts may be maintained freely at CSSF-regulated credit institutions; BCL statistical balance-of-payments reporting is limited to national statistical posting and does not restrict the holding or disposition of foreign-exchange receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), the Ministry of Finance, and the European System of Central Banks / ECB framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Luxembourg-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU/EEA passporting EMIs can support SEPA/euro settlement, multi-currency transaction accounts, and cross-border wire issuance, but they cannot lawfully provide full commercial credit, complex escrow, or investment-grade treasury instruments. For a Luxembourg SCSp engaged in cross-border trade-policy advisory and government-affairs lobbying, a foreign/EU EMI is suitable only for low-value operating disbursements and petty-cash management; it is not an adequate substitute for a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution with respect to capital escrow, large retainer collection, or FX hedging under bank confirmations.

Defense evidence: A complete beneficial-ownership matrix identifying every natural-person ultimate owner, each partner's economic interest and control rights; a certified copy of the partnership agreement (contrat de société), all side letters and deeds amending the agreement; a Luxembourg legal opinion confirming the SCSp's valid formation, capacity, and economic substance; and a signed AML/CTF declaration for the general partner and each direct limited partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-Luxembourg partners must be routed through a CSSF-regulated credit institution via SWIFT MT103 (single investor) or MT202/MT202COV (treasury-concentrated settlement) with strict compliance preparation. The remitter must use the BCL statistical purpose code appropriate to a direct equity injection (typically D610 for direct investment capital or, for certain fund-architecture flows, D620) and must include in the narrative the SCSp's full legal name, the Luxembourg RCS registration number, and the phrase 'partnership capital contribution / no consideration invoiced'. Before the first wire, the account officer and compliance team require the final approved KYC file, an incoming-funds questionnaire, and a copy of the board/general-partner resolution approving the subscription. Payments from unregulated third-party payers or from jurisdictions on the FATF grey list are blocked unless a prior case-by-case protective review is completed and documented.For recurring advisory-fee settlements to EU/EEA beneficiary bank accounts, the platform rail is SEPA Credit Transfer (X1/XML pain.001) using the beneficiary's IBAN and BIC, with the required purpose and reference text for VAT/accounting traceability. For non-EUR and rest-of-world payments, the applicable rail is SWIFT MT103 via a correspondent banking network; where the disbursement supports trade documents or subcontracting of foreign trade-policy services, a commercial irrevocable letter of credit may be issued under UCP600/ISBP745 to ensure documentary compliance. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined statutory rate (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% unemployment fund contribution calculated as 7% of the 17% IRC + 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City; municipal rate varies by commune from 6.75% to 11.9%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; 0% applies under the participation exemption regime where a qualifying EU parent or a corporate partner holding at least 10% for 12 months receives the dividend; for a transparent SCSp, ordinary profit allocations to partners are partnership distributions, not dividends, and are generally outside the WHT net.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 31 December of the calendar year following the relevant fiscal year (Art. 161 LIR), i.e., within 12 months after the fiscal year-end; the ACD commonly grants a two-to-three-month extension on written request. Quarterly prepayments are due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Identification RCS registration number, legal form (SCSp), registered office address, fiscal year-end date, and VAT identification number if separately registered. Indicate name and tax identification number of the appointed Luxembourg tax representative; declare the transparent status so that no entity-level CIT is assessed on pass-through income. Failure to file: administrative fine up to EUR 3,750 per return (Art. 224 LIR) and immediate official assessment (taxation d'office) with a 10% surcharge on the assessed tax where the return is omitted, incomplete, or imprecise. Late payment: statutory interest at 1.2% per quarter on arrears. Deliberate false returns escalate to tax fraud (Art. 226 LIR): fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg Local File / Master File documentation is triggered when aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 10,000,000 for goods or EUR 1,000,000 for services, financial transactions, and management fee recharges; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code; substance is enforced through LIR Art. 168 anti-abuse provisions, the ATAD general anti-avoidance rule, administrative TP circulars requiring a functions-risks-assets analysis, and the MLI principal-purpose test. A bare SCSp without a Luxembourg-based general partner, local staff, or substantive board decision-making is recharacterized as a letterbox shell, with treaty benefits denied, the corporate veil pierced, and source taxation reimposed at the 15% dividend WHT rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud (Art. 226 LIR)

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the entire evaded tax in cash; interest at 1.2% per quarter; criminal fine up to EUR 50,000; imprisonment up to 5 years (Art. 226 LIR); publication of the judgment and prohibition from holding public office in Luxembourg.

Director personal liability: The gérant or general partner may be declared joint debtor for the unpaid tax where fraud or gross negligence is proven (veil-piercing); travel bans, asset freezes, and preventive detention may be ordered pending criminal proceedings.

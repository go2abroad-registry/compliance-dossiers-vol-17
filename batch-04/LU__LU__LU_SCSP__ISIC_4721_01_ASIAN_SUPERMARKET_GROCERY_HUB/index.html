Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for income tax (partners are taxed directly on their allocable share); for VAT purposes the SCSp remains an independent taxable person in respect of its grocery-retail activity; territorial scope is worldwide taxation for Luxembourg-resident partners and Luxembourg-source taxation for non-resident partners.

Available election forms

Check-the-box-style opacity election under the 15 December 2015 partnership tax law (effective 2017; Art. 4(2) LIR) permitting entity-level treatment where all partners are themselves subject to tax and where international classification symmetry so requires; ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply where tax classification differs across partner jurisdictions; no opacity election is available in respect of VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual turnover (exclusive of VAT) triggering the small-business exemption under Art. 28 LTV; registration is mandatory above this ceiling and is required regardless of turnover for any non-established trader engaged in taxable retail sales in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to physical exports of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU within 90 days of transfer of ownership, provided the exporter retains complete transport and commercial evidence; zero-rating for intra-EU dispatches to registered business customers requires a valid EU VAT ID of the recipient with reverse charge in the destination state; related cross-border retail services to non-EU recipients may be zero-rated where use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU; the SCSp as a registered VAT person recovers related input VAT through its periodic returns.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronically lodged full/summary SAD accepted by Luxembourg Customs (DUAS/PLDA system) evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU
  • Bill of Lading — or Air Waybill/CMR transport document showing dispatch to a non-EU destination within the 90-day statutory export window
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank and exchange-control evidence confirming receipt of export sales proceeds from the foreign counterparty, retained for audit substantiation

Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing in Factur-X 3.0 format through the Peppol network with phased public-sector deadlines through 2026; no mandatory real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC), fiscal clearance or digital stamping applies to grocery retail; periodic VAT returns are filed electronically via the e-TVA/myGuichet portal on monthly, quarterly or annual cycles, with SAF-T audit files available on ACD demand.

AED and Luxembourg Customs jointly audit export files (customs declarations, transport logs, bank/FX payment traces); unjustified zero-rating is clawed back at the applicable rate (8% for grocery items or 17% standard) plus 0.9% monthly late-payment interest and an administrative penalty of up to 10% of the evaded VAT in negligence cases; deliberate falsification of export documents attracts criminal fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to five years, with a 5-year statutory reassessment window extended where fraud or forged documentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of current-account and capital-account items applies pursuant to Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EU free movement of capital framework; Luxembourg imposes no exchange control licensing, no convertibility restrictions and no segregation of retained earnings or blocked-account rules for either the SCSp or its foreign partners. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are unrestricted for Eurozone, EEA and third-country counterparties that are not subject to EU/UN targeted financial sanctions. The only practical frictions are (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical declaration by the beneficiary/paying bank for cross-border payments of at least EUR 12,500, (ii) EU/UN/HMT sanction screening of counterparties, and (iii) CSSF anti-money-laundering source-of-funds due diligence on capital subscriptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists in Luxembourg or at EU level. Exporters and retail importers are not required to repatriate foreign sales proceeds within any timeline, and no mandatory percentage of export receipts must be exchanged into Euro. Proceeds may be retained indefinitely in EUR or freely convertible foreign currency accounts held at Luxembourg or other EEA-licensed credit institutions, subject solely to the banks' BoP reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 per wire and standard invoice/transport documentary retention for anti-money-laundering and tax audits.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), as the national Central Bank and National Competent Authority reporting into the Eurosystem/European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institution licensing, AML/CFT compliance, and market conduct for banks and payment/e-money institutions in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, ancillary use only. A Luxembourg- or other EEA-authorised EMI passporting under PSD2 can provide multi-currency IBANs, API-driven treasury screens, card acquiring for the grocery-retail entity (POS/Interac) and expedited FX for incidental balances. However, an EMI is not licensed for credit/escrow, cannot issue commercial import letters of credit, often caps account balances below institutional capital-injection levels, and may decline a Luxembourg-domiciled, non-resident-owned SCSp because of opaque-partnership KYC friction. Consequently the EMI shall be limited to a float/acquiring overlay while BCL-licensed Tier-1 banks carry the principal liquidity and LC rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg City rate, comprising 15% corporate income tax (IRC), 1.05% unemployment solidarity surcharge (contribution au fonds pour l'emploi), and 6.75% municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal); the exact effective burden deviates by commune because the municipal multiplier ranges from 200% to 300%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Luxembourg imposes a statutory 15% dividend withholding tax on distributions of profits by a Luxembourg tax-resident corporate entity. An SCSp is fiscally transparent and generally has no legal personality, so profit allocations to partners are not treated as dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at the SCSp level. If, however, the SCSp sits above a Luxembourg operating SARL that distributes grocery-hub profits, that upstream dividend is subject to the full 15% WHT unless the parent-subsidiary participation exemption or a treaty-reduced rate is granted.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

A Luxembourg fiscal year ending 31 December N must be declared by 31 May N+1 in strict law. Where the return is filed electronically and the ACD has not served an assessment notice, commercial practice generally tolerates submission up to 31 December N+1; professional advisers should not rely on this without written ACD confirmation because late-filing surcharges start to run after the statutory date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Luxembourg constituent entity must prepare and file the Master File and Local File where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeded EUR 750 million in the preceding fiscal year, and must file Country-by-Country Reporting through the Luxembourg ACD for the same EUR 750 million threshold. For the Asian supermarket grocery hub operating below this threshold, no automatic TP file is due, but Article 56 LIR and the ACD Transfer Pricing Circular (Circular LIR 56/1 of 28 January 2016) require that all intra-group purchasing, centralized procurement services, and management-fee arrangements with affiliated Asian suppliers be contemporaneously documented, benchmarked, and disclosed to the tax auditor upon request within a reasonable period.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code or a separate ESR filing regime for SCSp vehicles. Instead, substance requirements are embedded in the GAAR under Article 6 AO, the tax abuse doctrine in Article 56 LIR, and the ACD Circular LIR 102/2 applicable to holding and partnership structures. A supermarket SCSp inherently satisfies substance when it employs store staff under Luxembourg contracts, leases or owns a commercial premise, keeps physical inventory of imported Asian condiments and vegetables, holds POS and banking records in Luxembourg, and takes management decisions locally. Absent such operations, the ACD may disregard the SCSp as an abusive intermediary, deny treaty benefits, and allocate the grocery margin directly to the foreign partner's head office.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Deliberate Tax Under-Declaration and Cash Sales Suppression

Statutory sanction: The ACD reassesses income at the aggregate 24.94% Luxembourg City rate, applies a late-filing or concealed-income surcharge of up to 10%, charges monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%, and assesses criminal tax fraud penalties. In a criminal prosecution for deliberate concealment, the court may impose a fine of up to double the evaded tax and imprisonment of the directing partner for up to five years.

Director personal liability: The director or managing partner who signs a false Form 600 or directs cash suppression is personally exposed to criminal liability and civil restitution for the evaded corporate income tax and municipal business tax. Piercing of the corporate veil is available against the SCSp's general partner when the SCSp is a transparent conduit and the true beneficiary controls the retail operation; courts may additionally impose a travel ban and disqualify the manager from holding Luxembourg directorships.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The ACD can disregard the SCSp under Article 6 AO and Article 56 LIR, reattribute all Luxembourg-source income to the true foreign enterprise, deny the treaty reduced rates, impose the full 15% withholding tax on actual or recharacterized distributions, and issue penalty assessments plus monthly default interest. The Luxembourg court may impose administrative fines and, for cross-border conduit arrangements, refer the case to the tax criminal chamber.

Director personal liability: The nominal director of a shell SCSp faces joint and several liability for the corporate income tax, interest, and penalties when it is shown that the director was a mere accommodation party. Civil restitution can extend to the value of assets diverted out of Luxembourg, and the director can be barred from operating a Luxembourg-registered business for a fixed term.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing of Asian Grocery Imports

Statutory sanction: The ACD makes a primary transfer-pricing adjustment restoring the grocery hub profit to the arm's-length resale price margin, adds a secondary adjustment treating the excess paid as a deemed distribution subject to 15% withholding tax, and levies interest at 0.6% per month. Failure to produce the contemporaneous Local File documentation entitles the tax auditor to presume income and to impose a documentation-related penalty alongside the tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: The JV director or procurement officer who knowingly signs inflated supplier agreements can be held personally liable for the levied withholding tax and penalty where Luxembourg courts find bad faith participation. In aggravated schemes involving invoices for goods never delivered, the directors face criminal liability for forgery and tax fraud, with cumulative custodial sentences.

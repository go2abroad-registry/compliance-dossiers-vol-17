Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital. It must comprise at least one general partner (commandité) with unlimited liability and at least one limited partner (commanditaire). Contributions may be in cash or in kind and are freely determined by the partners; Luxembourg law imposes neither full pre-payment nor a deposit into a blocked escrow account for SCSp registration. The constitutive deed must record the value and payment terms of each subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the communal fire inspection service; structural load verification for blast freezers, steam lines, and production machinery signed by an accredited structural engineer; workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including occupational risk assessment, electrical safety inspection, and an employee health surveillance program approved by the occupational physician.

Paid-in capital rule: The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital. It must comprise at least one general partner (commandité) with unlimited liability and at least one limited partner (commanditaire). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income/corporate taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT registration, returns, and indirect tax compliance. Official portals: Guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu (electronic filings), Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for the RCS matricule, and the AED eTVA electronic declaration system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Establish the SCSp by notarized limited partnership agreement and publish the constitutive act with the RCS via the Luxembourg Business Registers platform to obtain the 14-digit national business registration number (matricule). Step 2: Submit the AED VAT identification declaration (form 44) electronically through MyGuichet.lu, accompanied by the RCS extract, partnership agreement, and identity documentation of the general partner and limited partners. Step 3: AED issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) normally within 2–4 weeks; a non-EU-established partner or foreign taxable person carrying out taxable supplies in Luxembourg must simultaneously appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (Statute of 12 February 1979) unless covered by the non-established Union OSS / Import OSS schemes. Step 4: The SCSp must also register as an intra-Community operator (DEB identification) for goods movements and file recapitulative statements. ACD and AED compliance is coordinated through the single RCS matricule for all domestic filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is fiscally transparent (look-through) by default; profits, deductions, and VAT-relevant activities are attributed directly to general and limited partners, whether resident, EU, or non-resident. Territorial scope: Luxembourg taxation arises only at the partner level on the SCSp net income allocable to each partner; non-resident partners are taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source partnership income and are generally protected by the applicable double tax treaty. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is fiscally transparent (look-through) by default; profits, deductions, and VAT-relevant activities are attributed directly to general and limited partners, whether resident, EU, or non-resident. Territorial scope: Luxembourg taxation arises only at the partner level on the SCSp net income allocable to each partner; non-resident partners are taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source partnership income and are generally protected by the applicable double tax treaty. The entity itself is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax; no minimum statutory capital is required.

Available election forms

Statutory check-the-box election: the SCSp may elect, under Luxembourg income tax law (LIR Article 182bis), to be taxed as a capital company (opaque regime) where at least one partner is subject to corporate income tax or where all partners hold their interests as business assets; the election binds the partnership for at least five years and must be submitted with the ACD before the end of the first taxable year. US-style check-the-box classifications are respected for federal tax characterization but remain subject to Luxembourg hybrid-mismatch rules transposing ATAD (LIR Articles 168quinquies and 168sexies), which may recharacterize the SCSp or deny deductions where the partner jurisdiction treats it as opaque; single-entity tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) is not available to transparent SCSp vehicles, but Luxembourg VAT grouping under Article 62 of the VAT Law remains available where joint VAT liability and financial, economic, and organizational links are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT small undertaking exemption under the Luxembourg VAT Law), exclusive of VAT; the exemption is not available to non-resident taxable persons, taxable persons making exempt financial/real-estate supplies with input VAT recovery, or supplies of goods destined for importation; intra-EU distance-selling registration threshold for foreign established suppliers is EUR 10,000 (no threshold for imports or for services subject to the reverse charge); the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) relief applies to low-value consignments of goods up to EUR 150 imported into Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of frozen dim sum and dumplings physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the factory holds conclusive proof of removal from the EU territory within the statutory time frame (generally evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents). Sales to VAT-identified EU business customers qualify as zero-rated intra-Community supplies under Article 46 LTV with EC Sales List reporting and validation of the customer's valid LU/other EU VAT number; services directly connected with the exported goods (e.g., freezing, packaging, and processing work performed on movable goods destined for non-EU customers) may also qualify for zero rating where the related goods are dispatched outside the EU. Proof of export must be retained and available at the time the VAT return is filed; the AED may request further evidence, and the zero rate is denied where the goods are released for free circulation within the EU or consumed domestically.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document released via Luxembourg Customs / the EU Ex-Dat electronic export system evidencing exit from the EU)
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (maritime), CMR International Consignment Note (road), or Air Waybill (air) confirming physical dispatch from Luxembourg territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and warehouse dispatch records demonstrating consignment identity and revenue traceability
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 / Letter of Credit settlement documentation evidencing repatriation of export value (benchmark evidence used by the AED in substantiation audits)

No national real-time fiscal-clearance server exists in Luxembourg (contrast with Italy's SDI), but electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions since 1 November 2021 using the PEPPOL network and the European standard EN 16931 (LuxML format). A nationwide mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime was announced by the Luxembourg Government and is expected to be phased in by 2026, requiring structured electronic invoices via PEPPOL-compliant service providers. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) must be filed electronically through the AED eTVA/MyGuichet.lu portal; the DEB (Intrastat) declarations apply to goods movements where applicable; digital stamping or pre-clearance of invoices is currently not imposed on food-exporting factories.

The AED applies risk-based audit algorithms to export zero-rating claims and is empowered to demand the full documentation bundle within a specified response period; failure to substantiate the EU exit leads to retrospective re-invoicing of VAT at the domestic food rate (3%) or the standard rate (17%), together with recovery of input VAT disallowed, late-payment interest of 0.8% per month (or part thereof), and administrative fines. Fraudulent zero-rating or document falsification constitutes VAT fraud under Articles 59 and 62 of the Luxembourg VAT Law of 12 February 1979 and may be prosecuted criminally with fines, imprisonment, and publication or disqualification measures; the law prescribes an extended 10-year limitation period for fraudulent tax evasion and joint and several liability may be asserted against the SCSp's general partner and any tax representative. In serious cases, the judicial authorities may also trigger EU follow-up via the Eurofisc network and OLAF cross-border cooperation, exposing the exporter to parallel administrative and criminal clawback actions in multiple member states.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; no exchange-control authorisation is required for cross-border payments, trade settlements, or profit remittances. The SCSp is fiscally transparent, so profit distributions to partners are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, provided the partnership does not carry on a Luxembourg commercial activity that triggers entity-level taxation; residual Luxembourg-source income benefits from treaty-based reductions where the partner is resident in a treaty jurisdiction. Capital outflows are unrestricted, and repatriation hurdles are limited to standard anti-money-laundering (AML) due diligence and statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Luxembourg or the Eurozone; export receipts may be held freely in any currency, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. EU law prohibits exchange controls, so the SCSp is not required to convert, repatriate, or deposit export receipts within any prescribed timeline; only bank-level AML screening and the BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds apply, and the tax authorities require standard commercial documentation for the underlying frozen-food export transactions.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); monetary policy competence is vested in the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); there is no separate foreign exchange administration because Luxembourg is a Eurozone member and applies the EU free-movement-of-capital regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for routine operational collections and supplier payments through CSSF-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting under EU PSD2/EMD2; however, EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit (MT700), cannot hold funds as deposits protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL), are ineligible for credit intermediation, and operate under e-money issuance caps imposed by their CSSF licence; therefore, Low viability for capital escrow and trade-finance treasury functions. Legal constraint: safeguarding rules require e-money funds to be held in segregated pooled accounts, so the SCSp should confine EMI usage to sub-threshold operational flows and maintain its primary treasury and escrow accounts at a tier-1 credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from each partner's own bank account to the SCSp operational account, using a purpose code designating direct investment / capital contribution (Luxembourg balance-of-payments statistical code for equity injections) and a reference narrative citing the capital contribution agreement. The receiving bank may request a BCL balance-of-payments declaration for cross-border financial transactions above applicable statistical thresholds. No BCL approval is required; however, the transfer file must include the RBE extract, partner resolutions, the notarised contribution deed, a verification letter confirming the partner's ownership of the remitting account, and source-of-wealth documentation to satisfy CSSF AML directives.Two primary clearing rails are deployed for the industrial quick-freezing operation: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfers for direct TT settlement with raw-material suppliers of frozen seafood, flour, seasonings, and packaging materials in Asia and the Americas; and (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707 for documentary trade settlement of high-value commodity purchase contracts. For eurozone suppliers, ordinary SEPA Credit Transfers or instant SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT Inst) provide the most cost-effective rail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp entity level due to fiscal transparency; for Luxembourg-resident corporate partners, combined CIT + solidarity surcharge + municipal business tax = 24.94% in Luxembourg City (range approx. 20.99%-24.94% depending on commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by an SCSp to its partners because the SCSp is tax transparent and distributions are not classified as dividends. Dividends paid by a Luxembourg capital company to individuals (if applicable) are subject to 15% withholding tax, but the SCSp structure avoids this layer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Transfer Pricing Documentation (Master File and Local File) applies where the consolidated group turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50 million and intercompany transactions exceed: EUR 1 million for services, EUR 2 million for financial transactions, EUR 5 million for purchases of goods (e.g., raw materials for dumplings), and EUR 250,000 for intangible property/royalties. In the absence of a local file, the Luxembourg tax authority may estimate taxable profit under Article 56 LIR.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) similar to UAE, but the substance doctrine is embedded in the GAAR (Article 6 AEIL), the ‘abuse of law’ concept, and Article 9 BEPS hybrid rules. An SCSp operating an industrial frozen-dumpling factory must demonstrate real manufacturing presence in Luxembourg: qualified personnel, production facilities, warehouse and inventory at risk, board/manager decision-making, and proportionate working capital in the SCSp. A pure ‘letter-box’ SCSp distributing all profits to a zero-tax jurisdiction will be disregarded for treaty purposes and exposed to fiscal recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment for 10 years, withdrawal of treaty benefits, default interest at 0.6%/month, fine up to EUR 250,000 (or 2x tax evaded), prosecution under the General Tax Law and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and gérants are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; Luxembourg courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and custodial sentences up to 5 years.
Sham Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may disregard the SCSp and attribute the income directly to the ultimate controlling entity; recharacterization as an abusive arrangement; penalties of 20% to 40% of the tax underassessed; loss of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Promoters and managers face personal liability for unpaid taxes under the fiscal unity doctrine, potential removal from commercial register, and professional disqualification.
Anti-Money Laundering / PFS Obligations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million and possible criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for deliberate laundering; confiscation of assets; administrative sanction of revocation of business license for the factory under article 115 of the amended law of 12 November 2004.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally prosecuted as beneficial owners; travel bans and asset freezes are standard; civil liability towards the State for damage caused by laundering.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income to arm's length, late payment interest, 10% penalty for failure to file TP documentation, and potential criminal penalty if documents are falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the additional tax if they signed intercompany contracts that were artificial and they failed to obtain independent valuation.
Failure to File Annual Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Ex-officio assessment, 10% increase on the assessed tax, fixed fine of up to EUR 7,500, monthly interest 0.6%, and administrative injunction imposing closure of the commercial kitchen.

Director personal liability: The gérant is personally liable for the fine and for any tax shortfall if the default is willful; habitual default may trigger director disqualification under the Commercial Code.

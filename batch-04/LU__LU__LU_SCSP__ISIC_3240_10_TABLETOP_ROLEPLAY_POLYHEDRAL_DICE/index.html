Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 100-1 to 100-15 establishing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); supplemented by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) regulations and the Grand-Ducal regulation on fees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration (requête d'immatriculation) filed with the RCS, together with the partnership deed (statuts / acte de société), the declaration of conformity, and an extract for publication in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and excise; Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for business registration and matricule number; portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.public.lu

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must first file a certified copy of its constitutive documents, a power of attorney, and evidence of a registered office in Luxembourg with the RCS to obtain the RCS business registration number (matricule). After that registration, the entity applies to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number, submitting the RCS extract, identification of the local fiscal representative where no permanent establishment exists, and, for activity involving physical goods, an EORI number. Registration is completed electronically through MyGuichet.lu; a VAT number is generally issued within 10 business days if no customs or excise complications arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax at partnership level; each partner is taxed on their share of income based on their personal/resident status. For VAT and indirect tax purposes, the SCSp may still be treated as a taxable person when it supplies goods or services in Luxembourg, including export supplies.

Available election forms

Pursuant to Art. 170 LIR, a Luxembourg partnership (including an SCSp) may elect to become fully subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax, a treatment then generally applied to all partners unless revoked. Hybrid mismatch elections under the EU ATAD framework are constrained by Luxembourg domestic rules on classification of transparent entities and may be examined in an advance tax ruling from the Luxembourg tax authorities. There is no open 'check-the-box' regime, but the transparent default can be confirmed through a formal ruling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established businesses; non-established sellers may be subject to lower EU distance-selling or VAT OSS thresholds. The threshold applies to the taxable person's aggregate supplies; below threshold, registration may be opted into for legitimate business reasons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of goods physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU may be zero-rated at 0% VAT where the exporter proves, within 90 days of dispatch, that the goods have left the EU customs territory and are delivered to the foreign purchaser. The SCSp must be the contractual seller and exporter of record or hold a valid authorisation for triangulation. Ancillary services directly related to the exported goods, such as custom pouring, resin-casting, hardening, and packaging of polyhedral dice directly performed under a contract with the foreign buyer, may also qualify for 0% VAT if the services are actually used for the export and are listed in the customs/export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / e-Customs electronic export declaration) confirming the movement from Luxembourg to a non-EU destination and the customs office of exit
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, air waybill, or postal export receipt evidencing physical departure of the dice consignment from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank transfer statement demonstrating that the export invoice was settled in freely convertible foreign currency

Luxembourg does not currently operate a universal real-time clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for private-sector invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G (public procurement) and the PEPPOL network is accepted; B2B e-invoicing is optional but legal. Export VAT refund documentation and invoice data must be retained for at least 6 years, and all invoices must comply with EU VAT structural and content rules, including the obligation to state the VAT exemption reason for exports.

The AEDT can disallow the 0% treatment and assess 17% VAT if export evidence is missing, incomplete, or fabricated, even if the underlying transaction was real. This triggers default interest, penalty interest, and administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 (or 10 times the evaded tax in cases of intentional fraud). Criminal prosecutions may impose imprisonment for up to 5 years for tax fraud. Retrospective assessments and clawbacks extend to 5 years under the general limitation period and to 10 years where fraud or misrepresentation is established. Because the SCSp is transparent, partners remain jointly and severally liable for VAT debts arising in the SCSp's export chain unless the partnership is separately registered as a VAT debtor.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member State; the euro is fully convertible with an unrestricted current and capital account regime. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital and payments between Member States and third countries, subject only to EU/UN sanctions and AML/CTF measures. No prior exchange-control authorization, split-rate regime, differential currency pricing, or repatriation licensing exists for trade flows, capital subscriptions, partner loans, dividends, or profit distributions. A tax-transparent SCSp benefits from this regime: profits remitted to non-resident partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding taxation at the partnership level and encounter no FX-approval hurdle, provided the underlying transaction is compliant with anti-money-laundering verification and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts or cross-border service revenue. Export proceeds and crowdfunding/platform receivables may be retained in EUR or any fully convertible foreign currency (USD, GBP, JPY) in resident bank accounts. The only binding requirements are statistical and prudential: resident credit institutions must report cross-border transactions to the BCL for euro-area external statistics under ECB Regulation (EU) No 184/2005, and any payment to a sanctioned jurisdiction requires a prior special authorization under EU restrictive-measures law.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Central Bank of Luxembourg, acting within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); exchange-control and payment-system oversight is further exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for sanctions and capital-movement restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as a supplementary operational rail: the SCSp may onboard with EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions or Payment Institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Mangopay) operating under the CSSF or EU-pasported PSD2 licence. These EMIs provide rapid multi-currency account functionality, USD/EUR settlement, and direct integration with Stripe and PayPal payout flows. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must hold a valid licence in an EEA state and comply with client-fund safeguarding and segregation rules; (ii) an offshore non-EU digital EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a Luxembourg entity unless it operates through a licensed EU branch or agent; (iii) EMIs generally cannot provide credit, commercial letters of credit, or investment-grade treasury custody, so a tier-1 bank account remains necessary for capital escrow, documentary trade instruments, and serious liquidity management. The SCSp should therefore run a hybrid structure: tier-1 bank for fiduciary/escrow and trade settlement, plus an EMI for high-velocity episodic revenue from Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 or TARGET2/RTGS to the SCSp's designated Luxembourg-tier-1 account, with the MT103 field 70 remittance reference explicitly stating 'Capital subscription under SCSp partnership agreement'. The transaction is classified by the receiving bank under the BCL/ECB balance-of-payments statistical framework as direct investment / equity capital (functional category E1), and no prior exchange-control approval is required for euro-area or third-country investors. The sending bank applies the remitter's local FX approval regime (if any) and the correspondent chain passes SWIFT and EU sanctions screening. Evidentiary bundle for the receiving bank: executed deed of contribution, updated partner register, source-of-funds/source-of-wealth documentation, RBE filing confirmation, and the incoming MT103 confirmation.Supplier and operating payments settle through (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EEA suppliers via TARGET2/EBA Clearing, with the SCSp's euro IBAN; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA raw-material, tooling, and logistics suppliers, including a precise merchant/business purpose description and the BCL statistical declaration; and (iii) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for large cross-border raw-material purchases where payment against compliant shipping documents is required. Refund and backer-payout obligations are discharged through Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal platform rails or direct SEPA/Corporate-to-Bank credits to the backer's account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined: 17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + average 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (SCSp is a pass-through entity; profits are distributed to partners without withholding at the partnership level. If the SCSp were a taxable company, the statutory rate would be 15%.)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end, with automatic extension to 31 March if filed electronically.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required if group consolidated revenue > €50 million; Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €1 million (EUR).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to entities engaged in specific activities (e.g., holding, financing, IP). Manufacturing of dice is not a covered activity; however, the entity must demonstrate real economic presence (office, staff, decision-making) to avoid being classified as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 or 10 times the evaded tax; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, penalty of up to €100,000, and potential criminal charges.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for tax assessments and penalties; may face travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for entities; imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; revocation of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment, plus interest; fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil claims for damages.

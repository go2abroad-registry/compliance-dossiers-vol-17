Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Luxembourg SCSp; the capital is freely fixed in the partnership agreement and may be subscribed in cash, in kind, or in industry. Cash contributions are typically credited to a Luxembourg bank account opened in the SCSp's name and evidenced by a banker's certificate of deposit; no legal escrow account is mandatory, but the certificate of deposit forms part of the RCS filing dossier.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the office premises must be certified for fire alarm, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and evacuation routes under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 22 July 2014 and municipal fire-prevention rules. Structural load: the building-permit and certificate-of-conformity (certificat de conformité) files must be maintained at the registered office. Occupational health: the SCSp must register with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) as an employer, file the annual declaration of posts, and comply with the Law of 17 April 2019 on workplace conditions, including workstation ergonomics, electrical safety, and lighting thresholds; employees using screens for more than two continuous hours require the documented arrangements for protective eye examinations under national occupational-health rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes – ACD) for direct taxes; Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA – AEDT) for indirect taxes; integrated corporate and tax registration portal: guichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering as a Luxembourg SCSp must first file the partnership deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and obtain a RCS registration number. The RCS number serves as the national business registration identifier. Subsequently, the SCSp must apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number from the AEDT, using Form 642 (application for VAT registration), accompanied by the RCS extract, partnership deed, proof of place of effective management or fixed establishment in Luxembourg, and identification of all partners, including foreign partners. Foreign partnerships without a Luxembourg fixed establishment for VAT purposes may require a domestic VAT representative; a non-resident partner with taxable Luxembourg income may further need to register with the ACD for direct tax compliance and obtain a Luxembourg tax number (numéro d'identification collective / n° matricule). The applicant must submit the application via the AES (Registre électronique des agents) / MyGuichet.lu secure portal, and the AEDT typically issues the VAT number within 3 to 10 business days after receipt of complete documentation.

The applicant must submit the application via the AES (Registre électronique des agents) / MyGuichet.lu secure portal, and the AEDT typically issues the VAT number within 3 to 10 business days after receipt of complete documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent by default and is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax or municipal business tax; income and gains are taxed directly in the hands of partners. Luxembourg law does not provide an elective 'check-the-box' regime equivalent to the U.S. check-the-box system. However, certain elections can affect classification: (i) the SCSp may elect to be treated as an 'assujetti unique' for VAT purposes by forming a VAT grouping, which consolidates multiple related entities into one taxable person; (ii) in cross-border hybrid arrangements, the SCSp's transparency can be confirmed or modified by the ACD through a tax ruling (advance tax decision) under the Luxembourg Circular L.I.R. n° 14/4, provided the foreign tax treatment of the SCSp is demonstrated, allowing application of hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2; (iii) where the SCSp has a commercial activity and is not a pure asset-holding vehicle, the partners can effectively select the method of profit allocation under the partnership agreement, subject to arm's length and substance requirements. No election exists to convert the SCSp into a fully taxable corporation without a change of legal form, but it may be transformed into a S.à r.l. or S.A. with tax-neutral reorganisation rules in limited circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under Luxembourg law, a taxable person may qualify for the small business exemption where annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) does not exceed EUR 30,000. This threshold applies to supplies of goods and services made within Luxembourg by an entity established in Luxembourg, subject to exclusion for intra-Community acquisitions and for certain cross-border services that trigger mandatory registration, including electronically supplied services (SaaS/cloud EMR) and reverse-charge transactions where the customer is a taxable person established elsewhere in the EU. For an SCSp engaged in medical-clinic EMR SaaS with cross-border clients, the EUR 30,000 threshold is generally not effective to avoid registration where any B2B client is VAT-registered in another EU Member State; VAT registration in Luxembourg and/or identification through OSS (Union scheme) becomes mandatory regardless of domestic turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: exports dispatched or transported outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT where the exporter proves that the goods left EU territory within the legal timeframe (generally 90 days where transport is evidenced by transport documents) and that the recipient is a non-EU person. For services: SaaS/EMR medical-clinic software qualifies for zero-rating or no-Place-of-Supply taxation in Luxembourg where supplied to a non-EU customer (B2B or B2C effective use outside Luxembourg) and where the service is considered 'export' under Article 58 or Article 59 of the Luxembourg VAT law; B2B supplies to EU customers are taxable in the customer's country via reverse charge (article 44 EU VAT Directive), while B2C supplies of electronic services to non-EU consumers are zero-rated or outside the scope under the OSS Non-Union procedure. Zero-rating also applies to exports of ancillary goods (e.g., hardware/tablets pre-loaded with EMR software) only when a valid customs export declaration is made. Quantitative or qualitative conditions include (i) the exporter holds a valid status as a taxable person, (ii) outgoing cross-border movement of goods is substantiated with transport evidence, and (iii) for services, the effective use and enjoyment test or customer-established place requirement is met.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR transport document

Luxembourg has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate applicable to private B2B e-invoicing as of the current tax reporting period. E-invoicing is permitted under the general invoicing rules; invoices must comply with VAT invoicing requirements (Article 59 of the Luxembourg VAT law) and may be issued in electronic format subject to authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability. Public sector e-invoicing (B2G) is mandatory and operates through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network. VAT returns and EU sales listings (recapitulative statements) must be filed electronically via the eCDF / MyGuichet.lu portal; periodic standard VAT returns follow the calendar quarter generally, with monthly filing required for taxpayers exceeding certain thresholds; annual returns are mandatory. Cross-border digitally supplied services (medical software SaaS) must be reported through the OSS non-Union/Union scheme where applicable, and reverse-charge mechanisms on B2B supplies are subject to the normal EU place-of-supply rules. The AEDT may require historic invoice logs and structured e-invoice extraction during audits, and taxpayers must maintain sufficient electronic records for reconstruction of transactions.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs may conduct ex-post audits of zero-rating claims, requiring strict documentary correlation between export declarations, transport documents, payment flows, and accounting records. Misstated or fraudulent zero-rated export claims trigger reassessment of unpaid VAT at the standard 17% rate, plus default interest calculated at the statutory rate (currently 5% per annum, adjusted from time to time) running from the date the VAT fell due. Deliberate fraud or knowingly false declarations incur a tax penalty of between 10% and 20% of the tax evaded, while criminal prosecution under Articles 309 and 310 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code can result in fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years for the person responsible. Additionally, the taxable person may lose the right to zero-rating for future transactions, be removed from the OSS scheme, be jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT, and, in the case of abusive or artificial arrangements, face retrospective clawback under the anti-abuse rules and GAAR (Article 6 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law) with recharacterisation of the transaction as a domestic taxable supply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully integrated member of the euro area; the euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions in line with Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Capital account operations, including equity subscription to an SCSp, cross-border loans, and repatriation of profits, are unrestricted for EU/EEA residents and generally permitted for non-residents. No prior foreign exchange authorisation, surrender or approval requirement applies. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate/income tax confirmation of beneficial ownership and, for partnerships, the tax transparency treatment of the SCSp means no withholding tax at entity level. Any distribution to non-resident partners may trigger only domestic rules if the partner is a corporate entity in a non-treaty jurisdiction; no exchange control barrier exists. The EMR SaaS business, being a cloud-based software exporter, enjoys full current account convertibility for subscription billing in USD, EUR, and other major currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios under Luxembourg law. Exporters of software services, including licence fees, maintenance, and cloud subscription revenue, may retain receipts in foreign currency in domestic or cross-border bank accounts. The BCL only collects statistics for balance-of-payments reporting; enterprises are required to report cross-border payments of more than EUR 12,500 per individual transaction to the BCL for statistical purposes via the BCL's report system. No specific conversion to euros is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the European System of Central Banks and under the prudential supervision framework jointly co-ordinated by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro area monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for ancillary payments collection, but constrained for core treasury cash management. Luxembourg recognises Payment Institutions (PI) and Electronic Money Institutions (EMI) authorised under CSSF in line with the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). An offshore EMI without a Luxembourg or EU passport may not provide regulated payment services in Luxembourg. A cross-border EMI licensed in another EEA state may, under the freedom of establishment and passporting regime, open a single-currency or multi-currency account for the SCSp and process card and bank transfer payments. However, the SCSp will still require a tier-1 Luxembourg or EU credit institution for all material liquidity, debt, escrow, and guaranteed clearing transactions. The legal constraint is that an EMI cannot provide overdraft, credit, or any interest-bearing deposit product; all client funds must be safeguarded via a segregated account at a credit institution. For the EMR SaaS model, an EMI such as a Passported Fintech (e.g., Adyen, Airwallex, or similar) can be viable only as an additional collection rail for high-voltage, low-ticket subscription fees, while primary settlement must remain in a fully licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection into the SCSp from non-resident investors must be executed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (for high-value transactions) using the beneficiary account at a Luxembourg bank. The payment instruction must clearly state the beneficiary as 'LU_SCSP, Société en Commandite Spéciale' and include the purpose of payment code. For EU statistical reporting, use the BCL Balance of Payments classification code for equity capital injection (e.g., under direct investment, category 'Equity capital'). Recommended SWIFT purpose code is 'CAPT' (Capital Contribution) or a bilateral reference such as 'SCSp capital subscription'. The routing must be directly to the EUR IBAN of the operational account; if injected in USD, the receiving bank will convert at corporate rates or utilise a nostro Vostro arrangement. Mandatory verification for AML purposes includes a copy of the executed capital call notice, the partner's capital account ledger entry, and confirmation of the source of funds. The bank may require a certified copy of the subscribed capital entry in the internal accounts of the SCSp; no deposit of minimum capital is required, but the funds must be identifiable and traceable. Inbound payments from a non-EU jurisdiction of more than EUR 10,000 trigger direct control by the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) Luxembourg through the bank's automated monitoring system; no pre-clearance is required except for sanctions screening against target lists.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and operating costs (software vendor payments, cloud hosting fees, medical-clinic partner commissions, and legal/consulting invoices) should be routed primarily through SWIFT MT103 individual customer credit transfers, ensuring correct KYC on beneficiaries and a precise remittance information field matching the underlying invoice. For high-volume recurring payments to EU suppliers, the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) scheme offers cost-efficient EUR-denominated clearing; for USD disbursements, the bank's correspondent network and SWIFT MT103 are the standard rails. Where the SCSp enters into commercial contracts with major healthcare distributors, a Documentary Letter of Credit (Commercial LC) issued by a Luxembourg bank may be used; this is permissible provided the underlying transaction is genuine and the LC text reflects the specific SaaS deliverables. For subscription revenue collections from non-EU clients, the primary inbound rails should include Stripe Billing (with payout to the Luxembourg bank account) and direct SWIFT inbound via a multi-currency IBAN. All outbound cross-border payments must include the correct BCL statistical purpose code (e.g., 'Payment for IT services') and should be named in the accounting records to satisfy the annual statutory audit obligation under Luxembourg law. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for ancillary payments collection, but constrained for core treasury cash management. Luxembourg recognises Payment Institutions (PI) and Electronic Money Institutions (EMI) authorised under CSSF in line with the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). An offshore EMI without a Luxembourg or EU passport may not provide regulated payment services in Luxembourg. A cross-border EMI licensed in another EEA state may, under the freedom of establishment and passporting regime, open a single-currency or multi-currency account for the SCSp and process card and bank transfer payments. However, the SCSp will still require a tier-1 Luxembourg or EU credit institution for all material liquidity, debt, escrow, and guaranteed clearing transactions. The legal constraint is that an EMI cannot provide overdraft, credit, or any interest-bearing deposit product; all client funds must be safeguarded via a segregated account at a credit institution. For the EMR SaaS model, an EMI such as a Passported Fintech (e.g., Adyen, Airwallex, or similar) can be viable only as an additional collection rail for high-voltage, low-ticket subscription fees, while primary settlement must remain in a fully licensed bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% national CIT headline rate on taxable income above EUR 175,000; effective 16.05% after 7% solidarity surcharge; combined headline rate with Luxembourg City municipal business tax at 6.75% is 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions or allocations by a fiscally transparent SCSp, because the SCSp has no separate Luxembourg corporate taxpayer personality and allocations are not dividends. The ordinary Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding tax rate of 15% applies only when an actual Luxembourg tax-resident company distributes profits; such 15% may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg imposes no statutory de minimis transaction threshold for transfer pricing documentation; all cross-border related-party transactions, including EMR software licence fees, development cost contributions, and management charges, must be substantiated on an arm's length basis. Mandatory CbCR, Master File, and Local File obligations attach when the ultimate group consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. For smaller groups, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is nevertheless expected whenever related-party transactions exceed approximately EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate or a tax adjustment risk exists.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the Gulf state sense. However, substantive economic presence is a condition of Luxembourg tax residence, treaty access, and the anti-avoidance rule under ATAD and the Luxembourg General Tax Law. An SCSp that qualifies as a mere letterbox vehicle, lacking local offices, active management, employees, bank accounts, and real decision-making functions in relation to the EMR software business, will be vulnerable to treaty benefit denial, re-characterisation as opaque, and challenge under the general anti-abuse rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 250,000, together with civil fraud penalties of 50%-100% of the evaded tax, late-payment interest at 0.8% per month, forfeiture of reliefs and treaty benefits, and publication of the tax assessment.

Director personal liability: The SCSp general partner and any managing director who signed or caused a false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and can face civil restitution, asset freezing orders, travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of ATAD and Luxembourg GAAR to disregard the entity or arrangement; denial of treaty benefits; re-characterisation of the SCSp as a permanent establishment of the overseas parent; reassessment of profit plus interest and penalties of up to EUR 250,000; possible loss of Luxembourg corporate and partnership law favourable treatment.

Director personal liability: Because an SCSp is without legal personality, the general partner and managers are subject to unlimited commercial and tax liability; the corporate veil is not a shield. They may be pursued personally for taxes, restitution, and damages, and may face travel bans and imprisonment in the context of criminal tax or fraud proceedings.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: RBE non-compliance carries administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 plus daily default penalties; serious AML failures attract CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 1,250,000 or 10% of annual turnover, and criminal money-laundering sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal exposure is direct rather than dependent on veil-piercing: officers who approve or omit required AML filings face personal fines, imprisonment, civil damages to affected patients or clients, travel bans, and disqualification from directorships.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg General Tax Law and OECD TP principles, the tax authorities reprice the transaction to an arm's length result, impose a primary adjustment, and require a secondary adjustment where applicable; late interest of 0.8% per month and penalties of 10%-50% of the under-declared tax are triggered, increasing to 100% in cases of deliberate misconduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp general partner are personally liable for negligent or intentional transfer pricing documentation failures and resulting assessments; deliberate mispricing may expose them to criminal tax fraud charges, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.

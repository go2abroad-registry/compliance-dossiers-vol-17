Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum. An SCSp is exempt from minimum capital requirements: paid-up capital is freely set in the partnership agreement, may be zero, and no bank escrow certificate or blocked-account rule applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the CGDIS covering evacuation routes, fire-resistance rating, and extinguishing systems; ITM occupational clearance for cleanroom ventilation (ISO 14644), chemical storage, and machine guarding; municipal structural completion and building-use certificate prior to commissioning production lines.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum. An SCSp is exempt from minimum capital requirements: paid-up capital is freely set in the partnership agreement, may be zero, and no bank escrow certificate or blocked-account rule applies.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; taxpayer portal: MyGuichet.lu and https://pfs.guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

For an SCSp, register the limited partnership deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) to obtain the statutory 13-digit matricule number. Apply to AEDT for VAT identification using the applicable VAT registration application (Form 160 or electronic filing through MyGuichet); non-resident applicants without a permanent establishment may be required to appoint a tax representative in Luxembourg. The SCSp is treated as a separate taxable person for VAT purposes despite its income-tax transparency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Luxembourg. An SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory default for direct income and net wealth tax purposes. No election is available to treat a properly constituted SCSp as fully opaque; however, Luxembourg's anti-hybrid and ATAD rules may recharacterize tax treatment in cross-border securities or financing structures to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for established Luxembourg taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Intra-Community distance sales and cross-border digital services to Luxembourg consumers may trigger registration at the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold or, where applicable, the Luxembourg domestic EUR 35,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods that are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT export exemption under Luxembourg VAT law. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence that the goods left the EU, such as a valid customs export declaration, transport documentation, and commercial evidence identifying the foreign customer and destination. Certain services directly connected with exported goods or services consumed outside the EU may also be zero-rated with equivalent evidentiary support.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice and contract
  • Transport documents and Incoterms evidence

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time transaction-by-transaction fiscal clearance or mandatory B2B electronic invoicing for private-sector supplies. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for business-to-government (B2G) transactions using PEPPOL/FACTUR-X format. VAT compliance is based on periodic VAT returns and, for eligible taxpayers, submission of the Luxembourg SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Taxes) in XML. No digital stamping or real-time national clearance system is in force.

The 0% export exemption is presumptive and subject to post-clearance audits by AEDT. If the mandatory documentation bundle is incomplete, unreliable, or not timely retained, the supply is recharacterized as a Luxembourg domestic supply and VAT at 17% must be clawed back, together with default interest of 0.75% per month (or the statutory rate in force). Administrative penalties may reach 50% of the tax shortfall for negligence or intentional concealment; fraudulent export schemes are criminally prosecuted and carry additional fines and imprisonment. The ordinary reassessment period is five years, extended to ten years for fraud-related cases. Where export zero-rating is denied, supplier's corresponding input VAT deductions are likewise disallowed, amplifying the total financial exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No exchange control approvals are required for capital contributions, profit repatriation, or trade-related remittances. SCSp profit distribution follows the partnership agreement and is tax-transparent; remittance is subject only to standard anti-money-laundering and tax compliance checks, not foreign exchange authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no minimum conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be held in any freely convertible currency in Luxembourg or foreign accounts, subject to standard bank reporting thresholds and CSSF record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision, FX licensing, and cross-border payment infrastructure oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for fully offshore digital EMI providers. Luxembourg-recognised electronic money institutions (PSD2-licensed via CSSF) can offer virtual IBANs and cross-border clearing, but an SCSp engaged in surgical consumables manufacturing requires a full corporate current account with structured trade finance capabilities (SWIFT MT103, LC issuance), which most EMIs do not provide. Any EMI used must hold a valid EU passport and maintain a physical Luxembourg or EU registration, otherwise onboarding is rejected under CSSF outsourcing rules.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% for a fully taxable Luxembourg corporate entity (15% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City). A Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent and has no entity-level corporate income tax liability; profits and losses pass through to its partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Distributions or profit withdrawals from an SCSp do not carry Luxembourg dividend withholding tax because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not the payer of a dividend. If a Luxembourg resident company were the dividend payer instead, the statutory Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding tax is 15%, reduced to 0% under the Luxembourg parent-subsidiary participation regime where conditions of minimum holding period and shareholding are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg has no broad statutory monetary threshold for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, but the ACD expects a local file for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per category; a master file is required in practice when the multinational group has consolidated turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000; country-by-country reporting obligation starts at consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is a unitary national tax jurisdiction, and no separate sub-state Economic Substance Regulations apply as in certain Crown Dependencies. However, Luxembourg's general anti-abuse rule, OECD substance-over-form rules, EU ATAD principles and bilateral tax treaty beneficial ownership requirements require the SCSp to maintain real substance: qualified staff in Luxembourg premises, local management, operating expenditure, commercial risk and independent decision-making for surgical suture manufacturing operations. A SCSp used only as a conduit for profit stripping will be recharacterised and denied treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full understated tax with penalty surcharge up to 100% of the evaded tax; default interest at 0.8% per month; criminal fine of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years for intentional tax fraud.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the SCSp and, if the general partner is corporate, its directors/managers who sign or knowingly cause a false declaration are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax; courts may lift the corporate veil for abuse of the corporate form, with personal asset recovery, travel restrictions and disqualification from management following criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing the Luxembourg taxable base; disallowance of excessive interest; late payment interest at 0.8% per month; penalty of up to 10% of the under-declared tax; administrative fines for failure to produce contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and the local manager may be personally assessed for penalties if the deficient policy was knowingly advanced; tax administrations may pierce the partnership structure and recover under-declared tax from the general partner as an unlimited partner.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits; recharacterisation of the SCSp as a fully taxable Luxembourg entity; substitution of taxable base by anti-abuse assessment; removal from the commercial register and administrative dissolution for persistent lack of substance.

Director personal liability: The directors or others acting through the shell may be personally liable for unpaid taxes as de facto managers; travel bans, asset freezes and director disqualification apply where a significant tax debt is linked to abstract use of a shell vehicle.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 125,000 per violation under Luxembourg AML/RBE law, with repeated or serious violations exposing fines up to EUR 1,000,000; freezing of bank transactions, delayed disbursement and regulatory suspension of the manufacturing licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are criminally liable for intentional non-disclosure of beneficial ownership; imprisonment of up to 5 years and personal fines up to EUR 50,000; directors may be barred from holding office and repatriation/profit distribution may be blocked by judicial order.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operating under the Ministry of Justice of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; electronic filings accepted through the official portal www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 sur les sociétés commerciales), Title V (Articles 710-1 to 710-27) governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the accounting records; Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to the professions of craftsman, merchant and tradesman; Luxembourg Labour Code Articles L. 341-1 et seq. where temporary work placement is contemplated.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially authenticated deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif) and Articles of Association (Statuts), filed electronically with the RCS as the official statement of incorporation (déclaration de création d'entreprise).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 8 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The standard SARL requires a minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000; the entire nominal capital must be fully subscribed at incorporation, and the cash portion of each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked (escrow) bank account held at a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released to the company only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report (rapport du réviseur d'entreprises agréé). No proportional capital duty applies; only the fixed registration and publication fees are payable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), 33 rue de Gasperich, L-5826 Hesperange; self-service portal espaces.acd.public.lu and unified platform myGuichet.lu. Indirect taxes: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), 1-3 avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg; VAT portal espacetva.public.lu. Customs and excise: Administration des Douanes et Accises (ADA), single window myCustoms.lu and Luxembourg Single Window (ICMS). Central business register: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), www.lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL engaged in EOR/staffing: (1) Execute the notarial deed of incorporation and file it with the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) via LBR; publish in RESA; obtain the 12-digit RCS matricule number, which is the master company identifier. (2) Before any taxable transaction, file the VAT registration application (Formulaire 640, Module 101 via myGuichet.lu) with AEDT; the VAT identification number (TVA ID) is generally issued within 2-3 weeks. (3) The ACD automatically opens a direct tax file and assigns a fiscal dossier number once RCS registration is notified; all corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT) returns are filed under that number. (4) Obtain the mandatory establishment authorization ('autorisation d'établissement') from the Ministry of the Economy for temporary work agency / staff-leasing activities (classes 78.10-78.20 of the business classification); invoicing payroll services before the authorization is issued is unlawful. (5) Register with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) using the GemFlow/Editus platform for social security and payroll withholding accounts. (6) If foreign companies act without a Luxembourg subsidiary (e.g., a foreign EOR entity carrying on activities through a Luxembourg deemed establishment), the non-resident company must file a branch registration with RCS, obtain an EORI number from ADA for goods movements, and appoint an accredited Luxembourg VAT representative when no fixed establishment exists. Deadlines are strict: VAT registration precedes first taxable supply; a foreign enterprise that becomes liable must register within 15 days of exceeding the taxable threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; a SARL is a hardwired corporation. Available elections relevant to an EOR platform: (i) VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') under Article 60 of the VAT Law, allowing the SARL and its Luxembourg affiliates (with financial, economic, and organizational links) to file as a single taxable person; (ii) fiscal unity (tax consolidation) for CIT and MBT under Article 164bis LIR, requiring a 95% direct or indirect holding and a 5-year duration undertaking; (iii) participation exemption election for dividends under Section 166 LIR and capital gains under Section 166bis LIR where the SARL holds at least 10% (or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost) of a qualifying subsidiary for 12 consecutive months and either the subsidiary is subject to an equivalent tax or the SARL is itself a fully taxable resident company; (iv) VAT small-enterprise franchise election for turnover below EUR 35,000, incompatible with invoicing VAT to business clients; and (v) ATAD hybrid mismatch documentation elections pursuant to Sections 62-68 LIR requiring annual documentation of any deductible payments that could be treated as deductible in the recipient state. All elections are exercised in the annual CIT/VAT returns and require advance written documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is an integral Eurozone member; both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalised under EU capital movement rules (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Article 63). There are no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or proceeds conversion. Only standard corporate tax and withholding tax withholdings apply under domestic tax law, not FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any export proceeds surrender requirements. There is neither a statutory timeline nor a mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export and service proceeds remain freely convertible and transferable, subject to ordinary anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds and suspicious transaction reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking prudential and conduct supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (authorised as a Luxembourg payment institution or as a passport under the e-money framework) is highly viable for operational liquidity and cross-border payroll settlements, rated High. Other pan-European EMIs are acceptable for low-velocity remittance but are not recommended for capital escrow accounts nor for substantial treasury reserve buffers. Legal constraints include adherence to CSSF AML expectations, mandatory segregated client funds accounting, and a Luxembourg or EEA registered presence for the EMI; full Tier-1 bank will still be required for capital contributions and significant loan advances.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City, FY 2024 aggregate): 17% IRC + 7% Employment Fund surcharge on the IRC due (=1.19%) + 6.75% municipal business tax (TCC) on trade profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (Art. 104 LIR) on Luxembourg-source dividends paid by an SARL; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying parent holding at least 10% capital or a EUR 1.2 million acquisition price for an uninterrupted 12-month period; DTA rates typically range from 0% to 10%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory reference date: 31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end for a calendar-year taxpayer. The ACD routinely grants an automatic extension to 30 June where the annual accounts have been finalised; a further conventional extension to 31 December remains requestable on formal application.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 / Section A — Identification RCS number, LEI, legal form (SARL), registered office, statutory representative and administrative contact, tax consolidation reference. The Luxembourg SARL files under its own RCS/LU identity. Non-resident shareholders do not enter the form; the LU director or tax representative signs and must hold a power of attorney. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 2 / Section B — Accounting result Reported net profit or loss before tax per the audited annual accounts (CNC Luxembourg GAAP). Enter the book result of the LU SARL only; intercompany EOR bills from the non-resident parent/branch must be excluded unless properly re-invoiced. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 3 / Section C — Non-deductible additions Add-back of non-deductible expenses: excessive remuneration, fictitious payroll, fines, gifts, expenses paid to zero-tax or non-cooperative jurisdictions, and portions of interest disallowed under thin-capitalisation rules. If the EOR staffing fee is paid to an offshore back-office, the ACD will deny deductibility absent an arm's-length service agreement and substance evidence. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 4 / Section D — Participation exemption election Claim the 50% exemption on dividends or the full capital-gain exemption; state qualifying shareholding percentage, acquisition price and holding period. Non-resident investors providing a TRC and Form 91 will be attached to the SARL's annual file to support reduced withholding under the applicable DTA. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 5 / Section E — Loss carryforward offset Offset of prior-year tax losses against the current-year positive result; the ACD pre-fills the loss register and applies the 17-year carryforward limit. Losses of a foreign head office cannot be imputed to the LU SARL; only losses booked and recognised under Luxembourg tax law are eligible. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 6 / Section F — Municipal business tax (TCC) base Trade-income base after the EUR 175,000 abatement and apportionment to the commune of establishment. The EOR payroll services must be attributed to the LU commercial activity; non-resident back-office personnel do not reduce the Luxembourg TCC allocation. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.
Box 7 / Section G — Net wealth tax (ISF) declaration Net taxable wealth at the balance sheet date; deduction of the EUR 500,000 abatement and the 0.5% rate applied to the excess. A non-resident owner does not file ISF personally; the SARL files and pays, and may benefit from the minimum-tax regime on commencement of business. Late filing causes an automatic provisional assessment (taxation d'office), a late-declaration surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the outstanding balance, and, for repeated refusal to file, an administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 and loss of the right to claim tax losses.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No written statutory de minimis; ACD administrative guidance requires a contemporaneous Luxembourg Local File where annual intercompany transactions with a single associated enterprise exceed EUR 100,000, and a Master File where the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but enforces an equivalent minimum-substance doctrine through Art. 56 LIR, the general anti-abuse rule, and case law (Eqiom). A Global EOR staffing SARL must maintain a real physical office, resident executive director(s) with actual decision-making autonomy, local payroll records, and an operational staffing function; otherwise the ACD may recharacterise the SARL as a transparent conduit, reallocate profit to the foreign parent and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fictitious EOR Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Fraud penalty of 50% of the evaded tax (increased to 75% for premeditated fraud), late-interest of 0.6% per month, additional 7% Employment Fund surcharge on the penalty, and criminal fine of up to EUR 100,000 with imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years under the Luxembourg General Tax Law and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; courts may impose a travel ban, asset freeze, disqualification from management functions and confiscation of personal property.
Artificial Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and reallocation of profits to the foreign parent; denial of participation-exemption benefits and treaty relief; 50% supplementary tax on hidden distributions; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; DAC6 disclosure penalties for reportable cross-border arrangements.

Director personal liability: The sole/signing manager may be held personally liable for the tax shortfall in insolvency-style proceedings (Art. 495, Luxembourg Commercial Code), can be barred from acting as a director for up to 10 years, and may be subject to a cross-border travel ban pending recovery.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of EUR 50,000 for failure to deliver the TP documentation on demand; the tax authority may re-impute an arm's-length margin with a 20% to 50% penalty surcharge on the adjustment, plus enforcement interest.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who certified the tax return is jointly assessed for the re-assessed corporate tax, and, where the failure is intentional, may face criminal sanctions and disqualification from professional office.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CSSF and judicial fines of up to EUR 5 million or twice the AML benefit; daily default penalties for office renovation of the RBE; withdrawal of the professional licence of the EOR operator; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for money-laundering concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for laundering proceeds or financing anonymity structures; sanctions include custodial sentence, a multi-year travel ban, and personal restitution of all criminally derived benefits.
Payroll / Social Security Fraud

Statutory sanction: Surcharge equal to 10% of un-withheld wage tax plus 0.6% monthly interest; social security fraud penalty of up to 150% of evaded contributions; refusal to recognise foreign payroll rebilling as deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid salary withholding and social contributions; attachment of personal bank accounts and assets, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years for repeated payroll fraud.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in Luxembourg; the SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent, meaning it has no separate taxable personality for income tax, net wealth tax, or VAT and each partner is taxed directly on their allocable share. The general partner may lawfully allocate the VAT-taxable e-commerce activity to the partnership's permanent establishment and is treated as the taxable person for VAT compliance. No hybrid mismatch election is available domestically, but taxpayers must file an annual disclosure with ACD for any cross-border arrangements exploiting hybrid entities or instruments within the meaning of the ATAD II implementing law of 21 December 2018 and 20 December 2019; a fiscally transparent SCSp may be subject to symmetrical treatment alignment by treaty where the partner's residence state regards it as opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business exemption (art. 145(2) Loi TVA); for distance sales of consumer electronics to EU end consumers, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies, above which Luxembourg VAT registration for OSS is replaced by destination-state VAT via the Union OSS quarterly return; a non-established foreign seller has no registration threshold and must register in Luxembourg whenever a taxable supply occurs in the territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under art. 53 Loi TVA provided export is evidenced by customs documentation and the goods leave the EU within 90 days of supply. Intra-community supplies to EU-registered taxable persons are zero-rated under art. 54 Loi TVA when the recipient's valid VAT number is confirmed by VIES and the goods are dispatched to another member state; the exemption is subject to recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) and Intrastat filing. B2B electronic services and software hosting supplied to an EU business established outside Luxembourg or to B2C customers outside the EU qualify for 0% under the place-of-supply rules of arts. 42-44 Loi TVA, with the place of establishment of the customer determining territoriality.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Document Administratif Unique) with the resulting Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note or courier delivery scan evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, HS codes (e.g., 8471, 8517, 8543) and export reason
  • Payment proof and booked inward remittance (Stripe/PayPal settlement report or bank confirmation) evidencing that proceeds were credited to the Luxembourg account within the allowed period
  • European Community Sales List (EC Sales List) and Intrastat declarations for intra-EU zero-rated dispatches
  • OSS quarterly VAT return (VAT 500/503) where distance sales are taxed at the destination state

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping, or digital stamping regime exists in Luxembourg. B2B/B2C e-invoices must conform to EU VAT invoice rules (issuance within 15 days, sequential numbering, supplier/buyer identification, taxable amount, VAT rate and amount). Mandatory structured e-invoicing applies exclusively to B2G invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector bodies, which must be exchanged through the Peppol network in UBL/CIUS format. For online payments via Stripe and PayPal, the exporter must retain the complete payment reconciliation trail linking each transaction to the export proof; Manco/Data-related e-archiving must be kept for 6 years.

AEDT may retroactively claw back the 17% VAT not properly zero-rated, plus default interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% per annum) and a 10% late-payment surcharge (droit en sus, art. 84 Loi TVA) if export documentation is missing or fraudulent. Administrative fines range from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000, augmentable to twice the tax evaded for intentional concealment, and criminal sanctions provide imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years (arts. 80-83 Loi TVA). The reclamation window is 5 years from the end of the year in which the return was filed, extended to 10 years for offences committed with fraudulent intent; retroactive OSS-denial at the rate of the destination member state may also be imposed where the dispatch proof does not meet the substantive requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as an EU/EMU member state, applies full current and capital account convertibility with the euro as legal tender. There are no exchange controls, approval requirements, or restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, capital contributions, or loan repayments. Cross-border conversion between EUR, USD, CNY, and other freely traded currencies is conducted at commercial bank market rates. The only practical constraints are standard bank AML/KYC due diligence, tax reporting formalities, and the requirement that the SCSp maintain demonstrable economic substance and proper accounting records. Profit distributions from the SCSp are governed by the partnership agreement and follow the tax-transparent character of the structure, with no Luxembourg exchange-control permission required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose mandatory surrender, repatriation timelines, or minimum conversion ratios for export proceeds. Receivables from online consumer-electronics retail sales may be retained in EUR or foreign currency and converted freely through Luxembourg banks. Banks remain obliged to report monetary statistics to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes and to apply standard AML surveillance, but no statutory conversion or surrender obligation is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the prudential and AML/KYC banking supervisory authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-High as a complementary payment layer, but Low as the sole treasury infrastructure. A CSSF-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or an EEA-passported EMI may lawfully hold merchant funds, provide multi-currency IBANs, and execute FX conversion; however, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot offer deposit protection, documentary letters of credit, committed credit lines, or full treasury safeguarding. Stripe and PayPal operate as payment service providers or merchant-of-record processors rather than principal Luxembourg banks; they may settle consumer-electronics sales but should not be treated as the primary bank relationship. The SCSp should maintain at least one principal regulated Luxembourg credit institution account for capital escrow, trade settlements, and regulatory credibility. Legal constraints include the EMD2 safeguarding regime, client-money separation, CSSF AML/KYC obligations, and the fact that an EMI cannot execute commercial bank instruments such as SWIFT-backed commercial letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions to the SCSp must be routed through a regulated Luxembourg credit institution using the SWIFT MT103 message standard for non-EEA investors or SEPA credit transfer for EEA investors. The remittance instruction must carry the SCSp legal name, the reference to the executed partnership agreement and capital account, and a payment purpose narrative such as 'Capital Contribution to SCSp'. The receiving bank will typically require completion of its external-settlement questionnaire and a balance-of-payments reporting declaration to the BCL. Funds must be fully cleared and pass bank compliance before they are released to the operational treasury. For larger or institutional commitments, an escrow-capable account under a segregated internal ledger is recommended. The bank must also verify that the remitter is the identified investor and that no third-party funding occurs unless a fully documented assignment or contribution agreement is provided.Trade settlements for procurement of consumer electronics should use SWIFT MT103 for direct open-account supplier payments and SWIFT MT202 COV for correspondent-bank cover where required. Commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP600, typically issued via SWIFT MT700/MT710) provide secured settlement for larger or recurring supplier purchases. Bills for collection under URC522 remain permissible but are less common for consumer-electronics retail. Retail disbursements are settled through Stripe and PayPal merchant acquiring flows, with net proceeds converted to EUR or USD in the Luxembourg operational account. All outbound MT103 messages must include a purpose code mapped to trade in goods, supplier invoice numbers, and product category references so that the correspondent bank and clearing systems can process the payment without AML delays. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A statutory audit by an ILRE-registered independent auditor (réviseur d'entreprises) is compulsory if the SCSp exceeds at least two of the following three thresholds for two consecutive financial years: (a) total balance sheet EUR 4.4 million; (b) net turnover EUR 8.8 million; (c) average workforce of 50 employees. An audit is also mandatory where the partnership agreement so requires, where the SCSp forms part of a consolidated group filing consolidated accounts, or where the entity operates as a regulated professional of the financial sector (PFS) or investment-fund vehicle.

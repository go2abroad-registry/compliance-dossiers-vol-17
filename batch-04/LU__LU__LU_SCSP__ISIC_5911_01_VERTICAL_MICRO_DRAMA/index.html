E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

SCSp is treated as fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax; no general check-the-box election is needed for pass-through status. For VAT, the SCSp is itself a taxable person if it makes supplies. Luxembourg applies hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD, and certain contractual arrangements may elect for corporate-tax transparency treatment only where expressly provided by law (e.g., collective investment fund structures).

17%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to goods physically leaving the EU, provided the supplier retains transport and export evidence and the export occurs within statutory deadlines. For cross-border services and digital products (vertical micro-drama streaming, advertising, IP licensing), the place of supply determines Luxembourg VAT: B2B supplies to non-established recipients outside the EU are outside Luxembourg VAT; B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU recipients are also outside EU VAT, requiring proof of customer location, IP address evidence, and payment details.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Physical Departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Statement evidencing receipt of consideration
  • Contract or Licence Agreement identifying the non-EU buyer

Luxembourg mandates PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing for B2G supplies; no general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping applies to standard B2B invoices. EU ViDA proposals and Directive 2014/55/EU govern structured e-invoicing for public procurement; private-sector clearance remains optional.

Unsupported export zero-rating is treated as domestic taxable supply, giving rise to VAT due, statutory interest, fines up to EUR 10,000 per invoice, and penalties of up to 100-200% of evaded tax in cases of intentional misstatement. Input VAT clawbacks are assessed for the full correction period, and the Luxembourg tax authorities may conduct criminal fraud proceedings where export documentation is forged or manipulated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full Eurozone member. The euro is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EU free movement of capital rules. There are no exchange controls, no licensing for ordinary inward/outward remittances, and no prior regulatory approval for profit distribution or capital repatriation. For a fiscally transparent SCSp, profit allocations and capital withdrawals to non-resident partners are unrestricted and are generally not subjected to Luxembourg withholding tax at partnership level. Practical hurdles are confined to strict AML/CTF due diligence, source-of-funds substantiation, and compliance with the Luxembourg register of beneficial owners (RBE). The legal form's zero-minimum capital feature may trigger enhanced scrutiny but does not impair legal convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios exist under Luxembourg or Eurosystem law. Recipients of export revenue in foreign currencies are not required to repatriate within a fixed timeframe or sell the currency to the BCL or an authorized bank. Foreign-currency accounts may be freely maintained and managed. Receipts must, however, be accurately booked and reported for tax, statistical, and AML purposes, including through BCL balance-of-payments reporting provided by the relevant bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) within the Eurosystem/European System of Central Banks, supervised by the European Central Bank; banking prudential and AML supervision by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate foreign exchange administration exists; exchange control competence is effectively vested in the BCL for statistics, the Ministry of Finance for policy, and CSSF for transactional integrity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs), such as those authorized by the CSSF or an EEA home regulator under PSD2, may legally open e-money accounts for a Luxembourg SCSp. They offer fast onboarding, API-based transaction processing, and low-cost international transfers, but they are not credit institutions and cannot provide credit, substantial treasury services, or complex FX hedging. Legal constraints include (i) mandatory safeguarding of client funds, (ii) EMI compliance obligations under Luxembourg AML law, including full beneficial ownership identification, (iii) potential friction when the SCSp's general partner is a non-resident legal entity, and (iv) periodic transaction thresholds that limit suitability for high-value capital injections. An SCSp can use EMIs as supplementary rails for micro-drama payout distribution to creators or for small-value disbursements, but the primary clearing and settlement must remain with a tier-1 bank to ensure robust FX execution and multi-currency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from EEA partners must be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer with the reference remarks 'Capital Contribution – SCSp Name – Purpose Code EQUI/200' where permitted, or via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA contributors. The wire narrative must state 'Capital contribution to Luxembourg SCSp – not trade settlement' to avoid misclassification as goods/services revenue. The receiving bank will require a copy of the partner's KYC file, the executed capital call notice, and the signed partnership agreement before pass-through to the operational account. For statistical compliance, the bank will report the flow to BCL using the applicable balance-of-payments item (direct investment equity capital). The counterparty bank must be an authorized financial institution; funds from private individuals require an additional source-of-funds letter explicitly confirming the transaction is equity and not a loan. The SCSp should ensure all inbound transfers arrive in EUR where possible; foreign-currency contributions may be converted upon receipt or held in a multi-currency account, subject to the acceptable FX instruments set out below.Outbound settlements for production costs, creator royalties, advertising purchases, and local operational expenses will primarily use SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the EEA and SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-EEA recipients. For high-frequency/low-value payouts to small content creators, the SCSp may use a PSD2-licensed EMI or a payment facilitator integrated with the video distribution app; however, all payments above a treasury threshold must anchor on the tier-1 bank's MT103 corporate trial or SEPA Direct Debits to ensure full auditability. Commercial letters of credit are generally not applicable because the business sells digital services and intellectual property licences rather than physical goods; should a co-production agreement require a documentary collection, a revolving import/export LC can be opened with the main operating bank, using the contractual cash flow projections as the basis for the facility. Prudent treasury policy requires that all outbound payments carry a standardized narrative describing the nature of the disbursement (e.g., 'Licensing fee's, 'Production service fee', 'Creator payout'), and copies of the underlying contract are retained in the bank's KYC file to avoid AML red flags.FX Spot Contracts for same-day or T+2 settlement between EUR and USD, GBP, and other convertible currencies.Deliverable FX Forwards (outright forwards) with a maximum maturity of 24 months, supported by documented currency exposure from app-store revenues and production expenditure.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) entered into with OECD-based credit institutions for currencies without fully deliverable local markets, strictly as hedges for projected net receipts or royalty payments.FX Swap Contracts (short-term rollovers of matched principal and interest legs) for temporary liquidity rebalancing between the SCSp's operating and escrow accounts.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) for matching long-term multi-currency financial liabilities, subject to a documented economic hedging analysis and board approval.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% corporate income tax (IRC); plus 7% solidarity surcharge on IRC and municipal business tax (e.g., 6.75% in Luxembourg City), resulting in an aggregate statutory CIT/municipal rate of approximately 24.94% before net wealth tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (Luxembourg domestic WHT on dividends from resident corporations). For SCSp, distributions are generally treated as profit allocations of a transparent partnership rather than as dividends, so no 15% dividend WHT is enforced at SCSp level. Where the SCSp is deemed commercially transparent under the applicable DTA, distributions retain their character as business profits taxable only in the partner's state if no Luxembourg PE is present.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1 million aggregate cross-border related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File; a Master File / CbCR obligation arises for groups with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style ESR, but it enforces substance requirements through domestic GAAR (Article 6 LIR), Article 56bis LIR on transfer pricing, ATAD anti-avoidance rules, and treaty abuse provisions. For a SCSp to benefit from tax transparency and treaty access, it must maintain real management, staff, premises, and decision-making in Luxembourg; absence of substance may result in recharacterising the vehicle as tax opaque, denying treaty benefits, imposing CIT and administrative penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File (Fraude fiscale / Absence de déclaration)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil tax reassessment plus late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; late-filing surcharge up to 10%; publication of judgment in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Managing partners/directors who knowingly approved the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, and may suffer confiscation of assets, travel bans, restitution orders, and imprisonment.
Artificial Shell Vehicle / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Invocation of Article 6 LIR (Abuse of Law) and OECD BEPS Act. The vehicle may be disregarded for Luxembourg tax purposes, profits re-allocated to shareholders/beneficial owners, treaty benefits denied, and administrative abuse penalties plus interest applied.

Director personal liability: A serious lack of substance may result in lifting the corporate veil; the general partner may be held personally liable to the tax authority for the full tax creditor; travel restrictions and custodial sentence possible if fraud is shown.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may adjust taxable profit, disallow deductions, apply interest and penalties up to 10% of the adjustment plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; in gross negligence cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate TP documentation or failed to monitor inter-company pricing may be liable for the additional assessment; in aggravated cases they may be subject to a director ban and seizure of personal assets.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Filing Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines between EUR 1,000 and EUR 20,000 for failure to comply with UBO registration; in serious or recurring cases, criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment and exclusion from public contracts; ancillary sanctions include blocking accounts and suspension of distributions.

Director personal liability: The managing partner/directors are personally responsible for UBO declarations; intentional failure creates personal exposure to sanctions, travel bans and inability to act as director; also may be barred from accessing EU payment rails.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), resident at 6, Avenue du Swing, L-4367 Belvaux, Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended; specifically Articles 565-1 to 565-5 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) by the Law of 12 July 2013.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form C SCSp – Déclaration de création d'une société en commandite spéciale (RCS registration form) together with the contractual partnership agreement (contrat de société).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; partners freely determine capital contributions in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance must be obtained from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) confirming compliance with the Luxembourg fire protection code. A structural load certificate for the manufacturing space and an occupational health inspection by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) are required to certify safe working conditions, emergency exits and machine safety (CE marked equipment).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; partners freely determine capital contributions in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is required by law.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – the compression molding of food-grade silicone tactile sensory fidgets qualifies under ISIC 3240 as a small-scale novelty toy production; no sectoral manufacturing license is required under the Luxembourg Law of 24 July 1977 on the classification of dangerous establishments.

Category: No statutory operating permit; optional CE marking and Toy Safety Directive (2009/48/EC) compliance certificate required for market distribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for indirect taxes and VAT; central services and e-filing at www.guichet.lu / myguichet.lu, and the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) at www.lbrc.lu

E-government filing portal

Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a 13-digit registration number (matricule); file a direct tax registration notice with the ACD using Form 100 (and if applicable Form 100B) to obtain the fiscal number; file VAT registration Form 400 with the AEDT to obtain the VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits), which is mandatory for liable suppliers making taxable supplies above the exemption threshold, or voluntary where threshold not exceeded; foreign entities without a Luxembourg permanent establishment need no direct tax registration but must register for VAT for imports, distance sales, or domestic acquisitions above the thresholds, with possibility to appoint a fiscal representative when required by the AEDT.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a 13-digit registration number (matricule); file a direct tax registration notice with the ACD using Form 100 (and if applicable Form 100B) to obtain the fiscal number; file VAT registration Form 400 with the AEDT to obtain the VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits), which is mandatory for liable suppliers making taxable supplies above the exemption threshold, or voluntary where threshold not exceeded; foreign entities without a Luxembourg permanent establishment need no direct tax registration but must register for VAT for imports, distance sales, or domestic acquisitions above the thresholds, with possibility to appoint a fiscal representative when required by the AEDT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a domestic check-the-box classification regime. The SCSp is pass-through by default for income and net wealth tax purposes; no election is available to convert it into a corporate taxpayer. However, foreign investors may elect under their own tax classification rules (e.g., the U.S. check-the-box rules) to treat the SCSp as transparent or opaque; such classification mismatches are neutralised by Luxembourg's ATAD anti-hybrid rules, which operate automatically to deny deductions or reclassify income. For VAT, the SCSp remains an independent taxable person irrespective of direct-tax transparency and may voluntarily register below the threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for Luxembourg-established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers must register from the first euro when they make taxable supplies in Luxembourg, unless covered by the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) or OSS schemes; intra-community distance sales are registrable once aggregate remote sales cross the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplies qualify for 0% VAT where the silicone fidget toys are physically removed from Luxembourg and transported outside the European Union by the supplier or a carrier on the supplier's instructions, evidenced by a customs export declaration (SAD) accepted by the customs office of exit, together with transport documents and proof of delivery abroad. Services supplied to a non-EU business customer are outside the scope of VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules; services closely connected to the exported goods (e.g., freight, handling, inspection) may be zero-rated if provided to a customer established outside the EU and directly linked to the export. The supplier must keep full commercial, transport, customs, and banking records to prove the linkage between the sale and the physical export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD EX) validated by Luxembourg customs or the EU exporting customs office
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill/CMR) naming the exporter and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit evidence showing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport instruction / carrier or 3PL letter of shipment
  • Foreign customs entry document of the importing country (where available)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or e-invoicing stamping mandate is currently in force in Luxembourg. Taxable persons must issue invoices that satisfy the EU VAT invoicing rules and retain them for inspection; periodic VAT returns (Form 400) and European recapitulative statements (EC sales lists) are filed electronically via myguichet.lu/e-CDF. Structured e-invoicing (PEPPOL / e-Invoice EN 16931) may be used bilaterally, but no API reporting to the tax authority is required. Mandatory digital reporting is expected only upon implementation of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may re-open and reassess zero-rated export transactions within the statutory limitation period (generally 5 years, extended in cases of fraud or criminal proceedings). Any missing, false, or inconsistent export documentation causes the immediate reversal of the 0% treatment and makes the supply taxable at 17%, together with statutory interest and late-payment penalties. Deliberate or fraudulent export zero-rating attracts administrative fines up to 100% of the evaded VAT and criminal tax-fraud sanctions, including imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines, and the tax authorities may issue a retrospective recovery assessment for the entire amount together with a clawback of any input tax recovered. If export proceeds have not been repatriated within the 180-day foreign-exchange deadline, the relevant revenue may also be treated as non-export/taxable income and assessed accordingly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a euro-area member, applies the EU free movement of capital under Articles 63–65 TFEU; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There is no foreign-exchange control regime, no requirement of prior central-bank approval for inbound capital contributions, profit distribution, or repatriation, and no statutory restriction on transferring funds abroad. However, remittances must be executed through an authorised credit institution or payment institution and remain subject to CSSF anti-money-laundering checks, FATCA/CRS reporting, sanctions screening, and EU restrictive measures against targeted jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds in Luxembourg; a Luxembourg exporter may lawfully hold export receivables in EUR or foreign currency, and may convert freely at market rates. The only administrative obligations are statistical reporting to the BCL within the balance-of-payments framework when an invoiced export transaction exceeds the applicable reporting threshold, and general documentation/transfer-pricing compliance for related-party sales.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision, and payment institution oversight, and with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for sanctions and restrictive measures administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, only as a limited payment conduit. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide banking services in Luxembourg without a CSSF licence or an EU/EEA passport under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and no passport can be effective for a non-EEA EMI without a Luxembourg branch or authorised agent. EMIs may hold funds only as payment accounts, cannot grant credit, and are not suitable custodians for capital escrow; the SCSp should maintain a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank account as master treasury and use EMIs only for platform payouts with daily sweep to the master account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or working-capital injections must be wired by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent with the Luxembourg beneficiary account stated for LU_SCSP and the final credit made to the IBAN of the Tier-1 bank; the remitter must insert the BCL balance-of-payments purpose code corresponding to a direct investment in a Luxembourg partnership, generally mapped to the EU BoP class for financial account / direct investment equity, in SWIFT fields 50K and 70, and should quote the LEI of the SCSp and the bank's client reference. The bank will verify that the instruction is sanction-compliant, that the remitter is matched to the filed UBO declaration, and that the purpose code is consistent with a capital contribution; a copy of the capital call and signed subscription letter should be pre-delivered to the bank so funds can be credited to the capital escrow account without restriction. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €1,000,000; Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds €50,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as per EU ATAD and BEPS; SCSp is a partnership and generally not subject to corporate ESR, but must demonstrate real economic activity (office, staff, decision-making) to avoid being classified as a shell company or for tax residency purposes. The Luxembourg tax authority may challenge artificial arrangements lacking substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 or 100% of evaded tax; criminal prosecution; additional penalties up to 200% of tax due

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners may be held personally liable for civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits; reclassification as a taxable entity; retroactive tax assessments; fines up to €50,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1,000,000; criminal sanctions for money laundering; freezing of assets

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, imprisonment up to 5 years, and disqualification from serving as directors
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalties up to 10% of the adjustment; interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal charges for tax fraud in severe cases

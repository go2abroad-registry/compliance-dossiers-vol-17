Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016 and the Law of 23 September 2020; SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 to 710-25; electronic incorporation governed by the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 September 2017.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (Acte notarié de constitution) filed by the notary with the RCS via the LBR electronic portal and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up; cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution or with the notary (compte séquestre) until registration, evidenced by a certificate of deposit. Reduced minimum capital of EUR 1 is available for a micro-SARL under the 2016 reform where all shareholders are natural persons and the applicable small-entity thresholds are met.

Funding & unlock path: Class B low-occupancy office clearance: the landlord must provide the building fire safety dossier (dossier de sécurité) confirming compliance with the Law of 4 July 2017 on building safety. If the entity subsequently occupies a building with public access or high occupancy, a fire safety approval from the Administration des services de secours (CGDIS) is mandatory. Workplace clearance under the Labour Code is evidenced by the employer's registration with the CCSS for occupational accident coverage and the mandatory workplace health and safety declaration.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up; cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution or with the notary (compte séquestre) until registration, evidenced by a certificate of deposit. Reduced minimum capital of EUR 1 is available for a micro-SARL under the 2016 reform where all shareholders are natural persons and the applicable small-entity thresholds are met.

Not applicable to the described office-based regulatory consulting scope (ISIC 7490 / NAICS 541690). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (IMT) and net wealth tax (IS); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises for customs and export formalities; unified electronic access via MyGuichet.lu, the Luxembourg Business Registers portal at www.lbr.lu, and the ACD/AEDT online services at impotsdirects.public.lu and pbl.public.lu

E-government filing portal

1) Notarially incorporate the SARL and register with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) to obtain the Luxembourg business registration number (matricule RCS). 2) File the VAT registration application (form 100 or the MyGuichet electronic declaration) with the AEDT to obtain the national VAT identification number (format LU followed by 8 digits); a non-EU-established corporate parent or foreign branch may be required to appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative or to establish a fixed establishment for VAT purposes. 3) Register as an employer with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) for social-security contributions and with the ACD for salary-withholding tax; the SARL is simultaneously registered ex officio with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax upon notification of its registered office and corporate objects. 4) Activate the electronic filing channels (MyGuichet, eCDF, eTVA) and reconcile the RCS matricule with the VAT number and the CCSS employer registration for the monthly withholding and VAT declaration cycle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists: a Luxembourg SARL is irrebuttably treated as an entity-level corporate taxpayer subject to IRC, IMT, solidarity surcharge and net wealth tax on its worldwide income, with the participation exemption available for qualifying EU and third-country dividends and capital gains. Available statutory elections relevant to a MedTech regulatory consulting SARL include: (i) waiver of the EUR 35,000 VAT small-business exemption in favour of normal VAT registration; (ii) formation of a VAT group (assujetti unique) under the Luxembourg VAT law where financial, economic and organisational links exist; (iii) alignment and elective relief elections under the Luxembourg hybrid-mismatch rules (Articles 168ter to 168septies of the LIR, implementing ATAD 1 and ATAD 2); and (iv) the participation-exemption election exercised through the annual corporate income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 VAT-exclusive annual taxable turnover within Luxembourg for the small-business exemption; mandatory VAT registration applies once this threshold is exceeded; non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg have no registration threshold; the EU cross-border B2C distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 also applies; B2B supplies rendered to customers established outside the EU are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and therefore do not count toward the registration threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) physical exports of goods definitively dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU customs territory under Article 63 of the Luxembourg VAT law (transposing Article 146 of the VAT Directive), provided the exporter holds evidence that the goods left the EU within 90 days of dispatch; and (b) B2B services, including FDA 510(k) filing and CE Mark regulatory consulting, supplied to a customer established outside the EU, because the place of supply follows the customer's place of establishment under Article 44 of the VAT Directive and the supply is therefore outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (economically 0%). Eligibility requires written proof of the customer's non-EU establishment (valid local VAT or tax registration certificate, certificate of residence, or equivalent official document), a signed consulting contract defining the regulatory filing scope, and itemised documentation evidencing services actually rendered. Services supplied to EU-established customers do not qualify and remain taxable by reverse charge in the customer's member state under Article 196 of the VAT Directive.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) electronically lodged in the AES/ICS export system with the Administration des douanes et accises naming the exporting SARL as consignor
  • Transport and exit evidence: Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (airfreight), CMR consignment note (road), or written confirmation from the customs office of exit
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, packing list and, where applicable, a Certificate of Free Sale or export licence for medical devices
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT credit advice, or foreign-currency inward remittance proof evidencing settlement of the export receivable
  • For zero-rated offshore consulting services: signed services contract, itemised debit note, customer VAT or tax registration certificate or non-EU residence certificate, and the regulatory file reference such as the FDA 510(k) submission number or CE/Notified Body certificate reference

No real-time fiscal clearance engine, digital stamping or API-based e-invoice validation is currently in force for domestic Luxembourg B2B supplies; mandatory B2G electronic invoicing has applied to invoices addressed to Luxembourg public entities since 1 January 2023 (structured Peppol BIS 3.0 or equivalent format); since 2024 taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; Luxembourg is transposing the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, which will introduce transaction-based digital reporting and mandatory e-invoicing for domestic supplies as of 2030 and for cross-border supplies as of 2035.

Luxembourg VAT law does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline, but a 180-day inward-remittance settlement window is the documentary benchmark applied by the AEDT and Luxembourg banks when accepting zero-rating evidence; failure to show payment settlement within this window materially weakens the substantiation file and exposes the invoices to retrospective VAT reassessment. Export zero-rating and offshore-service invoicing are high-priority audit targets: the AEDT may claw back the VAT with statutory late interest for up to the general 6-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in fraud cases, and may impose administrative penalties of 25% to 100% of the evaded VAT under Articles 398 to 400 of the General Tax Law. Fraudulent intent exposes the SARL and its managers to criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; customs offences additionally attract fines of up to double the customs debt or the value of the goods under the Union Customs Code and Luxembourg customs legislation; participation in round-tripping or carrousel-type export chains may result in joint and several liability and denial of zero-rating for the entire invoice chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) enjoys unrestricted convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions in Luxembourg. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements, and no surrender obligations under national law. Full liberalisation of capital movements and payments is guaranteed by Articles 63 to 65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), subject only to the exceptional safeguard measures of Articles 64(2) and 66 TFEU, none of which are currently in force. Profit remittance by a SARL is not subject to exchange-control approval; the only hurdles are standard corporate formalities, namely (i) the statutory net-asset solvency test, (ii) the general meeting's distribution resolution, (iii) possible Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15% (reducible under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where conditions are met), and (iv) the bank's AML/KYC source-of-funds verification. Cross-border payments to or from sanctioned persons or entities are blocked under EU restrictive measures as enforced by the CSSF; therefore, counterparty sanctions screening is an implicit prerequisite to convertibility in practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds or service-fee receipts. The SARL may lawfully maintain EUR and USD balances in Luxembourg and abroad without BCL approval. The only compliance burden is statistical: credit institutions report balance-of-payments data to the BCL for individual client transactions exceeding the €12,500 direct-reporting threshold, and the SARL should accurately state payment purpose codes for these filings.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank, operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism of the European Central Bank (ECB). Foreign exchange and payment oversight is jointly exercised by the BCL and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A Luxembourg-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) or an EU-passported payment service provider (PSP) such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or PayPal Business can serve the SARL's low-risk consulting fee collections (bank wire and credit card rails) and provide multi-currency receiving accounts in EUR and USD. However, the EMI route has material legal constraints: (i) under Directive 2009/110/EC Article 6(3), an EMI may grant credit only when incidental and strictly limited to €150 per transaction and €500 aggregate outstanding at any time, so no overdraft or working capital facility is available; (ii) EMIs cannot provide FX hedging or term-deposit treasury products, which must be arranged with a credit institution; (iii) USD clearing is dependent on unregulated correspondent chains and may trigger enhanced OFAC screening; (iv) the EMI's onboarding relies on the same CSSF AML/CTF framework (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended), so UBO and source-of-fund checks are not waived; and (v) the BCL balance-of-payments reporting obligation rests ultimately on the SARL's accounting records, requiring the SARL to retain all payment purpose and counterparty data itself. Consequently, the EMI is viable as a collection-side overlay rail but is not a substitute for a full tier-1 corporate banking relationship in Luxembourg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (i) the shareholder's originating bank sends an SWIFT MT103 wire in EUR to the SARL's EUR IBAN at a Luxembourg tier-1 bank; (ii) the payment must carry a transparent purpose code such as 'Capital injection / CJ01' or the BCL balance-of-payments code for equity capital contributions, and the beneficiary account must be identified as the blocked capital escrow sub-account pending RCS registration; (iii) the Luxembourg receiving bank will hold funds on deposit blocked in favour of the notary until the deed of incorporation is filed with the RCS and the minimum capital certificate is delivered; (iv) the bank will require the notarised share subscription deed, the notary's attestation of full payment, updated RBE filings made within one month of any UBO change, and the business plan before lifting the block; (v) all deposit and release events must be reported by the bank to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics if the transaction exceeds the €12,500 threshold. For subsequent consultant-fee inflows and client credits exceeding the threshold, the SARL should instruct clients to state purpose code 'Service fee / S010' with the contract reference so as to avoid AML misclassification.Clearing rails for trade and regulatory settlements: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant (up to €100,000 per transaction) for disbursements to EU beneficiaries, including notified bodies for CE Mark reviews and EU-based subcontractors; (ii) SWIFT MT103 via a USD correspondent bank (typically CHIPS or Fedwire in the United States) for submission of FDA user fees to the US Department of Health and Human Services through the US Treasury lockbox, using the Treasury concentration bank's ABA routing (e.g., Citibank New York, ABA 021000089); (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) where the SARL imports physical medical device prototypes or pre-finances testing equipment, with the LC issued against shipping and inspection documents; (iv) EURO1/STEP2 high-value clearing for urgent cross-border corporate payments in EUR above the SEPA Instant cap; and (v) credit-card acquiring rails (Visa and Mastercard through a Luxembourg or EU-passported PSP, PCI DSS compliant) for inbound consulting-fee collection, with settlement to the SARL's operational account in EUR or, where enabled by the PSP, in USD before an internal exchange into EUR.Outright Deliverable EUR/USD FX Forward — OTC instrument executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank's short-form FX Master Agreement with a CSSF-regulated credit institution; permitted without prior authorisation for a non-financial counterparty (NFC-), and exempt from the EMIR clearing obligation so long as the SARL's rolling average gross notional remains below the €3 billion per-asset-class clearing threshold, which is far above the SARL's anticipated fee volumes.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. A Luxembourg-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) or an EU-passported payment service provider (PSP) such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or PayPal Business can serve the SARL's low-risk consulting fee collections (bank wire and credit card rails) and provide multi-currency receiving accounts in EUR and USD. However, the EMI route has material legal constraints: (i) under Directive 2009/110/EC Article 6(3), an EMI may grant credit only when incidental and strictly limited to €150 per transaction and €500 aggregate outstanding at any time, so no overdraft or working capital facility is available; (ii) EMIs cannot provide FX hedging or term-deposit treasury products, which must be arranged with a credit institution; (iii) USD clearing is dependent on unregulated correspondent chains and may trigger enhanced OFAC screening; (iv) the EMI's onboarding relies on the same CSSF AML/CTF framework (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended), so UBO and source-of-fund checks are not waived; and (v) the BCL balance-of-payments reporting obligation rests ultimately on the SARL's accounting records, requiring the SARL to retain all payment purpose and counterparty data itself. Consequently, the EMI is viable as a collection-side overlay rail but is not a substitute for a full tier-1 corporate banking relationship in Luxembourg.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate rate for Luxembourg City (domestic CIT 15% on first EUR 175,000 and 17% on excess, plus 7% solidarity surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% general domestic rate; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding, 12-month minimum) and under most DTTs with substantial corporate shareholdings; treaty rates for portfolio investors often between 5% and 15%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared and maintained where the annual book value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds EUR 500,000; Master File obligations apply to groups with consolidated turnover of EUR 750 million or more

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies economic substance analysis rather than annual ESR filings: the availability of IP Box benefits, the 0% Parent-Subsidiary exemption, treaty access, and participation exemption depends on genuine premises, operative staff, actual business-risk control, and decision-making in Luxembourg (Art. 56 LIR; ATAD and MLI/PPT)

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Concealment

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus a penalty of 50% to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal fine from EUR 10,000 to EUR 100,000; imprisonment up to five years under Article 1093 LIR

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or incomplete returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax and penalties; courts may impose travel bans, disqualification from board positions, and custodial sentences in aggravated cases
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Shortfall

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for Luxembourg tax purposes; recharacterization of payments as deemed dividends subject to 15% WHT; retroactive interest and punitive penalties up to EUR 100,000 under abuse-of-law rules

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the resulting barriers to withholding tax if they directed funds abroad while retaining no economic substance; personal asset confiscation can follow a fraud conviction
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax primary adjustment, secondary adjustment, exclusion of deduction, late interest, and a negligence penalty of 20% to 50% of the underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sanctioned the misstatement can be assessed jointly for the penalty share and prosecuted for tax fraud; a civil clawback extends to personal assets
AML / Financial Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Entity-level administrative fine up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover; freezing of assets and disgorgement; referral to the Public Prosecutor for criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors face up to five years' imprisonment for money laundering, personal monetary liability for both the entity's obligations and state claims, and an EU-wide prohibition on serving as a director of a regulated company

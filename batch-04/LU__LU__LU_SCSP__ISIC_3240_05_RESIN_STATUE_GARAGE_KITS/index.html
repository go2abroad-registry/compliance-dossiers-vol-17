Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income tax matters and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax matters. Central e-Government portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu. Corporate registry: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / lbr.lu) and the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). Direct tax information portal: impotsdirects.public.lu. VAT portal: aed.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp, a foreign-owned or cross-border structure must: (1) execute an initial notarial deed before a Luxembourg notary; (2) file the deed and required annexes through the RCS/eJustice portal, obtaining the RCS registration number and the Luxembourg national business number ('matricule'), which serves as the principal tax identifier; (3) file the registered RCS extract with the Administration des contributions directes if a direct tax file is needed; for a fiscally transparent SCSp the partners are assessed directly, but the partnership must file municipal business tax returns if it carries on a Luxembourg commercial activity; (4) apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu once taxable supplies commence or the annual VAT threshold is exceeded; the issued VAT number follows the format LU + 8 digits; (5) ensure foreign partner identification details, tax residence certificates and ultimate beneficial owner declarations are submitted for AML/KYC and withholding tax administration where required.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a Luxembourg SCSp, a foreign-owned or cross-border structure must: (1) execute an initial notarial deed before a Luxembourg notary; (2) file the deed and required annexes through the RCS/eJustice portal, obtaining the RCS registration number and the Luxembourg national business number ('matricule'), which serves as the principal tax identifier; (3) file the registered RCS extract with the Administration des contributions directes if a direct tax file is needed; for a fiscally transparent SCSp the partners are assessed directly, but the partnership must file municipal business tax returns if it carries on a Luxembourg commercial activity; (4) apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu once taxable supplies commence or the annual VAT threshold is exceeded; the issued VAT number follows the format LU + 8 digits; (5) ensure foreign partner identification details, tax residence certificates and ultimate beneficial owner declarations are submitted for AML/KYC and withholding tax administration where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp is statutorily a transparent 'société de personnes' for Luxembourg income tax purposes: profits, losses and distributions flow through to partners and are not taxed at partnership level for corporate income tax, although municipal business tax may be triggered by a Luxembourg commercial activity carried on through the SCSp. No domestic election is available to convert the SCSp into an opaque entity for general Luxembourg corporate income tax purposes. Partnership deed provisions may freely allocate income and loss, subject to substance and anti-abuse rules. Under EU ATAD anti-hybrid measures, if the SCSp is treated as a taxable entity in a foreign partner's jurisdiction, Luxembourg will apply corresponding provisions to deny mismatching deductions or alter the tax characterization where necessary; no check-the-box filing is made to the Luxembourg tax authority. An SCSp may elect into the Luxembourg VAT group provisions (assujettis uniques) if the statutory requirements are met, but that is an indirect-tax consolidation election and does not alter direct-tax transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for the Luxembourg small-business exemption; VAT registration is mandatory once this threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is available below the threshold. Non-resident suppliers, intra-Community distance sales and reverse-charge supplies may have separate registration obligations regardless of this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the supply of resin statue garage kits when the goods are sold by the SCSp and physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting on their behalf. The supplier must obtain and retain within 90 days of dispatch sufficient documentary proof that the goods have left EU territory, including customs export acceptance with item-level details and independent transport evidence. Services that are directly linked to exported goods, including transport and incidental handling from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU, are also zero-rated. B2B services supplied to a non-EU taxable person are generally outside Luxembourg VAT under place-of-supply rules if the recipient is an entrepreneur in a non-EU country; B2C digital services are covered by the One-Stop-Shop mechanism and are not zero-rated exports. For tangible goods, physical export and compliance with customs formalities remain the decisive conditions.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) electronically lodged in the Luxembourg Export Control System and released with a Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of lading, airway bill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and sales contract
  • Packing list and container/shipment manifest
  • Proof of transport, freight invoice and insurance documents covering the shipment
  • Incoterms statement and customs value declaration when required by customs
  • Customs broker power of attorney and forwarding agent confirmation of exit from the EU
  • Payment evidence or inward remittance certificate substantiating receipt of export proceeds where relevant for the financial and FX audit trail

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or government digital stamping regime applicable to private-sector B2B/B2C transactions. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices to Luxembourg public authorities, which must be structured as PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 XML and transmitted through a certified PEPPOL access point. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu / e-file. Export declarations are lodged in the EU AES/ECS system through Luxembourg customs, generating a Movement Reference Number; no additional private API stamping or equipment clearance is required.

The Luxembourg VAT administration (AEDT) may deny zero-rating and assess 17% Luxembourg VAT on the export sale if the required customs and transport evidence is incomplete, inaccurate or produced after the statutory verification deadline. In bad-faith cases, penalties range from 10% to 200% of the evaded VAT, plus late-payment interest, and criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment for VAT fraud. The ordinary statute of limitations for a VAT clawback assessment is five years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud. Retrospective clawback extends to all partners who benefitted from the zero-rating and to the SCSp itself; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day period without a valid economic or exchange-control justification may be treated as a substantial impediment to the export proof and may trigger recharacterization, additional penalties and denial of refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro and all freely traded currencies are fully convertible in Luxembourg. No exchange control authorization is required for current account transactions, capital account operations, profit remittances, or SCSp distributions, provided anti-money laundering (AML), tax reporting, and beneficial ownership declaration obligations are met. Non-resident partners may receive distributions without FX approval; only banking due diligence and tax transparency filings apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation deadline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances indefinitely in resident credit institutions. For inbound renminbi receipts derived from Chinese buyers, convertibility is subject to offshore CNY clearing constraints; USD and EUR balances may be held without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) regulates banking and payment institutions in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Luxembourg may support e-commerce settlement flows; however, the principal operating account of the SCSp should be held with a Luxembourg credit institution to ensure banking-secrecy compliance, smooth FX execution, and acceptance of capital injections. Non-EEA EMIs have no automatic passporting right and would require CSSF registration or local partnership with a licensed payment institution, which is commercially unattractive at this volume. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million (goods) or EUR 500,000 (services); Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million; both thresholds apply to the Luxembourg entity

Economic substance scope: SCSp must demonstrate economic substance if it benefits from tax treaties or EU directives; requires adequate physical presence (office, staff, equipment), decision-making in Luxembourg, and no artificial arrangements; failure to meet substance may lead to denial of treaty benefits and reclassification as a shell company under ATAD and OECD BEPS rules

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 or 2x the evaded tax; imprisonment up to 5 years; mandatory publication of judgment; loss of tax benefits

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and up to 5 years imprisonment
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification as a taxable entity, retroactive tax assessments, penalties up to EUR 250,000; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance; travel bans and disqualification from directorship
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment); freezing of assets

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; civil penalties, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax, interest (0.6% per month), and penalties up to 50% of the underpaid tax; potential criminal charges for tax fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for transfer pricing adjustments if they approved the mispricing; civil restitution and travel bans

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, “RCS”), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (“LBR”); electronic filing through guichet.lu and the LBR portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), together with the Law of 12 July 2013 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); SCSp provisions set out at Articles 118 et seq., including formation, contribution, management, registration and publication rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Partnership deed and registration declaration (contrat de société et déclaration d’enregistrement au registre de commerce et des sociétés), followed by mandatory publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (“RESA”).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; no statutory escrow account or notarial capital deposit is required. The amount and timing of contributions are freely set out in the partnership agreement. At least one general partner with unlimited liability and one limited partner whose liability is limited to their contribution must exist.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification of the registered office/warehouse must be obtained from the Inspection du Service d’Incendie et de Secours (ISIS) where premises are leased for storage. Structural load verification is required for racked warehouses and must be confirmed by the landlord’s structural engineer or an approved Luxembourg bureau de contrôle. Occupational safety clearance is ensured under the Labour Code by conducting a workplace risk assessment, appointing an external accident-prevention service, and providing mandatory staff training.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; no statutory escrow account or notarial capital deposit is required. The amount and timing of contributions are freely set out in the partnership agreement. At least one general partner with unlimited liability and one limited partner whose liability is limited to their contribution must exist.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority – for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority – for income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; unified electronic filing portal: MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign SCSp structure creates Luxembourg nexus: VAT registration is required as soon as any taxable activity is commenced in Luxembourg, whereas direct-tax registration is required only where a Luxembourg permanent establishment, real estate, or payroll presence exists. Step 2: Appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative unless the foreign entity is established in the EU/EEA or in a jurisdiction with which Luxembourg has concluded mutual administrative assistance and recovery arrangements. Step 3: File the Luxembourg VAT registration application (déclaration d'immatriculation à la TVA, Formulaire 102) electronically via MyGuichet.lu, signed by the general partner/managers. Step 4: Attach the foreign certificate of incorporation, excerpt from the home-country trade register, proof of identity of the general partner, and the VAT representative mandate. Step 5: The AEDT issues the national VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits and, where applicable, the ACD attributes a Luxembourg national identification number for direct-tax and social security purposes; the entity is inserted into the Luxembourg VAT register with effect from the registration date.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax purposes by default: no corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax is levied at entity level, and partners are taxed in their own hands on their profit shares. Statutory elections available: (1) under Article 182bis LIR, the SCSp may validly elect to be taxed as a capital company (option pour le régime des sociétés de capitaux), thereby becoming subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the election is binding for a statutory minimum period. (2) The SCSp may form or join a Luxembourg VAT group (assujetti unique) with related taxable persons, making the group a single VAT taxpayer. (3) The transparency default may be overridden under the ATAD hybrid mismatch rules where the same payment would otherwise produce dual inclusion or non-inclusion mismatch, requiring neutralisation at the partners' level. (4) The small-business VAT franchise exemption may be declined in favour of ordinary VAT taxation with input VAT recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (net annual turnover) for the small-business franchise exemption available to established Luxembourg taxable persons; no registration threshold applies to non-established foreign entities, which are registrable from the first euro of Luxembourg taxable turnover. For EU distance sales of goods to Luxembourg consumers: EUR 10,000 pan-EU harmonised threshold. An SCSp actively distributing electronic components will ordinarily exceed the franchise threshold and is therefore required to register for Luxembourg VAT from the date the threshold is exceeded or, where the option for ordinary taxation is exercised, from the first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods (excess multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors falling under ECCN 8542) physically exported outside the EU are zero-rated at 0% VAT (exempt with credit). The zero rate is conditional on: (1) the supply being directly linked to dispatch or transport by the supplier, or on the supplier's behalf, to a destination outside the EU customs territory; (2) export departure being completed within the statutory period (goods must leave the EU within 90 days); (3) the supplier holding complete documentary proof of physical removal before claiming the zero rate. Intra-EU B2B dispatches are likewise zero-rated where the customer's VAT ID is valid under VIES and the recapitulative statement is filed. Services connected to exported goods and rendered to non-EU business customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules, preserving direct or pro-rata input VAT recovery. No special export licence applies to EAR99 electronic components absent dual-use diversion risks.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document with Movement Reference Number) issued by Luxembourg or EU customs at export clearance
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and Incoterms 2020 contract evidencing exporter, consignee, quantity, unit prices and foreign destination
  • Transport title: ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR international consignment note proving physical delivery outside the EU customs territory
  • Warehouse release and removal records for the passive electronic inventory, together with customs exit confirmation
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank settlement evidence demonstrating collection of export proceeds and FX repatriation

Luxembourg currently operates no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for domestic B2B invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to B2G invoices addressed to the Luxembourg central government, which must be submitted in structured electronic format over the PEPPOL network. Periodic VAT e-returns must be accompanied, for medium and large taxpayers and certain high-risk sectors, by SAF-T digital accounting files (fichier d'échange), enabling AEDT analytical cross-matching of invoices, customs declarations and payment flows. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) legislative package will, upon transposition, introduce mandatory e-invoicing and transaction e-reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies on the EU-wide statutory timeline.

The AEDT cross-matches zero-rated sales declared in VAT returns against actual customs export declarations (MRN database) and bank inflows; any inconsistency or missing documentation triggers reassessment of the turnover at the standard 17% VAT rate, disallowance of connected input VAT credit, and issuance of an enforceable assessment with monthly default interest. Administrative penalties of up to EUR 250,000 apply, and late-filing surcharges accrue from the statutory due date. Deliberate fabrication of export documents, sham foreign counterparties, circular export schemes and fraudulent repatriation structures engage the criminal tax-fraud provisions of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), attracting fines and imprisonment, joint and several liability of the general partner and the appointed VAT representative, and retrospective clawback for up to 10 years in cases of established fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in a Euro-area economy. Luxembourg maintains no general exchange-control regime; the euro is the domestic currency and is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Capital movements, dividend distributions, profit repatriation, and capital repayment are unrestricted under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC. The legal basis for the SCSp being fiscally transparent means profit remittances to partners are generally treated as allocations rather than dividend distributions, mitigating Luxembourg dividend withholding tax exposure. Operational hurdles remain only in the form of AML/KYC documentation and, where applicable, statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into euros, and no explicit repatriation timeline. Export receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained, converted, or disbursed freely. The BCL requires only statistical balance-of-payments declarations for direct investment and trade flows, not advance approval or conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; no separate foreign-exchange control administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions authorized by the CSSF under the Law of 27 July 2018 may provide payment accounts and remote onboarding, but they generally cannot provide SWIFT MT103 treasury clearing, TARGET2 direct access, or commercial credit lines. Non-EU offshore EMIs are not viable as primary treasury rails because they lack SEPA participation, TARGET2 settlement, and CSSF oversight. The recommended use of an EMI is limited to low-balance operational float; capital escrow and trade disbursements should be routed through a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's bank to the SCSp's euro IBAN reference account. The payment narrative must clearly indicate 'capital contribution' and cite the applicable clause of the SCSp partnership agreement under the Luxembourg Law on Commercial Companies of 10 August 1915, as amended. No Luxembourg exchange-control approval or prior BCL authorisation is required, but statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL may apply. For inbound wires exceeding EUR 10,000 from non-EU jurisdictions, banks will require source-of-wealth evidence, a contribution resolution signed by the general partner, an updated subscription schedule, and confirmation that the funds are non-taxable capital in nature rather than trading revenue.Trade settlement is structured through (a) SEPA credit transfers for euro-denominated supplier invoices within the SEPA zone, (b) SWIFT MT103 (opc) high-value international wires for non-euro supplier payments via TARGET2 or correspondent clearing, and (c) irrevocable commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for large procurement contracts of multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors from Asian manufacturers. Documentary collections and sight drafts are used for lower-risk open-account trade.

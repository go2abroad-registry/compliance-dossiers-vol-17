Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) division, Luxembourg, served through the eJustice / MyGuichet one-stop-shop electronic portal.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 34-48 (Société en Commandite Simple rules) and Articles 49-50 (Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp)), as introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013; supplemented by the amended Law of 19 December 2008 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the amended Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de société (partnership agreement / deed of constitution) filed together with the electronic RCS A-Form (formulaire A) via the eJustice platform; statutory publication is effected in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital, no bank escrow / blocking account and no statutory capital account rules apply to an SCSp under the amended Law of 10 August 1915 (Articles 49-50); contributions and future capital calls, if any, are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and evidenced by contractual capital accounts rather than subscribed share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Communal fire-safety inspection for the storage and dispatch area, culminating in the fire-safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the municipal authority with the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours; occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) based on a workplace risk assessment and general protection statement under the Luxembourg Labour Code; in the absence of mezzanine racking or machinery, no structural load inspection is triggered, but any high-level shelf systems must respect the manufacturer's load ratings and the ITM storage-safety guidance.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital, no bank escrow / blocking account and no statutory capital account rules apply to an SCSp under the amended Law of 10 August 1915 (Articles 49-50); contributions and future capital calls, if any, are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and evidenced by contractual capital accounts rather than subscribed share capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — VAT and registration; consolidated e-portals: MyGuichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu, lbr.lu

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned SCSp must (1) execute the partnership deed (private deed suffices unless real property is contributed) and appoint an EU/Luxembourg-resident general partner; (2) register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) within one month, obtaining the RCS number; (3) publish the formation notice in the RESA electronic compendium; (4) file electronic form 645 / N°TVA registration on MyGuichet addressed to the AEDT, attaching the RCS extract, deed and IBAN; (5) obtain the Luxembourg national identification number (NIN) and a 'LU' VAT identification number issued by the AEDT; (6) designate a local VAT representative where the general partner and all partners are non-EU residents. No minimum capital is required; the SCSp is not subject to corporate income tax or municipal business tax owing to statutory fiscal transparency.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident-owned SCSp must (1) execute the partnership deed (private deed suffices unless real property is contributed) and appoint an EU/Luxembourg-resident general partner; (2) register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) within one month, obtaining the RCS number; (3) publish the formation notice in the RESA electronic compendium; (4) file electronic form 645 / N°TVA registration on MyGuichet addressed to the AEDT, attaching the RCS extract, deed and IBAN; (5) obtain the Luxembourg national identification number (NIN) and a 'LU' VAT identification number issued by the AEDT; (6) designate a local VAT representative where the general partner and all partners are non-EU residents. No minimum capital is required; the SCSp is not subject to corporate income tax or municipal business tax owing to statutory fiscal transparency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Fiscal transparency is statutory, not elective; no check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg. Available elections: (a) VAT fiscal unity (assujetti unique) under Art. 61 of the VAT Law; (b) small-business franchise election below EUR 25,000 VAT-exclusive turnover; (c) where the sole managing partner is an S.à r.l./S.A., corporate income tax and municipal business tax apply at that partner level. The ATAD reverse-hybrid rule (Law of 31 March 2017) may recharacterise the SCSp as taxable if all investors are non-EU residents and treat the vehicle as opaque in their own jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual VAT-exclusive turnover for the small-business franchise exemption; compulsory registration from the first euro where a Luxembourg establishment is used; for distance sales of musical instruments and audio gear to EU consumers, the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold triggers OSS registration and destination-state VAT liability.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated under Art. 61(1) of the VAT Law when goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or for the account of a non-EU customer and leave EU territory within 90 days of the supply; offshore services (software downloads, remote technical support, e-commerce fulfilment) to non-EU business customers are zero-rated under place-of-supply rules; intra-EU consumer distance sales remain taxable in the destination member state and are reported through OSS rather than as Luxembourg exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A) lodged via Luxembourg Customs' electronic AES/EOS platform, classifying goods under applicable HS codes (e.g. 9206/9207-8471/8518 for musical instruments and audio equipment)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract matching the declared customs value and identifying the non-EU buyer (Incoterms 2020)
  • Official proof-of-exit evidence: customs stamp, T1/transit discharge, or carrier exit confirmation from the shared EU customs systems (ICS/AES)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 settlement record evidencing foreign-currency payment into the SCSp's bank account
  • Carrier track-and-trace logs and Stripe/PayPal platform settlement reports retained for at least six years

No real-time clearance or digital-stamping mechanism exists; there is no transactional fiscal API approval. B2G electronic invoicing via PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / EN 16931 is mandatory since 1 January 2021; B2B e-invoices are legally accepted with recipient consent; VAT returns and e-CDF/e-FDF archiving are filed electronically through MyGuichet. Mandatory structured e-invoicing under the EU ViDA initiative is pending and not yet transposed.

The AEDT applies a standard 3-year assessment window, extended to 5 years (10 in cases of fraud) for export exemptions; missing or fabricated exit evidence triggers retroactive VAT reassessment at 17% plus statutory late-payment interest. Fines under Art. 65 VAT Law range from EUR 750 to EUR 12,500 per incident; fraudulent zero-rating claims are punishable under Art. 67 VAT Law with fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years, joint and several liability of the general partner, VAT-number suspension or deregistration, and inclusion in cross-border mutual-assistance exchange.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg provides full and unrestricted currency convertibility on both the current and capital accounts under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the Eurosystem legal framework. No exchange controls, foreign-currency account licensing, or profit-remittance restrictions exist for a fiscally transparent SCSp; distributions to partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax. Practical remittance frictions are limited to AML/CFT source-of-funds verification, CRS/FATCA reporting, ATAD substance documentation, and EU/UN sanctions screening applicable to the receiving jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-time requirements apply to export receipts in Luxembourg; exporters may freely retain and deploy EUR or any foreign currency. The sole regulatory overlay is the BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting regime, executed by resident credit institutions on behalf of the BCL, with direct enterprise reporting required where an individual cross-border payment exceeds the applicable statistical threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), as the national central bank within the Eurosystem, with banking supervision jointly exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); monetary policy is set by the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High as a secondary settlement rail for Stripe/PayPal sweeps; Low as the sole primary treasury institution. Legal constraints: under PSD2 and the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, only a Luxembourg-licensed or EEA-passported payment/e-money institution (EMI) may legally open an e-money account for the SCSp; an offshore (non-EEA) EMI cannot hold client funds unless it obtains a Luxembourg branch licence or an EEA passport. EMI balances are safeguarded in segregated accounts but are not covered by the Luxembourg Deposit Guarantee Scheme, and EMIs impose full RBE/UBO disclosure and source-of-funds files equivalent to credit-institution standards for pass-through partnerships.

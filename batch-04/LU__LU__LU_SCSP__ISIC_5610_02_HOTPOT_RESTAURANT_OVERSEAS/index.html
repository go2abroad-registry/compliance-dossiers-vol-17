A stand-alone virtual mailbox with no physical Luxembourg presence is not permissible for a bankable SCSp engaged in overseas investment intermediation such as the proposed hotpot restaurant holding structure. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des Douanes et Accises for customs and excise; registration and publication effected through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) platform (www.lbr.lu) and the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-established SCSp must appoint a Luxembourg-based authorised representative, file the constitutive deed (notarised) with the RCS via the LBR electronic portal, obtain the RCS business registration number (B number), and receive a national identification number (matricule) assigned by the ACD. VAT registration is then filed with the AEDT on Form 640 (for non-resident taxable persons) or Form 640G (for Luxembourg-established companies), producing a LUXXXXXXXXX VAT identification number valid for EU-recognised transactions; the entire process typically completes within 10–20 business days using an apostilled certificate of incorporation, partnership agreement, and proof of registered address.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign-established SCSp must appoint a Luxembourg-based authorised representative, file the constitutive deed (notarised) with the RCS via the LBR electronic portal, obtain the RCS business registration number (B number), and receive a national identification number (matricule) assigned by the ACD. VAT registration is then filed with the AEDT on Form 640 (for non-resident taxable persons) or Form 640G (for Luxembourg-established companies), producing a LUXXXXXXXXX VAT identification number valid for EU-recognised transactions; the entire process typically completes within 10–20 business days using an apostilled certificate of incorporation, partnership agreement, and proof of registered address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory default under the Luxembourg partnership law of 12 July 2013: no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax at the SCSp level, with partners assessed on their distributive share. No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Luxembourg domestic law; however, express transparency is confirmed in the partnership agreement, and a partner may make a US Form 8832 classification election for US federal tax purposes. An optional election to constitute a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 63 of the Luxembourg VAT Law is available for closely linked entities, and hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD (law of 21 December 2017) may be elected or documented to neutralise double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg-established taxable persons carrying out supplies above EUR 35,000 annual turnover are mandatorily VAT-registered; below that threshold, the small business exemption may apply absent a waiver. Non-resident entities (including an SCSp) that undertake any taxable transaction in Luxembourg, regardless of turnover, are subject to compulsory VAT registration on a reverse-charge or direct-liability basis; a foreign SCSp holding an overseas hotpot restaurant and condiment supply chain will generally have no Luxembourg VAT registration unless it imports goods for resale, engages in Luxembourg establishment, or derivatively registers to exercise EU VAT recovery rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods to non-EU destinations is zero-rated under Article 50 of the Luxembourg VAT Law where the exporter proves delivery outside the EU and is identified as the exporter on the customs export declaration; intra-EU dispatches of condiment products to a VAT-registered customer are zero-rated under Article 41 subject to valid EU VAT numbers and ESL reporting. Services physically performed offshore (including catering operations and overseas hotpot restaurant management fees) fall outside the Luxembourg territorial scope and are excluded from Luxembourg VAT. The invoice must include the customer's and supplier's VAT numbers, the zero-rated 'Article 50' reference, and be supported by transport and payment evidence retained for a minimum of ten years.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD/Douane single administrative document) lodged with the Administration des Douanes et Accises via DGCE/presentation to the LU export office
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms clause and proof of title transfer
  • Packing list and export carrier's certificate of arrival
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidence of payment from the non-EU purchaser

No national real-time transactional clearance or digital stamping obligation exists; Luxembourg applies the EU invoicing directive (2010/45/EU) permitting PDF/e-invoices with advanced electronic signatures or EDI under mutual agreement. Mandatory Peppol-based B2G e-invoicing applies to suppliers invoicing Luxembourg public authorities through the national e-Facturation platform. All VAT returns, recapitulative statements (European Sales List), Intrastat filings, and periodic listings must be submitted electronically via MyGuichet.lu (eTVA module) or a certified EDI channel; no continuous transaction control systems or automated hourly fiscal reporting are in force.

The AEDT and Luxembourg customs may retroactively deny zero-rating for up to ten years upon incomplete export proof, assessing output VAT at the standard rate plus late-payment interest (EURIBOR + 5 percentage points). Fraudulent claims or forged export documents trigger double taxation penalties (generally 10% to 25% of the evaded tax), criminal fines up to EUR 25,000 and possible imprisonment for VAT fraud under the Luxembourg Criminal Code; directors and general partners of the SCSp face joint civil liability for clawed-back VAT, statutory interest, and penalties, alongside potential blacklisting of the operator from the registered exporter scheme.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Luxembourg applies no exchange controls under EU law, and there are no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or partnership distributions from an SCSp. Capital contribution, profit distribution, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred to non-residents, subject only to AML/KYC verification and statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no repatriation deadlines for export receipts. All export proceeds may be retained in any currency, deposited in Luxembourg bank accounts or abroad, and are freely deployable. The only administrative duty is the monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments declaration to the BCL if cumulative cross-border flows exceed the reporting threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem; banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while capital movement and exchange control matters are governed by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the EU Capital Movement Regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions under EU payment services rules are viable for low-value operational payments, merchant acquiring, and prepaid cards. However, the SCSp structure with overseas direct-operated restaurants will likely face higher compliance scrutiny due to ownership complexity. EMIs typically cannot provide overdrafts or commercial letters of credit, and may require a partner bank for segregated trust accounts. The core treasury, capital escrow, and trade finance functions should be anchored at a Tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required where the Luxembourg entity's annual related-party transactions reach or exceed EUR 150,000 and the group's consolidated turnover is at least EUR 50 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million. Even below the threshold, comparable TP documentation must be available on request to support the arm's-length nature of controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a separate codified Economic Substance Regulations statute, but substance is enforced through the domestic GAAR (Article 6 LIR), OECD BEPS/ATAD implementation, transfer pricing rules and tax ruling practice. An SCSp claiming Luxembourg tax residence or treaty benefits must demonstrate substantive local economic activity: local management and decision-making, a registered office, employees, bank accounts and audited or prepared accounts. A shell SCSp without these attributes is re-characterised, its tax transparency may be denied, and treaty benefits may be rejected.

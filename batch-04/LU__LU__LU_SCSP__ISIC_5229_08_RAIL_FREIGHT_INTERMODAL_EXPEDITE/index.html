Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - An SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (IBP) and net wealth tax (IFF): the partnership itself is not taxed and each partner, whether resident or non-resident, is assessed on their allocable share of income under a look-through approach. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a distinct taxable person and must register and invoice in its own name.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law imposes mandatory pass-through treatment for the SCSp; no domestic check-the-box election or entity-level opacity election exists. In cross-border contexts, the SCSp may nevertheless be classified as an opaque entity under foreign tax law (e.g., US check-the-box), which triggers Luxembourg's hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch rules introduced by the Law of 21 December 2019 (ATAD 2). Partners must maintain contemporaneous documentation, disclose hybrid mismatch positions in their annual income tax returns, and notify the ACD where a mismatch exclusion or offset is claimed. The partnership agreement, executed before a notary, may provide for special profit-sharing allocations that are binding for direct tax purposes provided they are economically substantive and consistently declared.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies under Article 63 TFEU; the euro is fully convertible and there are no Luxembourg exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard corporate formality: annual accounts, tax compliance, substance proof, and, where applicable, withholding tax verification. The SCSp, as a pass-through partnership, generally distributes to partners without Luxembourg withholding tax, subject to specific income characterization and anti-abuse provisions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts under Luxembourg law. EU liberalization rules allow export proceeds to be retained in any currency or converted freely. Only standard AML/CFT record-keeping and economic substance documentation obligations are triggered for tax-resident entities.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, prudential supervision, and AML/CFT enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for limited payment-rail usage, but Low as a standalone treasury solution. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions regulated by the CSSF may provide euro-denominated SEPA payment accounts, multi-currency wallets, and basic SWIFT-correspondent indirect rails; however, they cannot substitute for a licensed credit institution for commercial Letters of Credit, sustained trade-finance facilities, or high-value MT103 clearing. Legal constraints include narrower account powers, restricted credit extension, and reliance on underlying sponsor banks; any EMI used must satisfy equivalent KYC and sanctions screening standards.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital injections are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the EEA or SWIFT MT103 for cross-border and non-EEA wires. The remitter must reference the correct European System of Accounts / Balance of Payments (BoP) purpose code for direct investment equity capital (typically code 'ID' for inward direct investment equity). Luxembourg banks require the capital-call notice, investor identification, source-of-funds support, and confirmation that the funds are free of sanctions exposure. No Luxembourg exchange-control approval is required.Outbound trade settlements use SEPA for euro-denominated EU payments, SWIFT MT103 for international wires to non-EEA beneficiaries, and commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600 for large equipment or rolling-stock purchases. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City: 17.00% corporate income tax, 1.19% employment fund solidarity surcharge, 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax applies to dividends paid by fully taxable Luxembourg companies; distributions or profit allocations by a transparent SCSp are generally not legal dividends and no withholding tax is levied at SCSp level, but the underlying profit is taxable in the hands of the resident or non-resident partners according to Luxembourg rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File transfer pricing documentation is triggered under Luxembourg administrative practice where cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 150,000 per counterparty or transaction category in a tax year; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required only where the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is embedded in domestic and international tax law through Article 6 of the Steueranpassungsgesetz (anti-abuse doctrine), ATAD anti-hybrid and CFC rules, beneficial ownership requirements in double tax treaties, and OECD PE attribution principles; an SCSp engaged in rail freight must show actual operational substance, local management, employees, premises, and risk management capacity to obtain treaty relief, participation exemption, or Luxembourg tax residence benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understatement of Taxable Profit

Statutory sanction: Tax fraud sanctions under Luxembourg tax law include fines up to EUR 250,000, imprisonment up to five years, payment of the evaded tax with a 10% late-payment surcharge and monthly 0.6% interest, loss of audit favorable treatment, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the SCSp are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax where the evasion was caused by their intentional or grossly negligent conduct; courts may pierce the corporate veil, issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose imprisonment in aggravated cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, retroactive recharacterization of the arrangement under Luxembourg GAAR and DAC/ATAD principles, reallocation of profits to a permanent establishment in the source jurisdiction, imposition of the 15% dividend withholding tax on recharacterized distributions, and reassessment penalties.

Director personal liability: The general partner directors may be held civilly liable for the full Luxembourg tax shortfall, professionally disqualified, made subject to asset freezing and travel bans, and prosecuted for participating in artificial arrangements intended to defeat tax.
AML / Trade and Financial Sanctions Infringement

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Luxembourg AML law and CSSF enforcement include fines up to EUR 1,000,000, imprisonment up to five years, freezing of the SCSp bank accounts, forfeiture of proceeds, and termination of correspondent banking relations; aggravated cases may lead to withdrawal of any national or foreign operating licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, debarment, travel restrictions, extradition, and civil claims; personal assets may be seized for failing to implement adequate AML controls, and shareholders may be made liable where the SCSp is a façade for unlawful financial activity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may adjust all affected profits to the arm's length standard, deny deduction of intercompany expenses, reassess CIT and municipal business tax, add late interest, and impose negligence penalties; if TP documentation is intentionally false, criminal tax evasion sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing certificates or knowingly approve non-arm's length freight invoices can be found jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, removed from board positions, barred from acting as managers, and sentenced to imprisonment in cases of intentional falsity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, 'ACD') for direct taxes; Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, 'AEDT') for VAT and indirect taxes; Customs and Excise Agency (Administration des douanes et accises) for customs and EORI numbers. National official portals: MyGuichet.lu and guichet.public.lu; VAT e-filing is performed through the AEDT's electronic VAT declaration service accessible via MyGuichet.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A Luxembourg civil-law notary authenticates the SCSp constitutive deed and partnership agreement (no statutory minimum capital applies to an SCSp). Step 2: File the incorporation dossier with the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, 'RCS') through the LBR e-Desk within one month of notarization; RCS registration is mandatory for a commercially active partnership and automatically transmits the entity data to the ACD. Step 3: The ACD issues the national identification number (13-digit 'matricule') and opens the direct tax account. Step 4: File the VAT commencement declaration (formulaire 010) with the AEDT within 15 days of commencing taxable economic activity; the AEDT assigns the Luxembourg VAT ID (format LU + 8 digits). An EU-established foreign entity may register directly; a non-EU/EEA-established entity must appoint an AEDT-approved VAT fiscal representative, who is jointly and severally liable for the entity's VAT. Step 5: Obtain an EORI number (LU + 8/9 digits) from the Customs and Excise Agency before the first import or export. Step 6: Where material, apply for an advance tax ruling from the ACD to confirm the pass-through classification, economic substance, and transfer pricing arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg applies statutory-form classification rather than a US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp is a contractual partnership without legal personality: it is fiscally transparent for corporate income tax and net wealth tax, with each partner taxed pro-rata on the allocable profit regardless of distribution, but it is directly subject to municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at the partnership level where the enterprise is commercial; wholesale distribution of petrochemical polymers is a commercial activity, making RCS registration the operative choice of regime. For VAT, the SCSp is a taxable person in its own right. Available elective mechanisms include: (i) free drafting of profit and loss allocation ratios and capital structure in the partnership agreement, with no withholding tax on distributions to partners; (ii) VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') election under Article 62 of the VAT Law for Luxembourg group members; (iii) ACD advance tax rulings and advance pricing agreements confirming transparency, substance, financing, and transfer pricing; (iv) ATAD and ATAD II hybrid-mismatch documentation and disclosure filings to prevent double non-taxation arising from the SCSp's transparent status and foreign-law entity mismatches; (v) elections regarding input VAT recovery under partial-exemption regimes where mixed-use activities exist; and (vi) business transfer and exit neutrality elections under the Luxembourg VAT regime. No tax-exempt-zone election is available for trading income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small-enterprise exemption under Article 64 of the Luxembourg VAT Law: annual turnover (excluding VAT) not exceeding EUR 35,000 qualifies for exemption; VAT registration is mandatory as soon as Luxembourg taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, or when the SCSp makes taxable B2B supplies, performs intra-EU acquisitions subject to VAT, or imports goods. Non-established suppliers making supplies of goods in Luxembourg are required to register directly (EU-established businesses) or via a fiscal representative (non-EU-established businesses), irrespective of the turnover threshold, where the applicable B2B or consignment-stock routing rules so provide.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT with recovery of input tax) applies under Article 52 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (implementing Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC) for exports of bulk virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules supplied by the SCSp to non-EU buyers, provided: (i) the goods leave EU customs territory within 3 months (90 days) of the supply date; (ii) the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration cleared by the competent export office; (iii) transport documents (bill of lading, CMR, or air waybill) are consistent with the invoice, Incoterms, and payment flows; (iv) the buyer is situated outside the EU and does not divert the goods back into the EU; and (v) the SCSp's accounting records permit verifiable tracing from order to export to remittance. Intra-Community supplies to EU/EEA business customers are zero-rated under Article 53 of the VAT Law subject to the customer's valid EU VAT ID, evidence of movement to the destination member state within 90 days, and timely EC Sales List filing. Ancillary transport and insurance services related to non-EU goods are zero-rated under the export-services rules, and offshore services to non-EU customers follow B2B reverse-charge location rules where the recipient is an established taxable person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD/EX1) with export ratification or confirmation from the customs office of export
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR consignment note / air waybill) evidencing physical exit of the goods from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / accepted proof of receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of analysis or weight certificate for the polymer granules
  • Proof of title transfer and Incoterms evidence, including transport contract and freight forwarding confirmation
  • For intra-EU zero-rating: customer VAT ID validation printout and EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) acknowledgment

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) system is in force for domestic B2B invoices in Luxembourg during the current tax year. Electronic invoicing is voluntary in B2B; every invoice, whether paper or digital, must preserve authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility pursuant to Article 68 of the VAT Law, with audit-proof archiving for 10 years. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G invoices to Luxembourg public authorities, which must be submitted in PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format through a certified PEPPOL access point under the public procurement legislation. VAT returns are filed electronically on a monthly or quarterly basis through the AEDT e-declaration service. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform, mandatory digital reporting and e-invoicing for cross-border B2B supplies will take effect from 1 July 2030; Luxembourg has not yet activated a domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate, but the SCSp's ERP and invoicing architecture should be ViDA-ready.

The AEDT audit unit subjects export zero-rating claims to document-and-flow scrutiny, cross-checking customs export declarations against transport documents, Incoterms, ERP trails, and bank and FX records. Where export substantiation is incomplete or inconsistent, the AEDT retrospectively reassesses the supply at the 17% standard rate with statutory default interest on the under-declared VAT (currently 0.6% per month of arrears), plus administrative fines under Articles 74-84 of the VAT Law, including up to EUR 10,000 per offence for defective records and up to 20% of the evaded tax for incorrect or incomplete declarations. Deliberate export fraud, including forged customs documents, fictitious consignments, carousel or missing-trader routing, and fabricated inward remittance certificates, constitutes criminal VAT fraud punishable under Articles 86 and 86ter of the VAT Law and the General Tax Law by fines and imprisonment, with custodial sentences of up to five years in aggravated or organised cases, confiscation of assets, and publication of the conviction; the reassessment period extends to 10 years in fraud cases. The AEDT may order seizure of funds and enforcement against the partners of the SCSp on a joint-and-several basis where the partnership's assets are insufficient. In practice, an export zero-rating claim failing the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark is presumptively challenged as a sham export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member, and the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange control laws, no approval requirements for profit remittances, and no restrictions on payments or transfers for international transactions. Cross-border capital movements are governed by EU Treaty freedoms and the EU Anti-Money Laundering / sanctions framework. The only practical hurdles are banking documentation, beneficial ownership transparency, and compliance with EU restrictive measures; these do not constitute convertibility or repatriation restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a fixed timeline. Exporters may freely hold and manage foreign currency receivables in euro or other currencies. Resident enterprises are required solely to report direct investment flows and external positions to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistical purposes when the relevant legal threshold (generally EUR 12.5 million per transaction or annual aggregate) is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the European Central Bank (ECB) in the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the supervisory authority for credit institutions and payment institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium – subject to critical constraints. A Luxembourg-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institution (EMI) under PSD2 may provide payment accounts, remittance services, FX conversion, and IBAN issuance for the SCSp. However, most non-bank EMIs do not have the capacity to issue irrevocable commercial letters of credit, receive large capital subscriptions, or handle trade finance documentation; therefore their use is feasible only as a secondary operational treasury rail. Offshore (non-EEA) digital EMIs generally cannot hold euro accounts on behalf of Luxembourg entities or access SEPA/TARGET2 clearing without a Luxembourg or EU branch or license, and are not considered acceptable primary banking infrastructure by Luxembourg counterparties or the CSSF.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are routed via SWIFT MT103 or, for euro payments, via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) / TARGET2. The payment must reference the exact legal name and registration number of the SCSp and identify the nature of the transaction as an equity contribution on the general ledger. For direct investment reporting to the BCL, the transaction must be flagged under the appropriate balance-of-payments classification. Funds are typically deposited into a dedicated capital escrow account in the SCSp's name and, upon admission of the limited partner, transferred to the main operational account. There are no foreign exchange surrender or licensing requirements for these capital installments.Clearing rails for trade settlements comprise: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for high-value wire transfers to counterparties and refiners; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit evidenced by SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT707 amendments, issued by the Luxembourg account bank or a confirming correspondent, to guarantee payment against presentation of compliant shipping and quality documents; (3) documentary sight/demand guarantees under URDG 758 for tender and performance obligations; and (4) SEPA instant credit transfers for low-value euro disbursements within the EEA. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: The SCSp, as a special limited partnership, is generally not captured by the OECD Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) unless it performs relevant activity. However, Luxembourg administrative practice and the ATAD implementation require adequate substance for treaty benefits. For a wholesale trading SCSp, substance is evidenced by: business premises in Luxembourg, full-time personnel with autonomous decision-making on pricing and contracting, physical inventory risk if the partner claims PE status, and board/director minutes evidencing control over risk. The Grand-Ducal Regulation of 21 June 2019 (permanent establishment and import of partnership assets) further requires that any Luxembourg PE of a foreign partner maintains accounting records, a dedicated bank account, and operational autonomy.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 120 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), tax evasion triggers criminal fines up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years (Article 399 of the Criminal Code). Additionally, a 50% civil penalty on the understated tax is imposed, plus late payment interest of 7.2% per annum. The taxpayer bears the burden of rebutting the presumption of taxable presence in Luxembourg.

Director personal liability: A director/manager of an SCSp who knowingly submits false tax declarations is personally liable for the full amount of unpaid taxes and penalties (joint and several liability under Article 1645 of the General Tax Law). The Director may face criminal conviction and travel bans; the court can order seizure of personal assets and restriction of cross-border movement if the unpaid tax exceeds EUR 100,000.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authorities may apply the substance-over-form doctrine and reclassify the SCSp as a fully taxable corporate entity (in which case the headline corporate regime applies). This triggers back-taxes, 50% negligence penalties, and potential denial treaty relief. The entity may also be struck from the Luxembourg Business Registers if it has no economic activity for three consecutive years, leading to dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on limited liability when the partnership is reclassified as a corporate dummy. Courts may pierce the veil and hold directors personally liable for all corporate taxes, penalties, and third-party trade debts. In severe cases, a ban of up to 5 years from serving as a director in Luxembourg is imposed, and the director’s own travel documents may be withheld pending payment of tax liabilities.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 250,000 for legal entities and EUR 250,000 for individuals (new Law of 13 January 2019). The Luxembourg Business Registers can publish non-compliance and suspend the entity's rights, including entitlement to tax treaty benefits. Additionally, criminal AML sanctions can reach 5 years’ imprisonment if money laundering is proven (Law of 12 November 2004).

Director personal liability: Directors of an SCSp are personally liable for AML penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to identify the need to file the RBE declaration. They may be subject to imprisonment up to 6 months for mere non-filing of RBE data. The court may impose a travel ban and order the winding-up of the entity, with the director forced to compensate all third parties for losses arising from the absence of transparency.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg primary adjustment (i.e., re-determination of taxable income) plus a secondary adjustment applied as a deemed distribution or capital contribution. A penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax can be levied if the taxpayer has not performed a correct transfer pricing study. Interest accrues at 7.2% per annum. The General Tax Law enables the tax authority to approximate profit margins using a default net margin of 5%-10% for wholesale trading if records are absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute a non-arm’s length transaction in breach of their fiduciary duties are personally liable for the resulting tax deficit under Luxembourg civil law (joint and several with the partnership). In criminal cases, the director may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of EUR 15,000 for false accounting (Article 119 of the Criminal Code).

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operating under the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, Articles 101-1 to 101-24 (société en commandite spéciale, as inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013); Law of 30 May 2000 on the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register, as amended; Law of 26 July 2017 on fully electronic RCS filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Single electronic application for registration (demande d'immatriculation) filed via the LBR eFile portal, accompanied by the executed partnership agreement (contrat de société) signed in writing or by LuxTrust electronic signature; the registered extract is then published in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. An SCSp has no statutory minimum capital and no legal capital requirement; the partnership agreement freely defines the capital and the contributions of the General Partner and the Limited Partners. Cash contributions require no escrow or blocked account: the subscribed amount is paid into the SCSp's account and evidenced by a bank confirmation; in-kind contributions are described and valued in the agreement. The SCSp is fiscally transparent (pass-through), so Luxembourg taxation occurs at partner level.

Funding & unlock path: CGDIS (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours) fire-safety survey and clearance for the commune's occupancy permit, including alarm and extinguisher compliance; structural engineer's certificate confirming floor load capacity for server racks (minimum 7.5 kN/m²); electrical installation certification; and workplace clearance from the Inspection du travail et des Mines (ITM) covering ergonomic screen workstations, hearing protection in audio booths, and electrical safety under the Luxembourg Labor Code.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue for direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD). Luxembourg VAT and registration duties authority: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT). Central business administration portal: guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SCSp in Luxembourg must first obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (RCS number) via the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (LBR). For tax purposes, the SCSp must then apply to the ACD for a Luxembourg identification number (matricule) covering direct tax obligations and register for VAT status where applicable with the AEDT using the VAT registration form (formulaire 640). Non-established taxable persons may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative if they have no fixed establishment in Luxembourg, unless an exemption applies under EU VAT rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing an SCSp in Luxembourg must first obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (RCS number) via the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (LBR). For tax purposes, the SCSp must then apply to the ACD for a Luxembourg identification number (matricule) covering direct tax obligations and register for VAT status where applicable with the AEDT using the VAT registration form (formulaire 640). Non-established taxable persons may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative if they have no fixed establishment in Luxembourg, unless an exemption applies under EU VAT rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, meaning Luxembourg does not impose corporate income tax at the partnership level; partners are taxed on their respective shares of income. Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election, but partnerships may apply for VAT grouping under Luxembourg VAT rules and may structure profit-sharing to alter the attribution of Luxembourg-source income, subject to anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD and the Luxembourg anti-abuse rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established Luxembourg small businesses; non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg are generally required to register for VAT irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports is available for goods dispatched or transported outside the EU provided the supplier retains proof of export and receives a valid EU customer number. For cross-border B2B services, including automated subtitle and AI voice services, the place of supply is normally the country where the business customer is established; the supply is therefore outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and zero-rated from a Luxembourg perspective. The supplier must obtain the customer's valid VAT identification number and maintain contemporaneous evidence of the commercial transaction and delivery of digital work.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) or electronic export declaration validated by Luxembourg customs
  • Bill of Lading or other transport documentation evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing settlement of the export transaction

Luxembourg does not impose a universal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is accepted and PEPPOL-compliant formats are supported. Submission of VAT returns and recapitulative statements is performed electronically through the AEDT portal. No API-based real-time pre-clearance of invoices is currently mandated for standard B2B services, including subtitle and AI services. Taxable persons must issue invoices in compliance with Luxembourg invoicing rules and retain SAF-T-compatible records for audit purposes.

The Luxembourg VAT administration may retrospectively deny zero-rating where the documentary evidence is insufficient, leading to reassessment of Luxembourg VAT at the standard 17% rate plus default interest. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect zero-rating claims can attract penalties of up to EUR 25,000 per offense or, in serious cases, tax fraud charges carrying fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and imprisonment for responsible officers. Directors and tax representatives of the SCSp may incur joint liability for VAT clawed back from a failed zero-rating claim.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies under Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-65 and EMU rules. The euro is freely convertible both internally and vis-à-vis foreign currencies; no Luxembourg restrictions exist on opening foreign-currency accounts, entering FX transactions, or remitting profits/distributions. SCSp distributions to non-resident partners are not subject to exchange-control licensing; applicable hurdles are fiscal only (anti-abuse, withholding tax on certain payments, transfer-pricing documentation, substance requirements) and AML/KYC onboarding requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no export-proceeds repatriation, surrender, or mandatory conversion requirement. AI subtitle/licensing receipts received in USD or third currencies may be retained in the SCSp's foreign-currency accounts; for Luxembourg statutory accounts they must be translated into EUR under applicable accounting standards. Such receipts remain subject to normal tax declaration, VAT treatment for cross-border digital services where applicable, and anti-money-laundering transaction justification.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); foreign-exchange and cross-border payment statistics are reported to the BCL, while credit institutions and payment institutions are supervised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg has no separate foreign-exchange administration; the Ministry of Finance is responsible for sanctions and exchange-control policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, low as a substitute for a regulated credit institution. EU/EEA-licensed EMIs and payment institutions (Wise, Stripe, Payoneer, Multitude) can legally passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and provide EUR/foreign-currency virtual IBANs, cross-border FX conversion, card acquiring, and faster settlement for the AI subtitle marketplace. They are not 'banks' for escrow, depositor protection, commercial loans, or documentary credits; funds held with EMIs are not protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme in the same manner. Offshore/non-EEA EMIs that have not passported cannot lawfully provide payment services to the SCSp in Luxembourg; use of such EMIs triggers CSSF regulatory exposure and bank KYC concerns. Optimal blueprint: use an EMI only for micro-transactions and marketplace pay-ins below 15% of monthly turnover, with all capital injections, profit repatriation, tax payments, and large trade settlements through BCL-supervised banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate corporate income tax (CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 1.19% + municipal business tax avg 6.75%) for taxable companies; 0% for SCSp as a tax-transparent pass-through entity unless opt-in under Luxembourg corporate law

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions by SCSp to partners because the vehicle is tax-transparent; if the SCSp voluntarily elects Luxembourg corporate taxation, standard dividend WHT is 15% (reduced to 5% for substantial shareholdings and 0% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Box 1 – Partner Identification Registered name, commercial registry number (RCS), tax number (TVA/RC), and registered office of the SCSp. Provide full legal name, country of tax residence, and foreign tax identification number; attach valid tax residence certificate (TRC) for treaty claims. Late filing triggers an administrative penalty up to EUR 1,250 per return; additional 0.6% per month late-payment interest on assessed tax; systematic default may lead to tax reassessment by the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (ACD) and criminal sanctions.
Box 2 – Net Taxable Profit or Loss Computed total net income from all sources before profit allocation, calculated in accordance with Luxembourg GAAP adjusted for tax depreciation and exempt income. Non-resident partners must report their allocable share of Luxembourg-source income in their domestic returns; the SCSp files this information on behalf of all partners. Late filing triggers an administrative penalty up to EUR 1,250 per return; additional 0.6% per month late-payment interest on assessed tax; systematic default may lead to tax reassessment by the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (ACD) and criminal sanctions.
Box 3 – Allocation to General and Limited Partners Allocation of net taxable income/losses among partners according to the partnership agreement (SCSp is tax-transparent, thus no partnership-level tax). Each partner's pro-rata share must be disclosed separately; non-resident partners are not taxed in Luxembourg on purely business income unless attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment. Late filing triggers an administrative penalty up to EUR 1,250 per return; additional 0.6% per month late-payment interest on assessed tax; systematic default may lead to tax reassessment by the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (ACD) and criminal sanctions.
Box 4 – Tax Credits and Advance Payments Claims for foreign-tax credit, investment tax credit, and withholding tax credits arising from Luxembourg-source dividends or royalties. Non-resident partners may credit underlying foreign taxes if eligible under DTA; submit DTA relief application together with TRC before the filing deadline. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific local monetary threshold for Master/Local File in Luxembourg; OECD transfer pricing principles are applied. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for multinational groups (including SCSp chain) with consolidated annual revenue exceeding EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) register as in other EU-adjacent jurisdictions, but the ACD applies a robust substance-over-form doctrine rooted in ATAD, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, and the OECD BEPS Minimum Standards. For an SCSp, economic substance is required to validate treaty residence, any tax-exempt income claim, and to avoid classification as a transparent shell. Evidence includes premises, staff, commencement of activity, real decision-making in Luxembourg, and adequate capitalisation vis-à-vis risk assumptions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Repayment of all underpaid taxes plus a surcharge of 10%–50% of the evaded amount; administrative fine up to EUR 125,000; imprisonment up to 1 year for wilful evasion under Article 110 LGBTQI+ (Luxembourg Income Tax Act).

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners face personal, joint and several liability for corporate tax debts if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution orders, asset seizures, travel bans (Courts of Luxembourg) and criminal incarceration up to 2 years for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under national anti-abuse provisions, the tax administration may disregard the SCSp's existence, deny treaty benefits, re-assess income and withholding obligations on a look-through basis, and apply a 5%–20% penalty on the revised tax liability; potential revocation of corporate benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for taxes arising from re-characterised transactions, subject to clawback of distributed profits, travel bans, and imprisonment where a knowing sham is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg AML law (law of 12 November 2004 and subsequent amendments): fines up to EUR 250,000 for legal entities; additional €5,000 per week of non-corrected breach; criminal sanctions include prison time up to 5 years for money laundering. The Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) enforces mandatory filings.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorised managers are personally obligated to identify beneficial owners; failure leads to personal fines, revocation of permits, prohibition from holding management functions, civil damages to third parties, and in severe cases imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: The ACD may estimate taxable income using best available information, apply a penalty of 5%–10% of the under-declared profit, and impose a specific penalty up to EUR 100,000 for serious non-cooperation; interest on late adjustments continues to accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete transfer pricing documentation or knowingly certify incorrect pricing are subject to fines up to 25% of the tax deficiency, disqualification as directors in Luxembourg, and criminal liability for aiding tax evasion if proven intentional.

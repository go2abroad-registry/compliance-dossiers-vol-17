No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; contributions are freely determined in the partnership agreement, may be cash or in-kind, and no blocked escrow account or bank certificate is required; the general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts, while limited partners are liable only up to their committed contribution.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; contributions are freely determined in the partnership agreement, may be cash or in-kind, and no blocked escrow account or bank certificate is required; the general partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts, while limited partners are liable only up to their committed contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — Direct Tax Authority (Luxembourg, 33 rue de Gasperich, L-5826 Hesperange; portal: myguichet.lu); Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — VAT & Registration Authority (Boulevard Prince Henri, L-1724 Luxembourg; e-file via eTVA portal, managed within MyGuichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

Phase 1: Foreign/investor entities must first appoint a Luxembourg-resident Réviseur d'Entreprises Agréé and a registered local tax representative (for non-EU principals) prior to VAT registration. Phase 2: File incorporation/registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via a certified notary; the RCS automatically transmits the corporate data to the AEDT. Phase 3: Upon RCS registration, the entity receives a 13-digit national identification number (N° d'identification — matricule), which is simultaneously used as the direct-tax TIN. Phase 4: For VAT, submit the eTVA registration form (Formulaire 1/2 — Déclaration de commencement d'activité) through the RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) compliance step; the AEDT issues a distinct VAT number with prefix 'LU' followed by 8 digits. Phase 5: For an SCSp (SOPARFI variant not relevant; pure SCSp), the transparent entity itself registers for VAT when it sells supplies in its own name; otherwise, the general partner (associé gérant) may register on behalf of the partnership. Phase 6: Required submission of statutory articles, proof of beneficial owner filing (RBE), EU VAT status certificate (if claiming prior business), and a statement of intended commercial activities in Luxembourg. Processing time: 5–15 business days for TIN; 1–3 weeks for VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is, by default, treated as tax-transparent (fiscally transparent) for Luxembourg income tax purposes. It is not itself a taxable person for CIT and city business tax (WIR); its partners — both general and limited — are directly subject to income tax (IRC or IRPP) on their respective shares of the SCSp's income. For VAT, however, the SCSp is considered a taxable person (assujetti) when it independently supplies goods/services, consistent with the EU VAT Directive principle that fiscally transparent partnerships may be liable for VAT in their own name for the activities they conduct. The manufacturing activity (ISIC 1079 self-heating meals) executed by the SCSp is thus directly attributed to the partners for income tax, while the VAT liability sits at the partnership level.

Available election forms

(i) Non-transparent election: The SCSp may irrevocably elect to be treated as a taxable entity for Luxembourg income tax purposes (option for corporate taxation), transforming the partnership into a fiscal subject akin to a capital company (ruling from the ACD required); once elected, the SCSp is subject to CIT at 17% (2025), municipal business tax (WIR), contribution to employment fund (7¢ top-up), and the solidarity surcharge. (ii) Partial transparency hybrid check: Luxembourg's transposition of ATAD (Article 4) provides an optional 'step-down' approach under controlled-foreign-company rules that can align the SCSp's transparency with a partner's home jurisdiction classification — a de-facto check-the-box function for cross-border investors. (iii) Partnership audit election: Under Luxembourg general tax law, the partners may elect (via filing of the common partnership return) that the SCSp's accounts be reviewed at partnership level for determination of distributed shares, creating binding effect on the tax authorities. (iv) Hybrid mismatch adjustments: Where a partner's home jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque, Luxembourg will apply the ATAD anti-hybrid rules, requiring an election to re-characterise the SCSp's income under the 'same-country' exception.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-business exemption, Article 57 VAT Law) — where the SCSp's worldwide supplies within Luxembourg do not exceed EUR 35,000 per annum, the entity may be exempt from VAT with no input VAT recovery; forming part of a VAT group overrides the threshold. Cross-border manufacturing and export activities above this threshold require mandatory EU-Article-214 registration. No business-to-consumer distance-selling threshold applies within Luxembourg (single EU regime from 2021 under VAT e-commerce package with EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold). Zero-rated and exempt-with-credit supplies count toward the threshold; physically exported goods are excluded. Registration is mandatory from the first euro if the entity makes intra-EU acquisitions above EUR 10,000 or holds a VAT-group election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An SCSp manufacturing self-heating instant meals in Luxembourg may apply the 0% VAT rate for (i) goods shipped to non-EU destinations (Article 162 VAT Law — export of goods outside the EU); (ii) intra-EU supplies to taxable persons in other Member States (Article 170 VAT Law — intra-Community supplies, including a valid customer VAT number and INTRASTAT where applicable); and (iii) the export of services directly linked to export (including freight, insurance, loading services) per Article 149 VAT Law. The zero-rating is strictly conditioned on: economic movement of goods leaving the EU within 90 days of invoice (customs export declaration accepted); the seller's documentation providing full traceable chain of custody (manufacturer → carrier → exit customs post); and the buyer being identifiable as a taxable or non-resident party. For the specific cargo 'Instant Hot Water Self-Heating Pot' (exothermic reaction packaging — CaO quicklime/water reaction), the product qualifies as generically manufactured goods; no special export authorization beyond general export control is needed since it contains no dual-use hazardous quantity of calcium oxide (nett content of exothermic material ≤ 100 g per unit). For intra-Community supply, the SCSp must verify the customer's valid VAT ID in VIES and, if the goods are shipped by the SCSp itself, report the transport in its own records with CMR/airway bill.

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL (since 2023 for larger contracts, fully effective 2024). No real-time fiscal clearance (no 'VAT live' reporting) — the Luxembourg system applies periodic digital VAT return filing (monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on turnover) via the eTVA portal. Standard B2B e-invoicing is voluntary in Luxembourg; the Government has committed to transposing the EU ViDA / standard e-invoicing mandate by 2028 (draft bill under preparation). Digital stamping of invoices is not used in Luxembourg. VAT filing is mandatory digitally for all registered persons. A full SAF-T (Standard Audit File – Tax) upload must accompany periodic returns for taxpayers with turnover exceeding EUR 50,000 (requested electronically by AEDT). Integration API: MyGuichet.lu and the AEDT system allow machine-to-machine submission of VAT returns and SAF-T via authenticated API for large ERP users.

The Luxembourg AEDT and Customs Authority jointly enforce export zero-rating. Audit triggers include: (i) serial or low-value exports to sanctioned territories, (ii) inconsistent bill-of-lading quantities versus freight manifests (commonly exploited with heat-reactive goods where chemical weight shifts at customs checkpoint), and (iii) SCSp entities with non-resident partners operating in high-volume consumer-goods sectors. Where zero-rating is rejected, the EUR fiscal creditor issues a VAT correction assessing 17% VAT on the full export value plus default interest of 0.8% per month (legal interest regime, Article 23 VAT Law) and a late-payment penalty of 5% at the time of assessment rising to 1% per additional month (max 20%). In cases of intentional falsification of transport documents or over-declaration of exothermic-material compliance, the penalties escalate: administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 (Article 60 VAT Law), criminal fines up to double the evaded tax, and imprisonment of managers in serious cases (Luxembourg Criminal Code Article 60bis/contextual VAT fraud provisions). Additionally, the customs authorities may impose a breach of customs-export controls (EUR 1,500 per false declaration) and suspend the exporter's authorised-economy-operator status. Clawback mechanics: an AEDT reassessment within a five-year statutory limitation period (which is extended to 10 years in cases involving intentional fraud or documentary forgery) automatically lifts the underlying VAT deductibility of the input costs and reclassifies the export supply as taxable; the partnership's partners (jointly and severally; Article 47 VAT Law) become personally liable for the VAT, interest, and penalties. In a cross-border structure, the clawback can further trigger non-deductibility of the foreign partner's share under hybrid-mismatch rules if the partner's jurisdiction has treated the SCSp as opaque, creating a second layer of taxation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Luxembourg is a Eurozone Member State and grants unrestricted cross-border capital movements under Article 63 TFEU. There are no exchange-control authorizations, prior-approval regimes, conversion quotas, or currency bands applicable to equity injections, profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, trade payments, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. The only operational filters are EU/UN sanctions screening, the AML/CFT Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended), and CSSF prudential obligations imposed on the depository bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirement: export proceeds must not be repatriated within any mandatory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. The SCSp may lawfully retain USD or CNY receivables in foreign-currency accounts held with a Luxembourg credit institution without BCL authorization. The applicable administrative requirements are limited to balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the bank (per Regulation (EC) No 184/2005), Intrastat/CN coding for EU trade, transfer-of-funds traceability under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, and, where the exothermic reaction composition contains controlled precursors, export licensing under the EU Dual-Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem, with the Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank and operational agent for TARGET2 and balance-of-payments statistics; prudential banking supervision is co-exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no separate exchange-control administration and no Ministry-of-Finance FX licensing body for euro or freely convertible currency operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Luxembourg authorizes a modest number of payment institutions and electronic money institutions under PSD2 and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, and EEA EMIs may passport into Luxembourg; however, most automated or offshore digital EMIs design their onboarding for incorporated legal entities and fail to accommodate a Société en Commandite Spéciale, which has no legal personality. Legal constraints are: (i) the EMI must be authorized by the CSSF or passport under PSD2 with a Luxembourg notice; (ii) the SCSp can only act through its general partner (associé commandité), requiring a formal mandate and legal opinion; (iii) client funds must be safeguarded through segregation with an EU credit institution; (iv) an EMI is not permitted to issue Commercial Letters of Credit, act as escrow agent, or provide prime-brokerage liquidity. Recommended architecture: maintain core liquidity and all LC/escrow operations at a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution and use a passported EU EMI only for low-value, high-frequency TT operational wires where the reduced service envelope is acceptable.

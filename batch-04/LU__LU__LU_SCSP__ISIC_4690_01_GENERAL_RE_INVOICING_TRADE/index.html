Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct and corporate income tax matters; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and stamp taxes; Administration des douanes et accises for customs and excise procedures. Central digital access portals: myGuichet.lu, guichet.public.lu, the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) e-registration portal, and the SUD electronic customs declaration system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SCSp with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through an appointed Luxembourg registered office/agent, obtaining the RCS matricule and the national business identification number. Step 2: Open the tax dossier (numéro de dossier) with the ACD/AEDT, which produces the Luxembourg corporate TIN for the entity. Step 3: Submit the VAT registration application to the AEDT Service de la TVA using the standard non-established taxable person procedure, including powers of attorney, identity documents of partners and managers, proof of the re-invoicing business activity and expected turnover; a non-resident entity without a Luxembourg permanent establishment may register directly for VAT or, when necessary, appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative. The Luxembourg VAT ID follows the format LU + 8 digits. Registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply is made in Luxembourg, and no registration fee is levied.

For VAT, the SCSp is treated as a taxable person where all partners are VAT taxable persons; mixed-partner structures are analysed at partner/co-ownership level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is a special limited partnership without separate legal personality and is fiscally transparent for direct tax purposes: profits are attributed and taxed directly at partner level (corporate partners are subject to corporate income tax and municipal business tax; individual partners to personal income tax), while the SCSp itself is not a CIT or municipal business tax taxpayer. For VAT, the SCSp is treated as a taxable person where all partners are VAT taxable persons; mixed-partner structures are analysed at partner/co-ownership level. The activity is ordinary territorial nexus-based; no special tax-exempt zone is engaged.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election regime exists in Luxembourg; tax transparency of the SCSp is a statutory attribute rather than an elective classification. Partnerships nevertheless remain subject to hybrid mismatch analysis under LIR Articles 168ter and 168quater (ATAD 1 and 2) and OECD BEPS Action 2 outcomes, including reverse-hybrid and import mismatch rules. For VAT, AEDT interpretative practice permits the SCSp to be treated as a VAT taxable person where all partners are taxable persons, effectively an optional consolidation of partner VAT status into the partnership; there is no US-style 338/168 or UK section 59A analogue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover small-business exemption threshold for Luxembourg-established businesses under Article 57 LVD. Non-resident re-invoicing intermediaries making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT from the first euro and cannot benefit from the SME exemption; intra-Community distance sales and acquisition thresholds also trigger registration in the relevant Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) may be claimed under Article 51 et seq. LVD for: (i) supplies of goods physically exported outside the EU, provided the goods leave EU territory within three months of dispatch and the supplier holds complete export evidence; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods where the customer supplies a valid Luxembourg or other Member State VAT identification number and goods are dispatched to another Member State, subject to EC Sales Lists, VIES and Intrastat reporting; and (iii) certain B2B services that are treated as supplied outside Luxembourg under the place-of-supply rules of Article 14 LVD, including re-invoicing and commissionaire services rendered by the SCSp to a non-EU principal and services ancillary to export transactions. The 0% rate is recorded in Box 5 of the periodic VAT return; the zero-rating cannot be applied merely because the customer is non-resident and the exporter must preserve the full documentary chain.

  • Export customs declaration — Single Administrative Document or electronic SUD/CDS export declaration, including the declaration number and proof of exit confirmation
  • Transport evidence — CMR waybill, bill of lading (B/L), air waybill (AWB) or multimodal transport document depending on mode of carriage
  • Commercial documentation — sales contract or purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and proof of title transfer
  • Payment and FX proceeds evidence — SWIFT MT103/telegraphic transfer credit advice, bank FX repatriation credit note, or an Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing foreign currency remittance to the SCSp bank account
  • Proof of physical exit from EU territory — customs exit confirmation (EUREX/NSTI records), officially stamped transport documents, or third-party warehouse/carrier certification

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector entities and must be exchanged in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format through a certified PEPPOL access point, with central procurement data submitted via the InMyBX electronic platform. There is no general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API data-streaming or digital stamping regime currently in force; B2B e-invoicing remains optional provided authenticity of origin and integrity of content are ensured. Luxembourg is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals, which will introduce additional cross-border B2B e-invoicing obligations in the future.

AEDT rigorously audits zero-rated export claims and coordinates with ACD reviews of economic substance and beneficial ownership. Incomplete or inconsistent export, transport or remittance evidence enables retrospective VAT reassessment for up to five years (ten years in cases of fraud), recalculating VAT at the standard 17% rate with default interest at 0.6% per commenced month, late-payment penalties, administrative fines generally ranging between 10% and 20% of the underdeclared tax, and disallowance of corresponding input VAT credits. Fraudulent export documentation attracts criminal tax-evasion penalties, including fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years, with joint and several liability of managers. The 180-day proceeds-repatriation benchmark supports the substance and economic-effect analysis of the SCSp re-invoicing trade; sustained non-repatriation may additionally trigger transfer-pricing recharacterisation and deemed distribution treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone jurisdiction with full and unrestricted capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-65 TFEU. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements, and no mandatory conversion or repatriation restrictions for trade proceeds. Profit remittance by an SCSp is tax-free at the partnership level given its fiscally transparent status, and payments to non-resident partners are generally made without Luxembourg withholding tax provided the applicable conditions under Luxembourg domestic law and relevant double tax treaties are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender regime exists in Luxembourg. Receipts from export activities can be retained in any currency or converted at the freely negotiated market rate. The only compliance requirement is indirect statistical reporting to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes and, where applicable, EU customs reporting, but no mandatory conversion ratio or timing is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the National Central Bank, together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the supervisory authority for banks and payment institutions within the Luxembourg financial centre. EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital are directly applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High practical viability for ancillary transactions, but not for core treasury. Passported or Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 can operate digital accounts for an SCSp, restricted to e-money issuance and payment services. Legal constraints include the inability of an EMI to grant credit or accept deposits, the absence of deposit guarantee coverage on e-money float, and the need for a separate Tier-1 bank account for LC operations or large trade settlements. Such EMIs are therefore suitable for low-value payables, FX conversion, and fast cross-border XT transfers, not as a standalone liquidity clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for subscription and capital contributions: transfer via SWIFT MT103 from the parent/contributing entity to the Luxembourg operational account, with the payment referencing the SCSp name, fully paid subscription and the applicable purpose code under the BCL statistical classification (direct investment equity or intra-group capital). Before the wire, the bank compliance team must receive: executed subscription agreement, capital contribution resolution, notice of intent, ultimate beneficial owner identification, source-of-wealth documents, and evidence of the entry in the Luxembourg Register of Commercial and Companies for the SCSp. Intermediary banks must be EU-approved and not blacklisted; the beneficiary account number must match the op account exactly.Use SWIFT MT103 for open account supplier payments and commercial letters of credit (UCP600) executed through SWIFT MT700/MT707 where the beneficiary requires LC protection. For EUR instant disbursements within SEPA, the XT_Transfer format can be used via the bank's real-time payment rail. The chosen rail must carry the proper purpose code, reference the underlying invoice, and be supported by transport documents, packing lists and invoice copies retained for AML/FI checks.

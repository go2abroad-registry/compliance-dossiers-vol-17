Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD / Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct income taxes, with the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT / Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority) for value added tax; principal electronic portal is MyGuichet.lu operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the Luxembourg State IT Center.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SCSp must execute a notarial deed of limited partnership agreement, which is filed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) to obtain a legal identifier (matricule / numéro d'identification national) for the SCSp. Thereafter, the SCSp, acting through its manager or registered agent, must apply separately to the AEDT for VAT registration if it makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg; for direct income tax purposes the ACD assigns a Luxembourg corporate tax reference number to the partnership and individual fiscal numbers to non-resident partners only as required for filing/withholding. Foreign entities may use an electronic registration file via MyGuichet.lu, with power-of-attorney documentation and valid passports/articles of incorporation apostilled as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — An SCSp is fiscally transparent by mandatory legal tax characterization: it is not a separate subject of Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (IAC), or net wealth tax; profits and losses are passed through to partners and taxed at partner level. For VAT purposes, the SCSp is treated as a taxable person if it independently carries on an economic activity, and it may be registered as a VAT debtor in its own name.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp's pass-through status is a statutory default and cannot be unilaterally converted to opaque corporate taxation; investors desiring entity-level taxation must instead use a separate corporate vehicle such as a S.à r.l. or S.A. Available elective mechanisms relevant to SCSp structures include: (i) VAT grouping election under Luxembourg VAT law allowing an SCSp to join a group of closely-linked Luxembourg taxable persons treated as a single taxable person; (ii) option to file consolidated tax returns does not exist; (iii) hybrid-mismatch management is limited to ATAD-aligned anti-abuse rules and mandatory disclosure of cross-border arrangements, with no elective deduction/income reclassification mechanism to eliminate hybrid outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for small enterprises established in Luxembourg under the Article 287(15) derogation; however, no threshold applies to non-established foreign suppliers or to supplies of electronically supplied services subject to reverse charge or domestic Luxembourg VAT, and once the EUR 35,000 ceiling is exceeded VAT registration is mandatory from the first day of the month following the threshold crossing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods qualifies for the 0% Luxembourg VAT export exemption where the seller dispatches or transports the goods directly out of the European Union within the statutory deadline (generally within three months from delivery/supply) and holds positive documentary evidence of physical export, including a recognized EU customs export declaration and transport documents. For offshore/digital services such as fleet route optimization and cloud-based telematics SaaS, zero-rating is not normally used; instead, the place-of-supply rules apply: B2B services to a non-EU customer fall outside Luxembourg VAT (customer location rule) and B2C electronically supplied services to a non-EU resident also remain outside Luxembourg VAT. If the recipient is a taxable person holding an EU VAT identification number in another Member State, the supply is subject to reverse charge in that Member State and no Luxembourg VAT is charged; for EU consumer customers the supplier may need to register in the applicable Member State or use the OSS/IOSS regime for digital services.

  • Exporter's electronic customs export declaration (SAD/Declaration submitted via Luxembourg Customs, Direction des Douanes et Accises, and validated under the EU Customs Decision System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU
  • Original commercial invoice and underlying contract or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 / account statement showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Proof of terminal/warehouse loading and delivery such as signed proof of delivery (POD) or customs transit completion documents
  • Any applicable export control or dual-use license issued by the Luxembourg Ministry of Economy or EU authorities

Luxembourg does not currently mandate continuous transaction controls, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or an API-based B2B e-invoicing clearance platform. Mandatory electronic invoicing exists only for B2G invoices issued to central and municipal public contracting authorities, which must be transmitted in structured electronic format (e.g. PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 or Factur-X/EN 16931) through an approved access point. For B2B and B2C invoices, paper, PDF, and EDI are accepted subject to recipient consent; all invoices must nevertheless be issued without undue delay and be retained in full for VAT audit purposes. Taxpayers filing Luxembourg VAT returns must do so electronically via MyGuichet.lu or the AEDT secure e-space, with standard monthly or quarterly VAT return obligations and annual recapitulative statements for intra-Community supplies.

Luxembourg imposes no pure exchange-control repatriation deadline, but a 180-day benchmark is commonly used by the AEDT and customs administration as the documentary window for export incidence; if export proceeds are not received or are repatriated significantly later without adequate commercial justification, the tax authorities may treat the transaction as not bona fide. Where export documentation is missing, insufficient, or fraudulent, the 0% rate is clawed back retroactively, the VAT becomes payable together with default interest at the statutory rate, and the AEDT may impose administrative penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded (typically 10% to 20% for non-willful deficiencies) plus fines; deliberate or fraudulent fabrication of export evidence is a customs and VAT criminal offense carrying imprisonment and substantial monetary penalties, and Luxembourg anti-fraud units coordinate with OLAF for intra-EU and cross-border enforcement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no Luxembourg exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements and no approval gates for capital contributions, partnership distributions, dividend-equivalent profit allocations, loan repayments or SaaS royalty receipts. Profit remittance by an SCSp to non-resident partners is unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital; only ordinary AML reporting and CRS/FATCA tax-transparency duties apply, with no beneficial-ownership withholding on profit distributions for a transparent partnership, unless Luxembourg withholding rules on non-resident partners are triggered by specific facts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline and no holding-period constraint on export or service proceeds. EUR and foreign-currency receivables may be retained freely in multi-currency accounts, and the logistics-fleet-dispatch SaaS may invoice global customers in USD, EUR or GBP without BCL reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB); Luxembourg maintains a fully liberalised, notification-only monetary regime with no separate central foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High for payment collection only. A CSSF-authorised Luxembourg EMI or an EU-passported EMI under PSD2 may lawfully operate as a settlement rail for low-risk SaaS receivables, including Stripe Billing via Stripe Payments Europe. However, EMIs lack deposit-guarantee coverage, cannot grant credit or commercial letters of credit, and impose treasury concentration caps. Offshore/non-EEA digital EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury rail because tier-1 Luxembourg banks will generally reject incoming settlement from unlicensed crypto-EMIs or remote, unregulated platforms, and CSSF AML/CFT guidance requires the SCSp to maintain its core liquidity at a supervised credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City; reduced 15% CIT on taxable profits up to EUR 175,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; SCSp distributions are not legally 'dividends' due to tax transparency and are therefore not subject to WHT; if the general partner elects corporate taxation, 15% WHT would apply on distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions per year and consolidated group sales above EUR 35,000,000; above these, a Local File must be kept. A Master File is required for groups with consolidated sales over EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No specific Economic Substance Regulation in Luxembourg, but GAAR under Article 166 LIR and the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in DTAs require genuine activity. The SCSp must maintain matrix of decision-making, local ERP server, call-recording records for dispatch optimization, formal partnership resolutions, and a physically attended office; absence of these will invalidate treaty access and can cause the partnership to be treated as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Shell Company (Artificial Transparent Structure)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp for tax purposes; re-characterization of profits as directly taxable in Luxembourg at partner level; penalty up to 25% of tax understated and monthly interest of 0.6%; criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 (Article 175 LIR).

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be personally liable for any unpaid tax, surcharges and interest; travel ban and imprisonment up to 2 years in aggravated fraud.

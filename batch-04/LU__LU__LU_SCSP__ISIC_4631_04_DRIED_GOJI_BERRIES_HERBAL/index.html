Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration (Administration des Contributions Directes, ACD) for income/corporate/wealth taxes; Luxembourg Indirect Tax and Registration Administration (Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (Administration des Douanes et Accises) for export clearance. Integrated national e-portals: MyGuichet.lu and guichet.lu (business e-filing), Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) for entity registration, and the AEDT electronic VAT filing channel.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Notarial incorporation of the SCSp and filing with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS); the registration automatically generates the national identification number (matricule) which serves as the corporate tax reference. Step 2 — Within 15 days of commencing activity, file the VAT start-of-activity declaration (Form 640, declaration d'assujettissement à la TVA) with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu; the AEDT issues a VAT identification number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits. Step 3 — A foreign non-resident entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a local establishment must register directly with the AEDT (no de minimis threshold; a Luxembourg VAT representative may be required) and must provide proof of foreign establishment, articles of association, and bank references. Step 4 — The ACD opens the corporate income tax and municipal business tax files automatically upon RCS registration; annual returns are filed electronically via the eCDF module of MyGuichet.lu. Standard VAT registration processing time: 2–4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is by default fiscally transparent under Luxembourg tax law: it is outside the scope of corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT); each partner is taxed on its distributive share of income as if realized directly (subject to the partner's residence and treaty position). For VAT, the SCSp has legal personality but is generally treated as a 'non-taxable person' for Luxembourg VAT purposes, with the partners jointly and severally regarded as the taxable persons carrying out the economic activity; however, where the partnership invoices in its own name, the SCSp may be registered for VAT as the invoicing vehicle. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is by default fiscally transparent under Luxembourg tax law: it is outside the scope of corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT); each partner is taxed on its distributive share of income as if realized directly (subject to the partner's residence and treaty position). For VAT, the SCSp has legal personality but is generally treated as a 'non-taxable person' for Luxembourg VAT purposes, with the partners jointly and severally regarded as the taxable persons carrying out the economic activity; however, where the partnership invoices in its own name, the SCSp may be registered for VAT as the invoicing vehicle. Territorial scope: transparency is a domestic-law default only; foreign characterization of the SCSp follows the relevant double tax treaty or foreign check-the-box rules.

Available election forms

1) Entity-level taxation option: the SCSp may elect under the law of 23 December 2016 to be treated as a Luxembourg tax-resident entity subject to CIT, MBT, and NWT ('opting partnership'), thereby also accessing the Luxembourg participation exemption; the election must be expressly provided in the partnership agreement and notified to the ACD, and it is irrevocable. 2) Hybrid-mismatch alignment: Luxembourg applies the ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (2018) and ATAD-2 branch/transparency rules (2020); partners may file alignment statements with the ACD to prevent double non-taxation or to allocate income consistently. 3) No U.S.-style check-the-box: the ACD does not provide an entity-classification form; the classification is evidenced through the partnership agreement, the RCS filing, and the election under (1).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 — no general turnover threshold for the SCSp: registration is mandatory for every economic operator established in Luxembourg from the date of RCS incorporation. A non-established foreign entity is likewise required to register for any taxable supply in Luxembourg unless the reverse-charge mechanism or the OSS/IOSS special schemes apply (EU cross-border distance-sales threshold: EUR 100,000). The optional small-business exemption (Art. 57 Loi TVA, annual turnover below EUR 35,000) is not available for export wholesale trading and does not eliminate the registration duty of an SCSp acting in its own name.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export VAT at 0% applies under Art. 42(I)(1) Loi TVA where the goods (i) are sold to a customer outside the EU, (ii) are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU customs territory, and (iii) are evidenced by export documentation issued in the supplier's name or by equivalent transport evidence accepted by AEDT practice. Dried goji berries (HS 1211/0813) and herbal botanicals must be accompanied, where the destination market requires it, by a phytosanitary certificate; zero-rating is independent of customs tariff status but is denied if the SAD/ECS export declaration contradicts the invoice (destination, recipient, INCOTERM). Export-related services (international transport, loading, handling) are zero-rated; services rendered to non-EU business customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule where no Luxembourg nexus exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration — SAD/summary declaration accepted through Luxembourg's ECS/ICS2 system (or the exporting EU Member State's customs office) evidencing the physical export
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR waybill (road), or Air Waybill (air) in the name of the supplier/consignor showing the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit proof (e.g., SWIFT MT103, account statement) evidencing the foreign customer's payment
  • Commercial invoice and pro-forma invoice cross-referencing the invoice number, HS classification 1211/0813, and the export declaration reference
  • Packing list, phytosanitary certificate, and EUR.1/certificate of origin (where the destination country applies preferential tariff treatment)

No mandatory real-time transaction clearance, fiscal-data API, or digital stamping regime applies to B2B or export invoices in Luxembourg. Electronically issued invoices are valid without prior AEDT approval. A structured e-invoicing obligation (PEPPOL format) applies to B2G supplies to the Luxembourg central administration from 1 January 2024, extended to the broader public sector from 2025. For export transactions, the AEDT requires the full VAT-invoice content set under Art. 5 Loi TVA and compliant electronic retention of source documents for 10 years.

Luxembourg law does not fix a statutory FX repatriation deadline for export VAT or CIT; the 180-day parameter is the prudent international trade benchmark used to evidence a genuine sale and to avoid transfer-pricing deemed interest adjustments on outstanding receivables. The AEDT audits export zero-rating claims on a documentary basis, with dried botanicals being a watch-listed category. Absence, inconsistency, or late production of the export-evidence package triggers retroactive reassessment of VAT at 17%, plus: (i) late-payment interest of 0.5% per month of outstanding tax; (ii) a civil delinquency surcharge of 5% per commenced month up to a maximum of 50% of the tax due; (iii) fixed administrative fines of EUR 250 to EUR 12,500 for formal record-keeping offenses; and (iv) for intentional fraud — including false SAD declarations, fabricated bills of lading, or round-tripping through shell customers — criminal prosecution under the 2018 tax-fraud reform with fines up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus joint and several liability of the SCSp's general partner for the evaded VAT and penalties. All export records must be retained for 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Inbound equity contributions, outbound trade settlements, and profit remittances from a fiscally transparent SCSp are unrestricted. Remittance frictions are confined to standard AML/KYC screening, EU/OFAC/UN sanctions compliance, CRS/FATCA UBO reporting, and documentary proof of the partnership's tax-transparent status (no Luxembourg withholding on distributions to non-resident partners).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may be retained in the original currency (e.g., USD, CNY, EUR) or converted at the SCSp's commercial discretion. The only regulatory overlay is statistical: Luxembourg credit institutions report cross-border payments and external positions to the BCL for Eurosystem balance-of-payments statistics; the SCSp must preserve SWIFT MT103 records, commercial invoices, and bills of lading for audit and reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg, operating within the Eurosystem; foreign-exchange and capital-control administration is liberalised under Article 63 TFEU, with prudential supervision shared by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection/disbursement layer; LOW as the sole treasury anchor. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, Singapore, Hong Kong) lack PSD2 passporting rights into Luxembourg and cannot provide credit, overdrafts, or FX derivatives; EU/EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Nium, Wise Business) may streamline low-value supplier payments and exotic-currency settlements. Legal constraints: PSD2 licensing/passporting requirements, CSSF notification, MiCA applicability to e-money tokens, and AMLD5 UBO disclosure. Many digital EMIs decline a partnership without legal personality (SCSp), so a BCL-regulated Tier-1 credit institution must remain the clearing, liquidity, and FX anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital is injected via SWIFT MT103 (EUR using SEPA/TARGET2; USD or CNY via correspondent MT103/MT202 COVER) carrying a capital-contribution purpose code (partnership equity). Verification comprises bank credit advice, executed partner resolution, updated capital account entries, and tax-advisor confirmation that the injection is equity rather than debt, preserving the SCSp's pass-through neutrality. Luxembourg applies no exchange controls, so no NOSTRO conversion licence is required; for material contributions, source-of-funds documentation must accompany the transfer so the settling bank can satisfy BCL external statistics and AML obligations.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers with standard trade purpose codes for inventory and supplier settlements; EU suppliers may be settled through SEPA Credit Transfers. For significant consignments, a commercial Letter of Credit (sight L/C) is opened by the SCSp's bank in favour of the supplier, or documentary collections (D/P, D/A) are used. Each disbursement is mirror-matched to the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and product certification to satisfy the bank's transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, and AML review.EUR/USD deliverable FX forward contracts (outright, up to 12 months) executed with a BCL-regulated credit institution under ISDA or a bilateral master agreement.EUR/USD FX swaps (swap-point, spot-forward) for rolling treasury management and alignment of USD receivables with EUR working-capital requirements.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for term alignment of long-dated USD trade contracts against EUR cost bases, subject to general-partner authorisation and EMIR reporting as a Non-Financial Counterparty.Vanilla EUR/USD options (zero-cost collars and put/spread structures) to protect minimum export margins while preserving upside participation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to emerging-market supply-chain currencies (e.g., CNY, VND), executed via an EU-authorised counterparty and used solely for cash-flow hedging rather than proprietary speculation.

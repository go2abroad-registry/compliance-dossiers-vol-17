Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a Luxembourg tax ID/VAT ID by registering on MyGuichet via Form 851 (VAT application), attaching notarized constitutional documents, RCS extracts, and appointing a Luxembourg tax representative where no EU establishment exists; the AEDT issues the VAT ID and a national matricule number. For corporate income tax matters, the ACD will assess and issue additional references after the first annual return.

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 10,000 for cross-border intra-Community distance sales; non-resident taxable persons must register for Luxembourg VAT from the first taxable supply unless the reverse charge applies; no general turnover threshold for domestic supplies by established taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of fertilizers (urea/potash) to destinations outside the EU is taxable at 0% subject to proof of physical export from Luxembourg/EU territory and transfer of title to the non-EU customer. B2B intra-EU supplies qualify as exempt intra-Community supplies if the purchaser is VAT-registered under a valid EU VAT ID and an EC sales list is filed.

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force; Luxembourg imposes B2G e-invoicing through PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0; B2B e-invoices are admissible if authenticity and integrity are assured (EN 16931 format accepted). VAT returns are filed electronically via MyGuichet on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis depending on turnover.

The Luxembourg VAT authorities (AEDT) may reassess exports within the general 5-year limitation period (extended under fraud), apply late interest at the statutory rate, and impose penalties for false zero-rating claims, including fines up to €250,000 or imprisonment for tax fraud in aggravated cases; customs offences are pursued under the national customs code and union customs law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full and unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions within the EU internal market, subject to EU-wide financial sanctions, anti-money laundering (AML) screening, and statutory reporting. The euro is fully convertible; no approvals are generally required for profit repatriation. Cross-border transfers within EEA are liberalized under the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) and TFEU Articles 63-65. For a Luxembourg SCSp, distributions and capital withdrawals may be made freely to non-resident partners, though the partnership must appoint a designated agent for the Luxembourg business licence (autorisation d'établissement) if applicable, and should preserve appropriate documentation of the underlying transaction string to evidence the absence of sanctions exposure and to maintain the substance of the entity for tax transparency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Luxembourg. Private commodity traders, including wholesale merchants of prilled urea and potassium chloride, are not obliged to convert or repatriate export receipts to the BCL. All foreign currency receipts may be held in commercial bank accounts in or outside Luxembourg; however, AML/CFT regulations require that the origin and destination of each international transfer be documented and beneficial ownership be disclosed. In practice, an exporter should maintain commercial invoices, transport documents, certificates of origin, and quality certificates to demonstrate the business purpose of each incoming transfer.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the main financial sector regulator; direct exchange controls are not administered by a separate foreign exchange authority because Luxembourg is an EU/EMU member state adopting the euro.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions are regulated by the CSSF and can offer IBANs and cross-border payment execution. They are suitable for routine trade payments, but most EMIs do not provide commodity-trade credit facilities, foreign currency accounts, or trade finance (LC issuance). Offshore digital EMIs from unrelated low-tax jurisdictions are generally not accepted as a primary treasury vehicle by Luxembourg counterparties for high-volume fertilizer trades; the beneficial ownership of these EMIs is subject to enhanced due diligence (EDD) and may trigger a 'prohibited presence' blocker if effective management cannot be evidenced. An optimal design uses a Luxembourg PSP/EMI for FX conversion and clearing alongside a tier-1 bank for commercial letters of credit and settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SCSp should receive equity contributions and partner loans from the partners' individual bank accounts (or from a Luxembourg holding company) via SWIFT MT103 / SEPA CT. The remitter must clearly reference the counterpart's name and the purpose of payment using the correct CBPR+ purpose code, commonly 'AFIN' for acquiring a direct investment or 'BEXP' for related operating expenses. For non-EEA partners, the transfer must be accompanied by an FX contract documentation, source of funds certificate, and the beneficiary account details of the SCSp with a Luxembourg IBAN. All inbound transfers should be tagged in the bank records with a reference to the partnership agreement and the partner's capital account ledger entries. To avoid the bank's automatic 'unexpected credit' investigation, the SCSp should pre-notify the account relationship manager 48 hours before a major capital inflow, providing a board resolution or a general partner resolution authorising the capital call. Any partner loan must be structured with an arm's length interest rate (typically EURIBOR + 1.5% to 3%) and documented through a loan agreement to avoid reclassification as capital for tax or AML purposes.Disbursements to third-party suppliers of urea (prilled, granulated) and potash (muriate/potassium chloride) are preferably settled via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for freight payment, and through standby letters of credit (MT700) or documentary collection for high-value consignments. The SCSp should instruct the issuing bank to apply the current UCP 600 ICC rules and to use the bank's trade finance template that includes commodity name, grade, Incoterms (e.g., CIF, FOB, CFR), delivery window, and the Bill of Lading as the trigger document. Trade disbursements between EUR-billing partners should use SEPA (XML ISO 20022) for speed and reduced charges, while all non-EEA suppliers require full SWIFT messages. For consignment programmes with suppliers in the Arabian Gulf or Baltic/North Sea ports, the bank will require a Trade Confirmation and a Certificate of Inspection issued by an independent surveyor (e.g., SGS, Intertek) to ensure that the shipment does not contain dual-use chemical impurities.

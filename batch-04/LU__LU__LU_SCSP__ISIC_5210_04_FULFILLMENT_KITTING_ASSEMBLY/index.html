Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and registration duties; unified electronic business portal: MyGuichet.lu (professional space, eCDF e-filing) and the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) database.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the SCSp partnership deed and file it with the RCS Luxembourg to obtain the RCS registration number and the matricule national. Step 2: Submit the single business and VAT registration application (Form 510) through MyGuichet.lu to the AED to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits); a non-established foreign entity must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT and enclose its foreign VAT registration certificate and commercial-register excerpt. Step 3: Register the direct-tax profile with the ACD: the SCSp remains fiscally transparent by default, so the partners are assessed on their allocable shares via Form 500 (individuals) or Form 520 (corporate partners); the SCSp itself obtains a separate corporate-tax file only if the Article 175(1) LIR commercial-activity exception to transparency applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for CIT, municipal business tax (MBT) and net wealth tax; there is no US-style check-the-box. Article 175bis LIR permits a partnership not exploiting a commercial enterprise to elect irrevocably to be taxed as a corporation (binding for at least 5 tax years). A fulfillment/kitting operation constitutes a commercial activity and may therefore trigger entity-level CIT/MBT under Article 175(1) LIR, which can be mitigated through a Luxembourg advance tax ruling (tax confirmation) on the intended partner-level allocation. ATAD II hybrid-mismatch rules override any elective treatment and require payment and invoice flows to be aligned with the transparent status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover for the established small-enterprise franchise (Article 94 of the VAT Law); non-established foreign suppliers are registrable from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg, subject to reverse charge where the customer is a taxable person (B2B place-of-supply rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) goods physically exported outside the EU, evidenced by transport and dispatch documentation proving departure from EU territory; (ii) ancillary services directly related to those goods, including freight handling, palletization, kitting, assembly and storage, supplied by the operator; and (iii) services supplied to non-EU B2B customers where the place of supply is outside Luxembourg. Intra-EU B2B dispatches from Luxembourg fulfillment centers are exempt with credit for input VAT, requiring the customer's valid EU VAT number and EC Sales List declaration. Luxembourg law does not impose a calendrical FX-repatriation deadline for VAT zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark is retained as the internal documentary-compliance standard for proving genuine export consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU lodged electronically with the Luxembourg customs authorities, or the export manifest for postal and express consignments)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency payment from the non-EU buyer)

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing since 1 January 2024 via the Peppol eDelivery network (eSpace) for invoices addressed to the Luxembourg State and public-sector bodies; no general mandatory B2B real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous-transaction-control API applies to the kitting/fulfillment sector; B2B e-invoicing remains optional, and VAT is settled periodically on the periodic VAT return (Form 1001).

The AED may reassess VAT within the standard 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or evasion. Unjustified zero-rating triggers retrospective clawback of the VAT due plus statutory late-payment interest, administrative fines under Article 50 of the VAT Law (EUR 50 to EUR 1,000 per infringement) and, in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal fines and imprisonment. The exporter bears the burden of proof and is jointly and severally liable for any input VAT recovered on zero-rated transactions that fail audit verification.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a euro-area Member State, applies full liberalisation of capital movements and current payments in accordance with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no foreign exchange controls, no BCL licensing gates, and no prior approval requirements for conversion between EUR, USD, GBP, CHF, or other freely convertible currencies. Both current account transactions (trade payments, services, wages) and capital account transactions (capital contributions, loan repayments, distributions, return of capital) are freely convertible and freely remittable. Profit remittance constraints are de minimis: remittances by an SCSp are blocked only by ordinary AML/KYC screening, transfer-of-funds information requirements under EU Regulation 2015/847, and sanctions compliance. The SCSp has no Luxembourg tax on profits at entity level because it is tax-transparent, but profit withdrawals paid to partners must satisfy source-of-funds and beneficial ownership documentation to prevent bank refusal on money-laundering risk grounds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Luxembourg. No period is prescribed for repatriation, and no percentage of trade receivables must be converted into EUR. Export proceeds from fulfilment, kitting, and assembly services may be retained in the original invoice currency or freely swapped into EUR, USD, or any other permitted currency. Banks may, however, require a commercial contract or customs/transport document for the receipt to remain within the permitted 'low-risk standard' tier under the CSSF AML framework, and all inbound export collections must be traceable to named counterparties.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); regulatory coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CFT, banking supervision, and payment service provider licensing under Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 and CSSF prudential rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operating cash flows, Low for regulated capital or escrow arrangements. Luxembourg permits CSSF-licensed or EU/EEA-passported e-money institutions and payment institutions to issue payment accounts to commercial clients under PSD2. An SCSp engaged in kitting and 3PL fulfilment can open multi-currency payment accounts with Airwallex, Currencycloud, or similar EMIs to collect low-value retailer/brand payouts and disburse supplier invoices in USD, GBP, and EUR with API automation. Legal constraints: no non-EU EMI may provide Luxembourg IBANs without a proper EU/EAA e-money licence or agent arrangement; EMI accounts are not bank deposits and fall outside the Luxembourg Deposit Guarantee Scheme of the FGDL; such accounts cannot be used for issuance/acceptance of documentary credits, do not provide counterparty credit facilities, and are not suitable as custodial capital escrow accounts. For the low-risk logistical receivables flows they are, however, a viable treasury rail because of lower onboarding friction, competitive FX spreads, and SWIFT/local-network settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital injections into the SCSp must be routed through SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR from EU/EEA accounts) or SWIFT MT103 / MT202 COV (for non-EU contributions or USD/GBP conversions), using the SCSp's dedicated capital sub-account to ensure traceability. The remittance instructions must include the full name and address of the transferring partner, the SCSp legal name, and a structured reference such as 'SCSP-CAP-INV-YYYYMMDD-001' equal to the capital call document. For non-EU remittances, the bank requires an indication of the economic purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment or partnership equity acquisition, supported by the notarised or written partnership agreement; no BCL foreign-exchange approval is needed. The receiving Luxembourg bank will verify that the remitting partner is the actual holder of the sending account and that the source of funds is compatible with the declared wealth; where funds pass through an intermediary, the full payment chain must be visible under Article 4 of EU Regulation 2015/847.The blueprint uses SEPA Credit Transfer for all EUR-denominated vendor payments within the EEA, leveraging the Luxembourg Target2 membership and instant SEPA settlement rails. For non-EUR supplier invoices, trade settlements are executed via SWIFT MT103 with corresponding bank cover, or MT202 COV for high-value intercompany reimbursements. Commercial letters of credit (documentary credits governed by UCP600 and ISP98 where required) are available through tier-one Luxembourg banks for international purchase of kitting components; issuance is by MT700 with MT707 amendments, with presentation documents routed through the nominated or confirming bank. For smaller, repetitive supplier payments and marketplace fee disbursements, the treasury overlay uses Airwallex local clearing routes in supported currencies, with automatic conversion into EUR at day-end and sweep to the primary BIL or Spuerkeess account. The outbound treasury team must maintain audit evidence of each disbursement: supplier invoice, purchase order, packing list, and sanctions/screen result per beneficiary.Outright forward FX contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, USD/EUR) with a licensed Luxembourg bank or EU investment firm under the ISDA Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex, provided the underlying exposure is a signed supplier invoice or forecast purchase order.Window or rolling forwards allowing periodic USD procurement hedges for kitting component imports without fixing every individual invoice.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with settlement restrictions, e.g., offshore CNY, receivable from emerging-market e-commerce counterparties, cleared in USD/EUR offshore and documented with a trade rationale.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS / CCIRS) solely for minimising basis risk on a committed multi-year USD supply agreement and matching EUR funding; no speculative option positions are permitted under the SCSp treasury policy.At-the-money FX spot-limit orders executed through the bank's electronic dealing platform without leverage, and FX swaps for temporary liquidity in EUR/USD, subject to daily counterparty credit limits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City aggregate: 17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions made by a transparent SCSp because such distributions are partnership profit distributions, not dividends. The Luxembourg statutory dividend withholding tax rate for ordinary opaque companies is 15%, and may be reduced or exempted under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a double taxation treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Luxembourg Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. The Luxembourg tax administration may require transfer pricing documentation for any material related-party transaction, and in practice expects robust contemporaneous documentation for SCSp transactions with related parties above EUR 100,000, even absent a formal statutory Local File threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive and Luxembourg general anti-abuse rules require genuine economic substance. An SCSp engaged in fulfilment kitting and assembly must maintain a Luxembourg registered office, a resident corporate general partner, decision-making records, bank account, payroll or subcontracting capacity for actual operations, and books showing that the partners assume real risk and control. Artificial arrangements without substance will be challenged, and treaty benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and undeclared Luxembourg-source income

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge up to 50% of the evaded tax plus late-payment interest; criminal prosecution for tax fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000; treaty relief is denied and the SCSp may be recharacterized as opaque and taxed at 24.94%.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly cause or participate in tax evasion can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties under abuse-of-rights and fraud doctrines; travel bans, asset seizures, and extradition requests may arise in cross-border criminal tax proceedings.
Shell company / artificial arrangement / sham SCSp

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the SCSp as a taxable Luxembourg entity; comprehensive reassessment at the combined 24.94% rate; denial of double taxation treaty benefits; imputation of hidden profits; 10% late-payment interest and significant fines; EU ATAD GAAR countermeasures.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency if they orchestrated the sham; disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies and travel bans are available measures in serious cross-border abuse cases.
AML/CFT and beneficial ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg AML/CFT law, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000 for individuals; legal persons may face administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000; suspension of management and publication of the sanction are possible.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers of the SCSp's general partner are personally exposed to criminal sanctions and disqualification if they knowingly facilitated money laundering or breached beneficial ownership disclosure duties; assets may be frozen and travel restricted during investigation.
Transfer pricing manipulation and false documentation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to taxable income, secondary adjustments, denial of cost deductions, interest accrual, and fines for gross negligence up to 50% of the deficiency; criminal fraud exposure if false documentation is used.

Director personal liability: Chief officers and directors who sign or approve false transfer pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax; personal fines and imprisonment apply under fraud provisions; professional disqualification may be ordered.

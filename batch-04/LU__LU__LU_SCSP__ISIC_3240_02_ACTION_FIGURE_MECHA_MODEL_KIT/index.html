Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual or domiciliation office is permissible for the SCSp provided it is supplied by a licensed Luxembourg domiciliation company and the address is declared to the RCSL as the registered office. Given the ISIC 3240 manufacturing scope, the SCSp will typically act as the investment and holding wrapper while the mechanical production, injection moulding, and packaging activities are carried out by an operational subsidiary with physical substance. Virtual office arrangements are therefore accepted at the Luxembourg holding level, but the partnership must retain access to its funds, records, and managerial oversight in Luxembourg. A purely artificial seat without any administrative substance may expose the SCSp to reassessment under the abuse-of-law doctrine and to non-cooperation findings in AML/CFT reviews.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp; capital is freely set in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is prescribed by law. Contributions may be in cash or in kind, and must be fully subscribed and paid as specified in the contribution clause.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Service (CGDIS) confirming compliance with the Grand-Ducal fire safety regulations; structural load inspection certificate from an approved engineering office; workplace safety approval from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering machinery, electrical safety and occupational health.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp; capital is freely set in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is prescribed by law. Contributions may be in cash or in kind, and must be fully subscribed and paid as specified in the contribution clause.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income/corporate taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; customs tariffs and export controls under Administration des douanes et accises. Primary e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu); company registration through Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

An SCSp must first be registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a notary and an identified registered office, which generates a national business registration number (numéro d'immatriculation). For VAT, the SCSp or a non-resident taxable person carrying on taxable activities in Luxembourg must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu; the application requires: proof of RCS registration, statutes, identity documents of managers/partners, description of activities, and expected turnover. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative, subject to applicable treaties and administrative practice. The SCSp itself generally receives a Luxembourg VAT identification number if it performs an economic activity, without being subject to corporate income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; SCSp is fiscally transparent by default under Luxembourg income tax law, municipal business tax law, and net wealth tax law. Income, gains, and deductions flow through to its partners. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; SCSp is fiscally transparent by default under Luxembourg income tax law, municipal business tax law, and net wealth tax law. Income, gains, and deductions flow through to its partners. The SCSp's registered office in Luxembourg creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment/fixed place of business only in appropriate circumstances; partners resident or with a permanent establishment in Luxembourg are taxed at the partner level. A non-resident partner with no Luxembourg permanent establishment is generally not subject to Luxembourg income tax on profits attributable to the transparent partnership, except where Luxembourg-source income categories apply.

Available election forms

No direct check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; tax transparency of the SCSp is mandatory and not avoidable for income tax purposes. Available elections include: (i) VAT grouping election per Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law to treat the SCSp with related entities as a single taxable person; (ii) option for the SCSp to become a taxable person for VAT if not already; (iii) application of EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (Law of 21 December 2018, as amended) may recharacterize a foreign transparent entity or partnership as opaque or transparent in cross-border situations; no unilateral domestic transparency election is available, but taxpayers may rely on the definitive classification drawn from the legal form and the place of effective management.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for non-resident suppliers: any taxable economic activity performed in Luxembourg (including distance sales above the EU-wide €10,000 threshold in certain cases) requires VAT registration. Resident small enterprises may benefit from the small-business exemption only if annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT). Intra-EU acquisitions by taxable persons require registration above the annual acquisition threshold of EUR 10,000, unless opted into Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied by the SCSp and physically exported from Luxembourg/Europe to a destination outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT (export exemption) if dispatch or transport is carried out by the supplier or a third party on behalf of the supplier and is substantiated by export documentation. The supplier must obtain and preserve proof of export within the formalities set by Luxembourg customs and the AEDT, generally no later than 90 days from the supply. Services directly connected to exported goods and certain cross-border services (including manufacturing under customs supervision) may also be zero-rated if the recipient is established outside the EU and the services relate to goods that will be exported; all relevant contractual and documentary evidence must support the economic substance of the transaction.

  • Customs export declaration (Luxembourg e-Douanes/ECS export notification, including the Movement Reference Number MRN)
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU (Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and payment/remittance evidence (bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation or payment receipt; inward remittance certificate may be requested by the tax auditor as supplementary proof of consideration)

No real-time national fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping is currently imposed on B2B or B2C e-invoices in Luxembourg. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory pursuant to Directive 2014/55/EU and must be transmitted through the Peppol network or an equivalent platform. For all invoices, the Luxembourg VAT law requires authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility; this may be ensured by business controls, e-invoicing, EDI, or advanced electronic signatures. Invoices must be retained for 10 years. Luxembourg is monitoring the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) initiative, which may introduce real-time digital reporting from 2027/2028.

The AEDT and Luxembourg customs audit export claims strictly. If the exporter cannot produce the required export proof within the legal deadline, the zero rating is denied and output VAT becomes payable at 17%, plus default interest of 0.6% per month (or the statutory rate in force) from the due date. A misdeclaration or fraudulent claim may result in a penalty tax of up to 100% of the tax evaded (10% for good-faith rectification, 25% for gross negligence, and up to 100% for intentional fraud), personal liability of managers, and criminal sanctions for customs fraud, including fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment. Input VAT already claimed in relation to the export is clawed back if the zero-rating is disproved, and the assessment remains open under the standard Luxembourg statute of limitations (generally five years, which is extended in cases of fraud or failure to file).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions in Luxembourg. There are no exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements, and no profit-repatriation restrictions for an SCSp. Non-resident partners may receive distributions through normal banking channels without BCL permit, subject only to AML/KYC documentation, sanctions screening, and any applicable withholding reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no mandatory reinvestment or escrow obligation for export receipts. An LU_SCSp may retain export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency in any bank account held with an authorised credit institution, and may freely convert, hedge, or disburse them, subject only to BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight of credit institutions coordinated with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective rate for Luxembourg City (CIT 17.00% + solidarity surcharge 7.00% of CIT + municipal business tax 6.75%). The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, so this rate is allocated and applied at the level of each partner on its profit share.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15.00% general Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends (LIR Article 147). A default transparent SCSp is not subject to dividend WHT on distributions to partners because the partnership is not a taxpayer; however, an SCSp that has elected non-transparent treatment may trigger the 15% WHT, reduced to 0% where the parent-subsidiary directive applies (≥10% holding, 12-month minimum holding) or under the participation exemption conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Paper filing: 30 June of the year following the fiscal year-end. Electronic filing: automatic extension to 31 December of the same year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File become mandatory when the worldwide consolidated group turnover reaches EUR 50,000,000; for smaller SCSp structures, the ACD may still request contemporaneous documentation for intercompany transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per category (ACD Circular L.I.R. No. 56/1-56bis/1, OECD TP Guidelines).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have UAE-style sectoral ESR, but the economic substance doctrine is enforced through general anti-abuse rules: Article 6 of the Tax Adjustment Act (abuse of forms), ATAD-implemented anti-hybrid and exit rules, and the substance circulars governing intragroup financing and IP holding companies. A SCSp deriving royalties from the anime figure IP or holding substantial cash must demonstrate real presence in Luxembourg: local strategic decision-making, a Luxembourg-resident manager, office premises, board/partner meetings, and actual business activity; absence of substance causes the ACD to disregard the SCSp, deny treaty and participation exemption benefits, and restate income to the beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / Falsified tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fraud penalties under the General Tax Law (AO): imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and/or a criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000; civil reassessment with 10% late-payment surcharge and 0.6% monthly delay interest; publication of the conviction and disgorgement of assets traced to the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt and civil restitution where they committed a faute grave or fraud (Company Law Article 495, Civil Code Article 1382); criminal courts may impose travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, and confiscation orders; imprisonment applies to the individual who signed the false return.
Shell company / Abuse of SCSp transparency

Statutory sanction: ACD requalification of the SCSp as an opaque corporation under the GAAR, reassessment with the 15% dividend WHT plus 0.6% monthly interest and 10% penalty, and denial of DTA benefits; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 for failure to provide substance information; potential strike-off of the entity from the RCS.

Director personal liability: Directors risk joint liability for all unpaid taxes through the faute de gestion doctrine, personal civil restitution of distributions made without substance, administrative travel bans during the tax fraud investigation, and disqualification from management for up to 10 years upon conviction.
AML / Beneficial ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from EUR 5,000 to EUR 1,250,000 under the amended law of 12 November 2004; criminal penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for wilful breaches; the RCS may suspend the partner voting rights and the ACD may freeze the SCSp bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly omit UBO disclosure incur personal liability for the AML fine, personal disqualification orders, travel bans in the context of the financial intelligence investigation, and imprisonment for deliberate laundering of undeclared route payments.

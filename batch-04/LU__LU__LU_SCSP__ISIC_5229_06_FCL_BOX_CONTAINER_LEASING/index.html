Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), L-4361 Esch-sur-Alzette, Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Part VII, Articles 944-1 to 944-11, as introduced by the Law of 23 July 2016 creating the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); complemented by the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the e-justice regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

The SCSp charter or partnership agreement (contrat de société / acte constitutif), executed by private deed or by notarial deed where real-property contributions are made, and filed with the RCS through its electronic filing interface or via a notary.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; partners freely determine subscription and payment terms in the charter. Cash contributions are deposited with a Luxembourg credit institution before RCS registration, but no mandatory escrow or blocked-account regime applies. In-kind contributions (e.g., transfer of container fleet assets) must be supported by a fair-value report of an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) annexed to the charter.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance of any container-storage site by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) in accordance with the Grand-Ducal regulation of 28 April 2015 on fire-protection requirements; structural load inspections of stacking racks, lifting equipment and storage yards by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) under the Luxembourg Labour Code (Code du travail); workplace health-and-safety clearance by the ITM before commissioning any depot, including verification of electrical installations and emergency-response plans.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; partners freely determine subscription and payment terms in the charter. Cash contributions are deposited with a Luxembourg credit institution before RCS registration, but no mandatory escrow or blocked-account regime applies. In-kind contributions (e.g., transfer of container fleet assets) must be supported by a fair-value report of an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) annexed to the charter.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), 33 rue de la Congrégation, L-1352 Luxembourg; portal: impotsdirects.public.lu. Indirect taxes: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); portals: pfi.public.lu and guichet.public.lu. Business registry and national entity identifier: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), lbr.lu; all administrative filings via MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu to obtain the national identification number (matricule / numéro national); (2) appoint a registered office in Luxembourg and file the partnership deed; (3) for VAT, apply to the AEDT on Form 640 for a 12-digit Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) before the first taxable transaction; (4) EU-established foreign partners trading through a Luxembourg branch may register directly; non-EU foreign entities carrying out taxable transactions must appoint a VAT fiscal representative (Art. 60(1) VAT Law); (5) as the SCSp is fiscally transparent, income-tax identification is effected through the partners' own domicile filings; (6) register with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) only if the SCSp employs personnel. Typical processing time: 2-4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg income tax purposes (Art. 2 and Art. 4 LIR; Art. 32(1) of the law of 10 August 1915). No corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax is due at entity level; income, gains, and expenses from the container-leasing activity are allocated pro rata to the partners according to their entitlement to profits/losses while retaining their commercial character. Exception: transparency is denied if bearer securities are issued or securities are admitted to trading on a stock exchange (Art. 2(2)(d) LIR). Typical processing time: 2-4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg income tax purposes (Art. 2 and Art. 4 LIR; Art. 32(1) of the law of 10 August 1915). No corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax is due at entity level; income, gains, and expenses from the container-leasing activity are allocated pro rata to the partners according to their entitlement to profits/losses while retaining their commercial character. Exception: transparency is denied if bearer securities are issued or securities are admitted to trading on a stock exchange (Art. 2(2)(d) LIR). VAT: the SCSp may independently qualify as a taxable person for VAT where it carries out an economic activity in its own name.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Available elections: (a) Section 182 LIR 'option de taxation' - an SCSp may irrevocably elect to be taxed as a capital company (CIT, municipal business tax, net wealth tax) provided all unlimited partners are Luxembourg-resident, capital is fully paid, a 5-year commitment is made, and no debt securities are listed; request must be filed within 3 months of the end of the fiscal year; (b) hybrid-mismatch alignment elections under Art. 168quater LIR and related provisions implementing ATAD 2 (Law of 20 December 2019, as amended), allowing taxpayers to neutralize deductible/non-deductible asymmetries through a formal election; (c) Art. 64 VAT Law election to waive the small-enterprise VAT exemption and register voluntarily; (d) Art. 63 VAT Law election to form a VAT group (unité TVA) for closely linked entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established small enterprises: EUR 35,000 annual turnover (Art. 57(1) VAT Law); non-established foreign taxable persons: no threshold, mandatory registration from the first taxable transaction (EUR 0); an SCSp engaged in cross-border container leasing is ineligible for the small-enterprise exemption where its partners are foreign businesses

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Container leasing is a supply of services (leasing of movable property). Zero/outside-scope results are achieved through two routes: (a) B2B place-of-supply rule (Art. 13(1) VAT Law) - where the lessee is an established business, the supply is located at the lessee's establishment: an EU lessee triggers reverse charge in its member state (billing without Luxembourg VAT, citing Art. 21 VAT Law / Art. 196 of the VAT Directive), while a non-EU lessee places the transaction outside Luxembourg VAT entirely; (b) effective use-and-enjoyment relief (Art. 14(1) VAT Law) - leasing of containers used exclusively in international deep-sea traffic or physically used outside the EU, even where the supplier is Luxembourg-established, is outside Luxembourg VAT. Export zero-rating under Art. 155 VAT Law applies only where containers are sold and physically dispatched to a non-EU destination (transfer of ownership, not mere bailment). Substantiation requires a signed lease agreement evidencing international traffic use, a container schedule (box numbers, lease period, destination port), and documentary proof of the lessee's establishment outside the EU (VAT/GST registration, commercial registry extract).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document) bearing the Master Reference Number (MRN) for container sales/exports to non-EU destinations
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or multimodal transport document (CMR/AWB) evidencing dispatch from the EU and arrival outside the EU, endorsed to the Luxembourg exporter or its freight forwarder
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer advice evidencing receipt of the invoiced amount (EUR or foreign currency) from the non-EU lessee or buyer
  • Executed container lease agreement (dry/depot lease) with asset schedule, lease term, destination, and customer's VAT/GST registration number or certificate of non-EU establishment

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping applies. Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing via the PEPPOL 3.0 network for supplies to Luxembourg public authorities and subsidized entities (effective 1 January 2022). B2B invoicing accepts paper, PDF, and structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) subject to EU authenticity rules (Art. 85-87 VAT Directive). SAF-T reporting is mandatory from 1 January 2024 for VAT-registered businesses with annual turnover above EUR 112,000. Intra-EU recapitulative statements (VIES/EC Sales Lists) and INTRASTAT declarations apply to goods movements above EUR 150,000 (arrivals) and EUR 460,000 (dispatches) annually; container leasing is reported as a B2B service on VIES where the lessee quotes a valid EU VAT number.

Luxembourg imposes no statutory euro-denominated FX repatriation deadline for export earnings; the 180-day benchmark functions as a documentary-reasonableness standard applied by the ACD/AEDT when auditing international service revenue. Where zero-rating is improperly claimed (e.g., EU-established lessee, missing reverse-charge evidence, containers used only within EU inland traffic, or fabricated export documents), the tax authorities retrospectively recharacterize the supply at 16% and impose: (i) late-payment interest on VAT at 0.6% per month (Art. 33 of the VAT enforcement regulation); (ii) late-payment penalties of EUR 250 to EUR 12,500 per taxable period (Art. 92(1)-(3) VAT Law); (iii) false-declaration penalties of EUR 250 to EUR 25,000; and (iv) criminal tax-fraud sanctions under Articles 396-399 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO), carrying imprisonment of up to 3 years and/or fines up to EUR 250,000 where fraudulent export documentation is proven. Because the SCSp is transparent, any direct-tax adjustments (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax) flow through to the partners, and the ACD may additionally assess arrears interest at 0.6% per month on underpaid direct taxes, with cross-border exchange of information under the DAC and OECD mutual assistance frameworks supporting recovery from foreign partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, as mandated by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is fully convertible; there are no exchange control restrictions, no surrender requirements, and no prior authorisation for profit repatriation, dividend distributions, or liquidation proceeds, provided the entity is tax-resident or properly structured and complies with general anti-money laundering, beneficial ownership declaration, and substance requirements. Cross-border payments are unrestricted within the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA), and non-SEPA transfers require only standard SWIFT messaging and counterparty bank compliance. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to documentary compliance: tax residence certificate, financial statements evidencing distributable reserves, and corporate authorisations; withholding tax leakage on outbound interest, royalty, or dividend flows may arise under domestic law (15% dividend WHT, 15% interest WHT, 10% royalty WHT) unless reduced by tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary and Interest-Royalty Directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Luxembourg. The Grand Duchy abolished all foreign exchange surrender obligations upon adoption of the euro and full capital liberalisation. Exporters must retain trade documentation (invoices, transport documents, customs declarations) solely for VAT, customs, and anti-money laundering audit purposes. There is no requirement to convert export receipts into euro; proceeds may be held in any currency in a Luxembourg or foreign bank account, subject to the entity's treasury policy and OECD/CSSF substance expectations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and payment oversight; no standalone foreign exchange administration authority, as Luxembourg applies complete capital liberalisation within the EU/EEA framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary operational accounts, LOW as primary treasury bank. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions supervised by the CSSF can provide SEPA and SWIFT-accessible payment accounts, FX conversion, and virtual IBANs. However, the SCSp's treasury function—container leasing collections from global lessees, disbursements to lessors, and FX hedging—would require a full commercial bank relationship for SWIFT MT103 correspondent coverage, documentary letters of credit, and trade finance instruments. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport are NOT viable for regulated treasury flows because they lack correspondent banking access, may not offer segregated client accounts compliant with CSSF circulars, and create AML/beneficial ownership transparency failures. A hybrid structure of a tier-one Luxembourg commercial bank as primary and an EU-licensed EMI for SEPA collections is acceptable only if the EMI holds funds at a qualifying credit institution and the SCSp maintains full audit trail integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for investor capital into the SCSp requires: (i) wire transfer from each limited partner's own bank account in the same legal name; (ii) use of SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes corresponding to 'Investment capital' / 'Capital contribution'—for euro-denominated transfers, EBA SEPA payment types may be used but SWIFT with narrative 'LU_SCSP_CAP_CONTRIBUTION_[DATE]' is preferred for audit clarity; (iii) the commercial bank will require a capital call notice issued by the GP referencing the SCSp partnership agreement and the specific investor's commitment schedule; (iv) the bank must verify the UBO profile of the transferring entity and may request ISD (International Swaps and Derivatives Association) or equivalent confirmation if contributions are structured as shareholder loans; (v) incoming funds should be credited to a segregated Capital Escrow Account and only released to the Operational Account upon completion of AML verification and issuance of a partner register entry; (vi) currency conversion of non-euro contributions is executed at standard market rates through the bank's FX desk, and conversion services must not be provided by unregulated counterparties. Non-euro injections must be accompanied by a beneficiary customer due diligence certification and, where applicable, a Loan Agreement or Subscription Agreement, to pre-empt money laundering red flags.Outbound disbursements for lease payments to container owners, sub-lessors, and service providers should use the following rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all non-SEPA or high-value international payments, with proper narrative identifying the underlying invoice (container lease serial, vessel/terminal location, and contract reference); (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro payments within the EEA under 100,000 EUR per transaction, with SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) only for operational expenses below 10,000 EUR where the beneficiary bank supports it; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) for large fleet acquisition or lease-in contracts requiring payment against shipping documents—issued by the Luxembourg bank and advised through a second-tier correspondent bank; (iv) Buyer's or Supplier's Credit facilities (avals or standby LCs) if the container leasing arrangement requires guarantees for terminal operators or ocean carriers; and (v) ACH Debit only for recurring service charges within the USA where the SCSp maintains a US-dollar sweeper through its Luxembourg bank's correspondent network. All disbursements must be backed by the invoice matching protocol: to prevent 'phantom invoicing', the treasury function must match the outward wire to purchase orders, delivery confirmations, and container inspection reports, and must maintain an audit trail for VAT reverse-charge or intra-community supply rules under Luxembourg VAT law.Outright FX Forward Contracts (deliverable) up to 12 months tenor, executed with the SCSp's Luxembourg bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank's standard FX Master Agreement; USD/EUR and CNY/EUR forwards are permissible for matching lease receipts in USD or renminbi from cross-border lessees.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted Asian or African currencies, where deliverable forwards are unavailable; the SCSp may use NDFs referencing the offshore CNY (CNH) or selected emerging-market currencies solely to hedge contracted lease income streams, provided the counterparty bank is OECD-regulated and the NDF underlying exposure is documented in the lease receivable ledger.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for converting long-term USD lease liabilities or USD-denominated fleet financing into EUR, or vice versa, on a rolling 1-5 year basis; analogised as interest rate and principal exchange with periodic re-fixing at ISDA fallbacks, permissible only if the counterparty is the primary Luxembourg bank or its investment-grade affiliate.FX Options (European-style barrier or vanilla) purchased, not written, to protect against adverse USD/EUR and CNH/EUR moves while retaining upside; option premiums are treated as treasury expenses and require board- or GP-approval under the SCSp risk management mandate, with a ban on uncovered option writing.Currency Futures (listed, exchange-traded) on CME or Eurex for short-dated (up to 6 months) USD/EUR and CNY/USD exposures, executed through a futures commission merchant and margined from the Operational Account; permissible only for transparent price discovery and not for speculative positions.NDS / FX Swaps with prime counterparties for short-term liquidity hedging of currency mismatches arising from repaying an intercompany loan in one currency while holding lease revenue in another; economic terms documented under a credit support annex, with MTM valuation each month.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for ancillary operational accounts, LOW as primary treasury bank. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions supervised by the CSSF can provide SEPA and SWIFT-accessible payment accounts, FX conversion, and virtual IBANs. However, the SCSp's treasury function—container leasing collections from global lessees, disbursements to lessors, and FX hedging—would require a full commercial bank relationship for SWIFT MT103 correspondent coverage, documentary letters of credit, and trade finance instruments. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport are NOT viable for regulated treasury flows because they lack correspondent banking access, may not offer segregated client accounts compliant with CSSF circulars, and create AML/beneficial ownership transparency failures. A hybrid structure of a tier-one Luxembourg commercial bank as primary and an EU-licensed EMI for SEPA collections is acceptable only if the EMI holds funds at a qualifying credit institution and the SCSp maintains full audit trail integrity.

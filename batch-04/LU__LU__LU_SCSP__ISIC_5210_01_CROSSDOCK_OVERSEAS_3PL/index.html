Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes. Single digital portal: MyGuichet.lu operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the State IT Center (CTIE).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident SCSp without an establishment in Luxembourg, the general partner or authorized representative must (1) file the Luxembourg VAT registration application via the AED using Form 640 (or through MyGuichet.lu), providing the partnership deed, proof of identity of the general partner, and an appointment of a Luxembourg VAT representative if the SCSp has no fixed establishment in Luxembourg; (2) obtain a Luxembourg national business number (numéro national / matricule) and, where required, a taxpayer ID for direct tax purposes from the RCS / LBR; (3) request a dedicated VAT ID for the SCSp as a taxable person for VAT purposes, which is independent from the transparency status for income tax; and (4) update the registration upon any change in activity, turnover, or constitutional documents.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident SCSp without an establishment in Luxembourg, the general partner or authorized representative must (1) file the Luxembourg VAT registration application via the AED using Form 640 (or through MyGuichet.lu), providing the partnership deed, proof of identity of the general partner, and an appointment of a Luxembourg VAT representative if the SCSp has no fixed establishment in Luxembourg; (2) obtain a Luxembourg national business number (numéro national / matricule) and, where required, a taxpayer ID for direct tax purposes from the RCS / LBR; (3) request a dedicated VAT ID for the SCSp as a taxable person for VAT purposes, which is independent from the transparency status for income tax; and (4) update the registration upon any change in activity, turnover, or constitutional documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An SCSp may elect, under Article 175 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), to be treated as a transparent entity (default) or, upon application to the ACD, to be subject to corporate income tax / net wealth tax as a 'deemed corporate taxpayer'. Hybrid mismatch elections are limited; Luxembourg follows the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and the 2023 OECD hybrid mismatch rules, so the SCSp's fiscal transparency is respected unless a valid election to be taxable is made. For VAT, the SCSp may independently register as a taxable person even though it is transparent for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for non-resident suppliers making supplies in Luxembourg; any taxable transaction triggers VAT registration. Domestic small business exemption: EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) applies only to established suppliers; cross-border and warehouse fulfillment activities are outside the small business exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU (export) and (b) certain internationally linked services (e.g., transport and ancillary services directly connected to exports). For the cross-dock / overseas 3PL model, physical export from a Luxembourg warehouse to a non-EU buyer must be evidenced by export documentation. The exporter must hold this evidence within 90 days (or longer if justified) and must reflect the export in the periodic VAT return. Services physically performed outside the EU or directly related to exported goods qualify for zero-rating if the recipient is outside the EU or the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A / Electronic Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR / rail consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (evidence of payment received from the foreign buyer)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of transport from Luxembourg to non-EU destination (carrier's certificate, GPS tracking, or customs stamped manifest)
  • Warehouse issuance / release order showing the goods left the bonded or VAT warehouse stock

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping exists in Luxembourg for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is optional for B2B and mandatory only for B2G supplies to central government entities (PEPPOL / CIUS format). Luxembourg has committed to EU ViDA, so future e-invoicing and digital reporting mandates may apply from 2030. Until then, taxpayers must issue electronic invoices in any format and ensure full archiving and transmission to Luxembourg 'eCDF' where applicable.

The Luxembourg VAT authorities may disregard a claimed zero-rating if the export proof is incomplete, the goods did not physically leave the EU, or the payment is not received within the contractual terms. In such cases, VAT is clawed back at the standard rate (17%) from the supplier plus default interest from the end of the applicable VAT return period. Luxembourg provides a five-year limitation period for VAT assessments (ten years where fraud or under-declaration is established). Administrative fines for wrongful zero-rating range from EUR 250 to the total tax evaded, and criminal penalties up to double the tax due and/or imprisonment may apply where fraudulent intent is proven, under the Luxembourg General Tax Code (AO) and VAT law (Loi TVA).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the SCSp level because the SCSp is fully tax-transparent; a Luxembourg taxable operating company is otherwise subject to an aggregate statutory rate of 24.94% (17% corporate income tax + 7% unemployment fund surcharge + municipal business tax at Luxembourg City rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% for dividends paid by a Luxembourg taxable company to shareholders. An SCSp is tax-transparent, and profit withdrawals by its partners are not legally classified as dividends; therefore no Luxembourg WHT is imposed on such distributions. A corporate partner may in any case rely on the Luxembourg participation exemption and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive to obtain a 0% rate where it holds at least 10% of the subsidiary's capital for 12 continuous months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Calendar-year returns are due no later than 31 May of the following year; e-filed declarations are generally automatically extended to 31 December where the taxpayer files through MyGuichet. Non-calendar-year partnerships file within the period announced by the tax office, typically by the sixth month after the close of the accounting period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Luxembourg Local File threshold applies to a pure pass-through SCSp; for a taxable Luxembourg entity performing 3PL functions, the tax administration follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and generally expects contemporaneous documentation where related-party cross-border transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per annum, or where the entity is a routine contract logistics provider. Master File and Country-by-Country reporting are mandatory where the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to offshore regimes, but the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, the MLI Principal Purpose Test, and Luxembourg case-law 'abus de droit' require genuine activity and business purpose. A cross-dock/overseas 3PL with actual employees, warehouse space, inventory management, contractual trade terms, and profit-and-loss accountability demonstrates sufficient substance; an SCSp without any operations or decision-making functions is challengeable and may be denied treaty and participation relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion - intentional under-declaration of SCSp allocable profits

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the entire taxable income, 10% late-payment surcharge, monthly interest of 0.6%, administrative fines up to EUR 250,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Luxembourg General Tax Law where intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, civil restitution, and monetary penalties. The corporate veil can be pierced where the SCSp is found to be a sham, leading to personal asset freezing, exit/travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell company / artificial arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, retroactive tax assessments, denial of deduction of intercompany charges, and re-characterisation of the arrangement under the abuse-of-law doctrine; administrative fines and criminal liability may follow.

Director personal liability: A general partner or corporate director may be personally assessed for taxes due when SCSp assets are insufficient, and directors can be held liable for fraudulent misrepresentation, subject to disqualification orders and criminal penalties for falsification of statutory accounts.
AML / money-laundering through 3PL cross-docking

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML fines for legal entities reach up to EUR 5 million or 10% of total annual turnover; criminal money-laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 250,000, increased to 10 years' imprisonment for aggravated offences.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and directors can be personally fined, banned from managing Luxembourg entities, and criminally prosecuted. Funds and assets involved are subject to confiscation, and travel restrictions may be imposed during investigation.
Failure to document transfer pricing and maintain substance

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment, recapture of all claimed deductions, gross-up of payments, 10% surcharge, monthly late interest, and administrative fines for deficient records. Where VAT registration is also required and omitted, backdated assessments and VAT penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for at least 5% of unpaid corporate taxes where accounting records are absent, incomplete, or falsified, and for monetary compensation orders in action civile claims; in serious cases, criminal sanctions including imprisonment apply.

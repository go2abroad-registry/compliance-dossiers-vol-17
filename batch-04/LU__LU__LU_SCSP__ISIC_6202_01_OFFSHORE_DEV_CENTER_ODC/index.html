Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), accessible via the eJustice.lu electronic filing portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended by the Law of 12 July 2013, Articles 901-1 to 901-16 governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); implementing Grand-Ducal regulations on the Luxembourg Business Registers and electronic filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Duly signed limited partnership agreement (contrat de société) and registration declaration (déclaration de constitution) filed electronically with the RCS.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal capital is prescribed for an SCSp. Limited partners are not subject to a mandatory paid-in amount, and no escrow account is required by law. Contributions are contractually fixed in the partnership agreement; opening a bank account may impose separate commercial funding conditions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local commune fire authority, coordinated with the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS); occupancy permit and structural load clearance for office use; occupational health and safety registration with the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) once employees are engaged.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes, and the Customs and Excise Agency (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT; primary digital portal: MyGuichet.lu (Guichet.lu) for all business tax e-filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg presence must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the single RCS e-filing to obtain the national business identification number (matricule). For a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp), the GP or designated partner submits the incorporation deed and the standard registration forms on MyGuichet.lu. After RCS issuance, the entity obtains its VAT registration number from the AEDT by filing form 010/011 or via the e-space. A SCSp with no economic substance may not require a permanent establishment tax account, but shall obtain a standing tax file reference. Non-resident partners must each register for Luxembourg tax purposes if taxable income or a Luxembourg permanent establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for direct tax purposes; partners are taxed on their distributive share. There is no general statutory election to treat a SCSp as opaque for corporate income tax, but the Luxembourg tax administration accepts certain tailor-made tax opinions or rulings for transparency confirmation. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (Law of 13 July 2021) apply and may reclassify payments to avoid double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (exclusive of VAT) for Luxembourg-established small taxpayers under the VAT lump-sum/small-business exemption; EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for cross-border distance sales of goods and certain B2C electronical supplies; non-established suppliers are generally subject to compulsory VAT registration on first taxable supply unless covered by the EU OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Luxembourg VAT applies to goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU by the supplier or on behalf of the customer, provided the goods leave the EU within statutory time limits. For offshore services such as software development and maintenance, export zero-rating or 'no place of supply in Luxembourg' treatment applies only if the B2B service is supplied to a business established in a third-country and is not physically performed in Luxembourg; the supplier must hold valid VAT number status evidence or other reasonable commercial information. The 0% rate is conditional on the exporter being able to demonstrate the physical movement out of the EU and the direct link between the supply and the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (D.A.E. / SAD export declaration with approved exporter status or ExA)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note or other transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg and arrival in a third country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement or SWIFT confirmation proving settlement of the exported supply consideration within the payment term

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for B2B invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only in the Business-to-Government (B2G) segment, using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format via the MyGuichet.lu platform. VAT returns are mandatory filed electronically using the structured eCDF file schema. No API-based fiscal stamping or pre-clearance is imposed as of 2025.

The Luxembourg VAT administration conducts ex-post substance reviews on export zero-rating evidence, requiring the complete export file to be retained for at least 10 years. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated zero-rating claims trigger immediate recovery of the VAT not paid, penalty interest of 0.75% per month, fines up to the amount of tax evaded, and criminal penalties in cases of serious tax fraud under Article 58 of the General Tax Law. Repatriation deadlines are monitored; late foreign exchange receipts without valid commercial justification may lead to deemed export refund denial and clawback of input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account under EU free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the Grand-Ducal Repeal of Exchange Control Regulations (1963 system abolished). No prior authorisation, licensing, or BCL approval is required for inward capital contributions, loan drawings, trade payments, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance friction is minimal: the SCSp is fiscally transparent, so distributions to non-resident partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, provided the partner is the effective beneficial owner and the income is not Luxembourg-source business profit attributable to a Luxembourg PE. CRS/FATCA reporting does apply; paying agents may request beneficial ownership and tax residency forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Luxembourg legislation. Accounts receivable from offshore software development services may be retained freely in EUR or foreign currency, credited to any domestic or foreign account, and converted at any time without reporting approval. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply: Luxembourg resident credit institutions report individual cross-border transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 to the BCL under the ECB/BCL BOP framework; the SCSp itself has no direct filing duty.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) operating within the Eurosystem; prudential regulation and AML supervision by Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); monetary policy and foreign exchange framework delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) pursuant to TFEU Title IV and the ESCB Statute.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Wise Business and Payoneer are usable for small contract proceeds and sub-contractor payouts, but neither reliably supports MT103 inbound capital calls, SAP-style purpose-code formatting, or commercial LC instruments. Legally, a UK or EU-authorised EMI may onboard a Luxembourg SCSp under passporting, yet most EMIs lack the CDD framework to verify a partnership with non-resident partners and may reject the file as opaque-for-AML. Where accepted, monthly volume caps and limited routing options make the EMI channel supplementary only; the primary operating account should remain at a Tier 1 Luxembourg credit institution.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Luxembourg headline CIT is 17%, plus a 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax averaging 6.75%, producing an aggregate standard rate of approximately 24.94% for corporate partners. The SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and is not subject to Luxembourg CIT at entity level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax for dividends distributed by Luxembourg resident companies. A transparent SCSp's profit allocations are not dividends and are not subject to this 15% withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year entities, the ordinary annual filing deadline is 31 March following the tax year; the e-filing portal automatically extends this to 31 December. For non-calendar fiscal years, the return is due no later than the last day of the fifth month after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 for annual related-party service transactions and EUR 1,000,000 for related-party goods transactions trigger mandatory Luxembourg Local File preparation. The Master File is required only where the worldwide group's consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750 million. Even below these thresholds, the ODC should maintain contemporaneous TP documentation because of the low-risk service-provider profile.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain sector-specific Economic Substance Regulations named as such, but economic substance is enforced through the Luxembourg GAAR, abuse-of-law jurisprudence, EU ATAD, and BEPS anti-abuse standards. The SCSp must demonstrate real local substance: physical office, Luxembourg-based decision-makers, locally contracted employees, local bank account, and actual performance of software engineering oversight. A pass-through invoicing vehicle with no local operational control will be recharacterized as a shell and denied tax treaty and EU directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all concealed profits with additional tax, administrative fraud penalties of up to 50% of the understated tax, fines up to EUR 25,000, and imprisonment up to five years for criminal tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers who participate in concealment are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties. Courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, civil restitution, disqualification from management, and imprisonment where bad faith is proven.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may disregard the SCSp as an abuse of law, reallocate income to the actual place of management, deny treaty and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits, and apply GAAR penalties up to 20% of the recharacterized benefit.

Director personal liability: Signing false substance declarations exposes directors to civil liability to investors and creditors, veil-piercing claims, forfeiture of management rights, and criminal forgery sanctions. Under evolving EU anti-shell rules, the resident director can become personally liable for the difference between substance and turnover.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing Luxembourg taxable income to the arm's-length amount, late interest at 0.8% per month, penalties up to 20% of the adjustment in serious cases, and separate fines for incomplete Master/Local File documentation.

Director personal liability: Officers who deliberately set non-arm's-length prices to strip Luxembourg profits are personally exposed to restitution of the extra taxes assessed on the partners and to disqualification orders. The tax administration may treat the arrangement as criminal fraud where the pricing structure is merely a sham.
AML / Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: CSSF AML fines can reach EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for severe violations; criminal money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 1.25 million, increased for continuous or habitual laundering.

Director personal liability: Managers knowingly operating payment structures designed to obscure beneficial ownership face personal criminal liability, asset freezes, travel bans, extradition proceedings, and imprisonment. The corporate veil is lifted because the SCSp is a façade for illegal financial flows.

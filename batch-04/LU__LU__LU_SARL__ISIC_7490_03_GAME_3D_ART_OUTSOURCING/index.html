Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed and fully paid share capital of EUR 12,000 (Article 180-2 of the Companies Law); cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL en formation with a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution, and the bank's blockage certificate is remitted to the notary; contributions in kind require a valuation report by a réviseur d'entreprises agréé (statutory auditor); the company must also build a legal reserve of 5% of annual net profits until the reserve equals 10% of the subscribed capital.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) - the Luxembourg Inland Revenue - for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, solidarity surcharge and withholding taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - for VAT and registration duties. Principal e-portals: MyGuichet.lu (national e-administration gateway), impotsdirects.public.lu (ACD secure filing), the AEDT eTVA portal for VAT returns, and the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) for corporate registration data.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL obtains its tax identity through the notarial incorporation flow: (1) the civil-law notary deposits the incorporation deed and statutory documents with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which assigns the 13-digit national identification number (matricule / RCS number), publishes the legal notice, and transmits the registration file to the Administration des Contributions Directes; (2) the ACD automatically creates the resident company's direct-tax dossier (numéro de dossier) - no separate TIN application is required for a Luxembourg-resident SARL, and all direct-tax correspondence thereafter uses the matricule as the domestic TIN; (3) for VAT, the SARL must file Form 640 (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu or in writing, disclosing business activity codes (ISIC 7490 professional B2B services), estimated annual turnover and bank details, after which the AEDT issues the LU VAT number and determines the eTVA filing cycle (monthly or quarterly); (4) a non-established foreign service supplier (one without a Luxembourg registered office) must additionally appoint a tax representative approved by the AEDT before obtaining VAT registration. Normal processing time is 2 to 4 weeks; no filing fee is charged for TIN or VAT-number issuance, although notary and RCS registration costs apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg law; every Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) is necessarily an opaque, entity-level-taxed corporate taxpayer. Available election-type mechanisms include: (i) advance tax ruling (agrément fiscal) from the ACD under coordinated circular L.I.R. 29/2014, which can confirm the participation exemption (Art. 166 L.I.R.), the SPF (Société de Participations Financières) regime (Art. 166bis L.I.R.), or the arm's-length character of cross-border cost-plus outsourcing arrangements; (ii) optional fiscal unity (intégration fiscale, Art. 164bis L.I.R.) allowing a Luxembourg parent and its 95%-or-more-owned Luxembourg subsidiaries to consolidate for corporate income tax and municipal business tax, requiring a joint election within the statutory deadline; (iii) election of the IP box regime (Art. 50ter L.I.R.) giving an 80% exemption of net qualifying intellectual-property income; and (iv) in VAT, election between national AEDT registration and the EU OSS regime for qualifying cross-border B2C supplies. ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid provisions apply mandatorily and cannot be disapprovided; they deny deductions where a foreign jurisdiction classifies the SARL as transparent while Luxembourg treats it as opaque.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg on both current and capital account pursuant to Article 63 TFEU and the dismantling of all Luxembourg exchange-control mechanism by the Grand-Ducal Decree of 1995. No prior administrative authorisation (e.g., BCL or Ministry of Finance approval) is required for opening foreign-currency accounts, borrowing abroad, effecting external payments, or remitting profits. Dividend repatriation is subject only to the standard 15% domestic withholding tax (reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, Luxembourg double-tax treaties, or the participation-exemption regime if the shareholder is an EU/associated company), with no FX or capital-control impediment. Anti-money-laundering and economic-sanctions screening are the only de facto constraints on payment flows, and the BCL requires statistical BOP reporting for non-resident flows above threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes zero statutory surrender ratios, zero mandatory conversion timelines, and no obligation to repatriate export proceeds to any designated account. Hard-currency receivables from non-resident 3D art clients may be retained indefinitely in either EUR or foreign-currency bank accounts wherever the bank permits. The only administrative duty is the statistical declaration of non-resident payments to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, which the onboarding bank performs automatically on the basis of transaction purpose codes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and responsible for balance-of-payments statistics and payment system oversight; prudential supervision of credit institutions and EMIs is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 5 April 1993.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium. A CSSF-licensed or EEA-passported e-money institution (EMI) or payment institution (PI) can legally provide a Luxembourg IBAN, inbound payment collection, and corporate cards, which is adequate for a receivables float. However, EMIs cannot provide primary treasury services such as EUR/USD FX credit lines, commercial letters of credit, short-term lending, or cash pooling; they are also exposed to lower counterparty treatment for settlement of large milestone invoices. An offshore (third-country) EMI has no standalone legal standing to operate a payment account for a Luxembourg SARL unless it acts through an EU-licensed partner. Recommended usage: secondary collection rail for PayPal-derived flows and micro-contractor payouts, never the sole treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where aggregate cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1.5 million in a fiscal year; Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; below these thresholds a concise arm’s-length pricing policy and intercompany agreement record must nevertheless be contemporaneously available.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Luxembourg does not apply a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but under Art. 56 LIR substance-over-form, the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, the Luxembourg tax residence certificate policy and the 2019 tax-administration circular on factual substance, the SARL must show real economic presence: physical office, resident staff or service providers performing actual 3D-art work, local bank account, board decisions in Luxembourg, and commercial contracts directing risk and functions. Transactional substance is tested through a full functional analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / corporate criminal fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with an extended 10-year tax limitation; penalty surcharge up to 200% of evaded CIT, MBT, and net wealth tax; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Art. 153 LIR and Art. 402 of the Criminal Code; monthly late-payment interest at 0.6% on evaded amounts

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly participate in concealment are subject to piercing of the corporate veil: joint and several civil restitution of the evaded taxes, travel bans, seizure of personal bank accounts, disqualification from directing a Luxembourg company for up to 10 years, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell company / artificial tax structuring

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Luxembourg tax residence certificate; refusal of treaty benefits and EU directive exemptions; complete disregard of the entity under substance-over-form; reallocation of income to the true beneficial owner; penalty of 100% on under-assessed tax; interest and compulsory liquidation exposure

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for all taxes re-assessed after the entity is disregarded; travel bans and asset freezes are issued in international fraud investigations; imprisonment for up to 3 years where the artificial structure is used to evade tax or money launder.
Transfer pricing / hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Art. 56bis LIR reallocating the taxable income to the arm’s-length standard; compensating assessment against the SARL; equalization of hidden distributions; 100% penalty for intentional mispricing; monthly interest; deemed dividend withholding tax of 15% on the adjusted excess

Director personal liability: Directors who personally approve artificial pricing or sign inflated invoices become jointly liable for the company’s tax deficiencies; the corporate veil is pierced where the shareholder and director are identical; civil restitution, prohibition from management, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for up to 3 years in case of fraud.
AML / cross-border payment misuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 per breach under the amended Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT; confiscation of funds; criminal money-laundering sanctions including fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; revocation of professional authorisations; inclusion in regulatory blacklists

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failures in the AML/CFT compliance function of the SARL; they face civil damages, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, criminal imprisonment up to 5 years, and permanent disqualification from holding office in Luxembourg financial or commercial enterprises.

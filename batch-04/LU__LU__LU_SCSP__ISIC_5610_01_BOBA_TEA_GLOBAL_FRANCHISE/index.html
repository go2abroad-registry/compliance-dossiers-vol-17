Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; coordinated through the Luxembourg single e-government portal MyGuichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

E-government filing portal

Procedure for foreign entities: (i) Apply for an RCS registration via MyGuichet.lu using the coordinated e-registration form; the RCS issues a national business number (matricule) that constitutes the statutory Luxembourg tax identification number (TIN) for the SCSp. (ii) For VAT, file a VAT registration declaration (formulaire 106/DC) with the AEDT, either on paper or via MyGuichet.lu, at least 15 calendar days before commencing taxable supplies. Non-established foreign entities are required to register directly with the AEDT regardless of turnover level (no threshold); a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the format 'LU' followed by 8 digits is issued together with a certificate of taxable status (attestation d'assujettissement). (iii) Foreign partners of a fiscally transparent SCSp without a permanent establishment in Luxembourg do not require a Luxembourg TIN for income tax purposes if their Luxembourg-source income is limited, but must supply their home-jurisdiction TINs to the SCSp for exchange-of-information and ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is a partnership without legal personality and is by default fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax and net wealth tax; profits, gains and losses flow through to its partners in proportion to their capital interests and are taxable only at partner level.

Available election forms

Opaqueness election (corporate check-the-box-style): Under Article 5 of the Law of 23 December 2016, an SCSp may irrevocably elect to be treated as a corporate taxpayer subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax and net wealth tax if (i) 100% of its partnership interests are held by Luxembourg corporate taxpayers that are fully subject to corporate income tax and net wealth tax, or (ii) its partnership interests are listed on a regulated exchange. The election is binding and applies from the start of the fiscal year. Hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments are governed by the ATAD 2 implementation (Law of 20 December 2019), under which the Luxembourg tax administration may re-characterize the SCSp's transparency or an investor's tax treatment where structured arrangements create double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes with a non-EU jurisdiction, and in such cases the taxpayer may elect the appropriate offsetting/mirror adjustment mechanism subject to advance tax-ruling confirmation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small businesses eligible for the small-business exemption; EUR 10,000 annual EU distance-selling and electronically-supplied-services threshold for EU-established suppliers; NO registration threshold for non-established (foreign) taxable persons - any taxable supply made in Luxembourg (including intra-Community acquisitions, imports, and deemed supplies of goods or services) triggers mandatory VAT registration unless reverse charge, import VAT deferral, or OSS/IOSS schemes apply. For the SCSp franchise business, registration is therefore driven by the existence of a Luxembourg taxable supply (e.g., domestic syrup sales to Luxembourg franchisees), not by a turnover ceiling.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the SCSp or on its behalf from the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg to a non-EU destination qualify for the 0% export exemption under Article 42(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the exporter holds contemporaneous documentary proof of removal from the EU within the required timeframe and the goods are dispatched/transported outside the EU. Franchise and royalty services (boba-tea brand licence, know-how, supply-chain advisory) sold by the SCSp to non-resident franchisees are business-to-business services; under the general B2B place-of-supply rule they are taxable in the customer's jurisdiction (reverse charge) and therefore fall outside Luxembourg VAT - no Luxembourg VAT is chargeable and no refund claim arises on those fees. Exported syrups, toppings, packaging materials and equipment sold to non-EU franchisees qualify for the zero rate; exports to EU franchisees are intra-Community supplies, zero-rated only with a valid EU customer VAT number and a correct EC sales listing.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / ATLAS AES export declaration) with customs office of export and exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing dispatch from the EU to a destination outside the EU
  • CMR road consignment note or other export transport document establishing removal from the EU territory
  • Commercial invoice referencing the export declaration, including HS codes, quantities, unit values and Incoterms
  • Proof of payment / bank transfer instruction or foreign inward remittance certificate confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Franchise contract, delivery notes, and warehouse/picking records evidencing the commercial substance of the export transaction

Luxembourg does not currently impose a mandatory B2B electronic-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. E-invoicing is accepted in structured formats (Factur-X/UBL, PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0, EDI) and must evidence authenticity of origin and integrity of content; paper invoices remain lawful. B2G invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities is mandatory in the European standard EN 16931 structured format through the PEPPOL network. No digital stamping, tax-authority clearance API, or invoice pre-validation is required for VAT purposes; however, recapitulative statements (Intrastat/EC sales listings), transaction-level reporting for e-commerce (OSS/IOSS), and the obligation to retain invoices and export evidence for at least 10 years apply.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may revisit export zero-rating in post-clearance audits; where the required export documentation is absent, incomplete or contradictory, the 0% exemption is clawed back and VAT becomes payable at 17% together with default interest of 0.8% per month (increased to 1% per month in cases of fraud or deliberate intent) from the date the tax would have fallen due. Unjustified or repeated zero-rating claims expose the SCSp to a 50% fraud surcharge (majoration d'impôt) on the evaded VAT, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), with penalties up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment ranging from 8 days to 5 years. Additional consequences include disqualification of the responsible manager, revocation or suspension of the statutory franchise/business licence, inclusion in the AEDT's public control list, and, for hybrid or conduit arrangements, recharacterization under ATAD 2 resulting in further disallowed deductions and joint liability of partners.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible within the eurozone; Luxembourg imposes no exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Part XI of the Luxembourg General Law on money and finance. Capital contributions, dividend and profit distributions, royalty payments, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted freely to any jurisdiction, subject only to EU/UN sanctions screening, AML/CFT verification, and the obligation of resident entities to report cross-border flows to the BCL for statistical purposes. The key practical hurdle is not currency convertibility but the transparent tax classification of the SCSp, which is a partnership without separate legal personality; profit attribution follows the partnership agreement, and no Luxembourg withholding tax applies on distributions to non-resident partners provided the partnership is not fiscally opaque and the partners are not Luxembourg residents engaged in a taxable permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Luxembourg or in the eurosystem legal framework. Export receipts in foreign currency (e.g., USD, CNY, TWD) may be retained in foreign currency accounts, converted to EUR at freely negotiated market rates, or used directly for settlement of trade payables, provided that the underlying transactions are economically substantiated and properly documented for BCL balance-of-payments reporting and CSSF AML supervision.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in conjunction with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the BCL administers balance-of-payments statistics and payment system liquidity, while the CSSF supervises domestic credit institutions, AML/CFT conduct, and the registration of financial sector participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with material legal constraints. A Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution (PI) authorised by the CSSF is acceptable for non-core collection of franchise royalty inflows, but the SCSp, having no separate legal personality, must open the account through its general partner; fund safeguarding rules require the EMI to hold client funds in segregated accounts, which can accommodate the SCSp but adds an intermediation layer. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK or Baltic-licensed) are viable only under the PSD2 grandfathering and passporting rules; post-Brexit UK EMIs cannot passport into Luxembourg, and a non-EEA EMI would require an EU-authorised agent or principal model, which is operationally burdensome and raises second-tier AML scrutiny. Given the franchise's cross-border royalty flow and ISIC 5610 business scope, a tier-1 domestic bank remains the default treasury anchor; a Luxembourg EMI may be used solely as a low-liquidity, high-throughput collection arm.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Verification includes the submission of the board resolution or general partner resolution authorising the capital call, the capital call notice evidencing the amount and due date, and the signed subscription schedule; the receiving bank will perform a beneficiary identity match against the KYC file and may seek confirmation from the remitting bank (MT199) where the purpose code is inconsistent or where the remitter is not an established EEA financial institution or a pre-listed shareholder.Trade settlements are executed primarily through the euro payment system (TARGET2 and TIPS) for intra-EEA supplier payments, and via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border conventional wire transfers; for irrevocable trade commitments, a commercial documentary letter of credit governed by UCP 600 is issued by the bank (confirmation advised for Chinese and Taiwanese syrup suppliers), with remittance via MT202 COV when intermediary banks are required. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Aggregate statutory rate for fully taxable entities in Luxembourg City: 24.94% (corporate income tax 17% plus solidarity surcharge 7% of the CIT plus municipal business tax 6.75%). The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: the entity itself bears no CIT, and income is taxed at partner level; the 24.94% headline rate is relevant only if the SCSp validly elects opaque CIT treatment or where a partner is a Luxembourg-resident corporate entity.

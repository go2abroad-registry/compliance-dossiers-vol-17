Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), R.C.S. Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (particularly Articles 1, 14, 15-1 and Title IV concerning special limited partnerships); Grand-Ducal Regulation of 20 May 2010 on the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS).

Formation / constitutional filings

Written partnership deed (contrat de société) and declaration of registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers; filing is made electronically via the LBR eDesk portal, followed by publication in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; contributions are freely determined by the partnership agreement. No escrow account or capital blocking requirement applies to an SCSp.

Funding & unlock path: No civil defense or occupational clearance applies for a purely digital platform. If the SCSp opens commercial offices or warehousing, the employer must submit a workplace notice to the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) and obtain a fire safety clearance from the Compagnie Grand-Ducale des Sapeurs-Pompiers (CGDIS) prior to occupancy.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; contributions are freely determined by the partnership agreement. No escrow account or capital blocking requirement applies to an SCSp.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
Pro-forma first resolution of the founding general partner of the SCSp: RESOLVED that the SCSp is established under the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915, as amended; that the partnership deed is approved and adopted; that the capital contributions of the limited partners are accepted with no statutory minimum; that the registered office is fixed at [address], Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; that [name] is appointed general partner and [name] is appointed manager for registration and administration; that all filings before the Luxembourg Business Registers and publication in RESA are authorized; that bank account opening is authorized; and that all costs incurred in the formation are ratified and payable by the partnership.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

N/A – no industrial license required for an electronic B2B wholesale marketplace operator under Luxembourg law; such operators are not considered industrial establishments.

Category: N/A – the business scope is ISIC 4791 / NAICS 425120 (electronic wholesale platform); no operating or manufacturing permit applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes (portal: https://impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (portal: https://aedt.public.lu); central business e-governance portal: https://guichet.lu; Luxembourg Business Registers portal: https://www.lbr.lu

E-government filing portal

For an SCSp formed in Luxembourg, the general partner must complete RCS registration through the Luxembourg Business Registers; this generates the national business registration number. For direct taxation, the SCSp itself is tax-transparent, so partners are identified through their own Luxembourg or foreign tax IDs; a Luxembourg tax number (NIF) may be obtained from the ACD via ordinary taxpayer registration if any partner is subject to Luxembourg tax. For VAT, if the SCSp is considered a taxable person carrying out an economic activity in its own name, a VAT registration application must be filed electronically with the AEDT through the MyGuichet / eTVA portal. Non-established foreign entities engaged in taxable Luxembourg B2B marketplace sales must register directly with the AEDT and appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative where required; the application must provide RCS extracts, identity of the general partner, proof of activity, and expected turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory default; no US-style check-the-box election exists. No election may be made to treat the SCSp as a taxable corporation for Luxembourg direct-tax purposes. Anti-hybrid rules under EU/ATAD II may re-characterise the entity or its partners in cross-border situations. For VAT, the SCSp may optionally join a Luxembourg VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') where legally established entities forming together a single taxable person so elect under the approval requirements of the AEDT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established Luxembourg taxable persons; non-resident suppliers have no threshold and must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg unless an alternative rule (e.g., reverse charge, OSS/IOSS) applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for supplies of goods physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods leave the EU and the exporter retains satisfactory proof. Zero rating also applies to intra-Community supplies to a registered EU customer. For services, zero rating or exclusion from Luxembourg VAT applies where the place of supply is outside the EU under Articles 44-48 of the Luxembourg VAT law (e.g., B2B services to a non-EU taxable person), or where services are directly connected with exported goods and satisfy documentary conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / ECS export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document such as CMR consignment note or Air Waybill)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (where applicable, evidencing foreign currency receipt and value transfer)
  • Proof of physical departure of goods from EU territory (e.g., customs stamp or electronic exit record)

Luxembourg does not impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for B2B e-invoicing. E-invoices are legally accepted provided they preserve authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility. B2G e-invoicing is handled through the PEPPOL network where mandated. There is no general API-based real-time VAT reporting obligation for B2B marketplace operators at present.

The AEDT may scrutinise export zero-rating claims retroactively and require the complete evidentiary bundle above within the statutory retention period. Missing, false, or fraudulent export documentation exposes the taxpayer to immediate VAT reassessment at 17%, acceleration of due VAT, statutory late-interest, and administrative fines ranging from 10% to 50% of the evaded VAT, plus criminal penalties in cases of intentional fraud. Refunds of input VAT linked to improperly zero-rated exports are clawed back with interest; repeat violations may also lead to loss of VAT group privileges and public procurement disqualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible, and Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current-account transactions, capital-account movements, profit remittances, partnership distributions, liquidation proceeds or service fees. Free movement of capital with third countries is guaranteed under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, subject only to EU sanctions and prudential safeguards. For the Luxembourg SCSp, which is fiscally transparent, foreign partners are taxed directly at their residence level; no Luxembourg withholding tax applies to distributions of profits or to liquidation proceeds from the SCSp except in limited cases where the partnership is treated as opaque. Consequently, there is no regulatory currency-conversion bottleneck for treasury activity; the only residual obligations are statutory bank reporting to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes and customer due diligence under CSSF regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg statutory law does not impose any deadline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into euro, and no obligation to surrender foreign currency earned from cross-border B2B marketplace sales. The SCSp may lawfully hold USD, CNY, EUR or any other settlement currency in its multi-currency operational accounts, and the decision to convert proceeds is a treasury optimization decision rather than a regulatory requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank of the euro area and supervisory counterparty for balance-of-payments statistics; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent national authority for banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision and payment-institution oversight; the European Central Bank (ECB) holds ultimate monetary and exchange-rate policy authority for the euro.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High. A Luxembourg-licensed or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institution may lawfully provide collection, payout and multi-currency treasury services to the SCSp under the PSD2 legal framework, provided client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts at an EU authorized credit institution. Offshore digital EMIs not authorized in the EEA cannot act as the primary payment account provider for a Luxembourg resident merchant without breaching Luxembourg reserved-payment-activity rules. The recommended architecture is therefore a CSSF-supervised commercial bank as the primary settlement bank, with the fintech EMI or Alibaba Pay treasury gateway used as a supplementary collection and supplier-disbursement layer, subject to contractual safeguarding and audit rights.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investor capital contributions shall be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the SCSp capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance instruction must reference the SCSp RCS registration number, the capital-call letter reference, and the payment narrative 'contribution to partnership capital of a Luxembourg special limited partnership'. The receiving bank will classify the credit for BCL balance-of-payments statistics under the BPM6 direct-investment equity category (FA.5); no central-bank authorization is required. Marketplace-treasury inflows from the Alibaba Pay settlement engine, representing B2B platform sales or commission revenue, shall be remitted to the SCSp operational account with the payment narrative identifying the platform settlement statement and the service-income nature of the transaction; the bank will record these as service receipts under the current account of the balance of payments with the underlying invoice and order-level settlement reconciliation attached to the credit file.Supplier disbursements within the EU shall be settled through SEPA Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 pain.001 messages across TARGET2 or STEP2 clearing, with SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for time-critical payments. For third-country suppliers, particularly merchandise suppliers in China, the United States or Hong Kong, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer directly to the supplier's account, while larger-value transactions may be structured through a documentary commercial letter of credit issued under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700 and settled through the issuing bank's trade finance desk. The B2B marketplace payout obligation to Alibaba Pay or its acquiring treasury should be executed via the bank's SWIFT gateway, with each payout carrying the order identifier, invoice number and platform reconciliation code. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17.00% national corporate income tax; plus 7.00% unemployment-fund solidarity surcharge on CIT; plus municipal business tax (Luxembourg City standard rate 6.75%); aggregate headline Luxembourg City rate equals 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15.00% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident non-transparent company. For a transparent SCSp, distributions to partners are generally characterized as profit withdrawals or capital distributions and are not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax. If the SCSp is corporate-taxed or a distribution is recharacterized as a deemed dividend under anti-abuse rules, the statutory 15.00% rate applies subject to DTA relief.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Tax year is normally the calendar year; the annual return is due by 31 May of the following year. The tax administration may grant extensions; electronically filed returns are generally expected before the extended administrative deadline of 31 December where extension is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Reporting and Master File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. Luxembourg does not publish a numeric Local File threshold, but any material cross-border controlled transaction should be documented. In practice, the tax administration requests Local File documentation within 30 days for transactions exceeding generally accepted materiality levels, including intercompany management fees, platform service fees, and goods procurement flows.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the substance doctrine is embedded in domestic abuse-of-law rules, OECD BEPS PPT principles, EU ATAD, and administrative practice. An SCSp must maintain a Luxembourg registered office, local directors or managers, real decision-making, employees, adequate capital, a bank account, commercial contracts, and transfer pricing documentation. A shell SCSp used only as a tax-transparent invoice conduit for a foreign B2B platform will be disregarded or recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Default assessment of the full taxable base, 0.6% monthly late-payment interest, administrative fines up to EUR 5,000, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud carrying fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Courts may order confiscation of assets equal to the evaded tax and damages.

Director personal liability: The general partner, manager, or director engaged in the fraudulent scheme may be held jointly and severally liable for the SCSp's tax debts under veil-piercing principles. Criminal conviction can result in imprisonment, disqualification from management, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and extradition or red-notice proceedings where flight risk exists.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authority will recharacterize the transactions at arm's length, disallow the non-arm's-length deductions, assess additional CIT, solidarity surcharge, and municipal business tax, and apply late-payment interest. Failure to produce TP documentation on request is independently penalized and may also trigger criminal tax penalties if the manipulation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who sign false TP certificates, prepare fictitious invoices, or knowingly execute artificial arrangements can be personally liable for restitution of the tax loss and may be prosecuted as accomplices to tax evasion. Directorial debarment and travel restrictions may accompany criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Abusive Tax Transparency Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Substance-over-form recharacterization; denial of tax transparency treatment; reallocation of profits to the place of effective management; denial of DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; registration may be struck out by the Luxembourg trade register; tax debts are reassessed with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Nominee managers, general partners, and beneficial owners who operate the SCSp as a sham may have the corporate veil pierced for the full reassessed tax liability. Courts may issue director disqualification orders, asset freezes, travel bans, and criminal penalties for abuse of law where fraudulent intent is shown.
AML / Money Laundering / Unauthorized Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Enforcement by the CSSF and the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit. Administrative fines may reach up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money laundering penalties include fines up to EUR 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. The court may order asset freezing, confiscation, and disgorgement of profits.

Director personal liability: Executive directors and the general partner face personal criminal liability for AML failures, private damages claims, attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment. Regulators may also impose temporary or permanent bans from managing financial or commercial entities.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS/LBR)

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended by the Law of 19 December 2016; Sàrl provisions under Articles 100–124; registration governed by the Law of 19 December 2016 on the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) and coordinated articles of association (statuts coordonnés), filed electronically with the RCS through the LBR/Guichet.lu portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for an Sàrl is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid-up upon incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited in an escrow account held by a Luxembourg notary or a Luxembourg credit institution and remain blocked until RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a certified auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for the commercial premises; structural load attestation from the municipal building inspection; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) and affiliation with an approved external occupational health and safety service.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD — Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified online portal MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu) for company registration, RCS incorporation, and tax ID activation; AEDT e-space (espace AEDT / MyGuichet) for VAT registration and filings.

E-government filing portal

A SARL is incorporated via a notary filing with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu, which automatically allocates (i) a national business registration number (numéro de matricule / RCS number L-prefix) used as the corporate tax TIN (NIF), and (ii) triggers a separate VAT identification number application to the AEDT. Non-resident foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment obtain a Luxembourg direct-tax TIN upon first filing via the ACD and must apply for a VAT number (form 640 T.V.A., available via MyGuichet.lu / AEDT) before making taxable supplies in Luxembourg. No physical presence visit is required; e-desktop or authorized tax representative may complete the procedure. The VAT ID format is 'LU' followed by 8 digits (e.g., LU12345678).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) is an opaque corporation resident in Luxembourg; it is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (communal trade tax / IAC), and net wealth tax on worldwide income/assets, with no statutory pass-through regime under Luxembourg domestic law.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under Luxembourg tax law; SARLs are per se corporate taxpayers. Available elective mechanisms: (1) VAT grouping (assujettissement collectif) election under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (2) Luxembourg fiscal unity / tax consolidation election for direct taxes under Section 164 LIR (limited to resident corporate groups); (3) notional interest deduction (NID) election on equity increases; (4) IP box election (80% exemption for qualifying IP income under Article 50bis LIR); (5) hybrid mismatch self-assessment elections per ATAD implementing law (Law of 21 December 2018), including alignment of the SARL's tax classification with an EU/EEA parent's classification exclusively for hybrid mismatch avoidance; (6) for a single-member SARL, optional U.S. federal check-the-box classification (Form 8832) as disregarded or partnership is available for U.S. purposes because the SARL is not a per se corporation under U.S. Treasury Regulation §301.7701-2(b)(8), but this does not alter Luxembourg domestic tax opacity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for compulsory VAT registration of established taxable persons carrying out any economic activity; however, the small business exemption (Article 57 — régime de la franchise) applies to resident businesses with annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT) for goods and services (threshold raised from EUR 30,000 effective 1 January 2024). For non-resident suppliers, VAT registration in Luxembourg is required when taxable supplies are made in Luxembourg, subject to EU reverse-charge, distance-selling (EU EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold via OSS), and deemed-supplier rules; the EUR 128,000 Luxembourg intra-EU distance selling threshold applies to goods but is superseded by the Union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the seller or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union (export under Article 155 of the EU VAT Directive / Articles 383–386 of the Luxembourg VAT Law), provided the goods physically leave EU territory within the statutory deadlines; and (ii) certain international services linked to immovable property abroad or, under the general B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 29 Luxembourg VAT Law), services supplied to a business customer established outside the EU, which are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT rather than zero-rated — including virtual office, mail scanning, and digital administrative services supplied to non-EU B2B clients. For the CMRA/mail-scanning activity, the core service is electronically supplied and will be out of scope of Luxembourg VAT if the recipient is a taxable person established in a non-EU country, or subject to reverse charge if established in another EU member state. Zero-rating for physical mail/parcel forwarding requires the export of goods evidenced by definitive export documents.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD / DAE) — Luxembourg customs export declaration (via LU-CLS / Descartes) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Commercial invoice and contract evidencing the service description and destination
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) with proof of physical departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank statement evidencing payment received from the foreign customer
  • Proof of identity and VAT status of the foreign recipient (VAT registration certificate or equivalent) for B2B place-of-supply substantiation
  • Audit trail of the digital service delivery (server logs, scan timestamps, mail forwarding receipts) for out-of-scope or zero-rated services
  • Transport documents and customs stamps for goods dispatched by the Luxembourg SARL to non-EU clients (when applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API clearance regime for domestic B2B/B2C invoices in Luxembourg. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only in the B2G segment (suppliers to Luxembourg public authorities must submit PEPPOL / BIS Billing 3.0 format via the national e-invoicing platform (Espace e-Facturation) under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 15 July 2022). B2B electronic invoicing is voluntary and must comply with EU VAT invoicing rules (Articles 218–240 of the EU VAT Directive); e-invoices must guarantee authentic origin, content integrity, and legibility (e.g., EDI or XML with audit trail). Luxembourg is preparing for EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) requirements, with domestic B2B e-invoicing expected to be phased in from 2028 onward (agreement pending at EU level).

Luxembourg customs and the AEDT conduct documented post-export checks (contrôles a posteriori) requiring proof of physical export and payment remittance within the statutory period; failure to substantiate zero-rating or out-of-scope treatment triggers immediate VAT reassessment at 17%, plus default interest (interest at the legal rate, currently 3.13% per annum for tax debts), and a negligence penalty of up to 10% of the tax due; fraudulent zero-rating claims (false customs declarations, fictitious exports, sham foreign establishments) constitute tax fraud (fraude fiscale) punishable by criminal fines up to EUR 250,000, imprisonment of up to 5 years, and retrospective clawback of VAT plus penalties for up to 10 years (statute of limitation; 10-year period applies in cases of fraud). The Luxembourg tax authority may also apply a 100% penalty for deliberate concealment and refer the matter to the Public Prosecutor's Office (Parquet) for prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg applies full capital-account and current-account liberalisation under EU law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66) and OECD instruments. No exchange control authorisation is required for foreign direct investment, loan servicing, trade settlements or profit remittances. The only administrative requirements are statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 and KYC/AML documentation substantiating the underlying economic purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies. Luxembourg and the euro area do not require export proceeds to be converted into euro; exporters may retain receivables in any currency. Receipts must be declared for BCL balance-of-payments statistics, but no timeline or forced sale obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the Luxembourg Ministère des Finances, together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to strict legal conditions. A Luxembourg or other EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 may provide account/IBAN and merchant acquiring services, but higher-risk virtual-office/CMRA business models trigger enhanced due diligence and possible account restrictions. Offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs are not viable for treasury-grade clearing because they lack SEPA/SWIFT direct membership, Luxembourg substance, and regulatory equivalence; use them only as a secondary collection rail, never for fiduciary escrows or tax-relevant flow.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Aggregate headline rate of 24.94% for a Luxembourg SARL tax-resident with management seat in Luxembourg City = national corporate income tax (CIT/IRC) of 17% + solidarity surcharge of 7% on CIT (1.19%) + municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) of 6.75% (Luxembourg City communal multiplier 225%; commune range 6.00%–12.50%). The ICC is deductible in computing the national CIT base, producing an effective aggregate rate of approximately 24.94% pre-deduction and roughly 24.66% after the ICC deduction.

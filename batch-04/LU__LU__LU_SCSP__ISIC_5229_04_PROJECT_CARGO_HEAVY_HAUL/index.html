Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg tax registration is split across three administrations. (1) Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes, address: 33 rue de Gasperich, L-5826 Hesperange, portal: impotsdirects.public.lu. (2) Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties, VAT desk: Bureau d'imposition TVA, 1-3 avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg, portal: aed.public.lu. (3) Administration des Douanes et Accises (ADA) for customs duties and EORI issuance, portal: douanes.public.lu. All statutory filings are electronically routed through the official single State gateway MyGuichet.lu (myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - RCS Trade Register: file the SCSp partnership agreement (contrat de societe; private deed suffices, no notarial act required unless real estate contributes) with the Registre de Commerce et des Societes (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu; RCS registration automatically generates the Luxembourg national tax identification number (matricule / numéro d'identification national) from the Registre National des Personnes Morales (RNM). Step 2 - VAT registration: submit Form 642 (Demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA) to AEDT with the RCS excerpt, partnership agreement, identity of partners and gérant, and bank confirmation; a foreign non-established taxable person may register directly, but must appoint an approved Luxembourg fiscal representative if established outside the EU/EEA; VAT number format: LU + 8 digits. Step 3 - EORI: apply to the Customs Administration (ADA) for a Luxembourg EORI number (format LU + 10 digits), required for every export declaration, obtainable through MyGuichet or the EU customs portal. Step 4 - Employer/social security: if the SCSp hires personnel, register as employer with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) and with ACD for withholding tax. Processing times: RCS 5-10 business days; VAT number 5-15 business days after complete submission; EORI 2-5 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible | Territorial_Scope: an SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct tax because it has no legal personality; income, gains and net wealth are allocated pro-rata to partners and taxed in their hands, and the SCSp itself is not subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (IBC) or net wealth tax (IFI). The general partner (commandité) is taxed on its allocated share as commercial profits (residence-based); non-resident limited partners (commanditaires) are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income, which for an SCSp is limited to Luxembourg real estate, Luxembourg permanent establishment income and certain Luxembourg-source royalties. Tax_Exempt_Zone: no Luxembourg free-zone or special economic zone exemption is available to the SCSp; however, if the SCSp is structured as a reserved alternative investment fund (RAIF/SIF) the transparency mechanism ensures tax neutrality at fund level.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; transparency of the SCSp is mandatory for direct-tax purposes. Available elections: (i) VAT option - under Article 12 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, a transparent partnership conducting an economic activity may register for VAT and exercise input-deduction rights (option pour la TVA), which is binding for at least five years; (ii) VAT grouping - Article 59 of the VAT Law permits financially, economically and organisationally linked entities to elect a single taxable person (assujetti unique); (iii) OSS/IOSS - optional registration for intra-EU distance sales of goods (annual threshold EUR 10,000) and for imports of low-value consignments, so avoiding multiple EU VAT registrations; (iv) hybrid mismatch alignment - Luxembourg's ATAD 2 provisions (LIR Articles 168a and 168b, Section Vbis) automatically deny deductions to the extent a hybrid entity or instrument causes double non-taxation; no elective reverse-hybrid treatment is available at SCSp level, but the SCSp may obtain a Luxembourg certificate of fiscal residence/allocation of income under double tax treaties to evidence transparency to foreign tax authorities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established taxable persons: small business franchise exemption (Article 57 VAT Law) applies up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover (effective 1 January 2023; formerly EUR 30,000). Non-established foreign taxable persons: NO threshold - mandatory VAT registration from the first euro of taxable supplies in Luxembourg. Intra-EU distance sales of goods by Luxembourg sellers: EUR 10,000 annual threshold, above which OSS registration is required. Exports and qualifying international transport services are zero-rated without registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to outside the EU are exempt with deduction (zero-rated) under Article 44(1)(a) of the Luxembourg VAT Law (implementing Article 146 of the VAT Directive). Zero-rating also covers international transport of goods and ancillary services (loading, handling, freight forwarding) under Articles 44(1)(b), 44(1)(c) and 45(1)(e) of the VAT Law. For OOG/project cargo heavy-haul logistics, the 0% rate applies when the transport and handling form part of a single export contract and are evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents; B2B logistics services to a non-EU principal are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under Article 58 of the VAT Law (place of supply - recipient location), while services physically performed in Luxembourg and not directly linked to an export remain taxable at 17%. Conditions to claim zero-rating: (i) goods must physically leave the EU; (ii) dispatch must occur by or on behalf of the supplier/exporter; (iii) the supplier must hold complete documentary proof (validated MRN, transport contract, commercial invoice, arrival evidence); (iv) the documentary proof must be obtained within 90 days of the time of supply (Article 335 of EU Implementing Regulation 282/2011; Luxembourg administrative practice, extension possible only with justified cause).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document filed via DUO/PLDA with validated Movement Reference Number - MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or CMR / Air Waybill (alternative transport evidence per Incoterms; multimodal bills of lading for OOG project cargo accepted)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / MT103 payment confirmation evidencing inward settlement of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and detailed OOG packing list (dimensions, weight, cargo marks and numbers)
  • Proof of arrival at the third-country destination (port warehouse receipt, customs entry stamp at destination, or third-party delivery confirmation)

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is legally equivalent to paper, but mandatory only for B2G supplies to Luxembourg public authorities (PEPPOL / EN 16931 e-inv format). For B2B taxpayers, e-invoicing is voluntary and can be issued through private billing systems; Luxembourg currently imposes no SAF-T obligation. Customs clearance uses EU UCC electronic systems: DUO/PLDA for export declarations and NCTS for transit. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, mandatory B2B e-invoicing and transaction-based digital reporting through an EU standard system is projected to enter into force on 1 July 2030 (goods) and phases thereafter; taxpayers should prepare their ERP/invoicing interfaces for this future API-based reporting mandate.

Luxembourg tax audits (AEDT for VAT/customs, ACD for direct taxes) apply strict documentary scrutiny to zero-rated export transactions under Articles 44 et seq. of the VAT Law and EU Implementing Regulation 282/2011. If the exporter cannot produce the full evidence trail (MRN, transport documents, arrival proof, payment evidence), the supply is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply at 17%, with VAT assessed together with late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (Article 53 VAT Law). Retrospective assessment windows: standard five-year period (Article 82 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law - AO), extended to ten years in cases of fraudulent concealment. Administrative fines under Articles 67-69 of the VAT Law may reach 100% of the tax evaded in bad-faith cases, and criminal tax fraud (fraude fiscale) under Article 396 AO / Article 489 of the Criminal Code carries fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Individual liability may extend to the gérant and general partner, and the EORI number or export licence may be suspended. Mitigation: maintain a complete and timely export file (MRN, B/L or CMR, SWIFT proofs of inward freight settlement), honour the 180-day FX settlement convention used in the project-cargo milestone credit terms (this 180-day deadline is a trade-settlement contract norm, not a hard Luxembourg VAT deadline; the hard VAT proof deadline is 90 days from the time of supply), and document any deviation with a customs-broker justification memorandum to prevent fraud characterisation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the Economic and Monetary Union and has adopted the euro (EUR) as its sole currency. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the convertibility of EUR or other currencies, and no differentiated treatment of current account versus capital account transactions. The EU free movement of capital principle (Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union) applies, meaning that capital contributions, dividends, profit distributions, loan repayments, and trade settlement payments can be freely executed within the EU and between the EU and third jurisdictions subject only to generally applicable anti-money-laundering and sanctions compliance, and to EU-level restrictive measures. Profit remittance hurdles are strictly legal/compliance-based rather than FX-control-based; no central bank approval or general permit is required for profit repatriation by an SCSp, provided the entity is in good standing, the beneficial ownership is transparent, and the underlying funds have been sourced and moved in accordance with AML/KYC standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Luxembourg. Export receipts, including project cargo and heavy haul international transport proceeds, may be retained in their original currency, converted freely, or held in any authorized payment account. There are no mandatory conversion ratios, no forced repatriation timelines, and no requirement to surrender export proceeds to the BCL or any other authority. For statistical balance-of-payments purposes, resident banks may classify cross-border transactions, including the crediting of export proceeds, and report aggregate data to the BCL, but this is an informational statistical obligation and does not create a surrender or repatriation obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) – the central bank and monetary authority of Luxembourg; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) – the competent financial sector supervisory authority responsible for the prudential and conduct supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury, but Low-Medium for the capital escrow and structural substance layer. Regulated Luxembourg or EEA-passporting electronic money institutions (EMIs) can be utilized for cross-border SWIFT/SEPA payment initiation, FX conversion, and disbursement settlement of project cargo service fees. They are subject to CSSF or home-state financial supervision, the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD4/AMLD5) rules. Legal constraints include: EMIs cannot accept deposits or grant credit; safeguarding obligations apply to customer funds; virtual IBAN or sub-account structures may be permitted but require transparent segregation; and certain Luxembourg banks may be unwilling to treat funds held through an EMI as evidence of operational substance. The SCSp's primary commercial banking relationship should remain with a full-license Luxembourg bank to meet preferred banking partner expectations for letter-of-credit operations and large-value project cargo payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Luxembourg SCSp are executed by international SWIFT MT103 or MT202-compliant wire transfers from the limited partners or their custodians to the SCSp's designated EUR or USD bank account held with a Luxembourg commercial bank. The transfer must clearly reference the currency purpose code and narrative corresponding to a capital contribution, for example 'SCSP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'LIMITED PARTNER SUBSCRIPTION PAYMENT', and the remitting institution must undertake standard KYC/AML verification. Luxembourg banks record cross-border payments for BCL statistics and may request a copy of the call notice, the limited partnership agreement, and a capital contribution schedule. Because Luxembourg applies no exchange controls, no prior central bank approval is required; however, the ultimate beneficial owners and the source of funds must satisfy the bank's amplified EDD requirements for any capital escrow above the bank's internal threshold.Trade disbursements for project cargo and heavy-haul logistics are settled via SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier and subcontractor payments, MT202 for nostro/mirror-account movements, and MT700/MT760 series for documentary letters of credit and standby LC backstops where required by contract. Commercial documentary letters of credit are the preferred rail for high-value outbound disbursements, especially for cross-border freight, port handling, haulage, and oversized cargo equipment purchases. For EUR-denominated payments within the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA), TARGET2-guaranteed SEPA Credit Transfers provide near-real-time settlement; for USD-denominated or third-currency obligations, the Luxembourg bank will clear through its correspondent banking network, typically via New York or Frankfurt correspondents. SWIFT gpi tracking should be requested to provide transparency and proof of execution for audit and milestone compliance.EUR/USD outright foreign exchange forward contractsEUR/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) when the project currency is non-convertible or the local deliverable market is inaccessibleEUR/CNY deliverable forwards and CNY NDFs for China-origin heavy-lift equipment and freight paymentsCross-currency interest rate swaps for hedging dual-currency project financing cash flowsVanilla EUR currency options and EUR dollar options for premia-protected cash-flow hedgingRange forward contracts and participating forwards to lock in exchange-rate corridors

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury, but Low-Medium for the capital escrow and structural substance layer. Regulated Luxembourg or EEA-passporting electronic money institutions (EMIs) can be utilized for cross-border SWIFT/SEPA payment initiation, FX conversion, and disbursement settlement of project cargo service fees. They are subject to CSSF or home-state financial supervision, the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD4/AMLD5) rules. Legal constraints include: EMIs cannot accept deposits or grant credit; safeguarding obligations apply to customer funds; virtual IBAN or sub-account structures may be permitted but require transparent segregation; and certain Luxembourg banks may be unwilling to treat funds held through an EMI as evidence of operational substance. The SCSp's primary commercial banking relationship should remain with a full-license Luxembourg bank to meet preferred banking partner expectations for letter-of-credit operations and large-value project cargo payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level because the SCSp is a fiscally transparent partnership; a Luxembourg-resident corporate partner is taxable at a top combined Luxembourg City rate of 24.94% (17% CIT + 7% unemployment-fund surcharge = 18.19%, plus 6.75% municipal business tax) for taxable income exceeding EUR 200,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for SCSp profit allocations — the SCSp has no legal personality and is tax transparent, so distributions are not dividends subject to the Luxembourg 15% statutory dividend WHT. The 15% rate applies only to distributions by resident companies (S.à r.l., SA, SCA, etc.).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory local currency threshold; the ACD may require Local File/Master File documentation on request (within 30 days). Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to Luxembourg-headquartered or constituent groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. For the SCSp, contemporaneous documentation should still be prepared whenever related-party transactions exist (e.g., OOG freight, charter, stevedoring and handling services to affiliated shippers).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (LIR Article 6, ATAD GAAR), the OECD principal-purpose test in DTAs, and ACD audit practice: the SCSp must demonstrate actual local management, physical office and logistics operations, local bank accounts, payroll, and genuine transactions in the OOG heavy-haul business; otherwise profits may be recharacterised and treaty or EU directive benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion / False Filing

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Luxembourg General Tax Act: imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or fine up to EUR 50,000; restitution of evaded tax; default interest of 0.6% per month; late-filing surcharge up to 10% of the assessed tax.

Director personal liability: The general partner (commandité) is jointly and severally liable without limitation; participating directors face personal civil restitution, seizure of assets, travel bans and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ACD upward adjustment of taxable income, recomputed CIT/municipal tax, default interest of 0.6% per month, penalty up to 10% of the deficiency, and separate daily fines for failure to produce requested TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false TP data or direct the SCSp without arm's length controls are personally liable for the assessed shortfall and are exposed to criminal sanctions for false certification.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under LIR Article 6 and ATAD GAAR; denial of DTA and EU Directive benefits; reclassification of the SCSp as taxable corporation at 24.94% plus net wealth tax; reassessment for up to 10 years; fines for failure to declare substance changes.

Director personal liability: General partner and managing directors are jointly liable for the re-characterised tax, with civil penalties, asset attachment and imprisonment for fraudulent concealment of the arrangement.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 125,000 for RBE filing failures; criminal fines for deliberate concealment of beneficial ownership; transaction freezing and adverse regulatory reporting.

Director personal liability: General partner and directors face personal fines, debarment, and imprisonment of up to 5 years where deliberate ML/TF predicate conduct is proven; travel bans may be imposed pending criminal proceedings.

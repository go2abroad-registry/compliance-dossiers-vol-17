Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital applies to a Luxembourg SCSp; partnership capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement, with no mandatory escrow account or statutory share capital deposit (Law of 23 July 2016).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGIDS) covering evacuation routes, extinguishing systems, and molten-metal fire hazards; workplace clearance and structural-load inspection by the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (Inspection du travail et des mines) for die-casting presses, ventilation systems, ATEX zones, and occupational health compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital applies to a Luxembourg SCSp; partnership capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement, with no mandatory escrow account or statutory share capital deposit (Law of 23 July 2016).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT - Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority), Ministry of Finance, Luxembourg; electronic portal: Guichet.lu / MyGuichet (https://guichet.lu); direct income-tax classification administered by the Administration des contributions directes (ACD); export/customs controls by the Administration des douanes et accises.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SCSp (no minimum share capital required) and obtain the Luxembourg national identification number (NIF / RCSL registration) through the Luxembourg Business Registers. Step 2 - File VAT registration Form 645 (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT, attaching the partnership agreement (contrat de société), RCSL extract, proof of registered office, identity documents of the general partner (gérant) and beneficial owners, and projected turnover by VAT rate; non-established SCSp must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative where required. Step 3 - AEDT issues a VAT identification number on the pattern 'LU' + 8 digits (e.g., LU12345678) within approximately 10-15 working days, effective from the date of first taxable supply. Step 4 - Maintain VAT records in Luxembourg and file periodic returns via MyGuichet; no separate direct-tax registration is required for the fiscally transparent partnership itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is statutorily transparent for income tax and is expressly excluded from the § 166 LIR election to be taxed as a corporation. Available elections: (i) VAT fiscal unity ('assujetti unique') under Article 60 of the 1979 VAT Law to group the SCSp with closely linked Luxembourg entities; (ii) waiver of the Article 57 small-business exemption (franchise) to recover input VAT and charge VAT on supplies; (iii) Union and non-Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) elections for distance sales of the die-cast models; (iv) option to apply customs inward-processing/export regimes designating the operating partner as exporter of record.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (Article 57 of the 1979 VAT Law, 2024 threshold). Non-established taxable persons have no de minimis exemption and must register from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Intra-Community distance sales of goods are subject to the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold unless the OSS election is exercised.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 74(1) of the 1979 VAT Law where the SCSp, as exporter of record, dispatches the die-cast scale models to non-EU destinations and proves, by the validated customs export declaration and transport documents, that the goods physically left EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch/procedure acceptance. The exemption also covers supplies placed under export, external transit (T1), customs warehousing and related outward-processing customs regimes. The commercial invoice must cite the statutory exemption, and the goods must be exported directly and without alteration. Luxembourg imposes no separate statutory deadline for repatriation of export proceeds; the 180-day parameter below reflects the standard commercial credit-term benchmark used in transfer-pricing documentation, bank export-financing, and credit insurance facilities.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) validated by the Administration des douanes et accises, bearing the MRN and naming the SCSp as exporter of record.
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU within 90 days: negotiable Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), or Air Waybill (air).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank SWIFT MT103 or credit advice - evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds from the overseas buyer.
  • Commercial invoice stating the Article 74(1) export exemption, INCOTERMS, quantity, unit price and zero VAT rate for the die-cast collectibles.

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping mandate. Statutory e-invoicing applies only to business-to-government (B2G) supplies: invoices to the Luxembourg State and public bodies must be structured EN 16931 electronic invoices (UBL/CII or PEPPOL BIS 3.0) transmitted via the PEPPOL network, per Directive 2014/55/EU. B2B/B2C e-invoicing is voluntary; paper, PDF, Factur-X and other agreed formats remain valid. The AEDT may request the Luxembourg Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T LS) in XML during a VAT audit, but no periodic SAF-T filing is imposed. Export invoices must state 'art. 74 L. 12.02.1979 export exemption' and the customs Movement Reference Number (MRN).

AEDT and customs auditors jointly verify MRNs, transport documentation, exit timing and the 90-day exit window. Missing, incomplete or contradictory substantiation triggers retroactive reassessment at the 16% VAT rate with default interest, a surcharge of up to 10% under § 168 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), increasing to 20% for intentional or fraudulent omissions, plus additional fines for failure to produce the SAF-T LS audit file. Forgery of export documents or systematic export-fraud schemes are prosecuted as VAT fraud under the Criminal Code, with substantial fines and imprisonment (up to 5 years); the clawback covers the full VAT amount plus interest rather than only the differential, and input VAT on raw materials may be denied. The general partner (gérant) acting for the fiscally transparent SCSp may be held jointly and severally liable for the VAT debt, and all export records must be retained for at least 10 years under the Commercial Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions between Luxembourg and non-EU states are unrestricted under Articles 63-65 TFEU, subject only to EU sanctions, standard KYC/AML duties, and CSSF/BCL statistical reporting. Profit remittance from an SCSp to non-resident partners faces no exchange-control hurdle; banks may request underlying documentation and a tax solvency/beneficial-ownership review, but the remittance itself is lawful and can be executed in EUR or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline. Export receipts in USD, CNY, EUR or other currencies may be credited to domestic or foreign accounts freely. The only incidental obligation is the resident bank's reporting of material inward payments to the BCL for monetary and balance-of-payments statistics; no conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and AML/CFT controls. Luxembourg applies the EU free movement of capital regime; the BCL administers balance-of-payments statistics, and there is no separate foreign exchange control administration, exchange licensing, or central bank approval requirement for cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as an ancillary payment rail, Medium as the primary treasury vehicle. EEA-licensed E-money Institutions (EMIs) holding a CSSF licence or an MFID/AMLD5 passport may lawfully issue multi-currency IBANs and process SWIFT/PayPal-style settlements, but they do not provide regulated credit, documentary LC, escrow segregation, or FX hedging in the manner of a regulated credit institution. PayPal may be used for collector sales, but it cannot replace banking-grade liquidity management and must itself satisfy EU AML/CFT obligations. All EMIs must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts and are subject to CSSF conduct supervision.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Luxembourg City, Grand-Duchy of Luxembourg; online e-file portal: www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales, LSC), Articles 100-1 to 100-15 (SCSp chapter introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013); complemented by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution (limited partnership agreement) together with the fiche électronique (electronic disclosure sheet), filed electronically via the LBR eFile platform and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no share-capital notion and no escrow account requirement under Articles 100-1 to 100-15 LSC; initial partnership interests are freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be subscribed in cash or in kind, with the contributions stated in the fiche électronique for information only.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for office-only registered premises. If the SCSp later operates a copper cathode warehouse in Luxembourg, the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) must approve fire-safety features, the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) is competent for workplace safety clearance, and storage racks require a structural-load verification under the Labor Code (Book IV) and the Law of 9 May 2014 on classified establishments.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no share-capital notion and no escrow account requirement under Articles 100-1 to 100-15 LSC; initial partnership interests are freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be subscribed in cash or in kind, with the contributions stated in the fiche électronique for information only.

FOURTH: all prior, incidental actions are ratified.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Ministère de l'Économie — Direction de l'industrie (no industrial licence applies to ISIC 4662); for environmental aspects: Ministère de l'Environnement, du Climat et du Développement durable — Administration de l'environnement.

Category: Not required — wholesale physical arbitrage of refined copper cathodes (ISIC 4662 / NAICS 423510) does not require a separate operating or manufacturing permit under Luxembourg law; a class-4 établissement classé declaration may apply only if closed metal storage is established in Luxembourg under the Law of 9 May 2014 on classified establishments.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), with e-portal www.aedt.public.lu and MyGuichet.lu for company and VAT filings. Direct income taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), www.impotsdirects.public.lu. The business registry (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), operated under the Ministry of Justice, issues the corporate matricule and is accessible via www.lbr.lu and the MyGuichet.lu e-filing portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp: (1) execute and file the partnership deed (contrat de société) with the RCS via a registered professional (avocat or fiduciary) on MyGuichet.lu to obtain the company matricule and the national registration number; (2) request VAT registration on AEDT Form 640 (national form; Form 640E for EU-established taxable persons; Form 640 for non-established persons, with appointment of an AEDT-approved tax representative where required); (3) receive the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits, shown on the RCS excerpt and the AEDT registration certificate; (4) obtain an EU EORI number (Luxembourg prefix LU) for customs exports; (5) file the local 'newly established taxable person' notification if non-resident partners have no Luxembourg permanent establishment; expected completion time is 2–6 weeks from complete filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax: it has no legal personality and is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax at entity level. Profits are allocated directly to partners: a Luxembourg-resident general partner is taxed on its share; non-resident limited partners are taxable only if the SCSp creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment (e.g., through Luxembourg management or decision-making) or derives Luxembourg-source fixed assets. For VAT only, the SCSp is treated as an independent taxable person and must register separately. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax: it has no legal personality and is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax at entity level. Profits are allocated directly to partners: a Luxembourg-resident general partner is taxed on its share; non-resident limited partners are taxable only if the SCSp creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment (e.g., through Luxembourg management or decision-making) or derives Luxembourg-source fixed assets. For VAT only, the SCSp is treated as an independent taxable person and must register separately. Territorial_Scope: non-resident partners with no Luxembourg PE are outside direct tax nexus; Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable; Entity_Level_Taxation: not applicable.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box election; the SCSp is transparent as a matter of legal form (law of 10 August 1915). Available mechanisms: (i) advance tax agreement or rescrit procedure with the ACD (Circular L.G. - A n°29) to confirm fiscal transparency, absence of PE, and allocation of profits; (ii) contractual election in the partnership agreement to determine partner profit shares, with mandatory proposal of substance documentation; (iii) the mandatory EU/ATAD anti-hybrid rules (law of 21 December 2018) will recharacterize effects where a double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion arises from differing classifications in Luxembourg and the partner jurisdiction; (iv) an option under the VAT Law to apply the intra-Community acquisition rule or the reverse-charge mechanism for certain cross-border B2B supplies, but no entity-classification VAT election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Luxembourg-established small business, the VAT registration threshold is EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excl. VAT) per the SME franchise regime of Article 57 of the VAT Law; businesses exceeding EUR 35,000 must register and charge VAT. A wholesale copper-cathode trading entity (ISIC 4662) will almost always exceed EUR 35,000 in turnover and is therefore required to hold a Luxembourg VAT identification number from the first taxable supply of goods, with no further threshold for non-established foreign suppliers (threshold EUR 0 in the absence of a Luxembourg fixed establishment); intra-EU distance-sales thresholds (EUR 10,000) do not apply to B2B wholesale metal trading.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of non-ferrous copper cathodes to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 42(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided: (a) the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to a non-EU country; (b) the goods leave the EU within 90 days from the date of supply; and (c) proof of export (electronic export declaration and transport documents) is held within the same 90-day period. Offshore services directly connected with the export (e.g., freight, handling, insurance, pre-carriage) may also be zero-rated under Article 44(2); intra-EU B2B supplies to a customer holding a valid EU VAT number are taxable at 0% under Article 43 with proper VIES validation and an Intrastat goods-movement declaration. The taxpayer must evidence the sale, movement, and consideration with contemporaneous documents.

  • Commercial invoice referencing LME Grade A copper cathodes and HS code 7403.11 (incoterms, quantity, unit price, warrant serial number)
  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD/EX-A) with MRN issued in the EU Export Control System (AES)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or CMR/air waybill evidencing departure from the EU within 90 days of supply
  • Packing list and independent weight certificate
  • LME warrant or registered warehouse receipt transferring title to the non-EU buyer
  • Bank documentary evidence: SWIFT MT700/MT103, documentary letter of credit, or remittance advice
  • Inward remittance certificate or FX repatriation record (settlement within 180 days delivery baseline)
  • EORI number and VIES confirmation of the intra-EU customer (for 0% intra-EU supply)
  • Intrastat declaration for intra-Community trade movements if applicable
  • Evidence of delivery terms and transport insurance policy

Luxembourg does not currently maintain a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping device, or API-based invoice-clearance mandate for domestic B2B transactions. Since 1 January 2020, electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G contracts through PEPPOL-BIS 3.0. B2B e-invoicing is permitted but not yet compulsory; the Ministry of Finance has announced the phased introduction of mandatory B2B e-invoicing, with draft legislation proposing PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices for Luxembourg VAT payers from 1 January 2026 (status: ongoing legislative process; deferral to 2027 under public discussion). For exports, the single electronic customs declaration (SAD/EX) must be lodged in the EU's Import-Control-System/Export-System (ICS2/AES), providing the CS/MRN registration number; no national stamping or pre-clearance of invoices is required.

The AEDT targets export zero-rating in commodity trading audits. If the exporter fails to produce the export declaration, transport proof, or evidence of payment within the statutory 90-day exit period, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply at 17% VAT, with retrospective reassessment. The general VAT assessments limitation period is 5 years from the end of the relevant calendar year, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud. Under-assessment penalties: late-payment interest at the statutory quarterly rate (approx. 3.75% p.a. in 2024), a 10% late-payment surcharge, and administrative fines up to EUR 15,000 for non-intentional documentation infringements. Intentional export fraud is criminally sanctioned with fines up to EUR 25,000 or imprisonment, and the export zero-rating is denied retroactively. The taxpayer must retain the full export and payment dossier for 6 years, and the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit) applies to transactions lacking economic substance or genuine commercial rationale, permitting the clawback of zero-rating benefits and the imposition of penalties in accordance with Article 10(1) and Article 11 of the VAT Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is the domestic currency; USD, CNY, GBP and all other freely traded currencies are convertible without BCL approval, prior licence, or repatriation obligation. Profit remittance by an SCSp faces no FX regulatory hurdle; distributions are governed by the partnership agreement and tax treatment at the partner level. Practical friction points are limited to EU/UN sanctions screening, AML source-of-funds verification, Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA)/Common Reporting Standard (CRS) reporting, and BCL balance-of-payments statistical declarations for transactions exceeding reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. A copper-cathode trading SCSp may retain USD or other foreign currency balances in multicurrency accounts, settle via SWIFT, and convert at commercially negotiated rates. All transfers of funds must comply with Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers; physical cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more require declaration under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision and AML/CFT oversight are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance holds policy competence for financial regulation and capital-movement legislation. Luxembourg has no dedicated foreign-exchange administration agency; exchange-control functions are discharged through EU treaty law, BCL statistical reporting, and CSSF enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury rails; acceptable only for ancillary operating expenses and small-value disbursements. Legal constraints: a non-EEA offshore digital EMI cannot provide payment accounts to a Luxembourg SCSp without a Luxembourg or EU/EEA authorisation under the Law of 20 May 2011 on payment services and e-money (PSD2 passporting); such authorisation requires substantial substance, a local compliance function, and CSSF prudential capital. A CSSF-licensed EMI can execute SEPA Credit Transfers and MT103-style payments through partner banks, but cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot hold LME warrants, and generally triggers higher correspondent fees, reduced settlement certainty, and enhanced compliance scrutiny for commodity-trading flows exceeding USD 1 million per transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the remitting bank initiates a SWIFT MT103 to the Luxembourg account bank, referencing the beneficiary as '[Name] SCSp' with its IBAN and specifying the remittance information field as 'Capital contribution in accordance with partnership agreement dated [date]; injection into LU__LU__LU_SCSP'. Luxembourg requires no specific FX purpose code; the receiving bank will request the SCSp's LEI, RBE extract, contribution resolution, and a completed CRS/FATCA self-certification. Non-EUR inbound funds are converted at the receiving bank's spot rate on T+2; there is no BCL approval or prior notification, but the BCL may collect the transaction in its statistical balance-of-payments survey. The bank will reject transfers from non-EU crypto exchanges, pooled third-party wallets, or opaque corporate vehicles lacking a documented economic rationale.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct settlement of LME warrant transfers and spot cargo purchases; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP 600 / eUCP 2.0) issued or confirmed by the Luxembourg relationship bank, payable against seller's presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, analysis certificate, and LME warrant delivery; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EU counterparties; (iv) for exchange-traded LME contracts, settlement via LME Clear requires the SCSp to maintain cash margin with a clearing member broker, funded through MT103 transfers to the broker's segregated client account; (v) MT202 for interbank cover payments when the SCSp instructs a non-bank counter-transfer.

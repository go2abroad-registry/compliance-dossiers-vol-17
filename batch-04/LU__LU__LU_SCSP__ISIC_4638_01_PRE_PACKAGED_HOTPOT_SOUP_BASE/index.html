Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des impôts (ADI) — the unified Luxembourg tax administration formed by the merger of the Administration des contributions directes and the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA; competent for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, VAT, registration duties and withholding taxes. Electronic portal: MyGuichet.lu, ADI e-TVA and e-CDF online services; commercial and taxpayer registration through the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) at lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SCSp partnership agreement and file the required extract, bylaws, authorized signatories, registered office address and identity of the general partner (associé commandité) with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu or an approved fiduciary; obtain the RCS registration number and the national matricule number. Step 2: Submit the VAT registration application to the ADI through the e-TVA portal or the MyGuichet company-creation workspace, attaching the RCS extract, proof of registered office, identity of managers and bank KYC documents; the ADI issues an 8-digit Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + eight digits) within approximately 10 business days. Step 3: Non-resident partners do not require a Luxembourg tax ID for entity formation; if the SCSp carries on commercial activity through a Luxembourg fixed place of business, the partners may trigger Luxembourg tax filing obligations. Step 4: There is no tax authorization or agrément for hotpot soup base distribution; however, a food-export health certificate from the Luxembourg Veterinary and Food Administration (ALVA) may be required by the importing country.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is transparent for Luxembourg income taxes: it is not subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) or net wealth tax (IF); income, gains and losses are allocated directly to the general and limited partners. For VAT, the SCSp is treated as an independent taxable person (assujetti) to the extent it makes economic supplies; the territorial scope of partner taxation depends on the partner's own residence, with non-resident partners exposed to Luxembourg tax only if the SCSp's activity constitutes a Luxembourg permanent establishment or fixed place of business for them.

Available election forms

The SCSp cannot elect to become opaque for income tax: the Article 175 LIR taxation option for partnerships is limited to entities in which all partners are indefinitely and jointly liable (in practice the SNC), a condition the SCSp cannot meet because limited partners (commanditaires) benefit from limited liability. Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; the pass-through classification is automatic and non-waivable. Partnerships cannot be part of a Luxembourg fiscal unity (Article 164bis LIR), which requires a 95 percent holding in a corporate subsidiary. ATAD hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch rules (law of 21 December 2018) apply mandatorily and no elective relief or hybrid neutrality option is available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control regime, no payment-licensing requirement for cross-border transfers, and no restriction on dividend, profit, or partnership-distribution remittance. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC guarantee the free movement of capital; the only applicable obligations are prudential banking compliance, ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting, and anti-money-laundering declarations. Profit distributions and capital-redemption proceeds of a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) are freely transferable to resident and non-resident partners in any currency without BCL approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain receipts in EUR or in any foreign currency in Luxembourg bank accounts without mandatory conversion. Export proceeds are subject to BCL/Eurosystem balance-of-payments statistical reporting via the instructing bank when the monthly reporting threshold for non-resident counterparties is exceeded; this is purely informational and does not restrict the use, holding, or onward disbursement of funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential banking supervision is exercised concurrently by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); monetary policy and exchange-rate policy are delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passported into Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2); Low for non-EEA offshore digital banks and EMIs that lack a Luxembourg credit-institution, EMI, or payment-institution licence or an accepted EEA passport. Unlicensed offshore fintechs cannot lawfully maintain payment accounts in Luxembourg and would expose the SCSp treasury structure to regulatory and correspondent-banking integrity risk. Only EEA-authorized EMIs (e.g., licensed in Germany, Lithuania, or the Netherlands), or Luxembourg-regulated banks should support the SCSp's operational clearing and settlement.

Where the partner holds non-EUR funds, the correspondent bank applies a market-rate conversion; no BCL pre-authorisation is required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); the headline central CIT rate is 17%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; distributions by an SCSp are not generally treated as dividends because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, and no Luxembourg withholding tax is levied on distribution proceeds paid to non-resident partners unless the profits are attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the end of the SCSp fiscal year, unless extension is granted by the Administration des contributions directes; calendar-year taxpayers commonly file by 31 December of the following year in administrative practice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate Luxembourg statutory threshold requiring Local File or Master File at entity level; CbCR applies to groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000,000. ACD can require contemporaneous Local File in a tax audit. In all cases, arm's-length contemporaneous documentation for cross-border hotpot soup base distribution, tolling, procurement, and treasury transactions is mandatory in practice regardless of turnover.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the form used by offshore financial centres, but the benefit of tax transparency, double tax treaty access, and preferential regimes is conditioned on real economic substance. The ACD applies the GAAR and anti-abuse principles under LIR Article 6 and OECD BEPS Action 6; the SCSp must demonstrate credible business purpose, premises, personnel, commercial risk, and decision-making in Luxembourg. Failure to do so may cause the SCSp to be recharacterized as a shell, ignored for tax purposes, or denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax; late payment interest at 0.6% per month; fines up to EUR 100,000; criminal tax fraud prosecution with imprisonment up to five years under Luxembourg general tax procedure and criminal code.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any managing director of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties and interest when they knowingly signed false declarations or directed fraudulent distributions; courts may pierce the SCSp veil, order civil restitution, freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, and issue disqualification orders.
Shell Company / Abuse of Tax Transparency

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the SCSp as taxable or subject to mandatory registration for corporate income tax; denial of the transparent partnership status; retroactive assessments of corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; penalty interest and fines; possible judicial dissolution as a sham enterprise.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil-piercing attaches to the general partner, de facto managers, and knowing beneficial owners; they may be held personally liable for quantified tax deficiencies, loss of treaty benefits, and tort damages; travel restrictions and asset attachment orders may issue.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Sanctions

Statutory sanction: CSSF-regulated professionals and the general partner face administrative fines and regulatory enforcement; violations of the Luxembourg anti-money laundering law and criminal code may result in fines up to EUR 1,250,000 or a fraction of turnover, asset confiscation, and imprisonment of five to ten years for organized money laundering.

Director personal liability: Managers, beneficial owners, and directors who knowingly execute or permit layered trade payments bear personal criminal liability; Luxembourg courts may levy confiscation of personal assets, impose travel bans, and enforce extradition requests; wrongful concealment of beneficial ownership nullifies limited liability protection.

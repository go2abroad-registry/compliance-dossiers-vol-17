Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope applies at partner level

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default under Article 2(2) LIR (no Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax at entity level). There is no general domestic check-the-box regime; however: (i) US investors may file Form 8832 to elect partnership or association status for US federal tax purposes because the SCSp is not a per se corporation; (ii) a joint option by all partners to subject the SCSp to corporate income tax/municipal business tax/net wealth tax is available for certain Luxembourg partnerships; (iii) ATAD II reverse-hybrid rules (Article 168septies LIR, in force from 1 January 2022) re-characterise the SCSp as a Luxembourg taxable entity where the partner's home jurisdiction treats it as opaque and the mismatch results in double non-taxation; (iv) hybrid-mismatch rules (Articles 168quinquies-168sexies LIR) restrict deductions for hybrid financial instruments and disregarded payments.

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital health-fitness app (ISIC 6201) - 0%/outside scope applies: (a) B2B supplies of electronic services: place of supply is the customer's establishment (Art. 44 VAT Directive); no Luxembourg VAT is chargeable when the customer is established outside Luxembourg, reverse charge applying for EU customers and outside scope for non-EU customers, evidenced by the customer's valid VAT ID or business registration; (b) B2C supplies directly by the SCSp: place of supply is the consumer's residence (Arts. 58/59 VAT Directive); non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT scope (effective 0%) and EU consumers trigger Luxembourg VAT unless the Union OSS is used to declare the consumer-country rate; (c) App-store distribution through Apple IAP/Google Play Billing: the marketplace is the deemed supplier (Art. 9a Implementing Regulation 282/2011), so the SCSp's supply to the marketplace operator is an out-of-scope/B2B reverse-charge supply; (d) Physical goods exports: 0% applies where the goods leave the EU within 90 days of dispatch, evidenced by the customs export declaration (SAD/EAD with MRN) and transport documents. Input VAT on related costs remains deductible under the normal rules.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or bank SWIFT payment statement evidencing receipt of foreign sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and service contract describing the digital/offshore supply
  • Customer VAT identification number or business registration certificate (for B2B services)

Luxembourg has no continuous transaction controls, no real-time fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing is permitted for B2B provided the invoice guarantees authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility (e.g., EDI, PEPPOL, or qualified electronic signature). B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via the PEPPOL network under the law of 17 July 2020 and EU Directive 2014/55/EU for all Luxembourg public-sector procurement. VAT returns, European Sales Lists and OSS declarations are filed electronically through eTVA; the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' initiative will introduce harmonised e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU supplies once adopted and is not yet in force.

Luxembourg applies no foreign-exchange controls or statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline (EU free movement of capital); the 180-day benchmark is used as commercial substantiation practice for the export/offshore service and payment flow. The AEDT may reassess within the standard 5-year VAT limitation period, extended to 10 years where fraud is proven. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating/outside-scope claims are clawed back with (i) the unpaid VAT at 17%, (ii) late-payment interest of 0.6% per month, and (iii) administrative fines up to EUR 12,500. Where intent is established, criminal tax-fraud penalties under Article 181 of the VAT Law of 12 February 1979 range from EUR 125 to EUR 12,500 plus imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years; customs export fraud is additionally sanctioned under the General Customs Law with fines up to twice the evaded duties and confiscation of the goods. Deemed-supplier arrangements with Apple/Google and the apportionment of Luxembourg-source revenue among SCSp partners are specific audit focus points.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area jurisdiction with fully liberalised capital and current account movements under EU law. The euro is freely convertible and there are no residual exchange control restrictions for non-resident investors in a Luxembourg SCSp. Profit distributions and capital redemptions to non-resident partners or unitholders may be made without prior regulatory approval. An SCSp is treated as fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax, and profit distributions are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax. Where a foreign partner is a tax resident in a non-EU jurisdiction, the bank may require additional evidence of economic substance and beneficial ownership to prevent treaty shopping or BEPS-related challenges, but no central bank conversion or inward/outward remittance permit is required. The only non-monetary hurdles are statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL for cross-border flows above certain thresholds, and CSSF/KYC documentation standards applied by Luxembourg banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or holding period applies to Luxembourg digital commerce entities. All export and app-store proceeds, including revenue from Apple, Google Play, and enterprise licensing, may be retained in EUR or in freely convertible foreign currency. For statistical transparency, cross-border payment transactions must be reported to the BCL under the balance-of-payments reporting framework, usually through the settlement bank or on a direct reporting basis if the SCSp transacts above the applicable thresholds. There is no timeline for surrender and no ratio requiring conversion into EUR.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and under the prudential supervisory oversight of the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional and Medium for EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions or Payment Institutions passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2; Low for offshore or unregulated digital EMIs. An offshore EMI without an EU banking licence or PSD2 passport cannot lawfully provide Luxembourg IBANs, clearing accounts, or regulated payment services. Even a licensed EEA EMI may be used only as a secondary treasury circuit for disbursements, not as the primary capital escrow provider, because EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot accept deposits or deliver full banking escrow services of the kind expected by CSSF for a corporate capital raise. The primary banking relationship for the SCSp should remain with a Luxembourg credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscriptions and shareholder loans are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's home bank to the Luxembourg SCSp's designated corporate account. The remittance reference must cite the subscription agreement number and the identity of the SCSp to allow the receiving Luxembourg bank to assign the proper balance-of-payments statistical category for direct investment or capital transfers to the BCL. For any non-EUR injection, the bank will require the underlying subscription in EUR or an executed FX conversion contract. The supporting documents must include the executed subscription agreement, call notice, source of funds evidence, beneficial ownership confirmation, and, where applicable, the Luxembourg partnership deed. Compliance with EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering checks is completed before value is credited.Operational outbound payments to EEA suppliers are executed through SEPA Credit Transfers using ISO 20022 XML with the SCSp's legal name and purpose reference. Payments outside the EEA are executed through SWIFT MT103 with a valid UETR and structured remittance data. High-value physical or licensing-based trade transactions may be backed by a Commercial Letter of Credit, although for a digital health app the primary clearing rails are MT103 and SEPA. The bank will apply collective mandate limits, dual-approval controls, and monthly BCL balance-of-payments reporting. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Conditional and Medium for EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions or Payment Institutions passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2; Low for offshore or unregulated digital EMIs. An offshore EMI without an EU banking licence or PSD2 passport cannot lawfully provide Luxembourg IBANs, clearing accounts, or regulated payment services. Even a licensed EEA EMI may be used only as a secondary treasury circuit for disbursements, not as the primary capital escrow provider, because EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot accept deposits or deliver full banking escrow services of the kind expected by CSSF for a corporate capital raise. The primary banking relationship for the SCSp should remain with a Luxembourg credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level (fiscally transparent partnership, not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax). A Luxembourg-resident corporate partner is exposed to the aggregate Luxembourg corporate tax burden of 24.94% in Luxembourg City, composed of 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and a 6.75% municipal business tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions made by the SCSp itself because a fiscally transparent SCSp is not a Luxembourg resident company and does not make statutory dividend withholdable distributions. Where the SCSp, or a partner, receives dividends from an underlying Luxembourg resident company, the domestic Luxembourg withholding tax rate is 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent–Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying 10% participation held for 12 months, and generally reduced to 5%–15% under most double tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers the requirement to prepare and maintain Luxembourg Master File and Local File documentation; EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers the Country-by-Country Report threshold. In audit practice, the ACD may require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation even below the EUR 50 million threshold where material related-party transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulations similar to the UAE, but it applies a substantive business-purpose doctrine, the statutory general anti-abuse rule (LIR §168bis), ATAD anti-hybrid rules, and extensive case law requiring genuine local substance to access treaty benefits and tax transparency. An SCSp engaged in a digital health app business should maintain a Luxembourg registered office, a Luxembourg-resident manager, local bank accounts, local books and records, minutes of partner and manager decisions, and actual strategic control over pricing, marketing, and intellectual property to withstand an ACD substance challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; the taxpayer is required to pay all taxes due plus a punitive surcharge and statutory interest at 0.6% per month; the ACD may reopen previously closed assessments and impose penalties equal to a substantial percentage of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The gérant and controlling directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes in cases of fraud or gross mismanagement; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment, confiscation of assets, and travel bans imposed during the investigation.
Failure to file annual Luxembourg tax return or nil return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on any assessed amount; discretionary assessment by the ACD with reversal of the burden of proof, leading to estimated high profits and denial of otherwise deductible expenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who have knowingly failed to file may be personally assessed for fines and interest; in serious cases, the failure to file after a formal demand may be treated as tax evasion and expose directors to criminal sanctions and civil restitution orders.
Transfer pricing and profit-stripping adjustment

Statutory sanction: The ACD will apply the arm's-length principle under LIR §164 and disallow the excessive deduction, computing additional taxable income in the SCSp or Luxembourg partner at the 24.94% aggregate corporate rate; a 10% tax penalty and 0.6% per month late interest will be added; serious cases are referred to the criminal tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp manager who approved the intercompany royalty may be held personally liable for the additional corporate tax if the arrangement was not properly documented or if they acted in bad faith; professional service providers may be subject to civil liability for assisting in abusive arrangements.
Abuse of SCSp transparency and shell company arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ACD may disregard the SCSp for tax purposes and treat a non-resident partner as having a Luxembourg permanent establishment, applying Luxembourg corporate income tax at 24.94% plus solidarity and municipal business tax; the entity may be denied all treaty benefits; administrative fines and penalties up to EUR 25,000 may be imposed.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be jointly liable for taxes assessed upon recharacterisation; criminal sanctions up to five years' imprisonment may apply where the shell arrangement is used to conceal the true economic beneficiary or to launder funds, and a travel ban may be imposed pending proceedings.
AML/CTF violation through misuse of app payment rails

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg anti-money-laundering law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) criminalises money laundering with imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 1,250,000; illicit proceeds and instrumentalities are subject to confiscation; administrative AML fines may be imposed in parallel.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate in or tolerate such schemes are personally criminally liable and may face imprisonment, confiscation of personal assets, director disqualification, and travel restrictions designed to secure the enforcement of monetary penalties.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg; electronic filing exclusively via the e-RCS portal at www.lbr.lu / rcs.lu.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies), as amended by the Law of 19 May 2023 on the modernisation of the SARL (in force 1 September 2023); SARL regime at Articles 710-1 to 710-15; registration and publication governed by the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE), Articles 1-36.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Articles of Incorporation/Statutes) executed either before a Luxembourg civil-law notary or, since the Law of 19 May 2023, by private written deed (sous seing privé) signed by all shareholders; filed electronically with the RCS together with the registration form (formulaire d'inscription) and the signed declaration of conformity (déclaration de conformité).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for SARLs since the Law of 19 May 2023 (in force 1 September 2023); the capital is freely set in the articles of incorporation (practically EUR 1 or more; cross-border BPO entities commonly adopt EUR 12,000). All subscribed cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and, under standard banking practice, are deposited in a blocked (escrow) account opened in the name of the company 'in formation' with a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution; the bank's blocked-capital attestation is filed with the RCS and the funds are released to the company only upon RCS registration.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and excise duties. Principal portals: impotsdirects.public.lu, ecdf.etat.lu, guichet.lu, and lbr.lu for RCS filings.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg-resident SARL is constituted by notarial deed filed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu). RCS registration automatically creates the ACD tax file and the 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which is the corporate TIN for direct taxes. The AEDT automatically assigns a separate VAT identification number formatted as LU + 8 digits. Non-established foreign suppliers not requiring incorporation must file direct VAT registration with the AEDT via the eCDF portal and receive a LU VAT ID without a Luxembourg registered office, unless supplies are reverse-charged to a Luxembourg VAT-registered customer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; a SARL is mandatorily an opaque resident company. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) for closely linked persons with AEDT authorization; (ii) Luxembourg fiscal unity (intégration fiscale, Article 164bis LIR) for a resident parent directly or indirectly holding at least 95% of the subsidiary, subject to ACD approval; (iii) the IP box regime (80% exemption of qualifying IP income, Article 50ter LIR) if the specific substance conditions are met; and (iv) documentation-driven arm's-length adjustments for ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, which otherwise apply automatically without election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

18%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg-established SARL: registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply unless the small-business exemption applies for annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (excl. VAT). Non-established suppliers have no de minimis threshold for domestic B2C supplies made outside the OSS/IOSS schemes; intra-Union distance sales and qualifying e-services to non-taxable consumers exceed the EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold before OSS registration is required. B2B services supplied to a Luxembourg VAT-registered customer are reverse-charged, so a non-established seller generally need not register for those supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Inbound helpdesk and outbound tele-sales BPO services supplied to a non-EU business customer are deemed supplied at the customer's place of establishment (Article 44 EU VAT Directive, Article 13 of the Luxembourg VAT Law). The supply is therefore outside Luxembourg territorial VAT and gives rise to a full input VAT deduction, producing an effective zero rating. Eligibility requires contractual and documentary evidence that the recipient is a taxable person established outside the EU and that the service is actually performed for that recipient. For physical goods, the 0% export exemption requires the goods to leave the EU within 3 months of supply and proof of export to be compiled within 90 days.

  • Executed BPO Master Services Agreement and Statement of Work
  • Non-EU customer tax residence certificate or equivalent VAT/business registration extract
  • Agent performance logs, call detail records, and service completion reports
  • Commercial invoice and foreign exchange receipt evidence (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice)
  • For ancillary physical exports: customs export declaration, bill of lading or airway bill, and inward remittance certificate

Luxembourg has no mandatory B2B e-invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping/API pre-clearance regime as of 2025. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in PEPPOL/UBL format for invoices issued under public procurement contracts. All tax invoices must contain the mandatory fields of Article 226 of EU Directive 2006/112/EC. For 2025, the standard VAT rate is temporarily 18% with reduced rates of 15%, 9%, and 4%; these revert to 17%, 14%, 8%, and 3% from 1 January 2026.

The AEDT may reopen up to five prior VAT years and reassess any unjustified zero-rated export at the applicable standard rate (18% in 2025) on the gross invoice amount, with input VAT clawed back and late interest accruing at 0.6% per month. Incomplete or contradictory export files are treated as documentation defects and may attract administrative penalties; deliberate misdeclaration or fraudulent documentation can lead to penalties of 50% or more of the tax evaded, criminal fines up to EUR 100,000, and imprisonment. Directors and officers may be held jointly liable where Luxembourg unlawful-act liability rules apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Luxembourg is a Euro-area member and applies zero exchange controls on the EUR; both trade payments and capital movements are free. Profit remittance requires no prior authorization: annual accounts must be approved, the net-asset test under the law of 10 August 1915 must be met, and a 15% domestic dividend withholding tax must be declared (treaty-reduced where applicable). Invoicing in USD, INR or PHP is unrestricted; foreign-currency balances may be retained indefinitely and conversion to EUR is freely executable at market rates. The servicing bank reports cross-border flows to the BCL for statistical purposes only.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion regime exists. A Luxembourg SARL may retain 100% of foreign-currency export and service proceeds in the original currency in domestic or foreign accounts; there is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. The only obligation is statistical: cross-border payments are reported to the BCL by the servicing credit institution under BCL reporting regulations, requiring no action by the company itself.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national monetary authority, operator of TARGET2 liquidity settlement and collector of balance-of-payments statistics; banking prudential supervision and AML/KYC enforcement lie with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Financial Intelligence Unit (Cellule de Renseignement Financier - CRF). Currency policy is governed by Eurosystem rules (TFEU Art. 63) and Council Directive 88/361/EEC; no Luxembourg-specific exchange-control authority imposes convertibility restrictions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for ancillary treasury use; Low as primary banking solution. Wise B2B and comparable EU-authorized EMIs/payment institutions (e.g., Revolut Payments, solaris and other PSD2-passported firms) are accepted for small-ticket cross-border disbursements to BPO subcontractors (INR, PHP, USD) and for fast collections from foreign clients. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and may not hold the statutory SARL share-capital blockage; (ii) funds on an e-money account are safeguarded but are NOT covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL); (iii) the EMI account is not eligible for Commercial LC, TARGET2 or cash-management sweeps; (iv) the main operating ledger, RBE/KYC nexus and statutory audit trail must remain at a fully licensed bank. Recommended policy: cap Wise/EMI settlement at 10% of monthly treasury volume and cascade all balances monthly to the principal EUR account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL incorporation capital (statutory minimum EUR 12,000 subscribed; at least EUR 2,000 paid-up before notarization) is deposited, prior to the notary deed, either with the Luxembourg notary or - preferred in banking practice - on a blocked account ('compte bloqué') opened in the name of the 'SARL en formation'. Routing protocol: SEPA credit transfer (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 from the UBO's account to the blocked account; the remittance information field must carry purpose code EQUI (equity investment) and the notary file reference together with the corporate name; the bank then issues the capital-release confirmation to the notary upon registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés. No exchange-control authorization is required for the inbound injection; the bank records the transaction for BCL balance-of-payments statistics. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate combined top rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT, yielding 18.19%, plus 6.75% municipal business tax); the standalone CIT marginal rate is 17% on taxable income above EUR 175,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; 0% applies where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or Luxembourg participation-exemption conditions are met, generally requiring a holding of at least 10% for 12 uninterrupted months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate Luxembourg statutory Local File or Master File filing threshold exists. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting begins for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. For all related-party transactions, including centralized call-center service fees, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be available on request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead, Article 56 LIR and the EU ATAD anti-abuse rules require genuine economic substance in Luxembourg. A remote BPO SARL must maintain local employees, physical facilities, operational management and real decision-making; otherwise profits may be reallocated and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution with fines up to EUR 100,000 (or higher for aggravated fraud) and imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative recapture of evaded tax with late-payment interest and default penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Form 210 returns or participate in fraudulent invoicing may be personally prosecuted. The corporate veil may be pierced for civil restitution of taxes and social debts; travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment may follow.
Artificial Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence and treaty benefits, reallocation of all taxable profits to the actual place of management, disallowance of intercompany deductions and application of Luxembourg GAAR/ATAD sanctions.

Director personal liability: Nominee and managing directors may be jointly and severally liable for reassessed corporate income tax, municipal business tax and withholding taxes; courts may issue director disqualification, travel bans and civil restitution orders.
Transfer Pricing and Permanent Establishment Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment, reclassification of the shortfall as deemed dividend subject to 15% withholding tax, late-payment interest and penalties on the additional assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for underserved transfer pricing may be held personally liable if the company becomes insolvent; failure to collect or remit withholding tax is separately sanctionable.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Compliance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML fines of up to EUR 500,000 for entities, deregistration from the RCS, freezing of bank accounts and separate administrative penalties for UBO register non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers may be personally fined, banned from company management, and criminally prosecuted for wilful failure to report suspicious transfers; civil confiscation of diverted funds may be ordered.

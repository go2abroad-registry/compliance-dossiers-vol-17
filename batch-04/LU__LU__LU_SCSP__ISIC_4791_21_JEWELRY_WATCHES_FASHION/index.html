Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Luxembourg Business Register / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) — online electronic filing and search portal at https://www.lbr.lu

Name reservation / lock period

Luxembourg does not operate a statutory name-reservation window; availability is tested at filing time. If the RCS rejects the proposed name, the incorporator may immediately submit a revised registration using a new name, or file a formal objection with reasons and supporting trademark/search evidence. A substantive refusal may be appealed before the Luxembourg administrative tribunal (Tribunal administratif) within three months of notification. Legal representation by a Luxembourg avocat is required for the administrative appeal. No extension of a reservation is available because no reservation is issued; the practical remedy is re-filing with an alternative name.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT/indirect taxes; central digital portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Luxembourg national identification number (numéro d'identification) via the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through MyGuichet if it has a registered establishment, branch, or fixed place of business. For VAT purposes, the entity must register with the AEDT VAT department (Service TVA) using the dedicated registration form and supporting corporate documents, proof of identity of managers/partners, a power of attorney if represented, and evidence of Luxembourg VAT nexus. A non-established foreign supplier with no permanent establishment but making taxable Luxembourg supplies may register directly for a VAT number through the VAT department or use the EU OSS scheme for distance sales. An SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes but is separately identifiable and registerable for VAT collection obligations when it carries on an economic activity in its own name.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the SCSp is a special limited partnership with no legal personality and is statutorily tax-transparent; Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax are imposed at the partner level; for VAT purposes the SCSp is treated as a taxable person to the extent it conducts independent economic activity, with the partnership itself liable for VAT remittance.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective entity classification regime exists in Luxembourg. The pass-through characterization of an SCSp is mandatory and derived from partnership law and direct tax law. Luxembourg has implemented EU hybrid mismatch rules via the law of 27 December 2019; where a foreign jurisdiction treats the SCSp as taxable, Luxembourg may adjust or deny deduction rights to prevent double non-taxation. Partners may submit supporting declarations and self-certifications under the hybrid mismatch framework, but no general election is available to convert the SCSp into a corporate taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for established Luxembourg small enterprises; EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold for cross-border B2C e-commerce; non-established foreign suppliers have no registration exemption and must register/use OSS from their first taxable supply in Luxembourg

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union, provided the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documentation. For online sales of jewelry, watches, and fashion accessories to non-EU consumers, zero-rating is claimable when the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf out of the EU. B2B services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place-of-supply rule) and are not treated as zero-rated exports; supporting evidence of customer status and place of establishment is required. The 0% rate is not conditional on FX repatriation under Luxembourg exchange control, but documentary proof of payment strengthens the audit file.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX-A)
  • Commercial invoice and sales order or contract
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of payment from the non-EU buyer
  • Packing list for the exported goods
  • Certificate of Origin or any preferential origin documentation where applicable
  • Delivery or handover evidence showing physical departure from the EU

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control API is currently mandatory in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G contracts via PEPPOL; B2B e-invoicing is permitted and accepted. EU ViDA reforms are expected to introduce structured e-invoicing and digital reporting in the future. The OSS/IOSS regimes are available for distance sales and import consignments, requiring indirect registration through the Luxembourg HMRC-comparable portal rather than domestic real-time clearance.

The AEDT may retrospectively deny zero-rating and reassess Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the full transaction value if export proof is missing, incomplete, or fraudulent. Interest on unpaid VAT accrues from the due date, and administrative penalties can be imposed for incorrect declarations, generally up to 10% of the tax for good-faith errors and higher for gross negligence. Deliberate fraud, forgery of customs or transport evidence, or abusive exploitation of the zero-rating regime may trigger criminal fines up to EUR 12,500 plus imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years under Luxembourg criminal law, joint and several liability of managing partners, and full clawback of any input VAT credits claimed in connection with the export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Luxembourg law: the euro is freely convertible, and no exchange-control authorisation is required for current-account payments (supplier invoices, fees, royalties) or capital-account transactions (equity contributions to the SCSp, partner loans, dividends or liquidation distributions to non-resident partners). Transfer of profits and return of capital are unrestricted, subject solely to AML/KYC documentation and CRF reporting obligations. USD, GBP and CHF proceeds received from Stripe and PayPal settlements may be converted to EUR freely at any Luxembourg-regulated credit institution without BCL notification. Practical barriers are documentary only: source-of-funds evidence for partner capital injections above EUR 10,000, contractual support for every trade disbursement, and sanctions screening of counterparties. No limitation exists on holding multiple currency accounts or maintaining offshore balances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender requirement for export or e-commerce receipts. A Luxembourg SCSp may lawfully retain 100% of foreign-currency revenue indefinitely, hold USD/GBP/CHF sub-accounts, and hedge at its discretion. For treasury discipline, the blueprint recommends an internal policy of converting PSP settlements to EUR within five business days and capping aggregate open foreign-currency exposure at 25% of projected monthly operating costs, unless the partnership's FX risk policy expressly permits a higher natural hedge.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) under the European Central Bank (ECB) umbrella; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Cellule de Renseignements Financiers (CRF) is the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit receiving suspicious transaction reports; the Ministry of Finance coordinates implementation of EU restrictive measures and sanctions. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration authority in Luxembourg; the BCL executes Eurosystem monetary policy and manages official reserves only and does not issue exchange-control licences.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: B+ (High as auxiliary settlement rail; Low-Medium as sole treasury bank). Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions and PSP frameworks suitable for the SCSp include: (i) MANGOPAY S.A., a CSSF-authorised EMI purpose-built for marketplace and platform payouts, well suited to splitting Stripe/PayPal merchant flows, inventory purchases and logistics fee disbursements; (ii) PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. & Cie, S.C.A., a CSSF-supervised credit institution that serves as the legal PSP entity for PayPal merchant settlement in the EEA; (iii) passported EU/EEA EMIs such as Wise Europe S.A. (Belgian NBB supervision), Airwallex (Dutch DNB supervision) and Revolut Payments UAB (Lithuanian BoL supervision), usable for low-cost FX conversion but not CSSF-authorised and without Luxembourg deposit guarantee coverage. Legal constraints under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (PSD2 transposition): e-money balances are not deposits and are not protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (Fonds de Garantie Déposants Luxembourg); EMIs cannot grant credit; and the merchant bears insolvency risk of the EMI. Mitigation protocol: cap aggregate EMI and PSP-pool balances at EUR 100,000, activate automatic daily sweeps to a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank account, and route all supplier disbursements above EUR 25,000 exclusively through bank rails (SEPA or SWIFT MT103).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate standard rate in Luxembourg City (17% national corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax; reduced 15% national rate applies on the first EUR 175,000 of taxable income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions by an SCSp because the partnership is fiscally transparent and distributions are treated as withdrawals of profit by the partners. For comparison, Luxembourg capital companies (S.A./S.à r.l.) apply a statutory dividend withholding tax of 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and the domestic participation exemption if the corporate shareholder holds at least 10% and satisfies the hold-period requirement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Luxembourg taxpayer is required to maintain a Local File and Master File when the group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and the Luxembourg entity's related-party transaction value exceeds EUR 1,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. The e-commerce SCSp will be expected to document cross-border intercompany sales commissions, trademark licences, payment processing fees, and warehouse logistic charges at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: The SCSp must not be a shell or an artificial structure. Although Luxembourg has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable to general commercial partnerships, the SCSp must evidence genuine commercial activity: local strategic decision-making, actual storage or contractual logistics, employees or authorised agents, and operational bank accounts handling the jewellery/watch e-commerce revenue. Where substance is absent, the Luxembourg authorities and foreign tax administrations may apply the OECD GAAR and EU ATAD anti-abuse rules to deny treaty benefits, recharacterise the SCSp as a taxable full corporation, or attribute its profits to an alternative permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 and/or imprisonment of 2 months to 5 years; administrative penalty of 10% of the evaded tax and late-payment interest of 0.6% per month; confiscation of illegally obtained proceeds.

Director personal liability: The general partner/manager of the SCSp is criminally liable if it knowingly signs a false tax return; courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold the manager's personal assets liable for unpaid taxes, seize travel documents, and impose a ban on company management.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment by the Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration plus compensatory interest; a 10% negligence penalty on the under-declared profit; separate administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 for documentation failures.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign distorted intercompany pricing can be jointly liable for the additional tax plus interest; intentional transfer pricing fraud exposes the general partner to criminal penalties and civil restitution; repeated mispricing may support a director disqualification order.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and Luxembourg tax-residence certificate; recharacterisation of the SCSp as a fully taxable Luxembourg entity; application of the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule resulting in additional tax of 25-30% of the reclassified benefit, plus interest and fines.

Director personal liability: The general partner may be personally forced to satisfy the recharacterised tax debts if the corporate form is used as a mere veil for personal trading; travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed where the manager is considered the de facto owner of the passive shell income.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from EUR 1,250 to EUR 1,000,000 or up to 5% of annual turnover for severe AML breaches; criminal liability for money laundering if the non-filing hides the proceeds of fraud, with potential imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The manager is directly punishable for RBE non-filing and AML failures; personal criminal fines, confiscation of bank balances, and a prohibition from managing corporate entities in Luxembourg can be ordered.
Late Filing, Ex Officio Assessment and Unlawful Distribution

Statutory sanction: Ex officio tax assessment by the Luxembourg authorities; administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000; late-payment interest at 7.2% per annum; directors may be jointly liable for taxes arising from illegal distributions made before the tax debt is settled.

Director personal liability: The general partner may be ordered to reimburse the company for distributions made without legal basis; civil and commercial courts can impose a travel ban and extend personal liability to all unpaid taxes and creditors where insolvency was knowingly aggravated.

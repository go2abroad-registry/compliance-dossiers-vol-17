Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Inland Revenue — for corporate income tax, net wealth tax and withholding taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — for VAT (TVA), registration duties and indirect taxes; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) — for commercial registration. Digital filing portals: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu) and the AEDT eTVA/e-filing platform.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SCSp (fiscally transparent partnership): (1) obtain the LBR matricule (12-digit business registration number) via MyGuichet.lu where the SCSp maintains a Luxembourg registered office or establishment; (2) file VAT registration application Form 1024 with the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg national VAT ID — mandatory from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg for non-established suppliers, with no turnover threshold, and appointment of an approved Luxembourg VAT representative required if the SCSp is established outside the EU; (3) apply to the ACD for a Luxembourg tax identification number (TIN) for direct tax filings using the taxpayer registration procedure with the ACD's central taxpayer identification service; partners report their distributive shares using their own TINs; the official legal entity identifier is the LBR matricule for non-tax administrative filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is a fiscal-transparent partnership (sans personnalité juridique) for Luxembourg income tax and net wealth tax purposes; profits pass through to partners and are taxable at partner level (no CIT or net wealth tax at the SCSp level), subject to ATAD hybrid-mismatch and reverse-hybrid anti-abuse rules; for VAT, the SCSp is treated as a separate taxable person in respect of its economic activity. — For a foreign-owned SCSp (fiscally transparent partnership): (1) obtain the LBR matricule (12-digit business registration number) via MyGuichet.lu where the SCSp maintains a Luxembourg registered office or establishment; (2) file VAT registration application Form 1024 with the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg national VAT ID — mandatory from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg for non-established suppliers, with no turnover threshold, and appointment of an approved Luxembourg VAT representative required if the SCSp is established outside the EU; (3) apply to the ACD for a Luxembourg tax identification number (TIN) for direct tax filings using the taxpayer registration procedure with the ACD's central taxpayer identification service; partners report their distributive shares using their own TINs; the official legal entity identifier is the LBR matricule for non-tax administrative filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is a fiscal-transparent partnership (sans personnalité juridique) for Luxembourg income tax and net wealth tax purposes; profits pass through to partners and are taxable at partner level (no CIT or net wealth tax at the SCSp level), subject to ATAD hybrid-mismatch and reverse-hybrid anti-abuse rules; for VAT, the SCSp is treated as a separate taxable person in respect of its economic activity.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law has no general check-the-box regime; fiscal transparency is the statutory default of the SCSp. Available elections: (i) VAT: the SCSp may elect to be a VAT taxable person and may join a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the VAT Law; (ii) US federal tax: the SCSp may file an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election to be classified as a corporation or as a partnership for US purposes; (iii) ATAD reverse-hybrid rule (Law of 21 December 2019): an SCSp controlled by taxpayers in a non-EU jurisdiction that treats it as a taxable entity is recharacterised as a Luxembourg taxable entity and loses pass-through treatment; (iv) commercial/accounting: the partnership deed may govern profit allocation and fiduciary arrangements, and cross-border PE/profit attribution elections may be available under applicable tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (franchise de TVA) for resident small businesses; zero threshold for non-resident/non-established suppliers — VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg; intra-EU distance sales of goods subject to the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold under OSS/IOSS rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate (0%) applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU, subject to proof of export within the legal time limits; (ii) services directly connected with international transport of goods or with exports, including marine cargo surveying and vessel hull damage inspection services supplied to non-EU insurers/underwriters, provided the B2B place-of-supply rules locate the supply outside Luxembourg (customer outside the EU), resulting in zero Luxembourg VAT with the right to deduct input VAT; (iii) services qualifying under Articles 44 and 46 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for international trade. The marine surveyor must hold documentary evidence of the customer's non-EU status (valid VAT registration certificate or equivalent) and of the actual cross-border use or physical export.

  • Customs export declaration (formal/ECS export declaration accepted by Luxembourg customs authorities)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/AWB multimodal transport document) evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank wire confirmation (SWIFT or SEPA) evidencing payment by the non-EU contractual counterparty
  • Signed marine survey and inspection service contract with the non-EU insurer or underwriter
  • Survey report and claim inspection certificate identifying the vessel/cargo, place of survey, consignee and insurer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or general B2B e-invoicing mandate in Luxembourg as of the current framework. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G supplies to Luxembourg public entities (PEPPOL/UBL format routed via MyGuichet.lu/Peppol). VAT returns and EC sales lists must be filed electronically through the AEDT's eTVA portal; API-based filing is available for large taxpayers. Taxpayers must retain a full audit trail for the statutory 10-year record-keeping period. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform will introduce mandatory B2B e-invoicing for intra-EU supplies once implemented.

The AEDT and Luxembourg customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Missing or inconsistent export, transport or FX-repatriation documentation within the 180-day evidence window triggers retrospective reversal of the 0% rate, assessment of unpaid VAT at 17%, default interest of 0.6% per month (Article 36 of the VAT Law), and administrative penalties under Article 43 of the VAT Law (fine of EUR 250–10,000 per offence; higher fines for serious irregularities). Fraudulent or abusive claims — false declarations, forged bills of lading, artificial routing schemes, or sham non-EU counterparties — constitute tax fraud punishable under Luxembourg criminal tax law with imprisonment (up to 5 years), fines of up to EUR 50,000, publication of the judgment, and debarment from tax clearance certificates. The limitation period extends to 5 years (10 years in fraud cases), and EU mutual assistance, DAC6 exchange and joint cross-border customs/VAT controls enable retrospective clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg does not operate exchange controls and applies the EU free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU. Current-account payments, including cross-border marine survey fees and reinsurance disbursements, and capital-account flows, including partner contributions, advances, and liquidation distributions, are fully convertible into EUR, USD, GBP, and other freely tradable currencies. No central bank conversion permit, transfer approval, or profit-remittance authorization is required for the SCSp. Profit distribution is subject to no exchange-control hurdle, but a Luxembourg paying bank will require documented distributable reserves, partner approval formalities, and standard AML/KYC confirmation of final beneficial owners. The practical compliance friction is not convertibility but evidentiary proof of source of funds and underlying economic substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds, including earnings from marine cargo and vessel hull survey services supplied to foreign insurers and underwriters. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency in the SCSp's bank accounts, freely reinvested, or converted in the spot or forward market. BCL statistics-reporting requirements may apply to resident entities for cross-border service receipts and capital flows, but such statistical reporting is not a repatriation or conversion control.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW as the primary treasury rail, MEDIUM as a supplemental outward-payment channel. An offshore digital EMI that is not authorized in the EEA cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Luxembourg-resident SCSp under the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 and the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004, as amended. An EEA-licensed EMI may passport into Luxembourg through the CSSF/BCL notification regime, but the bank will subject the SCSp to full CDD and may decline to treat the EMI as the main settlement account because of corporate treasury, waiver, and closing-risk concerns. For low-value 'Underwriter_Wire' and Wise-type supplier reimbursements, an EEA-authorized EMI can be a practical supplementary rail after the primary Luxembourg bank account is active and the treasury policy explicitly defines permissible EMI transaction limits and reconciliation controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital contributions should be routed over SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances from a partner or parent treasury, or over MT202 for interbank funding where the remitter is a financial institution. Euro contributions from an EEA partner may use a SEPA Credit Transfer to the SCSp's Luxembourg IBAN. The remittance narrative must identify the commercial purpose as 'Capital contribution to Luxembourg SCSp', 'Subscription of partnership interests', or 'Partner advance per partnership agreement'. Where a SWIFT purpose code is required, the sender should apply category B301 for contributions to corporate capital and B304 for intra-group partner loans; the receiving bank will require a pre-funded Capital Escrow Account, a copy of the capital call, RBE registration evidence, notarized partnership documents, and completion of partner KYC before the funds can be released to the operational account. The BCL's balance-of-payments statistics reporting must capture the transaction, but no regulatory pre-approval is required.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade and operational disbursements are SWIFT MT103 for direct invoice settlements and reimburseable survey expenses, SWIFT MT202/COV for USD bank-to-bank settlement chains, SEPA Credit Transfers in EUR for payments within the European Economic Area, and documentary commercial letters of credit issued or confirmed under UCP600 when the transaction involves procurement of vessel spares, repair services, or equipment subject to LC terms. For low-value, non-bankable counterparty payments, an EEA-authorized EMI such as Wise Business may be used after the principal Luxembourg bank account is operational, subject to a documented treasury policy limiting EMI exposure and requiring complete underlying invoice matching. The bank will require a signed disbursement instruction from the authorized manager, underlying contract or invoice, sanctions screening of the payee, and, where applicable, evidence that the payee is the true beneficial owner of the settlement amount.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards denominated in EUR, USD, GBP, and other G10 currencies to hedge underwriter premium receipts and claims disbursements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or restricted-deliverable currencies where the bank and client establish a genuine commercial exposure and the transaction complies with EMIR and CSSF hedging eligibility standards.Cross-currency swaps for longer-tenor partner advances, capital subscription obligations, or liability-driven matches between the SCSp's foreign-currency receivables and liquidity commitments.

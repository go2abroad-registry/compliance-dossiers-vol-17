Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law on Commercial Companies of 10 August 1915), as amended, Articles 102-1 to 102-13 (SCSp special limited partnership regime introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013 implementing AIFMD), supplemented by the Law of 12 July 2013 on Alternative Investment Fund Managers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Contrat de société / Acte de société (Partnership Agreement / Articles of Association), executed either as a notarial deed (acte notarié) or a private deed (acte sous seing privé) and filed electronically with the RCS via the LBR e-filing portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 41 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; no escrow or blocked-account requirement. The partnership agreement freely determines capital and contributions; prudent practice reserves a nominal working capital (e.g., EUR 1,000), and the private bank may require a funding comfort letter for account opening.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain the commune's occupation authorization and fire safety certificate under municipal building regulations; complete workplace clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under Articles L. 312-1 et seq. of the Labour Code, including emergency exits, first-aid equipment, and annual workplace risk assessment in accordance with the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 25 July 2006.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; no escrow or blocked-account requirement. The partnership agreement freely determines capital and contributions; prudent practice reserves a nominal working capital (e.g., EUR 1,000), and the private bank may require a funding comfort letter for account opening.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; online portals: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SCSp, the general partner or a Luxembourg-resident approved statutory representative must first file the partnership's incorporation deed and amendments with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns a national taxpayer identification number (matricule). The AEDT allocates a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number upon filing VAT registration form (Formulaire 640/645) through MyGuichet.lu. Non-resident partners do not require a Luxembourg tax ID solely for holding partnership interests, but the SCSp must provide the beneficial owner declarations under the RBE register and maintain a Luxembourg registered address.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a foreign-owned SCSp, the general partner or a Luxembourg-resident approved statutory representative must first file the partnership's incorporation deed and amendments with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns a national taxpayer identification number (matricule). The AEDT allocates a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number upon filing VAT registration form (Formulaire 640/645) through MyGuichet.lu. Non-resident partners do not require a Luxembourg tax ID solely for holding partnership interests, but the SCSp must provide the beneficial owner declarations under the RBE register and maintain a Luxembourg registered address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a general US-style check-the-box system. An SCSp is statutorily tax transparent by default and, as a general rule, is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax; its partners are taxed directly on their allocated share of income. Targeted elective alignment measures are available under the Luxembourg hybrid-mismatch rules implementing EU ATAD, allowing a taxpayer to file an election with the ACD to align Luxembourg characterization with a foreign jurisdiction's characterization in order to eliminate double non-taxation or double deduction outcomes. Where contrary classification is proven, the tax authorities may require a binding ruling before recognizing transparency for treaty purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for non-established foreign taxable persons; every taxable supply of goods or services in Luxembourg requires VAT registration from the first transaction. EU distance sales of goods threshold is EUR 10,000 annual across all EU Member States. Luxembourg-established small undertakings are exempt below EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover, but this exemption is not generally available to foreign entities making Luxembourg supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier obtains and retains documentary proof of export within the statutory time limit (normally by the end of the following month, with extension possibilities up to 90 days). Zero-rating also applies to certain intra-Community dispatches to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State, subject to the customer's valid VAT ID and inclusion in the recapitulative statement. Services supplied by an SCSp that is acting as a taxable person and whose place of supply is outside Luxembourg (e.g., services to non-EU business counterparties under the general B2B 'place-of-business' rule) are treated as zero-rated for Luxembourg VAT purposes, provided the supplier obtains sufficient documentary evidence of the non-resident status and business use of the recipient. Investment management and family-office advisory services provided to non-EU clients may fall under resource-of-service exceptions; a detailed written analysis and holding of contemporaneous evidence is required in every case.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading

Luxembourg currently imposes no domestic real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping for B2B e-invoices. E-invoicing is permitted where authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability are guaranteed, typically through EDI, PEPPOL, or qualified electronic signatures. E-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to Luxembourg public-sector entities using the Factur-X or UBL format. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu. No SAF-T filing obligation is in force for ordinary taxpayer groups. Luxembourg is actively monitoring the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposal, which may introduce real-time transaction reporting in future years.

The Luxembourg VAT authorities, through the AEDT, conduct targeted post-export audits and cross-checks with customs records. If zero-rating is claimed without valid export proof, the tax may be clawed back retroactively with VAT under-declaration interest at the statutory rate (currently 9% per annum or such rate as adjusted by the Luxembourg Parliament) and an administrative fine. Intentional or repeated fraudulent zero-rating, including false invoicing, false customs declarations, or sham export document fabrication, incurs penal surcharges of up to 200% of the VAT evaded, criminal fines, and imprisonment for responsible officers under Articles 150 et seq. of the Luxembourg VAT Law. In addition, the AEDT may impose joint liability on the supplier, the customs agent, and the managing partner and may re-open closed assessments for ten years in cases of tax fraud or use of abusive arrangements. The inward remittance certificate, banking flows, bill of lading, and customs export declaration must be consistent; any inconsistency is treated as prima facie evidence of simulated export and triggers full clawback with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full capital and current-account convertibility under the euro-area framework and EU Treaty freedoms (TFEU Articles 63-66). There are no exchange-control restrictions, prior authorisation requirements, surrender duties, or repatriation ceilings for resident or non-resident investors. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, partnership distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred within the EU/EEA and converted into any currency at commercial bank rates without BCL or CSSF approval. The only practical constraints are bank-level AML/KYC documentary requirements; no statutory convertibility rationing applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation. For an SCSp investment-holding entity, all euro and foreign-currency receipts may be retained in the original currency, remitted offshore, or converted freely at market rates. The BCL SHSS statistical reporting regime applies only to direct cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month or 50 transactions per year, and is purely statistical, carrying no conversion consequence.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and under European Central Bank (ECB) oversight; foreign exchange regulatory and statistical-reporting functions are shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (CSSF passport under PSD2) and EEA EMIs can technically provide SEPA and limited SWIFT capabilities, but family-office-grade structuring, securities custody, deposits at central banks, and sophisticated FX execution require a full credit institution. UK EMIs lost EU passporting post-Brexit and would require CSSF licensing or an EEA branch. EMIs cannot hold client securities, cannot offer MiFID investment services, and their safeguarding obligations do not provide deposit-guarantee protection; therefore they are unsuitable for SCSp capital deposits or investment clearing, and may serve only as pre-funding gateway accounts with enhanced governance constraints.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For euro-area contributors: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer directly to the SCSp's segregated EUR operational/escrow account; no FX purpose code is required intra-EEA, but the reference field must identify the partnership and the capital contribution and, for amounts above EUR 12,500 or 50 transactions annually, the BCL SHSS statistical purpose code for 'investment holdings / direct investment contributions' must be reported. For non-EEA flows (e.g., Switzerland, United Kingdom, Asia, GCC): SWIFT MT103 to the Luxembourg beneficiary bank, showing the SCSp as beneficiary, the family trust or foundation as remitter, IMF/BPM6-compatible purpose code for 'direct investment equity contribution' (ECB BoP classification code 5001/5002), and a free-format description: 'Equity contribution to Luxembourg SCSp investment holding vehicle (ISIC 6430)'. Verification requires documented source of funds, signed investment mandate, UBO attestation, execution of the bank's CDD file, and, where the contribution exceeds the bank's internal large-exposure notification threshold, an optional advance notice. No exchange-control approval is required.Clearing rails are selected by counterparty jurisdiction and instrument type: (i) intra-EEA obligations and fee payments: SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Direct Debit; (ii) non-EUR global disbursements and investment subscriptions: SWIFT MT103/MT202 cover payments with repayment purpose codes matching the underlying equity, loan, or capital-call instrument; (iii) private-equity capital-call funding: irrevocable transaction-matched SWIFT MT103 or standby/commercial letter of credit issued by the Luxembourg settling bank on behalf of the SCSp; (iv) physical-trade instruments where the family office holds operating assets: documentary and standby letters of credit under UCP 600 and URDG 758, with bill-of-lading verification by the negotiating bank; (v) securities settlement and custody-related cash movements: TARGET2-Securities (T2S) euro settlement via the custody bank, with same-day FX executed via SWIFT MT300 confirmation for non-euro trades. All outbound payments are matched against underlying contract value dates to eliminate daylight overdrafts and FX settlement risk.FX fixed-date and window forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP, and liquid EMEA/APAC crosses) documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA;Currency-option strategies: vanilla European-style FX options with upfront premium, bought zero-cost collars, and risk-reversal structures for downside protection on unrealised investment exposures;Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or illiquid currencies (offshore CNH, BRL, INR, RUB) where physical settlement is prohibited or impractical, executed with a bank counterparty under ISDA definitions;FX swaps (swap-outright combinations) for short-term liquidity alignment across value dates without creating a notional credit facility;Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) for long-term structural alignment of EUR-based family-office financing against USD or other-currency investment portfolios, including mark-to-market and collateral posting via CSDs;Currency-index swaps and payment-currency-swapped equity derivatives limited strictly to risk-management purposes, recorded at fair value through the family office's treasury ledger.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City composite rate: 17.00% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and 6.75% municipal business tax; municipal rates vary between approximately 20.85% and 27.04%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Luxembourg resident capital company. Distributions from a transparent SCSp to its partners do not trigger Luxembourg withholding tax at the SCSp level. If an underlying Luxembourg operating company in the family-office structure pays dividends to the SCSp, the payer must withhold 15% unless a treaty or the Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the rate to 0%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a corporate taxpayer with a tax year ending 31 December, the income tax return is statutorily due by 31 March of the following year. Where the return is filed electronically through a registered Luxembourg tax adviser, the ACD grants an automatic extension to 30 September of the following year. Further extensions may be granted on reasoned request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the obligation to attach the Luxembourg Transfer Pricing Schedule (Form 200F). The full Local File is required for relevant taxpayers above the same EUR 100,000 threshold; the Master File is generally required for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 50 million; CbC Report filings apply above EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a standalone statute; however, the ACD enforces substance requirements through the general anti-abuse rule, the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, the EU ATAD, and the doctrine of substance over form. A single-family office SCSp must have real management in Luxembourg, substantive local decisions, an office or contractual outsourcing arrangement with genuine service providers, local bank accounts, and adequate records to evidence beneficial ownership; otherwise it may be recharacterised as an opaque shell, denied treaty benefits, or regarded as a permanent establishment of the non-resident family.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Article 426 LIR and general criminal law: imprisonment from 1 to 5 years; criminal fine from EUR 5,000 to EUR 250,000; administrative tax increase of up to 50% to 100% of the evaded tax; monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%; exclusion from subsidies and public contracts in certain cases.

Director personal liability: General partners, managing directors and tax representatives who caused or approved the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Personal assets may be seized, directors can be subject to travel bans, and criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment, disqualification from board service, and international extradition proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the SCSp as an opaque resident entity or as a permanent establishment of the parent family/principal; denial of treaty and EU-directive relief; reassessment of Luxembourg income and net wealth tax with penalties up to 100%; loss of the participation exemption and net-wealth tax relief.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is possible where the SCSp is a fictitious contract used to commit fraud or evade mandatory law. The general partner and directors may be held personally liable for company liabilities and unpaid taxes if they have knowingly allowed an abuse of the corporate form; in insolvency, directors may face clawback actions and civil damages.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to the arm's-length margin; interest on underpaid tax at 0.6% per month; penalty for non-disclosure or late filing of up to EUR 250,000; loss of access to mutual agreement procedure protections where documentation is materially incomplete; risk of secondary adjustment and constructive dividend treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a non-arm's-length price can be liable for breach of fiduciary duty to the partnership and, in the event of fraudulent pricing, for restitution of the tax deficiency. Criminal liability attaches where transfer pricing is used as a sham to conceal diversion of profits.
AML / KYC / CRS / FATCA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended): CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 2.5 million or 10% of the entity's annual turnover; penal sanctions for directors and managers up to EUR 1.25 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; deregistration or closure of the vehicle; prohibition from conducting financial-sector activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be personally fined, disqualified, and criminally prosecuted for money-laundering facilitation. In cases where tax evasion by the family is channeled through the SCSp, the AML conviction can trigger restitution orders, freezing orders, and travel restrictions against the responsible individuals.

Competent tax authority

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp is by default fiscally transparent for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes. In limited circumstances, a Luxembourg partnership may elect corporate taxation under Article 175 of the Loi de l'impôt sur le revenu (LIR) if it fulfills capital-type conditions, but this election is generally unavailable for a Special Limited Partnership and is subject to strict anti-abuse review. The EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules apply to prevent arbitrage between Luxembourg transparency and foreign entity classification.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
17%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for Luxembourg-established businesses; non-established foreign suppliers are generally required to register from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg unless an EU-derived EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold applies to specific remote supplies. An aircraft engine dry leasing company whose services are subject to B2B place-of-supply rules outside Luxembourg may have no Luxembourg VAT registration obligation.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating applies to exports of goods where the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third party to a destination outside the EU and the export is evidenced within the legal deadline (generally 90 days using an EU Single Administrative Document / MRN). For aircraft engine dry leasing, which is treated as a supply of services for VAT purposes, Luxembourg VAT is not chargeable where the customer is established outside the EU (place of supply outside Luxembourg). Where the customer is an EU taxable person, the reverse charge applies in the customer's member state. The lessor may claim input VAT recovery if the leasing services are used for its taxable activities, subject to standard Luxembourg pro-rata rules.

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government API stamping requirement, and no Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) for standard commercial VAT invoices. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to Luxembourg public sector entities (B2G), which must comply with the PEPPOL/EN 16931 format. B2B e-invoicing is optional and must be accepted by the recipient if used; invoices must be archived in accessible form for audit purposes.

      
The AEDT may retrospectively disallow unjustified zero-rating, reassess VAT at the standard rate, and charge interest from the date the VAT would have fallen due. Civil fines may be up to the amount of tax evaded, and intentional fraud or falsification of export evidence is punishable under Luxembourg criminal tax law by fines and imprisonment. The statute of limitation for VAT assessment is generally 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraudulent or forged-document cases. In a cross-border aircraft leasing context, tax authorities will closely scrutinise genuine substance, commercial purpose, lease payment repatriation, and the documentary chain of physical export or place-of-supply determination.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes, with no approval quotas, license windows, or priority allocations for aircraft-engine lease rentals. Capital account transactions are unrestricted under TFEU Article 63 for EU and most third-country counterparties; no exchange-rate or reinvestment conditions attach to non-resident ownership of an SCSp. Profit remittance and capital distribution are not subject to any Luxembourg exchange-control authorization scheme; the only practical hurdles are bank-level evidence of legal ownership and compliance: the SCSp must confirm its fiscally transparent status, file beneficial-owner data with the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (LBR/RBE), and demonstrate economic substance if the leasing activity is managed from Luxembourg. Cross-border payment instructions require correct BCL statistical classification codes (BPM6 direct investment / services), and funds can be retained abroad in USD or EUR; no forced conversion to EUR applies. For lessees in sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions, convertibility is de facto limited by EU and UN sanctions compliance, not by currency regulation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Luxembourg has no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export or lease receipts. The aircraft-engine rental cash flows (typically USD from non-resident airlines) may be kept overseas or brought into Luxembourg at the operator's discretion, subject only to OECD transfer-pricing documentation and the SCSp's substance requirements. The operational equivalent of an export-surrender rule is the BCL/LUX 2011 statistical reporting regime, under which resident credit institutions must report every cross-border payment equal or above EUR 12,500 (interrelated transfers are aggregated) to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, with the relevant purpose code for aviation rentals. Additionally, under EU Regulation 833/2014 as amended and related CFSP decisions, any engine leasing linked to Russian or Belarusian parties is prohibited and must be notified to the competent authorities; there is no corresponding weapons- or aviation-export surrender system, so the compliance burden rests on sanctions screening and contract clauses requiring payment through a first-class EU bank.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the monetary authority for statistics, payment systems (TARGET2 participant), and foreign-exchange reserve operations within the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for all banks and payment institutions in Luxembourg. The euro-area framework (ECB regulations, TFEU Article 63 on free movement of capital, and EU Regulation 2018/1672 on cash controls) means that no separate foreign-exchange licensing authority exists for enterprise FX transactions. The BCL, together with the CSSF, enforces balance-of-payments reporting, sanctions, and conduct obligations that operate as the functional equivalent of exchange-control administration in Luxembourg.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: LOW for the primary treasury function of engine-leasing, and MEDIUM only for incidental disbursements under EUR 10,000. The legal constraints under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (MIFID/MPS transposition) and the Law of 1 August 2007 (E-Money Directive 2) prohibit electronic money institutions from granting credit, holding escrow funds for third parties, or providing a full commercial-banking currency envelope; an offshore EMI (UK FCA, Lithuanian, Maltese, or scoped-payment institutions) cannot open a proper 'capital escrow' account in Luxembourg because escrow-receiving and release functions are reserved to licensed credit institutions. In addition, the SCSp must maintain a settlement entity in the EEA to avoid creating a non-EEA transfer route that banks will reject on correspondent-banking grounds; use of an overseas EMI increased AML risk-weighting and exposes the general partner to breach of the Luxembourg 'bank secrecy plus banking-license boundary'. A local fintech such as an ACPR-licensed payment institution can be used as a payment conduit for maintenance parts, hangar fees, and crew-related refunds, but the main rental colletion and capital-call flows must remain on a Tier-1 bank account with SWIFT-grade messaging and LC capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For initial capitalization, the investors wire funds via SWIFT MT103 (beneficiary: LU_SCSP) to the account of the Luxembourg operating bank, referencing the SCSp LEI and the transaction purpose code for direct-investment equity (BPM6 category DI/equity). The bank executes the credit in EUR through T2 for EUR wires, or in USD via its correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS) then an internal FX conversion at the contractual rate, without prior authorization from the BCL. In-kind contributions (the turbofan engine itself) cannot be 'wired' but will be recognized through a step-in/novation agreement; the capital-entry accounting is registered by the GP in the SCSp accounts, and the bank will require a copy of the commercial invoice from the transferor to verify that the engine title has passed. The capital injection must be traceable from the investor's own bank account with no mixing of third-party funds; the bank will aggregate transfers from the same investor within 5 business days for BCL reporting and will reject 'back-to-back' capital of less than 10 days standing. If the capital is injected in USD and converted into EUR for the initial funding, the FX leg is executed by the relationship desk at the same-day spot rate; no CSSF license is required. For a capital injection of more than EUR 10 million the bank will also request a structural subordination letter and the GP's confirmation that no other lender has a prior claim to the contribution.Operational disbursements for engine rent, maintenance, and lease deposits are cleared via SWIFT MT101/MT103 with mandatory narrative purposes consistent with the BCL code. For a dry-lease engine, the majority of payments will be: (i) monthly rent to the lessee's order—cleared through SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or MT202 (batch payment) via the Luxembourg bank's correspondent; (ii) maintenance-reserve calls to the engine MRO—ideally processed as a commercial LC (UCP600) issued by the same Tier-1 Luxembourg bank, with the SCSp as applicant and the MRO as beneficiary; (iii) insurance premium payments to the aviation underwriter—cleared by the bank's direct debit or telegraphic transfer; and (iv) repayment of senior debt—executed by the facility agent via MT202. For EUR outflows the clearing rail is T2; for USD outflows it is the bank's interbank clearing through its Fedwire/CHIPS account; a payment to a sanctioned or restricted destination will be refused even if the funds are in USD and the destination is a non-U.S. bank. The bank will accept commercial LC issuance only against the SCSp's assignment of lease rentals to the facility lender; a documentary package (lease, engine serial number, auxiliary power unit hours, maintenance status) must be submitted 5 business days before the issuance request. For any disbursement above EUR 1 million representing a lease-security deposit or purchase-price payment, the bank requires either a second-signatory approval from the GP and the investor, or an executed and authentic escrow release instruction if the funds are held in the séquestre account; the release instruction must reference the specific engine purchase agreement and the escrow account number.EUR/USD outright FX forward contracts (plain vanilla) with tenors matching the monthly or quarterly lease-rental cash flow, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and cleared through the Luxembourg bank's treasury desk; no FX-license requirement, but EMIR and MIFID II eligibility must be verified for the SCSp as an eligible counterparty.EUR/USD FX swaps (roll-over and overnight parking) for the liquidity management of USD rental receipts and EUR disbursements, with collateral postings under a signed CSA or initial margin under EMIR for a non-cleared swap above the EUR 8 billion clearing threshold (bank-level threshold; SCSp qualifies as below-threshold).Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CRS) converting USD floating-rate lease income or USD-denominated debt into EUR fixed or floating exposure (e.g., USD SOFR to EUR €STR), permissible for the SCSp as a hedging instrument directly related to the engine-leasing receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging against a non-convertible or restricted lessee currency (e.g., RUB, TRY, NGN) only where the lessee's base currency is non-convertible; supported by the same ISDA/CSA documentation and subject to Luxembourg enterprise sanctions; no NDF is permitted if the underlying lease is disallowed under EU 833/2014.Zero-cost collar / butterfly structures on USD/EUR to cap conversion costs while retaining participation in favorable EUR depreciation, permitted only as an overlay to the lease receivable and with bi-annual compliance review by the bank's risk office.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for the primary treasury function of engine-leasing, and MEDIUM only for incidental disbursements under EUR 10,000. The legal constraints under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (MIFID/MPS transposition) and the Law of 1 August 2007 (E-Money Directive 2) prohibit electronic money institutions from granting credit, holding escrow funds for third parties, or providing a full commercial-banking currency envelope; an offshore EMI (UK FCA, Lithuanian, Maltese, or scoped-payment institutions) cannot open a proper 'capital escrow' account in Luxembourg because escrow-receiving and release functions are reserved to licensed credit institutions. In addition, the SCSp must maintain a settlement entity in the EEA to avoid creating a non-EEA transfer route that banks will reject on correspondent-banking grounds; use of an overseas EMI increased AML risk-weighting and exposes the general partner to breach of the Luxembourg 'bank secrecy plus banking-license boundary'. A local fintech such as an ACPR-licensed payment institution can be used as a payment conduit for maintenance parts, hangar fees, and crew-related refunds, but the main rental colletion and capital-call flows must remain on a Tier-1 bank account with SWIFT-grade messaging and LC capability.

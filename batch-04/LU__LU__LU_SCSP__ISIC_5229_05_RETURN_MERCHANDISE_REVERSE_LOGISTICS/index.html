Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); registration duty collected by the Recette d'enregistrement of the Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AED).

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), SCSp chapter as introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013 and restated by the Law of 23 June 2016; Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 September 2016 on the RCS; Law of 19 December 2008 on the electronic register and e-filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

RCS electronic incorporation declaration (Formulaire C — SCSp) filed on the lbr.lu e-filing portal, accompanied by the executed partnership agreement (contrat de société), identity documents of the associé gérant and partners, and proof of registered office.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 5 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; zero or flexible capital permitted. No escrow account, capital deposit certificate, or bank attestation is required by company law. Contributions are allocated to individual capital accounts in the partnership agreement, and paid-up amounts are subject only to AML/KYC verification at bank account opening.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration, responsible for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and the national business matricule; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Registration, Estates and VAT Administration, competent for VAT registration, VAT returns, export-evidence verification and VAT refunds. Digital access: MyGuichet.lu (single e-government gateway), the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS commercial-register filings, and the ACD/AEDT electronic filing portals (eCDF / MyGuichet secure workspace) for tax returns and correspondence.

E-government filing portal

Step-by-step for a Luxembourg SCSp and its foreign co-investors: (1) notarised constitution of the SCSp followed by registration with the RCS (Luxembourg Business Registers), which automatically generates the national business matricule (the identification number used by the ACD for direct-tax purposes and by the AEDT for VAT matching); (2) application to the AEDT for the Luxembourg VAT identification number before commencing taxable trade, submitted via MyGuichet.lu using the official VAT-registration form (formulaire 640) accompanied by the RCS excerpt, the partnership agreement and the identity of the general partner; (3) a non-resident enterprise without a Luxembourg establishment (e.g., foreign principal or logistics operator) carrying out taxable supplies or services in Luxembourg must obtain a Luxembourg VAT number or apply the domestic reverse-charge where available; if established outside the EU, it must appoint a Luxembourg joint-and-severally liable VAT representative and file an irrevocable power of attorney; (4) after documentary vetting — no minimum capital, no licence and no guarantee are required for this low-risk reverse-logistics scope — the AEDT issues the VAT number with the LU prefix; (5) the SCSp then files periodic VAT returns through eCDF and may apply for VAT refunds, including under the special EU refund mechanism for non-established taxable persons.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is a fiscally transparent partnership by operation of law; no election is needed for pass-through direct-tax treatment. Luxembourg nevertheless provides several statutory mechanisms: (a) an implicit VAT election — the partnership can be treated as an independent taxable person for VAT where it conducts an entrepreneurial activity, allowing the SCSp to centralise VAT registration, invoicing and input-VAT recovery; (b) the small-business franchise option to remain outside VAT below the EUR 30,000 turnover threshold, or to waive the exemption by optional registration; (c) the VAT-grouping election (assujetti unique) combining the SCSp with affiliated taxable persons into a single taxable person; (d) the EU-wide One-Stop-Shop and Import One-Stop-Shop elections for distance sales and import transactions; and (e) the hybrid-mismatch alignment and neutralisation rules transposing ATAD and the anti-hybrid Directive, under which a partner's interest in a transparent SCSp may be re-profiled where a foreign jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque; such arrangements must be disclosed in the Luxembourg corporate tax return. Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box system and there is no separate municipal registration beyond the single national tax identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General mandatory VAT registration threshold under the small-business franchise: annual net taxable turnover of EUR 30,000 (in force from 1 January 2025; previously EUR 25,000). Businesses below the threshold may voluntarily register and thereby deduct input VAT; exceeding the threshold terminates the franchise. For cross-border distance sales of goods, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 OSS threshold applies. A non-established foreign vendor has no domestic turnover threshold and must register for all Luxembourg taxable supplies, except where the place-of-supply rule allocates the supply to another EU Member State, the customer is liable under the reverse charge, or the sale is declared under the OSS/IOSS special schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (1) export supplies of goods — including returned merchandise, quality-graded goods, repaired or refurbished outputs, and recyclable scrap — where the goods are dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the EU customs territory; the supplier must be identified as exporter or hold the export mandate, and proof of departure from the EU must exist no later than the filing of the relevant VAT return; (2) ancillary services connected with exported goods (handling, packing, inspection, quality grading, testing, repair, freight and insurance) rendered in Luxembourg to the non-EU owner or non-EU customer, provided the supporting evidence links each service to the specific exported goods; (3) intra-Community supplies of goods to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State, which are exempt with credit (equivalent to zero-rating) against the customer's valid EU VAT number and compliant dispatch documentation; and (4) B2B services supplied to non-EU recipients that fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the general customer-location place-of-supply rule. The zero-rate is strictly construed; identical services supplied to a Luxembourg customer are taxable at 17%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/AES declaration in the ECS-2 environment, with validated exit confirmation from the EU customs office of exit), naming the SCSp as exporter or the principal party;
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR or rail consignment note (combined transport document) evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg and arrival outside the EU customs territory;
  • Commercial invoice together with the reverse-logistics file: return-merchandise authorisation, warehouse receipt, quality-grading certificate, repair/refurbishment work order or scrap/disposal certificate proving commercial substance;
  • Payment and inward remittance evidence: bank transfer records, SWIFT reference, or Airwallex/Local ACH transaction reports demonstrating receipt of the export consideration and its repatriation where applicable;
  • EC sales list / Intrastat filing proof and the customer's EU VAT identification number where the supply is intra-Community;
  • Any sampling, valuation or customs-supervision documentation required by the customs office during post-clearance audit.

Luxembourg imposes no real-time fiscal pre-clearance, no tax-authority invoice stamping and no mandatory B2B/B2C continuous transaction controls. The operative mandates are: (i) B2G e-invoicing through the national PEPPOL access point under the EN 16931 standard for public-procurement contracts; (ii) VAT-law requirements on invoice content, sequential numbering, issue dates and preservation of originals; (iii) the GOBD formal data-management obligations (ACD guidance of 2021) covering archiving, immutability, chronological traceability and accessibility of electronic books and source documents for audit; and (iv) electronic filing of all VAT returns, EC sales lists and Intrastat declarations through the AEDT portal on the statutory monthly or quarterly cycle. The AEDT therefore performs periodic, risk-based clearance through fraud-detection analytics rather than immediate clearance of each invoice.

Luxembourg applies a strict evidentiary standard to export zero-rating; every claim must be substantiated at the time the VAT return is filed and retained for ten years under GOBD rules. Penal consequences include: (i) recharacterisation of the supply as domestic taxable at 17% with clawback of VAT plus statutory late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) from the original filing date; (ii) administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 per offence for false or missing export evidence, invoicing irregularities, or refusal to produce the export file; (iii) criminal prosecution for deliberate tax fraud (fraude fiscale under Luxembourg criminal tax law and the Criminal Code), exposing the general partner and, given the transparency of the SCSp, the limited partners to fines of up to EUR 250,000 and custodial penalties of one to five years; (iv) application of the anti-abuse doctrine and Luxembourg arm's-length rules where the transaction lacks commercial substance (e.g., circular trade, fictitious returns, switched bills of lading), with reversal of the burden of proof and increased sanctions; and (v) freezing or set-off of VAT refunds, suspension of the VAT identification number, and publication of sanctions. Repatriation and export evidence must be completed within 180 days of the underlying export, failing which a discretionary clawback procedure may be opened.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full current and capital account convertibility under the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. No restrictions apply to foreign direct investment, profit remittance, repatriation of capital, or liquidation proceeds for EU/EEA and most third-country investors, provided the underlying transaction is documented, the UBO chain is transparent, and no sanctions or anti-money-laundering concerns exist. Payments may be made in EUR or any reserve currency; conversion is freely undertaken by credit institutions at prevailing market rates. Remittance of distributions from an SCSp to non-resident partners is treated as a pass-through profit share and is not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, but a beneficiary self-declaration and Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE) extract should be provided.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in Luxembourg. Export receipts from the reverse-logistics return of goods may be credited directly to a EUR or foreign-currency account. If a bank receipt is in a third-country currency, the foreign currency may be kept or converted at the entity's option. For statistical reporting, payments exceeding thresholds are declared to the BCL via the reporting agent (the bank) using the EUR/ECA payment code for services or trade; no conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) jointly regulate banking and treasury activity; Luxembourg operates within the European System of Central Banks, with the European Central Bank setting monetary policy and the euro as lawful tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for near-term payment processing through EU/EEA licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, but limited to payment initiation and e-money safeguarding. EMIs cannot grant credit, provide derivative hedging, or maintain fiduciary deposit accounts. Legal constraints require the EMI to hold funds in a segregated safeguarding account at a Luxembourg or EU-licensed credit institution; the SCSp should therefore keep its main capital base in a tier-1 credit institution and use the EMI only as a disbursement/collection rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound route: (a) SEPA Instant / SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for shareholders' capital contributions up to €100,000 per transaction; (b) SWIFT MT103 for larger or non-EUR contributions, with the beneficiary's BIC (Luxembourg BICXXX), IBAN, and exact legal name 'LU SCSp'. Payment instruction must reference the capital contribution notice, the SCSp's RBE number, and, for statistical purposes, a purpose code that the receiving bank maps to 'foreign direct investment capital contribution' (e.g., Luxembourg BCL balance-of-payments code for equity capital; if unsure, require the bank's regulatory reporting team to attach the correct EBA/BCL purpose code). For EUR wires from outside the eurozone, TARGET2 settlement is used, and the sender's bank must include the ISO 20022 structured remittance tag or SWIFT field 70 containing 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SCSP / [entity reference]'.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT – Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority) for VAT; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA – Customs and Excise Administration) for customs. Unified e-gateway: myGuichet.lu; VAT filing portal: eTVA; business registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS).

E-government filing portal

(1) Foreign entity must first obtain a Luxembourg RCS registration if it establishes a branch or uses the SCSp vehicle, via the LBR electronic portal (notarial deed or private deed filed within one month of formation); (2) upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns the 13-digit national identification number (matricule) for income tax purposes; (3) for VAT, the non-established taxable person must file Form 704 with the AEDT (via eTVA/myGuichet.lu), supplying proof of foreign business identity, EORI number, foreign VAT/TIN certificate and, unless OSS is used, appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT; (4) the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA LU) without any capital requirement; (5) for the SCSp, RCS filing precedes tax registration in all cases, and the SCSp registers for VAT in its own name when carrying out taxable activities; digital B2C suppliers to EU consumers may alternatively register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) without a full Luxembourg VAT ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg law: no entity-level corporate income tax (CIT) or net wealth tax is levied; each partner is taxed directly on their distributive share of income. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source items (Luxembourg permanent establishment, Luxembourg real property, and income attributable to Luxembourg sources); PE-attributable foreign profits may benefit from tax treaty relief or the Luxembourg participation exemption. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none — Luxembourg operates no special economic zones; the SCSp remains fully in the general territorial tax net. — (1) Foreign entity must first obtain a Luxembourg RCS registration if it establishes a branch or uses the SCSp vehicle, via the LBR electronic portal (notarial deed or private deed filed within one month of formation); (2) upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns the 13-digit national identification number (matricule) for income tax purposes; (3) for VAT, the non-established taxable person must file Form 704 with the AEDT (via eTVA/myGuichet.lu), supplying proof of foreign business identity, EORI number, foreign VAT/TIN certificate and, unless OSS is used, appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT; (4) the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA LU) without any capital requirement; (5) for the SCSp, RCS filing precedes tax registration in all cases, and the SCSp registers for VAT in its own name when carrying out taxable activities; digital B2C suppliers to EU consumers may alternatively register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) without a full Luxembourg VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg law: no entity-level corporate income tax (CIT) or net wealth tax is levied; each partner is taxed directly on their distributive share of income. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source items (Luxembourg permanent establishment, Luxembourg real property, and income attributable to Luxembourg sources); PE-attributable foreign profits may benefit from tax treaty relief or the Luxembourg participation exemption. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none — Luxembourg operates no special economic zones; the SCSp remains fully in the general territorial tax net.

Available election forms

(1) Article 165bis LIR election: the SCSp may irrevocably opt for opaque taxation as a resident corporate taxpayer subject to CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the election binds the entity for five fiscal years and is the functional Luxembourg equivalent of a check-the-box election. (2) Hybrid mismatch alignment: Luxembourg has transposed ATAD 2 (Law of 21 December 2018); where a partner jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque or a hybrid entity is used to create double non-taxation, Luxembourg adjusts the transparency classification ex officio. (3) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law allows closely linked Luxembourg taxable persons to form a single VAT person. (4) Treaty-based elections: non-resident partners may claim reduced withholding or exemption via Luxembourg certificate forms (e.g., withholding tax exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or tax treaties).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-trader exemption, Article 57 LTVT) for Luxembourg-established suppliers of goods and services; €10,000 EU-wide annual threshold for distance sales and electronically supplied services B2C (OSS); no threshold applies to non-established foreign suppliers or to B2B cross-border supplies of services — registration is triggered from the first euro of taxable activity in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT applies where goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or on its behalf; the exporter must obtain and retain proof of physical removal from EU customs territory within 90 days of supply. Intra-EU supplies: 0% VAT under Article 41 LTVT for goods/services supplied to VAT-registered customers in another Member State with valid VAT number and EC Sales List reporting. Cross-border services (LegalTech SaaS and e-signature services): under Article 43 LTVT, B2B services supplied to a taxable person established outside Luxembourg are outside Luxembourg's territorial scope and thus effectively taxed at 0% in Luxembourg; B2C electronically supplied services to EU consumers may be taxed under the OSS at the applicable EU destination rate, while exports of services to third-country consumers are zero-rated. Refund route for non-established taxpayers: Directive 2008/9/EC refund procedure via the Luxembourg VAT refund portal.

  • Customs Export Declaration — electronic IE-507/ASTER export declaration registered with the ADA customs system, bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) confirming release for export
  • Transport document proving EU exit — Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, Air Waybill, or carrier certificate establishing that the goods left EU territory within 90 days of dispatch
  • Commercial invoice (full Incoterms, exporter and overseas buyer identity, description, value, and explicit export/zero-rating statement)
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance — bank statement or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from a non-resident counterparty
  • Customer's non-resident status evidence — foreign VAT registration certificate, EORI confirmation, or written declaration of establishment outside Luxembourg/EU
  • Incoterms-linked contract documentation — e-contract, purchase order, or signed delivery confirmation evidencing transfer of risk and export intent

B2G mandate: invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities is mandatorily processed via PEPPOL (PEPPOL BIS 3.0, UBL or CII, EN 16931) from 1 January 2024 for large suppliers and phased in for all suppliers by 31 March 2025; connectivity via certified PEPPOL access points. B2B: no e-invoicing mandate is currently in force; draft Bill 8325 proposes mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting for B2B transactions from 1 January 2026. Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; no pre-clearance API exists — VAT is accounted for periodically via the eTVA portal, with EC Sales Lists and customs declarations transmitted through the Customs Single Window (GUM).

Export zero-rating claims are systematically audited against the customs export record, transport documents, payment flows and customer status evidence. Where substantiation is missing or false, the 0% rate is retrospectively reversed and the VAT is clawed back at 17%, plus statutory delay interest accruing monthly on the arrears. Administrative fines range from 10% up to 50% of the tax evaded depending on good faith, negligence, or deliberate concealment (Articles 60a and 61 LTVT); criminal tax fraud under the Luxembourg General Tax Law can attract imprisonment from 8 days up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to €100,000. The statute of limitations for reassessment and clawback is 5 years from the end of the relevant assessment year, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or absence of statutory records. Under the VAT collection rules, the partners of the fiscally transparent SCSp may be held jointly and severally liable for VAT debts attributable to the partnership.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility for current and capital account transactions. As a Eurozone member, Luxembourg applies Article 63 TFEU on free movement of capital; there are no exchange controls, no approval requirements for inward or outward transfers, and no restrictions on non-resident ownership of an SCSp. The SCSp is fiscally transparent, is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income, municipal business, or net wealth tax at entity level, and profit distributions to non-resident partners do not attract Luxembourg withholding tax absent substance deficiencies or anti-abuse findings; currency conversion and remittance are therefore unrestricted from an FX-policy standpoint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or conversion regime exists in Luxembourg. There is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to convert export receipts into EUR. Export and SaaS-subscription proceeds may be retained in any currency in any bank account, subject only to standard CRS/FATCA reporting, Luxembourg income-tax declarations by the partners, and BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting performed by resident banks.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), a member of the Eurosystem, together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; the BCL sets domestic monetary and payment-system policy, while the CSSF supervises banking AML/CFT compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscriptions are credited to the SCSp's dedicated capital-contribution account at the Luxembourg bank via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR. Remittance information must state the exact SCSp legal name and the partnership agreement execution date. The receiving bank will classify the transfer under the BCL/Eurosystem balance-of-payments framework as cross-border direct-investment equity capital; the SCSp should pre-file the partnership agreement, contribution schedule, and RBE certificate. First-time or high-value wires trigger enhanced due diligence and sanctions screening, but no prior foreign-exchange or central-bank approval is required; source-of-funds evidence and a payment-purpose memorandum must be immediately available. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level due to fiscal transparency; for qualifying corporate partners the aggregate Luxembourg CIT rate is 24.94% (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax for Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by Luxembourg resident companies; distributions made by a transparent SCSp to its partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at the partnership level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed by 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end, unless the Luxembourg tax administration grants a formal extension. Taxable partners must report their SCSp-derived income in the tax year in which the SCSp's fiscal year closes.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Identification Legal name, RCS number, Luxembourg tax identification number, registered office address, and contact details of the tax representative where applicable. Non-resident corporate or individual partners must state their overseas address, foreign tax identification number, and the Luxembourg tax representative's address when a trade/business is carried on in Luxembourg. Late filing triggers administrative penalties under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, default interest at 0.6% per month on unpaid taxes, and potential ex officio assessment by the Administration des contributions directes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation is required where related-party transactions exceed EUR 50,000 per transaction or EUR 150,000 in aggregate per fiscal year; the documentation must include contemporaneous Local File and Master File consistent with OECD TP guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the SCSp must demonstrate real economic substance through adequate office space, staff, decision-making, and accounting records. Substance deficiencies can trigger the Luxembourg GAAR, denial of tax residence certificates, and anti-abuse recharacterisation under Article 6 of the Luxembourg Tax Adaptation Law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment with default interest at 0.6% per month; penalties ranging from 10% to 100% of underpaid tax; criminal prosecution with fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign false declarations or participate in a fraudulent tax scheme face joint and several civil liability, asset seizure, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Compulsory transfer pricing adjustment, recalculated taxable base, late-payment interest, and penalties for incorrect tax returns; intentional manipulation is treated as tax fraud and carries criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for additional tax assessments if they certified false transfer pricing reports; directors may be prohibited from managing Luxembourg companies and may face confiscation or travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the SCSp as a tax resident of another jurisdiction, denial of treaty benefits, application of GAAR to reattribute income to the underlying shareholders, mandatory deregistration, and administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing applies where the SCSp is a sham; directors and shareholders are jointly liable for the entity's tax liabilities and may face criminal charges for abuse of law.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the amended Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004, including administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal sanctions of up to five years' imprisonment and confiscation of illicit funds.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal monetary fines, director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for willful AML failures; intentional breaches may result in unlimited liability.

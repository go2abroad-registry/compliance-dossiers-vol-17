Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal or paid-up capital is required for an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines the amount and payment schedule of capital contributions. Where contributions are agreed to be paid up, funds may be placed in an escrow or dedicated bank account pending RCS registration, but no statutory escrow requirement exists.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Grand Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours – CGDIS); compliance with structural load and occupancy requirements as certified by the competent communal building authority; workplace health and safety clearance from the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (Inspection du Travail et des Mines – ITM), including handling protocols for clinical laboratory reagents and risk assessment documents.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income and corporate taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Unified filing and portal access is provided through MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu; statutory company registration is lodged with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SCSp must (1) execute and file the partnership agreement with the RCS to obtain the national business matricule number; (2) register for Luxembourg VAT with the AEDT using Form 642/643, accompanied by the RCS excerpt, partnership agreement, proof of Luxembourg establishment, evidence of the intended taxable activity (wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents under ISIC 4649), and bank reference data; and (3) where applicable, file a VAT group election or small-business exemption election. The AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits), also entered in the EU VIES database. Non-resident partners generally do not require a separate Luxembourg TIN for the transparent partnership itself; the SCSp may act as VAT taxable person and, if needed, as withholding agent for Luxembourg-source amounts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct tax purposes: it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax. Partnership income and gains are allocated to and taxable at the hands of the partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and applicable tax treaties. For VAT purposes the SCSp may nevertheless be treated as a distinct taxable person when it carries on an economic activity, such as the commercial distribution of diagnostic reagents, and must register and comply with VAT obligations separately.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp's transparent classification is statutory and confirmed by advance tax ruling where necessary. Available elections and mechanisms include: (i) the election to apply the Luxembourg VAT grouping (assujetti unique) regime under Article 63 of the VAT law, subject to control and financial integration conditions; (ii) the small-business exemption election if annual taxable turnover is below EUR 35,000, unless mandatory registration applies; (iii) the option to renounce the exemption for small businesses to standard-rate supplies; (iv) contractual allocation of taxable profits and losses among partners, provided the allocation has economic substance and complies with the general anti-abuse rule; and (v) alignment of the partnership's treatment with hybrid-mismatch rules under the EU ATAD as implemented in Luxembourg, whereby the jurisdiction of establishment of the partners determines the transparency effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover excluding VAT). The small-business exemption applies below this threshold, but mandatory VAT registration exists from the first euro where the business makes intra-EU acquisitions, distance sales into Luxembourg, imports, or supplies of certain services to non-established taxable persons; wholesale distribution of diagnostic reagents in the ordinary course of business will normally require VAT registration regardless of threshold once the activity is regular and profitable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union (export supplies) and for intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State. The SCSp must demonstrate that ownership and transport risk have passed to the foreign buyer and that the goods left Luxembourg/EU territory. Standard commercial evidence includes the customs export declaration validated by customs, transport documents (CMR, bill of lading, or airway bill), sales contract and invoice, and proof of payment from the foreign customer. For diagnostic reagents distributed to non-EU laboratories, the export is zero-rated only if the customs formalities are completed in the name of the supplier and the supplier retains the authenticated export proof; if the customer acts as exporter, the supplier must obtain and retain the customer's export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Single Administrative Document submitted via Luxembourg customs systems) with customs exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill evidencing physical dispatch and destination outside the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment instruction demonstrating receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice with a clear zero-rating statement, Incoterms, and description of the IVD diagnostic reagents
  • Sales order, delivery note, packing list, and any certificates of analysis or compliance relevant to the underlying transaction

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital fiscal stamping for domestic B2B invoicing. Invoicing under the VAT law still permits paper or PDF/PDF-composite formats, subject to correct issuance and archiving. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only in B2G transactions for supplies to Luxembourg public authorities, requiring PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 XML through certified access points. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (EU sales lists) must be filed electronically via the AEDT e-filing portal (eCDF / MyGuichet.lu). Export transactions must be reported and maintained in the VIES-bound periodic declarations; documentary retention of at least 10 years is recommended for zero-rated export proof.

The AEDT and Luxembourg customs authorities perform targeted audits of export zero-rating claims, especially for high-value or high-risk invoices. If the exporter fails to produce complete and consistent proof within the audit window, VAT is retrospectively assessed at 17% plus statutory late interest. Administrative fines may reach 150% of the evaded amount, and criminal proceedings for fraudulent zero-rating can result in fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment for up to five years, or both. The fiscal clawback statute of limitation is generally five years, extended to ten years when fraud or deliberate concealment is established; the burden of substantiation rests on the supplier and must be retained for the full statutory record-keeping period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Both capital and current account transactions are unrestricted under Articles 63-66 TFEU as transposed into Luxembourg law; no foreign-exchange authorisation, capital permit, or central bank approval is required for profit remittance from a Luxembourg SCSp. The practical obstacles are limited to full AML/KYC documentation on ultimate beneficial owners and the obligation to demonstrate genuine economic substance sufficient for banking due diligence. An SCSp is pass-through for tax purposes, so no Luxembourg withholding arises on distributions to non-resident partners absent specific treaty base-erosion triggers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory repatriation timeline, no surrender percentage, and no forced conversion of export receipts into euros. Receivables from the cross-border distribution of IVD reagents may be retained in any convertible currency; exporters need only comply with BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting where aggregate flows exceed the applicable monthly threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) exercising joint supervision over credit institutions, anti-money-laundering, and foreign-exchange related conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. A CSSF-licensed payment or e-money institution, or an EU-passported EMI, can provide multi-currency transaction accounts, FX conversion, and open-API treasury automation aligned with the SCSp's payment waterfall. However, Luxembourg credit institutions generally do not treat EMI-held float as bank-grade collateral or escrow-grade liquidity, and the legal framework requires strict PSD2 pass-porting, safeguarding of partnership funds, and CSSF-compliant AML/CFT outsourcing if the EMI interfaces with regulated client monies.

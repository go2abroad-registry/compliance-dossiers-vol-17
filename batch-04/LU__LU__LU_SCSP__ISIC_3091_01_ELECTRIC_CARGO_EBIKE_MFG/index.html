Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an SCSp; contributions are freely determined by the partners in the articles of association and may be made in cash or in kind. No escrow account is legally required. Contributions must be credited to the SCSp's bank account and confirmed by a capital-receipt certificate before registration with the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Administration des services de secours (CGDIS) covering evacuation routes and extinguishing systems; structural load inspection certificate from an approved engineer for storage mezzanines and battery storage areas; occupational safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) addressing electrical safety, ventilation, noise exposure, and segregated storage of lithium batteries.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an SCSp; contributions are freely determined by the partners in the articles of association and may be made in cash or in kind. No escrow account is legally required. Contributions must be credited to the SCSp's bank account and confirmed by a capital-receipt certificate before registration with the RCS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue is bifurcated: the Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) administers direct corporate and personal income tax, while the Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) administers VAT, registration duties, and excise; the unified e-government portal is MyGuichet.lu, with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) handling entity registration and filing.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SCSp partnership agreement before a Luxembourg notary and file for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain the RCS registration number and national identification number. Step 2: Submit the business commencement declaration (formule 640) to the AEDT within 15 days of commencing taxable activity; a non-resident or non-established partnership must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative if no fixed establishment exists. Step 3: Receive the VAT identification number in the format LU followed by eight digits. Step 4: Enroll with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) for social security if personnel are employed. Step 5: Foreign partners must supply certified constitutional documents, a Luxembourg registered office address, and a local authorized manager (gérant) to complete direct-tax registration; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, individual partners are assessed directly by the ACD on their allocable share of income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. An SCSp is fiscally transparent by default but is statutorily re-characterized as a taxable corporate entity under the hybrid-mismatch override at Article 5bis K of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) to the extent a partner is not subject to net-basis taxation in Luxembourg, thereby neutralizing hybrid entity mismatches under ATAD. VAT-specific elections include: waiver of the small-business exemption under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, formation of a VAT group as a single taxable person under Article 60 of the VAT Law, and application of the reverse-charge mechanism for intra-Community acquisitions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover (small-business exemption threshold under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law); mandatory VAT registration is required absent any turnover threshold for imports, intra-EU acquisitions, distance sales, and reverse-charge transactions; the threshold is measured on a rolling calendar-year basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of electric cargo e-bikes to destinations outside the European Union are exempt with credit for input VAT (effective 0% rate) under Article 55 of the Luxembourg VAT Law provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and transport is evidenced; intra-Community dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are zero-rated when the customer's valid VIES VAT identification number is recorded; offshore B2B services benefit from zero-rating under the general place-of-supply reverse-charge rule pursuant to Article 58 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; supporting documentation must be retained for at least 10 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A / SAD single administrative document) lodged electronically through the Luxembourg customs clearance system (GUS/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT transfer confirmation demonstrating repatriation of export sale proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and executed sale contract with Incoterms 2020 allocation of risk and transport cost
  • Proof of transport (carrier track-and-trace report or customs-stamped logistics documentation)
  • Intrastat dispatch return or VIES recapitulative statement in respect of intra-EU zero-rated supplies

Mandatory business-to-government (B2G) e-invoicing via the Peppol eDelivery Network has been in force since 2021; there is no general mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for business-to-business invoices in the e-bike manufacturing sector; VAT SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) files must be transmitted electronically to the AEDT by prescribed taxpayers; Intrastat and VIES recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through MyGuichet; invoices must comply with EU Directive 2014/55/EU syntax and semantic standards.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT assessments under the standard 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud; statutory late-interest accrues from the original due date; the AEDT may impose administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 pursuant to Article 131 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, together with a 10% surcharge on the VAT due; willful tax fraud is a criminal offense under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years; exporters of high-risk goods face enhanced audit scrutiny and potential clawback of previously recovered input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account items under Articles 63-65 TFEU; there are zero residual exchange controls for euro-denominated flows or cross-border profit remittances. An SCSp tax-transparent entity faces no FX licence or convertibility restriction on dividend, interest, or liquidation distributions; only informational reporting applies (BCL balance-of-payments statistics, FATCA/CRS self-certification).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory timeline, minimum conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts; exporters may retain USD, CNY, GBP, or EUR balances freely in operational accounts. The only applicable duties are periodic balance-of-payments statistical declarations to the BCL and standard AML/CFT transaction reporting under the Law of 12 November 2004, as amended.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem, in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for prudential supervision and the European Central Bank for monetary and foreign-exchange policy; Luxembourg applies no national foreign-exchange control authority separate from the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

FEASIBILITY RATING: MEDIUM-HIGH for low-value disbursements, LOW for core treasury. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs under PSD2 may hold payment accounts, but a manufacturing SCSp requires a CSSF-licensed credit institution to access SWIFT MT100/MT700 clearing, documentary-credit issuance, trade-finance limits, and multi-currency nostro rails. Offshore digital EMIs cannot hold capital-escrow structures or issue commercial LCs; the recommended architecture is a Luxembourg core banking relationship augmented by one EMI payment gateway for small operational spend.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined for Luxembourg City: corporate income tax 17% + solidarity surcharge 0.70% (7% of CIT) + municipal business tax 6.75% (commune rate; ranges up to 11.20% outside Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory domestic dividend withholding tax is 15% (LIR Art. 101) for distributions by Luxembourg companies. For the SCSp specifically, profit allocations and capital distributions to partners are not dividends: the fiscally transparent partnership does not levy withholding tax at the entity level, and no WHT is applied on allocations of manufacturing or trading profits to either resident or non-resident partners. Where the SCSp receives dividends from an operating subsidiary, that subsidiary applies the 15% WHT at source, subject to full exemption under the Luxembourg participation exemption and to 0% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg TP documentation must be maintained where the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 1,000,000 for goods or services; EUR 300,000 for financial transactions (loans, guarantees, cash pooling); and EUR 100,000 for other transactions (including IP and management fees). A Master File is additionally required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 50,000,000. The transparent SCSp is subject to these rules where it carries on a commercial activity (e.g., intra-group procurement of components, licensing of design IP or intercompany financing for the e-bike manufacturing operation).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not codify a standalone Economic Substance Regulation statute, but economic substance is legally enforced through the domestic general anti-abuse rule (Steueranpassungsgesetz / Abus de droit doctrine), the ATAD general anti-abuse and interest limitation rules, and the administrative practice requiring a real Luxembourg presence for transparent vehicles. The SCSp must be managed in Luxembourg: general partner decision-making, local registered office, local bank accounts, and genuine commercial or holding activity commensurate with its income. Without substance, the tax authorities may recharacterise the SCSp as a fully taxable opaque entity, deny DTA and EU Directive benefits, retroactively reassess up to ten years, and apply a 10% abuse-related uplift. For the electric cargo bike OEM activity, the manufacturing intellectual property, purchase orders, inventory management and commercial risk must be substantiated in Luxembourg with economic functions and personnel.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fiscal fraud and wilful omission)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 (and up to double the evaded tax in aggravated cases); imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative 100% malevolent omission surcharge on the evaded tax; monthly late-payment interest at 0.8%; disallowance of loss carryforwards; seizure of assets through attachment orders.

Director personal liability: The general partner (commandité) has unlimited joint and several liability for the debts of the SCSp. Managers and directors of a corporate general partner are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded amounts; criminal courts may impose travel bans, entry restrictions, seizure of bank accounts, asset freezes, and imprisonment. Limited partners who participate in day-to-day management lose their limited liability shield and become jointly liable with the general partner.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the SCSp as a fully taxable Luxembourg resident company; assessment to 24.94% combined CIT and municipal business tax plus net wealth tax exposure; denial of treaty benefits and Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief; retroactive reassessment over 10 years; 10% abuse-of-law uplift on the taxable base; fines up to EUR 250,000 for tax fraud where the sham is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly liable for the reassessed taxes and penalties where insolvency follows the recharacterisation; the corporate veil is pierced in fraud cases, leading to personal civil liability, freezing orders, travel restrictions and criminal prosecution of the directing minds. Non-resident shareholders who knowingly structure a sham can be co-prosecuted as accomplices.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and non-arm's length pricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length profit with a 10% penalty surcharge for missing documentation; monthly default interest at 0.8%; where the undervaluation is wilful, penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax; protection of the tax base under the ATAD interest limitation rules (30% EBITDA cap) and reclassification of excessive debt as hidden profit distribution.

Director personal liability: Executives signing false or incomplete TP documentation face personal criminal liability for tax fraud; directors can be held civilly liable for the shortfall where the TP adjustment renders the SCSp insolvent; departure bans and asset seizures may be ordered to secure the Treasury's claims.
AML/UBO register and beneficial ownership compliance

Statutory sanction: RBE registration violations: administrative fines of up to EUR 1,250,000 and, for repeated breaches, criminal fines; AML compliance failures under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 as amended: administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; criminal liability for money laundering with imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 1,250,000; freezing of bank accounts and transactions by the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any AML compliance officer are personally liable; criminal conviction triggers personal fines, debarment from management, travel bans, and imprisonment; asset freezes apply to personal assets where the funds are traceable to laundering; limited partners who interfere in the management of the partnership lose their limited liability protection and become exposed to the same sanctions.

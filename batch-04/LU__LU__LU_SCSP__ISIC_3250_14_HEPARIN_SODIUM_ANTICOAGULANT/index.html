Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); independent public-law body; all filings are submitted through the e-Register electronic portal at www.lbr.lu; the RBE beneficial-ownership register is operated under the same LBR roof.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 23 July 2016 on the Special Limited Partnership (Société en Commandite Spéciale), codified into Title VII of the amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Articles 710-25 et seq.), as subsequently amended; the Law of 19 December 2019 establishing the Luxembourg Business Registers; the Law of 2 September 2011 on access to regulated professions for the industrial establishment component.

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Agreement (Contrat de Société / Statuts de la Société en Commandite Spéciale) accompanied by the managers' declaration of non-conviction and non-bankruptcy, registered electronically in the RCS via Form 'New legal entity - SCSp'.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 111 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; the Limited Partnership Agreement freely determines the amount and timing of partnership-interest contributions, and zero initial capital is lawful. No statutory escrow or blocked account is required for cash contributions, but any cash received before RCS registration must be credited to a Luxembourg credit-institution account and full beneficial-owner verification (KYC) must be completed under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 against money laundering and terrorist financing before the funds are used.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification issued by the Corps Grand-Ducal Incendie et Secours (CGDIS) following review of extinguishing-water containment bunds and solvent-storage areas; Seveso III Directive threshold classification review for ethanol and isopropyl-alcohol inventories; occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering structural load stability of the fractionation floor, blast-ventilation capacities, electrostatic grounding of solvent transfer lines, mandatory personal protective equipment zones, and annual medical-surveillance examinations for personnel exposed to solvent vapors; the physical occupancy permit is granted only after final combined inspection by CGDIS and ITM.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; the Limited Partnership Agreement freely determines the amount and timing of partnership-interest contributions, and zero initial capital is lawful. No statutory escrow or blocked account is required for cash contributions, but any cash received before RCS registration must be credited to a Luxembourg credit-institution account and full beneficial-owner verification (KYC) must be completed under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 against money laundering and terrorist financing before the funds are used.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct corporate and income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration; official portal: Guichet.lu / MyGuichet and AEDT online services (www.aedt.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

An SCSp established in Luxembourg obtains a Luxembourg national identification number (TIN) automatically upon registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the Registre National des Personnes Morales (RNPM). For VAT, the SCSp must file the standard VAT registration form (Formulaire 102/103) with the AEDT either via MyGuichet or in paper format; a non-resident foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must apply directly to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT number before making taxable supplies, appointing a fiscal representative if required by the AEDT.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — An SCSp established in Luxembourg obtains a Luxembourg national identification number (TIN) automatically upon registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the Registre National des Personnes Morales (RNPM). For VAT, the SCSp must file the standard VAT registration form (Formulaire 102/103) with the AEDT either via MyGuichet or in paper format; a non-resident foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must apply directly to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT number before making taxable supplies, appointing a fiscal representative if required by the AEDT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a check-the-box election; SCSp status is statutorily transparent for direct tax purposes (no Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax at partnership level, with partners taxed on their allocated share). For VAT, the partnership itself is the taxable person; no election to be disregarded is available. Under hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD), the SCSp may document its transparent classification and the treatment of its partners; a VAT fiscal unity election exists for closely related established entities, and certain elections may be required for VAT grouping and export-related reliefs, but no US-style entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established businesses; no threshold applies to non-established foreign entities, which must register immediately upon first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to supplies of goods that are consigned or transported by the supplier or a third party to a destination outside the European Union. The SCSp must hold evidence that the goods physically left EU territory within 90 days of supply, including transport documents. Export of pharmaceutical products such as heparin sodium anticoagulant qualifies if direct export is evidenced; services relating to exported goods (including processing, packaging, or quality testing) may also be zero-rated if performed for the non-EU customer and directly related to exported goods.

  • Customs export declaration (Electronic Single Administrative Document or export summary declaration) with the outward office of first exit records
  • Bill of Lading (for ocean freight) or Airway Bill (for air freight) evidencing transport to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and transport contract or Incoterms-based proof of transfer of risk/ownership
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit evidence demonstrating receipt of export consideration
  • Proof of customs exit (cargo movement reference / exit report from the Luxembourg or EU customs system)

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control regime. No API-based transaction reporting or mandatory digital stamping is currently in force. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL network for invoices to Luxembourg public authorities. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted and legally acceptable if authenticity of origin and integrity of content is ensured; there is no statutory obligation to use a national clearance platform.

Luxembourg tax authorities rigorously audit export zero-rating claims; insufficient or inconsistent documentation leads to immediate VAT assessment at 17% plus default interest at 0.6% per month. Administrative penalties range from 25% to 200% of the evaded VAT depending on intent. Fraudulent claims trigger criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg Criminal Code and VAT fraud statutes, with retrospective clawback for up to 10 years. Claims must be substantiated within the statutory time limits; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may be treated as a non-export transaction and zero-rating is denied with full VAT, penalties and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full capital and current account convertibility under the euro framework and Article 63 TFEU. No foreign-exchange controls restrict inward or outward capital movements. Profits, dividends, redemption proceeds, and shareholder loan repayments may be remitted freely without prior central bank authorisation. Obligations are limited to statistical reporting to the BCL and BoP classification for cross-border flows. The current account, including trade settlement for heparin crude and finished anticoagulant imports/exports, is unrestricted; services and financial account operations follow OECD BPM6 classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply in Luxembourg. Exporters are not required to convert export receipts into EUR or to repatriate proceeds within a statutory period. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained in current and settlement accounts at Luxembourg credit institutions. Only aggregate position and cross-border flow statistical thresholds under BCL reporting circulars must be respected, and all SWIFT MT103 instructions must include correct purpose and counterparty references for balance-of-payments compilation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises AML/KYC conduct and bank licensing compliance. The Luxembourg currency is the euro under the sole monetary policy authority of the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for a primary operational treasury account and Medium only as a secondary payment-rail interface under enhanced due diligence. An EU-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) can legally open passported accounts in Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive and E-Money Directive but cannot accept deposits or provide credit. For a pharma-scope SCSp engaged in active chemical fractionation of low-molecular-weight heparin crude, Luxembourg banks will typically require the core treasury relationship to be held at a fully regulated credit institution. EMIs are acceptable only for ancillary euro collections or low-value payments, subject to CSSF anti-money-laundering rules, transaction-velocity limits, and opaque ownership prohibitions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed by bank transfer in EUR from the contributing limited partner or parent company through a euro clearing network, preferring SEPA CT for EU originators or SWIFT MT103/MT202 for third-country payments. The beneficiary account is the SCSp operational or capital escrow account at the Luxembourg credit institution. The payment narrative must include the partnership name, the contractual capital contribution reference, and the BPM6 purpose code for direct investment or other investment, such as equity capital or shareholder loan. The bank will verify the identity of the remitter, the underlying partnership deed, the capital account entries, and the absence of split or structured transfers avoiding reporting thresholds. No Luxembourg exchange-control approval is required.Outbound trade settlements for heparin crude and anticoagulant intermediate purchases should use SWIFT MT103/MT202 or a commercial letter of credit, depending on counterparty risk. For high-value non-EU transactions, the preferred rail is an irrevocable documentary LC issued by the SCSp's Luxembourg bank in favour of the supplier, governed by UCP600 and transmitted via SWIFT MT700; the SCSp pays against compliant shipping documents under the LC. For low-value EU settlements, SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer is acceptable. All instructions must include full beneficiary details, invoice and customs references, the relevant BOP purpose code, and no ambiguous narratives.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority; online portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu. VAT registration and collection are administered by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity or a newly formed SCSp without an existing Luxembourg tax identification number must register through MyGuichet.lu or via the RCS desk, requesting a Luxembourg national tax number (N° d'identification). For VAT, the entity must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT before making taxable supplies, obtaining an individual Luxembourg VAT number. The SCSp is not required to obtain a corporate income tax number for fiscally transparent earnings, but partners are identified for tax reporting through partner-level declarations. Non-resident partners with no Luxembourg-filing facts generally require no separate Luxembourg TIN unless direct Luxembourg-source income is derived or registration is otherwise triggered.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident entity or a newly formed SCSp without an existing Luxembourg tax identification number must register through MyGuichet.lu or via the RCS desk, requesting a Luxembourg national tax number (N° d'identification). For VAT, the entity must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT before making taxable supplies, obtaining an individual Luxembourg VAT number. The SCSp is not required to obtain a corporate income tax number for fiscally transparent earnings, but partners are identified for tax reporting through partner-level declarations. Non-resident partners with no Luxembourg-filing facts generally require no separate Luxembourg TIN unless direct Luxembourg-source income is derived or registration is otherwise triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent: it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax or net wealth tax as an entity, and its partners are taxed directly on their distributive share. The partners may govern allocation and profit-sharing by the partnership agreement, subject to Luxembourg anti-abuse and ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules. A SCSp is generally a separate taxable person for VAT purposes where it performs taxable economic activities; no partnership-level transparent exemption applies to VAT. Luxembourg VAT grouping is available in defined circumstances, but a SCSp cannot elect full entity-level CIT treatment unless the tax-transparent status is excluded by explicit legal provision, treaty override, or restructuring into a nontransparent vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident taxable persons carrying on economic activity must register for VAT when annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 (small-enterprise exemption). Non-resident suppliers supplying goods by distance sale to Luxembourg consumers are required to register once EU-wide distance sales exceed EUR 10,000, or immediately for non-established suppliers making taxable supplies of services or goods without an exemption; pure passive endowment management and the distribution of grants by a charitable SCSp, when made without consideration, are not taxable supplies and do not trigger VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically transported by the supplier or a third party from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided the supplier retains objective transport and contractual evidence. Services directly related to exported goods, including handling, freight, and ancillary logistic services, may likewise be zero-rated where the ultimate benefit is in the foreign destination. Intra-EU supplies to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State are zero-rated with a valid EU VAT ID and periodic EC sales list declaration. Pure charitable grant distributions and non-economic endowment activities fall outside the scope of VAT and therefore do not amount to zero-rated supplies unless separately invoiced as services for consideration.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/Douane Luxembourg) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR (road), or rail consignment note evidencing physical export from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer statement evidencing payment by the non-EU customer
  • Customs broker certification or power of attorney where the clearance is delegated
  • Supporting grant agreement, endowment deed, or beneficiary documentation showing the foreign recipient and purpose where services are supplied in connection with charitable activities

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping. Standard e-invoicing is mandatory only for business-to-government (B2G) transactions through Peppol-compliant channels. B2B invoicing may be electronic if the customer agrees, and invoices must be issued within 15 days after the chargeable event. No pre-clearance API transmission to the AEDT is required before invoice issuance.

Erroneous or fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to assessment of the unpaid Luxembourg VAT at the standard rate of 17%, default interest of 0.8% per commenced month, administrative fines, and clawback of refunds for a period of up to five years under the VAT limitation statute. Intentional evasion or documentary falsification may be prosecuted as VAT fraud, attracting criminal penalties, personal director liability for the tax loss, and mandatory disclosure to the AEDT and public prosecutor. Misuse of charitable endowment status alongside false export documentation may additionally trigger revocation of any applicable exemption or fund license, restoration and clawback actions by the supervisory body, and AML or foundation-law sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro and all other major currencies are fully convertible. Luxembourg implements the EU free movement of capital and payments regime under Articles 63 to 65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Capital Movements Directive 88/361/EEC. There is no distinction between current and capital account convertibility for a Luxembourg SCSp; capital contributions, endowment inflows, grant disbursements, and profit remittances require no authorisation, exchange permit, or quantitative limit. Applied restrictions arise only from EU and United Nations sanctions, targeted financial restrictive measures, and AML/CFT screening obligations. As the SCSp is tax-transparent under Luxembourg law, no withholding tax or profit-remittance barrier is imposed at the entity level, provided the partnership has no Luxembourg permanent establishment generating taxable commercial profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. A Luxembourg-resident entity, including an SCSp, may retain export earnings, donations, or any inbound capital in the original currency without obligation to exchange into euro. The only compliance burden is the BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation, which requires transactions in excess of the thresholds published in BCL Circulars to be filed on the 'Report-It-ONCE' electronic platform. Non-compliance may attract administrative penalties but never a currency conversion liability.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg does not operate a discretionary foreign exchange approval regime; foreign exchange policy is anchored by the European Central Bank, while BCL compiles balance-of-payments statistics and administers statistical reporting under EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 and BCL Circular 2013/268. The CSSF exercises prudential supervision over licensed banks and payment institutions and enforces AML/CFT obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore or non-EU electronic money institutions. A charity endowment SCSp requires a full-service credit institution able to hold segregated multi-currency balances, execute SWIFT cross-border payments, issue documentary credit instruments where necessary, and support the CSSF substance expectations of regulated philanthropic vehicles. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs operating under the PSD2 passport provide only limited payment-account and money-remittance services and cannot provide commercial custody, treasury credit lines, or correspondent-banking access. Offshore EMIs, in particular, present unacceptable regulatory and jurisdictional risk and will not satisfy KYC due diligence or substance requirements. Such EMIs may be used solely as auxiliary collection points, never as the primary liquidity or settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Luxembourg SCSp must be executed by SWIFT MT103 single credit transfer or, for high-value transactions, by MT202 COV with detailed beneficiary information. The remitting bank must include the SCSp's full legal name, RCSL registration number, and a purpose code indicating a capital contribution or endowment commitment, together with the reference of the relevant limited partnership agreement clause. The Luxembourg receiving bank will classify the credit under the BCL's balance-of-payments statistical categories and will request the capital commitment schedule, the board or general partner resolution, and confirmation that the paying bank is the account-holding institution of the contributing partner or donor. Compliance is completed when the receiving bank verifies that the incoming funds match the executed deed of contribution and that no sanctions or AML indicators exist on any party to the payment.Grant disbursements and operational outflows are cleared via SWIFT MT103 wires executed from the SCSp's Luxembourg account to the beneficiary's first-class bank. For commercial trade settlements undertaken by any commercial subsidiary of the endowment, banks in Luxembourg may issue SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit, MT707 amendments, or documentary collections under UCP 600. The SCSp itself is principally a grant-making endowment vehicle, so payables are executed using TARGET2 for euro clearing, SEPA Instant where relevant, and CHAPS or Fedwire via correspondent institutions for sterling and U.S. dollar payments. 15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax for CIT-subject companies; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (holding of at least 10% for 12 months) and 0% for distributions made by an SCSp because the SCSp is not a collective income tax taxpayer and partner profit draws are not dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers, the return must be filed no later than 31 December of the year following the accounting year; periods based on a non-calendar fiscal year-end carry a filing deadline aligned to the end of the following corresponding accounting period unless the tax office sets an earlier notice deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The partnership return follows the same annual filing cycle as Form 111: ordinarily 31 December following the accounting year for calendar-year entities; the Luxembourg tax administration may grant a six-month extension upon request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 (group consolidated turnover in the preceding financial year) triggers mandatory Luxembourg Master File and Local File, with a country-by-country reporting obligation at the same EUR 750 million threshold; Luxembourg-connected entities with controlled transactions below this threshold must still maintain adequate contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence for inspection.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Cayman/BVI; however, the Luxembourg General Anti-Abuse Rule (ATAD Article 6) and domestic case law require a real economic presence, business purpose, decision-making, adequately risked capital and arm’s length staffing. An SCSp acting as a charitable endowment manager should maintain local substance or clearly documented external mandates; a present- or near-shell entity engaging in treaty-based passive income routing is open to recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits, DAC6 hallmarks and penal reattribution of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all under-declared tax; fraud penalty of 50%–150% of the evaded tax; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 or up to 10% of the sum evaded; confiscation of proceeds; imprisonment under Luxembourg fiscal criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners and de facto managers may be jointly and severally liable for tax, penalties and interest; courts may freeze personal assets, impose travel bans and enter custodial sentences after final conviction.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reallocation of profits to the beneficial owners under the Luxembourg GAAR; immediate recapture of exempt income; administrative penalties (commonly up to EUR 100,000) and DAC6/ATAD penalty assessments for undisclosed intermediaries.

Director personal liability: Where limited liability is disregarded, directors and managers may be treated as co-taxpayers for the unpaid corporate tax; civil restitution extends to avoided dividend withholding tax and interest; serious abuse may trigger travel restrictions during criminal investigation.
AML/CFT Breach (Anti-Money Laundering Law of 12 November 2004)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons; criminal penalties of up to 5 years’ imprisonment for natural persons; revocation of the foundation or SPF-like licence; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for executing forbidden transfers; the court may order civil damages, asset freezes, travel bans and, in serious organised-crime cases, pre-trial detention.
Failure to File / False Filing

Statutory sanction: Automatic tax surcharge up to 10% of assessed tax; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month capped at 10%; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000; repeat intentional omission treated as fiscal fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure regulatory filings may be declared personally liable where the SCSp is insolvent; they may be prohibited from serving as directors and, upon criminal conviction, may face travel bans and imprisonment.

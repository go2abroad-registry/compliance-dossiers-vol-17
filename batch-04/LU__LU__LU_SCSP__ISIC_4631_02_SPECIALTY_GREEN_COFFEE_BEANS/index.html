Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital requirement applies to an SCSp; partners freely determine capital contributions in the partnership agreement; no paid-up capital or escrow account is mandated by Luxembourg law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) issued on the basis of a site inspection of the warehouse, confirming exit routes, extinguishing systems and compliance with the Luxembourg fire regulations; structural load inspection certificate for the commercial warehouse proving suitability for stacked green coffee pallet loads; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), covering employee exposure to organic dust, safe handling of jute and burlap bags, forklift traffic management, and the establishment of a risk assessment under the Law of 17 June 1994 on workplace safety.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital requirement applies to an SCSp; partners freely determine capital contributions in the partnership agreement; no paid-up capital or escrow account is mandated by Luxembourg law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) - Registration Duties and VAT Administration; official e-portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.public.lu; direct income tax administration: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD).

E-government filing portal

The SCSp must first be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) to obtain its national business registration number (matricule). For VAT, a non-established taxable person applies to the AED before making taxable supplies: EU-established operators file the standard VAT registration application directly, accompanied by articles of incorporation, board power of attorney and proof of economic activity; operators established outside the EEA appoint and disclose an AED-approved fiscal representative who files in the representative's name. The AED issues a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by eight digits. Because the SCSp is income-tax transparent, no ACD corporate tax registration is required at entity level; partners report their shares in their respective home-country tax registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate and personal income tax (look-through), so profits and losses are allocated to partners; for VAT the SCSp is nevertheless a taxable person in its own right and holds its own Luxembourg VAT number.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime; partnership transparency is the statutory default. Under the amended Article 175 LIR, an SCSp carrying on a commercial activity and having exclusively corporate joint and several or limited partners is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax at entity level; in other circumstances the default look-through treatment applies and no elective entity-level tax election is available. Advance tax rulings may be obtained from the ACD for prospective transactions, and the ATAD hybrid and reverse-hybrid rules (Law of 21 December 2017, as amended) override any mismatches arising from partner-country classification. No separate US-style hybrid election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small businesses as the VAT franchise threshold; no de minimis turnover exemption applies to non-established foreign entities, which must register with the AED as soon as they intend to make taxable supplies in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of green arabica coffee beans (HS 0901) exported by the SCSp to customers outside the EU qualify for the 0% rate under Article 146(1)(a) of the VAT Directive, provided the seller holds documentary proof that the goods left the EU within the statutory period (generally 90 days from dispatch) and that delivery occurred to the non-EU customer. Intra-EU container distribution to a customer holding a valid Luxembourg and destination-state VAT registration number is an exempt intra-Community supply at 0% if the VIES recapitulative statement and the customer's VAT validation confirm the B2B trade. The 0% export rate applies only upon complete export evidence; otherwise the supply is reportable under Luxembourg domestic categories, which include the 3% super-reduced rate for foodstuffs or the 17% standard rate, and must be self-assessed accordingly.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) - EU customs export declaration discharged by the Luxembourg or point-of-departure customs office
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical departure of the container from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the SCSp as supplier and describing single-origin green arabica coffee beans under HS 0901
  • Packing list and container manifest matching the SAD quantities
  • Swift MT103 or documentary letter of credit settlement documentation together with an Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing foreign-exchange remittance within the contractual term
  • VAT registration validation output and VIES listing where intra-EU distribution is claimed

Luxembourg does not impose a general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping or continuous transaction control reporting. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL infrastructure for public-supply invoices; B2B and B2C invoices may be issued electronically under the invoicing rules of the Luxembourg VAT Code and the VAT Directive, subject to the authenticity, integrity and readability requirements, without prior AED clearance or pre-clearance fiscal stamping.

AED and customs auditors may reopen export zero-rating claims for five years, extended to ten years where elements of fraud or evasion are proven. Missing, inconsistent or fraudulent export proof causes recharacterisation of the turnover as a Luxembourg or intra-Community taxable supply at 17%, with default interest computed on a monthly basis and administrative fines of up to the tax evaded. Material false declarations may attract criminal penalties, including fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment, and the general partners of the SCSp may be held jointly and severally liable for the clawed-back VAT, interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone jurisdiction with full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro under Article 63 TFEU; capital and current account operations are liberalised both within the EU/EEA and toward third countries. No exchange controls, no prior authorisation for cross-border payments, and no remittance impediment on profits distributed by a fiscally transparent SCSp (pass-through). Payment instructions must nevertheless be tagged with the correct Balance of Payments reporting code, and the resident bank must complete AML/KYC verification prior to any execution. For green coffee import transactions from non-EEA origins (e.g., Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia), funds may be freely paid and received in EUR or USD without central bank approval; the only statutory constraints are EU asset-freezing and sanctions measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law and BCL regulations impose no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. Export receivables in USD or EUR may be held freely in foreign-currency accounts. The only statutory duty is the balance-of-payments and direct-investment reporting obligation under ECB Regulation (EU) No 184/2014, implemented by BCL circulars; the resident commercial bank files transaction-level reports with the BCL, and each cross-border operation must be assigned the correct classification code (category S — direct investment / trade in goods).

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Luxembourg's central bank and member of the Eurosystem. Prudential and anti-money laundering supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the BCL operates TARGET2 settlement and collects Balance of Payments and International Investment Position statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility. Luxembourg recognises CSSF-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and PSD2 passporting of EEA EMIs. For a specialty green coffee SCSp, a digital EMI may handle only ancillary operational payments (sub-threshold supplier prepayments, payroll, utilities) and cannot support documentary letters of credit, trade finance, or large-scale USD clearing. Offshore or non-EEA EMIs are not viable: they cannot lawfully hold in-scope euro payment accounts for a Luxembourg partnership, cannot execute SWIFT MT103 payments to coffee exporters at commercial scale, and would create an unrecognised money-transmission nexus for tax, AML and balance-of-payments reporting. The SCSp should designate a Tier-1 commercial bank as the primary liquidity rail and use EMIs solely as a secondary, low-value operating channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial and subsequent capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer (or SEPA/TARGET2 for euro) into the SCSp's dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. The payment message must reference the capital-call article of the SCSp partnership agreement, the capital-call notice number, and the Balance of Payments purpose code S301 (acquisition of share capital, direct-investment category) under the ECB Vademecum. Before the first inbound transfer, the bank must hold: (i) an RCS extract evidencing registration of the SCSp; (ii) the executed partnership agreement; (iii) the capital-call resolution adopted by the general partner; and (iv) the complete KYC/UBO pack of the remitting investor. For wires above EUR 1,000,000, the bank will normally trigger enhanced due diligence and expects pre-advice up to 48 hours before value date. Upon credit, funds remain frozen in the escrow account until compliance releases them to the operational account against a written activation request.Green coffee container purchases are settled through two principal clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account and prepayment terms with repeat, vetted suppliers, typically 30–60% prepayment with the balance settled against presentation of shipping documents; and (b) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700, payable at sight or by deferred payment at 60–120 days, in favour of the foreign exporter, primarily for first-time origin suppliers. Euro payments clear across TARGET2 through the BCL; US-dollar payments are routed through the bank's New York correspondent (e.g., JPMorgan Chase, Citibank or BNP Paribas New York) over CHIPS or Fedwire. Disbursements must match the invoice, proforma invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, and the bank's trade-finance price-verification rules. Any discrepancy in the documentary presentation must be cured by a discrepancy waiver or an LC amendment via MT707 before release of goods. For amounts exceeding the bank's single-transaction threshold (typically EUR 250,000–500,000), the treasury must submit a trade-settlement instruction with the underlying contract summary at least one business day in advance.Outright EUR/USD forward contracts (plain vanilla) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex against a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank dealing room; the SCSp, acting as a non-financial counterparty (NFC-), is exempt from the EMIR clearing obligation provided the OTC derivative clearing threshold is not breached, while all trades remain subject to EMIR reporting to an authorised trade repository.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in EUR/BRL, EUR/COP, EUR/ETB and EUR/IDR for hedging green coffee purchase-price exposure against emerging-market supplier currencies, net-settled in EUR or USD against an agreed fixing source.FX swaps converting surplus USD export receivables into EUR for treasury liquidity management, executed under the resident bank's Standard Settlement Instructions (SSIs).Cross-currency swaps aligning USD trade-loan liabilities with the EUR functional currency of the SCSp where foreign-exchange-funded trade facilities are used.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD FX options (European-style puts and calls) as premium-based hedges where the SCSp treasury policy permits option hedging; execution is subject to CSSF suitability rules and MiFID II client-classification confirmation before trading.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate standard Luxembourg City rate applicable to a fully taxable corporate taxpayer (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT base + 6.75% municipal business tax). The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default and is not itself subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax on its allocated income; the economic tax burden is imposed on the partners in their residence jurisdictions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on allocations and distributions made by the transparent SCSp to its partners. A transparent SCSp is not a Luxembourg withholding tax payor, and its distributions are not legally classified as dividends. By contrast, if the green coffee trading activity were instead held through a fully taxable Luxembourg S.à r.l. or SA, the statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax rate is 15%, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and to 0%-5% under most Luxembourg double tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory where the consolidating multinational group exceeds EUR 750,000,000 in annual consolidated revenue. Luxembourg has no published statutory minimum threshold for the Local File and Master File; in administrative practice the ACD requests the OECD transfer pricing documentation package where related-party flows engaged by the SCSp exceed approximately EUR 1,000,000 per annum, and a contemporaneous Local File must always be prepared where commodity pricing deviates from published green coffee benchmarks.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the statutory General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 6 of the General Tax Law), the ATAD abuse clause, and ACD administrative doctrine require genuine substance: local registered office, Luxembourg-based management decisions, active commercial negotiation of coffee supply contracts, bank accounts, books and records in Luxembourg, and physical presence commensurate with the activity. An SCSp that executes all green coffee procurement, logistics, insurance, and financing decisions from the origin country will be re-characterized as managed from abroad, exposing the structure to a Luxembourg permanent establishment assessment, denial of treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test, or annulment of the partnership's Luxembourg tax transparency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Artificial Inventory Loss Construction

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of hidden profits; administrative fine up to EUR 250,000; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; criminal tax fraud prosecution with imprisonment of up to five years and publication of the guilty verdict.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors may be jointly and severally liable for the SCSp's unpaid taxes; in fraud cases courts may pierce the partnership shield, attach personal assets of the directors, issue travel bans, and impose personal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR requalification of the structure; retroactive withdrawal of tax transparency; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; adjustment of taxable basis to the true economic beneficiary; fines and publication of the reassessment.

Director personal liability: Direct disqualification from managing Luxembourg entities, joint and several liability for the reassessed taxes and penalties, and criminal prosecution for aiding an abusive tax arrangement.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Register Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 under the Luxembourg AML framework (Law of 12 November 2004 and RBE Law of 13 January 2019); criminal sanctions with imprisonment of up to five years; freezing of distributions and closure of banking facilities.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability of the general partner's directors; travel bans and extradition exposure where the concealment forms part of a cross-border money-laundering operation; civil damages to creditors and co-investors.
False Tax Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Correction of the taxable basis, fines up to EUR 250,000, publication of the conviction, and imprisonment of eight days to five years under Luxembourg tax criminal law applicable to directors and tax representatives.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the SCSp limited liability on proof of bad faith: personal recovery of the entire tax debt, disqualification from corporate management, freeze of personal bank accounts, and potential extradition if the director is resident abroad.
Trade Sanctions and Origin Evasion

Statutory sanction: Freezing of assets and funds by the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; customs and excise penalties up to the full value of the goods plus fines up to EUR 250,000; criminal sanctions under Luxembourg and EU sanctions regulations, including imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Direct personal liability of the general partner's directors for sanctions violations; travel bans, asset freezes, and confiscation of personal property; criminal prosecution simultaneously in Luxembourg and the originating jurisdiction.

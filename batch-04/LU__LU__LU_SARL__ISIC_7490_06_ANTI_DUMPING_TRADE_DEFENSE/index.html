Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice; incorporation is filed electronically by the authenticating notary.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended; SARL-specific provisions under Articles 100-1 to 195-1; general provisions on companies and legal persons under Articles 1 to 89.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) and articles of association (statuts), executed before a Luxembourg notary and filed with the RCS through the LBR e-Registre channel.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash upon incorporation; funds must be deposited with a Luxembourg credit institution or held in the notary's segregated account until the RCS registration is effective, with a bank blocking certificate or notary attestation delivered.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance: obtain a fire-safety inspection certificate for office occupancy from the competent fire brigade and an approved bureau de contrôle (fire classification, evacuation routes, extinguishers). Structural clearance: commissioning of a certified structural surveyor to confirm the office floor load and occupancy class. Occupational clearance: Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) workplace visit, emergency plan, first-aid equipment, and CCSS employee registration before commencement of staff operations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash upon incorporation; funds must be deposited with a Luxembourg credit institution or held in the notary's segregated account until the RCS registration is effective, with a bank blocking certificate or notary attestation delivered.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Income Tax Authority; registration portal: MyGuichet.lu / Gestionnaire de Tâches; VAT authority: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative and register with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) to obtain a business registration number; apply for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) with the AEDT using the RCS extract and articles of association; corporate income tax registration is effected automatically with the ACD upon RCS filing; non-resident companies with a permanent establishment or VAT-liable activities in Luxembourg must register for VAT via form 563 and obtain a 10-digit national identification number (matricule) assigned by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is a resident company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) on worldwide income if managed and controlled in Luxembourg; no pass-through election available for SARL). — A foreign entity must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative and register with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) to obtain a business registration number; apply for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) with the AEDT using the RCS extract and articles of association; corporate income tax registration is effected automatically with the ACD upon RCS filing; non-resident companies with a permanent establishment or VAT-liable activities in Luxembourg must register for VAT via form 563 and obtain a 10-digit national identification number (matricule) assigned by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is a resident company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) on worldwide income if managed and controlled in Luxembourg; no pass-through election available for SARL).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or partnership pass-through election for a SARL; optional joint taxation regime for qualifying family holding companies is not applicable; may elect for the special 80% participation exemption on dividend and capital gains if holding thresholds are met; can elect for VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 62 VAT Law; can elect for the Luxembourg IP box regime (50% exemption of qualifying IP income); no hybrid mismatch elections available under domestic law beyond the EU ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 – no general registration threshold; any taxable person supplying goods or services in Luxembourg, including foreign businesses with a fixed establishment or distance sales exceeding EU distance-selling thresholds (EUR 10,000 for cross-border B2C goods/services within the Union) or intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000 per year, must register for Luxembourg VAT; non-established taxable persons must register from the first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations and certain supplies of services related to international trade (including services directly linked to export of goods, transportation, loading, and ancillary services) are zero-rated under Article 56 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; the supplier must retain proof of the physical departure of goods from the EU, commercial documentation evidencing the supply, and, for services, evidence that the recipient is established outside the EU and the service qualifies under the place-of-supply rules; anti-dumping trade defense consulting services are generally B2B supplies to non-resident clients, which are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the general place-of-supply rule (B2B Article 44 VAT Directive) rather than zero-rated; professional consultancy related to export of goods can be zero-rated if directly linked to the export transaction and the supplier obtains written confirmation of the non-EU status of the customer.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) – Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Exit from EU (electronic T1 or ECS statement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation for export proceeds
  • Incoterm contract and proof of delivery terms
  • Vendor declaration of non-EU status (if services)

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B VAT in Luxembourg; however, all taxpayers must submit monthly/quarterly VAT returns electronically via the eCDF platform (Electronic Cash Desk of the AEDT); B2B e-invoicing is permitted under EU Directive 2014/55/EU and Peppol BIS 3.0 standards are accepted; no digital fiscal stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) requirement; foreign taxable persons must file through the VAT One Stop Shop (OSS) for distance sales and the Reverse Charge mechanism applies to certain cross-border supplies.

Misuse of zero-rating for exports triggers reassessment of VAT at 17%, plus interest at the legal rate of 3% per annum; fraudulent claims carry a criminal fine of up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years (Luxembourg General Tax Law, articles 396–398); the AEDT can impose an administrative penalty of up to 10% of the tax evaded, withhold refunds, and conduct retrospective clawbacks for up to 5 years (10 years in cases of tax fraud); taxpayers must produce export documentation on demand or face full VAT reversal and professional disqualification for trade-consulting firms.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible on both capital and current account. Luxembourg applies the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Article 63 prohibition on all capital movement restrictions within the EU, and profit remittance for SARL distributions is unrestricted once annual accounts are approved and the statutory minimum capital is unimpaired. Anti-dumping defense consulting fees are current account services, freely transferable subject only to ordinary invoice and tax reporting. There are no exchange-control approvals, pre-invoice currency authorization, or quantitative limits on dividend repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Luxembourg. Exporters and service providers may retain all foreign currency revenues in designated accounts or convert to EUR at will. No statutory timeline for repatriation is imposed for services; only standard transfer pricing, invoicing, and anti-money-laundering transaction documentation apply. For goods exports, EU customs and VAT obligations apply, but no central bank surrender timeline exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; foreign exchange administration is exercised under EU capital movement law with no separate Luxembourg forex control agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Luxembourg-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under CSSF supervision can operate cross-border via SEPA and SWIFT rails. Offshore digital EMIs not passporting under PSD/EU may not open bank accounts or provide payment services to a Luxembourg SARL without entering into a custodian-bank or agent relationship with a licensed credit institution. Pure digital EMI accounts present moderate friction for statutory anti-dumping defense work because the bank account must support high-value retainer flows, documentary evidence trails, and potentially court-ordered attachment. For non-core incidental collections, a CSSF-licensed fintech EMI account can be complementary but is not acceptable as the sole treasury rail.

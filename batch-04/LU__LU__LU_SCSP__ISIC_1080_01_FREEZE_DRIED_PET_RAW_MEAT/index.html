Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; electronic portals: RCS Connect / Luxembourg Business Registers, MyGuichet.lu, and mytax.lu (AEDT e-filing).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SCSp by notarial or private deed and file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu using the 'RCS e-filing' service; registration generates a 14-digit national business matriculation number and an RCS identification number. Step 2: For VAT, submit the standard VAT registration application to the AEDT; a non-established foreign entity must use Form 563, attaching the deed of constitution, articles of association, foreign commercial register extract, proof of intended taxable activity (freeze-dried pet food manufacture and export), and a power of attorney; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format 'LU' followed by 8 digits. Step 3: For direct taxes, the ACD assigns a corporate income tax reference number based on the RCS data; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, no separate CIT file is opened at the entity level and each partner receives a pro-rata allocation of income for domestic or foreign tax assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is by default fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes; profits, losses, gains, and tax attributes flow through pro-rata to partners. For VAT, however, the SCSp operates as an independent taxable person and is separately registered in its own name for supplies made in Luxembourg. The SCSp is not a Luxembourg tax resident for direct tax purposes, and a commercial-name or commercial-activity characterization does not alter this transparency unless a valid opaque election is made.

Available election forms

Under Article 175 bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), an SCSp may irrevocably elect opaque taxation (treatment as a corporate taxpayer) for a minimum period of 5 fiscal years, provided the partnership is entirely composed of corporate partners and the election is jointly requested by all partners to the ACD before the start of the relevant fiscal year; the election covers CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. No US-style 'check-the-box' regime exists; Luxembourg relies on statutory form and administrative circulars. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD/interest-limitation and anti-hybrid provisions in Articles 168bis to 168quater LIR) apply where a transparent SCSp is treated as opaque in a foreign jurisdiction; the SCSp must document partner-level characterization to avoid double non-taxation and must file an annual tax transparency statement with the ACD where hybrid mismatches are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 — annual taxable turnover threshold below which a Luxembourg-established taxable person qualifies for the small-business exemption from VAT (Article 57 VAT Law). For a non-established foreign supplier (e.g., a foreign investor holding the SCSp), no threshold applies: any taxable B2B or B2C supply of goods or services physically performed or located in Luxembourg triggers mandatory VAT registration with the AEDT irrespective of turnover. The SCSp itself, being established in Luxembourg, applies the EUR 35,000 threshold to its Luxembourg taxable supplies excluding incidental real-estate and financial transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods transported or dispatched outside the European Union by the SCSp, its customer, or a third party acting on the SCSp's behalf qualify for the 0% rate under Article 41 VAT Law, provided the goods leave EU territory and the SCSp retains conclusive export evidence within the statutory period (approximately 3 months from delivery). Intra-Community supplies of freeze-dried pet food to VAT-registered customers in another EU member state qualify for 0% under Article 43 VAT Law, subject to the customer's valid VAT number, a valid recapitulative statement (EC Sales List), and proof of transport. Ancillary services directly linked to the exported goods (freight, handling, quality inspection, cold-chain monitoring) are also zero-rated where performed for the non-EU customer. Services to non-EU B2B customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the general place-of-supply rule. Free-zone and customs-warehouse storage of finished goods prior to export does not trigger VAT if the goods remain under customs supervision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document — SAD) or electronic AES export declaration with confirmed Exit Summary Declaration and customs release stamp from the Administration des douanes et accises
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR waybill, or Airway Bill evidencing physical dispatch and arrival outside the EU (or in the destination member state for intra-Community supply)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing the transfer of export proceeds to the SCSp's bank account and matching the invoice amount

Luxembourg does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for B2B invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G contracts under the PEPPOL network (eFactura format) since 2021. For B2B and B2C, paper or standard electronic invoices (PDF/EDIFACT/UBL) remain accepted; however, all VAT returns (Form 400) and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via the AEDT mytax.lu portal using authenticated API or web forms, and EC Sales Lists are filed separately. There is no pre-clearance of invoices before issuance; the AEDT retains the right to require retrospective digital audit files (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) in a standardized XML schema in case of audit.

The AEDT and the Luxembourg Customs Administration rigorously audit export zero-rating claims; where the SCSp fails to produce valid transport documents, customs clearance proof, or a remittance certificate within the inspection period, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT is reassessed at 17% with statutory late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) calculated from the date the VAT would have been due. Fraudulent or negligent claims trigger penalties under the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung, §§123 and 124) of up to 100% of the tax evaded; intentional fraud constitutes a criminal offense under Article 73 of the VAT Law and Article 496 of the Penal Code, punishable by imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 250,000. The AEDT may also impose joint-and-several liability on the foreign partners of the SCSp for VAT clawed back under the pass-through principle, and customs authorities may apply separate customs-debt sanctions for incorrect export declarations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member state; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account items. Under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and Luxembourg domestic implementing law, there are no exchange controls, no approval requirements for foreign direct investment or portfolio investment into or out of Luxembourg, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or profit remittance to non-residents. Current account payments are unrestricted; profit repatriation by an SCSp to non-resident partners is lawful, subject only to Luxembourg income tax withholding for certain types of share-dealing income or preferential tax statuses (e.g., no withholding tax on interest or royalties; possible taxation at partner level depending on tax residency and treaty eligibility). Cross-border payments in EUR or any convertible currency may be executed freely via EU payment systems; however, transactions exceeding EUR 10,000 or involving certain counterparties may be subject to EU anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing declarations and BCL statistical reporting. The domestic securities and FX market has deep liquidity; no surrender or mandatory conversion was documented under the 'know-your-customer' review for this manufacturing sub-activity at the time of drafting (Q3 2025).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Luxembourg; exporters are not obligated to repatriate export receipts to Luxembourg banks, nor to convert foreign exchange earnings into EUR within any specific timeline. Export invoices may be denominated in foreign currencies, and the resulting foreign currency balances can be held onshore in Luxembourg bank accounts or offshore, free of conversion constraints. Banking practice requires that all incoming cross-border receipts be properly labelled with economic substance and transaction purpose codes for balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical filings, but no conversion ratio or mandatory surrender period applies. For customs and VAT purposes, export declaration evidence (SAD/EX and BCL BoP code) must support the underlying trade invoice and FX settlement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank for the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and financial market conduct supervision; macro-prudential and balance-of-payments statistics reporting is coordinated through the BCL and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are technically feasible as ancillary payment or e-wallet providers for Luxembourg-domiciled SCSp's operational expenses only, provided that they hold a valid authorisation under EU PSD2 and are either based in the EEA with passporting rights into Luxembourg or hold an EEA branch license approved by the CSSF. The viability rating is 'Medium-Low' for primary corporate treasury clearing because (a) Luxembourg banks generally require a domestic or EEA-licensed account for tax and BoP reporting, (b) offshore EMIs (e.g., UK or non-EEA) lack the automatic passport under PSD2 and may need an alternative arrangement or licensing, and (c) strict AML and travel rule requirements under the EU Transfer of Funds Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/847, as amended) require originator and beneficiary information to be transmitted, which many offshore EMIs cannot fully support for commercial supplier payments. For inbound capital contributions from non-EEA partners, an EMI account is not recommended because of heightened sanctions, beneficial ownership transparency and source-of-funds validation requirements. If used as a supplementary disbursement rail for small value trade settlements, contractual and audit trail concerns remain; the primary clearing should be with an established Luxembourg authorised credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners or the general partner into the SCSp's Luxembourg bank account must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pain.001 message with a precise purpose code. For balance-of-payments and BCL statistical reporting, the appropriate transaction nature is 'Direct investment – Equity capital' (CODE 80), further detailed in the BCL's BoP classification as 'Capital contributions to SCSp (unincorporated entity)'. The wire instruction must clearly reference the capital call notice, the SCSp's corporate identity (LEI 254900...), and the bank reference of the partner's own account. The sender bank is requested to include the ultimate originator's BIC, account number, full name, and address in compliance with the Transfer of Funds Regulation. The Luxembourg receiving bank will also validate against the RBE register and AML source of funds. For large contributions over EUR 10,000 or where the partner is in a high-risk third country, the bank shall request a written capital contribution agreement, proof of partner identity, and a source-of-funds declaration. Inbound contributions from EU member states are unrestricted; non-EU inflows must pass through correspondent banks that may require a SWIFT MT202 COV message for interbank transfers to ensure transparency. The recommended protocol is to issue internal funding instructions with the payment narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION LU-SCSP [Legal Entity ID] — NOT FOR TRADE SETTLEMENT'.Outbound disbursements for the purchase of raw meat, freeze-dried pet ingredients, packaging material, and supply chain services will be executed by SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or through commercial documentary letters of credit (L/C) when counterparties require trade finance protection. For ordinary supplier invoices, priority payment rail is SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Goods – Trade Settlement' according to the ISO 20022 category of 'TREA' or 'TRAD'. For large, repetitive high-value commodities, an irrevocable, confirmed commercial documentary letter of credit (UCP600) may be opened by the SCSp's Luxembourg bank in favour of the supplier, with a sight draft or usance dependent on credit terms. The L/C should be denominated in EUR where feasible; if the invoice is in USD or other convertible currency, the SCSp may enter into a foreign exchange forward to fix the settlement amount. Payment disbursements from the SCSp's operating account to the general partner for management fees, reimbursable expenses, and share-dealing income must be separately coded; these are not trade payments and require clear underlying contractual agreements. All outbound transfers from Luxembourg must include the SCSp's LEI and bank reference in the originator field, and must not reference any sanctioned beneficiary; SWIFT opaque dictionaries are automatically screened by the clearing banks. For payments to countries requiring advance import licences, the SCSp's bank may request a copy of the commercial contract, customs declaration, and any relevant veterinary health authorisation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 for related-party transactions (Local File); EUR 50,000,000 for Master File

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities engaged in certain activities. For a manufacturing SCSp, substance is demonstrated through physical presence, employees, and operational assets. The entity must maintain adequate substance to avoid being classified as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional tax of 10% on evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes, subject to travel bans and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, imposition of 15% withholding tax on distributions, and penalties up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years; revocation of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines and subject to travel bans and imprisonment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l’enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and VAT identification; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, net wealth tax and direct tax file numbers; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) for customs and EORI registration; Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) / Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for commercial registration; single e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://www.myguichet.lu) with public filings on https://www.lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — Commercial registration: Incorporate or register the SCSp with the RCS via MyGuichet.lu/eJustice using a notarised partnership agreement (the SCSp is a commercial company under the Law of 23 July 2016; registration is mandatory; no minimum capital requirement). Obtain the RCS number (numéro d’immatriculation) and the Luxembourg Business Register extract. STEP 2 — Tax file: Request a direct tax file number (numéro de dossier) with the ACD for the Luxembourg corporate income tax and municipal business tax assessments; the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, so the ACD will attribute income to the general and limited partners. STEP 3 — VAT registration: File the VAT declaration of commencement of activity (Form 560) with the AEDT within 15 days of starting taxable operations, either electronically via MyGuichet.lu or in paper form; the AEDT will issue a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 11 digits. A foreign entity with no Luxembourg establishment providing services to a Luxembourg taxable person is generally not required to register because the customer applies the reverse-charge mechanism; however, a foreign SCSp/GP that makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg, performs distance sales, or opts for the franchise waiver must appoint a local VAT representative (assujetti établi) if it has no fixed establishment in Luxembourg. STEP 4 — Customs/EORI: Where goods are exported or imported, obtain a Luxembourg EORI number from the ADA (format LU + RCS number) and file customs declarations electronically. STEP 5 — Employer/non-resident partner formalities: Register with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) only if employing staff; non-resident partners are not subject to Luxembourg personal income tax on carried interest unless the profits are attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is by default fiscally transparent: profits, losses, carried interest and management fees are taxed directly in the hands of the partners (no Luxembourg CIT/municipal business tax/net wealth tax at entity level). Available statutory elections: (1) Article 5(1) LIR election — the SCSp may irrevocably elect to be treated as a resident corporate taxpayer (assujettissement à l’impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and thereby become subject to Luxembourg CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the election is binding on all partners. (2) Hybrid mismatch adjustment elections under Article 168ter LIR (ATAD 2) allowing re-attribution of payments and offsetting of double-deduction/deduction-no-inclusion outcomes. (3) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law. (4) Option to waive the small-business VAT franchise exemption (Article 57 VAT Law). (5) At constitutional level, the SCSp may optionally be endowed with legal personality under the Law of 23 July 2016, but this does not automatically alter fiscal transparency absent an Article 5(1) LIR election. There is no US-style check-the-box regime; the Article 5(1) LIR election is the functional equivalence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 — small-business franchise exemption threshold for annual taxable turnover excluding VAT (Article 57 VAT Law; threshold increased from EUR 30,000 to EUR 35,000 effective 1 January 2023). The exemption is optional (taxable person may waive it). There is no threshold for compulsory registration of a Luxembourg-established entity performing taxable transactions, for intra-community acquisitions (registration required where the acquisitor exceeds EUR 10,000 union-wide distance-selling threshold), or for foreign suppliers that are deemed to have their place of supply in Luxembourg. A venture capital GP/SCSp earning management fees will typically exceed EUR 35,000 and must register from day one.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations: 0% VAT applies under Article 54 of the Luxembourg VAT Law provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, the customer, or a third party on behalf of either, and the supplier obtains and retains evidence of export within 90 days of the time of supply pursuant to Article 45a of Implementing Regulation (EU) No 282/2011. Cross-border B2B services to customers established outside the EU: place of supply is outside Luxembourg under Article 10 of the VAT Law (B2B general rule), and the service is therefore outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (effective 0% with no right of artificial input deduction in Luxembourg). Management of special investment funds: management services provided to UCITS, SIF, SICAR, RAIF, or an SCSp fund that qualifies as a 'special investment fund' under the CJEU B-Finance criteria (collective investment of multiple investors, risk-spreading, regulated or equivalent foreign fund) are VAT-exempt without credit under Article 44(1)(c) of the VAT Law — this is the core treatment for a VC GP; if the underlying fund does not qualify, management fees are taxable at 17%. Carried interest and profit allocations to the GP are not consideration for a supply and fall outside the scope of VAT. Capital calls and disposal proceeds of fund investments are outside the scope of VAT. The GP/SCSp must map each revenue stream (management fee, carried interest, transaction break fees, board/consulting fees) separately, because only export-of-goods scenarios trigger the 0% export rate, while qualifying fund management is exempt, and ancillary advisory services may be taxable.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Single Administrative Document (SAD) / electronic export declaration (IE-529) bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) issued by Luxembourg Customs (ADA) or the competent EU customs office of export
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR or handing-over certificate issued by a carrier/logistics provider confirming physical departure of the goods from EU territory — to be obtained within 90 days from the time of supply
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the non-EU buyer, Incoterms, and the exact nature of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds — functionally required as audit evidence under AML/CRS and Luxembourg trade-finance diligence practice, although EU/Luxembourg law itself imposes no compulsory currency-repatriation certificate
  • KYC/UBO documentation of the non-EU customer, warehouse dispatch records, and where applicable periodic supplementary declarations and Common Transit Convention documents

Luxembourg applies a phased e-invoicing regime: (1) B2G e-invoicing is mandatory since 1 January 2024 for invoices issued to the Luxembourg public sector, transmitted via the PEPPOL network in the EN 16931 semantic data model and routed through the national e-Factura platform — this includes invoices for fund management services rendered to public pension/reserve funds if such bodies are contracting authorities. (2) B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary (PEPPOL, EDI, or PDF with qualified electronic signature are all accepted); Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, no API pre-clearance, no digital stamp (bollino), and no continuous transaction controls (CTCs) comparable to Italy or Hungary. (3) The EU ViDA reform, when adopted (expected entry into force mid-2030s), will introduce mandatory EU-wide B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting; Luxembourg has announced readiness to transpose ViDA without early adoption. (4) All e-invoices must be archived in their original electronic format for 10 years in accordance with Articles 84 and 86 of the Luxembourg VAT Law.

Luxembourg and EU law impose no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline: the free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) guarantees unrestricted transfer of export proceeds, and the 180-day benchmark reflected in this dossier is a trade-finance and documentary-credit convention, not a legal clawback trigger. Audit scrutiny: the AEDT and the ACD may reopen and reassess VAT and income tax within 5 years for good-faith cases and up to 10 years in cases of fraud or absence of good faith; the customs authorities may likewise revoke the 0% export treatment when proof of export is not obtained within 90 days, resulting in retrospective VAT assessment at 17% on the full export value. Clawback of input VAT deductions is automatic where zero-rating or exemption is denied. Penalties: late VAT payment accrues statutory default interest; formal non-compliance fines range from EUR 250 to EUR 12,500; negligent understatement attracts a 10% surcharge on the evaded tax, rising to 50% in cases of intent; criminal tax fraud (Article 2 of the Law of 17 March 1987 and Article 62 of the VAT Law) is punishable by fines up to EUR 25,000 and/or imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years. The GP and the fund are jointly and severally liable for the VAT due on invoices issued by the SCSp; directors and managing partners may face personal liability for fraudulent zero-rating and for failure to correct false export evidence, and reputational damage includes debarment from public procurement and inclusion in the AEDT non-compliant database.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The euro is a reserve currency fully convertible for current and capital transactions within the EU's free movement of capital and payments (Article 63 TFEU). Luxembourg imposes no import/export of capital authorisations, no foreign-exchange surrender or rationing, no parallel-market controls and no repatriation ceilings. Capital contributions into the SCSp, reinvestment of proceeds, profit and carried-interest distributions and management-fee remittances may move in any currency with no central-bank approval. Residual friction points are bank-level AML/CFT screening, the BCL's statistical balance-of-payments declaration (filed by the bank, with possible resident-enterprise information requests for direct-investment flows) and investor-side exchange controls in the LP's home jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg has no export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio or holding-period requirement. Management fees, carried interest and other service receipts denominated in USD or other foreign currencies may be retained in original currency in a Luxembourg foreign-currency account, or converted at arms-length bank rates, without reference to the BCL. The only administrative duty is statistical: cross-border receipts above the de-minimis threshold (typically EUR 12,500 per transaction) are reported to the BCL by the receiving bank as part of the Eurosystem balance-of-payments reporting framework; no export dossier is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Luxembourg's Eurosystem central bank, responsible for monetary operations, financial stability and balance-of-payments statistics; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) — the competent financial-sector and AML/CFT supervisor for credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions; Ministère des Finances — exchange-control policy in the narrow statutory matters (none apply to standard venture-capital treasury flows); European Central Bank (ECB) — ultimate monetary authority of the euro area. No central-bank licence or exchange-control approval is required for an SCSp GP's ordinary treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — viable only as an ancillary rail, not as the primary treasury. Luxembourg-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs authorised by the CSSF/ECB under PSD2) will accept an SCSp where the partnership has an EEA-based general partner and the account mandate is properly executed, but the SCSp's default lack of separate legal personality, the contractual nature of the partnership, and non-EEA UBOs cause many automated KYB engines to reject onboarding. Funds held at an EMI are e-money tokens, not bank deposits, and are outside the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (Fonds de Garantie Dépôts Luxembourg); balances should be capped at an operational float (recommended below EUR 10,000) and swept daily to a tier-1 bank account. Legal constraints: the EMI account agreement must be signed by the general partner in its own name on behalf of the SCSp, the partnership agreement must empower the GP accordingly, and PSD2/EMD2 permits EMI accounts only for payment services, so an EMI cannot legally hold the long-term escrow or committed capital, which must sit with a licensed credit institution as a deposit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR - Local File mandatory if consolidated gross turnover reaches EUR 5,000,000 or related-party transactions with a single counterparty exceed EUR 500,000 (financial transactions above EUR 100,000); Master File required when the group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report due when group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style ESR codes, but substance is imposed through Art. 6 AAR LIR (GAAR), ATAD interest limitation rules, CJEU jurisprudence, and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test; administrative practice (Circular L.I.R. 56/1) requires a Luxembourg office, qualified staff, local business direction, and board/meeting minutes. A VC GP SCSp must demonstrate real presence, employment substance, and economic decision-making to preserve treaty benefits and fiscal transparency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Civil tax penalty of 50% of the evaded tax plus late interest of 0.8% per month; criminal fine between EUR 250 and EUR 12,500 per count, doubled on recidivism; imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years (Art. 319 LIR).

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly assessed for the evaded corporate tax where willful misconduct is proven; criminal conviction triggers restitution orders, director disqualification, and travel bans for outstanding tax claims above EUR 15,000.
Shell Company / GAAR (Artificial Arrangement)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation under Art. 6 AAR LIR with re-qualified taxable income and a 10% penalty; denial of DTA and EU Directive benefits under the CJEU Danish Cases and MLI Principal Purpose Test; retroactive assessments plus 0.8% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: GP directors face joint and several restitution of the adjusted tax; in bankruptcy, the action en comblement du passif (Art. 495 of the Law of 10 August 1915) exposes directors to personal liability for the entire tax shortfall.
AML / CFT Breach (Medium-Risk Enhanced)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1.25 million for legal entities under the Law of 12 November 2004; confiscation of proceeds; CRF (Cellsule de renseignement financier) sanctions; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors are personally liable for CDD failures; criminal prosecution applies when intent is shown; travel restrictions and freezing orders may be issued during cross-border AML investigations.
Withholding Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: Retroactive withholding at 15%; penalty of 10% of the under-withheld amount under Art. 174 LIR; 0.8% monthly late-payment interest; denial of refund claims for five years.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as paying agents are personally liable for uncollected withholding tax if distributions are made before tax settlement; criminal fraud sanctions apply where certificates are falsified.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment under Art. 56 LIR; upward adjustment of the taxable base to the arm's-length level; penalty of 10% on the additional assessment; 0.8% monthly interest; denial of mutual agreement procedure relief if documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the pricing are jointly and severally liable for the additional assessments; severe cases trigger professional disqualification and criminal penalties for fraudulent reporting.

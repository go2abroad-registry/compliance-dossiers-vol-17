Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is generally permissible in Luxembourg for an SCSp conducting a low-risk financial newswire API business, provided that a physical registered office is nonetheless established in Luxembourg by way of a corporate domiciliation service. The virtual office must involve a reliable and fully managed correspondence address, a physical presence comprising employer or management oversight in case of regulatory inquiries, and compliance with the registered office obligations under the Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended. The virtual office must be provided by a registered corporate domiciliation agent in accordance with the Law of 31 May 1999 governing the occupancy of a registered office (Loi du 31 mai 1999). A simple post-office box or purely electronic virtual office will not meet the statutory substance requirements. The SCSp must maintain its central administration place in Luxembourg, and the newswire API activity must achieve taxable nexus in Luxembourg.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers office; RBE unit for beneficial ownership; RESA electronic gazette for legal publication

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 1, 27-30 and Articles 902-1 et seq. (SCSp regime inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013 on Alternative Investment Funds)

Formation / constitutional filings

Constitutive partnership agreement (contrat de société / société en commandite spéciale) executed by private deed or notarial deed, together with the RCS declaration form (Formulaire B) and the appointment of the manager (gérant), filed electronically via LBRonline

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) - portal acd.public.lu; Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) - VAT portal pvat.public.lu; unified e-government filing portal MyGuichet.lu; commercial registry operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SCSp (Societe en Commandite Speciale) by notarial or private deed and register the filing with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu); obtain the RCS matricule (national business identification number) and proof of registered office in Luxembourg. Step 2 - For indirect taxes, submit the AEDT VAT registration application through the MyGuichet.lu single portal; the AEDT assigns the SCSp a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. Because the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent, it does not obtain a separate corporate income-tax TIN; it files an annual partnership information return, and each partner - including foreign investors and the corporate or individual general partner - is taxed on its allocable share. Non-resident partners must obtain a Luxembourg direct-tax matricule and file a non-resident tax return where the SCSp's commercial activity in Luxembourg creates a permanent establishment for them under the applicable double tax treaty. Foreign entities acting as investors or as corporate general partner must supply certified corporate existence and tax residence certificates at registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent under Luxembourg law (Article 50 of the Law of 12 July 2013 on alternative investment funds and Circular L.I.R. No. 14/3). It is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax; income, gains, deductions and credits flow through to partners. For VAT, the AEDT applies fiscal transparency to contractual partnerships without legal personality where the SCSp lacks independent economic activity, but the SCSp remains a liable person for VAT administration and must satisfy registration, invoicing and return obligations.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime; legal form governs tax classification. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT grouping election under Article 60 of the VAT Law, allowing the SCSp to join a VAT group with its general partner and affiliates (joint and several VAT liability applies); (ii) real-estate VAT option under Articles 44-45 of the VAT Law, waiving exempt treatment for real estate supplies; (iii) OSS / non-Union OSS election on the EU One-Stop-Shop portal for B2C electronic services, permitting Luxembourg VAT compliance for cross-border supplies to consumers in other Member States; (iv) partnership profit-allocation election, where the partnership agreement can define profit shares, subject to substance and economic-contribution defences; and (v) ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch alignment, where a foreign jurisdiction characterises the SCSp as opaque, requiring automatic counter-measures for double deduction or non-inclusion rather than a taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 - annual taxable turnover threshold for the VAT small-business exemption (franchise) under Article 47 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (raised from EUR 15,000 to EUR 35,000 effective 1 July 2020). The franchise is unavailable to non-established foreign suppliers and to persons opting into the normal regime; foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg have no turnover threshold and must register from the first supply, or use OSS for EU B2C e-services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: physical export of goods from the EU customs territory qualifies for zero rating under Article 62 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the supplier obtains proof of exit (accepted customs export declaration and transport documents) and retains it for the statutory record-keeping period. Services: the financial newswire API constitutes electronically supplied services (Annex II, EU VAT Directive); where supplied to a taxable customer outside Luxembourg (intra-EU or third-country), Luxembourg VAT is reverse-charged or is outside territorial scope (B2B place of supply at the customer's establishment); where supplied to an end consumer resident outside the EU, no Luxembourg VAT arises (place of supply is the customer's residence). The SCSp must document the non-Luxembourg or non-EU status of each customer (validated VAT ID via VIES for EU business customers, business registration or residence evidence for third-country customers), the executed subscription or API framework agreement, and the actual receipt of consideration through the selected payment rails (SWIFT wire or Stripe). Input VAT on costs attributable to zero-rated or outside-scope exports remains creditable, and the resulting credit is refundable through the periodic Luxembourg VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EU customs declaration accepted at the office of export, with MRN reference and exit confirmation at the office of exit)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note proving physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and export contract evidencing the international consignment chain (supplier, buyer, consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds on the SCSp's account
  • For services: customer's foreign business registration or VAT ID, executed subscription or API framework agreement, and digital usage logs or API authentication records supporting service delivery

A phased B2B e-invoicing mandate applies under the Luxembourg e-invoicing law adopted in 2024: from 1 January 2025, every Luxembourg-established taxable person must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2026, such taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices (e.g. Peppol BIS 3.0, EN 16931/UBL format) to Luxembourg-established B2B customers, unless the recipient is outside scope. Luxembourg does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance (continuous transaction controls) or digital-stamping system; e-invoices flow over the Peppol network without prior tax-authority validation. E-invoice metadata is accessible to the AEDT during audits. For cross-border B2C e-services (financial newswire API subscriptions), the EU One-Stop-Shop portal (Union OSS and non-Union OSS) is the statutory e-reporting and remittance channel; intra-EU B2B services are reported via quarterly recapitulative statements, not through e-invoice clearance.

Luxembourg law imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is applied here as a documentary-substantiation benchmark and commercial prudential standard. Failure to evidence receipt of export proceeds within a reasonable period may prompt the AEDT to question economic reality and to disallow zero rating. If export zero rating is insufficiently documented, the AEDT retroactively claws back the VAT exemption and assesses Luxembourg VAT at 17% plus late-payment interest (currently 0.6% per month or fraction of a month) and, in cases of negligence, an administrative penalty that can reach up to 100% of the evaded tax. Intentional misstatement or false export documentation constitutes tax fraud (fraude fiscale) under the Luxembourg General Tax Law as strengthened by the Law of 6 March 2019, carrying imprisonment of up to five years and significant criminal fines, as well as loss of good tax standing. Customs-law non-compliance (inaccurate export declarations) triggers penalties under Article 42 of the Union Customs Code as implemented in Luxembourg customs law, together with post-clearance recovery of customs duties within applicable statutory limitation periods (generally up to three years, extended where prosecutable offences are present). The AEDT recovery period for under-assessed VAT is generally up to five years, extended to ten years where fraudulent intent is shown. Refund claims built on sham export paper are treated as unjustified VAT refunds and are clawed back with the above penalties plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the sole legal tender; Luxembourg applies full capital and current account convertibility under the EU Treaty and the EU Capital Movements Directive (Article 63 TFEU). No exchange controls exist for non-resident investors; profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted. Deductions or distributions are subject only to standard corporate, withholding tax, and DAC6/ATAD reporting obligations. Balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL is mandatory for resident enterprises for cross-border flows above EUR 12,500 per transaction, but this constitutes statistical monitoring, not approval or restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion requirements apply. Luxembourg and the euro area impose no mandated timeline or ratio for converting export or service receipts into local currency. Receipts in foreign currency may be retained, converted, or hedged freely; only statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL are required where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the prudential and AML/CFT supervision authority for credit institutions and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs), whether domestic or passporting under PSD2, are viable for operational sub-accounts and low-value newswire subscription receipts. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA licence or Luxembourg passport are legally barred from providing payment services to a Luxembourg entity on a cross-border basis; capital escrow and regulatory funds must be held with a fully licensed credit institution. Fintech EMIs generally cannot issue documentary credits or offer commercial LC capacity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: for equity and shareholder loans into the Luxembourg SCSp, use SWIFT MT103 via the injecting bank to the BIC of the SCSp's Luxembourg account; SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) with the dedicated purpose code 'B040' or 'C001' or an accurately aligned ECB BOP statistical code, depending on the commercial substance; purpose code 'EQUI' or 'N005' (equity injection) is commonly accepted by BCL for balance-of-payments reporting. Verification bundle: signed capital call notice, subscribed partnership units or contribution deed, board or managing partner resolution, bank debit/credit advices, and a confirmation from the Luxembourg registered office.

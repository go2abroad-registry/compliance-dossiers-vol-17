Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. An SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no court escrow or blocked account requirement. Partner contributions are freely contractually determined and may be made in cash, in kind, or as services, without notarial attestation of payment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety conformity certification for the registered office under the communal fire-prevention regulations; occupational safety registration with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM); no structural load inspection is required for standard administrative premises.

Paid-in capital rule: None. An SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no court escrow or blocked account requirement. Partner contributions are freely contractually determined and may be made in cash, in kind, or as services, without notarial attestation of payment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for entity registration; single digital government portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu, with electronic filing via the eCDF module of the tax authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SCSp limited partnership agreement before a Luxembourg notary and file the constitutive dossier with the RCS Luxembourg, which issues an 11-digit national business registration number ('matricule') that serves as the statutory corporate tax reference. Step 2: Submit the VAT registration application (Formulaire 640/641 as applicable) to the AEDT through MyGuichet; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits. Registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable turnover for any person carrying out an economic activity in Luxembourg; no minimum capital is required and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Step 3: Register with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) if the SCSp employs staff. Step 4: Non-resident partners do not require a Luxembourg TIN unless a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg-source income exists; the entity matricule remains the sole direct-tax reference for the partnership.

The SCSp is not a separate taxpayer for Luxembourg net wealth tax, and no minimum net wealth tax applies at entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The SCSp is a contractual partnership without separate legal personality and is mandatorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal); income is allocated to partners and taxed at their level. The SCSp is not a separate taxpayer for Luxembourg net wealth tax, and no minimum net wealth tax applies at entity level. For VAT, the SCSp is independently a taxable person where it conducts an economic activity. Territorial scope: resident partners are taxed on worldwide income, with the participation exemption (95% exemption for qualifying dividends and capital gains) available; non-resident partners are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income, principally through a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or general optional entity classification exists under Luxembourg domestic law; partnership transparency is compulsory. Statutory elections available: (i) small-business VAT franchise election under Article 57 VAT Law where annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000; (ii) if the SCSp qualifies as an 'investment entity' under the EU Minimum Tax Directive (2022/2523, implemented by the Luxembourg law of 22 December 2023), an election to be treated as a Tax Transparent Investment Vehicle (TTIV) or Taxable Investment Vehicle (TIV) for GloBE/Pillar 2 purposes; (iii) joint VAT-group election (assujetti unique) under Article 22(1) VAT Law; (iv) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration election for EU-wide B2C electronically supplied services; (v) partner-level use of the participation exemption and ATAD-compliant hybrid-mismatch consistency elections for cross-border investors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons: registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable turnover. A small-business franchise exemption is available where annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000 and quarterly turnover does not exceed EUR 8,750, subject to conditions and prior claims; the franchise cannot be used for intra-Community acquisitions or imports. Non-established suppliers of digital services to Luxembourg consumers are required to VAT-register either directly or via the OSS; the SCSp developing the financial-portfolio-tracking App should use the Union OSS scheme to declare B2C electronic supplies in all EU Member States of the consumer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods: physical exports to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 56 VAT Law where dispatch or transport to a third country is evidenced; intra-EU supplies qualify for 0% domestic VAT with a valid customer VAT ID and inclusion in the EC Sales List. (2) Services: B2B supplies of the financial-portfolio-tracking software are outside Luxembourg VAT where the customer is established outside Luxembourg (EU customer: reverse charge; non-EU customer: no Luxembourg VAT). (3) B2C electronically supplied services are taxed where the consumer is established: 17% Luxembourg VAT applies only to Luxembourg-resident consumers; supplies to other EU consumers are reported through OSS at the consumer-state rate; supplies to non-EU consumers fall outside the scope of EU VAT. (4) Exemption note: only if the App genuinely performs discretionary management of special investment funds (UCITS/AIF) does the Article 44(1)(m) VAT exemption apply; standard analytics and portfolio-tracking functionality constitutes a taxable digital service at the standard rate and is not eligible for zero-rating.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU

B2G electronic invoicing has been mandatory since 1 January 2024 through the Luxembourg central e-invoicing portal in PEPPOL format. A mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime for domestic transactions is scheduled for 1 July 2026 (implementation decrees pending, aligned with the EU ViDA package). Luxembourg does not currently apply a real-time fiscal pre-clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls regime; the model is post-audit with electronic archiving. Taxpayers must accept structured electronic invoices (EN 16931-conformant) and retain SAF-T-style electronic files and the EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) for intra-EU supplies.

Luxembourg law does not impose a formal statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as the operational documentary-retention standard for export substantiation. If the exporter cannot produce the complete evidentiary bundle, the AEDT may claw back the zero rating: VAT becomes payable at the standard rate (17%) plus late-payment interest of 0.8% per month and late-filing surcharges (up to 10%). Fraudulent export zero-rating, forged transport documents, or sham disposal of goods constitute criminal VAT fraud under Article 71 of the VAT Law, attracting fines up to EUR 250,000, imprisonment of up to 5 years, administrative penalties of up to 200% of the evaded VAT, and a reassessment period extended from 5 to 10 years. Incorrect OSS filings trigger additional penalties in the Member State of consumption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the European Union and the Eurozone; the euro is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls, no approval requirements for inward or outward remittances, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Capital and money market operations are fully liberalized under EU Treaty provisions and the Capital Movement Directive. The only administrative obligation is statistical reporting to the BCL for balance-of-payments (BoP) and international investment position (IIP) purposes when cross-border flows exceed de minimis thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or maintain mandatory conversion ratios. Luxembourg does not impose any repatriation, holding, or conversion obligations on export receipts; proceeds may be retained in any currency. Reporting to BCL is limited to BoP statistical filings for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 as part of the monthly reporting framework for enterprises engaged in external trade.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking licensing and AML/CTF oversight of financial market participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility only for ancillary payment facilitation. A locally authorized Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under CSSF, or an EU passported EMI from another EEA state, can legally open a corporate account and provide IBANs, payment initiation, and virtual accounts. However, for the SCSp as an unregulated private investment vehicle, any EMI solution is not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank account due to regulatory capital constraints, higher compliance requirements, and the absence of commercial lending or treasury management infrastructure. Restricted-use sub-accounts may be acceptable for low-risk software subscriptions, but the primary treasury account must be held with a fully licensed credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital contributions and shareholder loans to the SCSp must be paid from a verified corporate or personal bank account in the name of the capital provider to the SCSp's designated RMB receipt account (or a EUR account at a Tier-1 bank). Wire transfers must carry an accurate purpose code and a clear reference to the capital contribution clause of the partnership agreement. Under Luxembourg BCL statistical reporting, any inbound transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported in the monthly BoP declaration with the proper classification code for direct investment (e.g., participation in capital) or loans. No forex conversion is required; the euro is the reporting currency and the SCSp may maintain multi-currency accounts. Inbound transfers from non-EEA jurisdictions must include a full remitter name, address, and UBO/beneficial ownership breakdown to satisfy the CSSF's AML/CFT rulebook (Regulation 12-02), and the bank will verify the underlying documentation (capital contribution statement, notarized resolution where requested) before credit.Outbound trade settlements should be made via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for cryptocurrency-independent software SaaS payments; for invoice settlements above an agreed bank threshold, an MT103 with field 70 specifying the service description and invoice number is standard. Commercial letters of credit (L/C) are generally not required for low-risk digital services but may be used when the SCSp expands into physical infrastructure or high-value subscription licensing, in which case a confirmed irrevocable L/C issued by a Luxembourg or EU bank is recommended. For SEPA-based recurring payments within the EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is the default rail with a maximum execution time of 1 business day. All outbound disbursements must be backed by the underlying contractual agreements kept in the SCSp's treasury record; no cash or bearer-instrument disbursements are permitted. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility only for ancillary payment facilitation. A locally authorized Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under CSSF, or an EU passported EMI from another EEA state, can legally open a corporate account and provide IBANs, payment initiation, and virtual accounts. However, for the SCSp as an unregulated private investment vehicle, any EMI solution is not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank account due to regulatory capital constraints, higher compliance requirements, and the absence of commercial lending or treasury management infrastructure. Restricted-use sub-accounts may be acceptable for low-risk software subscriptions, but the primary treasury account must be held with a fully licensed credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 100,000 per counterparty per fiscal year; Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report threshold is EUR 750,000,000 group turnover.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg enforces the ATAD general anti-abuse rule and the OECD economic-substance standard. An SCSp claiming Luxembourg tax residence or treaty benefits must demonstrate real operational substance in Luxembourg: qualified decision-making employees, registered physical office, actual board meetings, own risk management, and proportionate expenditure. Passive shell or cash-box SCSp structures used without genuine substance are recharacterized and denied residence certificates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate False Statement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment for up to 10 years, default interest of 0.6% per month, fiscal penalty up to 25% of the underpaid tax, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 1,000,000 under Luxembourg tax fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors who sign or approve knowingly false tax declarations are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax. Courts may order freezing of personal assets, travel bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment in serious cases.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Tax administration applies the arm's length principle, recharacterizes the transactions, adjusts Luxembourg taxable income, disallows abusive deductions, imposes penalties up to EUR 100,000 plus monthly interest, and may deny treaty relief where arrangement lacks commercial rationality.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length payments are personally liable for resulting corporate tax, and a Luxembourg court may pierce the corporate veil if the SCSp is used as a pure conduit for abusive profit stripping, including personal attachment orders.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Refusal or retroactive withdrawal of Luxembourg tax residence certificate, denial of treaty withholding relief, full re-taxation in Luxembourg under GAAR, administrative fines up to EUR 100,000, and mandatory strike-off if the RBE or trade register data are false.

Director personal liability: General partners and formal directors may be held civilly liable for restitution of unpaid taxes; criminal investigation may lead to asset freeze, travel prohibition, individual fines up to EUR 250,000, or imprisonment for fraudulent use of corporate form.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for RBE non-compliance up to EUR 250,000 and daily penalty of EUR 250 per day; AML Law 2019 sanctions up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to four years; regulatory prohibition of future payments and business restriction.

Director personal liability: Managing partners, compliance officers, and beneficial owners incur personal criminal liability, civil damages, disqualification from managerial roles, travel bans, seizure of personal and corporate assets, and imprisonment where the platform is used for money laundering.

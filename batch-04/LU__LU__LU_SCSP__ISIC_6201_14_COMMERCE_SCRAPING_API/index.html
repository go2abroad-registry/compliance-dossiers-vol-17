E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and SCSp partnerships without a Luxembourg seat must obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number before effecting their first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Procedure: (1) Confirm whether the SCSp creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment; for direct-tax purposes the fiscally transparent SCSp requires no entity-level corporate tax registration, as income is taxed at partner level. (2) Submit VAT Form 106 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) to the AEDT either directly via MyGuichet.lu or through a Luxembourg fiscal representative (mandatory for non-EU principals; optional for EU principals). (3) Provide the SCSp partnership deed (notarized or apostilled extract), identity and residence evidence of the general partner and limited partners, service contracts and API gateway agreements, hosting or proxy-server infrastructure documents showing Luxembourg nexus, certificate of foreign tax registration, and VIES confirmation where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is a contract-type special limited partnership without legal personality under the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915, as amended. Under Article 184 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), the SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent: no corporate income tax, no municipal business tax, and no net-wealth tax arise at the entity level. Each partner (general or limited) is taxed individually on their allocable share of partnership income; foreign partners are then subject to the territorial and residence rules of their home jurisdiction. The SCSp therefore does not appear as a separate taxpayer on the Luxembourg direct-tax register.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no statutory check-the-box election comparable to US Federal Form 8832; SCSp transparency is a mandatory legal default under Article 184 LIR. Available elections and instruments are: (i) drafting the partnership agreement with flexible profit and loss allocations, subject to economic-substance review and the general anti-abuse provision under Article 6 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (AStG); (ii) applying for an advance tax ruling from the ACD to confirm entity classification, permanent-establishment treatment and anti-hybrid outcomes binding on the tax authorities; (iii) electing symmetrical treatment for hybrid entities under ATAD I and II (Articles 168ter et seq. LIR) so as to eliminate double non-taxation or double deduction mismatches; (iv) for US partners, making a US check-the-box election to treat the SCSp as an association taxable as a corporation — such election creates a hybrid entity whose Luxembourg tax consequences must then be neutralized under the anti-hybrid rules unless the partners make offsetting deemed-payment adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established suppliers: EUR 0 — no monetary threshold; VAT registration is immediately triggered by any taxable supply made in Luxembourg. Luxembourg-established small businesses: annual turnover exemption up to EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT), with compulsory registration thereafter. Cross-border B2C distance sales of e-services within the EU: EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold; below this threshold Luxembourg VAT applies, above it registration or the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme in the customer's EU Member State becomes mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the relevant digital services (public commercial web scraping and proxy API gateways): B2B supplies to non-EU established customers are taxable at the customer's place of business under the general place-of-supply rule, giving an effective Luxembourg VAT rate of 0% with the customer self-assessing any local VAT. B2B supplies to EU customers are reverse-charged to the recipient (Article 58 LVT) and are likewise outside Luxembourg VAT. B2C e-services to non-EU consumers fall outside Luxembourg's territorial scope. Physical export of goods (e.g., server hardware or media embodying the API service) qualifies for the 0% export rate under Article 42 LVT provided dispatch or transport to a third-country destination is completed within 90 days of despatch and evidenced; intra-Community supplies require the customer's valid EU VAT number and listing in the recapitulative statement. In all cases, the supplier must retain complete documentary proof of delivery, identity of the recipient, and actual payment for at least 10 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document / EAD) — required only for physical goods, not for purely digital API services
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / air waybill evidencing transport to the third-country destination, where physical hardware or media is exported

No real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC), no mandatory digital stamping, and no Italian-style clearance system apply in Luxembourg as of 2025. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies exclusively to business-to-government (B2G) supplies via the PEPPOL network and UBL/BIS 3.0 structured formats, pursuant to the Grand-Ducal Regulation implementing EU Directive 2014/55/EU. B2B e-invoicing remains optional and may use PDF, Factur-X/ZUGFeRD, or UN/EDIFACT formats. Invoices must be archived for 10 years; OSS-return documentation and customer-location evidence (IP addresses, bank details, country of establishment certificates, SIM cards, billing addresses) must also be retained for 10 years.

The AEDT may reassess zero-rated or non-established supplies within the standard 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud, falsified documents or abusive arrangements. On challenge, the full 17% Luxembourg VAT becomes due together with late-payment interest of 0.8% per month (9.6% per annum). Administrative fines range from EUR 250 to EUR 12,500 per invoice under Article 63 LVT; where the exporter knowingly submits forged export evidence or conceals the real recipient, criminal fines up to double the evaded tax and imprisonment may apply under Article 64 LVT. Partner and general-manager joint-and-several liability can further attach under Luxembourg anti-abuse principles where the data-pipeline services are routed through an artificial structure to disguise the true exporter or beneficiary. Payment received materially later than 180 days may be treated as evidence of a rescinded sale, supporting denial of the zero rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both capital and current account transactions throughout Luxembourg. There are no statutory exchange controls, no prior-authorisation duties for profit remittances, and no restrictions on incoming direct investment. Dividends or profit shares paid by a taxable Luxembourg entity are subject to potential Luxembourg withholding tax, but SCSp tax-transparent vehicles are not themselves subject to withholding at entity level; foreign partners are taxed according to their own residence status. The only practical hurdles arise from EU/UN sanctions, AML customer-due-diligence obligations, and the requirement that all cash payments exceeding EUR 2,500 be settled through a credit institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg has no export-proceeds surrender rules and imposes no mandatory conversion ratios. Export/API revenue held in foreign currency may be retained freely. However, BCL Regulation 2017/01 and CSSF reporting obligations require resident companies (including SCsps) to report cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 (or equivalent) for balance-of-payments statistical purposes within a defined calendar month. Failure to report may result in administrative fines, but no surrender or conversion of foreign currency is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in close coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) under the Eurosystem; cross-border capital movement oversight is additionally governed by the CSSF for financial-sector participants and by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. A Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2 may offer full IBAN, SEPA and SWIFT access, making it viable as a treasury gateway. However, a pure offshore EMI without a Luxembourg/EU licence cannot be used as a primary settlement bank because the CSSF requires payment institutions to be authorised in an EU/EEA state. Additionally, the target payment rail of USDC/USD can only be integrated through a CSSF-registered Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP), because under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004, as amended by the Law of 15 August 2019, any entity engaging in virtual-asset custody, exchange or transfer is subject to a VASP licence. Therefore, the recommended fintech stack is: (1) a Luxembourg-licensed EMI for fiat SEPA/SWIFT, (2) a CSSF-registered VASP for regulated USDC conversion, and (3) an internal record-keeping system that maps each digital wallet to a legal entity and invoice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound partner capital contributions must be transferred by SEPA or SWIFT to the SCSp's dedicated EUR escrow account maintained with a Luxembourg/EU bank. Payment reference must clearly state: (1) the SCSp commercial name and RCS number (e.g., 'RCS Luxembourg BXXXXX'), (2) the purpose of payment as 'Capital Contribution to SCSp', and (3) an appropriate BCL balance-of-payments code (e.g., 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'). For USD injections, use SWIFT MT103 field 70 to include the same description and ensure that the converting bank is an EU-authorized institution. Any cash component above EUR 2,500 is unlawful. The bank must conduct a source-of-funds verification and record the transaction in the BCL statistical report (Form PROC/IT) when the value exceeds EUR 500,000.Trade settlements (e.g., server infrastructure, bandwidth, proxy IP licensing fees) must be cleared through the corporate account using SWIFT MT103 (for cross-border non-EUR payments) or SEPA Instant/SEPA Direct Debit (for EUR payments within the SEPA zone). For supplier financing, a commercially accepted method is an Import/Export Letter of Credit (UCP 600) opened by the Luxembourg bank in favour of the vendor; the SCSp must provide the purchase contract, a pro-forma invoice, and an insurance certificate. For smaller recurring API vendor fees, card rails (Visa/Mastercard) through the linked EMI are permissible. USDC outbound payments, if required by vendors, must be executed only via a CSSF-registered VASP, and each transfer must be accompanied by a treasury file containing the invoice ID, the wallet address, and the smart-contract hash.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with an EU- or Luxembourg-authorised credit institution, subject to EMIR reporting when done bilaterally.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for less-convertible currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) permitted for trade-flow hedging, documented under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with a credit support annex.FX swaps (short term up to 1 year) to cover temporally segregated EUR and USD liquidity positions, booked under a CSA or GMRA if collateralised.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) limited to committed facilities and hedges of the SCSp's long-term capex, executed only with a bank rated at least A- by Standard & Poor's.European-style currency options for premium-based risk management, permitted only when purchased with full premium payment upfront, not when written (sold) by the SCSp unless the bank has separately approved a derivatives policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High. A Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2 may offer full IBAN, SEPA and SWIFT access, making it viable as a treasury gateway. However, a pure offshore EMI without a Luxembourg/EU licence cannot be used as a primary settlement bank because the CSSF requires payment institutions to be authorised in an EU/EEA state. Additionally, the target payment rail of USDC/USD can only be integrated through a CSSF-registered Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP), because under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004, as amended by the Law of 15 August 2019, any entity engaging in virtual-asset custody, exchange or transfer is subject to a VASP licence. Therefore, the recommended fintech stack is: (1) a Luxembourg-licensed EMI for fiat SEPA/SWIFT, (2) a CSSF-registered VASP for regulated USDC conversion, and (3) an internal record-keeping system that maps each digital wallet to a legal entity and invoice.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (aggregate maximum: 17% CIT plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax at the Luxembourg City rate; effective CIT brackets are 15% up to EUR 175,000 and 17% above, excluding the municipal surcharge). An SCSp is fiscally transparent under Art. 175 LIR: the partnership itself pays no Luxembourg CIT, MBT, or net wealth tax; each partner is taxed directly on its distributive profit share. A non-resident corporate partner is subject to Luxembourg CIT only on Luxembourg-source profit attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% is the Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding tax rate applying to deemed qualifying distributions; however, because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, a profit distribution is not treated as a dividend as long as the SCSp does not opt into full CIT taxation. If an SCSp is nonetheless treated as a Luxembourg tax-resident corporation in the hands of the partner, the statutory 15% WHT may apply, subject to treaty relief and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for a qualifying EU/EEA corporate partner holding at least 10% for 12 months).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 450,000 in aggregate controlled related-party transactions mandating a Luxembourg Local File and Master File under the Luxembourg transfer-pricing documentation rules; if the related party is resident in a non-cooperative jurisdiction, the threshold is EUR 100,000 per transaction or aggregate exposure above EUR 450,000, and a contemporaneous benchmarking study must be kept. For the public commercial web scraping API business, the arm's-length gross margin on API gateway access and revenue-sharing data resale must be documented.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not enact a separate Economic Substance Regulation; substance requirements are embedded in the ATAD general anti-abuse rule (Art. 6 LIR), the abuse-of-law doctrine (Art. 56 LIR), and the OECD/G20 BEPS standards. An SCSp deriving EUR-scale API commercial income must maintain real substance: Luxembourg office premises, local qualified management and technical staff, board decisions made in Luxembourg, and an active bank account. Corporate partners and directors must justify the SCSp as a genuine commercial venture, not a shell conduit, to avoid reclassification and denial of treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and deliberate filing omissions

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at the full 24.94% aggregate rate; a tax fraud fine of EUR 250 to EUR 50,000; late payment interest at 0.6% per month; and under Art. 397 LIR, imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for wilful tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The general partner’s director who signs false schedules faces joint and several civil liability for the unpaid CIT, plus criminal prosecution under Art. 397 LIR, a travel ban and asset freeze under the Luxembourg General Tax Code collection measures, and disqualification as director.
Shell Company / artificial arrangement / substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp under Art. 56 LIR (abuse of law); the profit is re-attributed to the Luxembourg principal; a 10% abuse penalty; denial of treaty benefits under the OECD principal-purpose test; and a EUR 250,000 penalty under DAC 6 for failure to disclose the reportable cross-border arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for tax restitutions, subject to criminal penalties for aiding tax avoidance, a 5-year imprisonment risk under money-laundering provisions if assets are concealed, and an individual travel ban in summary tax collection proceedings.
Transfer pricing mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment under Art. 56(3) LIR; secondary cash adjustment; a penalty of 10% of the upward adjustment; late interest at 0.6% per month; and disallowance of any non-arm's-length expense paid to the SCSp.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the intercompany agreements bears personal civil liability for the additional tax if the SCSp is insolvent, and criminal charges apply for preparing false transfer-pricing documentation.
AML / KYC / Beneficial Ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004 (as amended), fines up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years; the RBE regime imposes administrative fines up to EUR 125,000 for non-filing or false BO declarations; the Luxembourg CSSF can impose a separate enforcement penalty if the payment activity requires a payment institution license.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability for AML breaches, personal fines, removal from the board, travel bans, and seizure of assets involved in the money laundering chain; using an unlicensed payment flow can also trigger prohibition orders against the directors.

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Companies Act of 1915), Articles 944-1 et seq. (SCSp regime introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013); Loi modifiée du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés (RCS Law), Articles 1 and 9; Loi du 13 janvier 2019 sur le registre des bénéficiaires effectifs (RBE Law).

Formation / constitutional filings

Constitutional deed (acte de constitution — partnership agreement / contrat de société) in notarial or private-writing form, filed electronically via the LBR e-filing portal (Form E-01 'Demande d'inscription').

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required for an SCSp; the capital amount, contribution terms and payment schedule are freely set in the partnership agreement; no escrow account or bank attestation of deposit is mandated by Luxembourg company law. The limited partner's obligation to contribute becomes binding upon execution of the constitutional deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (attestation de sécurité incendie) from the Administration des services de secours (CGDIS) for the warehouse and office; structural-load certificate from the municipal building authority; electrical-installation conformity certificate; workplace clearance with the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM), appointment of a safety delegate, and annual occupational-safety update where staff exceeds regulatory thresholds.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required for an SCSp; the capital amount, contribution terms and payment schedule are freely set in the partnership agreement; no escrow account or bank attestation of deposit is mandated by Luxembourg company law. The limited partner's obligation to contribute becomes binding upon execution of the constitutional deed.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income taxes; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) for customs. Principal portals: MyGuichet.lu (business registry filings), eCDF (electronic VAT/customs filing), and the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) for company registration.

E-government filing portal

For an SCSp: (1) Execute the partnership agreement (contrat de société; notarial deed required only if real estate is contributed) and file constitutive documents with the RCS through MyGuichet.lu; (2) obtain the national business number (OUID) from the RCS, which is the master company identifier; (3) complete VAT registration with the AEDT via the eCDF/MyGuichet.lu portal; the AEDT issues a separate VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) a non-resident foreign entity that has no Luxembourg establishment but is liable for Luxembourg VAT must register directly with the AEDT and appoint a local fiscal representative (représentant responsable de la TVA) approved by the AEDT; (5) where goods are imported or exported, the entity must hold an EORI number (LU + OUID) issued by the ADA. Total processing time: approximately 2 to 6 weeks from RCS filing to operative VAT number.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is a partnership without legal personality and is fiscally transparent for direct tax purposes: no corporate income tax, no municipal business tax, and no net wealth tax at entity level. Each partner is taxed on its allocable share of SCSp income under Luxembourg domestic law (corporate income tax for corporate partners; personal income tax for individual partners). A foreign partner is taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source profits attributable to the SCSp. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a separate taxable person with its own VAT registration. The SCSp does not qualify for the sheltered SPF (Société de Participations Financières) regime, which is limited to non-commercial holding vehicles. — For an SCSp: (1) Execute the partnership agreement (contrat de société; notarial deed required only if real estate is contributed) and file constitutive documents with the RCS through MyGuichet.lu; (2) obtain the national business number (OUID) from the RCS, which is the master company identifier; (3) complete VAT registration with the AEDT via the eCDF/MyGuichet.lu portal; the AEDT issues a separate VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) a non-resident foreign entity that has no Luxembourg establishment but is liable for Luxembourg VAT must register directly with the AEDT and appoint a local fiscal representative (représentant responsable de la TVA) approved by the AEDT; (5) where goods are imported or exported, the entity must hold an EORI number (LU + OUID) issued by the ADA. Total processing time: approximately 2 to 6 weeks from RCS filing to operative VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is a partnership without legal personality and is fiscally transparent for direct tax purposes: no corporate income tax, no municipal business tax, and no net wealth tax at entity level. Each partner is taxed on its allocable share of SCSp income under Luxembourg domestic law (corporate income tax for corporate partners; personal income tax for individual partners). A foreign partner is taxable in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source profits attributable to the SCSp. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a separate taxable person with its own VAT registration. The SCSp does not qualify for the sheltered SPF (Société de Participations Financières) regime, which is limited to non-commercial holding vehicles.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for the SCSp; fiscal transparency is mandatory under Luxembourg tax law as confirmed by Circular L.I.R. n° 14/4. Available elections include: (a) solidarity VAT grouping under article 60 of the Loi TVA, allowing the SCSp to be combined with its partners or certain related Luxembourg entities as a single taxable person for VAT; (b) the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales of subscription boxes and the Import-One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election for low-value consignments; (c) the small-business franchise exemption under article 57 of the Loi TVA, available only to Luxembourg-established taxable persons with annual turnover below EUR 35,000 (non-resident suppliers cannot apply); (d) ATAD-based hybrid-mismatch adjustments under the law of 21 December 2019, which may reclassify the SCSp as opaque where a foreign partner treats it as taxable for its own tax purposes; the SCSp must retain contemporaneous documentation of each partner's classification to avoid adverse recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident or non-established suppliers — VAT registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable activity in Luxembourg. Luxembourg-established small businesses may apply the article 57 franchise exemption up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover. For intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers (B2C), the annual EU-wide threshold is EUR 10,000; once exceeded, the destination member state's VAT must be collected through the OSS. B2B intra-EU acquisitions require a VAT number regardless of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Subscription boxes physically dispatched to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% export rate under article 40 of the Loi TVA, provided the goods leave the EU within the administrative expectation of 90 days from dispatch and the export proof is retained. Intra-EU dispatches to taxable persons (B2B) are exempt as intra-Community supplies under article 39 of the Loi TVA if the recipient's valid EU VAT number is quoted on the invoice and in the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Intra-EU B2C distance sales are not zero-rated; they are taxable in the consumer's member state via the OSS (or at the Luxembourg rate if the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold is not exceeded). Subscription boxes containing excise goods, e.g., alcohol, tobacco, or mineral oil samples, are excluded from OSS, distance-sale simplifications, and standard zero-rating unless specific excise and customs conditions are satisfied. Ancillary international freight and logistics services related to the export of the boxes may be zero-rated under articles 42-43 of the Loi TVA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Document Unique d'Accompagnement) filed electronically with the Administration des douanes et accises (e-Customs/ECS), referencing the EORI/OUID number, HS codes, weights, and proof of exit from the European Union
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), or CMR consignment note (road) evidencing physical transport to a destination outside the EU; where the buyer arranges carriage, the seller must obtain a certificate of arrival or equivalent customs export proof within 90 days
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank statement, SWIFT MT103/MT202, or payment confirmation showing settlement by the foreign buyer. Luxembourg does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline, but a 180-day documented settlement window is recommended to substantiate commercial genuineness and to reduce AEDT audit challenge

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate applies in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for B2G transactions (to public entities via the PEPPOL network in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format). Luxembourg has adopted legislation enabling mandatory B2B e-invoicing, expected to be phased in from 2026/2027 in line with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package; however, no binding B2B e-invoice or API clearance requirement is currently in force. Invoices must satisfy the authenticity requirements of the Loi TVA (reliable audit trail, XML/EDI archive with readable representation), and invoices for exports or intra-EU supplies must include the required reference to the VAT exemption clause.

AEDT audits of zero-rating claims focus on the completeness of the export evidence package (SAD, transport document, payment proof, and separate HS-code declaration). If zero-rating is disallowed, the AEDT retroactively assesses VAT at 17% on the supply price, plus default interest at 0.8% per month (9.6% per annum). Fraudulent claims — including falsely dated customs declarations, circular shipments, or misdescribed destinations — are prosecuted under article 65 of the Loi TVA, carrying fines from EUR 500 up to EUR 12,500 per violation (or one to five times the tax evaded in aggravated cases), publication of the sanction, and imprisonment of up to 5 years when referred to the criminal courts. The limitation period is 5 years for good-faith underdeclarations and 10 years in cases of fraud or bad faith. Where a rejected intra-EU B2B exemption results from an invalid or inactive recipient VAT number, the SCSp may be held jointly and severally liable for the counterparty's VAT under article 52 of the Loi TVA. Unsubstantiated export refunds are clawed back with interest and may trigger automatic audit of all subsequent zero-rating claims for the same product category.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Luxembourg maintains no exchange controls and imposes no prior authorisation requirement on current-account or capital-account flows, consistent with Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Profit remittances and capital distributions from the SCSp are freely executable through authorised credit institutions; the only constraints are EU/UN financial sanctions (e.g., Council Regulation (EU) No 833/2014) and Luxembourg AML/CFT rules (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended). Cross-border payments require BCL balance-of-payments statistical purpose codes but do not require convertibility licences.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose surrender, repatriation deadlines, or conversion ratios on export proceeds. A subscription box exporter (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 454110) must only file statistical declarations to BCL/STATEC for balance-of-payments purposes and comply with VAT rules (reverse charge for B2B supplies of goods outside Luxembourg). All foreign currency may be retained freely in operational or escrow accounts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Eurosystem member; monetary and FX policy set by the European Central Bank (ECB); banking supervision coordinated by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Luxembourg Law of 5 April 1993.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility. Luxembourg recognises CSSF-licensed payment institutions and e-money institutions under PSD2 (Law of 10 November 2009, amended) and the E-Money Law of 2 August 2011. EMIs issuing local IBANs (e.g., Satchel, Mangopay, Paydo, or passported EU EMIs registered with the CSSF through the EBA passporting framework) can lawfully serve the SCSp's cross-border subscription payments and hold safeguarded client funds. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot provide credit to the SCSp; (ii) client funds must be safeguarded with a Tier-1 credit institution and are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL); (iii) the SCSp should maintain a separate Tier-1 bank account for capital escrow, FX collateral, and commercial LC issuances; (iv) card schemes (Stripe Billing, Authorize.Net) require an acquiring relationship — for the EU/EEA market, Stripe's Luxembourg/IE acquiring is acceptable, whereas Authorize.Net's legacy Visa Europe arrangement has limited EU-wide applicability and a local acquiring sponsor is recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (EEA: TARGET2/SEPA-equivalent) to the SCSp's EUR account at a Luxembourg bank. The remitter must encode BCL balance-of-payments purpose code 'F6' (direct investment, equity capital) or 'LF 8' (intercompany loan) and reference the SCSp's RCS number and the capital call identifier. The beneficiary bank verifies the partnership agreement, the GP resolution, the capital call notice, the UBO record, and applicable sanctions lists before crediting. Non-EUR injections are converted on the spot market without BCL approval. Chargebacks and returns are reconciled under the cash application schedule recorded in the subscription-box general ledger.Three clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU suppliers settle via TARGET2; (ii) SWIFT MT103 / MT202 COV for non-EEA suppliers using gpi tracking and ISO 20022 purpose codes; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) in SWIFT MT700 format issued by the Luxembourg bank for merchandise imports. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined 24.94% (Luxembourg City) = 17% CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax + 7% employment-fund surcharge on CIT; the SCSp itself is fiscally transparent, so the 17%/24.94% rate applies only to corporate partners on their allocable share or upon a check-the-box-like opaque election.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions from a transparent SCSp (no corporate taxpayer, no withholding). If the SCSp or its operator elects opaque corporate taxation, only then does the statutory 15% dividend WHT apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 local-file trigger for related-party transactions when the MNE group has consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750,000,000; master file and CbCR similarly anchored to the EUR 750,000,000 group threshold.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated ESR statute; Luxembourg enforces substance through the GAAR (AO Art. 6), the OECD principal-purpose test in tax treaties, the Bevola/Danish-case anti-shelter jurisprudence, and the ATAD. The subscription-box business must demonstrably exercise subscription management, fulfillment, procurement and risk control in Luxembourg to justify profit attribution to the local PE; otherwise profits are reallocated and treaty benefits are denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years (AO Art. 426); statutory reassessment extended to 10 years; late-payment interest at 0.6%/month; confiscation of illegal gains.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate GP face joint civil liability for the tax debt of the partnership under the 'action en comblement du passif' where the debt results from an abusive fault; criminal courts may impose management bans, travel restrictions for serious fraud offenses and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Requalification as opaque or reallocation of income (AO Art. 61); denial of treaty benefits under PPT; municipal business tax assessment plus 0.6%/month interest; penalties up to EUR 25,000; automatic AEOI look-through flagging (DAC6/hub-and-spoke arrangements).

Director personal liability: Civil veil-piercing under the doctrine of abuse of law, personal restitution for dividends extracted without substance, and criminal liability where shell-company operations facilitate tax fraud.
AML / Money Laundering (Predicate Tax Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004, as amended: administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; criminal sanctions up to 5 years' imprisonment; freezing orders by the FIU; compelled deregistration and liquidation of the SCSp.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal fines and imprisonment for the GP's directors when they authorize or tolerate suspicious transactions; travel bans and director-disqualification orders issued by the Luxembourg criminal court.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment with 0.6%/month interest; penalties up to EUR 250,000 for incomplete documentation (AO Arts. 166–171); potential double taxation if the corresponding adjustment is not obtained abroad; criminal exposure where false documentation is submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false TP documentation face criminal liability for tax fraud; civil liability for the uncollected corporate tax under the comblement du passif action.

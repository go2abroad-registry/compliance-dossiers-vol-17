Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority, L-2946 Luxembourg (portal: myguichet.lu / AEDT e-space); direct income tax: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD)

E-government filing portal

SCSp is registered at the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) to obtain the national business number (matricule) filed by the general partner; Luxembourg VAT ID (LU + 8 digits) is obtained by filing VAT registration Form 640 with the AEDT before commencing taxable activity. Non-EU-established taxable persons must appoint an AEDT-approved tax representative in Luxembourg; EU-established persons may register directly. The SCSp as a fiscal-transparent vehicle has no separate direct-tax TIN and partners are identified by their domestic TINs.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — SCSp is registered at the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) to obtain the national business number (matricule) filed by the general partner; Luxembourg VAT ID (LU + 8 digits) is obtained by filing VAT registration Form 640 with the AEDT before commencing taxable activity. Non-EU-established taxable persons must appoint an AEDT-approved tax representative in Luxembourg; EU-established persons may register directly. The SCSp as a fiscal-transparent vehicle has no separate direct-tax TIN and partners are identified by their domestic TINs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent (no CIT, net wealth tax or municipal business tax at entity level); partners are taxed directly and the partnership agreement determines profit attribution. No check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg law. For VAT: the SCSp may elect to be treated as a taxable person when exercising an economic activity, may join an 'assujetti unique' VAT group, or benefit from the small-business franchise (turnover ≤ EUR 35,000). ATAD 2 reverse-hybrid characterization may apply where partners are resident in jurisdictions that treat the SCSp as opaque, displacing transparency for deduction disallowance purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business franchise (exemption); no threshold for non-resident suppliers of B2C e-services (OSS registration from first euro) or for B2B cross-border services subject to reverse charge; EUR 10,000 intra-Community threshold for distance sales and e-services to non-taxable persons

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to export supplies of goods where the customs export declaration (EAAD/MRN) is lodged and physical exit from the EU is evidenced within 90 days (Art. 55 VAT Law and AEDT circulars). B2B remote screen-share/IoT telemetry services to non-EU customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope (no Luxembourg VAT, not 'zero-rated' in the strict sense); B2B services to EU customers are reverse-charged in the recipient's jurisdiction, conditional on the customer's valid EU VAT ID; B2C supplies of digital services to EU consumers are taxed where the consumer is established via the VAT OSS scheme (no threshold for non-EU-service suppliers), while B2C supplies to non-EU consumers remain outside Luxembourg VAT. Zero-rating substantiation for software/telemetry services includes contractual IP/user documentation, network logs and payment evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EAAD) with MRN evidencing EU exit (for goods)
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill (signed/dated by carrier)
  • Commercial invoice in the name of the purchaser stating the export exemption basis
  • Protocole/inter-party evidence of the exported service scope, licence agreement or remote-access logs (for remote screen-share and IoT telemetry services)
  • Bank statement/proof of inward payment evidencing consideration received and FX repatriation (inward remittance certificate as issuer-bank certified equivalent)
  • Valid EU VAT ID of the customer (for reverse-charge B2B services within the EU)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or digital stamping regime in force. Luxembourg has no mandatory B2B e-invoicing yet; B2G invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices via the Peppol network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0). Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age package (Council Directive amending Directive 2006/112/EC, adopted 11 March 2025), Luxembourg will transpose mandatory e-invoicing and digital e-reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies by 1 July 2030 and expects domestic B2B e-invoicing obligations from 1 January 2028. Invoices (paper or electronic) must be retained for 6 years.

AEDT audits export/zero-rating files for up to 6 years; missing or falsified export evidence triggers retroactive clawback of the exemption plus default interest of 0.6% per month and a late-payment surcharge of 10%; intentional misstatements attract a tax increase of up to 50% of the evaded VAT, and fraud (Art. 425/426 General Tax Law) carries criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The general partner and the SCSp are jointly and severally liable for VAT debts; refund claims are deferred while the export dossier is under verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies comprehensive euro-zone convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions, implemented through EU primary law (TFEU Article 63 free movement of capital) and national law. The euro is freely convertible; profit remittances, partnership distributions, and the repatriation of invested capital by the SCSp's non-resident limited partners are not subject to exchange-authorisation, licensing, or surrender requirements. The only practical hurdles are (i) statutory AML/CFT and UBO transparency checks (Law of 13 January 2019 on the UBO register), (ii) source-of-funds verification under CSSF Circular 17/650, and (iii) mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting of cross-border payment flows routed through Luxembourg banks (generally reported by the bank once a transaction exceeds EUR 12,500). No material convertibility hurdle exists for a remote-screen-share/IoT software business receiving subscription revenue from global customers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Luxembourg. Software subscription and licence income is treated as current-account service revenue; proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency without forced repatriation or conversion. The SCSp is required only to record receipts in its statutory books and to facilitate statistical declarations where the payer is a non-resident; the servicing bank automatically captures and files these data with the BCL under the Grand-Ducal balance-of-payments reporting framework. Voluntary conversion of foreign-currency receipts into EUR is unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); banking prudential supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overall feasibility: Medium. EEA-licensed and appropriately passported Payment Institutions / Electronic Money Institutions (PSD2) are legally usable for operational treasury functions: collecting Stripe/Paddle settlement payouts, making international contractor payments, and executing FX conversions. They are NOT permissible for capital-subscription escrows or long-term reserve deposits, because deposit-taking is statutorily reserved to credit institutions under the Law of 5 April 1993. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs present material legal, tax-substance, and AML objections for a Luxembourg SCSp and should be avoided for the primary banking relationship.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Notarised partnership deed, certified extract from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register, UBO register extract under the Law of 13 January 2019, a powers-and-binding-opinion letter confirming that the General Partner alone acts for the SCSp, and a legal opinion from a Luxembourg law firm explaining the SCSp's transparency and the capital-commitment schedule.
Non-resident technology founders with remote residence and opaque source of initial capital injection

Root cause: Non-resident technology founders with remote residence and opaque source of initial capital injection

Defense evidence: Professional source-of-wealth letter, six months of personal and corporate bank statements evidencing legitimate accumulation, audited or accountant-certified financials of prior ventures, payslips or capital-gain documentation, and, for contributions above EUR 1 million, an independent audit attestation or reputable tax-adviser declaration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from limited partners must be routed via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments up to EUR 100,000 per transaction, or via SWIFT MT103 (with or without cover) for larger sums and non-EUR currencies. The General Partner must issue a payment instruction letter naming the SCSp as beneficiary, quoting the BCL balance-of-payments statistical purpose code applicable to equity-capital/direct-investment remittances, and attaching the subscribed capital schedule from the partnership deed. The receiving bank verifies funds under CSSF Circular 17/650: each contributing limited partner must provide FATCA/CRS self-certification, identification documents, and source-of-funds evidence matching the transacting entity; funds settled from third-party accounts must be accompanied by a formal gift/shareholder-funding declaration acceptable to the bank.Trade settlements and operating disbursements are executed through SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-originated vendor invoices, SEPA Instant for urgent EUR payments below EUR 100,000, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EU counterparties and multi-currency payments; SWIFT gpi tracking is applied for confirmation and compliance screening.

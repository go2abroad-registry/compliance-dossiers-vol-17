Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), accessible via the e-RCS/eJustice e-filing portal; RESA publication service for legal notices.

Governing statute

Articles 103-1 to 103-5 of the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 concerning Commercial Companies (as amended by the Law of 12 July 2013 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale), read with Articles 1831 et seq. of the Civil Code on partnership contracts.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation (registration application) filed with the RCS together with the executed partnership agreement (convention de société); subsequent publication in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement for an SCSp; partnership capital is freely fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be contributed in cash or in kind, with capital calls at the discretion of the General Partner.

Funding & unlock path: CGDIS (Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps) fire-safety certificate and emergency response clearance; structural load and electrical safety inspection by an approved contrôleur agréé; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including radiation protection zoning, personal dosimetry, and X-ray shielding verification for the DR detector test benches.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue – Administration des contributions directes (ACD); indirect taxes: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); online portals: MyGuichet.lu and eCDF (échange de données informatisé).

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp, the initial statutory registration is effected before the Registre de commerce et des sociétés (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu, providing the entity with an RCS number (13-digit / incorporation register number) which serves as the corporate reference. For VAT purposes, a VAT identification number is requested from the AEDT using the national registration form (n° 750) once the SCSp begins taxable transactions or reaches the taxable threshold. As an SCSp is fiscally transparent, direct income tax identification is not issued to the entity; partners with Luxembourg-source income obtain individual TINs from the ACD. Non-resident partners may apply for a Luxembourg income tax identification number where they are subject to Luxembourg withholding or assessment obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available under ordinary Luxembourg law; SCSp status is statutorily defined as fiscally transparent. The terms of the partnership agreement nevertheless determine the partners' allocation of taxable income, and the partners may invoke the ATAD anti-hybrid rules to reclassify a foreign 'reverse hybrid' double non-taxation event. A written election can be made to treat the SCSp as an opaque entity only where the partnership qualifies as a taxable subject under Luxembourg corporate tax law (factual/substantive test) – e.g., for VAT invoicing or for state-mandated tax returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover under the small business exemption; mandatory registration without threshold for importers, intra-EU acquirers exceeding the EUR 10,000 distance-sale threshold, and taxable persons performing reverse-charge transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically removed from the territory of Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU qualify for zero-rating (0% VAT), provided the goods are dispatched/transported by the exporter or on behalf of the exporter within 90 days of the supply and proof of export is obtained. Associated services to exported goods may also be zero-rated where they are directly linked to the export. The taxpayer must hold evidence of transfer of ownership and the transport out of the EU, for example via the customs export declaration and transport documents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Exit Summary Declaration) with movement reference number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT/TT wire confirmation) evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of delivery / customs clearance at the destination country

Luxembourg mandates electronic invoicing for B2G contracts via the PEPPOL network; there is no general B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime, and no digital stamping requirement. Taxpayers must store all invoices for 10 years and use the electronic filing platform for VAT returns. For exported goods, an electronic customs declaration (Export Summary Declaration) is filed through the customs clearance system.

The Luxembourg VAT administration audits export documentation within the standard 3-year (up to 5-year in case of fraud) assessment window. If the documentary burden of proof is not satisfied, the zero rating is reversed and VAT is assessed at 17% on the net sale price, plus default interest of 0.6% per month (or the applicable legal rate) and possible penalties. Material misrepresentation or fraudulent export claims trigger clawback of any refund, administrative fines up to 100% of the evaded tax, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment (in serious cases up to 5 years), in accordance with the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung).

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. As a euro-area member state, Luxembourg applies Articles 63–65 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC guaranteeing unrestricted movement of capital between member states and with most third countries. The SCSp may freely invoice in EUR, USD, or other convertible currencies; there are no BCL approval procedures, pool requirements, parallel-market restrictions, or prior licensing steps for inward equity, shareholder loans, trade payments, or outward profit distributions. The only practical profit-remittance frictions are tax-driven (e.g., 15% Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends unless treaty-reduced; the SCSp is pass-through so distributions by the partnership to non-resident partners are generally outside that WHT net, but partner-level taxation in their residence state applies) and bank-level EDD reviews of source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline on export proceeds from the sale of digital X-ray flat panel detectors (ISIC 3250). Receipts in EUR, USD, or other freely usable currencies may be retained, swept, or converted at corporate discretion. The only associated obligations are aggregate reporting of the entity's external financial position to the BCL (via the resident reporting bank) and the general AML transaction-monitoring duty under the Law of 12 November 2004.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential supervision and AML/CFT oversight vest in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector and the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing (as amended through the Law of 25 March 2020 transposing the 4th and 5th EU AML Directives). The BCL operates the Luxembourg component of TARGET2 and collects balance-of-payments statistics from resident credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low–Medium feasibility. Luxembourg-authorized payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) licensed by the CSSF under the Law of 11 November 2021 (payment services, PSD2) and the Law of 21 December 2009 (electronic money) may provide virtual IBAN accounts to an SCSp, but EMIs cannot issue documentary credits (UCP 600), lack correspondent access to CHIPS/Fedwire for high-value USD trade settlements, and typically apply sub-EUR 250,000 daily turnover caps. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport are effectively blocked for a Luxembourg SCSp because the resident bank, the CSSF, and the UBO register requirements create a substance/traceability barrier; they are viable only as contingency disbursement rails for low-value service payments, never for the primary OEM trade cash flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City standard combined rate: 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a Luxembourg resident fully taxable company. A fiscally transparent SCSp is generally outside the dividend withholding tax net because profit allocations are taxed at partner level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 30 June of the year following the tax period for a standard 31 December year-end; the Luxembourg tax administration routinely grants an extension to 31 December upon timely request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 in consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Country-by-Country reporting. Luxembourg does not set a statutory EUR threshold for Master File/Local File, but the tax administration expects OECD-aligned local and master file documentation to be available on request for all controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but EU ATAD, the Luxembourg General Anti-Abuse Rule, and substance-over-form doctrines apply. The SCSp must have real economic activity, actual management, premises, staff, and decision-making capacity in Luxembourg to preserve treaty benefits and resist recharacterisation as a letterbox entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with penalties of 10% to 20% of evaded tax; monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%; criminal prosecution with fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners may be jointly and severally liable for evaded tax where they signed false filings or caused the default; courts may impose asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of transactions; refusal of treaty benefits and participation exemptions; reassessment with penalties and interest; possible disqualification of the tax transparent status.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced so that directors and shareholders are treated as beneficial owners; civil restitution, director disqualification, and travel bans may be ordered.
AML-CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg anti-money laundering law, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 1,250,000; administrative sanctions may reach EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious compliance failures.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal prosecution and administrative fines; regulators may impose bans on holding management functions and travel restrictions in cross-border investigations.
Fatal Filing and Record-Keeping Default

Statutory sanction: Mandatory ex officio assessment, late-filing fine up to EUR 2,500, monthly late-payment interest, and a 10% to 20% surcharge; when combined with fraudulent conduct, criminal sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors may be held civilly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; courts may impose director disqualification, travel bans, and detention in egregious cases.

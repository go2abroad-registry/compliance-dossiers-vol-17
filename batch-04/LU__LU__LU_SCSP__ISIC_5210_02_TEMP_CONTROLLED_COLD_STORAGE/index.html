Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible for the statutory seat of an SCSp only where a licensed Luxembourg registered-office provider accepts domiciliation at its own physical premises; the SCSp must otherwise be reachable at the registered office for court summons, tax correspondence and AML/CFT supervision. For an operating ISIC 5210 refrigerated logistics business storing perishable pharmaceuticals, virtual office arrangements are commercially insufficient: the entity must obtain and maintain physical custody of, or lease, a suitable cold-storage facility subject to Good Distribution Practices (GDP) licensing under Directive 2001/83/EC and EU Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/161, with temperature monitoring, qualified-person oversight, qualified personnel and insurable stock; moreover, if Luxembourg tax residence is claimed for the pass-through partnership, effective central management and business conduct must demonstrably take place in Luxembourg. Virtual office use is therefore limited to ancillary administrative purposes and cannot satisfy commercial-substance or GDP requirements.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital requirement for an SCSp; no mandatory paid-up capital or escrow account requirement. Contributions may be in cash or in kind, and may be payable at the dates fixed in the partnership agreement. Limited partner liability is contractually capped at the contribution, whereas the general partner is liable without limit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified digital filing via MyGuichet.lu / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés).

E-government filing portal

Apply for inclusion in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through an approved filing intermediary, obtaining a national business number (Numéro d'identification). Use that number to file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu; the VAT number is issued immediately in the format LU00000000. The SCSp itself is not subject to corporate income tax and no CIT ID is required; the partners are taxed directly. If the SCSp elects to be treated as a taxable corporation under LIR Article 5 bis, a separate CIT tax file must be activated with the ACD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

SCSp is fiscally transparent by default. Pursuant to LIR Article 5 bis, an SCSp may elect irrevocably to be treated as a body corporate for direct tax purposes if all its partners are corporate taxpayers; this election is exercised in the annual corporate return. No check-the-box regime exists. For indirect tax, no election affects VAT status; the SCSp is a separate taxable person for VAT purposes when it carries on an economic activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the small-business exemption; registration is compulsory once the SCSp's taxable supplies in Luxembourg exceed this threshold. Non-established foreign entities supplying services in Luxembourg under the general B2B place-of-supply rule do not benefit from the threshold and must register unless the reverse charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For cold-storage warehousing services supplied by the Luxembourg SCSp to a taxable customer established outside the EU, the place of supply is outside Luxembourg and the supply is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT, resulting in a 0% effective charge (not a statutory exemption). Where the SCSp itself physically exports goods from a customs warehouse to a non-EU destination, the goods supply is zero-rated under the export exemption; related storage and handling services can qualify as ancillary export services if performed directly in connection with fully-completed exports and the services' value is supported by prescribed evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD) filed via the LLMAM e-Douane system and validated with MRN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill/CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice showing 0% VAT and, where available, the customer's non-EU VAT number or equivalent proof of taxable status
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing actual forex receipt from the non-EU customer
  • Warehouse movement/issue records and temperature-log evidence substantiating physical investigation by customs authorities

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. For B2G supplies, electronic invoicing through PEPPOL BIS is mandatory. B2B e-invoicing is optional and governed by private contract; common formats are ZUGFeRD/FACTUR-X and UBL. EU ViDA proposals may introduce sector-specific e-invoicing obligations after 2028, which the SCSp should monitor.

Wrongful zero-rating or unfounded export claims subject the SCSp to retrospective VAT collection with statutory late interest (currently 0.88% per month in arrears), administrative fines up to 100% of the evaded tax under Article 55(13) TVA, and criminal liability in cases of deliberate fraud (fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment). Supporting documentation must be retained for at least 6 years for VAT purposes and 10 years for customs purposes; incomplete documentation triggers automatic clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Free and full convertibility. Luxembourg is a Eurozone member; Article 63 TFEU prohibits all restrictions on capital movements and payments between Member States and third countries. The capital and current accounts are completely liberalized: profit remittances, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred without prior administrative authorization or central bank approval. Foreign-currency accounts are permitted and may be converted to EUR at market rates. The only practical requirements are statutory bank reporting of balance-of-payments statistics under BCL regulations (including a purpose code on each transfer) and verification of the economic substance of the transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no obligatory surrender of export proceeds, no repatriation deadlines, and no mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters and trading entities may retain receivables in any convertible currency and time their conversion freely. Generic EU payment transparency duties under Regulation (EU) 2015/847 (originator and beneficiary information) apply to all cross-border transfers, but these are informational and not exchange-control constraints.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate foreign exchange administration body exists; the BCL performs monetary policy and balance-of-payments statistical functions, while the CSSF supervises banks and payment institutions for AML/KYC and prudential matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for low-value SEPA collections and disbursement automation; Low for capital custody and large trade settlements. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Banking Circle S.A., Mangopay) are supervised by the CSSF under the PSD2 regime and the 2009 e-money and payment services laws, and can provide multi-currency IBANs. However, subscription capital for an SCSp and substantial cross-border supplier payments should remain in a tier-1 commercial bank, because EMIs typically decline partnership structures with non-resident UBOs, lack depositary-grade escrow, and are not designated counterparties for BCL statistical reporting in the same manner as credit institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg City rate comprising 17% national corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax, and 6.75% municipal business tax; municipal component varies by commune

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; reduced to 1% for dividends paid to a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the share capital, alone or jointly, for an uninterrupted 12-month period; DTA rates of 0% to 15% may apply under Luxembourg's treaty network

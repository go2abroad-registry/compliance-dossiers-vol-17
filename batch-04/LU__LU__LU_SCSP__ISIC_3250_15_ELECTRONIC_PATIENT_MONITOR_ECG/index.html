Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital and no mandatory paid-up capital for the SCSp; capital commitments are freely contractually set; no escrow or capital-blocking bank certificate is required by the RCS for registration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; single electronic portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

The SCSp must first obtain a Luxembourg national identification number through registration with the Luxembourg Business Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS). After RCS registration, the SCSp or its duly authorised tax representative submits the VAT registration dossier to the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu, providing constitutive documents, RCS extract, description of economic activities, anticipated turnover, and a Luxembourg bank confirmation; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg TVA identification number immediately or within the standard 15-day processing window.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no domestic check-the-box election; the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default. Available statutory elections include: (i) option to be part of a VAT group as an assujetti unique under Article 62 of the Luxembourg VAT law; (ii) option for the VAT small business exemption under Article 57 if annual taxable turnover is below EUR 35,000 (input VAT recovery foregone); (iii) election for the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-EU distance sales; (iv) election under double tax treaties and domestic ATAD rules for the tax treatment of hybrid entities to be aligned with partner jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic Luxembourg-established taxable persons have no general registration threshold; registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction, except for the optional small business exemption up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover. Non-resident suppliers with no Luxembourg establishment are required to register from their first taxable supply in Luxembourg. For intra-EU distance sales by an EU-established supplier, the annual cross-border threshold is EUR 10,000, beyond which VAT registration or OSS usage in Luxembourg becomes mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the exporter retains evidence that the goods physically left the EU within the statutory export window and that the export is supported by customs documentation. Also eligible for 0% are certain services directly related to the exportation of goods (e.g., loading, handling, transport and customs brokerage) and intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered EU customer where the customer's VAT identification number and EC Sales List requirements are satisfied. The SCSp must be the exporter according to the customs declaration and hold original transport documentation evidencing exit from the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD / DMS Export Declaration with exit confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (For sea carriage), Air Waybill (For air cargo) or CMR road consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidence of payment receipt in the exporting entity's bank account
  • Commercial invoice
  • Transport contract or freight forwarding instructions
  • Proof of delivery to the non-EU customer or to the customer's appointed forwarder

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction control system. E-invoicing is permitted provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are ensured. B2B e-invoicing may be used by mutual consent; all VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu. No fiscal clearance API or government-side real-time validation of individual invoices is required.

The Luxembourg AEDT rigorously audits export zero-rating claims. If the export proof is incomplete, forged, or the goods are returned to Luxembourg or diverted for domestic use, the VAT initially zero-rated becomes immediately payable at the standard 17% rate. Administrative late-payment interest of 0.6% per month accrues from the date the VAT would originally have fallen due. Fines of up to 50% of the evaded tax may be imposed for negligence or misstatement; deliberate VAT evasion or falsification of export documentation carrying criminal liability may result in fines up to EUR 250,000 and custodial sanctions. Persons involved in abusive circular export schemes are jointly and severally liable for the VAT clawed back.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully integrated eurozone jurisdiction with unrestricted capital and current account convertibility for legal entities and partnerships. The euro is freely convertible, and there are no exchange-control restrictions on the import or export of capital, dividend remittance, profit distribution, or liquidation proceeds. Capital movements to and from non-EU states are governed by EU free-movement-of-capital principles and subject only to standard financial sanctions screening, AML/KYC checks, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL. Profit remittances from a Luxembourg SCSp to partners are not subject to FX approval or currency conversion mandates; however, tax-related considerations such as domestic anti-abuse rules and possible withholding obligations on certain passive income streams remain independently applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export receipts within a prescribed period. Export proceeds and any foreign-currency holdings may be retained on account, converted at commercial market rates, or used directly for offshore or onshore disbursements, subject only to internal bank compliance and EU sanctions regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Central Bank of Luxembourg, acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, AML/CFT supervision, and payment system oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with strong legal constraints. Luxembourg-authorised and EEA-passported electronic money institutions and payment institutions may be used for low-value operational collections, FX conversion, and SEPA/wire transfers. However, digital EMIs generally do not support full trade-finance products (including standby and commercial letters of credit), high-limit escrow facilities, or nuanced multi-currency treasury structures. The Luxembourg SCSp must maintain at least one relationship with a fully licensed credit institution or a large internationally active bank to access robust SWIFT MT103 clearing, LC confirmation, and cross-currency hedging. EMIs cannot provide credit, are not eligible for client-money safeguarding equivalent to a banking licence, and are often subject to stricter sub-limit transaction thresholds. Legal constraints under the EU Payment Services Directive 2 and Luxembourg law of 10 November 2009 require EMI accounts to be opened in the name of the SCSp with full beneficial-owner transparency and seamless AML/KYC integration.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective headline rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax above EUR 175,000, plus 7% solidarity surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax; 15% on the first EUR 175,000). The Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is fiscally transparent, so the rate is assessed at the partner level on allocated profit, not at the SCSp level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividend distributions (LIR Section 148); full relief applies to qualifying EU parent-subsidiary distributions and to non-EU shareholders under the 10%/12-month participation rule; distributions of profit allocations by a transparent SCSp are not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return for a fiscal year closing on 31 December must be filed by 31 December of the following calendar year; for non-calendar fiscal years, the deadline is 31 December of the year following the accounting close. Extensions must be requested before the statutory deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File apply when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 750 million and the Luxembourg entity executes cross-border controlled transactions; a simplified Local File is required where annual intercompany transactions exceed EUR 1 million. The SCSp must document the arm's-length allocation of ECG-device assembly and calibration margins to the resident partner.

Economic substance scope: ESR applicability: no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations of the offshore-centre type exist in Luxembourg; substance requirements are enforced through the GAAR (LIR Section 6), the ATAD anti-abuse rule (Law of 20 July 2020) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. For a transparent SCSp, the ACD requires a real registered office, local bookkeeping, board/managerial decision-taking in Luxembourg, a Luxembourg bank account and adequate staff; absence of these factors triggers recharacterisation as opaque, denial of treaty benefits and application of the full 24.94% aggregate rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 6,000 (LIR Section 177); where fraud is established, criminal conviction under the Luxembourg Criminal Code carries a fine of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; ex officio reassessment plus 10% understatement surcharge.

Director personal liability: The SCSp's general partner and its directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid partnership taxes; courts may pierce the corporate veil to recover the Treasury claim from personal assets, impose travel bans, and order civil restitution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Luxembourg Registration, Estates and VAT Administration; consolidated single-access portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

The SCSp is fiscally transparent, so the partnership itself is not ordinarily the direct taxpayer. Registration sequence: (1) enroll the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu to obtain the RCS registration number and the statutory matricule (national business identification number). (2) Apply for a Luxembourg VAT number with the AEDT using the electronic VAT registration form (form 010C) where the SCSp makes taxable supplies and does not benefit from the EUR 35,000 small-business exemption; a non-resident partner acting through the SCSp must also appoint a Luxembourg tax agent or VAT representative (représentant responsable) when the SCSp has no fixed establishment in Luxembourg. (3) Register each non-resident partner for direct tax purposes with the ACD via the non-resident registration procedure (e-form 160A) to obtain Luxembourg income tax reference numbers. Supporting documents include the notarized partnership deed, RCS excerpt, proof of registered office, identity documents of general partner(s) and ultimate beneficial owners, and a completed AML/KYC pack for the depositary or bank. The AEDT routinely issues the VAT number within 15 business days of a complete filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Luxembourg. The SCSp is default-transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; income, gains, and net wealth are attributed pro rata to partners. If all partners are collective entities fully liable to Luxembourg corporate income tax, the partners may jointly elect to treat the SCSp as opaque and subject it to entity-level taxation; that election is binding and irreversible for the life of the partnership. EU anti-hybrid rules (implemented by the Luxembourg Law of 21 December 2018) may neutralize mismatches where the SCSp is treated as transparent in Luxembourg and opaque in the partner jurisdiction; no specific hybrid-mismatch re-election mechanism exists beyond reliance on the general anti-abuse rule and ATAD 1/2 carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual turnover below which the small-business exemption under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law may apply); EUR 10,000 (EU-wide cross-border distance-sales and OMP threshold above which Luxembourg VAT applies to B2C sales of IoT hardware by an EU-established retailer); no registration threshold for a Luxembourg-established taxable person holding an RCS commercial registration or for supplies of goods subject to excise rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% Luxembourg VAT applies to: (i) export supplies of goods physically leaving the EU customs territory, provided transport begins no later than 90 days after the supply and the goods leave the EU within that period; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a counterparty holding a valid VAT registration number confirmed on VIES; (iii) supplies of services directly linked to exported goods or to offshore goods for processing, where the recipient is established outside the EU and the place-of-supply rules put the supply outside Luxembourg. The SCSp retailing smart-home IoT hardware may claim zero rating for direct exports to non-EU customers, but distance sales of EU-stored goods to EU consumers are taxed at destination through the Union OMP scheme or local registration once the EUR 10,000 threshold is exceeded.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A / DAU) lodged electronically through Luxembourg Customs or the ICS system, showing the SCSp as exporter
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank documentation proving receipt of export payment in foreign currency

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL (BIS 3.0) in force since 1 January 2020 for all invoices issued to Luxembourg public-sector buyers. Mandatory domestic B2B structured e-invoicing (EN 16931/PEPPOL) was enacted by the Luxembourg law of 18 July 2024 and applies in phases from 1 January 2026 (large taxpayers) and generally from 1 January 2027 (all taxable persons). No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) engine, or digital stamping platform operates at present; periodic VAT returns, VIES recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu or the AEDT e-filing portal, and the new e-invoicing regime introduces structured audit-file (SAF-T) extraction expectations in line with the future e-reporting framework.

Luxembourg VAT law and Customs law impose rigorous scrutiny of export substantiation. If zero-rating is claimed without satisfactory customs exit proof, the AEDT retrospectively claws back VAT at the standard 17% rate with statutory late-payment interest of 0.6% per month. Administrative fines reach EUR 4,000 per violation for invoicing or registration breaches; intentional fraud triggers fines of up to EUR 25,000 or one to three times the evaded tax, plus imprisonment under the Luxembourg criminal code for forgery of customs declarations, transport documents, or export evidence. The standard limitation period for assessment is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or bad faith. Documentary retention of the full export bundle for at least 10 years is recommended given the extended fraud look-back window and OMP compliance risks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully euroised EU member state with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. There is no FX control authority and no exchange licensing regime for capital injections, trade settlements, profit distributions or liquidation proceeds. The SCSp is fiscally transparent, so distributions to partners are generally outside Luxembourg withholding tax and may be remitted freely in EUR or any convertible currency, subject only to global EU sanctions, AML/KYC checks and statistical reporting. Profits and capital may be converted without prior central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency without restriction. However, cross-border payments above the BCL de minimis statistical reporting threshold (EUR 12,500) must be declared by the reporting bank to the BCL under the ECB balance-of-payments and international investment position framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in conjunction with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for payment and FX licensing supervision, and the Ministry of Finance for balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. A Luxembourg-authorised EMI or payment institution operating under PSD2 and CSSF Circular 18/698 may be used as a high-speed collection rail for e-commerce sales from Stripe and Shopify Payments, but it may not engage in credit-taking, investment services, or unrestricted treasury management. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA passport are not viable for Luxembourg cross-border clearing because they cannot lawfully provide payment services in the EEA and would not be accepted by tier-1 banks as underlying treasury rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level (fiscally transparent partnership); corporate partners subject to combined CIT of 24.94% in Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 5.25% municipal business tax + 7% solidarity surcharge) on their distributive share of profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a real-estate rich Luxembourg corporation, but SCSp is fiscally transparent: distributions to partners are not subject to Luxembourg dividend WHT and are not treated as dividends; subsequent profit distributions by a corporate partner may be subject to Luxembourg WHT at 15% when paid to individual non-resident shareholders (reduced to 5% under DTA for qualifying corporate shareholders).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million group consolidated revenue triggers the mandatory preparation of a Master File and a Luxembourg Local File; an additional activity threshold requires Local File preparation where the Luxembourg entity's related-party cross-border transactions exceed EUR 1 million for goods/services royalties (or EUR 250,000 for intragroup financing transactions). Below these thresholds, a 'light' TP file (functional analysis, pricing evidence, comparability analysis) must be available upon request to justify arm's-length behaviour; the SCSp's IoT retail operations normally have no regulatory licence, so the TP risk is limited to payments for intragroup procurement, e-commerce platform fees, IP licensing, and group management charges.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no dedicated standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to the Cayman or UAE codes, but fiscally transparent SCSp must prove real economic presence in Luxembourg to preserve treaty access and to avoid being recharacterised as a domestic taxable company under General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). The tax administration applies the OECD BEPS substance indicators: local management, full-time employees, active decision-making in Luxembourg, adequate IT infrastructure, and a physical office. A pure letterbox SCSp engaged in IoT retail with no personnel or operational substance will be denied DTA benefits, and profits may be retroactively reallocated to the controlling partner's jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – deliberate concealment of income or profits

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 10% of the recalculated taxable base, increased to 50% in cases of fraud; criminal fine up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; plus monthly late interest (0.8%) accruing from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors and SCSp general partners may be jointly and severally liable for the amount of unpaid corporate tax and interest where the tax loss is attributable to their wilful misconduct; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and a prohibition on exercising management functions in Luxembourg for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / letterbox SCSp (no economic substance)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a fully taxable Luxembourg corporation; denial of the SCSp's pass-through treatment; reassessment of corporate tax at 24.94% plus penalties up to 10% of previously avoided tax; potential 10% equalisation tax on payments to non-cooperative tax havens; under the EU ATAD, an exit tax may also be triggered.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable for the SCSp's tax deficiencies up to the full amount of unpaid tax and penalties; courts can order civil restitution of evaded taxes plus interest, and impose director disqualification orders, asset seizures and travel bans pending recovery.
AML/KYC Failure (Stripe/Shopify Payments flows)

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fine up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; imprisonment of up to 5 years for individuals; mandatory liquidation notifications; revocation of banking/payment licences (if any) and public disclosure of sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners face personal criminal liability, disqualification from board positions, and direct civil restitution of funds laundered through the SCSp; travel bans and preventive non-conviction based confiscation orders may be issued by Luxembourg courts.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax corrections (upward adjustment of taxable income) plus a 10% non-deductible penalty on the adjusted amount if TP documentation was absent; late interest at 0.8%/month; in serious cases, secondary adjustment and equalization tax may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return and deliberately approve the false TP policy are personally jointly liable for the adjustment and penalties; criminal sanctions for qualified negligence can include imprisonment up to 2 years, travel bans, and a prohibition on managing a commercial entity in the EEA.
Failure to File Tax Return / Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Lump-sum penalty up to EUR 1,200 per missed return plus 10% of the estimated unpaid tax; if non-filing is deemed fraudulent, the penalty increases to 50% of assessed tax, and criminal fines up to EUR 12,500 can be ordered; the tax office may issue an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) that treats the SCSp as taxable at 24.94%.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly and severally liable for all taxes assessed on the d'office basis, including penalties and monthly interest; courts may issue a payment order attaching personal bank accounts, and a travel ban limiting departure from Luxembourg until sufficient assets are confiscated.

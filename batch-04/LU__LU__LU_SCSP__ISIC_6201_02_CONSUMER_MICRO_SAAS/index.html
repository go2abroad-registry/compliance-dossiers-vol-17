Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated electronically by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); the RCS single window is attached to the District Court of Luxembourg and accepts e-filing through the LBR portal at lb.lu.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law on Commercial Companies of 10 August 1915), as amended — Special Limited Partnership (SCSp) regime under Articles 943 et seq., as introduced by the Law of 23 July 2016 on the Special Limited Partnership.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution / Contrat de Société (Limited Partnership Agreement) together with the RCS e-filing declaration; statutory extracts are published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA), the official electronic gazette of Luxembourg.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital, paid-up capital, or escrow requirement applies to an SCSp; partner contributions are purely contractual and are performed only to the extent and in the form agreed in the Limited Partnership Agreement.

Funding & unlock path: No heavy civil-defense permit is required for a lightweight software business; for any commercial domiciliation or office premises, the lessor must evidence fire-safety conformity with the standards of the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS), and if employees are present, the SCSp must comply with workplace-safety obligations under the Luxembourg Labour Code with the right of the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) to conduct verification visits (visite des lieux de travail).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital, paid-up capital, or escrow requirement applies to an SCSp; partner contributions are purely contractual and are performed only to the extent and in the form agreed in the Limited Partnership Agreement.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue – Administration des contributions directes (ACD), 33, rue de la Forêt, L-1533 Luxembourg; portal: https://acd.guichet.lu. VAT and indirect taxes administered by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); portal: https://e-tva.aed.public.lu. National business and tax filer portal: https://guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a Luxembourg VAT number before performing taxable or electronic supplies in Luxembourg by filing with the AEDT via the eTVA application module or by submitting the paper form 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' together with proof of foreign registration, statutes or partnership agreement, and list of directors. Non-established digital suppliers may alternatively register under the EU OSS scheme using a single identification in the country of establishment; where no EU establishment exists, direct Luxembourg VAT registration is compulsory. For direct corporate tax, non-resident entities with a Luxembourg permanent establishment must register with the ACD via the form 'Justification of ITIN/Matricule' and also obtain an RCS registration number at the Luxembourg Business Register. The SCSp, when fiscally transparent, normally does not require a CIT number but each partner is identified individually for Luxembourg tax filings if they are residents.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entities must obtain a Luxembourg VAT number before performing taxable or electronic supplies in Luxembourg by filing with the AEDT via the eTVA application module or by submitting the paper form 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' together with proof of foreign registration, statutes or partnership agreement, and list of directors. Non-established digital suppliers may alternatively register under the EU OSS scheme using a single identification in the country of establishment; where no EU establishment exists, direct Luxembourg VAT registration is compulsory. For direct corporate tax, non-resident entities with a Luxembourg permanent establishment must register with the ACD via the form 'Justification of ITIN/Matricule' and also obtain an RCS registration number at the Luxembourg Business Register. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. SCSp classification is governed by the LIR: default pass-through transparency unless the partnership is recharacterized as opaque under EU ATAD reverse-hybrid rules or under a ruling with the ACD. Available elective mechanisms include: (i) an SCSp may apply for an advance tax ruling to confirm transparent treatment; (ii) option to be subject, under limited statutory conditions, to Luxembourg corporate income tax and municipal business tax for partnerships that engage in certain commercial activities; (iii) for VAT, registration, and filing under the OSS/Union scheme to centralize cross-border B2C e-supplies; (iv) election to constitute a VAT group under Article 60 VAT Law when control and financial links exist. Hybrid mismatch elections and notifications under ATAD rules must be disclosed in the annual tax declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 first-euro registration for non-established electronic-service suppliers; established taxable persons may benefit from the small-business exemption when annual turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000 (VAT Law Art. 57, VAT-exclusive); intra-EU distance sales of electronically supplied services become liable to Luxembourg VAT or OSS filing once the aggregate value of distance sales exceeds EUR 10,000 (excl. VAT) across the EU in a calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of tangible goods where the goods physically leave the European Union within the statutory shipment period and the supplier retains proof of export as required by Article 55 of the VAT Law and Customs Administration guidance. Intra-EU B2B supplies of services may be zero-rated under the reverse charge when the customer provides a valid EU VAT ID and the supply is correctly entered in the EC Sales List. Electronically supplied services (Micro-SaaS) to a non-EU consumer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT, subject to evidence of customer location (billing address, IP address, payment bank country); supplies to EU consumers are taxed in the destination member state, and the Luxembourg supplier may account for VAT through the OSS scheme, thereby avoiding local registration. Zero-rating documentation for digital exports is not customs-based but must evidence the non-EU status of the recipient and the nature of the cross-border electronic service.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) submitted through the Luxembourg Customs authority or via the EU Export Control System (ECS)
  • Transport evidence demonstrating physical departure from the EU: bill of lading, CMR waybill, air waybill, rail consignment note, or postal export receipt
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export or SaaS subscription contract specifying the destination country, supply description, currency, and payment terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement proving receipt of settlement from the foreign customer within 180 days of supply
  • Supporting residence/location evidence for the customer: third-country tax ID, IP geolocation report for digital services, or VIES confirmation of the customer's EU VAT number where applicable

Luxembourg has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance API, or digital stamping regime for domestic B2B/B2C supplies as of the current tax year. E-invoicing is accepted in EDI/structured formats (e.g., Factur-X, PEPPOL) and is mandatory for public sector e-invoicing via PEPPOL network. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and OSS returns must be filed electronically through the AEDT eTVA portal; the platform supports XML/Web-service exchange for integrated filing by large volume taxpayers. The EU ViDA package, if adopted on schedule, will require cross-border e-invoicing reporting and digital registration by 2030, but has not yet been transposed into Luxembourg national legislation.

If export zero-rating is claimed without the requisite documentary evidence or the foreign payment is not repatriated within the 180-day benchmark, the AEDT will re-assess output VAT at the standard 17% rate on the undisclosed export turnover. Default interest accrues at the Luxembourg statutory late-payment rate. Administrative fines range from EUR 50 to EUR 4,000 per missing or false invoice/document, and are increased to up to 10% of the underpinned tax amount for gross negligence. Involving false customs declarations or forged transport documents constitutes tax fraud and attracts criminal penalties, including imprisonment of up to fifteen years and fines under the Luxembourg Criminal Code. Any input VAT credit previously claimed on costs allocated to the zero-rated export is retrospectively clawed back, with increased assessment time-limits; VAT assessments may be raised for up to five years, or ten years in proven fraud cases. Substantiation records must be retained for at least six years from the annual VAT return deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg in law and in practice. There is no exchange-control law, no capital-import or capital-export authorization, and no approval requirement for current-account or capital-account operations. Free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and by Luxembourg statutory provisions, subject only to EU sanctions, asset-freezing measures, and statistical reporting to the BCL. Profit remittance by a transparent SCSp is not blocked or taxed at the partnership level; non-resident partners are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax on partnership distributions, and the only Luxembourg income-tax exposure arises from a Luxembourg permanent establishment or a Luxembourg tax-resident status of a partner. Settlement of SaaS subscription proceeds through merchant-of-record platforms is freely permitted and does not require CSSF or BCL approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg, as a euro-area EU Member State, imposes no statutory surrender period, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timing for export receipts or cross-border service proceeds, whether denominated in EUR or in foreign currency. Exporters and service providers may freely retain, convert, or transfer foreign-currency balances. The only compliance obligations are the cross-border balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL under Regulation (EU) No 184/2014 and adherence to EU asset-freezing and restrictive-measures regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the central bank of Luxembourg and a member of the Eurosystem. Financial-sector prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Foreign-exchange policy for Luxembourg is determined within the European System of Central Banks and the euro-area framework, with no domestic exchange-control authority or licensing regime for current or capital FX transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for EEA-licensed e-money institutions and payment institutions relying on the PSD2 passporting regime under the Law of 1 February 2018 on payment services; such institutions can onboard the SCSp without a Luxembourg authorization, although the bank or EMI will typically demand the partnership deed, an RCS excerpt, UBO declarations, and a Luxembourg legal opinion. Low for non-EEA offshore digital EMIs, which generally cannot provide payment services or issue IBANs in Luxembourg without a CSSF license or without relying on a merchant-of-record structure. Legal constraints are elevated because the SCSp has no legal personality, the ultimate beneficial owners are resident outside Luxembourg, and the revenue stream is settled through foreign merchants of record; tier-1 banks therefore remain the safer clearing-rail backbone, with EMIs used only as secondary disbursement accounts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

The SCSp has no legal personality and no corporate deed retrievable from an official company register; banks struggle to determine the true client and the binding authority.

Root cause: The SCSp has no legal personality and no corporate deed retrievable from an official company register; banks struggle to determine the true client and the binding authority.

Defense evidence: Notarized or signed partnership agreement and the SCSp's terms; extract from the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) evidencing registration, registered office, and registered agent; certification of good standing from the RCS; a written banking mandate and power of attorney granted by the general partner; a Luxembourg legal opinion confirming the existence of the SCSp, its contractual nature, its tax transparency, and the scope of partner authority.
Universal-Beneficial-Ownership (UBO) and substance red flag: a partnership with no minimum capital, no employees, and partners dispersed across high- or medium-risk jurisdictions.

Root cause: Universal-Beneficial-Ownership (UBO) and substance red flag: a partnership with no minimum capital, no employees, and partners dispersed across high- or medium-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: RBE (registre des bénéficiaires effectifs) filing receipt and UBO declaration; percentage ownership and control chart of all partners above the 25% threshold; domiciliation agreement with a licensed Luxembourg corporate-service provider; substance file including leases, service-provider contracts, and invoices; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds declarations for each significant partner, supported by bank statements, tax returns, or audited accounts.
Cross-border subscription revenue is settled through foreign merchant-of-record platforms (Paddle and Lemon Squeezy), which can mask the true commercial counterparty and create an uncontrolled money trail.

Root cause: Cross-border subscription revenue is settled through foreign merchant-of-record platforms (Paddle and Lemon Squeezy), which can mask the true commercial counterparty and create an uncontrolled money trail.

Defense evidence: Executed merchant-of-record agreements with Paddle and Lemon Squeezy; complete payout and settlement statements for at least three months; access to the live SaaS product and published terms of service; PCI DSS compliance attestations of the platforms; IP assignment or licensing agreements with the SCSp; explanatory memorandum mapping the customer payment flow from consumer to platform to the Luxembourg entity.
Pass-through tax classification can be misinterpreted as a tax-evasion vehicle, especially when profits are distributed to foreign partners without Luxembourg withholding.

Root cause: Pass-through tax classification can be misinterpreted as a tax-evasion vehicle, especially when profits are distributed to foreign partners without Luxembourg withholding.

Defense evidence: Written Luxembourg tax advice confirming the SCSp's fiscal transparency under the amended law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies and the 2013 SCSp legislation; confirmation that the SCSp is not an opaque corporate-type partnership; a copy of the annual Luxembourg tax filing (or exemption declaration); a partner-level tax indemnity and reporting covenant; documentation of any Luxembourg municipal business tax or net-wealth-tax exposure where applicable.
Fully digital founder group and absence of an executive office in Luxembourg triggers heightened risk-of-impersonation and business-authenticity scrutiny.

Root cause: Fully digital founder group and absence of an executive office in Luxembourg triggers heightened risk-of-impersonation and business-authenticity scrutiny.

Defense evidence: Certified copies of passports and apostilled identification documents; letters of reference from an existing CSSF-regulated institution; a domiciliation/registered-agent certificate from a Luxembourg service provider; recorded videoconference KYC interview with time-stamped logs; detailed business and treasury plan for the Micro-SaaS operation; clean criminal-record certificates and PEP/sanctions declarations for all named officers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions must be wired in EUR through SEPA Credit Transfer from an EU/EEA bank account or through SWIFT MT103 from a non-EEA bank, with the remitter's name matching the partner register and the reference field identifying the SCSp and the capital subscription. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank will require the source-of-funds evidence, the partnership deed, and a UBO declaration before crediting. The bank applies the ECB balance-of-payments statistical purpose code for direct-investment equity capital (the bank-specific 'DI-EQUITY' or equivalent code) and enters SWIFT field 70 with a clear capital-contribution description. Non-EEA remittances are subject to sanctions screening and, where the remitter sends USD, to an FX conversion at the bank's daily EUR reference rate. Funds are first parked in a partner capital collection account and are released to the operational account only after full onboarding due diligence and confirmation of the partnership's RCS registration.For the Micro-SaaS commercial model, the SCSp executes mostly low-value, high-frequency outflows using SEPA Credit Transfer for EU payables and SWIFT MT103 (with UETR) for non-EU payables such as cloud infrastructure, subprocessors, contractors, and legal or accounting fees. Inbound merchant-of-record flows from Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are received as net payouts to the Luxembourg IBAN, which constitutes the primary clearing rail for SaaS subscription revenue. Commercial letters of credit are technically inappropriate and should not be used for licensing, subscription, or cloud-service settlements; for any future high-value cross-border software resale or distribution arrangement, a SWIFT MT103 with purpose code for software/royalty fees and the corresponding VAT or e-invoicing reference will provide a sufficient and auditable clearing trail.Outright deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/GBP forward contracts (vanilla, maturity generally up to 12 months) executed with a CSSF-authorized credit institutionFX spot transactions at the partner bank's daily ECB euro reference rateCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP fixed-floating and floating-fixed) with an EEA-authorized investment firm, subject to EMIR clearing, margin, and transaction-reporting obligationsVanilla FX options (European and American, long or short) with an EEA-authorized counterparty, subject to the appropriate MiFID II suitability and EMIR reporting frameworkNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for underlying currencies that are not freely deliverable within the euro-area settlement system (e.g., CNH or INR) and executed with a licensed counterparty; NDFs are not available or economically justified for EUR as the euro is fully convertible and deliverable

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% (statutory national CIT); combined national effective 18.19% after the 7% solidarity surcharge; aggregate Luxembourg City effective burden approx. 24.94% when municipal business tax is included.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax applies to dividends paid by Luxembourg-resident collective entities; transparent SCSp distributions are profit allocations, not legal dividends, and are not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due by 31 March following the fiscal year-end for paper returns, and by 31 May for e-filed returns; extensions are available on reasoned application to the Luxembourg direct tax administration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory Luxembourg Local File or Master File for a micro-SaaS SCSp falling below SME thresholds. General Luxembourg guidance requires contemporaneous OECD-compliant documentation for controlled transactions once related-party revenue or charges exceed EUR 1,000,000 per counterparty in a fiscal year; Master File and CbCR become mandatory when multinational group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies substantive anti-abuse doctrine through Article 6 of the Tax Abatement Act, the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, and the OECD principal purpose test. An SCSp holding only SaaS subscription contracts with no local employees, no bank account, no decision-making, and no physical activity may be treated as a shell, with profits reallocated or treaty benefits denied. The micro-SaaS must carry out genuine functions, use local assets where IP is developed, and bear real economic risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative re-assessment, 10% negligence surcharge, 0.6% monthly interest, and criminal prosecution for fiscal fraud. Luxembourg criminal tax law provides for fines up to EUR 200,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for intentional false declarations and concealment of taxable income.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any de facto manager who signs false declarations are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; personal asset seizure, travel bans, and director disqualification may be imposed in criminal proceedings.
Shell Company and Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, recharacterization of the SCSp as a taxable Luxembourg entity, application of ATAD GAAR with a corrective tax equal to the denied tax advantage, and anti-abuse interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner may be held personally liable for the entire re-assessed tax if the SCSp is used as an alter ego and partnership formalities are ignored; courts may lift the partnership veil and restrict the individual from managing Luxembourg entities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: OECD-compliant transfer pricing adjustment, secondary recalculation, and additional tax with monthly interest. Intentional TP documentation falsification attracts penalties up to 100% of the underpaid tax and potential criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who knowingly approve fabricated intercompany agreements can be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax; serious cases lead to disqualification and criminal prosecution for forgery.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML and RBE sanctions include administrative fines, criminal fines, and imprisonment for money laundering of up to five years; the Luxembourg supervisory authority may also suspend the entity's operations and restrict its bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any AML responsible officer face personal criminal liability and civil forfeiture if they collaborated in laundering subscription proceeds or deliberately concealed beneficial ownership; professional debarment and travel restrictions may apply.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Luxembourg VAT, registration and indirect tax authority; operational portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet Public (https://guichet.public.lu); direct tax counterpart: Administration des contributions directes (ACD); customs and EORI matters: Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs and Excise).

E-government filing portal

1) Obtain the Luxembourg national business registration number ('matricule') from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — required before VAT registration for any non-resident entity carrying out taxable supplies in Luxembourg. 2) Submit Form 1 (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) electronically via MyGuichet to the AEDT, annexing the SCSp partnership agreement (contrat de société), proof of identity of the general partner (commandité) and the registered office/correspondent in Luxembourg, expected date of commencement of taxable activities, and any foreign VAT identification evidence. 3) AEDT issues a VAT identification number in the national format LU + 8 digits (TVA LU00000000), which is linked to the LBR matricule. 4) The SCSp must simultaneously obtain an EORI number (format LU + 8 digits, identical stem to the VAT number) for customs/export operations. 5) For foreign managers or non-resident general partners, a Luxembourg tax representative may be required for VAT compliance; a written power of attorney must be filed with the AEDT. 6) The entity must complete periodic VAT registrations and, where turnover thresholds are exceeded, intra-EU recapitulative statements and Intrastat declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) has no separate legal personality and is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT) and net wealth tax; the partnership's income, gains and assets are imputed to the partners pro rata under their respective tax status. Notably, the SCSp is nevertheless treated as a separate 'taxable person' for VAT purposes under the Luxembourg VAT Law, so the SCSp itself must register for VAT, issue VAT invoices and file periodic VAT returns in respect of its manufacturing and sale of surgical laparoscopy towers, notwithstanding its pass-through treatment for income tax. if the SCSp engages in commercial activity, it can be individually liable to municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), with the tax computed at partnership level but effectively allocable among the partners; the entity may also be required to register for payroll withholding if it employs staff.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; partnership transparency is ex lege and cannot be overridden for income tax purposes, although the foreign qualification of the SCSp as opaque can give rise to hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD (implemented in the Luxembourg Income Tax Law). Available elections include: (i) election to form a VAT group ('assujetti unique') under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, subject to prior AEDT approval; (ii) option to tax real estate rental under Article 44 of the VAT Law, where the SCSp owns property used in the manufacturing facility; (iii) election for joint corporate tax filing/fiscal unity under Article 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law — only available to fully taxable resident companies, not to an SCSp; (iv) optional VAT reverse charge for imports and certain intra-EU acquisitions under Articles 47 et seq. of the VAT Law; (v) small-enterprise franchise election under Article 57 of the VAT Law where annual turnover is below EUR 35,000, subject to the non-application of the franchise to exports and to non-resident taxable persons; (vi) hybrid mismatch transparency position statement, whereby the Luxembourg tax administration may be requested to confirm the transparent classification of the SCSp in relation to a specific foreign jurisdiction to prevent double taxation or double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold for a manufacturing exporter: registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable activity. A domestic small-enterprise franchise (régime de la franchise) exempts resident established taxable persons whose annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000 (exclusive of VAT), but the franchise does not apply to non-resident taxable persons and does not cover export transactions where zero-rating must be claimed. The manufacturing of surgical laparoscopy towers (ISIC 3250 / HS 9018) conducted through an SCSp therefore requires immediate Luxembourg VAT registration, EORI registration, and customs presence irrespective of projected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies to the permanent export of goods (including Ultra-HD 4K minimally invasive rigid endoscopes and laparoscopic towers) outside the EU, pursuant to Articles 42 et seq. of the Luxembourg VAT Law, where all of the following cumulative conditions are met: (1) physical export from the EU is completed and evidenced by a valid customs export declaration (EX-A / ECS) bearing the Luxembourg EORI number; (2) the risk of loss or damage is effectively transferred to the non-EU buyer under the underlying sale contract (SWIFT LC / TT payment, Incoterms such as FOB, CIF, CIP or DAP) before the VAT liability arises; (3) the goods are transported to a third country within the administrative evidence window (Luxembourg administrative practice: normally 3 months, extendable up to 6 months in justified cases; the 180-day benchmark below constitutes the documented due-diligence standard for refund acceleration); (4) the seller's records convincingly demonstrate the actual destination and the commercial reality of the export, including transport documents issued in the name of the seller or the seller's agent; (5) for services directly related to and ancillary to the exported towers (installation supervision, commissioning, and end-user training at the non-EU hospital), zero-rating is available where the customer is a non-EU taxable person and the benefit of the services is localized outside the EU; (6) the VAT invoice must show the statutory mention 'Exonération – exportation article 42' and the AEDT may require a written confirmation of export from the buyer or its customs broker.

  • Customs Export Declaration — EX-A / ECS computerized declaration with MRN (Movement Reference Number), Luxembourg EORI number, commodity code per the surgical-laparoscopy product classification (HS 9018 / 8528 subcomponents as applicable), declared VAT position 0.00, and actual exit office within the EU.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR — transport contract evidencing physical departure from EU territory, route, container or airwaybill reference, consignee in the non-EU country, and cross-reference to the customs MRN and commercial invoice.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — documentary banking evidence of the export sales proceeds, such as SWIFT MT103 credit advice, LC settlement record, or TT wire confirmation showing receipt of the foreign currency proceeds by the SCSp's account; under Luxembourg VAT practice this is part of the commercial-diligence bundle used by AEDT on refund audits, although an EU member state may not lawfully impose a substantive 180-day repatriation condition as a validity requirement of the VAT zero-rating.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list — signed commercial invoice containing the export-exemption reference, full description of the Ultra-HD 4K laparoscopy towers, unit values, Incoterms, packing list, insurance certificate if any, and the buyer's purchase order.

No real-time fiscal clearance (CTC), digital stamping or continuous transaction control exists in Luxembourg for B2B invoicing. Mandatory electronic obligations include: (i) B2G e-invoicing to the Luxembourg State and public sector entities via the PEPPOL network with structured formats (UBL / Factur-X) since 1 January 2021; (ii) mandatory electronic filing of periodic VAT returns, recapitulative statements (DEB), and Intrastat declarations through the e-File / MyGuichet platform; (iii) invoicing under Article 74 of the VAT Law requiring full authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility, with archival for 10 years; (iv) transmission of customs export declarations via the EU ECS system with MRN confirmation; (v) optional use of EDI or qualified e-signature as accepted authenticity controls; there is no additional API-based stamping requirement for the medical-device export supply chain.

AEDT audit scrutiny of export zero-rating for surgical-hardware manufacturers is intensive: inspectors cross-check the ECS customs declarations, MRN status, transport manifest, insurance files, and bank statements against the SWIFT LC / TT proceeds credited to the SCSp. Where export evidence is incomplete or inconsistent, the AEDT recharacterizes the supply as taxable and reassesses VAT at 17%, applying late-payment interest (statutory rate: 0.6% per month, or the periodically published statutory interest variant) from the original return due date. Penalties escalate as follows: (i) administrative non-fraud penalties up to EUR 10,000 under the general tax law; (ii) fraud penalties of up to the amount of tax evaded, with exclusion of refund claims; (iii) criminal sanctions for deliberate VAT fraud pursuant to Articles 127–128 of the VAT Law, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 25,000 per invoice batch; (iv) retrospective clawback of refunded VAT, compounded by the 17% re-assessed VAT, interest, and penalties, and sustained by joint and several liability of the general partner (commandité) and any tax representative of the SCSp; (v) the taxpayer must retain all export documentation for 10 years, as the absence of records creates a rebuttable presumption against zero-rating. Luxembourg applies the 180-day repatriation benchmark as an administrative due-diligence and refund-acceleration cut-off, although it is not a formal statutory validity condition of the exemption under EU law; non-repatriation within 180 days without commercial justification will materially heighten audit scrutiny and the risk of ECS-exit refusal or reverse-charge reassessment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Luxembourg applies unrestricted capital and current account movements under Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. No prior authorisation, surrender requirement, or FX licensing applies to profit remittance, capital repatriation, trade settlement, or loan servicing. Payments are limited only by EU/UN sanctions screening, CSSF financial-sanctions guidance, and AML/CFT obligations under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 as amended; corporate outflows are governed by corporate-law distribution tests (Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended) and tax substance, not FX control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Luxembourg; exporters may retain export proceeds in EUR or any foreign currency in BCL-supervised bank accounts without restriction. Cross-border movements must be recorded by the reporting bank under ECB Balance of Payments / BCL VAD statistical reporting and under Regulation (EU) 2015/847, including originator and beneficiary data for every wire transfer; cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more require a customs declaration under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); supranational monetary authority is the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential banking supervision by Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

MEDIUM-HIGH for EU-authorised/PSD2-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) operating in Luxembourg (e.g., CSSF-licensed or EEA-passported payment/electronic-money institutions). EMIs are viable as supplementary multi-currency settlement wallets for trade receivables and vendor disbursements, but are legally incapable of holding deposits, issuing commercial letters of credit, or providing credit lines under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and the EU PSD2 framework. Capital escrow and LC-backed trade flows must therefore remain with a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution.

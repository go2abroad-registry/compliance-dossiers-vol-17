Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 9 rue du Laboratoire, L-1911 Luxembourg; electronic filing via the RCS e-filing portal maintained by the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Governing statute

Amended Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as consolidated, notably Articles 710-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); supplemented by the Law of 10 August 2016 (modernisation of the 1915 Law) and the Law of 23 September 2020 (optional simplified SARL regime, not used); RCS procedure governed by the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Authentic notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) executed before a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically with the RCS; the formal extract of the articles is published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard SARL requires minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash or in kind at or before the execution of the notarial deed; funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the future corporate name at a Luxembourg credit institution or a CSSF-authorised bank, with the bank's blockage certificate delivered to the notary and the funds released only upon production of the RCS registration extract; in-kind contributions require a notary's valuation report per Article 710-7; the optional S.à r.l.-S with EUR 1 minimum capital is not adopted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance certificate required under the amended Law of 10 February 2014 on fire safety of buildings, verified with the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de sauvetage (CGDIS); structural load clearance and occupancy authorization derived from the landlord's building permit and final occupancy permit; workplace safety inspection by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) under the Labor Code (Code du travail), covering electrical equipment verification, emergency evacuation planning, first-aid arrangements, and ergonomic workplace requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard SARL requires minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash or in kind at or before the execution of the notarial deed; funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the future corporate name at a Luxembourg credit institution or a CSSF-authorised bank, with the bank's blockage certificate delivered to the notary and the funds released only upon production of the RCS registration extract; in-kind contributions require a notary's valuation report per Article 710-7; the optional S.à r.l.-S with EUR 1 minimum capital is not adopted.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — international media relations and crisis public relations (ISIC 7310 / NAICS 541820) is an unregulated professional service in Luxembourg; no industrial establishment permit is required under the amended Law of 2 September 2011 on access to professions. Reference point for any future regulated activities: Ministère de l'Économie, Direction générale des classes moyennes.

Category: Not applicable — no sector-specific operating, manufacturing, or professional license is required for an overseas PR communication firm; the only administrative entitlements are the RCS registration, VAT registration with the AEDT, and, if employees are hired, employer registration with the CCSS.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT competence; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct tax competence; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) for commercial registration. All official filings and pre-registration procedures are conducted via the MyGuichet.lu digital single portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a Luxembourg seat must first obtain an RCS registration number (matricule) if it establishes a local establishment, or apply directly to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number using the single national registration form. The RCS matricule is an 11-digit business identifier and serves as the prerequisite for VAT ID issuance. The AEDT subsequently issues a 13-character VAT number with the 'LU' prefix. Where the foreign entity does not create a fixed establishment in Luxembourg, it may be required to appoint an indirect VAT representative (représentant responsable) established in Luxembourg pursuant to Article 65b of the Luxembourg VAT Law.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL is a resident capital company with full legal personality, subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax). It is not fiscally transparent and no tax-exempt zone regime applies to active PR service income within Luxembourg territory. — A foreign entity without a Luxembourg seat must first obtain an RCS registration number (matricule) if it establishes a local establishment, or apply directly to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number using the single national registration form. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL is a resident capital company with full legal personality, subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax). It is not fiscally transparent and no tax-exempt zone regime applies to active PR service income within Luxembourg territory.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity tax classification follows the legal form. Available elections include: (i) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the VAT Law; (ii) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax between a 95%-owned Luxembourg parent and its subsidiaries; (iii) joint taxation for SARLs with a family character; and (iv) hybrid mismatch neutralization adjustments under the ATAD-derived rules. No entity classification election is available for a standard SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) for the small-business exemption under Article 57 of the VAT Law; €10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold for cross-border B2C supplies; mandatory registration without any threshold for non-established foreign suppliers and for intra-EU acquisitions exceeding €10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to (i) intra-Community B2B supplies of goods supported by the customer's valid EU VAT identification number; (ii) exports of goods outside the EU with proof of physical removal; (iii) international services ancillary to the export of goods; and (iv) B2B services, including international PR, media relations, and crisis communication services, rendered to customers established outside Luxembourg, which are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the business-to-business place-of-supply rules and are economically equivalent to zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice referencing the underlying service contract
  • Transport evidence (CMR consignment note, airway bill, sea waybill)
  • EC Sales List / recapitulative statement for intra-EU supplies

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently in force in Luxembourg. B2B e-invoicing in Factur-X, UBL, or PEPPOL formats is permitted but optional. Export and intra-Community supplies must be substantiated through the Luxembourg Single Window (Guichet unique) customs declarations and Intrastat filings where applicable. Accounting records must be archived in accordance with the FAIA (Fichier d'Assurance, d'Intégrité et d'Authenticité) integrity standard and retained for 10 years for audit purposes.

The AEDT conducts risk-based desk and field audits of zero-rating and reverse-charge claims; the taxable person bears the burden of proof for the export or intra-Community removal of goods and for the overseas status of service recipients. Incomplete or defective documentation triggers retrospective VAT clawback plus delinquency interest on the Luxembourg VAT due, currently assessed at 1.6% per month, and administrative fines of up to €4,000 per infringement. Intentional VAT fraud under Paragraph 396 of the Luxembourg Abgabenordnung is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or a fine of up to €50,000, with directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully freely convertible. Luxembourg applies no exchange control regime and imposes no restrictions on current account or capital account transactions, implementing full liberalisation under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Profit remittances, dividend distributions, repayment of shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to Luxembourg corporate law solvency tests (Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies) and AML/KYC verification by the instructing bank. No prior BCL, CSSF, or Ministry of Finance authorisation is required for any cross-border transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or foreign-currency receivables. Cross-border service fees and retainer income generated by the overseas PR firm may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency (USD, GBP, CHF) in multi-currency accounts or converted to EUR at freely negotiated market rates without administrative approval.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), integrated in the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of banks, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate viability with legal constraints. Luxembourg-licensed payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) regulated by the CSSF (e.g. EEA-licensed PSPs) may offer faster onboarding and convenient cross-border disbursement, but are legally restricted to payment services: they cannot accept deposits repayable under the Banking Act 1993, cannot grant credit, and are subject to safeguarding requirements on client funds. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA licence have low viability: they cannot access TARGET2 or direct SEPA membership, and Luxembourg credit institutions generally refuse to act as clearing correspondents for unlicensed EMIs. Recommended structure: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as the principal operating and treasury rail, with an EU/EEA-authorised EMI as a supplementary card-acquiring, small-disbursement, or payout rail only.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective rate for Luxembourg SARL taxable income above EUR 175,000 (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% communal business tax in Luxembourg City; reduced combined rate of 22.80% for taxable income not exceeding EUR 175,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Luxembourg SARL; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% holding for at least 12 months) and often 5% or 0% under comprehensive double tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when annual intra-group loans/deposits exceed EUR 100,000 or other annual intra-group transactions in goods, services or intangibles exceed EUR 250,000; Master File mandatory when the multinational group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime in Luxembourg; substance requirements are enforced through the domestic General Anti-Abuse Rule, the ATAD interest limitation and CFC rules, the EU anti-tax-avoidance jurisprudence, and tax residence tests requiring real registered office, local management decisions, personnel and business premises; a pure letter-box SARL without genuine economic substance in Luxembourg is exposed to denial of treaty benefits, shell-company reclassification and register-based penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge of 10% to 20% of the evaded tax plus default interest; criminal conviction for tax fraud carries fines of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years in serious cases.

Director personal liability: The gérant or director who caused the false filing is jointly and severally liable for the tax, related interest and penalties; courts may order civil restitution, disqualification from managing a Luxembourg company, and travel restriction to leave Luxembourg while the tax debt remains unsatisfied.
Shell Company / Letter-box Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence certificate, retrospective CIT reassessment, refusal of treaty relief, application of the GAAR, dismantling of tax neutrality, and monetary penalties for false declaratory filings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the restated corporate taxes if the shell structure was maintained under their express instruction; travel bans and director-disqualification orders are applied where the tax exposure exceeds EUR 125,000.
Abusive Profit Repatriation / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the transaction as a taxable deemed distribution, denial of the participation exemption, anti-abuse reassessment with default interest, and administrative penalty of up to 10% to 20% of the additional tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the abusive distribution without solvency testing are exposed to civil restitution to the company and creditors, garnishment of personal assets, and judicial liability orders for unpaid corporate tax and social contributions.
AML / RBO / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 per infraction for RBO non-compliance; criminal money-laundering liability involving fraud-linked proceeds attracts fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the statutory AML compliance officer are personally amenable for the design and enforcement of KYC procedures; in money-laundering convictions, civil confiscation of personal assets, travel bans and imprisonment of the directing mind are routine.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Mispricing

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment of taxable profits, recharacterisation of the transaction, payment of additional CIT and communal business tax plus monthly default interest, and a separate documentation penalty capped at a percentage of the adjusted profit in flagrant cases.

Director personal liability: The managing director signing group service agreements and tax filings can be personally targeted by the ACD for tax not assessed as a result of intentional mispricing; civil liability actions by minority shareholders and creditors may follow.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital, no paid-up capital requirement, and no escrow account requirement under Luxembourg law for an SCSp; capital contributions are freely determined by the partners and recorded in the partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety inspection and clearance by the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) covering alarm systems, extinguishers, and unobstructed egress; occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) for electrical installations (Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 February 2019), first-aid equipment, ergonomic workstations, and emergency procedures; no structural-load modification is required for a standard office lease, but a structural load certificate is retained for landlord sign-off.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital, no paid-up capital requirement, and no escrow account requirement under Luxembourg law for an SCSp; capital contributions are freely determined by the partners and recorded in the partnership agreement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), 33 boulevard Roosevelt, L-2015 Luxembourg (https://impotsdirects.public.lu). VAT, registration duties and estate taxes: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) (https://pfi.public.lu). Business registry and statutory identification: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS and RBE (https://www.lbr.lu). E-filing of VAT and related declarations: eCDF (electronic Customs and Fiscal Declarations) portal; general e-government services: MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

An SCSp used as a ship crew recruitment agency must be incorporated and filed at the RCS through the LBR to obtain the national business identification number (numéro d'identification, 17-digit matricule) and complete the RBE beneficial-ownership registration. VAT registration is effected with the AEDT (via eCDF/MyGuichet) using VAT Forms 1 or 2 (for established and non-established persons respectively), resulting in a Luxembourg VAT identification number (N° TVA) within 2–4 weeks. Since the SCSp is fiscally transparent, it is not a direct-tax taxpayer itself, but it must file an annual informational declaration (déclaration de renseignements) with the ACD allocating income to general and limited partners. Each partner, including non-resident corporate partners, must obtain a Luxembourg taxpayer number (N° d'immatriculation) and file its own tax returns. Foreign entities conducting crew-management activity through a Luxembourg permanent establishment must register that PE with the ACD within one month of commencement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory default under Article 2(1) of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR). It is not subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) or net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) at entity level; income, expenses and tax attributes pass through pro rata in accordance with the partnership agreement and are taxed at partner level. Resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed in Luxembourg only on Luxembourg-source income, including profits attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment. Crew recruitment income (ISIC 5222) is ordinary business income. No tax-exempt economic zone applies in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

(i) Opt-in to corporate tax treatment: under Article 2(2) LIR, as amended by the Law of 23 July 2016, an SCSp may elect, with the unanimous consent of all partners and subject to statutory conditions, to be treated as a capital company subject to CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the election is filed with the ACD and is binding for at least five fiscal years. (ii) Hybrid mismatch rules: Luxembourg applies EU ATAD 1/2 hybrid mismatch provisions (LIR Articles 168quinquies et seq.), relevant where a foreign jurisdiction classifies the SCSp as a separate taxable entity (e.g., via a US Form 8832 check-the-box election); the reverse-hybrid rule requires a Luxembourg transparent entity to be treated as opaque if formed in Luxembourg, controlled by a foreign partner, and the partnership income would otherwise be untaxed. (iii) VAT grouping: the SCSp and its Luxembourg fixed establishment(s) may form a VAT group (assujettis unis) with other Luxembourg VAT taxable persons. (iv) No general unilateral check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg domestic direct tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

18%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 — small-business franchise exemption (Article 50 Loi TVA): a Luxembourg-established taxable person whose annual taxable turnover excluding VAT does not exceed EUR 30,000 may be exempt from VAT registration and invoicing obligations; registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded or when the business opts to register to recover input VAT. Non-established foreign suppliers of B2B crew services must apply Luxembourg reverse-charge and place-of-supply rules; registration is required where the place of supply is Luxembourg. The EU EUR 10,000 cross-border distance-sales threshold applies only to B2C supplies of goods and electronically supplied services. Reference rate note: the standard VAT headline rate increased from 17% to 18% with effect from 1 January 2025.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% export VAT rate may be claimed under Article 44(1)(a) of the Loi TVA, implementing Article 148(1)(a) of EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC, which exempts with credit the supply of services for the direct needs of seagoing vessels used in maritime traffic (commercial, industrial, rescue or fishing operations) and their cargo. A ship crew recruitment and management agency (ISIC 5222) may zero-rate services such as crew placement, manning, payroll administration, and crew changeover/transport when the services are directly linked to a qualifying vessel. Eligibility conditions: (i) the vessel must be a seagoing vessel operating on the high seas for commercial, industrial, rescue or fishing purposes, not a pleasure craft; (ii) the service must be supplied at the request of and for the operational needs of the shipowner or operator; (iii) the supplier must obtain and retain contemporaneous evidence of vessel identity and use and of the direct-need link; and (iv) the service must not be a standalone headhunting or HR service disconnected from a specific vessel, which would be taxable at 18%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ÉvD/MRN) and exit confirmation — required only for physical export of goods to non-EU destinations; not applicable to pure crew services
  • Bill of Lading or Vessel Manifest evidencing the vessel's international voyage and crew deployment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank confirmation of fee repatriation from the foreign shipowner or paying agent
  • Crew management agreement with the shipowner/operator together with the vessel's IMO registration certificate confirming seagoing commercial use

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, government API integration or digital stamping for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is permitted under EU standard EN 16931 (Peppol BIS 3.0 UBL); B2G e-invoicing for public procurement has been mandatory since 18 October 2020 for contracting authorities operating e-invoicing. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and paper or PDF invoices are generally valid. VAT returns, EU recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) and INTRASTAT declarations are filed electronically through the AEDT eCDF portal. No government approval of invoice data is required in real time. Luxembourg has committed to the EU ViDA reforms calling for mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting for cross-border supplies by 2030, but the current regime remains an open e-invoicing framework.

Luxembourg has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline specific to export services; the 180-day benchmark aligns with the UCC 150-day exit-confirmation rule applied by analogy to goods and with the requirement that zero-rating evidence be available no later than the filing deadline of the periodic VAT return. The AEDT scrutinises export and zero-rating claims with elevated intensity, and the burden of proving vessel qualification and direct need rests on the taxpayer. If a claim is disallowed, the AEDT issues a corrective assessment restating standard-rate (18%) VAT, and the following sanctions may apply: (1) statutory late-payment interest of 0.8% per month (9.6% per annum); (2) administrative penalties up to 10% of the assessed VAT, increased to 20%–50% in cases of bad faith or deliberate concealment; (3) criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating with fines up to EUR 125,000 or twice the tax evaded and imprisonment for responsible officers; (4) extension of the standard 5-year assessment period to 10 years in fraud cases, permitting retrospective clawback of input VAT credits and reclassification of exports; and (5) personal and joint liability of the SCSp's general partner for VAT penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member with a fully liberalised capital account. Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible for residents and non-residents, with no general exchange control restrictions. Profit remittance, liquidation proceeds, and investor distributions are freely transferable, subject only to anti-money-laundering (AML/KYC) due diligence, EU/UN sanctions screening, and standard tax formalities (including verification of the SCSp's tax transparency status and potential anti-abuse withholding rules under the ATAD and Luxembourg domestic law).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced repatriation timeline exists under Luxembourg law. Export and related service receipts may be retained in any currency. Reporting is required solely for Balance of Payments statistics under ECB Regulation (EU) No 2013/33 and BCL instructions; entities must file monthly or quarterly statistical returns when transaction thresholds are met, but no commercial restrictions or conversion obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking prudential supervision; BCL acts within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and applies ECB monetary and FX reporting frameworks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. EU-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed under PSD2 and passporting into Luxembourg may be used as supplementary clearing rails for lower-risk crew payroll disbursements and seafarer money transfers. However, such EMIs cannot replace a Luxembourg licensed credit institution for receiving investor capital contributions, holding client funds in escrow, or executing structural corporate transactions. Offshore (non-EEA) digital EMIs are not viable due to CSSF supervisory expectations, European AML/CFT rules, and the absence of reliable EUR correspondent banking relationships for a Luxembourg-domiciled SCSp.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All investor capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent DVP/FCY instruction) to the SCSp's main Luxembourg bank account. The remittance information (field 70/77B) must include the SCSp partnership reference, the definitive capital call notice number, and the investor's UBO code. Purpose codes should follow BCL Balance of Payments classification, typically 'S.2.2' for investment in the own capital of the partnership, with a matching OECD BPM6 code where applicable. For non-EUR payments to non-SEPA jurisdictions, SWIFT MT103 wires via correspondent banks are used. For vessel supplies and larger operational procurements, commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) issued by the SCSp's bank in favour of the supplier provide settlement security. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

SCSp is fiscally transparent; no CIT at partnership level. Luxembourg resident commercial companies are taxed at 17% CIT (above EUR 175,000) + 7% solidarity surcharge (1.19%) + municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), yielding an aggregate headline rate of 24.94%.

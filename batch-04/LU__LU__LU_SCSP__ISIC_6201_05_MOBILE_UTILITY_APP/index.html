Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 1-3 rue de la Forêt, L-2929 Luxembourg; electronic filing portal at lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (the 1915 Law), as amended by the law of 23 July 2016, notably Articles 944-16 to 944-26 instituting the Société en commandite spéciale (SCSp); Loi modifiée du 19 décembre 2002 sur le registre de commerce et des sociétés et les comptes annuels (RCS law); Loi modifiée du 2 septembre 2011 réglementant l'accès aux professions d'artisan, de commerçant, d'industriel et à certaines professions libérales (establishment permit regime); Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 September 2016 on environmental impact assessment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Partnership Agreement (Statuts / Contrat de Société) in notarial or private-writing form, filed with the RCS; mandatory legal notice published in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations); registration certificate (extrait) issued by the RCS.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory capital requirement for an SCSp; contributions and payment terms are freely determined in the Partnership Agreement. No Luxembourg escrow account is required for cash contributions. In-kind contributions consisting of registered assets must be certified by a Luxembourg réviseur d'entreprises and effected by notarial deed where asset registration law so requires. The General Partner bears unlimited liability; each Limited Partner's liability is capped at its agreed capital contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the CGDIS for the registered office and any workplace (Class B low-occupancy office, fire extinguishers, unobstructed exit routes and emergency signage); building structural-load and occupancy permit approved by the competent communal authority; workplace safety clearance from the ITM after completion of the single risk assessment; mandatory registration of employees with the CCSS for social security coverage.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital requirement for an SCSp; contributions and payment terms are freely determined in the Partnership Agreement. No Luxembourg escrow account is required for cash contributions. In-kind contributions consisting of registered assets must be certified by a Luxembourg réviseur d'entreprises and effected by notarial deed where asset registration law so requires. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and registration; official single portal: guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Registry (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) through a Luxembourg domicile agent to obtain an RCS number. Step 2: Apply to the ACD for the Luxembourg national identification number (NIF) for the SCSp. Step 3: Apply separately to the AED for a VAT ID using the 'Déclaration d'inscription à la TVA' (VAT 100) via the MyGuichet electronic portal. Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, each partner must obtain its own NIF; the SCSp obtains a NIF for administrative filing purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg CIT purposes; profits are allocated directly to partners. Resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate.

Available election forms

Under Article 178 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), a transparent partnership may elect, under limited conditions, to be jointly taxed as a corporate body. Other statutory elections include the VAT group election (assujetti unique), the IP box regime election (80% exemption of qualifying IP income), and the notional interest deduction (NID) election. The SCSp does not have a direct U.S.-style check-the-box election, but hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by Luxembourg implementation of the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business exemption (Article 57 Luxembourg VAT Law); no registration threshold applies to non-established foreign suppliers, who must register after the first taxable supply in Luxembourg, subject to EU distance-sales and OSS rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU may be zero-rated provided the goods physically leave the EU within the prescribed timeline and the supplier retains full documentary evidence of export. Digital services and mobile utility applications supplied to non-EU customers (B2B or B2C) fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT because the place of supply is outside the EU; this requires documentary substantiation of the customer's non-EU status (e.g., IP address, business registration records, OECD residence certificate). To claim zero-rating on goods, the exporter must hold an EORI number, lodge a customs export declaration, and maintain the export contract/order.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via Douanes Luxembourg)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR (road) or Air Waybill (air)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank evidence of receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and export reason code
  • International contract / purchase order from the non-EU buyer
  • Proof of EU exit (customs endorsed export movement document, e.g., T2L, T1, or AEF)

Luxembourg currently does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping system. B2B e-invoicing is permitted with recipient consent. B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory since 1 June 2020 for invoices to the central Luxembourg administration using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format. VAT returns and EU recapitulative statements are filed electronically through the AED's eCDF portal. Luxembourg is preparing for the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) digital reporting and e-invoicing framework, but no continuous transaction control mandate is yet operational.

Luxembourg tax authorities may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating and claw back VAT, plus late payment interest at the current annual statutory rate of 8%. Administrative fines may reach EUR 10,000 per invoice or up to 100% of the evaded tax in cases of gross negligence. Intentional fraudulent export zero-rating is a criminal offence under Article 397 of the General Tax Code, punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years, monetary penalties, and an extended 10-year limitation period running from discovery of the fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-zone member with full capital and current account convertibility. No exchange controls, no profit remittance restrictions, and no tax withholding on dividends or interest under EU directives for qualifying entities. Remittance of profits from a SCSp is treated as a distribution to partners and is not subject to withholding tax in Luxembourg; however, banking compliance and anti-money-laundering obligations apply. In exceptional national-security or EU sanctions cases, temporary capital restrictions could be activated, but none are currently in force.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Luxembourg. Export receipts and all commercial receivables may be retained in EUR or freely converted into any other currency. The SCSp may hold multi-currency accounts without central bank approval. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) with prudential oversight by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); foreign exchange policy is governed by the European Central Bank (ECB) within the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 for daily collection from app stores (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing) and low-value cross-border disbursements. Limitations: EMIs cannot extend credit, hold client funds as term deposits, or provide full treasury/liquidity management; they also lack the robustness of SWIFT-backed clearing for large capital injection or trade settlement. The SCSp should use an EMI as a secondary payment gateway, not as the primary treasury bank. Legal constraint: EMI passports must be validated in Luxembourg and the EMI must comply with CSSF AML/CFT outsourcing requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the overseas investor's regulated banking institution to the SCSp's Luxembourg IBAN. Recommended purpose codes: 'INVEST' (Investment) or 'PCAP' (Capital Contributions) according to the ECB's purpose code classification. The receiving bank will require the capital contribution to match the subscribed amounts in the partnership agreement. For non-EUR transfers, the sending bank performs FX conversion at issue, or the SCSp may instruct the receiving bank to convert using the ECB reference rate. All inbound transfers must be accompanied by a detailed remittance instruction containing the SCSp full legal name, partner name, and partnership agreement article referencing the contribution. Enhanced due diligence is required for transfers above EUR 10,000 or from third-country high-risk jurisdictions.The SCSp's outbound clearing rails should consist of: (1) SWIFT MT103 for recurring operating payments, license fees, royalties, and subcontractor settlements; (2) Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) for any physical infrastructure procurement or significant commercial contracts, primarily unconfirmed and issued by the Luxembourg financial institution; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for all euro-denominated intra-EU payments below EUR 100,000, as this offers lower fees and immediate settlement. For app store disbursements, funds are generally accumulated in the app store's clearing house and settled on a net basis via ACH or SWIFT to the designated bank account. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% base CIT for fiscal year 2024/2025, plus a 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT and municipal business tax at a maximum of 6.75% in Luxembourg City, producing a combined top statutory burden of approximately 24.94%. The SCSp itself is fiscally transparent; no CIT is levied at the SCSp level.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax for dividends distributed by a Luxembourg capital company. In the SCSp, profit distributions to partners are not statutory dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and has no CIT personality. Where the SCSp holds shares in an operating company, dividends from that company qualify for 0% WHT under the Luxembourg participation exemption or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding or ≥ EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost and ≥12 months holding).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million consolidated group turnover for the Master File; EUR 15 million annual Luxembourg entity turnover for the Local File. Groups with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750 million must also file a Country-by-Country Report under OECD/EU CbCR requirements. In practice, the ACD may request arm's-length documentation for any related-party transaction even below these thresholds, particularly where mobile app IP royalties or intragroup development fees are present.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a bespoke Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Cayman Islands. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the Luxembourg GAAR under LIR Article 6, ATAD interest limitation rules, the DAC6 mandatory disclosure regime and the OECD principal-purpose test. An SCSp developing a mobile utility app must show real substance in Luxembourg: local offices, employees, active management decisions, a Luxembourg bank account, local accounting and IP ownership aligned with functions, assets and risks. Absent substance, the ACD may recharacterise the arrangement as artificial, deny tax treaty benefits and impose taxes with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / wilful misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Tax increase up to 20% under Article 169 LIR, late-payment interest of 0.6% per month, fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of one month to five years under Luxembourg criminal tax law.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly liable for restitution of evaded taxes and may face travel bans, asset freezes, and in criminal cases imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced where the SCSp was used as a fraud facade.
Artificial shell arrangement / GAAR

Statutory sanction: The ACD recharacterises the structure under LIR Article 6 and assesses tax at the real owner at the combined 24.94% rate, plus 10% statutory annual interest and a concealment surcharge of up to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused or benefited from the artificial structure may be personally liable for the additional taxes assessed against the company, with civil restitution ordered by the Luxembourg administrative courts.
AML / beneficial ownership registration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 5 million for legal persons, suspension of commercial activities for up to 10 years, public naming of sanctions and FIU penalties; suspicious transaction reports are mandatory.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors may be imprisoned for six months to five years, receive travel bans and personal fines up to EUR 250,000; the corporate veil is pierced in AML offences where the SCSp was the instrumentality of money laundering.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,000; each share must be paid up to at least 25% in cash, and the corresponding funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or with the Caisse de Consignation until the company is registered. No public offering of shares is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the local commune fire brigade (corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours) required for all occupied premises; structural load inspection and electrical installation certificate required for retrofitted offices; workplace risk assessment, emergency evacuation plan, and occupational health clearance under the Labor Code, supervised by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM).

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,000; each share must be paid up to at least 25% in cash, and the corresponding funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or with the Caisse de Consignation until the company is registered. No public offering of shares is permitted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct tax: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), Luxembourg. VAT: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT). Principal e-portals: MyGuichet.lu (single administrative gateway), ACD e-file for corporate income tax returns, and AEDT e-cabinet for VAT returns and registrations.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg-resident SARL (including 100% foreign-owned): (1) execute notarial deed and file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), obtaining the RCS number and central corporate matricule, which serves as the national TIN. (2) Notify ACD of the effective business start to open the corporate income tax file and municipal business tax account. (3) Submit VAT registration (Formulaire 640) to AEDT via MyGuichet to obtain a LU VAT identification number; a non-EU entity not established in the EU must appoint an approved Luxembourg fiscal representative for standard VAT registration. (4) File beneficial ownership details in the RBE register. The same matricule is used across CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Luxembourg-resident SARL (including 100% foreign-owned): (1) execute notarial deed and file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), obtaining the RCS number and central corporate matricule, which serves as the national TIN. (2) Notify ACD of the effective business start to open the corporate income tax file and municipal business tax account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through classification is available; an SARL is conclusively a resident capital company taxed at entity level. Available elections and mechanisms: fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for Luxembourg-resident companies directly or indirectly held at least 95%; VAT grouping election (assujetti unique); participation exemption election on share disposals (holding ≥10% or acquisition price ≥EUR 6,000,000, with 12-month holding requirement); dividend participation exemption (50% deduction) under section 115 LIR; election to treat a Luxembourg permanent establishment under treaty non-enterprise rules; ATAD interest-limitation and hybrid-mismatch notifications; and, for qualifying B2C digital services, the non-Union OSS election in lieu of domestic VAT registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for non-resident businesses; any taxable supply with a Luxembourg place of supply triggers mandatory registration. Domestic SME franchise exemption applies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000, subject to Article 57 conditions and loss of input VAT deduction. For businesses using the Union OSS/non-Union OSS for distance sales or B2C electronic services, local registration may be avoided below the EUR 10,000 EU-wide cross-border threshold. For this B2B data-labeling/RLHF BPO, the decisive test is the customer's place of establishment under the general B2B place-of-supply rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exports: 0% VAT is available if the goods leave the EU, supported by a valid customs export declaration (SAD/ECS), transport documents, and timely proof of export; intra-EU dispatches are zero-rated subject to the customer's valid EU VAT ID. For services such as AI data labeling and RLHF curation: where the customer is a business established outside Luxembourg, the place of supply is outside Luxembourg under Article 17 of the VAT law, so no Luxembourg VAT is chargeable; where the customer is an EU business, the intra-EU B2B reverse-charge rule applies; B2C remote services to non-EU consumers are outside scope. Input VAT on export-related costs is deductible. Documentation must evidence the customer's business status and establishment to prevent reclassification as a taxable domestic supply.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/ECS electronic customs system) for physical goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport evidence: CMR waybill, bill of lading, or airway bill showing departure from Luxembourg and arrival outside the EU
  • EU exit confirmation or customs-endorsed proof of export for zero-rating purposes
  • Customer contract or purchase order evidencing B2B service and place of establishment (for services)
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT confirmation, or bank statement evidencing payment and FX repatriation

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping for B2B invoices. B2G e-invoicing to central public bodies is mandatory in PEPPOL format since 18 April 2022. Taxpayers may issue electronic invoices freely, but authenticity, integrity, and legibility must be guaranteed and invoices archived for 10 years. AEDT applies risk-based ex-post audits; no transaction-by-transaction API clearance is required.

AEDT may retroactively reassess improper zero-rating and export exemptions: ordinary limitation periods apply (commonly five taxation years), extended to ten years in cases of fraud. If zero-rating is not substantiated, VAT at 16% becomes due with late-payment interest of 0.6% per month, penalty surcharges up to 100% of the tax in cases of bad faith, and criminal sanctions for forged export or remittance documentation. False export claims also invalidate input VAT deduction and may exclude the taxpayer from OSS and franchise regimes. Luxembourg law does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day convention should be maintained in the export file to corroborate commercial reality and support transfer-pricing positions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a founding Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible and there are no exchange-control or prior-authorization statutes. Both current-account flows (intercompany service fees, royalties, dividends) and capital-account flows (equity injection, loan disbursement, asset transfers) are unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU and the Luxembourg Monetary Law of 28 May 2004. Profit remittance faces no FX hurdle; dividends paid by an LU SARL to non-resident shareholders are subject to the domestic 15% Luxembourg withholding tax unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders or lowered under an applicable double-tax treaty. Practical friction is limited to CSSF AML/KYC documentary verification of wire beneficiaries, the bank-side source-of-funds checks, and BCL balance-of-payments statistical notification executed automatically by the resident bank. No convertibility permit is required for any currency operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation and no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exist under Luxembourg or EU law. The SARL may retain all export receipts from AI data-labeling and BPO services in EUR or in any freely convertible currency (USD, GBP, INR, PHP) for an indefinite period without conversion to EUR. The only compliance duties are accurate financial-statement disclosure of trade receivables and automatic BCL balance-of-payments reporting by the resident bank for each cross-border receipt above the de-minimis reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 per transaction or EUR 125,000 per calendar year. Mandated conversion timeline: none; mandatory conversion ratio: 0%.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with financial-sector and AML/CFT supervision vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banks, electronic money institutions, and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High (conditional). Luxembourg recognizes EU/EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorized under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and passported into the Grand Duchy, enabling fintech providers such as Wise (passported from the Netherlands/Belgium) and PayPal (PayPal Europe) to serve as secondary disbursement rails for mass micro-payouts (PayPal Mass Pay, Wise Business) and low-value operating expenses. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot hold the SARL statutory capital (minimum EUR 12,000) nor act as the designated settlement account for Luxembourg corporate tax and social-security payments; (ii) the EMI must maintain safeguarding arrangements and comply with CSSF AML/CFT rules applicable to payment services; (iii) funds held with an EMI are e-money, not bank deposits, and are outside the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme; (iv) the treasury must fund the EMI from a concentration account held at a Tier-1 credit institution to mitigate commercial-failure risk. The optimal structure is a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as principal treasury hub, with Wise and PayPal as overlay mass-payout rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity-injection protocol: (1) wire via SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 pain.001 into the beneficiary IBAN at the Luxembourg Tier-1 bank, with beneficiary name matching exactly the RCS-registered SARL name; (4) provide the bank with the notarized subscription statement and board resolution approving the capital increase; (5) for wires from non-EU jurisdictions, submit source-of-funds documentation and UBO identification; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High (conditional). Luxembourg recognizes EU/EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorized under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and passported into the Grand Duchy, enabling fintech providers such as Wise (passported from the Netherlands/Belgium) and PayPal (PayPal Europe) to serve as secondary disbursement rails for mass micro-payouts (PayPal Mass Pay, Wise Business) and low-value operating expenses. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot hold the SARL statutory capital (minimum EUR 12,000) nor act as the designated settlement account for Luxembourg corporate tax and social-security payments; (ii) the EMI must maintain safeguarding arrangements and comply with CSSF AML/CFT rules applicable to payment services; (iii) funds held with an EMI are e-money, not bank deposits, and are outside the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme; (iv) the treasury must fund the EMI from a concentration account held at a Tier-1 credit institution to mitigate commercial-failure risk. The optimal structure is a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as principal treasury hub, with Wise and PayPal as overlay mass-payout rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if entity revenue > EUR 1 million and group consolidated revenue > EUR 50 million; Master File required if group consolidated revenue > EUR 50 million

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies general substance requirements (real staff, premises, decision-making) to avoid shell company status; no specific ESR for BPO, but must demonstrate operational substance in Luxembourg

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 200% of evaded tax; criminal prosecution; imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of income; penalty up to 10% of underpaid tax; potential liquidation

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly operated a shell entity; travel bans and director disqualification
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freezes
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty up to 10% of underpaid tax; interest on arrears

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil restitution and travel bans

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified e-government portal: MyGuichet (myguichet.lu), guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may obtain a Luxembourg business number (matricule) and national tax ID by filing its partnership deed with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through a Luxembourg registered office agent, followed by VAT registration with the AEDT on Form 700 via the MyGuichet platform. If the entity has no Luxembourg establishment but makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg, it must apply directly for a VAT identification number to the AEDT VAT department, using Form 700 and appointing a Luxembourg tax representative where required. Once registered, the entity receives a matricule and a VAT number (LU plus eight digits), which are used for filing VAT returns and recapitulative statements.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity may obtain a Luxembourg business number (matricule) and national tax ID by filing its partnership deed with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through a Luxembourg registered office agent, followed by VAT registration with the AEDT on Form 700 via the MyGuichet platform. If the entity has no Luxembourg establishment but makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg, it must apply directly for a VAT identification number to the AEDT VAT department, using Form 700 and appointing a Luxembourg tax representative where required. Once registered, the entity receives a matricule and a VAT number (LU plus eight digits), which are used for filing VAT returns and recapitulative statements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent under Luxembourg law; no partnership-level corporate income tax classification election is available. Partners may elect, within their own jurisdictions, to apply look-through or check-the-box treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD 1 and 2, transposed in 2019-2020) restrict outcomes where a hybrid entity obtains disproportionate deductions or non-inclusion. VAT grouping election under Article 60 LVD allows a fiscal unity for VAT purposes if all legal conditions are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excluding real estate); no registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg; OSS distance-selling threshold EUR 35,000 for non-established EU suppliers; B2B reverse charge applies where the customer is a Luxembourg VAT-registered business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 42 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (LVD), provided the goods are physically removed from the EU within the legal deadline (generally three months, extendable to six months) and documentary evidence of export is retained. Supply, repair, chartering or modification of ocean-going vessels used in international seafaring may be exempt or zero-rated under Article 43 LVD. For shipyard export of new container vessels, the supplier must be in possession of the export declaration, transport documents, and proof of payment to maintain zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic AES export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or sea waybill / through bill of lading, evidencing carriage to a destination outside the EU)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/MT103 proof of foreign payment
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export delivery note
  • Certificate of non-EU destination / Form A or EUR.1 where preferential origin is claimed (as applicable)

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime currently applies in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is permitted; Peppol BIS 3.0 is mandatory for invoices to Luxembourg central public authorities. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via the MyGuichet portal (AEDT). Continuous transaction controls are planned but not yet in force.

The AEDT may audit zero-rated export transactions and will disallow the zero rate if the documentary evidence is incomplete, false or not presented within the statutory period. Consequences include immediate assessment of the unpaid VAT at the standard rate, statutory default interest (currently 5% per annum), and administrative fines under Article 87 LVD of up to 100% of the evaded VAT in cases of intentional concealment. Criminal penalties under Article 90 LVD may be imposed for fraud, including imprisonment. Retrospective clawbacks are permitted for up to five years, or ten years in cases of fraud, under Luxembourg tax procedure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital-account and current-account convertibility are fully liberalised: Luxembourg, as a euro-area member, has eliminated all exchange-control restrictions for residents and non-residents under Articles 63–65 TFEU and the Luxembourg implementing legislation. Profit remittance and distribution hurdles are de minimis; an SCSp generally may remit partner distributions, loan repayments, and sale proceeds without prior central-bank approval, subject only to standard CSSF anti-money-laundering verification, ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting, and tax-compliance filings confirming the pass-through character of the partnership. There is no separate capital-export licensing regime for shipbuilding project finance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Eurozone law imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation requirement for export proceeds. Shipbuilding export receivables held by the SCSp can be retained in EUR, USD, or any third currency in Luxembourg and offshore correspondent accounts, subject to purely informational reporting: EU Regulation 2015/847 funds-transfer data rules (threshold EUR 1,000), ECB/BCL balance-of-payments declarations for transactions above EUR 12,500, and standard CSSF suspicious-transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank and operates within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism alongside the European Central Bank (ECB). Foreign-exchange and cross-border capital-movement oversight is shared between the BCL, the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance, and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and exchange-related licensing under the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM only as an ancillary payment channel for small disbursements. A Luxembourg-authorised or EU/EEA-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2 cannot perform the core treasury functions of this mandate: it may not take deposits as a credit institution, may not issue commercial letters of credit, may not enter into ISDA-documented FX derivatives, and is structurally unable to host maritime installment escrow arrangements. Offshore digital EMIs located outside the EEA face a further legal constraint: unlicensed non-EEA payment providers cannot lawfully solicit Luxembourg entities, and large correspondent banks will reject all nostro traffic from such intermediaries. Capital escrow, LC issuance, and FX hedging must therefore remain exclusively with CSSF-licensed credit institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners inject equity via SWIFT MT103 wires denominated in EUR or USD. The instructing bank must reference the ECB Balance-of-Payments functional category F.61 (Direct investment – equity) or F.62 (Reinvestment of earnings), and the receiving Luxembourg bank requires the SCSp subscription agreement, the capital-call notice number, and a purpose-code description such as 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – LU SCSp / SHIPYARD EQUITY'. CSSF AML rules require identification of beneficial owners for every transfer above EUR 10,000; where the remitter is a corporate in a high-risk jurisdiction, the defence package must include proof of funds: audited financial statements, source-of-wealth statement, and transaction rationale. No mandatory conversion applies; the Luxembourg account may be credited in original currency and the SCSp may open segregated USD sub-accounts for subsequent shipbuilding disbursements.Shipyard milestone payments and trade settles through four regulated rails: (i) standard operational disbursements via SWIFT MT103/MT202 through BGL BNP Paribas and BIL correspondent chains; (ii) milestone payments under a Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit issued per UCP 600 with a confirmed Luxembourg advising bank; (iii) standby letters of credit (ISP98) and advance-payment/refund guarantees issued under URDG 758 in favour of shipowners and equipment vendors; and (iv) documentary collections under URC 522 for progress invoices. All EUR payments may alternatively clear through SEPA; USD payments clear via Fedwire/CHIPS through the banks' New York correspondents. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City combined rate: 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and 6.75% municipal business tax; SCSp itself is fiscally transparent so no entity-level tax and allocations are taxable in the partners' hands)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard statutory rate for Luxembourg-source dividends paid to non-residents; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive/participation exemption when the 10% participation and 12-month holding conditions are satisfied; SCSp profit distributions to limited partners are generally not treaty-protected because the SCSp is tax transparent and no withholding is levied at partnership level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is mandatory when Luxembourg consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Local File is mandatory when the Luxembourg entity has cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 and total turnover above EUR 10,000,000; lower thresholds apply if the group operates in highly digitalised or intangibles-intensive sectors.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply the Gulf-Cooperation Council style ESR, but the general anti-abuse rule, the OECD key-substance requirement under DTA/BEPS, and the EU ATAD require the SCSp to maintain genuine business purposes: actual employees, meaningful board/partner decision-making in Luxembourg, and operational risk management for the shipyard's vessel construction, procurement, and financing activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Declaration of SCSp Income

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment at 24.94% with late-payment interest at 0.8% per month; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for evasion of Luxembourg corporate income tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the general partner who sign or approve false allocations are jointly and severally liable for evaded corporate tax, interest, and penalties; corporate veil is pierced and courts may impose asset freezes, travel bans, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp's transparent tax status, retroactive assessment at 24.94%, denial of participation exemption and DTA treaty benefits, plus fines under the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule.

Director personal liability: The general partner and ultimate beneficial owners are jointly assessed on the reclassified profit; where the unpaid tax balance exceeds EUR 250,000, courts routinely issue travel bans and freezing orders against directors.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory penalty up to EUR 500,000 and confiscation of the proceeds of the ship sale; if the matter is prosecuted as financing of serious crime, the criminal fine can reach EUR 1,250,000 with imprisonment for the responsible manager.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to file the RBE or who authorise unvetted escrow brownouts may be held civilly liable for loss and suffer disqualification, travel restrictions, and imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Fabrication

Statutory sanction: Correction of taxable income under Luxembourg law (Articles 56/164 LIR), additional assessment at 24.94%, late-payment interest, and a penalty of up to 10% of the adjusted amount; double taxation is not relieved if documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer pricing declarations or creating fictitious invoices are personally liable for restitution of the tax shortfall and may be prosecuted for tax fraud, with criminal fines and jail terms.
Unlawful Distribution / Stripping of Capital

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax at 15% assessed on the distribution, invalidation of the distribution vis-à-vis creditors, and personal liability of the general partner to restore funds under Luxembourg partnership law; fines can reach EUR 75,000.

Director personal liability: General partner managers are personally liable to third-party creditors for unlawful distributions and face civil restitution orders, provisional attachment of personal assets, and restriction from managing commercial companies.

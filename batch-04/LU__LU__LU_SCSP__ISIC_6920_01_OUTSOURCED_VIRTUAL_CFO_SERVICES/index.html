Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely fixes the capital (cash or in-kind). Subscribed capital must be effectively made available to the partnership treasury and evidenced by a bank attestation for the RCS file; no escrow or blocked account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification per the Grand-Ducal Regulation on workplace fire safety (mandatory evacuation plan, fire extinguishers, and building operator certificate); structural safety inspection evidenced by the leaseholder's building occupancy certificate; occupational health and workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering lighting, ergonomics, and electrical safety; mandatory employer registration with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) for workplace accident coverage.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely fixes the capital (cash or in-kind). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for statutory registration; official digital gateway: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu) and the AEDT electronic filing portal (eCDF).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Instruct a Luxembourg resident certified public accountant (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) to draft and file the SCSp constitutive act; Step 2: Register the SCSp in the RCS in Luxembourg and obtain the national identification number (matricule), which constitutes the statutory TIN for the partnership; Step 3: File VAT registration Form 46/V with the AEDT via the eCDF/MyGuichet portal to obtain a VAT number in the format LU-8 digits (no per-partner VAT auto-registration is required as the SCSp is fiscally transparent); Step 4: Ensure each partner notifies the ACD of its foreign TIN/residence so that profits are apportioned and reported by the partners individually, not by the SCSp; Step 5: Maintain a Luxembourg registered office, a local authorized representative (personne de contact), accounting ledgers in EUR, and the partnership's bank/payment-rail settlement records (Stripe_Billing and Wise) for substance documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended by the Law of 12 July 2013) and Article 175 LIR — profits, losses, and income are allocated directly to the partners in accordance with the partnership agreement; the SCSp itself is not an income-tax taxpayer, and assets/liabilities are attributed to the partners for Luxembourg tax purposes.

Available election forms

There is no US-style 'check-the-box' election under Luxembourg law; the SCSp's transparency is mandatory. Available statutory options include: (i) an application under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) with affiliated Luxembourg entities; (ii) designation of a managing general partner as the sole accountable person for wage-tax and withholding declarations; (iii) partner-level elections under Article 161 LIR aligning the partners' fiscal year with the SCSp's financial year; and (iv) disclosure/election mechanisms under Article 168quater LIR (ATAD hybrid mismatch rules) to neutralise any conflicting foreign classifications of the SCSp. Non-resident partners may further file a US check-the-box election (Form 8832) if they are US taxpayers, but such election has no effect on the Luxembourg transparency treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 statutory threshold for non-established suppliers: any foreign entity or partnership making taxable supplies in Luxembourg (B2B services subject to Luxembourg VAT or B2C supplies physically performed in Luxembourg) must register immediately. Luxembourg-established businesses may benefit from the small-business franchise exemption where their annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000, provided the supplies are domestic B2C services/goods; the franchise does not apply to intra-EU acquisitions, distance sales, or services supplied to VAT-identified EU customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% with full input VAT deduction applies to: (i) physical export of goods outside the EU if the goods leave the EU within prescribed time limits and the supplier holds a customs export declaration; (ii) intra-EU B2B supplies of the virtual CFO services, for which Luxembourg VAT is not chargeable and the customer's Member State applies the reverse charge, provided the customer communicates a valid EU VAT identification number; and (iii) services supplied to customers established outside the EU (territorial outside of scope, not technically zero-rated) where the place of supply is the customer's country. For the SCSp, the standard service export will be outside scope for non-EU customers and reverse-charged for EU B2B customers; documentary status-evidence is mandatory to validate the customer's identification, including the financial institution's (Stripe/Wise) settlement records.

  • Luxembourg customs export declaration (SAD) and transport evidence (Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR) — applicable only if physical goods are incidentally exported by the SCSp; not required for pure virtual CFO services
  • Signed engagement letter / service contract stating the customer's country of establishment and the cross-border (offshore) nature of the service
  • Customer's valid EU VAT identification number (verified via VIES) or, for non-EU customers, an official foreign tax identification number / Chamber of Commerce extract
  • Stripe_Billing and Wise transaction reports, together with BIC/SWIFT inward remittance certificates evidencing payment from the non-Luxembourg customer
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) filed electronically within the applicable calendar month where intra-EU B2B services are supplied

As of the current filing year, no real-time B2B fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate applies in Luxembourg for services such as virtual CFO advising. B2G invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector entities must be submitted electronically in the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format. All VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically via the AEDT's eCDF/MyGuichet interface (monthly, quarterly, or per transaction depending on turnover), and the ACD may require SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) electronic accounting data on request or for large taxpayers.

If zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is improperly claimed, the AEDT assesses Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the gross consideration together with moratorium interest at 0.6% per month of delay; deliberate non-disclosure attracts an administrative penalty of 10-20% of the tax evaded and an additional fine of EUR 250-10,000 against the responsible general partner. Fraudulent documentary claims (false customs declarations or fictitious invoices) expose the SCSp and its general partner to criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years, with the normal five-year assessment period extended to ten years. Retroactive clawbacks of repaid input VAT and denial of deduction rights apply where the export evidence is defective, and uncollected foreign receivables remaining beyond the 180-day benchmark without commercial justification may be recharacterised as Luxembourg-source income under CFC/PE substance rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; Luxembourg operates no exchange-control or capital-authorisation regime for either current-account or capital-account flows. The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender; service fees, partner capital contributions, and profit allocations may be freely converted into any internationally traded currency. Profit-remittance hurdles are absent at entity level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and distributions carry no Luxembourg withholding tax; the only practical gate is the banking counterparty's documentary due diligence covering source of funds and partner identification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export or service receipts. Invoices issued by the SCSp may be collected in any freely convertible currency and retained or converted at the operator's discretion; only CRF AML reporting thresholds apply to physical-cash movements and unusual transactions under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with financial-sector supervision exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and anti-money-laundering intelligence by the Cellule de Renseignement Financier (CRF); monetary policy is delegated to the European Central Bank within the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High for operational cash-flow accounts only, with strict legal constraints. The SCSp may onboard with EEA-authorised electronic money institutions - Wise Europe / Wise Payments Europe S.A. (authorised in Belgium and Ireland, passporting into Luxembourg) and Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland) - under Directive 2009/110/EC. Such accounts are e-money wallets, not credit-institution deposits; they are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme and may not hold third-party escrow, partner capital escrow, or legal-charge cash. Consequently, Stripe Billing payouts and Wise disbursements are restricted to operational turnover; all partner capital, reserved liquidity, and client-fee escrow funds must be held with a CSSF-regulated Tier-1 credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner contributions are remitted by SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR, with Field 70 stating 'Apport en capital - SCSp contribution per LPA [date]' and referencing the RCS registration number, plus ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC (capital payment) or INTC (intra-company) where applicable. The receiving bank requires the signed limited partnership agreement, the extrait du RCS, partner resolutions authorising the injection, and a UBO certificate. No BCL, CSSF, or CRF prior approval is required; EUR contributions are not monitored, and non-EUR funds are converted to EUR at spot with ECB reference-rate pricing. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High for operational cash-flow accounts only, with strict legal constraints. The SCSp may onboard with EEA-authorised electronic money institutions - Wise Europe / Wise Payments Europe S.A. (authorised in Belgium and Ireland, passporting into Luxembourg) and Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland) - under Directive 2009/110/EC. Such accounts are e-money wallets, not credit-institution deposits; they are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme and may not hold third-party escrow, partner capital escrow, or legal-charge cash. Consequently, Stripe Billing payouts and Wise disbursements are restricted to operational turnover; all partner capital, reserved liquidity, and client-fee escrow funds must be held with a CSSF-regulated Tier-1 credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

SCSp is fiscally transparent and not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax at entity level; resident corporate partners are taxed at combined headline rate of 24.94% (CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 7% of CIT + municipal business tax up to 6.75% for Luxembourg City).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at SCSp level for distributions to partners because the vehicle is fiscally transparent; if treated as a Luxembourg resident company due to disguised equity characterization, the statutory rate would be 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Partner-level returns follow the partner's own taxable year; where a Luxembourg tax return is filed, the statutory deadline is generally 30 June following the year of the fiscal year-end, with automatic extension to 31 December if filed electronically, subject to extension requests.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File in Luxembourg is due when transactions with related parties exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year (goods) or EUR 500,000 (services); Master File is required when a worldwide group has consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The SCSp must maintain genuine economic substance in Luxembourg where it performs 'virtual CFO' services, including real management, employees, business premises, and records; although SCSp is not directly within Luxembourg's ESR box of the AIFM/UCITS regime, the tax administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine and OECD BEPS standards to deny treaty benefits to shell or letterbox entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income plus late interest at 0.6% monthly; administrative penalty up to 10% of evaded tax; criminal penalty up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for wilful fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners may be compulsorily wound up, held jointly and severally liable for taxes when assets are stripped, subject to travel bans and, upon criminal conviction, imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company Abuse / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg residence certificate; disqualification from EU directives and tax treaty relief; retroactive penalty tax of 50% on disallowed deductions.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for restitution of all diverted taxes, including personal seizure of bank accounts; professional ban and travel ban may be imposed.
AML / Bookkeeping Integrity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 500,000; license revocation by the Luxembourg regulatory authority; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 250,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unauthorized disclosure and AML breaches; they face civil damages, restitution orders, and incarceration in serious cases.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed monthly penalty up to EUR 1,000 per month per missing documentation; tax adjustment with markup or recharacterization of the transaction; late-interest surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the corporate tax deficiency if they knowingly or negligently approved non-compliant intercompany policies, leading to personal asset seizure and travel restrictions.

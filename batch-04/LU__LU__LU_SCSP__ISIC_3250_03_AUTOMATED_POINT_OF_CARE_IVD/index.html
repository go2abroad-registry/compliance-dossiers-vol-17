Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Trade and Companies Register, 13 rue Erasme, L-1468 Luxembourg; electronic filing via the e-RCS portal under the trusted third-party (TTP) accreditation mechanism.

Governing statute

Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law on Commercial Companies of 10 August 1915), as amended by the Law of 12 July 2013 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (Articles 101-108); Loi du 19 décembre 2002 sur le registre de commerce et des sociétés; Règlement grand-ducal of 23 December 2016 on electronic filing and RESA publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution (Declaration of Constitution) filed electronically with the RCS, accompanied by the signed SCSp partnership agreement (contrat de société) and followed by mandatory publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first register in the Luxembourg trade and companies register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) through the Luxembourg Business Registers portal, obtain the national identification number (matricule), and then apply for VAT identification via MyGuichet.lu / AEDT. The SCSp is registered by its general partner and is issued a Luxembourg national number and, where applicable, a VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. An SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent; it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax or net wealth tax, and its income is allocated to partners for taxation in their respective jurisdictions. Optional mechanisms include VAT grouping (assujetti unique), the simplified partnership taxation regime for direct tax, and disclosure/elections required under hybrid mismatch rules where the partnership is treated as transparent or opaque under foreign law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business exemption; non-established foreign taxable persons have no general registration threshold and must register from their first taxable supply unless reverse-charge, OSS, or IOSS applies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods supplied by the Luxembourg IVD manufacturer and physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier retains satisfactory evidence of export. Qualifying services include directly connected transport, handling, and offshore processing services provided to non-EU customers. The customer must be a taxable person located in a third country where the transfer of ownership or effective use occurs outside the EU.

  • Validated EU customs export declaration (SAD / Export Accompanying Document)
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note signed by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof evidencing receipt of payment from the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

Luxembourg does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a national continuous transaction control system. Electronic invoicing is permitted and PEPPOL B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for public procurement. Cross-border sellers of digital services may use the EU OSS/IOSS without local VAT registration; no real-time API clearance is currently enforced.

Luxembourg tax and customs authorities apply risk-based export verification and may require complete documentation at audit. If zero-rating is not objectively substantiated, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Luxembourg at 17% VAT, with late-payment interest accruing and administrative fines. Where fraudulent documentary claims are established, criminal prosecution may follow, allowing retrospective clawback of tax, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro-area regime: full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account operations with no exchange-control approval requirement; Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital. The Luxembourg SCSp may freely open multi-currency accounts, retain USD/GBP/CHF/CNY proceeds, remit dividends and redeem partnership interests without BCL permission. The only operational constraint is the BCL Balance of Payments (BoP) statistical declaration duty under ECB Regulation (EU) No 1841/2018: every cross-border transaction at or above EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to BCL by the client through the resident credit institution. Distributions from a fiscally transparent SCSp are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, but confirmatory legal certainty should be obtained via an advance tax ruling (tax clearance) from the Administration des contributions directes (ACD).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory conversion, no repatriation window, and no surrender obligation on export receivables. The IVD manufacturer may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at any CSSF-licensed institution, use them directly for offshore trade settlements, FX hedging, or treasury allocations, without central bank approval. The sole residual duty is statistical: each cross-border transaction equal to or above EUR 12,500 must be reported to BCL via the banking counterparty; EU and UN sanctions, dual-use and medical-device export controls apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating as the national central bank of Luxembourg within the Eurosystem under the monetary policy and foreign-reserve authority of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions rests with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for operational treasury and global supplier disbursements; Low for statutory capital escrow. Luxembourg recognises Payment Institutions and E-Money Institutions under PSD2 (Law of 28 July 2020, amended, CSSF Regulation 12-02), including CSSF-licensed EMIs such as MultiPass (Luxembourg) and passported players such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, and Wise Platform; these EMIs can deliver virtual IBANs in EUR/GBP/USD, SWIFT MT103 equivalent transfers, and competitive mid-market FX. Legal constraints: (i) e-money balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (Fonds de garantie des dépôts) and must be safeguarded in segregated accounts at a credit institution; (ii) EMIs do not hold a banking licence under the Law of 5 April 1993 and therefore cannot operate a true escrow or hold client money in a protected capital-escrow structure; (iii) full UBO/KYC documentation of the SCSp still applies. Recommended architecture: keep capital and investor funds with a tier-1 bank and use the EMI as a settlement overlay for fast micro-supplier payments, corporate card programmes, and emerging-market currency routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All investor subscriptions above EUR 12,500 must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 (SEPA pacs.008 for euro contributions) with Field 70 (Remittance Information) containing 'SCSP [name] - CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - LPA CLAUSE [X]', the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference), the remitter name exactly matching the UBO register, and the applicable ECB BoP purpose code (e.g., 'DK' for direct-investment equity capital or 'V' for participation capital) as instructed by the receiving bank. The receiving bank verifies the remitter against the RBE/UBO files, reconciles the amount against the capital-commitment schedule of the LPA, confirms the absence of third-party interposition, and reports the transaction to BCL under its BoP reporting obligation. For amounts below EUR 12,500, the bank must still report aggregated flows. Recommended architecture: keep capital and investor funds with a tier-1 bank and use the EMI as a settlement overlay for fast micro-supplier payments, corporate card programmes, and emerging-market currency routing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate for a Luxembourg City taxable corporate (17% CIT + 1.19% unemployment solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); the SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and is not subject to corporate income tax at entity level

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard statutory rate; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and 0% under the domestic participation exemption where the corporate partner holds at least 10% (or EUR 1.2m) for 12 months; for a transparent SCSp, distributions of profits to partners are not subject to dividend withholding tax at the partnership level because the profits retain their character and source in the partners' hands

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory and must be filed annually with the tax return where the group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50 million; Master File is required where the group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750 million; Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (Law of 28 December 2018 and Grand-Ducal Regulation of 6 December 2019). An SCSp with IVD manufacturing and intercompany supply must also maintain contemporaneous benchmarking documentation below the thresholds if it has related-party transactions

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, Articles 6 and 6bis of the Tax Adaptation Law (GAAR), ATAD rules, and the OECD principal purpose test demand genuine economic substance: real employees, physical premises in Luxembourg, local decision-making, and risk management commensurate with the IVD manufacturing functions. The SCSp must demonstrate commercial rationale for its existence; passive cash-box or conduit arrangements without substance risk reclassification, denial of treaty benefits, and full 24.94% re-taxation with penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Art. 396-399 General Tax Law); 10% omission surcharge plus 0.9% monthly default interest; civil re-assessment at the full 24.94% combined rate with compound penalties for gross negligence

Director personal liability: Directors, managers (gérants), and authorised signatories face joint and several civil liability for misstated returns; personal assets may be seized; travel bans and preventive detention may be ordered where departure would jeopardise collection; criminal conviction leads to disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies
Shell Company / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reclassification under Article 6 Tax Adaptation Law and denial of treaty benefits; retroactive taxation at 24.94% aggregate rate; penalty interest of 0.9% monthly; application of EU ATAD III (Unshell) minimum substance indicators and mandatory automatic exchange of information with partner-state tax authorities

Director personal liability: Managers and corporate directors may be held personally liable for the full tax re-assessment where they knowingly implemented the abusive arrangement; directors face fines and up to 5 years' imprisonment for complicity in fiscal fraud; ATAD III disclosures expose directors to cross-border reputational and regulatory action
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 250,000 per missing or materially deficient document; primary tax adjustment of the Luxembourg taxable base; additional 10% 'considerable under-declaration' surcharge; default interest at 0.9% per month on the corrected tax

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements or approve transfer pricing without supporting documentation risk personal civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall; courts may issue travel bans where the director's exit could impede collection; criminal sanctions for knowingly preparing false TP documentation
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million under the Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended); criminal money-laundering penalties: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000 for simple laundering, and up to 10 years for aggravated or organised laundering; freezing of bank accounts and corporate assets; professional disqualification

Director personal liability: Directors, AML compliance officers, and the RCS-designated administrator (where applicable) are personally liable for failed supervision; travel bans and passport seizure are available preventive coercive measures; statutory managers may be disqualified from practising for up to 10 years and imprisoned; the corporate veil is pierced for gross negligence in AML control
Non-Filing of Accounts / Record-Keeping Default

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 plus daily penalties for continued default; compulsory winding-up proceedings before the commercial court; disqualification of the responsible manager; interest and surcharges on any resulting tax adjustment

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for damages caused to third parties and creditors resulting from the absence of reliable financial statements; personal conviction for filing false accounts; travel bans where non-filing masks assets; permanent prohibition from holding managerial office in Luxembourg entities

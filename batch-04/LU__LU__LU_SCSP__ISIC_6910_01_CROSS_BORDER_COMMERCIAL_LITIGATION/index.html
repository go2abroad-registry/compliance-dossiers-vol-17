Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS), operated by the Ministry of Justice via the eJustification online portal; publication official gazette: Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 101-103 as inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013 on the Special Limited Partnership (SCSp), read with the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers (Loi modifiée du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés) and the Grand-Ducal Regulation governing electronic filing and publication in the RESA.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Deed of Constitution) executed before a Luxembourg notary or by private deed, followed by mandatory registration filing with the RCS and statutory publication in the RESA under the amended Law of 19 December 2002.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital is required for an SCSp; the partnership deed freely determines capital and contribution obligations in cash or in kind. No escrow account rule applies to partnership capital. Contributions are recorded in the subscription schedule; statutory audit and filing obligations may arise only if the SCSp exceeds large-enterprise thresholds under the Law of 19 December 2002 on annual accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate and occupancy permit issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) and the municipal Commission de la sécurité after on-site inspection, verifying compliance with the 2021 Regulation on fire prevention in work premises; structural load inspection certificate from a Luxembourg-certified engineer confirming the office floor load-bearing capacity meets IBC-equivalent codes and Grand-Ducal Regulation on public building stability; and occupational health clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering means of evacuation, emergency lighting, first aid provision, and ergonomic workstation compliance

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum subscribed or paid-up capital is required for an SCSp; the partnership deed freely determines capital and contribution obligations in cash or in kind. No escrow account rule applies to partnership capital. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg imposes no general exchange controls; profit remittances, partnership distributions to non-resident partners, settlement proceeds, and liquidation distributions are unrestricted. The SCSp is fiscally transparent and Luxembourg levies no withholding tax on its distributions; residual hurdles are limited to standard KYC and source-of-funds validation, CSSF disclosure thresholds, FATCA/CRS reporting, and economic-substance verification for the active litigation business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable within Luxembourg or the euro area: there is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for foreign-currency receipts. Foreign-currency receivables (e.g., settlement awards in USD or GBP) may be held in current-account balances or converted at market rates without BCL authorization. If the paying counterparty is located in a jurisdiction with its own surrender regime, that counterparty must satisfy its local central-bank rules before wiring funds to Luxembourg.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions and payment services is exercised jointly by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). There is no separate domestic foreign-exchange administration; capital and payment liberalization derives from Articles 63–66 TFEU and Luxembourg financial sector legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for a Luxembourg-authorised or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2; Low for third-country or offshore digital EMIs lacking a Luxembourg branch or passport. Legal constraints: e-money accounts are safeguarded, not bank deposits, and therefore lack Deposit Guarantee Scheme protection; CSSF Circular 20/721 and professional-conduct rules expect lawyers' client monies and pooled IOLTA-style funds to be held in segregated client trust accounts at a credit institution rather than on an EMI float. Recommended allocation: reserve the main IOLTA and escrow clearing functions exclusively for Tier-1 credit institutions and cap EMI usage to minor operational disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner capital injections and client settlement inflows via SWIFT MT103 with a compliant purpose-code architecture: classify partner contributions under the direct-investment category of the ECB balance-of-payments reporting, and litigation fees or settlement proceeds under the legal-services service category. Reference the SCSp's Luxembourg IBAN in field 57A, include an explicit purpose memo in field 70, and upload the underlying contract or court order into the bank's documentation workflow. EUR-denominated transfers may clear via SEPA Credit Transfer; non-EUR currencies settle through correspondent banking. Credit is deferred until sanctions screening and counterparty KYC are clean, and cash received from unrepresented third parties may be rejected under client-money rules.Disbursements for counsel fees, expert witnesses, court registries, enforcement agents, and adverse-cost awards clear via: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers; (ii) SWIFT MT202 or MT202 COV for correspondent-leg cover; (iii) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for urgent intra-EUR payments; and (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit or escrow-release protocols for staged settlement payoffs against court orders. Each instruction must be signed by the general partner, pre-screened for sanctions, and supported by an invoice, court order, or settlement memorandum. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard dividend withholding tax on dividends distributed by Luxembourg corporations; 0% on distributions by the SCSp because it is fiscally transparent. Qualifying corporate partners resident in the EU/EEA or in a treaty country may obtain full exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0%) and Luxembourg's participation exemption regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by 30 June of the following year; electronic filing automatically extends the filing deadline to 31 July. The Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration (ACD) may grant further extension upon written request.

Late filing of the annual partnership return triggers a fixed administrative penalty of EUR 150 plus an additional surcharge of 10% on the assessed tax if filed after an official reminder. Late payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month (7.2% annually). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 (group consolidated revenue) for CbC Report and Master File; Luxembourg Local File is required when intra-group controlled transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per goods/services category or EUR 100,000 for financial transactions and management fees, per Article 56bis LIR and the Luxembourg TP Circular.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not enact UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies the ATAD GAAR, CFC rules, and judicial doctrines (abus de droit, substance-over-form) requiring genuine business purpose and real economic substance for treaty and EU directive benefits. Administrative circulars (notably LIR.S.234-1) require evidence of local decision-making, local employees, office premises, independent commercial risk control, and practical management for an SCSp to obtain a tax residency certificate or benefit from the participation exemption and DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at up to 100% of the tax evaded, payment of late interest at 0.6% per month, criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000, imprisonment for up to 5 years, publication of the judgment in the press and prohibition from practicing as a managing partner.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners found personally complicit may be jointly and severally liable for the SCSp's tax debt, face asset seizure, travel bans and administrative immobilisation through the Luxembourg tax collection procedure (avis à tiers détenteur), plus disqualification from administering legal entities.
Shell Company / GAAR (abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: The ACD may disregard the arrangement and re-characterise it as a taxable Luxembourg company; a 25% administrative surcharge on the under-assessed tax, late interest at 0.6% per month, and denial of treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits with retroactive effect.

Director personal liability: General partners and managers who knowingly structure a shell may incur civil liability for the re-characterised profits, be subject to personal tax assessments under the CFC/anti-abuse rules, and, where cross-border tax fraud is proven, face extradition, travel bans and penal sanctions under the Luxembourg Criminal Code.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The ACD may make a unilateral re-assessment using the arm's-length margin it determines, add late interest at 0.6% per month and impose a 25% negligence penalty (50% if bad faith); repeated non-cooperation may trigger criminal administrative sanctions of up to EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP certificates or who instruct accountants to manipulate transactional data may be held personally liable for the gross-up penalties and face criminal conviction for aiding tax evasion; the Luxembourg commercial register may impose a temporary ban against running a business.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 1,250,000 for the legal entity; imprisonment of up to 5 years for responsible individuals; corrective order to file the UBO declaration with a per-violation fine of EUR 15,000; possible freezing of the SCSp's bank accounts under the 2004 AML Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and professional partners are personally exposed to money-laundering liability under Criminal Code Articles 506-1 et seq., including confiscation of assets, travel bans, prohibition from professional practice, and extradition if the predicate offence occurred abroad; the Luxembourg Financial Sector Supervisory Commission may publish a formal sanction against the individual.
Procedural Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed EUR 150 late-filing penalty, automatic 10% surcharge on taxes due, default interest at 0.6% per month, and estimated assessment by the ACD which is immediately collectable.

Director personal liability: Although not criminal per se, a general partner who is a director signatory of the SCSp's registration documents may be held civilly liable for the estimated tax if the partnership has insufficient assets, and a deliberate pattern of non-filing may lead to a travel ban under the Luxembourg General Tax Code enforcement measures.

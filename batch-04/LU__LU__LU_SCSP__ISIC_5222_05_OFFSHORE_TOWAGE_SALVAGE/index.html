Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD / Luxembourg Inland Revenue) — portal: eSpace (espace.impotsdirects.public.lu) and MyGuichet.lu (www.myguichet.lu). Indirect taxes (VAT, registration duties, stamp duties): Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — registration and returns via MyGuichet.lu. Customs: Administration des Douanes et Accises (IDA) via the Luxembourg Single Window for export clearance. All national registrations are coordinated with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — RCS: The SCSp must be registered with the RCS through MyGuichet.lu (Form A, creation of a special limited partnership) despite lacking separate legal personality; the RCS issues the business registration number (matricule). Step 2 — Direct tax: upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically allocates the 11-digit Luxembourg national identification/tax number (numéro d'identification); a non-resident enterprise without an RCS presence files the direct-tax registration form with the ACD via MyGuichet.lu eDesk. Step 3 — VAT: file Form 640 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' with the AEDT (online via MyGuichet.lu), obtaining a Luxembourg VAT number in format LU + 8 digits; a fiscal representative established in Luxembourg is required for non-EU persons without an establishment. Step 4 — Complete any social/statistical declarations through the Joint Social Security Centre (CCSS) as a complement to fiscal registration. Fully digitized filings via MyGuichet.lu are activated within approximately 10 business days for manual review cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — By default, the SCSp is fiscally transparent: it is not subject to corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT) or net wealth tax (NWT) at entity level; each partner is assessed directly on his distributive share of income. Foreign partners are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate, consistent with Territorial_Scope. No Tax_Exempt_Zone applies. The SCSp is not itself the VAT taxable person for its supplies; the gérant/managing partner or a designated representative supplies the towage/salvage services in a representative capacity.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. However, under Article 2 of the Law of 18 December 2015 (amending the Tax Adaptation Law, Steueranpassungsgesetz) and ACD Circular L.G. - A n° 72 of 26 January 2016, an SCSp may elect opaque status (fully subject to CIT/MBT/NWT) by including an express clause in the partnership agreement or by filing a declaration with the ACD; the election is binding for at least five consecutive fiscal years. For hybrid mismatch mitigation (ATAD implementation, Law of 21 December 2018), partners may apply domestic alignment tie-breaker elections to prevent double non-taxation where Luxembourg transparency conflicts with the partner's jurisdiction classification; such elections are permitted only within the limits of EU anti-hybrid rules and the applicable double tax treaty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-business exemption under Article 57 Loi TVA; raised from EUR 30,000 effective 1 January 2023). This threshold applies to Luxembourg-established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers engaged in Luxembourg taxable activities must register without regard to the threshold except where the reverse-charge or OSS/IOSS regimes apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports to non-EU third countries are zero-rated under Article 49 Loi TVA if physical transport outside the EU is proven (electronic customs export declaration accepted; exit evidence within 3 months). Services: (i) B2B supplies to a non-Luxembourg business customer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply = customer's establishment, Article 58 Loi TVA), effectively 0% with reverse charge in the customer's country; (ii) services to meet the direct needs of sea-going vessels used in international navigation, including tugboat towage and maritime salvage of such vessels and their cargo, are exempt with credit (zero-rated) under Article 51 Loi TVA, transposing Article 148(d) of the VAT Directive; (iii) salvage/towage performed on the high seas outside Luxembourg territorial waters generally falls under the B2B place-of-supply rule (customer location) or qualifies as a zero-rated ancillary service to international transport. To claim 0%, the taxpayer must evidence the customer's non-resident/VAT status, a written standard-form contract of towage/salvage (e.g., Lloyd's Open Form, BIMCO TOWCON/TOWLINE), the commercial invoice, payment proof and, where goods are involved, the customs export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic export entry filed with the Luxembourg IDA or the customs office of EU exit) evidencing EU departure and the exit date for zero-rating of goods
  • Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill) for the towage/salvage voyage, showing vessel, route and cargo as proof of international transport/salvage and the export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or foreign-exchange confirmation) evidencing receipt of the contractual consideration in foreign currency within the commercial credit period (standard commercial benchmark: 180 days; not a Luxembourg statutory deadline)
  • Original or certified copy of the towage/salvage contract (Lloyd's Standard Form of Salvage Agreement or Offshore Towing Agreement) specifying parties, services, day rates/lump sum, and place of performance
  • Commercial invoice marked zero-rated (0% TVA) referencing the applicable Luxembourg VAT article and identifying the non-EU customer, vessel IMO number and voyage
  • Supporting transport evidence: vessel Lloyds/IMO registration, port-call certificates, master's signed towage/salvage confirmation, or assistance/fixture notes

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API validation gateway or digital stamping applies to standard B2B supplies. Luxembourg has adopted a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate (Budget Law 2024, adopted December 2023): from 1 January 2026, every VAT-registered business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027, mandatory issuance applies to businesses with an annual turnover exceeding EUR 100,000, using the PEPPOL network (structured formats such as UBL and Factur-X). B2G e-invoicing has been obligatory via PEPPOL since 2020 (transposition of Directive 2014/55/EU). VAT records and e-invoices must be archived in their original structured electronic form for at least 10 years; the AEDT does not operate an electronic clearance/validation gateway and no fiscal stamping is required.

Luxembourg does not impose a statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export zero-rating claims; documentation of payment/repatriation within a commercial period (commonly 180 days) is nevertheless an audit expectation, and proceeds that remain offshore or uncollected may trigger transfer-pricing adjustment, deemed distributions to partners, or CFC/reporting consequences. Where the AEDT establishes that zero-rating is unjustified or fraudulent (forged export declarations or Bills of Lading, circular towage invoicing, false high-seas salvage claims, missing exit proof), the authorities claw back the unpaid VAT at the standard rate (17%) plus statutory late-payment interest (0.6% per month), impose administrative penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded in cases of bad faith, and may pursue criminal sanctions: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 250,000 for natural persons, and fines up to EUR 500,000 for legal entities, under Article 53 of the Loi TVA and the amended General Tax Law (2022 reform). The gérant/managing partner and partners may be held jointly and severally liable. In fraud cases, the limitation period extends to 10 years, permitting retrospective reopening of assessments, suspension of the VAT number, and publication of sanctions on the tax-fraud register.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-zone member with full capital and current-account convertibility under TFEU Articles 63-65. There are no exchange controls, prior authorisation requirements, or restrictions on repatriation of profits for SCSp partners whether resident or non-resident. Inward and outward payments are subject exclusively to EU sanctions regulations, EU AML/CTF rules, and CSSF/BCL reporting thresholds. Partner profit distributions from an SCSp are generally taxable at the level of each partner and, for Luxembourg-resident taxable partners, subject to standard corporate/personal income rules; no special remittance tax or conversion requirement applies. For non-resident partners, no Luxembourg withholding on liquidation proceeds is imposed if the SCSp is treated as fiscally transparent under Luxembourg law, but distributions in liquidation/redemption may be classified as income if anti-abuse rules and substance indicators are not met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Luxembourg. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency and in any EEA bank account or Luxembourg foreign-currency account. Under EU Regulation No 184/2016 (balance-of-payments statistics) and BCL rules, resident entities must report annual transactions with non-resident counterparties exceeding EUR 250,000 for services, financing, and investment flows; foreign-currency balances are reported quarterly for monetary financial institution statistics. Invoices and receipts from offshore towage and salvage services to non-EU clients should be coded under the appropriate balance-of-payments classification (services) and relevant purpose code; no surrender or conversion is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential supervision and financial-market conduct oversight shared with Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); Ministry of Finance responsible for legislative framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI viability is Low to Medium. Only EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized under PSD2 and supervised in Luxembourg or passported from another EEA state may lawfully execute payments into or out of Luxembourg. Fully offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs (e.g., operating under unregulated or partial crypto/digital licences) cannot provide clearing or settlement services for a Luxembourg SCSp without a Luxembourg banking or EMI licence. A CSSF-authorised EMI or a passported EEA EMI may be used for operational liquidity and small-value towage-mobilisation payments, but it is not a substitute for a tier-1 bank for capital injections, escrowed salvage awards, or high-value Lloyds Standard Form disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into the Luxembourg SCSp must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's tier-1 bank to the SCSp's Luxembourg euro or USD account. Luxembourg banks apply BCL balance-of-payments reporting and require the purpose code to reflect inward direct investment in partnership capital (e.g., 'CAPCONT' or EU BoP code for direct investment equity). Beneficiary reference must include the SCSp name and project ID. For salvage/towage contracts structured as escrow releases, the escrow bank must transmit funds with the escrow release instruction and a copy of the legally binding salvage agreement or Lloyds Open Form; the receiving bank will verify that the remitter is not a blocked entity and that beneficial ownership aligns with the RBE filing. Payment details should be pre-agreed via a sworn account-signing mandate and the bank's 'customer information sheet' to prevent delays.Outbound disbursements for towage and salvage operations—fuel purchases, dual-purpose transport, vessel repairs, crew wages, bar-kept salvage charges, and legal fees—are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 for individual payments and MT202 COV for bank-to-bank transfers. For high-value project settlements, Luxembourg banks offer commercial letters of credit (L/C) and documentary collections; a standby L/C is commonly issued in favour of the salvage counter-party or port/terminal operator. Domestic Luxembourg clearings use the TARGET Instant Payment Settlement (TIPS) and SEPA CT for euro operations. Simultaneous transfer facilities in USD are executed via the correspondent network of the Luxembourg bank (e.g., BGL BNP Paribas, BIL, Société Générale, or Spuerkeess), and contract denominations under the Lloyds Standard Form of Salvage Agreement (LOF) are typically USD-denominated, requiring direct USD settlement with same-day value.Outright Euro/US Dollar FX forwardsOutright Euro/Sterling FX forwardsCross-currency swaps (USD/EUR) with deliveryFX options and option combinations (collars, strangles)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted convertible currencies, executed through CSSF-regulated credit institutions and cleared under EMIR where applicable

Only EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized under PSD2 and supervised in Luxembourg or passported from another EEA state may lawfully execute payments into or out of Luxembourg. Fully offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs (e.g., operating under unregulated or partial crypto/digital licences) cannot provide clearing or settlement services for a Luxembourg SCSp without a Luxembourg banking or EMI licence. A CSSF-authorised EMI or a passported EEA EMI may be used for operational liquidity and small-value towage-mobilisation payments, but it is not a substitute for a tier-1 bank for capital injections, escrowed salvage awards, or high-value Lloyds Standard Form disbursements.

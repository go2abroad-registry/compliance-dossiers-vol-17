Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg direct tax authority; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Luxembourg VAT and indirect tax authority; unified e-portal: MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities, obtain a Luxembourg business registration number ('numéro d'immatriculation' / RCS number) by registering with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through MyGuichet.lu (or via a notary or certified agent). Upon RCS registration, the Luxembourg tax administration automatically assigns a national identification number (matricule d'identification) for tax purposes. Before commencing VAT-taxable activities in Luxembourg, foreign non-established entities must register for VAT via the VAT registration desk of the AEDT, using form No. 1000/1 (VAT registration form) submitted electronically or through a tax representative; a Luxembourg VAT ID (prefixed 'LU') is issued after the tax office validates the application. If the entity makes only B2B supplies subject to reverse charge or supplies outside Luxembourg, registration may not be required; however, an SCSp carrying on trading as a fixed base in Luxembourg will generally need to register for Luxembourg direct taxes (corporate/income tax and municipal business tax) as it will be transparent with partners taxed individually.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election; the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent and has no separate tax personality. No election to be taxed as a corporation is available for an SCSp (unless it is a commercial company under a different civil form). However, tax elections may arise under the Tax Code in relation to partnership filing: the SCSp itself is not taxable, but partners may elect to allocate Luxembourg-source income only if they hold beneficial interest. Also, hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD transposed) require an analysis when the foreign partner's home jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque; no elective elimination of a hybrid mismatch is permitted, but Luxembourg has optional application of CIT grouping rules (intégration fiscale) only for corporate entities, not partnerships. There is no direct election for the SCSp to be treated as a transparent entity in another country; tax-treaty and EU law shape recognition.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover excluding VAT) for Luxembourg-established small businesses under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; for foreign entities with no establishment in Luxembourg, there is no registration threshold – a VAT registration obligation arises as soon as they make taxable supplies in Luxembourg unless the customer self-assesses the tax under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the European Union qualify for zero-rated supply (0% VAT) provided the export is evidenced by transport documentation and the goods physically leave EU territory within statutory time limits (usually 3 months from supply). For outbound services (including algorithmic trading and financial services) supplied to a customer outside the EU: (i) if the customer is a business (taxable person), the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under the B2B rule, and no Luxembourg VAT is charged; (ii) if the customer is a non-taxable person outside the EU, the supply is generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (non-taxation), or may be exempt if it qualifies as a financial service. To claim zero-rating on exports of goods, the supplier must hold and submit proof of export to the AEDT: customs export declaration (SAD), transport documents (B/L or AWB), and commercial invoices; a written declaration or certificate from the carrier may also be required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – Electronic export declaration via Luxembourg customs/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and proof of payment (bank transfer/Inward Remittance Certificate where relevant)

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance (no e-invoicing transmission obligation, no API/digital stamping) for B2B or B2C invoices. E-invoicing may be used voluntarily if the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are ensured (VAT Directive compliance). E-invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities (B2G) is compulsory under Directive 2014/55/EU via the Luxembourg e-invoicing platform. VAT returns (ELMA) and annual recapitulative statements (Intrastat/ECS) are filed electronically on MyGuichet.lu; no continuous transaction control (CTC) or SAF-T filing is required.

The AEDT conducts targeted audits on zero-rating claims, requiring full documentary evidence within prescribed time limits. If the supplier fails to obtain export certification within 90 days (or the extended periods provided by the Grand-Ducal Regulation), the zero-rated supply is retrospectively clawed back to taxable status at the standard rate (17%); the taxpayer is liable for VAT, late interest (at the annual statutory rate of 3.6% on the VAT due) and may face penalties under Article 11 of the Luxembourg Tax Adjustment Law and Articles 512/513 of the VAT Law. Fraudulent misuse of zero-rating (false declarations, fake export documents, falsified proof of transport) is criminal tax fraud, punishable by fines up to EUR 250,000 (or 50% of the evaded tax), imprisonment (1–5 years), and publication of the judgment; civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded may be imposed. In addition, all partners of the transparent SCSp may be held jointly liable if the fraud was committed for the benefit of the partnership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg uses the euro as its sole legal tender and is fully bound by the EU Treaty provisions on the free movement of capital and payments (Articles 63 to 66 TFEU). There is a complete absence of exchange controls, prior administrative authorisations, or quantitative limits on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance by non-resident partners of a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is unrestricted; the SCSp is fiscally transparent so its trading gains flow through to partners without Luxembourg withholding tax, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting and AML/KYC scrutiny. Transfer of funds in EUR via SEPA/TARGET2 and in foreign currency via SWIFT is wholly liberalised, provided the account-holding bank satisfies EU financial sanctions (OFAC/EU asset-freeze) and CSSF transaction-monitoring obligations. No exchange-rate approval, no investment screening, and no local-financing ratio apply to proprietary algorithmic trading capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to liquidate foreign-currency receivables into EUR. The SCSp's proprietary HFT and market-making activity under ISIC 6499 / NAICS 523130 generates no physical-export documentary collections; therefore the partnership may lawfully retain, hold, and reinvest any EUR or foreign-currency balances freely. In the event of future service fees charged to non-resident counterparties, such receivables may likewise remain in the currency of choice without declaration to the BCL and without ECB balance-of-payments reporting beyond the standard statistical thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as an Eurosystem member together with the European Central Bank, with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent national regulator for banking conduct, AML/CFT enforcement, payment services licensing, and foreign-exchange transaction monitoring in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as principal treasury utility, MEDIUM as auxiliary low-value payment channel. The CSSF authorises Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions under Luxembourg laws implementing PSD2 and EMD2, and EU/EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into Luxembourg. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they may not accept deposits, cannot provide the segregated collateral, exchange-membership margin, prime-brokerage or settlement guarantees required for ultra-low-latency algorithmic market making, and their own counterparty-risk profile is generally incompatible with the SCSp's daily settlement volumes. An EMI may be used only for small operational disbursements if it operates under a full EU banking passport relationship, holds client funds safeguarded by a Tier-1 EU bank, executes wire transfers exclusively via SWIFT MT103/TARGET2, and contractually disclaims any margin or collateral custodial function. Blueprint recommendation: rely on the Tier-1 bank relationship for all material treasury flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital must reach the SCSp's Tier-1 Luxembourg account exclusively through SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the beneficiary's IBAN/BIC of the Luxembourg branch, with the purpose field (Tag 70) citing: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SCSP PARTNERSHIP SUBSCRIPTION'. For EEA-sourced EUR, a SEPA Credit Transfer with the ISO purpose code 'CAPC' or 'COMC' is acceptable; for USD-sourced capital, route via Fedwire or CHIPS to the Luxembourg bank's New York correspondent with narrative referencing the SCSp LEI, the partnership deed date, and the partner's capital-account identifier. Each injection above EUR 100,000 requires the bank's prior green-light verification of the remitter's updated KYC file; aggregate deposits above EUR 1M per calendar day trigger the CSSF statutory transaction-monitoring UBO confirmation. There is no CSSF/Bcl authorisation threshold for capital import, no economic-approval requirement, and no ECB exchange-control filing; the bank must nevertheless record the equity versus shareholder-loan character under Luxembourg thin-capitalisation and TBI (transfer-pricing) rules for its internal accounting classification.Execution follows four clearing rails: (1) TARGET2 RTGS for all EUR-denominated payments to exchanges, central counterparties and the prime broker, providing finality and immediate availability of liquidity; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-TARGET2 counterparties and for manual start-of-day / end-of-day funding balances; (3) Fedwire and CHIPS for USD margin calls and USD-cleared derivative settlements; (4) CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) for foreign-exchange legs, including the EUR/USD and EUR/GBP flows generated by hedging instruments, thereby eliminating Herstatt risk. Commercial Letters of Credit are not used in this HFT model; instead trade disbursements are structured as prime-brokerage daily margin transfers, CCP variation-margin settlements through Euroclear/ Clearstream, and netted ISDA payments, all implemented as straight-through processing segregated by sub-account between partner capital and proprietary trading capital. Every outbound message must carry the trade-purpose narrative code ('MARGIN TRANSFER', 'TRADE SETTLEMENT', 'TREASURY PLACEMENT' or 'FX HEDGE SETTLEMENT') and be retained in the CSSF-required transaction-monitoring log with a 10-year record-retention policy.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards for the liquid G10 pairs (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/JPY, EUR/AUD) with tenors up to 12 months, executed under the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions and covered by the Prime Brokerage CSA, used purely to neutralise the grid exposure accumulating from market-making in multiple instruments and currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to emerging-market currencies where physical delivery is impossible or legally impractical (USD/CNH, USD/KRW, USD/INR, USD/BRL), settled against the ECB or Reuters fixings, with ISDA documentation and CLS settlement where available.Cross-currency swaps (XCY currency interest-rate swaps) for two-leg notional and coupon exchange in EUR versus USD, GBP, or JPY, applied to matched-funding of long-term margin balances and strategic counterparty financing, cleared via LCH SwapClear or EurexOTC Clear, thereby achieving CVA-risk reduction.Exchange-traded FX futures on Eurex and CME for intra-daily delta hedging of the inventory, eligible without any CSSF authorisation because all trading is for the SCSp's own account and is classified as a treasury hedging activity under Annex I of the partnership deed.FX options (vanilla, and barrier options in limited circumstances) written by a licensed EU/EEA or Swiss bank, confined to risk-reduction strategies directly referable to the net open currency exposure arising from the algorithmic portfolio and not for speculative yield-seeking.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as principal treasury utility, MEDIUM as auxiliary low-value payment channel. The CSSF authorises Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions under Luxembourg laws implementing PSD2 and EMD2, and EU/EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into Luxembourg. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they may not accept deposits, cannot provide the segregated collateral, exchange-membership margin, prime-brokerage or settlement guarantees required for ultra-low-latency algorithmic market making, and their own counterparty-risk profile is generally incompatible with the SCSp's daily settlement volumes. An EMI may be used only for small operational disbursements if it operates under a full EU banking passport relationship, holds client funds safeguarded by a Tier-1 EU bank, executes wire transfers exclusively via SWIFT MT103/TARGET2, and contractually disclaims any margin or collateral custodial function. Blueprint recommendation: rely on the Tier-1 bank relationship for all material treasury flows.

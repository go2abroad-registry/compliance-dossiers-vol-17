Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital and no escrow/paid-up requirement for an SCSp under Luxembourg law (Law of 10 August 1915, Art. 944-1). The partnership capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement; contributions may be in cash or in kind, may be staggered, and no bank escrow or capital deposit certificate is legally required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGIIS) covering fire alarms, extinguishers, evacuation routes and sprinkler verification; structural load inspection of production halls by an approved engineering bureau per the Law of 4 December 1967; occupational safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) including workplace air quality, accident prevention, chemical agents, and periodic inspection/certification of retort autoclaves and pressure vessels under Directive 2014/68/EU and the Luxembourg Grand-Ducal Regulation on pressure equipment.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Upon registration the RCS/LBR attributes the national identification number (matricule) which functions as the statutory TIN for direct tax and annual accounts filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp lacks legal personality and is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg direct tax law: income, gains, losses and net wealth are allocated pro rata to the partners and taxed at partner level (resident individuals: personal income tax; corporate partners: corporate income tax plus municipal business tax). The SCSp is nevertheless a separate taxable person for VAT, for customs/EORI purposes, and for excise/licensing formalities relating to food manufacture.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply U.S.-style check-the-box. Available elections and characterization mechanisms: (1) Article 185 LIR option for a qualifying commercial partnership to be taxed as a capital company (irrevocable, requires joint request to the ACD; results in entity-level CIT); (2) waiver of the small-enterprise VAT exemption under Article 57 of the VAT Act so as to obtain full input VAT deduction; (3) ATAD II hybrid-mismatch alignment under Articles 168b-168c LIR, allowing the SCSp and its foreign owners to apply consistent characterization (transparent vs opaque) where the counterpart jurisdiction taxes the SCSp as opaque; (4) OSS registration for intra-EU distance sales of packaged food (Articles 63bis-63quater VAT Act); and (5) Article 185-aligned cross-border rulings via the ACD advance ruling procedure confirming transparency for treaty purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established small businesses (Article 57 VAT Act small-enterprise exemption); zero-threshold registration obligation for non-established taxable persons and for intra-Community acquisitions; EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods governed by the One Stop Shop; food manufacturing engaged for commercial purposes is ordinarily registered from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of instant rice noodle products physically leaving the European Union qualify for zero-rating with credit (exemption with right of deduction) under Article 56(1)(a) of the VAT Act provided: (i) property in the goods has transferred to a non-EU buyer; (ii) the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU within the applicable documentary timeline; and (iii) the SCSp retains the full customs/transport evidence bundle. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are exempt under Article 56(1)(c) with a valid EU VAT number. Offshore ancillary and logistics services supplied to non-EU counterparties are outside the Luxembourg VAT scope under place-of-supply rules or reverse-charged under Articles 58-60 VAT Act; freight services to third countries may be zero-rated under Article 56(1)(f).

  • Customs Export Declaration (AES/ECS declaration released via the Luxembourg customs single window) with unique MRN and exit confirmation date
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR multimodal transport document evidencing dispatch to the non-EU destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit notification from the foreign buyer evidencing cross-border payment and FX repatriation to the SCSp's Luxembourg bank account
  • Commercial invoice and packing list displaying full cross-reference to the MRN and transport document
  • Where goods are sold under EXW/FCA, a written export declaration from the buyer or authorization letter confirming the buyer's lodgement of the export customs declaration

Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing in the four-corner PEPPOL model under the Law of 27 November 2018 and implementing regulations for invoices issued to Luxembourg public contracting authorities after 1 March 2022; PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is the required format. No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no AED pre-validation applies to B2B or B2C invoices; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and governed by private agreement. Export invoices must be readily referable to the customs export MRN in the AED audit context.

The AED audits export zero-rating on the basis of documentary evidence existing at the time of assessment. Where the bundle is incomplete, inconsistent or unavailable, the supply is reclassified as a domestic Luxembourg sale and assessed at the standard VAT rate (16%) with statutory late-payment interest from the original due date. The normal assessment window is 3 years, extended to 5 years for established tax evasion; criminal fraud cases may run beyond 5 years. Administrative fines of EUR 250 to EUR 12,500 per infraction apply for non-registration, record-keeping, issuance and electron-proof deficiencies under the VAT Act. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims (falsified transport documents, sham intermediaries, premature/phoney exit declarations) expose the SCSp, its general partner and its directors to criminal fines up to EUR 25,000 and custodial penalties under the Law of 12 February 1979, joint liability for the clawback VAT, possible loss of authorized-exporter and simplified declaration statuses, and debarment from future export facilitations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Luxembourg applies complete liberalization of capital and current account movements under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no prior approval requirements for inward or outward transfers within the EU/EEA. Profit remittances face no exchange control hurdle; practical frictions arise only from bank-level AML/KYC screening, EU sanctions checks, and the mandatory filing of the Luxembourg balance-of-payments statistical return for any transfer exceeding EUR 12,500. Non-EU remittances may require underlying commercial documentation and, for SCSp pass-through distributions, a certificate of tax residence to evidence beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or repatriation requirement exists under Luxembourg or Eurosystem law. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely and converted to EUR at market rates at the receipt bank. Mandatory conversion ratios are not applicable. Only statistical reporting (balance-of-payments basis) applies to outbound/inbound transactions above EUR 12,500.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem. Banking supervision and AML/CTF enforcement are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passportable into Luxembourg under PSD2 can support basic EUR/USD euro-denominated collection and payment flows. However, most EMIs are reluctant to onboard partnership structures with multi-tier beneficial ownership, and none offer commercial letters of credit, trade finance, or physical custody. Offshore EMIs outside the EEA lack passporting rights and will be blocked by correspondent banks, making them unusable for material treasury operations.

However, most EMIs are reluctant to onboard partnership structures with multi-tier beneficial ownership, and none offer commercial letters of credit, trade finance, or physical custody. Offshore EMIs outside the EEA lack passporting rights and will be blocked by correspondent banks, making them unusable for material treasury operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline for Luxembourg City (national Corporate Income Tax 'IRC' 15% + solidarity surcharge 7% of IRC + Municipal Business Tax 'ICT' 6.75%). The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: the rate is applied at partner level only, or at entity level if the SCSp validly elects to be taxed as a corporation. Non-resident partners are taxed only on the Luxembourg-source share attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment, subject to treaty allocation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a Luxembourg corporation. For a pure transparent SCSp, profit distributions to partners are not treated as dividends and no Luxembourg withholding tax is levied; the 15% statutory rate binds only where the SCSp has elected opaque corporate taxation or where the distribution is disguised as a shareholder remuneration mechanism.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate annual value of related-party transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000; Master File mandatory where the consolidated group turnover reaches EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting notification threshold at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue; all thresholds stated in EUR (local currency of Luxembourg).

Economic substance scope: The UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) does not apply in Luxembourg. Instead, Luxembourg enforces substance through (i) the ATAD GAAR and the abuse-of-law doctrine (gestaltungsmissbrauch), (ii) place-of-effective-management residency tests, (iii) the statutory nexus requirement for the IP exemption, and (iv) the 2018/2019 substance rules for tax ruling applicants. A manufacturing SCSp operating a retort noodle plant with staff, plant, equipment, inventory and business controls inherently satisfies substance; passive holding or invoice-centric SCSp arrangements remain at risk of recharacterisation and treaty-benefit denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Fiscal Fraud (soustraction frauduleuse d'impôts)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years; administrative surcharges of 10%-20% on the evaded tax, late-payment interest at 0.6% per month, confiscation of the proceeds, and loss of all deduction and exemption claims.

Director personal liability: Directors and gérants are personally liable for the evaded tax where they ordered or tolerated the concealment; criminal conviction triggers civil restitution, removal from management, passport surrender and travel bans pending proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable profit to arm's length level, reclassification of excessive payments as non-deductible or constructive dividends, 0.6% monthly late interest, coercive fines up to EUR 1,200, and, in intentional cases, criminal tax fraud prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements knowingly below arm's length expose themselves to joint liability for the corporate tax shortfall under the extended-liability doctrine; they may be barred from managing a Luxembourg entity following a fraud conviction.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement, denial of pass-through status, imposition of the 15% dividend withholding tax on remittances, retroactive assessment against the partners, 10% abuse-of-law surcharge, and mandatory disclosure penalties under DAC6 up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: The gérant who established the sham SCSp may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate taxes and interest; criminal courts may also impose custodial sentences and travel restrictions where the sham facilitated large-scale cross-border tax evasion.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering via Swift LC / TT Wire Trade Payments

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons and imprisonment up to five years for natural persons under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004 (as amended); confiscation of the funds, freezing of the LC proceeds, and professional debarment of the compliance officer.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve suspicious LC/TT transactions without adequate KYC and sanctions screening are personally liable for AML fines and civil damages; in aggravated cases the courts impose exit bans, freeze personal assets and issue international arrest warrants.
Director Personal Liability and Veil-Piercing for Corporate Tax Debts

Statutory sanction: Action en comblement de passif: directors must make good the company's deficit where a faute grave et caractérisée caused the insolvency; bankruptcy ban prohibiting management of a Luxembourg company up to 10 years; civil fines and restitution orders.

Director personal liability: Personal bank accounts and assets of directors can be attached; the ACD may obtain a travel ban (interdiction de sortie du territoire) pending criminal proceedings; imprisonment up to five years applies for abuse of corporate assets or fraudulent misrepresentation of the SCSp's tax position.

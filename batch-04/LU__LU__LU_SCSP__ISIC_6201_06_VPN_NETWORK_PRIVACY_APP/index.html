Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, including the special limited partnership provisions introduced by the Law of 23 July 2016; applicable sections include Articles 184-1 et seq. and the general provisions on partnerships, together with implementing regulations of the Registre de commerce et des sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Contrat de société / Acte constitutif (Limited Partnership Agreement) and the Statement of Registration (Déclaration de constitution) filed electronically through the LBR e-filing portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 29 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp. Contributions are freely agreed in the limited partnership agreement in cash, in kind, or as services. If cash contributions are made, they must be deposited in a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution and can be released only after RCS registration; a banker's certificate evidencing the deposit must be available for the RCS file.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the competent commune or the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) for the registered office; structural load inspection for server racks if the floor load exceeds 150 kg/m2; occupational health and safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering emergency exits, electrical installation, and ergonomic workstations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp. Contributions are freely agreed in the limited partnership agreement in cash, in kind, or as services. If cash contributions are made, they must be deposited in a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution and can be released only after RCS registration; a banker's certificate evidencing the deposit must be available for the RCS file.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes and corporate income tax: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD, Luxembourg Inland Revenue). VAT and excise duties: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT, Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Administration). Unified digital portal: secured Business eSpace via MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate and file the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a civil-law notary or online RCS e-filing; the RCS registration automatically issues the national identification number (matricule) of Luxembourg format. Step 2: Submit the statutory commencement-of-activity declaration to the AEDT through the Business eSpace; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU followed by 8 digits). Step 3: As a fiscally transparent special limited partnership, the SCSp files an annual partner income-allocations declaration with the ACD; the SCSp itself is not registered for corporate income tax or net wealth tax. Step 4: Non-resident corporate partners do not need a separate Luxembourg TIN unless the SCSp's local operations create a deemed permanent establishment, in which case the partner registers with the ACD competent regional tax office. Step 5: For B2C electronically supplied VPN services to EU consumers, the SCSp registers for the OSS Union scheme on the AEDT/VD online portal. A foreign supplier without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative unless reverse charge or the OSS scheme applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory design and lacks legal personality under the law of 12 July 2013. It is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax or net wealth tax; profits are attributed directly and pro rata to the general and limited partners. Non-resident corporate partners are taxable in Luxembourg only on income attributable to a deemed permanent establishment arising from the SCSp's VPN SaaS activity conducted from Luxembourg.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Relevant statutorily available elections: (i) irrevocable election for full tax transparency under Article 14/4 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LITL) for commercial partnerships, although the SCSp is already transparent by default; (ii) VAT group election as a single taxable person (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (iii) waiver of the EUR 35,000 small-business VAT exemption upon exceeding the threshold; (iv) OSS Union scheme election for cross-border B2C supplies of VPN and digital services; (v) BEPS/ATAD II hybrid-mismatch election and declaration via Form 103 to the ACD where dual classification of the SCSp is identified.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established taxable persons under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 in a 12-month period, mandatory VAT registration and invoicing apply. Non-established suppliers have no registration threshold. Electronically supplied VPN/digital services to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's member state from the first transaction irrespective of turnover, so OSS registration is required without any threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VPN network and privacy software constitute electronically supplied services under Article 58 of Directive 2006/112/EC. Supplies to non-EU B2B customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT where the customer is established outside the EU; supplies to non-EU B2C consumers are zero-rated with the place of supply determined by the consumer's usual residence evidenced through IP geolocation, billing address and payment gateway data. Physical goods exports are zero-rated provided the goods depart EU customs territory within the legal timeframe. EU-destination supplies follow normal rules: B2B supplies are subject to reverse charge in the customer's member state; B2C supplies are declared through the Luxembourg OSS scheme in the destination member state.

  • Luxembourg export declaration (Single Administrative Document) and Movement Reference Number registered in the EU Export Control System (ECS/ICS2)
  • Commercial invoice and executed contract identifying the non-EU customer and the exact nature of the VPN/digital service or goods
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from EU customs territory
  • Bank transfer receipt, Stripe or Paymentwall payout statement evidencing receipt of consideration
  • IP geolocation logs, device records and subscriber registration data evidencing non-EU place of supply

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls or mandatory digital stamping for domestic B2B supplies. E-invoicing is interoperable under PEPPOL/EN 16931 standards and mandatory for B2G invoices submitted through the national e-space procurement portal. Cross-border intra-EU B2B e-invoicing and transaction-based reporting are not yet mandatory; they become effective from 1 July 2030 under the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package.

The AEDT and ACD may audit export zero-rating and place-of-supply claims within a five-year assessment period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or false declarations. Unsubstantiated or fictitious export exemptions trigger retrospective VAT assessment at the 17% standard rate, statutory default interest of 0.8% per month, and administrative fines of 10% to 50% of the tax at stake depending on intent. Criminal sanctions for documentary VAT fraud include fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment from eight days to five years. The SCSp's general partner bears joint and several civil liability for the partnership's VAT debts. Luxembourg imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline due to free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU; the 180-day parameter reflects the international trade-finance and AML/CTF benchmark applied by Luxembourg credit institutions to SaaS export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted: there is no FX licensing, no exchange-control approval, no investment-screening or profit-repatriation permit regime. An SCSp is a tax-transparent contractual partnership without separate legal personality; profit distributions to non-resident limited partners are generally exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax when the entity remains fiscally transparent and adequate substance is evidenced. Remittance friction is limited to bank-level AML/KYC source-of-funds confirmation, CRS/FATCA classification and UBO transparency. No convertibility hurdles apply to EUR/USD, EUR/GBP or EUR/CHF flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds or any other foreign-currency receipts. Export collections may be retained indefinitely in designated foreign-currency accounts. Compliance is limited to ECB/BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting: the servicing bank applies the ECB classification codes to each cross-border flow, and corporates maintain the supporting invoices and contracts for audit. No proportional conversion ratio is prescribed for EUR-denominated or multi-currency holding.

Central bank supervision: The Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and Luxembourg's representative in the Eurosystem. Banking and e-money supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration; currency matters are governed by the EU liberalisation rules (TFEU Articles 63-66).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High (restricted). European-authorised e-money and payment institutions such as Payoneer Europe, Wise Europe, Revolut and Wallter are viable for settlement sub-accounts and for aggregating Stripe and Paymentwall proceeds, but they are not a substitute for a primary Luxembourg bank account. They are legally constrained by EEA passporting under PSD2 and MiCA, impose materially lower transaction caps, require narrow CDD acceptance for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced VPN/network-privacy profile, and create monthly reconciliation friction. Offshore non-EEA, unlicensed or MSB-type digital EMIs are not viable for a Luxembourg-compliant treasury and will be rejected by CSSF-regulated banks as sub-account counterparties; they may be used only as light collection gateways subject to full OFAC/EU sanctions screening and an immediate onward sweep to BCEE, BIL or BGL BNP Paribas. Recommended treasury pattern: Stripe and Paymentwall as acquiring gateways; Payoneer or Wise Business as an EEA-licensed collection and settlement EMI; and BIL or BCEE as bank of record for capital, tax, hedging and disbursement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner subscriptions enter Luxembourg via SWIFT MT103 to the SCSp account at a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank, with the SCSp represented by its general partner. The remittance instruction must identify the underlying partner, the economic purpose and the ECB/BCL balance-of-payments classification: equity contributions are coded as direct investment equity capital using BoP matrix codes 511 or 512, and must not be coded as general services or software licensing. The receiving bank executes sanctions and OFAC/EU screening, source-of-funds enhanced due diligence, CRS/FATCA classification and BCL external statistics registration. Contributions above EUR 100,000 are prepared for treasury or risk-committee approval with the signed subscription deed, general-partner resolution, updated register of partners, UBO declaration and RBE filing. EU-based partners may settle in euro via SEPA Credit Transfer or T2 for high-value same-day finality; cross-currency injections flow through the bank's correspondent network, with US-dollar transfers using SWIFT, CHIPS or Fedwire, and are converted at arm's length euro reference rates. No foreign-investment approval is required, as Luxembourg operates full capital-account liberalisation under TFEU Article 63.Outbound payments run over multiple rails: EUR disbursements within the EEA use SEPA Credit Transfer; high-value and urgent euro payments of EUR 1 million or above settle via T2 or TARGET2; USD, GBP and CHF disbursements are executed by SWIFT MT103 through the bank's correspondent accounts using CHIPS and Fedwire in the United States and CHAPS and FPS in the United Kingdom, accompanied by full legal names, sanctions pre-screening and valid BoP purpose codes such as other business services or computer and information services. Hardware procurement and co-location capacity purchases are supported by irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 and documentary collections under URC522 arranged through the trade desks of BGL BNP Paribas or Société Générale Luxembourg. Treasury sweeps between the Luxembourg bank account and EEA-licensed EMI sub-accounts use SEPA Instant Credit Transfer. Every disbursement in a non-euro currency is covered either by an existing forward contract or converted on the payment date; no export-surrender permit is required.EUR/USD and EUR/GBP deliverable plain-vanilla foreign-exchange forwards executed with an EEA-licensed bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and a reduced foreign-exchange single-transaction confirmation.Cross-currency swaps, mark-to-market and collateralised under standard Credit Support Annexes, for long-term USD and GBP revenue and cost conversion.FX swaps and plain spot FX operations for working-capital conversion.Zero-cost collars and structured forwards accepted only when the underlying exposure is fully business-linked and the product passes the CSSF suitability and CRD governance tests.Non-deliverable forwards limited to currencies with non-deliverable regimes such as TRY and RUB, booked only with EEA credit institutions and subject to EMIR and CSDR margin rules.Foreign-exchange options designated as hedges under IFRS 9 cash-flow hedging, with hedge documentation, ratio testing and rebalancing provisions issued at inception.Natural hedging through euro and US-dollar money-market positions and currency-synchronised receivables and payables. Crypto-asset, digital-asset or synthetic-currency linked hedging instruments are expressly non-permissible for this Medium_Risk_Enhanced treasury profile.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High (restricted). European-authorised e-money and payment institutions such as Payoneer Europe, Wise Europe, Revolut and Wallter are viable for settlement sub-accounts and for aggregating Stripe and Paymentwall proceeds, but they are not a substitute for a primary Luxembourg bank account. They are legally constrained by EEA passporting under PSD2 and MiCA, impose materially lower transaction caps, require narrow CDD acceptance for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced VPN/network-privacy profile, and create monthly reconciliation friction. Offshore non-EEA, unlicensed or MSB-type digital EMIs are not viable for a Luxembourg-compliant treasury and will be rejected by CSSF-regulated banks as sub-account counterparties; they may be used only as light collection gateways subject to full OFAC/EU sanctions screening and an immediate onward sweep to BCEE, BIL or BGL BNP Paribas. Recommended treasury pattern: Stripe and Paymentwall as acquiring gateways; Payoneer or Wise Business as an EEA-licensed collection and settlement EMI; and BIL or BCEE as bank of record for capital, tax, hedging and disbursement flows.

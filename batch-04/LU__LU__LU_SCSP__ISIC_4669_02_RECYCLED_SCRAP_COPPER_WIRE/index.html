Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; contributions may be in cash or in kind, fixed freely in the partnership agreement; cash contributions must be credited to a Luxembourg bank account in the partnership's name; in-kind contributions require a valuation annex; notarial deed is mandatory only if real property is contributed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) for any storage premises, including fire separation distances for metal stacking, smoke detectors and extinguishers; structural certificate from a recognized engineering office validating the load-bearing capacity of floors and racks under the Eurocodes; occupational clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering the workplace risk assessment under Article L.312-1 of the Labour Code, provision of PPE (gloves, safety boots, eye protection) for handling shredded copper wire, noise exposure limits under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 7 October 2009 implementing Directive 2003/10/EC, and periodic inspection of lifting and handling equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; contributions may be in cash or in kind, fixed freely in the partnership agreement; cash contributions must be credited to a Luxembourg bank account in the partnership's name; in-kind contributions require a valuation annex; notarial deed is mandatory only if real property is contributed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; portal: MyGuichet.lu / rcs.lu

E-government filing portal

Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Register (RCS) to obtain the business registration number (numéro d'immatriculation). The SCSp, having no legal personality, is entered as a fiscally transparent partnership. Apply to the AEDT for a VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) using the simplified registration form for non-resident entities; non-established foreign traders may appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT procedures. The direct tax TIN for individual and corporate partners is assigned by the ACD; no separate tax ID is issued to the SCSp for income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Tax transparency is mandatory for the SCSp under Luxembourg domestic law; no check-the-box election exists. Available statutory options include VAT grouping (assujetti unique), fiscal unity for corporate income tax if the entity were taxable, the 80% participation exemption regime for qualifying shareholdings held through the SCSp, and the notional interest deduction. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2 may impose a DTT-dependent reclassification if partners reside in jurisdictions that treat the SCSp as opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 taxable turnover per annum for small undertakings established in Luxembourg; non-established foreign suppliers are subject to mandatory VAT registration without a turnover threshold. Intra-Community distance sales of goods to consumers trigger registration under the OSS regime once the EUR 10,000 EU-wide cross-border threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of recycled scrap copper wire to buyers outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 41 of the Luxembourg VAT Law provided the goods are physically exported to a third country and the supplier holds documentary proof of export within the prescribed period. Intra-EU supplies to a taxable counterparty are zero-rated as intra-Community supplies when the buyer provides a valid EU VAT identification number, the EC Sales List is filed, and transport evidence is retained. The zero-rating remains available even if the scrap is cut, shredded, or compacted in Luxembourg, provided no installation, assembly, or processing that destroys the commodity nature occurs before export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-Customs EX summary declaration and export entry)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR consignment note with proof of physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and mandatory vehicle scale ticket or pre-shipment weight certificate for scrap metal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / Telegraphic Transfer credit confirmation evidencing payment receipt within the repatriation deadline

Luxembourg does not operate a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance system; no digital stamping or API-based clearance is currently imposed. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted, and B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL/factur-X is mandatory for public sector contracts. VAT returns (RL) and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through the AEDT portal; export declarations are lodged via the e-Customs system.

Claims for export zero-rating are subject to declaration-level and documentary audit by the AEDT. Failure to produce conclusive export evidence within the statutory period triggers reassessment of the 17% VAT and default interest at 0.6% per month, plus administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 or higher in aggravated cases. Deliberate or fraudulent export misdeclaration attracts criminal sanctions under Article 102 of the VAT Law, including imprisonment up to four years and fines; VAT refunds already credited are clawed back with compound interest. Export records and underlying transport documents must be retained for at least ten years from the end of the year to which they relate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the Economic and Monetary Union and adopted the euro as its sole legal tender; there are no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Both capital flows and profit remittances are freely convertible, subject only to general EU sanctions screening, AML/KYC checks by resident credit institutions, and mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL. Dividend and profit distributions by an SCSp to foreign partners are unrestricted and are not subject to withholding tax at source, provided the entity is treated as fiscally transparent. No prior administrative authorization is required for cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts under Luxembourg law. Export proceeds from recycled copper scrap sales may be retained in euros or foreign currency accounts at any authorised credit institution without surrender obligations to the BCL. The SCSp must, however, report all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics within the applicable monthly reporting window, and must comply with EU sanctions and cash-control regulations where physical cash is involved.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; customs and excise controls are administered by the Administration des douanes et accises.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) that hold neither a Luxembourg licence nor a valid EU passport under PSD2 are not permitted to provide payment accounts to a Luxembourg-resident SCSp. The CSSF requires any EMI offering services in Luxembourg to be authorised or passported; non-EU fintechs face significant legal barriers. Even where passporting exists, most digital EMIs do not support documentary credit operations, commodity trade settlement, or the high-limit SWIFT transactions required for this trading scope. The SCSp should rely exclusively on tier-1 banks for core treasury functions; an EMI may be used only as a supplementary low-balance expense rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be routed through the general partner's bank account or directly into the SCSp's segregated capital escrow account at a CSSF-licensed credit institution via SEPA (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR). The payment instruction must reference the SCSp LEI and the specific contribution clause of the partnership agreement, and the respective partner must complete the bank's source-of-funds declaration. For balance-of-payments accuracy, purpose code 601 (direct investment) should be used where applicable. Any contribution exceeding EUR 12,500 must be included in the BCL statistical return. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the SCSp level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and has no Luxembourg corporate income tax liability; for ordinary resident corporations the aggregate headline CIT rate in Luxembourg City is 24.94% (17% IRC + 7% unemployment solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at the SCSp level because the SCSp is transparent and not a resident company. Distributions of profits to partners are not dividends for Luxembourg WHT purposes. If the same economic profit is later paid by a non-transparent Luxembourg resident company, the domestic dividend WHT rate is 15%, with treaty relief commonly reducing it to 0% to 5%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR/€

Economic substance scope: The formal Economic Substance Regulations of the Gulf jurisdictions do not apply in Luxembourg. However, the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule (Article 6 LIR), the ATAD CFC rules, and the Principal Purpose Test under tax treaties require real economic activity. A pure conduit SCSp owning no people, premises, procurement function, or trading substance in its recycled copper scrap activity may be disregarded for treaty benefits, recharacterized, or exposed to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all disguised income; monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%; administrative penalties up to EUR 12,500; in aggravated cases imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years; exclusion from public contracts in Luxembourg; publication of the conviction in commercial registers.

Director personal liability: The general partner or manager who directed the fraud is jointly and severally civilly liable for the tax loss; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment, prohibition from managing a commercial entity, weighing of assets for restitution, and seizure of passport/travel restrictions where a flight risk is established.
Transfer Pricing Distortion

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax administration recharacterizes the transaction under Article 56bis LIR and adjusts taxable income upward; penalty of up to 10% of the additional corporate tax due, plus 0.6% monthly interest; secondary tax may be imposed in the foreign buyer's jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certified the distorted TP policy can be held civilly liable for the adjusted tax plus penalties; criminal referral for document falsification may trigger imprisonment, director disqualification, and personal attachment orders.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of Article 6 LIR GAAR and Treaty Shopping defenses; denial of Luxembourg tax residency certificates; denial of treaty benefits under the MLI principal purpose test; reallocation of profits to the ultimate parent; liquidation of the entity may be forced by RCS; administrative fines and costs assessed.

Director personal liability: The General Partner is exposed under the SCSp partnership agreement and civil law to unlimited liability for partnership debts if the entity is found to be fictitious; shareholders or managers may be ordered to repay diverted profits; criminal liability arises where the shell was used to commit fraud, papers can be seized, and a travel ban may be issued during criminal investigations.
AML / CTF and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Breach of Luxembourg AML laws (Law of 12 November 2004 as amended and EU Directives) carries administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or twice the economic benefit recovered; sanctions violations trigger asset freeze and criminal penalties of up to EUR 1 million and imprisonment of 1 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: AML duties apply to beneficial owners and managing partners individually; culpable directors can be disqualified from managing financial or commercial entities, ordered to pay restitution, subjected to monitoring by the CAA, and, in serious proceedings, remanded in custody with a travel ban imposed.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible with substantive limitations. Luxembourg law (Art. 710-1 et seq. of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended; RBE Law of 13 January 2019; CSSF Reg. 12-02) requires a genuine registered office different from the statutorily declared administrative seat, and the tax administration (Administration des contributions directes, 'ACD') applies the real-activity test to avoid deemed-residence deviations. For a low-risk professional translation and dubbing agency with no regulated license, a serviced-office or 'domiciliation' arrangement — where a licensed corporate domiciliation agent provides a physical Luxembourg address, mail handling, and meeting facilities — is widely accepted and constitutes current market practice (LBR-registered domiciliation agents are regulated by the Ministry of Justice under Article 27 of the Law of 19 December 2002). However, a pure virtual mailbox with no local decision-making, no local management, no local bank account engagement, and no documentary evidence of substance in Luxembourg would expose the SARL to: (i) reclassification of its tax residence abroad, (ii) denial of Luxembourg VAT registration, and (iii) potential criminal-liability exposure of the managers for fictive registered office. Recommended practice: a serviced office in Luxembourg City with at least one Luxembourg-resident director or an executive manager domiciled in Luxembourg; the localization agency's project-management and invoicing functions may legitimately be performed remotely, but the SARL's statutory minutes, accounting records, and VAT filing obligations must be materially anchored in Luxembourg.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL requires a minimum issued share capital of EUR 12,000 (approximately USD 13,000), fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked account at a Luxembourg bank in the name of the company in formation and released only upon RCS registration; contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's valuation report and effective transfer of the asset to the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for office premises under the Grand-Duchy Fire and Rescue Service (CGDIS); structural load certificate from the landlord or a registered engineer for heavy studio or acoustic equipment; workplace health and safety clearance under the Labor Code; electrical installation verification for recording and dubbing equipment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); central e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu)

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL obtains its national registration number (matricule) automatically upon incorporation through the Luxembourg Business Registers. For VAT, the SARL must submit Form 1 to the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu within 15 days of commencing taxable activity; non-resident entities without an establishment in Luxembourg must register when making taxable supplies in Luxembourg or when distance sales to Luxembourg consumers exceed the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box election. The SARL is irrebuttably treated as a taxable corporate entity. ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply to neutralize mismatch outcomes. Elective regimes include: VAT group registration as a single taxable person (Article 61 of the VAT Law), option to tax the letting of immovable property (Article 44(2)), and election to benefit from the participation exemption (75% dividend deduction and full capital gains exemption) for qualifying shareholdings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg-established taxable persons have no mandatory registration below an annual taxable turnover of EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT). A non-established supplier is required to register for Luxembourg VAT if it makes taxable supplies of goods or services in Luxembourg and the reverse charge is unavailable, or if its distance sales to Luxembourg consumers exceed the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold (formerly EUR 100,000 before 1 July 2021).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods outside the EU qualifies for zero-rating where goods leave Luxembourg unmodified, export is evidenced within 90 days, and legal ownership has transferred to a non-EU purchaser. Intra-community supplies (ICS) are zero-rated where the customer supplies a valid EU VAT number and the transaction is listed in the EC Sales List. Services provided to non-EU business customers are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction). Services performed for a non-EU private customer (e.g., multilingual localization) are zero-rated if the effective use and enjoyment of the service is outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) and/or electronic proof of exit
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or other transport document evidencing physical departure from Luxembourg
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation demonstrating foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and export contract or client purchase order

Luxembourg currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping requirement, or mandatory continuous transaction control. E-invoicing is permissible and accepted, VAT returns must be filed electronically via the eCDF channel (monthly/quarterly), and B2G invoicing functions through the PEPPOL infrastructure on an optional basis.

Where export zero-rating is applied without the required documentary evidence, Luxembourg VAT authorities may assess the VAT not paid, charge interest at the statutory rate, and impose administrative fines of 10% to 100% of the tax deficiency depending on the degree of negligence. Intentional fraud triggers criminal penalties under Article 58 of the VAT Law, including imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 50,000, plus a five-year retrospective clawback period (10 years in case of proven fraud).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: the euro is freely convertible and Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control body, no prior-approval mechanism and no quantitative limits on current or capital transactions. Article 63(1) TFEU prohibits all restrictions on the movement of capital and payments between Member States and third countries. A Luxembourg SARL may invoice foreign clients, hold foreign-currency accounts, convert EUR into any currency, and repatriate profits without BCL or CSSF consent. Profit-remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than currency-related: (i) Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15% (reducible to 0% under the EU Parent–Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 months, or under an applicable tax treaty); (ii) the commercial-company net-assets distributability test (Article 111 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended) requiring an approved balance sheet and, where reserves are distributed, a statutory auditor's certification; and (iii) for larger remittances, banks may request evidence of current corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and municipal business tax filings. No BCL authorization, economic-substance visa, or exchange quotation is required for ordinary treasury activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion of export or service proceeds for resident enterprises. A SARL engaged in professional multilingual translation and media dubbing (ISIC 7490) may retain all export receipts in foreign currency in any EU-resident bank account. The only associated obligation is statistical: BCL and STATEC balance-of-payments surveys may require resident service exporters to report invoice-level proceeds on a quarterly or annual basis, purely for compilation of national accounts. This reporting is ex-post, non-restrictive, and has no liquidity-management or conversion consequence.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the National Central Bank of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and a member of the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent supervisory authority for credit institutions, payment institutions and e-money institutions under the amended AML/CFT Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF Regulation 12-02. The European Central Bank (ECB) holds ultimate monetary and euro-liquidity authority via T2/TARGET Services. Luxembourg directly applies the EU Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR), Directive (EU) 2018/843 (5AMLD), and Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers of funds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for disbursement-only usage. Wise Europe SA (e-money institution licensed by the National Bank of Belgium and passported into Luxembourg under PSD2/E-Money Directive) and Payoneer (EU payment-/e-money-licensed passporting) are viable for low- to mid-value B2B contractor payouts and multi-currency conversions for a localization agency. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI cannot issue the bank attestation (certificat de dépôt) required by the notary for the €12,000 SARL registered capital; that capital must be deposited and blocked at a Luxembourg credit institution; (ii) Regulation (EU) 2015/847 fund-transfer information and CSSF AML duties apply identically, so the EMI will require the same UBO and source-of-funds evidence at onboarding; (iii) tier-1 banks do not treat EMI balances as reference liquidity for credit assessment; and (iv) EMI sanctions filters are less configurable than correspondent-bank filters, so high-value flows to sensitive jurisdictions should remain on SWIFT MT103. Routing rule: tier-1 bank for statutory capital, client receipts, tax payments and balances above US$ 100,000; Wise B2B/Payoneer for recurring vendor and freelancer payouts below that threshold.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The €12,000 registered capital of a Luxembourg SARL must be fully paid in cash, subscribed and deposited before incorporation. Procedure: the notary instructs the chosen Luxembourg credit institution to open a blocked capital account (compte bloqué/séquestre) in the name of the future SARL; the shareholder executes a SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst, settled in approximately ten seconds via TIPS/T2) if the remitter holds an EEA bank account, or a SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA remitters, with remittance text referencing the notary file and the future RCS number. After the notarial deed is passed and RCS registration obtained, the blocked balance converts automatically to the operational current account. Additional equity contributions (share premium, capital increase, super dividend re-injection) arrive by SEPA SCT/SCT Inst or MT103 using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (capital contribution) or 'CINV' (capital investment); shareholder loans use 'LITI'. The receiving bank must verify complete remitter and beneficiary information under Regulation (EU) 2015/847; any capital flow at or above EUR 10,000 triggers enhanced due diligence requiring the underlying notarial deed, shareholder resolution, and source-of-funds evidence. No BCL authorization or FX license is required for inbound capital; USD-denominated equity contributions are cleared via SWIFT gpi correspondent channels and pass through the bank's OFAC/EU sanctions filters with purpose code 'CAPC' restated for regulatory consistency.Eurozone and EEA disbursements: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) via TARGET2/STEP2/TIPS, settled T+0 with no correspondent deduction, using the vendor's IBAN and the service invoice reference. Non-EEA and non-EUR disbursements: SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT gpi tracking through the Luxembourg bank's correspondent network — USD via a U.S. clearing agent (Fedwire/CHIPS), GBP via CHAPS or Faster Payments, and JPY via BOJ-NET. For high-value licensing or media-rights acquisitions above EUR 100,000 where trade-settlement security is required, a commercial documentary letter of credit (advised or confirmed by the Luxembourg bank, payable at sight under UCP 600) or a documentary clean collection is permissible. For recurring mid-value contractor payouts between US$ 5,000 and US$ 50,000, Wise B2B and Payoneer multi-currency virtual accounts provide local clearing in more than 40 markets at lower cost; however, treasury policy should escalate any single vendor payout above US$ 100,000 to a direct MT103 to preserve a clean audit trail. All outbound payment narratives must state the service contract reference and the applicable VAT treatment (reverse charge or OSS), with monthly reconciliation to the SARL's Luxembourg VAT returns.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY) for firm localization and dubbing receivables/payables, executed under a 2002 ISDA Master Agreement with an EU/EEA credit institution; non-financial counterparty hedge-only trades are exempt from mandatory clearing under EMIR Refit thresholds, and no BCL or CSSF license is required for treasury end-user hedging.FX swap agreements combining a spot leg and a forward leg, used to roll or rotate existing hedge maturities and to manage timing mismatches in licensing cash flows; same ISDA and EMIR treatment as outright forwards, with settlement within two business days exempt from uncleared margin rules.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for material intercompany or media-rights financing with tenor above 12 months, subject to EMIR trade-repository reporting, daily variation-margin arrangements with EU counterparties, and IFRS 9 hedge documentation evidencing hedge-only intent.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD and EUR/JPY FX options (ATMF calls and puts, zero-premium collars) to hedge contingent tender exposure for multilingual media localization contracts, with premium sizes constrained by a board-approved hedging policy and paid in EUR from the operational account.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in euros against restricted-currency exposures (CNY, INR, NGN, BRL, TRY) arising from emerging-market vendor payments to dubbing studios and subtitling suppliers, settled in EUR; the bank executes under an ISDA NDF confirmation and requires the underlying USD- or EUR-denominated vendor invoices to validate the hedge-only purpose.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for disbursement-only usage. Wise Europe SA (e-money institution licensed by the National Bank of Belgium and passported into Luxembourg under PSD2/E-Money Directive) and Payoneer (EU payment-/e-money-licensed passporting) are viable for low- to mid-value B2B contractor payouts and multi-currency conversions for a localization agency. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI cannot issue the bank attestation (certificat de dépôt) required by the notary for the €12,000 SARL registered capital; that capital must be deposited and blocked at a Luxembourg credit institution; (ii) Regulation (EU) 2015/847 fund-transfer information and CSSF AML duties apply identically, so the EMI will require the same UBO and source-of-funds evidence at onboarding; (iii) tier-1 banks do not treat EMI balances as reference liquidity for credit assessment; and (iv) EMI sanctions filters are less configurable than correspondent-bank filters, so high-value flows to sensitive jurisdictions should remain on SWIFT MT103. Routing rule: tier-1 bank for statutory capital, client receipts, tax payments and balances above US$ 100,000; Wise B2B/Payoneer for recurring vendor and freelancer payouts below that threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (group consolidated revenue) and EUR 1,000,000 (related party transactions) trigger mandatory Local File and Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) primarily to pure holding, financing, and IP companies. For a translation/localization agency, substance is presumed if it has adequate premises, employees, and operational activity. However, the company must avoid being classified as a shell entity under EU anti-abuse rules (ATAD) and Luxembourg's general anti-abuse rule (GAAR).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional tax penalties up to 100% of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes, subject to travel bans and asset freezes; criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 15% WHT on deemed dividends; penalties up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for unpaid taxes and subject to disqualification from serving as directors in Luxembourg.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 for legal entities; suspension of business license; criminal sanctions for responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to €250,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years for willful AML violations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of the adjustment, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SCSp. The amount and form of the partners' contributions are fixed contractually in the partnership agreement; no escrow or blocked bank account is required. Contributions may be in cash or in kind, and the general partner's powers and liability profile are governed by the agreement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration); VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); central corporate and tax registration portal: MyGuichet.lu / https://pfi.public.lu

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first appoint a local approved statutory agent and file through MyGuichet.lu for incorporation with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). The RCS assigns a 13-digit business identification number which doubles as the corporate tax file number. VAT registration with the AEDT is mandatory where taxable activity in Luxembourg exists, resulting in an LU+8-digit VAT identification number; otherwise a separate VAT registration application is submitted via the same digital portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (SCSp is fiscally transparent by default; income is taxed at partner level) — Foreign entities must first appoint a local approved statutory agent and file through MyGuichet.lu for incorporation with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (SCSp is fiscally transparent by default; income is taxed at partner level)

Available election forms

The SCSp may elect under Article 175 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law to become subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax, overriding fiscal transparency. No US-style check-the-box regime exists. The EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules may restrict the tax benefit of transparency/opaqueness elections in cross-border scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the Luxembourg small-enterprise exemption; however, non-resident and digital suppliers have no de minimis threshold and must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of digital royalty-free music licenses is an electronic service. Luxembourg VAT is not chargeable where the place of supply is outside Luxembourg: (i) B2B recipient established in a third country; (ii) B2C non-taxable recipient established outside the EU; or (iii) EU B2B recipient where the reverse charge applies in the customer's member state. Zero-rating/outside-scope treatment requires documentary evidence of the recipient's foreign establishment and absence of effective use or exploitation of the license in Luxembourg.

  • VAT invoice (or self-billing/credit note) accompanied by a place-of-supply determination narrative
  • Signed royalty-free stock music license agreement specifying customer, territory, usage scope and royalty/fee amount
  • Proof of customer's foreign status: EU VAT ID for intra-EU B2B supplies, or foreign business registration/passport for third-country supplies
  • Payment evidence: Stripe/PayPal settlement reports and bank statements showing actual receipt from the foreign customer
  • Inward remittance certificate or FX bank memo for repatriated licence fees

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via the Peppol infrastructure since 1 January 2024; no continuous transaction control (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies to B2B invoices; Luxembourg VAT returns are filed periodically through MyGuichet.lu.

The AEDT may reassess unsubstantiated zero-rated or outside-scope positions within a five-year limitation period (extended for intentional non-compliance), issuing Luxembourg VAT at the standard 17% rate plus default interest of 0.8% per month and administrative fines up to 100% of the tax evaded. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may trigger criminal prosecution with imprisonment and additional financial penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full euro-area convertible-currency status. EU Treaty provisions (Art. 63 TFEU) and the EU Capital Movements Directive guarantee unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control authorisation is required for cross-border income, royalties, or profit distributions. An SCSp is tax-transparent, so profit distributions by the partnership are generally treated as direct distributions to partners and are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, provided the partners are not Luxembourg tax residents and no Luxembourg commercial or real-estate fixed base is created. Anti-hybrid (ATAD), CRS/FATCA reporting, and substance requirements must nevertheless be documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion applies. Export receipts and licensing-income proceeds may be retained in EUR or a foreign currency, and conversion is freely available at market rates through authorised banks. No BCL approval or repatriation deadline exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in the Eurosystem, together with the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises banking AML/KYC conduct and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg; Low for offshore, non-EEA digital-only EMIs. Legal constraints: Offshore EMIs cannot offer accounts or payment services to a Luxembourg SCSp unless they hold a Luxembourg/EEA authorisation or operate through a licensed branch; PSD2 (Directive 2015/2366) restricts deposit-taking and mandates client-fund safeguarding in a credit institution; the CSSF applies AML/CFT outsourcing rules (CSSF circulars 18/667, 19/732) that require a local access point, UBO identification, and transaction monitoring. Platforms such as Stripe and PayPal can be used only as payment collection fixtures connected to a licensed acquiring bank, not as the primary treasury account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (aggregate benchmark: 17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax for portfolio distributions; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Luxembourg participation exemption conditions. SCSp profit allocations are tax-transparent and are not normally subject to entity-level Luxembourg dividend WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg law does not set an explicit statutory de minimis threshold for Local File / Master File. In audit practice, material related-party transactions above EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate attract mandatory contemporaneous documentation review; consolidated group revenue above EUR 750,000,000 triggers the statutory CbC report threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations framework, but it enforces substance-over-form doctrine through Article 6 AbgrG and Article 56bis LIR, the OECD anti-BEPS principles, and anti-avoidance case law. A stock-music licensing SCSp must demonstrate real Luxembourg substance: qualified staff, active strategic and operational decision-making, a Luxembourg bank account, actual EQ-building, and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Failure to prove substance may result in denial of treaty benefits and reclassification as a fully taxable resident entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of omitted income; fraud surcharge of 20%-100%; late-payment interest of 0.6% per month; criminal fine up to €250,000; imprisonment up to 5 years under the Luxembourg Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: The general partner and managing directors who sign or cause a false declaration to be filed are jointly and severally liable for taxes evaded; travel bans, asset seizures, director disqualification, and imprisonment may follow.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (Abuse of Law)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax transparency and treaty benefits; reclassification as a corporate resident subject to Luxembourg CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; re-assessment plus 25% penalty for non-compliance with substance documentation requests.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who knowingly set up a shell arrangement are personally exposed to restitution claims, tax damages, coercive collection measures, travel bans, and criminal liability for abuse of law.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment with a 10%-20% surcharge, monthly interest at 0.6%, administrative fines up to €250,000 for non-production of records, and discretionary best-judgment assessment against the SCSp.

Director personal liability: Directors who obstruct transfer pricing audits face personal administrative fines; in egregious cases the corporate veil is pierced and managing partners are held personally liable for the tax difference plus accrued interest.
AML / Payment-Conduit Anomaly (Commercial Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The underlying licensing activity is not itself an AML-licensed profession, but suspicious transaction reporting and AML penalties apply if the payment pattern is used to conceal tax evasion or money laundering; penalties under Luxembourg AML law may reach €1,000,000 for culpable institutions and individuals in abuse cases.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors may be personally criminally liable for money-laundering predicates if they direct payment flows to evade tax or intentionally obscure beneficial ownership; asset freezes and travel bans may be imposed.

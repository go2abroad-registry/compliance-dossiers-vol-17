Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory capital requirement for a SCSp. Partnership interests are freely determined by the Partners and may be subscribed in cash or in kind. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; however, the competent customs and excise authority will require a customs bond or bank guarantee (typically EUR 150,000 to EUR 500,000) for bonded wine/spirit warehouse operations before the excise license is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by Service Incendie et Secours (SID) confirming compliance with COT (Commission de la sécurité incendie) regulations for alcohol storage: automatic fire-extinguishing system over tanks > 100 m3, fire hydrant spacing ≤ 50 m, smoke extraction, and firewater containment volume based on largest tank. Structural load inspection certificate from a registered engineer/ bureau de contrôle agréé certifying racking and floor loading at a minimum of 5.0 kN/m2 warehouse capacity. Occupational safety clearance issued by Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering emergency evacuation, handling of ethanol, manual handling risk assessment, and adequate ventilation in cellar/storage areas (ATEX zone classification by SUBROGATION under Luxembourg mining regulations).

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital requirement for a SCSp. Partnership interests are freely determined by the Partners and may be subscribed in cash or in kind. No escrow account is statutorily mandated; however, the competent customs and excise authority will require a customs bond or bank guarantee (typically EUR 150,000 to EUR 500,000) for bonded wine/spirit warehouse operations before the excise license is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://myguichet.lu)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities must first obtain a Luxembourg business number (matricule) via RCS registration if carrying on a commercial activity, then file VAT registration with AEDT using Form 645 (or via MyGuichet.lu). For a SCSp without legal personality, the partnership itself registers via the RCS and is allocated a national business number; the tax identification number (TIN) is issued by ACD for direct tax purposes and a separate VAT number by AEDT. A Luxembourg tax representative may be required for non-resident partnerships.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Non-resident entities must first obtain a Luxembourg business number (matricule) via RCS registration if carrying on a commercial activity, then file VAT registration with AEDT using Form 645 (or via MyGuichet.lu). For a SCSp without legal personality, the partnership itself registers via the RCS and is allocated a national business number; the tax identification number (TIN) is issued by ACD for direct tax purposes and a separate VAT number by AEDT. A Luxembourg tax representative may be required for non-resident partnerships.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A SCSp is fiscally transparent by default. However, a partnership may elect to be taxed as a body corporate under Article 5(2) LITL if all partners agree and the partnership is deemed to have corporate status for legal purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (transposed into domestic law) apply to recharacterize mismatches; elections are made by written notice to ACD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small enterprise exemption); no threshold for non-resident suppliers performing taxable activities in Luxembourg; EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance sales threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods to non-EU destinations where physical departure is proven within the seller's books; to services directly linked to exported goods and performed for a non-EU customer; and to services supplied to a third-country established customer that are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT. Intra-EU supplies are not zero-rated but are reverse-charged in the destination state when the buyer has a valid EU VAT number and the supply is covered by a VIES statement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A) / EU Summary Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR/AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or binding bank proof of payment

Luxembourg maintains no real-time fiscal clearance model and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing or digital stamping. Electronic invoices in PEPPOL/EU-compliant formats are acceptable. VAT returns and VIES recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu; no real-time API clearance is imposed.

Luxembourg tax authorities (AEDT) may reassess within 5 years (10 years in cases of fraud). Fraudulent zero-rating triggers VAT reassessment, default interest, administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 (or four times the evaded tax), criminal penalties up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment under VAT law, and potential suspension or revocation of the bonded warehouse license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the Euro as legal tender with full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no licensing for ordinary trade payments, and no restrictions on profit or capital repatriation for qualifying pass-through vehicles such as the SCSp, provided transactions do not breach EU/UN financial sanctions, money laundering rules, or UBO disclosure obligations. Remittance hurdles are limited to documentary compliance: proof of underlying commercial transaction, beneficial ownership identification, and statistical reporting through BCL balance-of-payments surveys.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds in Luxembourg. All export receipts may be freely retained, repatriated, or set off against import disbursements. The BCL imposes no conversion ratio and no time limit; however, resident entities must provide periodic balance-of-payments data on cross-border flows exceeding statutory thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); no separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists. EU exchange-rate and capital-movement rules apply via the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA licensed Electronic Money Institutions under PSD2. Legal constraints: such EMIs can provide payment accounts and FX execution without full credit institution license, but they are generally unsuitable as primary treasury counterparties for bonded warehouse transactions because they do not offer commercial letters of credit, trade finance, or warehouse receipt financing. Offshore, non-EU/EEA digital EMIs are rated Low or Prohibited for entities holding bonded tax-excise goods due to CSSF AML/CFT expectations requiring a regulated EU bank or licensed EMI with physical or contractual substance, full UBO chain transparency, and audit trail over excise duty status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for capital contributions to the SCSp is a SWIFT MT103 by the investor from its bank to the Luxembourg account, referencing the partnership as beneficiary, the SCSp account IBAN and the following remittance info: (a) word 'Capital Contribution' or 'Capital Account Payment'; (b) the ECB Balance-of-Payments purpose code for direct or portfolio investment, commonly 'D' for direct investment or 'A0101' for capital subscription; (c) the notarial or constitutive deed reference; (d) the investor's UBO identifier. Banks may request a signed capital contribution certificate and a copy of the deed. TARGET2 is the intra-EUR clearing rail; SWIFT is used for non-EUR (USD, GBP). Inbound amounts equal to or above EUR 10,000 may trigger CSSF statistical reporting; banks apply standard OFAC and EU sanctions screening on counterparties.For wine and spirits bonded trade, permitted outbound rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 direct transfer for supplier payments, customs duty/VAT payments and warehouse fees; (2) documentary commercial letters of credit (MT700) and standby LCs (MT760) for structured purchases of aged stock; (3) bank payment obligations (BPO) where matched trade data exists; (4) SEPA instant transfers within eurozone for small operational expenses. All outbound disbursements must be supported by invoices, warehouse warrants or delivery orders, and the bank will apply dual-use trade sanctions screening. In EUR, settlement occurs on TARGET2 with irrevocable finality; GBP, USD and other currencies clear through SWIFT with bank correspondent chains. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline (CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 7% of CIT + Luxembourg City municipal business tax coefficient 3.5 yielding 6.72%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under the Luxembourg participation exemption and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders (>=10% shareholding or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost); 15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax for portfolio non-resident shareholders not qualifying; 0% on profit distributions by a fiscally transparent SCSp because profits are transparently allocated to partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory single local-file threshold. Mandatory Master File / CbCR under Luxembourg CbCR law (EU Directive 2016/881) where the group's consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000. Administrative practice additionally requires a Local File for any SCSp-controlled transaction exceeding EUR 1,000,000 in a tax year, particularly for bonded warehouse licence holders. The local file must include transfer pricing method selection, comparability analysis, and the SCSp's profit allocation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD and EU ATAD general anti-abuse rules. An SCSp conducting bonded wine/spirits trade must demonstrate real economic substance: (i) regular management decisions in Luxembourg, (ii) local bonded warehouse licences and customs declarations, (iii) own office, staff and physical inventory risk, and (iv) compensation commensurate with functions and risks. Under the definition in Luxembourg's Circular L.I.R. n° 100/4, a 'letterbox' SCSp with passive income from spirits trading is disregarded for treaty benefits. Since Luxembourg is not on the EU non-cooperative list, formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to UAE/DFC are not imposed, but the substance requirement is embedded in the arm's length standard and tax residency law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional incorrect income declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO) Art. 396-397: fine up to EUR 25,000; an additional fine of 10-25% of the tax evaded; imprisonment for up to 5 years. The tax authorities may issue an estimated assessment without time limit.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and the general partner of the SCSp may be jointly and severally liable for the tax evaded if they personally drafted false declarations; travel bans and asset freezes may be ordered; criminal conviction leads to director disqualification of 5 years.
Snapshot / Shell Company (economic substance failure)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration will lift tax transparency and reclassify the profits as Luxembourg-source income subject to CIT + municipal business tax (24.94%) plus 15% dividend WHT upon deemed liquidation. DTA benefits denied and deemed profits assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit letterbox operations are jointly liable for CIT and municipal business tax; the court may pierce the veil under Art. 59 CBCS and order civil damages equal to the revenue authority's legal costs.
AML / Excise Duty Evasion under EU Alcohol Warehousing

Statutory sanction: Breach of Luxembourg Customs and Excise Code Art. 57: seizure of goods and vehicles plus fines up to EUR 1,500 per litre of pure alcohol or in any case EUR 250,000; compliance with AML Law of 12 Nov 2004: administrative fine of EUR 250,000 for entities and EUR 1,250,000 for repeat offence.

Director personal liability: Individual managers face up to 5 years' imprisonment for excise fraud and money laundering, travel bans from Schengen area, confiscation of personal assets, and bank account freezes; professional capacity as board member is lost upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg imposes an upward transfer pricing adjustment and applies penalties: 10% of the re-characterised amount + late interest at monthly rate 0.6%; if deliberate, the adjustment is treated as a deemed dividend subject to 15% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve thin-capitalised transactions are personally liable for the additional tax and WHT to the extent the company is insolvent; criminal penalties under AO Art. 399 for aiding tax evasion.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg Inland Revenue for corporate and income tax: www.impotsdirects.public.lu; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT: aedt.public.lu; business and national identification number registration via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the Single Point of Contact at guichet.lu / rcs.lu.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity establishing an SCSp must first file the partnership agreement, appointments and registered office details with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu. Upon RCS registration, the Luxembourg Business Registers issues a 13-digit national identification number used as the general tax reference. For VAT, if the entity makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg, Form 1610 (VAT registration application) must be filed with AEDT before trading; no local permanent establishment or physical presence is strictly required to register, but a Luxembourg VAT identification number is mandatory once registration is due. Inward/outward VAT registrations and modifications are filed electronically through the AEDT e-space.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box system. An SCSp is fiscally transparent by default and is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax; each partner is taxed directly on its allocable share. There is no general election to convert the SCSp into a corporation, but an exceptional 'opting partnership' may be possible under national private law with civil-form conversion, and VAT grouping election under Article 62 of the Luxembourg VAT Law allows an SCSp to join a VAT group with other Luxembourg taxable persons. Atad-aligned hybrid mismatch declarations and foreign classification filings must be made to the ACD where a partner's treaty treatment conflicts with Luxembourg transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover, excluding VAT, for Luxembourg-established taxable persons eligible for the small business exemption; non-established foreign entities have no exemption threshold and must register from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg, subject to intra-EU distance sales thresholds under the Union OSS (EUR 10,000 per calendar year).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for supplies of goods that are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf, provided the goods leave the EU within the statutory proofs and the supplier holds the required customs export evidence. Intra-EU dispatches to a taxable person in another Member State are also zero-rated when the customer has a valid EU VAT identification number and the supplier obtains the customer's VAT number, transport document, and an intra-Community declaration in the recapitulative statement. Freight and ancillary transport services related directly to exports and services physically performed outside the EU are zero-rated if the underlying statutory conditions and documentary evidence in the Luxembourg Circular on export services are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Signed delivery receipts or customer confirmation of receipt
  • EU VAT identification number validation and intra-Community purchase orders for EU zero-rating
  • Inward Remittance Certificate showing foreign exchange repatriation, where exported proceeds are received in foreign currency

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no API-based tax clearance for standard B2B invoices. Invoices to the Luxembourg public sector must be submitted through the Peppol network in Peppol BIS 3.0 format; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and any structured or unstructured electronic document is valid provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are guaranteed. VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu or the AEDT portal, but there is no real-time pre-clearance approval of each invoice.

Luxembourg tax authorities scrutinise export zero-rating claims through documentary audits, automated risk profiling, and cross-checks against customs declarations and transport data. If the mandatory customs documentation bundle is incomplete, not retained, or not produced on request, the zero-rating is denied and the taxpayer is assessed Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the full amount plus late-payment interest and penalties, and the corresponding input VAT credits are clawed back. Where false or forged documentation is knowingly submitted, administrative penalties may be imposed and criminal proceedings may be initiated under the Luxembourg VAT Law, the General Tax Code and EU anti-fraud instruments, with fines and imprisonment applicable upon conviction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully integrated Eurozone member; capital-account convertibility is complete under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, with no exchange-control authorization for the conversion of EUR into foreign currency. Current-account profit remittances and distributions of an SCSp pass-through to non-resident partners are freely executed via SEPA or SWIFT, subject only to Anti-Money-Laundering driven source-of-funds documentation and CSSF CDD requirements. No distinction is made between capital-account and current-account convertibility in practice; barriers are limited to UBO identification, sanctions screening, and the SCSp's lack of legal personality (contractual-partnership status) which requires counterparty acceptance of a named account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Luxembourg. The SCSp's cross-border parcel consolidation revenue is treated as a sale of logistics services/goods brokerage; receipts may be retained in EUR, USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in the entity's name, without any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. BCL reporting applies only for statistical balance-of-payments thresholds, and banks may record the ISIC 5229 service-payment economic purpose for regulatory filings; repatriation and conversion remain unrestricted under EU capital-movement rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential and AML/CFT supervision of banks and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), with the BCL responsible for monetary policy, payment system oversight (TARGET2, LIPS), and balance-of-payments statistics collection.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex is rated HIGH for operational cross-border clearing: it holds EU EMI licenses passported into Luxembourg under PSD2, enabling scalable multi-currency sub-ledgers (USD, CNY, EUR, GBP), interbank-margin FX conversion, local-clearing collections (e.g., US ACH, CN cross-border sweep, SEPA instant), and direct API integration for the parcel consolidation hub's payment engine. Legal constraint: the SCSp has no legal personality, so the EMI account must be denominated in the SCSp's statutory denomination with the general partner (gérant) acting as the financial signatory; despite this, larger Luxembourg financial institutions may refuse to recognize EMI receipts as bank-confirmation evidence for capital verification and commercial LC issuance. Therefore the viable architecture is a dual-rail strategy: a Tier-1 a domestic bank for escrow, LC, and audit-grade confirmation, plus Airwallex as the high-velocity operating-clearing rail. Regulatory watch: CSSF Guidance and the Loi du 12 novembre 2004 (AML) require that the EMI perform full UBO verification of the SCSp's partners; use of a dedicated SCSp sub-account rather than a personal account of the general partner is mandatory to preserve legal separation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% standard CIT (unchanged for tax year 2025); plus 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT, producing a 18.19% national effective CIT rate for corporate partners. In Luxembourg City the municipal business tax rate of 6.75% applies, bringing the combined marginal corporate tax burden to approximately 24.94%. The SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not itself subject to CIT; tax is borne by the partners on their allocable share of profits.

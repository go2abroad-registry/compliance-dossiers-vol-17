Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Funding & unlock path: For the administrative registered office, no fire brigade permit is required unless the premises are open to the public; comply with Règlement grand-ducal of 4 November 1994 for fire safety of commercial premises. If a warehouse is operated, obtain a fire safety certificate, structural load clearance, and a workplace inspection under the Luxembourg Labour Code (Code du travail) and Council Directive 89/654/EEC.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum capital requirement for an SCSp; contributions (cash or in kind) are freely set in the partnership agreement. No escrow account or notarized capital certificate is required under Luxembourg law.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — Incorporate the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL), obtain the notarial deed, appoint a general partner (associé commandité) and make the required deposit; the RCSL issues the registration number (matricule / numéro de registre). STEP 2 — Apply for the tax identification number (numéro d'identification fiscale) with the ACD by filing the standard registration data for the SCSp and for any partner who is a Luxembourg resident taxpayer; the ACD issues the matricule fiscale used for direct tax declarations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax purposes; the partnership itself is not subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) or net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune); partnership profits and losses flow through to partners in proportion to their entitlement and are taxed at partner level — Luxembourg-resident corporate and non-corporate partners on worldwide income, non-resident partners on Luxembourg-source income attributable to their Luxembourg participation; the SCSp remains fully subject to VAT registration and filing obligations as a taxable person for indirect tax purposes. No Luxembourg tax-free zone or special economic processing zone applies to the SCSp; territoriality is the standard residence/worldwide regime for Luxembourg residents and source-based for non-residents.

Available election forms

(a) §175bis LIR optional election: a Luxembourg commercial partnership (including an SCSp) whose partners are exclusively fully taxable corporate bodies or Luxembourg permanent establishments of foreign companies may irrevocably elect to be treated as a resident capital company, causing opaque entity-level corporation tax (available only upon prior written option, binding on all partners and subsequent years); (b) VAT small-enterprise waiver: renouncement of the Art. 146bis VAT Law €35,000 small-business exemption so as to apply normal VAT and recover input VAT; (c) e-commerce special schemes: election to register under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for intra-EU distance sales and the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) for low-value consignments; (d) transfer pricing and ATAD hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory statutory responses rather than elective check-the-box mechanisms — Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; foreign-law entities are functionally classified as transparent or opaque under domestic law and double-tax-treaty allocation rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State. Under Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, both current and capital transactions are freely convertible and may be repatriated without prior exchange-control approval. There are no exchange-control restrictions on inbound equity subscription, operating working capital, loan repayments, dividend/profit allocations to non-resident partners, or trade settlements. The SCSp is treated as a contractual partnership for Luxembourg tax purposes, so no Luxembourg withholding tax applies on distributions to qualifying non-resident partners. The only legal impediments arise from EU sanctions and asset-freeze measures, anti-money-laundering obligations, and bank KYC requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds from the online sale of garden hydroponics and household gardening equipment. Export receipts may be held freely in EUR or foreign currency and converted at market rates. Banks are required only to report cross-border payment statistics to the BCL under the euro-area balance-of-payments regime for official statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential and AML/CFT supervision for credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL acts as Luxembourg’s central bank and member of the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. An EEA-licensed e-money institution or payment institution registered in the EEA and passporting into Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive may provide euro IBAN accounts, merchant acquiring, online payouts, and limited FX conversion for a cross-border e-commerce treasury. However, EMIs cannot extend commercial credit, offer traditional deposit products, or serve as a full-service corporate bank; funds held through an EMI are safeguarding accounts rather than bank deposits. Offshore third-country EMIs are not viable for routine Luxembourg treasury operations due to EU licensing constraints and CSSF/FIU reporting expectations.

Defense evidence: Current extract from the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE), SCSp partnership deed, notarised limited partnership agreement, full ownership chart, UBO declaration forms, passports and proof of address for all partners, corporate certificates of good standing, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth statements, and a written memorandum explaining the commercial purpose of the partnership.

Root cause: Cross-border payments to suppliers in jurisdictions that differ from the invoicing entity or from the ultimate consignee's country

Defense evidence: Supply contracts, purchase orders, vendor invoices, Incoterms on transport documents, pro-forma commercial invoices, customs and export declarations, ultimate consignee confirmation, bank reference for the SCSp, and a group treasury memorandum explaining the payment routing and containing a sanctions and beneficial-ownership risk assessment.
Initial capital contribution or subscription amount originating from a third-country bank with no prior Luxembourg nexus

Root cause: Initial capital contribution or subscription amount originating from a third-country bank with no prior Luxembourg nexus

Defense evidence: Bank statement or credit confirmation identifying the remitter, board resolution of the SCSp authorising the capital call, partner subscription agreement, partner source-of-funds declaration, underlying commercial documentation supporting the transfer, and a written undertaking to provide complete balance-of-payments documentation to the BCL through the bank if requested.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SCSp must receive partner capital contributions into a bank account opened in its own name at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution. The remitter sends a SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR transfers or a SEPA / SEPA Instant credit transfer for EUR payments, with the payment's remittance information field containing the SCSp's legal name, Luxembourg business registration number, and the reference 'Capital contribution' or 'SCSp equity subscription'. Luxembourg applies no exchange-control permit requirement; however, the bank requires a copy of the capital call, the limited partnership agreement, and identification of the contributor before crediting the funds. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% headline CIT for taxable income exceeding EUR 200,000 (progressive schedule: 15% up to EUR 175,000; 16% on EUR 175,001 - EUR 200,000; 17% above EUR 200,000). Default SCSp status is fiscally transparent: no CIT is charged at the partnership level; each partner is taxed on its allocable share. If the SCSp validly elects to be subject to CIT under Art. 175bis LIR, the aggregate corporate burden in Luxembourg City is 24.94% (17% CIT + 1.19% employment fund surcharge equal to 7% of the CIT base + 6.75% municipal business tax).

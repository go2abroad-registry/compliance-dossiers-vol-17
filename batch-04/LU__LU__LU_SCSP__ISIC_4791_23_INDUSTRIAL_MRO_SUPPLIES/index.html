Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; official e-government portal: myguichet.lu; direct tax portal: impotsdirects.public.lu; VAT portal: enregistrement.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp carrying on taxable economic activity: (1) obtain RCS matricule registration through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés / myguichet.lu if the SCSp has Luxembourg establishment, activity, or certain transactions requiring registration; (2) apply to the AEDT for VAT identification via myguichet.lu using the VAT registration application, submitting the RCS extract, partnership agreement, proof of registered office or non-resident address, manager/partner identity documents, and estimated turnover; (3) AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) a non-established taxable person must, where required, appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before registration; (5) use the assigned number for periodic VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct taxes (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax); partners are taxed directly on their profit shares. For VAT, the SCSp is a separate taxable person to the extent it makes taxable supplies in its own name; if economically inactive, no VAT registration is required. — For a Luxembourg SCSp carrying on taxable economic activity: (1) obtain RCS matricule registration through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés / myguichet.lu if the SCSp has Luxembourg establishment, activity, or certain transactions requiring registration; (2) apply to the AEDT for VAT identification via myguichet.lu using the VAT registration application, submitting the RCS extract, partnership agreement, proof of registered office or non-resident address, manager/partner identity documents, and estimated turnover; (3) AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) a non-established taxable person must, where required, appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before registration; (5) use the assigned number for periodic VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and Intrastat declarations where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct taxes (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax); partners are taxed directly on their profit shares. For VAT, the SCSp is a separate taxable person to the extent it makes taxable supplies in its own name; if economically inactive, no VAT registration is required.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp's transparency is the statutory default. Under Article 175 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law, a qualifying commercial partnership carrying on a commercial activity whose partners are all taxable corporate entities may opt to be treated as a fully taxable entity, subject to AEDT approval and ongoing compliance; the election does not alter VAT treatment. Hybrid mismatch rules are applied to prevent non-taxation where Luxembourg classification differs from foreign classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold for taxable persons established in Luxembourg; any taxable economic activity requires VAT registration. A simplified small-enterprise franchise exemption applies to locally established taxable persons whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000, subject to compliance conditions; for intra-Community distance sales by non-established e-commerce suppliers, the EU EUR 10,000 annual Union distance-selling tolerance applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of industrial MRO goods by a Luxembourg SCSp is eligible for Luxembourg 0% VAT only if the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or for the supplier's account, and the supplier holds conclusive evidence that the goods left the EU no later than 90 days after supply. The customer must be a non-EU purchaser for direct export, or an EU taxable person acting in good faith where an exempt intra-Community dispatch is validly established. The supply must be supported by an accepted customs export declaration and transport proof. Supplies of digital catalog services or services unrelated to the exported goods remain taxable at the applicable Luxembourg rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of Physical Export (CMR/Air Waybill/FIATA or carrier statement)
  • Non-EU Customer Purchase Order or Supply Contract
  • Bank SWIFT/MT103 Payment Confirmation

As of 2025, Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or invoice-by-invoice API approval regime. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL network. From 1 January 2026, domestic B2B e-invoicing will be mandatory for taxpayers established in Luxembourg; invoices must be exchanged in a PEPPOL-compliant structured electronic format and transmitted through the Luxembourg national e-invoicing platform. No pre-clearance or tax-stamping requirement exists, but exchange data must be retained for VAT audit and recapitulative reporting.

The AEDT has broad audit powers to cross-check export declarations, transport documents, bank settlement, and VAT returns with customs data. Where zero-rating cannot be substantiated, the AEDT reassesses output VAT at 17% plus default interest (currently 0.6% per month) and applies administrative fines of up to 10% of the tax for good-faith errors. Fraudulent export claims, forged documents, or circular transactions trigger higher penalties, criminal fines, imprisonment, a clawback period extended to ten years, and joint liability of directors, tax representatives, and service providers; in ordinary cases the clawback period is five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility is guaranteed under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and payments and the euro monetary regime. Luxembourg maintains zero exchange controls; no repatriation, approval, or licensing hurdles exist for dividend, interest, fee, or profit remittances from an SCSp. Remittances are subject only to standard AML/KYC screening and, where applicable, EU sanctions and tax formality obligations (e.g., withholding tax certification, beneficial ownership documentation).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no compulsory conversion ratios, and no repatriation timelines. Euro-denominated and foreign-currency export receipts may be retained freely, held in correspondent accounts, or converted at market rates through any licensed EU payment institution or commercial bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem framework and the European Central Bank (ECB); external financial conduct and AML supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). There is no standalone foreign exchange administration in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High, but subject to strict legal boundaries. Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the CSSF regime may provide multi-currency corporate e-wallets, but they are not licensed to grant credit and often cannot offer full commercial letters of credit or trade instruments. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport are generally not viable for an SCSp due to EBA regulatory expectations, local substance requirements, and bank-correspondent due diligence; unlicensed foreign EMIs must be avoided for treasury liquidity. A CSSF-authorised EMI or an EU-pasporting EMI can serve payment-rail purposes for MRO catalogue collections and disbursements, but the primary operating account must remain at a fully supervised credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the SCSp must be initiated via SWIFT MT103 from the limited partner's bank, with precise value-dating instructions to the Luxembourg counterparty bank (LUX IBAN), and must reference the partnership's capital call notice and the purpose code 'IFZ' or 'General capital contribution to a partnership' in EU statistical terms. Contributions received from non-EU jurisdictions are cleared through the correspondent banking network (e.g., CHIPS, Fedwire) and credited in EUR after any necessary TARGET2 settlement. For funds above €10,000 or from non-EEA parties, banks require a copy of the capital call, identification of the remitter and ultimate originator, and confirmation that the contribution is recorded as paid-in capital in the SCSp ledger. No exchange control permission is required; however, TARGET2/SWIFT message and sanctions screening must clear. SEPA CT (SEPA Credit Transfer) may be used for EUR injections from EEA bank accounts; wire transfer must include legal entity identifier (LEI) or SWIFT BIC of the partnership to avoid blocked credits.Payments to suppliers for industrial MRO procurements are executed by SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) with accompanying commercial documentation (invoice, packing list, contract) available to the remitting bank. Larger contract values and open-account terms may also use documentary letters of credit (Commercial LC, UCP 600) issued by Luxembourg banks, with export/import settlements routed through TARGET2 or major clearing systems. For intra-EU trade, SEPA Direct Debit or SEPA CT is permissible; for non-EU suppliers, the standard rail is an MT103 with appropriate payment purpose code (e.g., 109 for supplies/goods) and a smooth S.W.I.F.T network. Outbound disbursements from the Capital Escrow Account require matching to treasury policies, and over a threshold (typically €50,000 cumulative per day) the bank may request the underlying purchase order and proof of trade to maintain low-risk AML standing.EU-market standard FX Forward contracts with maturity up to 12 months, executed under ISDA/CSSF-compliant master agreements with Luxembourg or OECD counterparty banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for certain excluded currencies where delivery is unavailable; subject to institutional counterparty eligibility and not prohibited by BCL/ECB regulationsCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) for long-term liquidity hedging of multi-currency MRO collectionsFX spot transactions through authorised credit institutions; no mandatory interbank central clearing for micro/mid-sized treasuriesOption-based EUR/USD collars (purchased options) permitted for hedging known procurement exposures, provided no speculative leverage is introduced into the SCSp treasury

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp entity level (tax transparent). For a fully taxable Luxembourg S.A./S.à r.l., the CIT headline rate is 17% plus 7% solidarity surcharge, giving 18.19%; adding Luxembourg City municipal business tax yields a combined top effective rate of approximately 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on SCSp profit distributions because the SCSp is tax transparent and its allocations are partnership profit distributions, not dividends. The standard Luxembourg dividend WHT is 15% for capital companies, reducible to 0% or 5% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and DTA relief where substance and holding-period requirements are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 group consolidated turnover for the Master File; EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions for the Local File; Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory at EUR 750,000,000 group turnover. These threshold references align with Luxembourg's application of Article 56bis LIR and OECD TP Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not enact separate Economic Substance Regulations of the offshore type. However, the doctrine of abuse of law, BEPS minimum standards and EU ATAD GAAR require an SCSp to show genuine business activity, local management decision-making, premises, and a commercial rationale. An SCSp engaged in MRO e-commerce must have real substance; otherwise the tax administration may disregard the entity and reattribute its profits to the place of effective management or to the beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; civil reassessment on presumed income; 10% late-filing surcharge; monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%.

Director personal liability: Managers, authorized signatories and tax representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax. Courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and extradition under EU and mutual legal assistance instruments.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp for tax purposes, reallocation of income to the jurisdiction of effective management, denial of DTA benefits, additional tax assessments, and application of abuse-of-law penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may become personally liable for restored tax and penalties when partnership assets have been stripped or transferred; criminal liability for fraud may include imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income, denial of deductions, penalties up to 10% of the tax deficit (up to 50% for gross negligence and 100% for wilful deceit), and monthly interest of 0.6%.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve materially false TP documentation are jointly liable for additional corporate and municipal tax; serious cases attract criminal fines, imprisonment, and piercing of the SCSp veil.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,250,000 or 10% of annual turnover under Luxembourg AML law; public publication of non-compliance; criminal imprisonment up to five years for money-laundering offenses.

Director personal liability: Natural persons responsible for AML compliance are personally liable; corporate-veil defenses are unavailable in criminal proceedings; foreign authorities may issue travel bans and extradition requests through mutual legal assistance.

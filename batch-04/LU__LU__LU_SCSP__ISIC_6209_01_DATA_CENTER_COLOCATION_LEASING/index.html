Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct income taxes (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (portal: aed.public.lu); Luxembourg Business Registers / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for business registration and the national business number (portal: lbr.lu); single e-administration portal: guichet.lu / myguichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale): (1) execute the partnership agreement (private deed permitted; notarial deed required only in the case of a public offering) and file it with the RCS through a Luxembourg-approved professional within one month of formation; (2) obtain the national business number (matricule / numéro d'identification) from the Luxembourg Business Registers, which serves as the SCSp's registration identifier for tax correspondence and is required for VAT enrolment; (3) lodge the VAT registration application with AEDT (form 102 or via MyGuichet.lu) before commencing taxable colocation/hosting supplies, attaching the RCS extract, the partnership agreement, identification of the general partner and responsible manager, and evidence of the Luxembourg establishment and bank account; (4) receive the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits + check digit; (5) because the SCSp is fiscally transparent for direct taxes, no corporate income tax account is opened in the SCSp's own name, and each partner is taxed individually, with non-resident partners deriving Luxembourg-source income required to obtain an appropriate Luxembourg tax reference; (6) amend the RCS/AEDT registrations within 10 days of any change and, where the SCSp has no fixed establishment in Luxembourg, appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative for compliance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT) and net wealth tax (NWT); profits are attributed to and taxed at the level of the general and limited partners according to their profit-sharing ratios. Luxembourg-resident corporate partners are subject to CIT and MBT on their allocated share; resident individual partners are subject to progressive income tax; non-resident partners are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income (for example, income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg immovable property). For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is an independent taxable person and is entitled and obliged to register, invoice and deduct input VAT in its own name.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; the transparency of the SCSp is statutory and cannot be excluded unilaterally. The following elections and filings are available where relevant: (a) the option to levy VAT on an otherwise exempt lease or supply of immovable property under Article 43(2) of the Luxembourg VAT Law, commonly used for data-centre premises, subject to AEDT notification and tenant qualification; (b) the VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') election for qualifying Luxembourg group entities, available from 1 January 2024; (c) the fiscal unity ('intégration fiscale') election for CIT/MBT purposes among Luxembourg-resident companies with a 95% or greater direct or indirect holding chain, not accessible to the transparent SCSp itself; and (d) targeted transparency and hybrid-mismatch disclosures and documentation under the DAC6 and ATAD 2 rules where the SCSp's classification differs between Luxembourg and a foreign investor jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established foreign taxable persons: any taxable supply in Luxembourg requires VAT registration from the first transaction. For Luxembourg-established businesses, the small-enterprise franchise exemption applies to annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000; once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, or on voluntary surrender of the franchise, VAT registration becomes mandatory. For intra-Community distance sales and B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services supplied by a Luxembourg business to other Member States, the annual pan-EU cross-border distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 applies, below which supplies remain taxable in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to (i) the export of goods physically dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union, subject to proof of exit; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a taxable person in another Member State, subject to the customer's valid EU VAT number and ECSL listing; and (iii) certain services related to movable tangible property or non-EU 'use and enjoyment' scenarios where the customer is a non-taxable person and the effective use takes place outside the EU. For data-centre colocation and hosting services, the B2B place-of-supply rule (customer's establishment) normally removes Luxembourg VAT entirely, with the customer accounting for reverse charge, provided the customer's taxable status and establishment are properly documented. Caution: where the supply is characterised as a supply relating to Luxembourg immovable property (for example, dedicated rack-space leasing tied to a specific site), Luxembourg remains the place of supply, no zero-rating applies, and the standard 17% rate (or the exempt-lease regime with an option to tax) governs the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD lodged with Luxembourg Customs via the ECS/ICS system, containing the MRN number issued at exit);
  • Transport and exit evidence: Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), or air waybill (air);
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (for non-EU arm's length exports) or the corresponding SWIFT MT103 / ACH wire record evidencing receipt of the export proceeds;
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and, where applicable, dual-use or other export licences;
  • For zero-rated cross-border B2B services: written confirmation of the customer's status and place of establishment, including the customer's valid EU VAT registration number or a foreign tax-residence certificate.

Luxembourg does not operate any real-time continuous transaction control (CTC), digital stamping, or pre-clearance API requirement; invoice issuance remains paper/PDF-based, subject to the EU authenticity, integrity and legibility rules. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only to public-supplier (B2G) invoices addressed to Luxembourg contracting authorities, which must be transmitted via the national e-invoicing system using a PEPPOL-compatible format (UBL/CII). For B2B, e-invoicing is voluntary; however, all VAT records must be maintained in a standard electronic audit environment (SAF-T-LU / eCDF accounting file and the statutory VAT register) and must be delivered to the AEDT on request without prior notice.

The AEDT exercises active audit scrutiny over zero-rated exports and reverse-charge claims: the exporter must hold complete proof of export on file at the time the VAT return is filed; otherwise the AEDT will re-tax the supply at the standard 17% rate with retrospective effect. Late tax is subject to default interest of 0.6% per month. Formal infringements, including missing declarations, missing evidence and defective record-keeping, attract fines from EUR 25 to EUR 5,000. In cases of deliberate misstatement or fraudulent zero-rating, fines may be imposed up to 200% of the evaded VAT, and criminal proceedings can lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years; the general partner and managing persons of the SCSp are jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest and penalties. VAT assessments remain subject to the Luxembourg statutory limitation period, generally 5 years, extended to 10 years in the case of intentional wrongdoing or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area jurisdiction with fully convertible current and capital account transactions. Under EU Treaty Articles 63-65 and the Luxembourg law implementing capital movement liberalisation, there are no foreign exchange controls, no prior authorisation for capital inflow or outflow, and no restrictions on profit remittances or partnership distributions. Convertibility is effectively total for EUR and any freely tradable currency. The only practical requirements are ordinary AML/KYC verification, BCL transactional reporting under balance-of-payments rules, and tax compliance documentation where non-resident withholding or substance assessment applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory obligation in Luxembourg to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank claim over export receipts. A data-center colocation operator receiving cross-border rental or hosting payments in EUR or foreign currency may retain, convert, or use such funds freely. Resident entities are required to report cross-border payments for statistical purposes to the BCL when amounts exceed the applicable reporting threshold, using the standard external transactions declaration and appropriate purpose code; such reporting is informational, not a surrender mechanism.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and in cooperation with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the European Central Bank (ECB). The BCL is responsible for monetary stability, payment systems oversight, and external-balance-of-payments statistical reporting; the CSSF supervises credit institutions and compliance conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Established payment and e-money institutions (for example certain PSD2-licensed EMI platforms offering multi-currency IBANs) can provide ancillary euro and foreign-currency settlement, virtual accounts, and payment initiation services. However, under CSSF practice they are not acceptable as the sole treasury rail or as a primary escrow/capital holding bank for a Luxembourg SCSp engaged in commercial leasing. Legal constraints arise from the distinction between e-money safeguarding and bank deposits, the absence of a full banking licence, cross-border outsourcing restrictions, and heightened correspondent-bank due-diligence expectations. Such EMIs may be used only as a secondary collection tool for small operational flows, subject to the operator's liability, safeguarding limitations, and a positive due-diligence outcome by the Luxembourg mandated bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections should be routed directly from the limited partner's own bank account to the SCSp's fully opened Luxembourg bank account using SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 credit transfer with a valid UETR. The beneficiary BIC/IBAN must exactly match the bank account file, and the remittance reference must include a clear capital-call identification number and the words 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' to avoid misclassification. For balance-of-payments reporting, the transaction must be assigned by the reporting bank to the direct-investment / equity-capital category in the BCL external transactions declaration. The SCSp should pre-notify the bank's relationship manager with the capital-call notice, the investor's identity and source-of-funds file, and confirmation that there will be no third-party aggregation; each investor must pay separately. Inbound cross-border wires above the bank's internal threshold may be placed in a temporary escrow/suspense account only if the bank has pre-agreed that treatment; otherwise the funds should remain in the main operating account pending reconciliation.For euro-denominated payments within the EU and EEA, use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer through the Luxembourg bank's TARGET2 access. For non-EUR or non-EEA trade settlements, use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary banking details, UETR tracking, and the relevant commercial invoice or lease reference in Fields 70 and 72. High-value procurement of servers, cooling systems, or electrical equipment may be settled through a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) issued by the Luxembourg bank in favour of a confirmed supplier, using the underlying purchase agreement and supplier performance terms. Subsidiary or recurring data-center lease receipts may be collected via direct debit under SEPA B2B Direct Debit from EU clients, while non-EU clients should initiate SWIFT MT103 or use an approved corporate payment API with same-day reconciliation. All correspondent clearing should use SWIFT gpi to ensure transparent end-to-end payment tracking and to satisfy the bank's AML monitoring obligations.Outright deliverable FX forwards between EUR and USD or other freely convertible currencies, executed under an ISDA 2002 master agreement and credit support annex with a Luxembourg or EU-regulated counterparty, limited to the SCSp's documented commercial exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for hedging underlying receivables or payables in a currency with legal or practical convertibility restrictions, provided the economic exposure is fully evidenced and the NDF is not speculative; settlement may be in EUR or USD.Cross-currency swaps and cross-currency basis swaps to match USD-denominated colocation income or lease liabilities to the EUR functional currency, with tenors aligned to the underlying contracts and EMIR reporting obligations satisfied.Vanilla EUR/USD or EUR/GBP currency options (European-style calls or puts), used strictly for downside protection while allowing upside participation in a hedged commercial transaction.Short-dated FX swaps (swap de change) for temporary liquidity grid management between the SCSp's EUR, USD, and other transactional currencies, under limits approved by the treasury committee and with each leg documented under a money-market confirmation.Exchange-traded EUR/USD futures or cleared forwards, subject to the SCSp being admitted as an eligible clearing client or professional counterparty, used only to hedge net forecast cash flows, with margin and collateral arrangements separately approved by the relationship bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium. Established payment and e-money institutions (for example certain PSD2-licensed EMI platforms offering multi-currency IBANs) can provide ancillary euro and foreign-currency settlement, virtual accounts, and payment initiation services. However, under CSSF practice they are not acceptable as the sole treasury rail or as a primary escrow/capital holding bank for a Luxembourg SCSp engaged in commercial leasing. Legal constraints arise from the distinction between e-money safeguarding and bank deposits, the absence of a full banking licence, cross-border outsourcing restrictions, and heightened correspondent-bank due-diligence expectations. Such EMIs may be used only as a secondary collection tool for small operational flows, subject to the operator's liability, safeguarding limitations, and a positive due-diligence outcome by the Luxembourg mandated bank.

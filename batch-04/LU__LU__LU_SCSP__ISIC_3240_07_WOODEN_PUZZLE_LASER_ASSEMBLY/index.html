Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes, corporate income tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Central electronic platforms: Guichet.lu / myGuichet (national administrative portal), Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) online RCS portal (www.lbr.lu), and the eTVA / eSpace Déclarant (ESD) electronic filing system for VAT returns, listings and declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SCSp by notarial or private deed and file the instrument with the RCS through the LBR/Guichet.lu single window; the SCSp obtains a statutory 15-digit national identification number (matricule / numéro d'identification national), which constitutes the entity TIN and is referenced on the RCS extract; no minimum capital is required. Step 2 - Register for Luxembourg VAT (TVA) with the AEDT before commencing taxable supplies by filing Form 6401 (déclaration d'assujettissement); the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU00000000). Step 3 - Register the SCSp with the ACD through the ESD corporate tax module, file the annual partnership and partner allocation information returns where a Luxembourg-resident general partner or a Luxembourg permanent establishment exists, and obtain confirmation of the fiscally transparent status. Step 4 - For binding certainty on pass-through classification, VAT treatment of export supplies and hybrid mismatch outcomes, file an advance tax ruling request (résolution anticipée) with the ACD before the first taxable transaction; rulings are typically issued within three months. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted, and no separate municipal tax registration exists in this unitary national jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope - The SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg direct taxes: corporate income tax and municipal business tax are assessed at the partner level, and net wealth tax is borne by corporate partners; resident partners are taxed on worldwide income, non-resident partners only on Luxembourg-source income effectively connected with a Luxembourg permanent establishment. For VAT, however, the SCSp is a taxable person in its own name: it registers, issues invoices, files returns and claims input VAT credits and refunds; export zero-rating is claimed in the name of the SCSp.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election, but Article 3(2) LIR allows a partnership (including an SCSp) to elect treatment as an opaque corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the election is filed with the ACD and binds for a minimum of five years. Under the ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch framework as implemented in the LIR, a reverse-hybrid SCSp is recharacterized as a Luxembourg resident taxpayer on its undistributed income where another jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque and the income would otherwise escape taxation; partner-jurisdiction classification outcomes (for example a US Section 7701 check-the-box election on the SCSp) are assessed for hybrid mismatch purposes, and advance tax rulings can confirm the transparency or opacity outcome before operations commence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover exclusive of VAT (small-business 'franchise en base' exemption under Article 57 LTV; EUR 30,000 until 31 December 2024); mandatory VAT registration with the AEDT arises immediately upon exceeding the threshold. A separate EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales of goods and digital services under the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme, above which Luxembourg VAT is chargeable and returnable through the OSS portal; no sub-national indirect tax registration exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched or transported out of the EU customs territory to a third country are taxable at 0% under Article 56 LTV, provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days of dispatch and the exporter holds the transport and customs evidence; related input VAT on production (CO2 laser cutting, wood and plywood inputs, packaging, logistics) is deductible and any excess input VAT credit is reimbursed by the AEDT in the periodic VAT refund cycle. Services supplied to non-EU business customers (design, assembly services, software, royalties and related technical services) fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rules of Article 17 LTV, with the customer's country applying reverse charge or local assessment; proof of the customer's non-EU establishment and valid VAT identification is required. Export operations are not subject to Luxembourg customs duties on exit, and zero-rating entitlement is substantiated through the export documentation bundle and the inward remittance evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A export formalities lodged with the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration under the EU Customs Code; retained copy of the electronic export declaration and the export summary)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill, stamped or signed by the transporter, evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - bank credit advice, SWIFT/MT103 or account statement evidencing final settlement of export proceeds within the repatriation window (customary export-proof requirement of the AEDT and Luxembourg banks, notwithstanding the absence of statutory exchange controls)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) platform, digital stamping or invoice authorization regime applies in Luxembourg. A B2B e-invoicing mandate takes effect on 1 January 2026: every Luxembourg-established taxable person must issue structured electronic invoices in the EU standard format (XML / EN 16931) for domestic B2B supplies taxable in Luxembourg, transmit them electronically with interoperability through the PEPPOL network, and retain the machine-readable invoice and transmission trail. Non-compliance attracts administrative fines under the LTV and creates documentary deficiencies in export-refund and audit review procedures.

The AEDT and ACD conduct ex-post documentary audits of export supplies; on request, the exporter must produce the complete evidence bundle within the statutory response period, generally one month, extendable on justified grounds. Failure of proof retroactively revokes the 0% rate: 17% Luxembourg VAT is assessed on the export invoice, late-payment interest accrues from the original VAT return due date, and the clawback is executed through rectified VAT returns. Assessment and clawback rights lapse after five years, extended to ten years where fraud is established. Intentional or materially false zero-rating claims, forged transport documentation, or schemes under which the goods never left the EU are prosecuted as tax fraud under the LTV: administrative fines from EUR 250 to EUR 25,000, criminal fines up to double the evaded VAT, and imprisonment of up to five years for aggravated fraud; collateral consequences include refusal or withdrawal of tax clearance certificates, enhanced multi-year audit cycles before both tax administrations, and de-risking action by payment processors such as Stripe and Amazon Pay.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg enjoys full and unrestricted currency convertibility as a euro-area member state. The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender. There are no exchange-control laws, no requirement for central-bank approval of cross-border capital movements, and no restriction on capital-account transactions, as the free movement of capital, payments, and investment is guaranteed by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Profit distribution and capital repatriation by an SCSp to non-resident partners (including US, Asian, and other non-EU investors) may be effected without prior BCL or CSSF authorisation, provided that the payee's beneficial ownership is fully documented and that no EU/UN/OFAC sanctions list hits are triggered. The only compliance overlay is the European macro-prudential statistical framework: banks automatically report cross-border transactions on behalf of resident clients to the BCL Balance of Payments (BOP/ITRS) reporting system for any payment of EUR 12,500 or above; such reporting is self-executing by the banking counterparty and does not impose a client-specific authorisation burden.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules in Luxembourg. A Luxembourg exporter (including an SCSp selling wooden puzzles through e-commerce rails or wholesale trade channels) is under no obligation to repatriate, convert, or deposit export receipts into a domestic account within any prescribed timeline. Export proceeds denominated in foreign currency (e.g., USD, GBP, CNY) may be retained freely in foreign-currency accounts or converted into EUR at commercial market rates selected by the treasurer. The only applicable duties are documentary: the SCSp must reflect export proceeds in its VAT returns (where the place of supply is B2C within the EU or intra-EU distance sales), maintain transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border related-party trade, and preserve underlying customs/invoice evidence for bank BOP coding verification.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), a constituent member of the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; prudential banking supervision within the Grand Duchy is exercised jointly by the BCL and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which acts as the AML/CFT supervisory authority and licensing body for banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-High, with structural limitations. A Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensed by the CSSF under the amended Law of 21 December 2009, or an EEA EMI passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2, can provide the SCSp with EUR IBANs, multi-currency virtual accounts, SWIFT/SEPA payment initiation, and FX conversion at transparent rates, and can settle Stripe/Amazon Payouts efficiently. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot grant credit, cannot provide documentary letters of credit, cannot hold client funds for more than a limited safeguarded period, and is not an acceptable venue for partner capital escrow where third-party investor protection is expected. UK-authorised and non-EEA EMIs (including US money transmitters) may not onboard a Luxembourg SCSp after the Brexit transition without a Luxembourg branch or an EEA passport, and any account servicing that suggests deposit-taking outside the EMI perimeter triggers CSSF enforcement. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City aggregate rate: 17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + municipal business tax). The SCSp is fiscally transparent; the rate applies to corporate partners/general partner on their taxable share of profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on actual profit distributions by an SCSp because of fiscal transparency; where a Luxembourg company pays a dividend to a non-resident corporate partner outside the participation exemption, the domestic rate is 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 local net turnover triggers a full Luxembourg Local File; group consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 triggers Master File and CbC Report. Below these thresholds, a contemporaneous Luxembourg TP file is nevertheless strongly recommended.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but applies the OECD principal purpose test, EU ATAD anti-abuse provisions, and the domestic abuse-of-law doctrine. An SCSp must demonstrate real economic activity in Luxembourg (office, staff, decision-making, risk control) and commercial substance to preserve tax transparency and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Article 396 of the General Tax Law (AO): imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 25,000, plus reassessment of unpaid taxes with interest and a late-filing surcharge of up to 10%.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors who knowingly execute the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the tax debt, subject to civil restitution orders, exit bans from Luxembourg during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: The ACD may disregard the legal form, refuse treaty benefits, recharacterize the income, deny the participation exemption, impose dividend/interest withholding taxes on deemed distributions, and impose administrative fines up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally responsible for the tax re-established after disregard of the entity, may face disqualification as managers, asset seizures, travel bans, and criminal liability if the arrangement is deemed fraudulent.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 under the Luxembourg AML framework and RBE law; criminal sanctions for money laundering include imprisonment of up to 5 years; RBE registration can be suspended and bank account freezing/blocking orders may be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML compliance failures; criminal fines and imprisonment apply if wilful money-laundering is proven; personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and bans from serving as company directors are common ancillary sanctions.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The ACD may adjust taxable profits to an arm's-length level, apply default interest, impose documentation penalties up to EUR 5,000, and, if concealment is established, initiate criminal tax proceedings with fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who are signatories to the intercompany agreements and responsible for TP documentation may be jointly liable for the primary tax adjustment plus interest; travel bans, management disqualification, and imprisonment are possible in cases of deliberate tax evasion.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Luxembourg VAT and indirect tax authority; digital portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu; direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD); business register: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR, lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — File RCS incorporation of the SCSp through a Luxembourg notary/Etablissement de Crédit Professionnel (ECP) and obtain the RCS identifier from Luxembourg Business Registers; Step 2 — Obtain the national identification number (matricule) from the Registre national des personnes morales via the LBR; Step 3 — Submit VAT registration application (eForm via MyGuichet.lu or paper Form 642) to AEDT, indicating 'assujetti non établi' status where no Luxembourg permanent establishment exists; Step 4 — Appoint a jointly liable tax representative ('répondant responsable') established in Luxembourg where the SCSp or its general partner is established outside the EEA; Step 5 — Receive the LU + 8-digit VAT identification number (LU VAT ID) within approximately 2–4 weeks; non-established taxable persons must register before the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg without any turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp has no separate legal personality and is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate and personal income tax (each partner is taxed on its distributive share of income); for VAT purposes, the SCSp is nevertheless recognized as an independent taxable person (assujetti) acting in its own name and registers under its own LU VAT number; no entity-level corporate income tax or municipal business tax is levied at the SCSp level, and foreign partners are not subject to Luxembourg income tax unless the SCSp creates a Luxembourg permanent establishment for them.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style 'check-the-box'; the SCSp's transparent treatment is mandatory and cannot be waived. Available elections relevant to the structure: (i) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 7 of the VAT Law for Luxembourg-established entities sharing financial, economic, and organizational links; (ii) joint and several liability designations in the partnership agreement; (iii) ATAD/hybrid-mismatch alignment declarations under the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2018 (elections on inclusion of income, deduction allocation, and symmetry of transparent versus non-transparent treatment); (iv) option for the EU distance-sales regime and the EUR 10,000 intra-Community threshold; (v) VAT deferral/postponement election for importation of raw cane sugar under the Luxembourg customs deferral regime (Article 51 of the VAT Law). No election exists to render the SCSp opaque for corporate income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business franchise exemption, applicable only to taxable persons established in Luxembourg; non-established foreign suppliers enjoy no exemption threshold and must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg; intra-Community acquisition threshold for Luxembourg taxable persons: EUR 10,000 per annum; import VAT on VHP raw cane sugar (CN 1701.13) is due on importation at the standard rate but may be deferred and simultaneously deducted by a registered SCSp under the postponement regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the physical export supply of VHP raw cane sugar to destinations outside the EU customs territory provided: (i) the sugar is dispatched or transported to a third country by the SCSp, its agent, or the buyer's carrier; (ii) transport commences and the goods leave the EU within 90 days of the supply date; (iii) an accepted customs export declaration (standard export declaration / EX-A document in the DUO customs single window) is issued in the SCSp's name; (iv) commercial export evidence (B/L, CMR or AWB, commercial invoice, packing list, bank or LC payment traces) is retained; (v) the SCSp holds a valid LU VAT ID and reflects the transaction on its EC Sales List where the movement is intra-Community. Services directly connected with the export (ocean freight, stevedoring, inspection, handling) are either outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply outside the EU under Articles 53 and 59 of the VAT Law) or zero-rated as export-related services. Sugar shipped directly Brazil-to-third-country without entering the EU is outside Luxembourg VAT entirely; inward Luxembourg costs (freight, brokerage, warehousing) are subject to the reverse-charge mechanism and recoverable as input VAT by the registered SCSp.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / EX-A) validated in the Luxembourg DUO customs single window evidencing EU exit
  • Negotiable ocean Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) naming the SCSp as exporter/shipper of record
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and weight/polarization certificate (ICUMSA analysis) evidencing VHP raw cane sugar under HS 1701.13
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 wire record or Revolving LC draw-down instrument evidencing receipt of the export proceeds
  • EC Sales List, Intrastat declaration (where applicable), insurance certificate, and certificate of origin supporting the complete supply chain

Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing under the Luxembourg law of 16 March 2023: Peppol BIS 3.0 / EN 16931 electronic format, phased obligation to receive e-invoices: (i) from 1 January 2026 for taxable persons with turnover above EUR 100 million; (ii) from 1 January 2027 for turnover between EUR 10 million and EUR 100 million; (iii) from 1 January 2028 for all Luxembourg VAT-registered persons. No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls (CTC) reporting to AEDT, and no digital stamping requirement — the model is Peppol-network exchange with a 10-year post-audit document retention mandate.

AEDT audits place the burden of proof on the exporter to demonstrate physical exit of the goods within 90 days. Missing, contradictory, or forged export documentation triggers retrospective reclassification of the supply as taxable at 17%, recovery (clawback) of any input VAT deducted in the export chain, statutory monthly late-interest on the assessed arrears, and fines of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 per offence under Articles 64–65 of the VAT Law of 12 February 1979. Fraudulent zero-rating (forged B/L or customs stamps, circular cargo, fictitious buyers) exposes the SCSp and its general partner (associé gérant) to criminal tax-fraud prosecution with imprisonment under Luxembourg criminal law and Article 408 of the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), joint and several liability of the general partner, disgorgement of the entire tax benefit, and compound penalties. Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, 180-day settlement is the documentary-credit benchmark (UCP 600) and the arm's-length transfer-pricing norm under OECD guidelines, and longer deferrals without commercial justification may attract recharacterization and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full and unrestricted euro convertibility on both current and capital account pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the Luxembourg law of 19 December 2002 on the register of credit institutions, and the consolidated law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector. No foreign-exchange authorization, conversion quota, prior approval, or dedicated licence is required for an SCSp to receive capital contributions in EUR, USD, BRL, or any freely convertible currency, nor to remit dividends, profit shares, trade settlements, or loan repayments. The SCSp is a tax-transparent partnership without legal personality by virtue of the Law of 10 August 1915 as amended by the Law of 23 July 2016; consequently, no Luxembourg withholding tax is imposed on profit-sharing distributions to non-resident partners, and remittance is blocked only by EU/UN sanctions screening and CSSF anti-money-laundering checks. The practical convertibility assessment is therefore unrestricted for legitimate commodity-trade treasury flows related to wholesale raw cane sugar shipment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for physical cargo receipts. A Luxembourg SCSp engaged in wholesale raw cane sugar may leave USD, EUR, or third-currency trade receivables offshore or retain them in multi-currency account balances at any Luxembourg or EEA credit institution without obtaining BCL consent. The only operational encumbrance is the resident bank's duty under the BCL balance-of-payments statistical framework to report cross-border transactions and positions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, and the obligation under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 to attach complete originator and beneficiary information to every wire transfer.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) exercising domestic monetary-policy operations within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision and the AML/CFT oversight of credit institutions and payment institutions are carried out by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The Ministry of Finance is the competent national authority for capital-movement statistics and crypto-asset reporting, while the BCL collects balance-of-payments data from resident banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for the required clearing rails. A Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution under the amended Law of 10 August 1915 and the PSD2 framework is legally prohibited from granting trade credit, issuing or confirming documentary Letters of Credit, or operating a revolving-credit facility; EMIs can only provide e-money wallets and PSD2 payment-account execution. Since the SCSp treasury blueprint is dominated by Revolving LC issuance and ship-payment settlements under SWIFT MT700/MT103, a full-scope credit institution with a Luxembourg banking licence is mandatory. Non-EU offshore EMIs are also operationally blocked by CSSF outsourcing circulars, the register-of-beneficial-owners transparency rules, and the prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking, which confines offshore EMIs to incidental expense payments only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital is paid into the SCSp operational or dedicated capital-escrow account via SWIFT MT103, MT202, or MT202COV from the partner's bank, with the payment order stating the number and date of the capital call clause of the partnership agreement. The transaction is classified for BCL statistical purposes as direct investment (European balance-of-payments code EC/P001) and is transmitted through TARGET2 for EUR or through the bank's New York correspondent for USD. No prior BCL, CSSF, or Ministry of Finance approval is required for the injection. The paying and beneficiary banks must each meet Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 originator and beneficiary data requirements; for contributions exceeding EUR 12,500 the Luxembourg bank records the transfer in the BCL BoP reporting system. For a non-EEA partner, the bank will file the transaction under the multi-currency capital contribution stream and may ask for an audited source-of-funds pack; no stamp duty, capital duty, or inward-investment fee is assessed on the SCSp.Outbound payments for physical VHP raw cane sugar are executed through three complementary rails. Primary rail: revolving Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Luxembourg credit institution under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700/MT707, advising through the supplier's bank and settled at sight through SWIFT MT103 or through deferred-payment acceptance and refinancing in the correspondent banking network. Secondary rail: documentary collection under SWIFT MT400 for CIF or CFR shipments where LC cover is not required. Tertiary rail: controlled direct wire transfers via SWIFT MT103 for advance payments, post-shipment balances, or demurrage and inspection charges under a Master Sugar Purchase Agreement. EUR payments clear through TARGET2 with immediate finality; USD payments clear through CHIPS/FedWire and require 48-hour liquidity pre-funding in the correspondent account; payments in BRL, THB, or INR are routed via member bank correspondents in the local clearing systems. All outbound transfers are pre-screened against EU and OFAC sanctions and are reported for BCL statistical aggregates; no exchange-control declaration is required.Deliverable EUR/USD outright forwards with a maximum tenor of 24 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a local bank master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing BRL PTAX, THB Bangkok Fix, or INR RBI reference rates to hedge the local-currency cost components of raw cane sugar logistics and mill settlement.Cross-currency basis swaps (EUR/USD) to convert USD trade proceeds into EUR capital-call and distribution liquidity.Zero-premium FX collar options (long put/short call) on EUR/USD for protection of the SCSp's gross margin without upfront premium cash flow.FX rollover swaps under the same bank master agreement for straddling the LC settlement dates and the physical vessel laycan windows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for the required clearing rails. A Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution under the amended Law of 10 August 1915 and the PSD2 framework is legally prohibited from granting trade credit, issuing or confirming documentary Letters of Credit, or operating a revolving-credit facility; EMIs can only provide e-money wallets and PSD2 payment-account execution. Since the SCSp treasury blueprint is dominated by Revolving LC issuance and ship-payment settlements under SWIFT MT700/MT103, a full-scope credit institution with a Luxembourg banking licence is mandatory. Non-EU offshore EMIs are also operationally blocked by CSSF outsourcing circulars, the register-of-beneficial-owners transparency rules, and the prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking, which confines offshore EMIs to incidental expense payments only.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined corporate income tax 17.00%, solidarity surcharge 7% of CIT, and Luxembourg City municipal business tax 6.75%), before any credits; the standard business tax may be reduced to 18.94% for holding companies under special regimes, but for a commercial wholesale SCSp the combined rate stands at 24.94% when taxable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; however, for a transparent SCSp, profit distributions to partners are generally not treated as dividends and no Luxembourg WHT applies if the SCSp does not have Luxembourg corporate personality. Where a corporate partner is an SA/S.à r.l. investing through an SCSp, the 15% WHT may apply and may be reduced to 0% under the holding/inclusion exemption in the parent-child directive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg statutory threshold for Country-by-Country reporting is EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue. For Local File and Master File, the ACD follows OECD principles; prudent threshold for mandatory documentation in practice is aggregate controlled cross-border transaction value exceeding EUR 500,000 per year.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies a de facto economic-substance doctrine through its GAAR (Article 6 AO), the EU ATAD principal purpose test, and administrative Circulars L.L. 2021/02 requiring substance for treaty benefits. For SCSp used in a commodity arbitrage structure, no statutory ESR regime exists, but the entity must demonstrate real activity, decision-making in Luxembourg, risks borne, and no letterbox arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to 50% of the evaded tax; criminal convictions carry imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and a fine of EUR 5,000 to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors personally joint and several for the full amount of evaded tax plus penalties if they knowingly assisted or caused the filing; court can order restitution, travel bans, and prohibitions from managing Luxembourg entities.
Artificial Shell Entity Counterfeit Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence certificate; income reallocation to the effective management jurisdiction; potential nullification of treaty benefits and 100% assessment on the foreign principal plus interest at 3% per month.

Director personal liability: If the director signed the statute declaring a fake activity, they face loss-of-subsidy, debarment from the local commercial register, and personal liability for the undistributed assets under company law sec. 720-1 et seq.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: ACD adjustment to arm's length profit, surcharge of 10% for voluntary disclosure failure, and 1% per month war interest on under-payment; may be recharacterized as hidden dividend with 15% withholding over cash forms.

Director personal liability: Director may be personally liable for a transfer-pricing adjustment if the intercompany price was fixed with intent to avoid tax, and under the Commercial Code they are liable to the company and third parties for abusive management.
AML-Forfeited Gains and Substance Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1 million and penal confiscation; senior officers can be disqualified for up to five years; criminal penalties under law of 14 November 2014.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable to the company for failure to implement AML controls; travel bans may be ordered during penal proceedings.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) is the Luxembourg indirect tax authority; direct income and corporate tax affairs are handled by Administration des contributions directes (ACD). The unified electronic portal is MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity performing taxable activities in Luxembourg without a Luxembourg establishment must apply directly to the AEDT for a VAT identification number using the standard application form, supported by proof of identity, articles of incorporation, confirmation of foreign VAT registration, and evidence of the taxable activity. If required, a Luxembourg fiscal representative must be appointed. Luxembourg resident entities receive a national business number at RCS registration and obtain a separate VAT number through the AEDT upon filing the application after registration or before commencing taxable operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity performing taxable activities in Luxembourg without a Luxembourg establishment must apply directly to the AEDT for a VAT identification number using the standard application form, supported by proof of identity, articles of incorporation, confirmation of foreign VAT registration, and evidence of the taxable activity. If required, a Luxembourg fiscal representative must be appointed. Luxembourg resident entities receive a national business number at RCS registration and obtain a separate VAT number through the AEDT upon filing the application after registration or before commencing taxable operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is disregarded as a separate taxpayer for Luxembourg direct tax purposes; there is no general check-the-box-type election. Tax transparency is the default statutory classification. For VAT purposes, the SCSp is treated as a separate taxable person. Advance tax rulings confirming transparency are available through the Luxembourg tax administration. Hybrid mismatch rules in Luxembourg domestic legislation may recharacterise a transparent partnership if a foreign jurisdiction treats the same SCSp as taxable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The Luxembourg domestic VAT registration threshold is EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover. For intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C e-services, the mandatory VAT registration threshold for non-resident EU suppliers is EUR 10,000 of annual cross-border turnover unless the supplier elects to charge local VAT via the One-Stop Shop (OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported or dispatched to any non-EU destination if the exporter retains documentary evidence that the goods left the EU. Supply must be made to a customer with a permanent business destination outside the EU, and title must pass; e-commerce parcels shipped directly to non-EU consumers qualify if the seller holds proof of export and transport. Intra-EU B2C e-commerce sales are not zero-rated and are instead taxable in the destination member state via OSS. Offshore services relating to the fast-fashion retail business are generally outside scope unless physically used in Luxembourg.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic ECS declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice assigned to the first non-EU buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Luxembourg/EU
  • Packing List and proof of consignment matching the customs declaration
  • Third-country customs entry documentation or electronic transport tracking record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of payment from the non-EU buyer

Luxembourg does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance of e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is required for suppliers to the Luxembourg public sector using the PEPPOL format. Private B2B and B2C e-invoices must satisfy VAT invoice content and archiving requirements but are not subject to mandatory clearance. OSS digital filings are made via the national OSS portal.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may perform retrospective audits within the standard five-year assessment window and assess under-declared VAT at 17% plus monthly interest of 0.6%. Where zero-rating is claimed without substantiating export documentation, the supply is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply, resulting in full VAT liability, input credit disallowance, administrative fines up to EUR 10,000, and in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal penalties including imprisonment up to five years and fines of up to EUR 50,000. Proceeds not repatriated within 180 days of export invoice maturity are subject to heightened anti-abuse scrutiny and may trigger retroactive clawback of export deductions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible and is the sole legal tender of Luxembourg. Luxembourg applies no exchange-control regime; unrestricted free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed under Article 63 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC). Both current-account flows (e-commerce sales proceeds, trade disbursements) and capital-account flows (equity contributions, partner loans, profit distributions, liquidation surplus) are permitted without prior authorization, and there are no profit-remittance hurdles for a non-resident-owned SCSp. The only compliance friction is BoP statistical reporting: every cross-border transaction, including SEPA and SEPA Instant transfers, must carry a BCL purpose code captured by the servicing bank, with third-country transactions reported by the bank on a monthly basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg and the Eurozone impose no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandated repatriation period, and no minimum conversion ratio for foreign-currency receipts. The SCSp may retain USD, GBP, and CNY e-commerce revenue in domestic multi-currency sub-accounts or in offshore settlement accounts and convert only as dictated by operational cash-flow needs.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises Luxembourg banks, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions, while BCL collects balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical data via resident credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Moderate-to-high as a secondary collection and settlement rail; Low as the sole treasury account. Luxembourg-licensed entities operating under PSD2, such as PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. & Cie S.C.A. and Six Payment Services Luxembourg, are acceptable for merchant settlement and BNPL reconciliation. EU/EEA payment institutions and EMIs may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 without a branch, and Luxembourg banks will normally accept settlement flows from passported EMIs. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business, Multitude) may be used only for foreign-currency supplier payouts and never as the main treasury repository, because an unlicensed or non-passported EMI arrangement exposes the SCSp to CSSF regulatory scrutiny and degrades its Low_Risk_Standard AML classification. Contractual structuring must ensure that end-of-day balances are swept to a Luxembourg-licensed credit institution before onward disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections must be routed for the account of the SCSp and executed by SWIFT MT103, or SEPA Credit Transfer where the remitter holds EUR, to the Luxembourg concentration account at a BCL-reporting bank. The transfer must reference the SCSp name, the general partner, and a unique injection reference (e.g., SCSP-CAP-001). The bank will assign a BoP purpose code from the code families defined under Regulation (EU) No 184/2005, typically one covering direct or other investment equity and loans; the servicer must confirm the exact code at onboarding. For each injection the bank file must contain: (i) a signed capital-call or loan-drawdown notice; (ii) the relevant partnership agreement or resolution; (iii) UBO confirmation for the funding limited partner; and (iv) proof of origin of funds for transactions above EUR 100,000. Where the injection originates from a jurisdiction with outbound capital controls, such as China's SAFE or India's FEMA, the remitting partner must obtain the home-country outbound investment approval before transfer, and a copy must be deposited with the Luxembourg bank.EU supplier payments are cleared via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) through EBA STEP2 or TIPS with near-real-time settlement. Non-EU suppliers are paid by SWIFT MT103 GPI in the invoice currency (EUR, USD, or GBP) with full structured remittance advice and a BoP trade purpose code. For large wholesale purchase orders above EUR 250,000, the SCSp should issue an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit via the account bank's trade desk using MT700/MT707 messages, or a documentary collection (cash against documents) for established suppliers. Supplier advances are prohibited unless covered by a standby LC or a contractual trade-finance facility. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% for FY2024; 18% for FY2025 onward; plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax (Luxembourg City base rate 6.75%) producing aggregate burden of 24.94% (FY2024) and 26.01% (FY2025) for corporate partners. The SCSp itself, as a fiscally transparent partnership, is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 months and meeting substance conditions; SCSp distributions are generally outside Luxembourg dividend WHT where the partnership is transparent and the distribution is a return of capital or profit share.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000: annual related-party transactions exceeding this threshold trigger the duty to prepare, maintain, and produce on request contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File); consolidated groups with revenues of EUR 750 million or more must also prepare a Master File and CbC Report.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations but imposes substance requirements through ATAD general anti-abuse rules, the Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse clause, PE attribution rules, and administrative circulars on holding companies. A fast-fashion e-commerce SCSp claiming tax benefits must demonstrate real local activity: strategic management decisions in Luxembourg, employees, office premises, e-commerce platform oversight, inventory and fulfilment control, bank accounts, and compliance records; absence of substance enables recharacterization of the SCSp as a foreign-managed shell and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Evasion surcharge of 50% of evaded tax plus monthly late interest of 0.6%; criminal tax fraud penalties up to EUR 250,000 fine and 1-5 years imprisonment, extended to 10 years with aggravating circumstances; forced tax assessment and seizure of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and fines where they controlled the filing and concealed facts; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification orders, with imprisonment up to 10 years.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: ATAD GAAR recharacterization of the arrangement, denial of Luxembourg treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits, reallocation of profits to the foreign beneficial owner's jurisdiction, reassessment with penalties of 10%-50% of additional tax, and possible compulsory liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for re-assessed taxes, deregistration of the entity, travel restrictions pending investigation, and personal fines for participating in artificial designs.
Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Arm's length primary adjustment, secondary recharacterization of payments as constructive dividends or equity contributions, monthly interest at 0.6%, penalty of 10%-50% of the reassessed tax, and application of CFC rules to redirect passive income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation face personal civil restitution, joint and several liability for the group companies' tax debts, and criminal liability for falsification of accounting records.
AML and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law imposes imprisonment of 5-10 years (aggravated up to 10 years), fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or five times the value of laundered assets, confiscation of bank accounts and goods, forced liquidation, and publication of sanctions.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced in money laundering cases: directors, officers, and the general partner incur joint restitution obligations, travel bans, and freezing orders; imprisonment applies to individuals knowingly directing the laundering scheme.

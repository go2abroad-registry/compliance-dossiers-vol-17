Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, including Article 1 and Articles 143–154 (limited partnerships), and as supplemented by the Law of 12 July 2013 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp).

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Agreement (Acte de Société / Contrat de Société) filed electronically with the RCS; publication extract lodged in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an SCSp; no statutory escrow account requirement applies. Partner capital commitments and contribution obligations are defined exclusively in the limited partnership agreement and are documented in the partner capital accounts.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDV) for VAT; central filing portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and RCS registration via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first file incorporation documents and register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via a certified filing intermediary or notary if capital is introduced. Following RCS registration, the entity obtains a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) from the Luxembourg State Register. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 300 / 400 VAT) with the AEDV, providing the matricule, registered office address, activity description, and supporting constitutional documents. Non-resident entities acting through no local establishment must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT if required by the AEDV. Income tax registration is effected through the ACD upon receipt of the matricule and may require a specific direct tax file reference for partnership returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is statutorily tax-transparent under Luxembourg direct tax law; no general check-the-box election exists analogous to the US, but may be regarded as a hybrid entity under foreign tax election rules, triggering Luxembourg anti-hybrid (ATAD) analysis. The partners may align fiscal treatment through partnership agreement allocations subject to Luxembourg substance and arm's-length principles. No corporate income tax election is available to a transparent SCSp.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-enterprise exemption; no registration threshold applies to non-established foreign entities, intra-EU acquisitions, or supplies of electronically supplied services to non-taxable EU persons, which trigger mandatory Luxembourg VAT registration or OSS/MOSS use

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when goods leave Luxembourg/EU customs territory in an export transaction evidenced by the Single Administrative Document (SAD) and received by a customer outside the EU. The supplier must prove the physical removal from the EU within the statutory time frame, hold transport and title-transfer documentation, and maintain an export declaration. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers may also be zero-rated under article 38 of the Luxembourg VAT law if the customer supplies a valid EU-VAT identification number and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. Offshore services, including SaaS and SEO analytics digital services, fall under the B2B general place-of-supply rule: if the customer is a taxable business outside Luxembourg/EU, the place of supply is outside Luxembourg, and the supply is outside the VAT scope; if the customer is an EU/global taxable person, the supply is taxable at the customer's jurisdiction under reverse-charge, and Luxembourg zero-rating/refund treatment may require supporting professional-services documentation and service recipient VAT status.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD) for goods exports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport evidence
  • Sales invoice with export reference
  • Purchase order and contract of sale
  • Proof of payment / bank transfer records (often including inward remittance certificate, where available)
  • Certification of the receiving entity's non-EU status or EU VAT number for intra-EU supplies
  • Proof of delivery to non-EU destination (POD, warehouse records, or customs clearing agent report)

Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing through the Luxembourg government e-invoicing platform (Peppol / Factur-X) for public procurement; no general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API clearance regime exists for B2B invoices. Traditional VAT invoices remain valid; e-invoicing is optional in B2B under private agreement, subject to authenticity-of-origin and integrity-of-content requirements.

Luxembourg VAT authorities apply Article 84 and related provisions of the law of 17 February 2012: failure to substantiate export zero-rating gives rise to immediate VAT clawback, interest at the statutory late-payment rate, and penalties up to 50% of the evaded VAT where abuse or offender intent is found. Knowingly issuing false export declarations or fraudulent e-invoicing may trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under Luxembourg tax procedural law. Documentary evidence must be retained for at least six years; in the event of export refund audits related to a third-country entity, the AEDV may request additional business-meaning and substance documentation under anti-fraud measures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro-area convertibility applies in Luxembourg. Both current-account and capital-account operations are free under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), transposed into Luxembourg law without exchange-control restrictions. There is no requirement to obtain central-bank approval for capital injections, profit distributions, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds of an SCSp. The SCSp is eligible for tax transparency under Luxembourg law, so profits allocated to resident or non-resident limited partners are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax and can be remitted abroad once the partnership accounts evidence the profit allocation. Practical hurdles are documentary and statistical rather than regulatory: credit institutions must complete AML/KYC client files under the amended Law of 12 November 2004, and the BCL requires banks to report cross-border payments and positions in the balance-of-payments statistical framework at the Eurosystem transaction-level threshold (currently EUR 12,500). No capital stay-in, local-investment mandate, convertibility licensing, or repatriation lock-up applies to a revenue-generating MarTech SaaS business held by an SCSp.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline on export receipts, including digital subscription and analytics-licence revenue collected through Stripe, Paddle, or direct SWIFT MT103 credits. Foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained in multi-currency corporate accounts at a Luxembourg credit institution or converted into EUR at freely negotiated commercial rates. The only applicable controls for digital export services are the buyer-side VAT/GST registration rules and the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) filing obligations, neither of which constitutes a foreign-exchange control. Banks may record the underlying commercial invoice for BCL balance-of-payments statistics, but no surrender certificate or BCL clearance is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), Luxembourg's central monetary authority and a member of the Eurosystem; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended, and CSSF Regulation No 12-02 of 14 December 2012. The Ministry of Finance retains policy oversight for foreign-exchange and monetary affairs; neither the BCL nor the CSSF operates a prior-authorisation foreign-exchange control regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg requires mandatory Local File documentation only when the group has at least 50 employees and either an annual turnover of EUR 10 million or a balance-sheet total above EUR 10 million; full documentation (including Master File) is triggered when the group has at least 250 employees, an annual turnover above EUR 50 million, and a balance-sheet total above EUR 43 million; the Local File threshold is EUR 10 million in annual revenues, which is the relevant trigger for the SCSp’s SEO analytics tool; failure to file the prescribed documentation after formal request triggers a penalty of EUR 2,500 to EUR 25,000 per file, and the tax administration may shift presumptions in transfer pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose separate Economic Substance Regulations in a manner analogous to UAE ESR, but the 2019 Luxembourg tax reform added a substance requirement in articles 168bis and 231 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law, requiring that entities benefiting from tax treaties, participation exemptions, or hybrid mismatch relief maintain locally present personnel, premises, and effective decision-making; the OECD’s substance concept is applied through exchange-of-information rulings and the GAAR under article 6 AbgO, so the SCSp that performs algorithmic content analytics through Luxembourg-based staff and infrastructure will meet the minimum substance threshold, while a pure letterbox company with no local economic activity will be disregarded for treaty purposes.

Statutory sanction: Repayment of the entire concealed tax plus interest at 0.6% per month; a tax adjustment penalty of 10% of the evaded tax or EUR 25,000 for gross negligence; criminal tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and a fine of EUR 250 to EUR 50,000 under articles 129 and 130 of the Luxembourg general tax law.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners of the SCSp are jointly and severally liable for tax debts of the partnership where they personally participated in the concealment, where the corporate body has been dissolved without sufficient assets, or where the general partner has knowingly organised the false allocation; civil restitution of the tax plus judicial interest, travel bans under criminal procedure, and, in aggravated fraud cases, imprisonment of the natural person up to five years.
Shell_Company_and_Artificial_Routing

Statutory sanction: Disapplication of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test of the Multilateral Convention (BEPS MLI), reclassification of the entire subscription stream as Luxembourg-source income, a 10% gross-up assessment, and fines under article 6 AbgO for abusive tax structures; civil penalties up to EUR 25,000 and refusal of the territorial advance tax ruling.

Director personal liability: The director or general partner who signs the annual accounts knowing that the partnership lacks substance may be personally assessed for the additional tax as a representative taxpayer, ordered to restore the funds distributed on a sham basis, and be subjected to a five-year directorship ban in Luxembourg if the filing was made in bad faith.
AML_and_Compliance_Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Luxembourg AML law of up to EUR 1 million for legal persons and EUR 125,000 for responsible officers per breach; the Luxembourg regulator may suspend the professional activity and the Commercial Register may order the dissolution of the vehicle; the court may impose a criminal fine up to EUR 5 million for breach of the law of 12 November 2004 as amended.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for deliberate or seriously negligent AML failures, are disqualified from serving as a manager in any Luxembourg financial entity, and may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to five years where the partnership is found to be an instrument for laundering the proceeds of illegal SEO/content-fraud activities.

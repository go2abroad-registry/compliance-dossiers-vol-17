Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a pure SCSp operating as a commodity brokerage (ISIC 4631) with no regulated financial activity, a virtual office (i.e., a registered address service provided by an accredited service provider without physical occupancy) is generally permissible, provided the SCSp maintains a registered office with a real street address (not a PO Box) in Luxembourg and a registered agent. The Luxembourg RCS does not require physical on-site staff or premises for a passive partnership; however, a minimum of one shareholder and one general partner (which can be a corporate entity) with a Luxembourg registered office is required. The LBR/corporate law permits the use of a service-provider address (domiciliation), but the provider must be an approved 'domiciliation agent' (agrément) under the law of 31 May 1999. The permissibility may be curtailed if the brokerage activity expands into handling client money or becomes a regulated commodities exchange, but for low-risk standard commercial activity it is acceptable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT matters. Primary digital pan-Luxembourg portal: myGuichet (https://myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident economic actor registering for Luxembourg tax purposes must first appoint a Luxembourg fiscal domicile representative or agent in accordance with Arts. 155 and 156 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) and Art. 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (LTVL). For VAT, the foreign entity files a pre-registration dossier with the AEDT through myGuichet or by paper, providing the statutory incorporation deed, balance sheet or forecast turnover statement, a description of the business activities to be performed in Luxembourg, and the national identifier of the foreign enterprise. The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) and, where a permanent establishment arises, a separate direct-tax registration number is enforced by ACD. The SCSp itself is not registered for corporate income tax where fiscally transparent; instead, its partners report their allocable shares.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident economic actor registering for Luxembourg tax purposes must first appoint a Luxembourg fiscal domicile representative or agent in accordance with Arts. 155 and 156 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) and Art. 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (LTVL). For VAT, the foreign entity files a pre-registration dossier with the AEDT through myGuichet or by paper, providing the statutory incorporation deed, balance sheet or forecast turnover statement, a description of the business activities to be performed in Luxembourg, and the national identifier of the foreign enterprise. The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) and, where a permanent establishment arises, a separate direct-tax registration number is enforced by ACD. The SCSp itself is not registered for corporate income tax where fiscally transparent; instead, its partners report their allocable shares.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is, by operation of law, fiscally transparent; it is not itself a corporate income taxpayer. No generic check-the-box election exists. However, the agreement of the partners may expressly provide that the SCSp carries on a commercial activity and, if the structure falls within the scope of Art. 175 LIR (which requires a legal-personality company with full liability shareholders), tax transparency is excluded and the entity becomes opaque. For cross-border hybrid mismatch purposes, the tax classification of the SCSp must be documented under the Luxembourg ATAD/MLI implementation, and an election to align or reclassify the entity may be filed with the Luxembourg tax administration within the statutory filing window of the annual partnership return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established taxable persons, no EUR threshold applies and registration is compulsory as from the first taxable supply or acquisition in Luxembourg. Resident small undertakings enjoy a GBP/EUR annual turnover exemption up to EUR 35,000 (Article 169 LTVL) unless conducting export, intra-community or distance-selling transactions. The special EU pan-European distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year. Bulk grain, soybean, and corn export transactions from Luxembourg to non-EU destinations are not counted against the domestic threshold and always trigger full registration rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is available only where the bulk agricultural commodities are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the EU, ownership has transferred to the purchaser, and the supplier holds full documentary proof of dispatch/transport and customs export clearance. Offshore services and maritime vessel brokerage fees linked to the grain trade are zero-rated only where the supply falls under Article 146 VAT Directive, the services relate to goods destined for export, the customer is outside the EU, and the relevant B/L or customs certificate substantiates the offshore use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1, electronic export summary accepted via Luxembourg PDS/Douane system)
  • Transport Documents — Bill of Lading, CMR, or multimodal transport document covering the export movement
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order with Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT wire proof demonstrating receipt of export proceeds
  • Warehouse or silo/dispatch evidence (weighbridge certificates, export inspection certificate) establishing the exact shipped quantity of soybeans/corn

No real-time tax clearance or transaction-level digital stamping is currently enacted in Luxembourg for B2B commercial supplies. E-invoicing is mandatory only for domestic public procurement through the ePEK platform. For export zero-rating, the taxable person must issue an invoice under Article 232 of the EU VAT Directive as implemented by the LTVL, maintain an invoice numbering sequence, include the mandatory indication of the 0% rate and the export dispatch and customs declaration numbers, and file the periodic VAT return by the statutory deadline (monthly or quarterly). The Luxembourg VAT returns are submitted electronically through myGuichet; VAT payment, Portuguese, and cancellation of exports are processed via the AEDT, with no gateway API obligation other than the application-to-application reporting in the myGuichet secure channel.

Export zero-rating is subject to stringent tax administration and customs post-clearance audits. If documentary proof is missing, incomplete, or internally contradictory at the time of audit, AEDT will withdraw the zero rate and assess VAT at 17% on the invoice amount. The assessment carries late-payment interest of 0.6% per month or part thereof, a civil administrative penalty of up to 10% of the tax at issue, and a 5% surcharge for late filing where applicable. Fraudulent simulation of exports, document falsification, or shareholder-linked offshore shell recipients will trigger criminal sanctions under Articles 900 to 911 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law, including monetary fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of responsible directors, together with an unlimited retrospective reopening (limitation suspended during criminal investigation). The clawback period is twenty years where fraud is established and ten years in cases of gross negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility on both the current and capital accounts pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the euro-area legislative framework. No exchange controls, settlement licensing, or prior-authorisation regimes apply to trade-related payments, investment flows, or profit remittances. The Luxembourg SCSp may freely receive, hold, and disburse USD, EUR, CNY, and all other freely convertible currencies through licensed credit institutions; the sole profit-remittance hurdles are exogenous tax-documentary requirements (e.g., EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15% on taxable corporate vehicles, and CRS/FATCA reporting), which bind exclusively in the compliance layer rather than the FX regulatory layer. Capital account repatriation of liquidation proceeds or return of capital is unrestricted, subject only to AML/KYC verification of the beneficial owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no holding-period constraints on export receipts derived from bulk grain, soybean, or corn shipments. Export proceeds may remain indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at a Luxembourg or EEA-authorised bank and may be applied directly to pay for ocean freight, warehouse charges, or new inventory without any BCL conversion requirement. The only affirmative duty is statistical: resident entities must report cross-border flows to the BCL through the balance-of-payments monthly return (export of goods code 100-level transaction classification, or the applicable direct-investment/trade advance code) and must preserve the underlying contract of sale and shipping documents for inspection by the BCL/CSSF.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for the banking supervision of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) and supervised by the CSSF may lawfully open payment accounts for an SCSp without a domestic banking licence; however, they cannot extend credit, cannot issue Irrevocable Transferable Letters of Credit, and their maximum transaction thresholds commonly truncate bulk-vessel settlements. The SCSp should therefore limit EMI use to ancillary disbursements such as demurrage, survey fees, and small balancing payments. Legal constraint: the EMI must hold the SCSp's e-money float in segregated safeguarded accounts, all funds must pass a full AML/KYC screening identical to that of a universal bank, and any financial intermediation signal will create a presumption of shadow-banking that the SCSp must rebut with an independent legal opinion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection protocol: (i) all shareholder/partner capital contributions adhere to SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with strict reference to the notarised capital call letter and the SCSp's RCS registration number; (ii) for USD inflows, settlement is cleared via Fedwire/CHIPS through a tier-1 correspondent, and for EUR inflows via TARGET2/SEPA; (iii) the BCL balance-of-payments monthly statistical return codes the transaction as foreign direct investment equity in an investment vehicle (advanced economy code applicable to direct-investment shares in a Luxembourg holding), the general partner must retain the original credit notification; (iv) the bank applies a two-stage verification: first, an OFAC/EU/UN sanctions screen on the remitter, the intermediary, and the beneficiary; second, a purpose-code verification comparing the narrative field of the MT103 against the underlying subscription certificate; (v) no cash or bearer-transfer injections are permitted, every inbound capital flight must be traceable to a regulated banking institution.Primary clearing rails are: SWIFT MT103/MT202 for settlement of grain invoices, demurrage, freight, and insurance premia; irrevocable and transferable documentary Letters of Credit issued and confirmed under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for the purchase contracts of soybean cargoes; and documentary collections under URC 522 (D/P and D/A) as a standby mechanism. For EUR payments, liquidation occurs through TARGET2; for USD payments, through the correspondent banking network via Fedwire/CHIPS with clearly marked commodity-trade purpose code; for CNY and BRL legs, settlement runs through a top-tier correspondent bank under the Chinese (CIPS/SWIFT CNY) and Brazilian (SISBACO) arrangements. The SCSp must maintain pre-negotiated unutilised credit limits at its relationship bank before drawing the LC; every outgoing instruction must be supported by the sales contract, the final invoice, and the bill of lading number to satisfy Article 5 of UCP 600 and the bank's trade-finance compliance programme.Deliverable FX forward (EUR/USD and EUR/CNY) booked over-the-counter with the relationship bank or a G-20 authorised broker-dealerFX swap (swap points) for short-term liquidity transformation between the SCSp's USD inflow dates and EUR disbursement datesNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on the Brazilian real (BRL), Indian rupee (INR), Argentine peso (ARS), and Ukrainian hryvnia (UAH) for grain invoice legs where the local currency is non-convertible on deliveryCross-currency basis swap (EUR/USD) tenor-matched to the underlying working-capital finance facilityFX option package, including zero-cost collars and participation forwards, to hedge the seasonal volatility of soybean and corn price-linked cash flowsCommodity-linked FX forward (dual-currency instrument) structured by the relationship bank for simultaneous commodity price and exchange-rate risk transfer

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) and supervised by the CSSF may lawfully open payment accounts for an SCSp without a domestic banking licence; however, they cannot extend credit, cannot issue Irrevocable Transferable Letters of Credit, and their maximum transaction thresholds commonly truncate bulk-vessel settlements. The SCSp should therefore limit EMI use to ancillary disbursements such as demurrage, survey fees, and small balancing payments. Legal constraint: the EMI must hold the SCSp's e-money float in segregated safeguarded accounts, all funds must pass a full AML/KYC screening identical to that of a universal bank, and any financial intermediation signal will create a presumption of shadow-banking that the SCSp must rebut with an independent legal opinion.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg-City aggregate: 17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% is the standard Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a capital company. However, the SCSp is a transparent partnership and its periodic profit allocations are not legal dividends; thus Luxembourg does not impose dividend withholding tax on SCSp distributions. If an SCSp is recharacterized as opaque under a foreign treaty or domestic reconstruction, relief may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, or 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax year is the calendar year. The return for tax year Y must be filed by 31 December of Y+1 (e.g., FY ending 31 December 2025 is due 31 December 2026). The ACD may grant an extension upon written request; no automatic extension is available.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 (group consolidated revenue threshold under the EU CbCR / Master File / Local File regime); below that threshold, Luxembourg ACD may still require contemporaneous TP documentation for all related-party commodity transactions where annual related-party turnover exceeds EUR 2,000,000 or where the profit margin of the SCSp is outside an arm’s length range.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations modeled on the UAE/Guernsey regimes; however, domestic GAAR (LIR § 6, Steueranpassungsgesetz) and EU anti-abuse law impose substantive requirements. An SCSp conducting bulk grain and soybean/corn brokerage must maintain a physical office, local management with genuine decision-making authority, adequate staff and expenses proportionate to turnover, and an actual commercial risk profile. A letter-box SCSp will lose treaty and EU directive benefits and be recharacterized as an abusive conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Comprehensive administrative reassessment of all omitted income; fraud penalties up to 50% of evaded tax plus late-payment interest; criminal fine of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO §§ 397 et seq.); attachment and seizure of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The general partner of an SCSp and its managers are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and interest; criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as director.
Shell Company / Abuse of Treaty

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax treaty benefits, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, and domestic participation exemption; full recharacterization of revenues as Luxembourg taxable income; GAAR adjustment plus default interest; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 and possible 10% penalty on additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be ordered to pay the recovered tax and damages; personal forfeiture orders; travel bans during criminal investigation; imprisonment for intentional sham-arrangement conduct.
AML / Sanctions / CDD Failure

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for AML breaches; criminal money-laundering penalties of up to 5 years’ imprisonment and fine up to EUR 500,000; confiscation of trade proceeds; suspension of operations for repeat violations.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, managers, and the general partner face personal fines and criminal prosecution; professional debarment from financial services; travel bans; personal seizure of assets; imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering grain-commodity funds.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm’s length upward adjustment of Luxembourg taxable income; late-payment interest; penalty of 10% of the adjustment (up to EUR 25,000); extension of the statute of limitations on tax assessments; refusal of loss compensation and foreign tax credit.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false TP declarations are jointly and severally liable for the understated tax; criminal sanctions follow if false documents are provided to the ACD; travel bans and personal financial restitution may be ordered.

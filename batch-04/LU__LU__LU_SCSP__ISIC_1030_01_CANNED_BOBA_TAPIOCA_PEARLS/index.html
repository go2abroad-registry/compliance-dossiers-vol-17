Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) electronic filing and search portal, accessible via https://www.lbr.lu and https://www.ejustice.public.lu

Name reservation / lock period

No formal statutory name-reservation system exists in Luxembourg; the name becomes protected only upon effective RCS registration of the SCSp. If the RCS registrar rejects the proposed name due to identity, confusion or prohibited token, the applicant may: (i) submit a written request for informal registry review through the LBR MyBusiness portal within 15 calendar days of the refusal; (ii) if the refusal is maintained, file a formal administrative appeal before the Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg, commercial chamber, within one month of notification, represented by a Luxembourg avocat-à-la-cour; (iii) where the refusal concerns a sensitive token, obtain the prior no-objection letter from the competent ministry or CSSF/CAA before refiling; (iv) alternatively, amend the constitutional deed by a new notarial or private deed selecting a compliant name and proceed with a fresh RCS filing. The one-month court deadline may not be extended, but a new name application may be resubmitted immediately following a definitive refusal.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and NIF issuance; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration, eTVA returns and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) for customs and export formalities. Official portals: MyGuichet.lu, eCDF, eTVA and guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Before commencing taxable activity, the SCSp must be registered in the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCSL). For VAT, the SCSp or its statutory representative files the VAT registration application with the AEDT; non-established EU suppliers may use OSS/IOSS for distance sales, while non-EU suppliers generally must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative and register from the first taxable euro. The ACD issues the 13-digit matricule (NIF) for direct tax compliance; the SCSp holds a NIF notwithstanding its tax-transparent status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp is mandatorily transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax and net wealth tax, with partners taxed on their allocable share of income and gains; it constitutes a distinct taxable person for VAT purposes; the SCSp has no legal personality and no minimum capital requirement.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; SCSp transparency is mandatory and cannot be waived. Relevant statutory mechanisms include the VAT grouping election under Article 60 of the LTVL for closely-linked Luxembourg taxable persons, the freedom in the partnership deed to allocate profit shares binding Luxembourg direct tax allocation, and the corporate fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election, which is unavailable to transparent SCSp taxpayers. EU ATAD hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically to neutralise divergent cross-border classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business (franchise) exemption, applicable only to Luxembourg-established businesses; exceeding the threshold triggers compulsory registration from the first euro. Non-established foreign suppliers have no local threshold, and EU distance sellers may use the EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold unless they opt for the OSS scheme or local Luxembourg registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) exports of canned tapioca pearls physically dispatched or transported outside the EU, evidenced by an export declaration and transport proof, and (ii) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers where a valid customer VAT ID is held, the goods leave Luxembourg, and the recapitulative statement is duly filed. Closely related offshore services such as packing and logistics are zero-rated when directly linked to the exported goods; other B2B services follow the reverse-charge place-of-supply rule.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration and exit confirmation via the Luxembourg and European customs system)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR, airway bill) evidencing physical removal of the goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or treasury evidence confirming repatriation of export proceeds within the FX deadline)

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / Factur-X) for invoices rendered under public procurement contracts; no real-time B2B fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control or digital stamping of commercial invoices is currently in force; compliance is ensured through eTVA returns, SAF-T audit files, EC Sales Lists and customs export records.

The AEDT may jointly verify export evidence with the customs authority; insufficient, false or fraudulent substantiation triggers reassessment of Luxembourg VAT at the domestic rate (17% standard or the applicable food rate), statutory default interest at 0.6% per month, late-payment surcharges and administrative fines, with criminal prosecution available in fraud cases; previously granted zero-rated refunds and input VAT credits are retrospectively clawed back within the statutory prescription periods, and the customs export confirmation may itself be revoked upon verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the EU Economic and Monetary Union. The euro is freely convertible and there are no exchange controls on either current account or capital account transactions. The free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is permitted without prior approval; however, dividend-like distributions to certain investors may trigger Luxembourg withholding tax unless an exemption under the participation exemption regime, a tax treaty, or an EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies. The SCSp is tax-transparent, and its partners are taxed at partner level; there are no statutory hurdles to profit repatriation beyond standard documentary evidence of distribution approval and substance substantiation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement. Sellers may retain export income in any currency, convert it at free-market rates, and use it freely. There is no mandated percentage or statutory timeline for repatriation into euro, and no obligation to deposit funds with the BCL or a licensed bank beyond standard anti-money laundering (AML) record-keeping under Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004, as amended.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the primary banks and financial markets supervisors under the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2018 and the CSSF Law of 12 July 2013.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

The viability of using an offshore electronic money institution (EMI) is Medium with significant legal constraints. Under PSD2 and the CSSF's E-Money Regulations, only EU/EEA-licensed EMIs that have successfully completed a passport notification to Luxembourg (or have an establishment in Luxembourg) may operate. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot provide services to a Luxembourg SCSp without a banking or payment-services license in Luxembourg. The SCSp should treat non-EU EMIs as ineligible for capital custody and trade settlement because they are not subject to BCL prudential supervision; where a fintech solution is desired, an EU-licensed EMI with a valid Luxembourg passport and a segregated client-funds account is acceptable for transactional flows only and not for escrow of partnership capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SCSp capital contributions may be wired from international investors to a Luxembourg bank account using standard SWIFT MT103 messages. The remitter should populate purpose code F2 (Direct investment) and include narrative: 'Capital contribution to an SCSp under Luxembourg law' with the unique partner identification if available. Where the foreign partner uses an OECD harmonised transaction system, the BOP code 'S121' or 'S12' (capital transfers) is acceptable if the bank accepts those values. The receiving bank must complete the CSSF-mandated customer due diligence before the first credit. For high-value injections, a signed capital subscription agreement, bank account confirmation letter, partnership deed, and proof of tax residence of the investor are required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City combined headline: 17% corporate income tax + 0.7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax; an SCSp is fiscally transparent unless it validly opts into corporate taxation, in which case the combined headline rate applies at entity level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax for distributions by corporate payers. Distributions by an SCSp are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not a legal entity subject to CIT; where an SCSp receives dividends, 15% may be withheld at the underlying corporate payer, reducible to 0% under the Luxembourg participation exemption or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where conditions are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and transfer pricing documentation are mandatorily required when intra-group transactions with related parties exceed EUR 1,000,000 per fiscal year; Master File is required when the global group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Below the threshold, the SCSp must still retain contemporaneous evidence to substantiate arm's-length pricing upon ACD request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is enforced through the OECD BEPS precondition, EU ATAD GAAR, Luxembourg's abuse-of-law doctrine, and the ACD's practice governing tax residence certificates. A manufacturing SCSp must demonstrate real substance in Luxembourg: actual production and aseptic tubing capacity, qualified employees, decision-making by Luxembourg-based managers, local bank accounts, and adequate capitalisation. Absent substance, the ACD may deny treaty benefits, reclassify the SCSp as a taxable resident company, and disregard the partnership's separate existence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% to 50% of the under-assessed tax under LIR Articles 119-120; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000; monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%; criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to twice the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and beneficial owners who knowingly sign or direct false filings are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts, interest, and penalties; criminal courts may impose travel bans, prohibition on holding corporate mandates, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company and Artificial Structuring

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and reassessment under the Luxembourg abuse-of-law rule (LIR Art. 6) and ATAD GAAR; denial of tax residence certificates and DTA benefits; disregard of the SCSp's separate legal personality; restated taxable profits in the hands of the actual owner; additional tax, interest, and penalties up to 25% of the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and indirect shareholders may be personally assessed for unpaid corporate and municipal tax; repeated abuse can trigger criminal sanctions, asset forfeiture, and travel bans against the orchestrating individuals.
Anti-Money Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 and the Criminal Code, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of six months to five years and fines for natural persons; legal persons face fines up to EUR 5,000,000, confiscation of proceeds, publication of the judgment, and potential withdrawal of the manufacturer's/export licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable where they knew or should reasonably have known of the laundering; courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders on personal assets, and disqualification from commercial management.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income under LIR Art. 164 and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; disallowance of excessive deductions; penalty for negligent misdeclaration up to 50% of the adjustment; 0.6% monthly interest; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing documentation may be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties; intentional mispricing linked to fraud can lead to criminal liability and travel bans.
Total Failure to File or Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: 10% surcharge on the total assessed tax, 0.6% monthly interest, fine up to EUR 25,000; ACD may proceed to official assessment, forced collection, and revocation of business registration; repeated non-filing is treated as criminal tax fraud.

Director personal liability: The gérant or manager of the general partner is personally liable for unpaid tax debts arising from intentional or grossly negligent non-filing; personal bank accounts may be attached, and a travel ban may be imposed where flight risk or asset dissipation is demonstrated.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; digital access via guichet.public.lu and MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign SCSp or its representative first registers with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a notarized deed, then files the statutory registration forms 102/103 with the ACD and VAT form 640/641 with the AEDT. Non-EU resident investors may be required to appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for process service and VAT compliance. The competent authority issues a Luxembourg national tax identification number (matricule) and a separate VAT number upon acceptance of the application.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign SCSp or its representative first registers with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a notarized deed, then files the statutory registration forms 102/103 with the ACD and VAT form 640/641 with the AEDT. Non-EU resident investors may be required to appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for process service and VAT compliance. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent and is taxed at the level of its partners unless an optional election to be treated as a corporate taxpayer is available under Article 175 LIR and all partners consent. For cross-border hybrid arrangements, partners must document and file ATAD hybrid mismatch disclosures with the ACD, including Form 700 and supporting classification analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business exemption; however, non-established foreign taxable persons making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT from the first euro unless the EU one-stop-shop distance-selling or non-established trader thresholds apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT when physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union, supported by a validated customs export declaration, transport documents, commercial invoices, and proof that legal title and risk passed to a non-EU customer. Services qualify when supplied to a recipient established outside the EU and the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs outside Luxembourg; where the recipient is a taxable person, the reverse-charge rules may apply instead of zero-rating.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (EX-A) or electronic export summary declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls for B2B invoices. Electronic invoicing is permitted subject to partner consent and authenticity/integrity safeguards. B2G invoices must be submitted electronically through the national PEPPOL infrastructure to the Luxembourg Public Sector Integrated System. VAT records must be retained for six years and made available to the AEDT upon request.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary verification. Inadequate or contradictory evidence within the statutory inspection window triggers reassessment at the 17% standard VAT rate plus late payment interest. Negligent insufficiency exposes the taxable person to administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; fraudulent zero-rating claims may be prosecuted under Article 42 of the Luxembourg VAT Law and result in criminal fines and imprisonment for the managing partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control approvals, no inward/outward capital declarations, and no restrictions on non-resident ownership of accounts. Liberalisation derives from Articles 63-66 TFEU (free movement of capital), Regulation (EU) 2018/1151 on European business statistics, and the EU Anti-Money Laundering framework. Profit remittance and SCSp distributions are unrestricted for both EU-resident and third-country LPs; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent (Luxembourg income tax law and the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended by the Law of 12 July 2013), no Luxembourg withholding tax applies at partnership level unless the entity has elected taxable-corporate status, in which case standard corporate distribution rules would apply. The only practical obstacles for foreign LPs repatriating proceeds are bank-level KYC gates, EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening, and BCL statistical reporting on cross-border flows above reporting thresholds; a GP distribution resolution and proof of Luxembourg tax/VAT good standing are required by banks in practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg maintains no mandatory surrender, conversion, or holding-period rules for export proceeds. The EUR-based SCSp may retain USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency in onshore or offshore accounts, may net export receivables against import payables, and may extend open-account trade terms without central-bank consent. Residual obligations are limited to: (i) customs export declarations via the EU AES/ICS2 systems for the medical-scanner consignments; (ii) cross-border cash declarations above EUR 10,000 under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672; and (iii) crypto-asset transfer-data ('Travel Rule') transmission under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, which does not apply to conventional wire transfers but is referenced for completeness.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); banking supervision and AML/KYC enforcement are delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under CSSF Circular 12/552 and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended; the BCL operates the T2/TARGET2 settlement infrastructure and the Luxembourg Interbank Payment System (LIPS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW-MEDIUM viability. Luxembourg law authorises Payment Institutions (Law of 10 November 2009) and Electronic Money Institutions (Law of 21 December 2012), both supervised by the CSSF; local EMIs such as Payconiq International and PPRO Financial can supply multi-currency IBANs, SEPA/T2 instant payments, and corporate cards. However, EMIs generally: (i) do not maintain correspondent banking for high-value USD MT103 clearing; (ii) cannot issue, advise, or settle commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT760) or documentary collections; and (iii) restrict physical-goods trade flows (medical scanners) under AML policies oriented to e-commerce/consumer payments. Offshore EMIs (UK, Singapore, UAE) cannot passport into Luxembourg post-Brexit without an EU branch or agent, so an unlicensed foreign EMI is not legally viable for the SCSp's bank-account duties under CSSF Circular 12/552. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as primary treasury and LC rail; a CSSF- or other-EU-authorised EMI only for payroll card and small procurement spend, never for trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound LP capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR, typically USD) or SEPA/T2 credit transfer (EUR) to the SCSp's operational IBAN at the Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. Remittance information (field 70) must state 'SCSP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [LPA reference and GP resolution date]' and, where requested by the receiving bank, the Luxembourg BCL statistical purpose code for direct-investment equity capital. No exchange-control permit or pre-clearance filing is required; however, the bank will block or delay the credit unless the KYC source-of-funds dossier is already complete. For USD injections, utilise the bank's New York/United States correspondent to avoid mid-tier clearing holds; for EUR, ensure the target IBAN is held in the SCSp's name (the GP is a signatory only, and the IBAN holder must be the partnership).Two parallel rails: (1) Pre-shipment deposits and milestone payments via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers, with field 70 quoting the purchase order and underlying LC reference; (2) Post-shipment settlement via irrevocable, confirmed at-sight commercial letters of credit: the buyer's bank issues the LC to the Luxembourg advising/confirming bank through SWIFT MT700, and payment is made by MT103/MT202 upon presentation of compliant documents (commercial invoice, packing list, on-board bill of lading, certificate of origin, CE certificate, and insurance policy). For recurring low-ticket spare-part consignments, the SCSp may shift to open account with export-credit insurance (Euler Hermes/Coface) while still clearing through MT103 corporate e-banking.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the electronic filing window on MyGuichet.lu.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), particularly Article 906-1 et seq. for the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) as inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013; RCS filing regime and publication obligations under the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and the Annual Accounts of Undertakings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of incorporation (acte de constitution / contrat de société) of the SCSp, executed before a Luxembourg notary or by private writing and submitted to the RCS in the standardized electronic RCS constitution extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital. The SCSp freely defines capital and financing in the partnership agreement; zero capitalization is permitted. If contributions are made, they may be credited to an account in the SCSp's name and blocked until the RCS registration certificate is issued; no statutory escrow or obligatory notarial consignment applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety conformity attestation (attestation de sécurité incendie) issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for offices and any bonded storage; workplace compliance inspection by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering emergency exits, work equipment, first aid, and health-and-safety documentation; structural stability and load-bearing records to be maintained by the registered-office owner and presented upon ITM audit.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital. The SCSp freely defines capital and financing in the partnership agreement; zero capitalization is permitted. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes. Both authorities operate through the unified MyGuichet.lu digital gateway (eTVA, e-CDF); official portals: guichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident NVOCC must first obtain the Luxembourg national business number (matricule) via registration of the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) where a local establishment exists; it then files VAT Form 645 ('Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, attaching the constitutive deed, RCS extract, proof of Luxembourg place of management, and identity of the authorised representative. The AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format 'LU' + 8 digits) within approximately 3-6 weeks. Where the foreign NVOCC supplies only B2B services to Luxembourg recipients, the recipient self-assesses VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism and no local VAT ID is mandatory; a foreign supplier seeking input-VAT refunds may use the EU e-VAT refund portal without creating a Luxembourg VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent and has no legal personality: it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) or net wealth tax; each partner is taxed personally on their allocable share of profits, with non-resident partners taxable only where the SCSp constitutes a Luxembourg permanent establishment for them (normally present for an SCSp managed and controlled from Luxembourg). The SCSp enterprise remains the assessment unit for municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and is treated as an independent taxable person for VAT and customs purposes. — A non-resident NVOCC must first obtain the Luxembourg national business number (matricule) via registration of the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) where a local establishment exists; it then files VAT Form 645 ('Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, attaching the constitutive deed, RCS extract, proof of Luxembourg place of management, and identity of the authorised representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent and has no legal personality: it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) or net wealth tax; each partner is taxed personally on their allocable share of profits, with non-resident partners taxable only where the SCSp constitutes a Luxembourg permanent establishment for them (normally present for an SCSp managed and controlled from Luxembourg). The SCSp enterprise remains the assessment unit for municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and is treated as an independent taxable person for VAT and customs purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; transparent treatment is mandatory for the SCSp. Available mechanisms include: advance tax rulings from the ACD confirming fiscal transparency and profit allocation; the Article 57 VAT small-business franchise (opt-in to normal VAT taxation); VAT grouping (assujetti unique) with Luxembourg affiliates; and fiscal year alignment. Anti-hybrid rules (Article 168ter/168quater LIR implementing ATAD 2) restrict deduction/non-inclusion mismatches where a foreign partner treats the SCSp as an opaque entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to Luxembourg-established taxable persons: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. The Article 57 small-business exemption applies only up to EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover (taxpayer may opt into normal taxation). Non-resident NVOCCs face no mandatory registration threshold for B2B services (reverse charge applies); registration is triggered by intra-Community acquisitions of goods or by B2C supplies performed within Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% exemption-with-credit applies to international maritime transport of goods and to NVOCC brokerage, ocean freight, container handling and customs-clearance services directly linked to goods entering or leaving the EU (Article 148(a)/(d) of EU Directive 2006/112/EC; Articles 53 et seq. of the amended Luxembourg VAT law). Eligibility requires a direct contractual and economic link between the service and a voyage on an ocean-going vessel with a port of loading and a port of discharge inside and outside the EU; transportation organised by the NVOCC as principal (contracting carrier) qualifies in full, whereas purely domestic agency or commission components performed solely within Luxembourg are taxable at 17%.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Export Accompanying Document / MRN generated in the EU Automated Export System evidencing lodging and physical exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading: ocean Master/Through Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill in the name of the shipper/NVOCC and the foreign consignee, confirming the cross-border port-to-port route
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 or credit advice proving receipt of the foreign currency payment from the overseas counterparty within the 180-day benchmark

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory: suppliers to Luxembourg public entities must transmit invoices via the PEPPOL network in EN 16931 / PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format; paper or PDF invoices are unacceptable in public procurement. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for B2B invoices; compliance rests on standard Article 226 invoice data, electronic VAT returns (eTVA via MyGuichet.lu), European Sales List filings, and periodic submission of the Luxembourg SAF-T-LV XML audit file by selected VAT payers.

Luxembourg imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; 180 days is the customary banking and customs documentary benchmark for matching export proceeds. The AEDT may reassess zero-rated exports within 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraudulent or incomplete files; failure to produce the required documentation triggers recapture of the 17% VAT, a 10% statutory surcharge (majoration) and monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%. Forged Bills of Lading, false customs declarations or orchestrated round-tripping constitute VAT fraud, punishable by criminal fines and imprisonment with OLAF coordination in cross-border cases; the general anti-abuse rule also permits retrospective disregard of artificial freight-route or invoice-chain structures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates within the eurozone under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions, with no approval or surrender requirements for non-resident partners, no foreign exchange rationing, and no separate domestic FX convertibility regime. Profit remittances by an SCSp to non-resident partners are legally free of exchange-control restrictions, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification, tax compliance under the Luxembourg tax code, and, where applicable, withholding tax characterization under double tax treaties. For a NVOCC freight forwarding operation, payments to non-EU carriers, port authorities, and service providers are generally unrestricted, but must be properly documented to satisfy EU anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (AML/CTF) obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists under Luxembourg law. Because Luxembourg is a eurozone member and has fully liberalized capital movements under EU Directive 88/361/EEC and Articles 63-66 TFEU, ocean freight forwarding/NVOCC export-entitlement receivables may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible currency. No mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline applies. Nevertheless, the SCSp must observe EU statistical reporting thresholds imposed by the BCL for cross-border transactions and positions (positive and negative) exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month, with consolidated reporting required for intra-EU and extra-EU movements through the BCL's direct reporting system.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank under the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) exercises licensing and prudential supervision over Luxembourg credit institutions and payment/electronic money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Luxembourg-licensed fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating under CSSF authorization and PSD2 are viable for low-volume operational treasury functions of the SCSp, such as vendor payments and travel expenses, with a Medium feasibility rating for this NVOCC business. Offshore digital EMIs not established in the EEA or not licensed by the CSSF have Limited viability due to (i) lack of access to Target2 payment systems, (ii) restrictions in correspondent banking relationships, (iii) inability to issue commercial letters of credit, and (iv) stricter enhanced due diligence requirements imposed on SCSp partnerships by Luxembourg banks. The SCSp should rely on onshore tier-1 banks for core clearing, FX, and trade finance rails, using EMIs only as secondary fee-management tools.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the SCSp must be routed by wire through a correspondent bank to the Luxembourg resident account held at a BIL, BGL BNP Paribas, or Société Générale Bank & Trust. The remitter must use SWIFT MT103 with the payment purpose code clearly indicating either a direct capital contribution (e.g., 'C1 — Equity capital' under Luxembourg/BCL BoP coding) or a partner loan ('C4 — Intercompany financing'); the receiving bank will require the SCSp's legal name, UBO identification, and copy of the executed partnership agreement. Verification includes BCL monthly external position reporting if aggregated flows exceed EUR 12,500, completion of the bank's internal source-of-funds form, and confirmation of the economic substance behind the contribution to prevent re-characterization as a loan for tax, AML, or thin capitalization purposes. Capital call notices must reference the SCSp's partnership agreement and identify the partner, contribution date, and currency.Outbound disbursements for NVOCC trade settlements operate through the standard international payment rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for freight payments, port fees, trucking charges, and customs duties, using ISO 20022-structured remittance information and the correct tariff/industry purpose codes (e.g., BOPS 'ITR' transportation codes); (ii) commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the Luxembourg bank at the request of the SCSp's client for ocean freight pre-financing, using UCP 600 rules, with freight forwarder documents of title such as the master bill of lading and forwarders' certificate of receipt; and (iii) ACH direct debits for low-value domestic EU disbursements via SEPA infrastructure. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master file required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million. Local file required only if the Luxembourg entity engages in intercompany transactions where the total value exceeds EUR 100 million per fiscal year (threshold applied to both inbound and outbound transactions).

Economic substance scope: SCSp must have real economic substance (own office, employees, management decisions, bank accounts in Luxembourg) to benefit from treaty access and avoid being characterized as a self-complacent shell enterprise. The Luxembourg ACD applies substance over form and may disregard the partnership under GAAR anti-abuse provisions, especially if the entity is managed/administered abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 1 million or twice the evaded tax, and imprisonment up to 10 years; civil restitution plus default interest of 0.6% per month; loss of claim to treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners are jointly liable to pay evaded taxes, may be subject to travel bans and seizure of assets, and in aggravated cases imprisonment for up to 10 years.
Shell company / Sham arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disregard of partnership, re-characterization of profits, retroactive tax assessment including 10% increase, and denial of DTA access; administrative fines up to EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for corporate taxes, interest, and fines; possible prohibition on managing a company in Luxembourg for up to 5 years.
AML / Sanctions breach (medium risk NVOCC)

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to EUR 5 million for legal entities and up to EUR 1 million for directors under the 2004 AML law; seizure of illicit commissions; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability as beneficial owner; personal fines; deportation for non-EU directors; revocation of supervisory mandates throughout European Economic Area.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the profit adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000; increase of taxable base; interest at 0.6% per month; possible disallowance of deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to maintain proper transfer pricing documentation are collectively liable for the penalty when negligent; personal fines up to EUR 50,000 may be imposed.

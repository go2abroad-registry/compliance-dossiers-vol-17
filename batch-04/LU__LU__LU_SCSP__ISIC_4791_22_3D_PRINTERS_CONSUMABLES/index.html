Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), administered by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, in particular Articles 101 et seq. concerning the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp), as amended by the Law of 23 July 2016.

Formation / constitutional filings

Signed partnership agreement (contrat de société) and the supporting registration declaration filed electronically through the Luxembourg Business Registers eDesk.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an SCSp and no escrow account is required. Capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement; in-kind contributions must be supported by a statutory auditor valuation report to the extent required by the Law of 10 August 1915.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the competent municipal fire brigade and civil defence services for any commercial premises; structural load inspection for mezzanine, racking and equipment storage; electrical installation conformity certificate; workplace risk assessment and health-and-safety clearance under the Luxembourg Law of 17 June 1994 on Occupational Health and Safety; and, where required, a positive post-visit civil defence report for the warehouse or showroom.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an SCSp and no escrow account is required. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg: Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income/wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; primary official portal for registration and payment: myGuichet.lu (MyGuichet) and guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign/non-resident entity making taxable B2C supplies in Luxembourg must file an online VAT registration application via MyGuichet.lu before commencing taxable activity. Required supporting documents include certificate of incorporation or equivalent, excerpt from the Luxembourg RCS or foreign trade/company register, articles/statutes, proof of business address, identity documents of partners/managers/directors, and, where no EU establishment exists, appointment of a Luxembourg fiscal representative if required by the VAT administration. Upon acceptance, AEDT issues a Luxembourg national tax number and a VAT identification number (LU followed by 8 digits), normally within 10-15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign/non-resident entity making taxable B2C supplies in Luxembourg must file an online VAT registration application via MyGuichet.lu before commencing taxable activity. Required supporting documents include certificate of incorporation or equivalent, excerpt from the Luxembourg RCS or foreign trade/company register, articles/statutes, proof of business address, identity documents of partners/managers/directors, and, where no EU establishment exists, appointment of a Luxembourg fiscal representative if required by the VAT administration. Upon acceptance, AEDT issues a Luxembourg national tax number and a VAT identification number (LU followed by 8 digits), normally within 10-15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no IRS-style check-the-box election. An SCSp (Société en commandite spéciale) is mandatorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax and net wealth tax purposes; it is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax in its own name. There is no statutory corporate election for transparency. Luxembourg applies ATAD-Nexus anti-hybrid and mismatch rules that may reclassify payments or deny deductions if a hybrid mismatch arises with the residence jurisdiction of a non-resident partner. Taxpayers may obtain a binding advance tax ruling from the ACD confirming the transparent classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a taxable person established in Luxembourg, the small business VAT exemption threshold is annual taxable turnover of EUR 35,000 excluding VAT; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory Luxembourg VAT registration. For non-established foreign sellers supplying goods or e-commerce services to Luxembourg consumers, registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction and no general turnover threshold applies; the EU distance-sales/OVR threshold rules may in some EU-supply scenarios require use of the one-stop shop (OSS) rather than separate Luxembourg registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% Luxembourg VAT rate applies to exports of goods physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier retains adequate commercial and customs proof that the goods have left the EU. For online retail of 3D printers, resin filaments, and consumables, zero-rating is available where the non-resident buyer's identity and destination address are established, transport to a non-EU country is documented, and the customs export declaration is made. Services that are directly related to exported goods or performed for non-EU customers may also qualify for zero-rating under the general B2B place-of-supply rules, subject to documentary substantiation.

  • Customs export declaration (EU Single Administrative Document / export declaration) containing the MRN and customs office of export
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier/consignment note evidencing dispatch to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the 3D printers, resins, and consumables
  • Contract or purchase order of the non-EU buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment settlement evidence showing that export proceeds are paid from outside the EU

Luxembourg does not currently impose a general mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping requirement. Electronic invoicing is accepted where authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability are ensured. Luxembourg does not require acceptance of e-invoices through Peppol or a dedicated national clearance API for standard B2B sales. Businesses must retain invoice records, export-supporting documentation, and periodic VAT returns (e.g. monthly/quarterly) filed electronically via the Luxembourg eCDF / eTVA platform or through the official portal. For intra-EU supplies of goods, recapitulative statements may also be required.

The AEDT may refuse zero-rating and retroactively assess VAT at 17% plus default interest where export proof is incomplete, inconsistent, or unavailable at the time of audit. False or fraudulent export documentation may trigger administrative fines, restoration of VAT and input VAT clawback, loss of deduction rights, and, in severe intentional cases, criminal tax penalties. Luxembourg tax law requires exporters to retain all substantiating documents for at least six years. Although Luxembourg maintains no formal foreign-exchange control or statutory FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating, the 180-day inward remittance benchmark is treated as a documentary best-practice indicator; failure to repatriate or evidence payment may heighten audit scrutiny and payment-abuse allegations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control legislation. Cross-border transfers on current and capital account are unrestricted under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EU single market. The SCSp is tax-transparent, so profit remittances are made directly to partners without Luxembourg withholding tax, provided the SCSp is not characterized as an AIF (Law of 12 July 2013) and has sufficient local substance. Practical blockage points are limited to EU/UN sanctions screening, Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 cash declaration thresholds, and bank-level AML 'payment integrity' reviews; none of these impose convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no timeline for repatriation, no surrender ratio, and no mandatory conversion requirement for export receipts. Revenue from online retail of 3D printers and consumables may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency in Luxembourg or foreign accounts. Only the BCL balance-of-payments statistical declaration regime applies, and penalties for non-declaration are administrative rather than currency-fiscal.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating as part of the Eurosystem; prudential banking supervision is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit (CRF/Cellule de Renseignement Financier) exercises AML/CFT oversight; the Ministry of Finance retains residual sanction and monetary policy coordination powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High via Luxembourg-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Mangopay SA and Payconiq, and EU-passported EMIs including Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Qonto and Pliant. These rails provide rapid onboarding, API-linked accounts, multi-currency IBANs and near-interbank FX. Legal and operational constraints: (i) the SCSp lacks separate legal personality, which many automated EMIs fail to underwrite - the partnership agreement, gérant/managers' mandate and RBE extract are mandatory; (ii) tax-transparent entities are frequently flagged by automated merchant acquirers; (iii) EMI client-money safeguarding is ring-fenced but not a bank deposit guarantee; (iv) most EMIs still cannot replace Tier-1 SWIFT MT103 B2B trade settlement to APAC suppliers. Recommended architecture: Stripe/Shopify payout settlement to a passported EMI sub-account for working-capital agility, with the primary clearing and liquidity account retained at BCEE or BIL.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate CIT + solidarity surcharge + Luxembourg City municipal business tax (15% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding on distributions treated as dividends from a Luxembourg resident company. An SCSp is fiscally transparent; distributions to partners are not dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg WHT unless the partner is deemed to have a Luxembourg PE and the distribution is recharacterized as a taxable dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Compulsory Local File for controlled cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 500,000; Master File if the multinational group has consolidated turnover of at least EUR 50 million; Country-by-Country Report if group consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute like the UAE. Instead, substance requirements are enforced through the participation exemption, permanent establishment attribution, ATAD CFC rules, and the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit / Statutory Anti-Avoidance rule under AO 6 and Luxembourg case law). To avoid treaty-shopping or PE recharacterization, the SCSp must maintain a genuine Luxembourg office, local bank account, local management decisions, employees, and business records for the online retail of 3D printers and resin filaments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment for up to 10 years, late interest of 0.6% per month, financial penalty up to EUR 250,000, and imprisonment of the responsible individuals under Luxembourg criminal tax law.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may have the corporate veil pierced; they are jointly liable for unpaid taxes, face travel bans and asset seizures, and may be sentenced to imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ACD may disregard the SCSp, recharacterize it as taxable or opaque, deny treaty benefits, impose 24.94% CIT on deemed underlying profits, and apply 15% WHT on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: The nominal corporate general partner and its directors are personally exposed to restitution claims, disqualification, and criminal sanctions for facilitating abusive structures.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustment, secondary adjustment recharacterizing excess profit as dividends, refusal of deduction, penalties up to 10% of the adjusted profit, and monthly interest accrual.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute unfavorable pricing without supporting documentation can be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties, and may face criminal prosecution for evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years under Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004, as amended; administrative fines up to EUR 1 million; freezing orders and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp's authorized agent are criminally liable, subject to civil restitution, disqualification from conducting financial services, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Mischaracterization of Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT if the recipient is a shareholder, plus late-interest, penalties, and potential deemed income assessments.

Director personal liability: Designing and approving sham payment structures exposes directors to personal liability for the grossed-up withholding tax, damages for breach of fiduciary duty, and criminal sanction in fraudulent cases.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; central e-services and filings via MyGuichet.lu; official portals impotsdirects.public.lu and pfi.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint a Luxembourg general partner or service provider and obtain a Luxembourg registered office for the SCSp; obtain the sectoral business licence (autorisation d'établissement) and food-safety approval required for UHT oat-milk manufacturing. 2) File the SCSp instrument and UBO details electronically with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), obtaining the RCS registration number. 3) Apply to the AEDT for VAT identification through MyGuichet using the VAT identification request form, stating turnover and activity; if the SCSp is non-established, also appoint an approved tax representative (responsable solidaire). 4) Apply to the ACD for the direct-tax file or matricule number where direct tax filings are required; for a transparent SCSp the partners report their share on their own Luxembourg or foreign returns. 5) Register as an employer with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) if staff are hired.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent for direct tax purposes; profits are taxed at partner level and the partnership itself is not subject to corporate income tax, although it may be subject to municipal business tax to the extent it exercises a commercial activity.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. The transparent classification of the SCSp is the statutory default. In a hybrid situation, when another jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque, Luxembourg will apply ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules and the parties must document the divergence, report any resulting deduction or non-inclusion mismatch, and align the transaction with the substance requirements; no separate voluntary entity-classification form is available for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established micro and small businesses, excluding one-off exempt supplies; non-established foreign suppliers are not covered by the exemption threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT from their first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies when UHT oat-milk products are physically transported from Luxembourg out of the EU to a non-EU customer, title and risk pass to that customer, and the exporter holds credible transport and customs evidence. Services: B2B international services are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT when the place of supply is outside the EU; zero-rating may apply to services directly connected with export transport and ancillary services. The supply is not zero-rated when goods are placed on the Luxembourg domestic market or dispatched to another EU Member State.

  • Customs export declaration (single administrative document or SAD) with export customs office validation
  • Bill of lading, CMR consignment note or air waybill proving physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract, purchase order or letter of credit
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 / TT wire confirmation evidencing foreign exchange settlement
  • Delivery and acceptance proof from the non-EU customer or warehouse records
  • EC sales list and Intrastat declarations where an EU intra-Community dispatch is separately exempted

No national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate is currently imposed on B2B invoices in Luxembourg. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for public-sector suppliers through the PEPPOL network. Structured e-invoices such as UBL, CII and EDIFACT are accepted for audit; Luxembourg will transpose the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age e-invoicing and digital reporting requirements once the EU framework is adopted.

Export zero-rating is scrutinised through documentary audits; if satisfactory contemporaneous evidence is not produced, the Luxembourg VAT authority reassesses the supply at 17%, charges default interest from the due date, applies administrative fines of up to 100% of the underpaid VAT, and may seek clawback of refunds. Criminal tax-fraud proceedings can lead to fines up to double the evaded tax and imprisonment. The ordinary limitation period is five years, extended to ten years in cases of intentional fraud or serious tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a participating Member State of the European Monetary Union; the euro is fully convertible. There is no exchange control law, no capital transfer authorization regime, and no restriction on current or capital account movements under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Profit and capital remittances are not subject to FX control hurdles. The SCSp, being a transparent partnership for Luxembourg tax purposes, benefits from the same unrestricted capital movement principles; however, any bank transaction is subject to normal EU AML reporting, CSSF supervision, and counterparty compliance screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no legal requirement to repatriate or liquidate export receipts in Luxembourg or any other Eurozone jurisdiction. Proceeds may be retained in any currency in a euro-denominated or multicurrency account, subject only to standard banking documentation and AML/CFT record-keeping under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004, as amended, and EU Regulation 2015/847.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem framework, alongside the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for foreign exchange and balance-of-payments oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA offshore digital EMI providers and High-Medium for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) exercising a valid passport into Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2, Directive (EU) 2015/2366). An offshore EMI without an EEA passport cannot lawfully hold client funds or provide payment accounts for the SCSp. Even with a valid passport, an EMI may only issue e-money and provide payment services; it may not operate as a full commercial bank for purposes of taking deposits, holding capital under an escrow with bank-guarantee features, or granting overdraft/trade credit. The principal legal constraint is the CSSF’s requirement that all payment services providers conduct their own, non-delegable AML/KYC assessment of the SCSp, its general partner, the registered office, and all beneficial owners. The EMI is therefore viable only as a supplementary settlement rail, not as the primary treasury bank for UHT plant construction and trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the general partner of the SCSp to issue a capital call notice and receive funds via SWIFT or TARGET2 into the designated operational account. There is no Luxembourg exchange control approval requirement, but each inbound transfer must be tagged with clear remittance information such as 'SCSp capital contribution – UHT plant project' or 'Limited partner loan under section 4 of the partnership agreement'. The bank will apply standard AML/KYC verification of the remitter, the underlying partnership deed, the RCS registration, and the source of funds. Purpose codes used by correspondent banks may be generated automatically from the beneficiary field; however, the SCSp must ensure that the remitter states the OECD or local category code for foreign direct investment in manufacturing (Type: direct investment, sector ISIC 1050) when required by the remitting jurisdiction, while Luxembourg itself imposes no reporting beyond the BCL statistical balance-of-payments declaration. For amounts exceeding EUR 10,000 or any cash equivalent, EU Regulation 2015/847 payment information requirements apply and must be embedded in the wire transfer message. Inbound USD or third-currency wires should be routed through a US or intermediary correspondent in accordance with OFAC sanctions screening; the receiving Luxembourg bank will use the MT103 or Fedwire cover payment instructions and will reconcile the final credit to the SCSp's EUR or multicurrency account.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 / MT202 for direct wire transfers; for supplier payments in Europe, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer within the Single Euro Payments Area; for structural equipment and packing-line purchases, Commercial Letters of Credit governed by ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) and the eUCP. TARGET2 is the mandated euro settlement system for all large-value EUR transactions and ensures irrevocable final settlement. For commodity contracts involving oat concentrate and packaging film, the SCSp should use an irrevocable, confirming bank LC confirmed by the seller’s bank; the corresponding documents will be negotiated through SWIFT MT700, MT710, MT720, and MT754 messages. The bank may require a separate reimbursement account for LC maturities; outbound payments must be accompanied by invoices, delivery confirmations, and inspection certificates to meet KYC and anti-money-laundering transaction monitoring. The SCSp can also use the same bank’s trade finance front end to create electronic bank payment obligations (EBO) for supply-chain finance, provided the beneficiary bank accepts parametrized payment undertakings. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA offshore digital EMI providers and High-Medium for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) exercising a valid passport into Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2, Directive (EU) 2015/2366). An offshore EMI without an EEA passport cannot lawfully hold client funds or provide payment accounts for the SCSp. Even with a valid passport, an EMI may only issue e-money and provide payment services; it may not operate as a full commercial bank for purposes of taking deposits, holding capital under an escrow with bank-guarantee features, or granting overdraft/trade credit. The principal legal constraint is the CSSF’s requirement that all payment services providers conduct their own, non-delegable AML/KYC assessment of the SCSp, its general partner, the registered office, and all beneficial owners. The EMI is therefore viable only as a supplementary settlement rail, not as the primary treasury bank for UHT plant construction and trade disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate for corporate partners in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax); SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and not a CIT taxpayer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on SCSp profit distributions because the SCSp is pass-through; had the profit been paid as a dividend by an opaque Luxembourg company, the statutory rate would be 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and CbCR threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group turnover. Local File: no separate statutory monetary threshold; the Luxembourg tax administration may request supporting TP documentation for any related-party transaction in an audit. Administrative expectation: a full local-file TP study should be in place when related-party cross-border turnover exceeds EUR 1,000,000 per annum.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it applies OECD BEPS minimum standards, the ATAD anti-abuse rules, and Luxembourg GAAR. The SCSp must demonstrate real physical substance, local management, personnel, and economic purpose in Luxembourg to avoid reclassification as an abusive shell and to obtain treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT omission

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by ACD plus default interest at 0.6% per month; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the SCSp has unlimited liability; directors who knowingly signed false declarations are jointly and severally liable for taxes, interest, and fines, and may face asset seizure and travel bans.
Shell Company Misuse / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp under GAAR, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, reclassification of profit distributions as taxable dividends, and application of 24.94% aggregate CIT plus net wealth tax at partner level.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold the general partner and de facto directors personally liable for reinstated tax liabilities, including concealed partnership debts and pecuniary penalties.
AML / UBO Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons and criminal penalties including imprisonment for up to five years for natural persons; the regulator may block distributions and revoke the partnership's license if required.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers, directors and the managing general partner are personally liable for fines and civil confiscation of illicit profits; repeated violations result in disqualification from managing Luxembourg entities.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may adjust profits to arm's-length, levy a 10% documentation penalty and monthly interest, and recharacterize excess royalty as a taxable deemed distribution subject to 15% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for the restored corporation tax and dividend withholding tax; professional directors who approved false TP files face joint liability and disqualification.

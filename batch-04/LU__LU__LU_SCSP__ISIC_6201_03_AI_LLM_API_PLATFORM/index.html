Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, Articles 905-1 et seq. (SCSp regime introduced by Law of 12 July 2013); Civil Code, Book III, Title X.

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of Formation (Contrat de Société or Authentic Notarial Deed) filed via RCS e-file and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés (RES)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital. Contributions are freely determined in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is required for cash contributions.

Funding & unlock path: For a leased office/data-centre suite, obtain the fire-safety certification under the Luxembourg Fire Safety Code, verify electrical installation conformity and structural load certificates, and comply with Article L.411-10 of the Labour Code for occupant health and safety. If the gateway is purely virtual with no physical office, a registered agent address suffices and no site-specific occupational clearance is required.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital. Contributions are freely determined in the partnership agreement. No escrow account is required for cash contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; consolidated electronic filing portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

The SCSp, being an unincorporated special limited partnership, is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax purposes but must register for VAT when making Luxembourg-taxable supplies. A non-established operator must file the VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu prior to the first taxable activity. Required package: partnership agreement (contrat de société), RCS extract (where the SCSp is registered), identity documents of the general partner, and proof of business establishment. The AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU followed by 8 alphanumeric characters); the ACD uses the RCS matricule number for corporate tax records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Default fiscal transparency applies to the SCSp; partners are taxed individually in their respective jurisdictions. Under Article 175 LIR, the partnership may elect, subject to statutory conditions, to be taxed as a corporate taxpayer. Luxembourg's anti-hybrid rules (Law of 21 December 2018 transposing ATAD) re-characterize mismatching transparent entities and deny benefits where double non-taxation arises. There is no U.S.-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is governed by domestic LIR principles and OECD partnership report guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established taxable persons: the first euro of Luxembourg-taxable supplies triggers VAT registration. Domestic small-business exemption (franchise d'assujettissement) applies up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover for service providers. Intra-EU distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C digital services are subject to the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold, above which the OSS/IOSS regimes apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to third-country destinations qualify for a 0% VAT rate upon export. For AI/LLM API services (electronically supplied services), place-of-supply rules apply: B2B supplies to non-Luxembourg customers are outside Luxembourg VAT (reverse charge), B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT, and B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's member state via OSS. Zero-rating for services is substantiated by documented customer location, business status, and use-and-enjoyment analysis rather than physical customs clearance.

  • Executed AI/LLM API service agreement stating customer jurisdiction and service delivery terms
  • VIES-validated VAT identification number of the B2B customer inside the EU
  • Business register extract or equivalent corporate proof for non-EU B2B customers
  • Tax invoice with correct place-of-supply coding and VAT treatment statement
  • Inward remittance / FX settlement evidence (SWIFT, Stripe, or USDC on-chain records) supporting repatriation

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax reporting, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing. Invoices must satisfy Article 75 LGTVA content requirements; OSS/IOSS returns are filed through the EU single portal administered by the AEDT. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package is expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for Luxembourg taxable persons by 2030.

The AEDT anti-fraud unit (ULAF) and the ACD audit division may challenge export zero-rating and OSS positions. Unsupported claims trigger VAT reassessment, statutory late interest, and fines; fraudulent schemes expose the entity to criminal penalties (imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 250,000), retrospective clawback of input VAT credits, publication of judgments, and cross-border intelligence sharing through Eurofisc.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full EMU member state; the euro is fully and freely convertible for both current account payments (AI subscription fees, API royalties, cloud hosting costs, licensing) and capital account transactions (capital contributions to the SCSp, profit distributions, and liquidation proceeds). No exchange-control restrictions, conversion licenses, or repatriation permits apply. Non-resident partners may remit partnership earnings without BCL approval, subject only to general EU AML/CFT due diligence, tax transparency obligations (e.g., CRS, FATCA, DAC6 where relevant), economic substance expectations, and compliance with EU/UN sanctions. For a tax-transparent SCSp, partnership distributions are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, but the bank may require an up-to-date beneficial ownership declaration and proof of the distribution resolution before processing the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period obligation for export receipts. All proceeds from AI LLM API services, denominated in EUR or foreign currency, may be kept on account, reinvested, or transferred abroad without BCL consent. The only ongoing administrative obligations are statistical classification under EU Balance of Payments (BOP) Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 and BCL reporting instructions: receipts for software-enabled API services are classified under the BOP services item 'Telecommunications, computer and information services'. No timeline or conversion percentage is prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting in conjunction with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance, the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CFT supervision, and the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro monetary policy and BOP statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for an unregulated overseas EMI, Low for non-EEA digital banks. Luxembourg law permits the use of EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) for payment processing, but established tier-1 Luxembourg banks generally reject EMIs as the primary treasury account for a partnership with non-resident partners because the EMI cannot guarantee local beneficiary AML, sanctions, and economic substance oversight. A viable hybrid model is to use a regulated EU EMI (e.g., a CSSF/ECB-licensed EMI) only as a low-value middleware for streaming API customer settlement, while the main SCSp treasury and all capital flows are cleared through a Luxembourg commercial bank. Any cryptocurrency-backed EMI or crypto-currency payment gateway (USDC/crypto) must be isolated from corporate treasury and can be used only for customer invoicing if an EU-located, AML-compliant payment service provider handles conversion to fiat and passes clean funds to the bank account. Full bank KYC of the EMI fallback is mandatory.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident corporate partners without a regulated manager or local substance

Root cause: Non-resident corporate partners without a regulated manager or local substance

Defense evidence: Updated partnership agreement (contrat de société); notarised certificate of registered office from the Luxembourg notary; RCS excerpt (extrait RCS) proving valid registration of the SCSp; Luxembourg tax authority confirmation of EORI and VAT status; detailed ownership structure chart identifying all ultimate beneficial owners; CRS self-certifications and FATCA forms for each partner; proof of source of funds through bank references, audited financial statements, or certified tax filings; professional reference letters from an EU lawyer/accountant.
Use of cryptocurrency or USDC in the customer payment flow

Root cause: Use of cryptocurrency or USDC in the customer payment flow

Defense evidence: Written business model narrative explaining token-token and token-fiat settlement; list of regulated flat-on-ramp partners and blockchain analytics reports (e.g., Chainalysis or Elliptic) showing the absence of high-risk wallet exposure; a group AML policy compliant with AMLD5/AMLD6; customer onboarding and identity verification procedures for API subscribers; detailed invoices for all commercial transactions; confirmation that no crypto-to-crypto transfers occur from treasury accounts.
High transaction velocity relative to declared substance and partnership equity

Root cause: High transaction velocity relative to declared substance and partnership equity

Defense evidence: Substantiated 24-month business plan and financial projections; explanation of the SCSp's operating role and revenue model; evidence of Luxembourg economic substance such as local office lease, local manager or service provider contract, local bank account, and board of partners minutes; sample customer contracts with AI API usage terms; work-flow diagrams connecting the SCSp, the monetised IP, and the API platform; bank reference letters and tax clearance certificates from relevant jurisdictions.
Opaque beneficial ownership or offshore tax haven exposure

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership or offshore tax haven exposure

Defense evidence: A signed declaration confirming the SCSp's place of effective management and tax-filing position; beneficial ownership register (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs) confirmation or, if the SCSp has no natural-person beneficial owner, a legal memorandum with a negative declaration; validity proof of all corporate partners; residency certificates and public economic substance evidence for each partner; a copy of the audit mandate if the partnership is voluntarily audited; regulatory and reputational-due-diligence reports on partners.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound non-resident capital contributions must be wired to the SCSp's operational or escrow account using the SWIFT MT103 format. The payment must clearly reference the partnership legal name, the SCSp's RCS registration number, the injection resolution or subscription agreement, and the BOP direct-investment purpose code. For equity contributions, the ECB/BOP code is F.432 (direct investment – equity capital); for loans from partners, F.431 (direct investment – other capital) must be used. Correspondent clearing should run through TARGET2 for EUR wires or via the SWIFT network for USD/EUR SWIFT MT103 messages. Intermediary banks must be provided with the recipient bank's BIC, the SCSp's IBAN, and the beneficiary account label. The bank may require the partners' source-of-funds evidence, CRS/FATCA certifications, an executed capital contribution deed, and confirmation that the SCSp is not subject to EU sanctions or restrictive measures before crediting the account.Outbound trade settlements and operational payments from the SCSp should be routed as follows: (i) EUR disbursements to EU counterparties via TARGET2/SEPA with a SEPA Credit Transfer or, for large-value, a SWIFT MT103 with TARGET2 clearing; (ii) non-EUR payments via SWIFT MT103 with the correspondent bank's dedicated currency clearing; (iii) trade-related commitments or infrastructure hardware purchases using a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707 issued under UCP 600) or a standby LC / bank payment undertaking for deferred obligations; (iv) recurring AI cloud costs and contractor payments via MT103 or SEPA direct debit with invoice scanning for BCL BOP reporting.

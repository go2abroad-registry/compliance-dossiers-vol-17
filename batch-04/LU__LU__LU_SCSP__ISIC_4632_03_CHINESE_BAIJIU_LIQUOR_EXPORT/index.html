Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax purposes; there is no domestic check-the-box regime. Available elections: (i) upon unanimous request of all partners, the SCSp may irrevocably opt under Article 175bis LIR to be treated as a taxable corporation (corporate income tax and municipal business tax), but the option is restricted by the ATAD I/II implementation and is generally inadvisable for a cross-border export platform; (ii) hybrid mismatch alignment elections under Article 168sexies LIR allow the SCSp to align its transparency classification with a foreign jurisdiction to avoid deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (iii) a VAT grouping election under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law permits the SCSp to join a Luxembourg VAT group as a single taxable person.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign entities: no registration threshold — mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg. Luxembourg-established entities: annual turnover exemption of EUR 35,000 under Article 57 of the VAT Law, but the small business exemption does not cover export-linked supplies and is excluded for entities engaged in VAT-exempt export operations needing input VAT recovery; in practice an exporting SCSp registers from inception.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime applies in Luxembourg as of 2025. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary (subject to customer consent). E-invoicing via PEPPOL is mandatory only for B2G invoices to Luxembourg public-sector entities. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically through the AED's eCDF portal on a monthly or quarterly periodicity. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework is expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies from 2030, but no such mandate is currently operative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Luxembourg applies the EU free movement of capital and payments (Article 63 TFEU), allowing unrestricted current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance by the SCSp is not subject to exchange control approval or scheduled convertibility restrictions. Any practical exit depends on the bank's AML/KYC verification and tax documentation rather than FX permission. The SCSp is fiscally transparent, so profit distribution follows the partnership agreement and the partners' tax residence; no Luxembourg booking of retained export proceeds is blocked unless a sanctions or freezing measure applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender, minimum conversion ratio, or retention timeline applies in Luxembourg/Eurozone. Exporters may keep CNY, USD or other permitted currencies in an authorised credit institution. Cross-border trade settlements are subject only to ordinary BCL/CSSF reporting, including monthly balance-of-payments statistical declarations by resident banks for large flows, customer due diligence, and suspicious transaction reporting to the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the integrated financial and AML supervisor; Ministry of Finance for financial-services legislation; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to moderate. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EEA authorisation cannot lawfully provide EUR IBAN accounts or access TARGET2/SEPA clearing. An EEA-authorised EMI can be used for ancillary multi-currency FX conversion, but EMIs generally do not issue documentary letters of credit and many reject partnerships or distributors of alcoholic beverages under trade-based AML risk policy. The SCSp should therefore treat EMIs as a supplementary operational rail, never the primary clearing or escrow rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% (standard CIT for profits above EUR 200,000); 24.94% aggregate rate in Luxembourg City including municipal business tax and solidarity surcharge

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% for dividends paid by a Luxembourg fully taxable corporate entity; SCSp distributions are not dividends and are outside WHT. If the SCSp opts for opaque corporate taxation, distributions are subject to 15% WHT (0% under parent-subsidiary privilege or applicable DTA).

Box 2 (Tax year) Opening and closing dates of the fiscal year; exact accounting reference period; any short or first-year period. Use the same fiscal-year dates as the SCSp financial statements; align with the partnership agreement's accounting period. Late filing: administrative fine up to EUR 10,000; monthly late-payment interest 0.6%; default surcharge up to 10% of assessed tax; false declarations attract criminal penalties up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Box 3 (Net result) Net pre-tax profit or loss per the audited/unaudited financial statements before partner allocations. Report amounts in EUR; foreign partners must receive their allocable share in the SCSp's functional currency, translated to EUR. Late filing: administrative fine up to EUR 10,000; monthly late-payment interest 0.6%; default surcharge up to 10% of assessed tax; false declarations attract criminal penalties up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Box 4 (Adjustments and exemptions) Add back non-deductible expenses (fines, non-arm's-length payments, excessive management fees); deduct tax-exempt income (participation exemption, IP box exemption, foreign PE relief). Non-resident partners may only claim adjustments related to Luxembourg-source income (PE, real estate) or items expressly covered by a tax treaty. Foreign partners report their Luxembourg PE income on separate non-resident returns; no dividend withholding tax applies to a transparent SCSp distribution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 annual value of related-party transactions (goods/services/financial) triggers a mandatory Local File; groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million must also file a Country-by-Country Report and Master File.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Luxembourg ESR code; however, the ATAD GAAR and the OECD/Cadbury-Schweppes jurisprudence require real economic substance to claim treaty benefits and deduct intercompany expenses. An SCSp exporting baijiu must maintain local substance: registered office, local management decisions, operational staff, a Luxembourg bank account, and supporting contractual evidence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Luxembourg General Tax Law and Criminal Code Art. 391; additional civil penalties of up to 100% of evaded tax plus 0.6% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Managers/directors who sign false returns are personally liable for evaded taxes, civil restitution, criminal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent acts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; recharacterization of income; additional CIT/municipal tax up to 24.94%; penalties up to 10% of the reassessed tax; in egregious cases, judicial dissolution under Luxembourg company law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for resulting tax assessments and unlawful distributions; travel bans and director disqualification can follow where fraud or negligence is shown.
AML and Licensing Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1 million under Luxembourg AML law; criminal sanctions up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment; revocation of licence and debanking.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and directors face personal criminal liability, pecuniary sanctions, director disqualification up to 10 years, and travel bans for serious breaches.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by the Luxembourg tax authorities, plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; separate penalty up to EUR 25,000 for missing documentation; criminal tax fraud sanctions if wilful.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation are exposed to criminal conviction, personal civil liability for the group's tax exposure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.

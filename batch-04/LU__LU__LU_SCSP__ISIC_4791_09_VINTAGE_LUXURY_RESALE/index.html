Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l’enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT and indirect taxes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – direct taxes; electronic filing via MyGuichet.lu / eCDF portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first be inscribed with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS/LBR) to obtain a business registration number ('numéro d'immatriculation'). For VAT, the taxable person files the registration request with the AEDT through the 'eCDF' e-filing platform (form 'Formulaire 2015 – Déclaration de début d'activité' or the dedicated non-established taxable person registration). The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits, with check code) without any registration threshold for non-established operators. Direct tax TIN for non-resident partners is the 'matricule' assigned by the ACD for withholding/assessment purposes. The representative procedure: foreign entities without EU establishment may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative for registration if they are not established in the EU, subject to AEDT approval.

Tax ID type & application path: SCSp (Société en commandite spéciale / Special LP) is fiscally transparent (Pass-Through_Eligible) by statutory default: no corporate income tax, no Luxembourg net wealth tax, no municipal business tax at entity level (unless the SCSp is treated as carrying on a commercial activity for municipal business tax purposes). Income is allocated to and taxed at partner level. Although transparent for direct tax, the SCSp is a distinct VAT person and will be VAT registered in its own name. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

SCSp (Société en commandite spéciale / Special LP) is fiscally transparent (Pass-Through_Eligible) by statutory default: no corporate income tax, no Luxembourg net wealth tax, no municipal business tax at entity level (unless the SCSp is treated as carrying on a commercial activity for municipal business tax purposes). Income is allocated to and taxed at partner level. Although transparent for direct tax, the SCSp is a distinct VAT person and will be VAT registered in its own name. Cross-border partners: transparency applies only if the partnership classification is recognized in the partner's jurisdiction; otherwise hybrid mismatches arise.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. The SCSp's transparency is statutory and non-elective for income/wealth tax (no domestic opacity election for SCSp). Limited alignment elections are available under the ATAD/hybrid-mismatch rules: a taxpayer may apply the OECD nexus approach to align the tax treatment of the transparent entity with the classification in the investor's jurisdiction, and may elect to treat the SCSp as a 'fiscally transparent entity' under Article 2(1)(m) LIR for treaty-look-through. An additional statutory 'option' exists for a Luxembourg partnership to request to be treated as taxable for municipal business tax purposes if it conducts a commercial business (no comparable domestic election for CIT).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for established small businesses (franchise regime under Art. 57 VAT Code). For non-established/non-resident taxable persons there is NO registration threshold: VAT registration is mandatory immediately upon the first taxable transaction performed in Luxembourg (distance-selling exemption EUR 10,000 intra-Community applies under the OSS regime, EU-wide, not as a Luxembourg registration threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated when physically exported from the EU by the seller or by the customer (non-established) outside the EU, provided the goods leave the EU within the EU legal deadline (3 months from supply date under Art. 146(j) Directive 2006/112/EC; in Luxembourg within the period documented by CMR/B/L/Customs stamp, generally verified). Conditions: transfer of the right to dispose of goods to the customer, proof of dispatch/transport outside of the EU, and export declaration accepted by Customs (Art. 54 VAT Code). Intra-Community supply to a VAT-registered EU customer also 0% if the customer's VAT number is quoted and EC Sales List filed. Services: B2B services to a non-established customer fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply = customer jurisdiction), and B2C services are taxable in Luxembourg only when effectively enjoyed there; qualifying cross-border services related to goods (export handling, logistics) may be zero-rated. Margin scheme (Art. 57 VAT Code) applies to the sale of second-hand/vintage luxury goods, so VAT is chargeable only on the margin, not on the full selling price; when goods are exported, zero rating applies to the margin.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no SdI/CTC (continuous transaction controls), no digital stamping or API-based clearance in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is legally valid (equal to paper under Art. 10 QR / Directive 2014/55/EU); the Government supports Peppol BIS infrastructure voluntarily and since 2021 mandates Peppol only for receiving invoices from Central Government suppliers (not for traders). VAT compliance is report-based: e-filed periodic returns, EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations via the eCDF portal; there is no live API tax clearance obligation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a euro-area member state, imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The euro is fully convertible; profit remittances, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable abroad after satisfying applicable corporate law formalities and tax reporting obligations. No central bank approval is required for cross-border transfers, although EU sanctions regimes, freeze orders, and CSSF AML/CFT reporting obligations must be observed. The absence of capital controls and the full application of EU capital-movement freedom make the Luxembourg SCSp a highly efficient treasury vehicle for inbound and outbound flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg maintains no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratios or timelines for foreign currency receipts. Exporters and e-commerce operators may retain all receipts in foreign currency accounts with Luxembourg credit institutions. Cross-border transactions are subject only to the BCL's statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations under EU/ECB data collection rules, requiring accurate purpose coding and record retention, but not the sale or repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent authority for banking prudential supervision and anti-money-laundering compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions operating under the EU passport. An EEA-based EMI registered with the CSSF or passported into Luxembourg can open settlement accounts and provide payment services to the SCSp, provided it holds appropriate safeguarding accounts with a credit institution. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits or grant credit; their activities are limited to payment transactions, e-money issuance, and remittances. They must comply with CSSF AML/CFT rules and the EU Payment Services Directive. For escrow and luxury resale flows, an EMI combined with a commercial bank account offers operational efficiency without replacing bank-held treasury buffers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and capital injections to the SCSp must be routed through a licensed Luxembourg credit institution or an EEA-authorized EMI. For direct equity subscription, use SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires with the beneficiary being the SCSp's bank account and the remitter stated as the investing legal entity or individual. The wire reference should include the purpose code under the ECB/BOP reporting framework: for direct investment, use the EU Balance of Payments code for 'Direct investment – equity capital' (category F2/F6 or equivalent). Mandatory verification documents include the capital commitment letter, notarized subscription document, partnership agreement extract, anti-money-laundering source-of-funds evidence, and proof of economic substance in Luxembourg if required. For escrow payouts from marketplaces, funds should be routed to a designated virtual account or settlement sub-account with clear remittance information linking to the corresponding sale transaction.Trade settlements from the SCSp to overseas luxury suppliers or consignment sellers are executed by SWIFT MT103 for amounts below bank-specific thresholds and where documentary checks are limited. Larger and higher-risk purchase transactions should be supported by an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by a Luxembourg or EU bank, transmitted via SWIFT MT700, and settled against presentation of compliant shipping, insurance, and commercial invoice documents. For continuous e-commerce payouts, the SCSp may use the payment rails of licensed EMIs or the bank's host-host API, with same-day settlement in euros and SWIFT or SEPA-based beneficiary payments. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City aggregate; 17% corporate income tax plus 7% unemployment/solidarity surcharge plus 6.75% municipal business tax; the first tranche of taxable profits below EUR 175,000 may benefit from the reduced 15% CIT bracket)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on Luxembourg-source dividends; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when a 10% qualifying shareholding has been held for 12 months; treaty relief often reduces the rate to 5% or 15%. For a transparent SCSp, periodic distributions are not recharacterised as Luxembourg-source dividends for entity-level WHT, but partners remain taxable in their own jurisdiction of residence)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by 31 May of the following calendar year; the Luxembourg tax administration commonly grants extensions to 31 December. Quarterly instalment payments are normally required during the year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

Box 1 / Identification Enter the SCSp's RCS number, 13-digit Luxembourg national registration number, legal name, registered office, legal form code, and fiscal year-end. Non-resident partners are not separately listed unless they have a Luxembourg permanent establishment or the ACD requests a transparency schedule; attach a schedule of partner identities and residence countries. Late filing or non-filing may trigger an administrative increase of up to 10% of the tax due, late-payment interest of 0.6% per month capped at 12%, fixed administrative fines where no tax is due, and, in cases of wilful persistent non-compliance, criminal proceedings with fines and imprisonment.

Only the share of income attributable to a Luxembourg fixed base or permanent establishment is taxable in Luxembourg. Remote e-commerce sales fulfilled and managed outside Luxembourg should be excluded for non-resident partners. Box 4 / Loss Carryforward Schedule Claim deductible tax losses carried forward within the permitted limit and attach a schedule showing year of origin, amount, and offsetting in the current year. A non-resident partner may offset only losses that are economically attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment; foreign-source losses cannot be claimed on the Luxembourg declaration. Late filing or non-filing may trigger an administrative increase of up to 10% of the tax due, late-payment interest of 0.6% per month capped at 12%, fixed administrative fines where no tax is due, and, in cases of wilful persistent non-compliance, criminal proceedings with fines and imprisonment.
Box 5 / Related-Party Transactions Disclose all controlled transactions with associated enterprises, including inventory purchases from related luxury-good suppliers, management fees, loans, and any IP or brand licence fees. Non-resident partners should separately report cross-border related-party transactions in their home jurisdiction; the Luxembourg entity must support the allocation with transfer pricing documentation where applicable. Late filing or non-filing may trigger an administrative increase of up to 10% of the tax due, late-payment interest of 0.6% per month capped at 12%, fixed administrative fines where no tax is due, and, in cases of wilful persistent non-compliance, criminal proceedings with fines and imprisonment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 250,000 (Local File is mandatory when controlled related-party transactions exceed EUR 250,000 in the fiscal year; a Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million)

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the ACD enforces substance requirements through Article 56 LIR, OECD BEPS principles, and the general anti-avoidance rule. The SCSp must demonstrate real activity — premises, employees, strategic decision-making, board records, bank accounts, and commercial risk — in order to preserve pass-through tax treatment, treaty benefits, and the arm's-length character of cross-border transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Conscious Omission in Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of additional tax plus 0.6% monthly default interest, administrative penalty of up to 20% of the deficient tax, forfeiture of hidden assets, and criminal prosecution with court-imposed fines and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors or the General Partner can be held jointly and severally liable for tax debts when they knowingly participated in concealment; courts may pierce the corporate veil, imposing civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment.
Artificial or Shell SCSp with Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence, denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of profits in the partner's residence jurisdiction, application of the GAAR, and an administrative penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted tax; commercial registry dissolution may also be pursued.

Director personal liability: The General Partner and de facto managers may be personally assessed if the entity is treated as a sham; beneficial partners lose treaty protection, become taxable in their home state, and may be subject to director disqualification and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Primary income adjustment plus compensatory interest, ad valorem penalty of 10-20% of the adjustment for deliberate non-compliance, and separate court-ordered fines for incomplete documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may face personal criminal liability; the tax adjustment may be treated as constructive distribution, exposing officers to civil restitution and cross-border collection measures.
Anti-Money Laundering and KYC Failure in Luxury Resale

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Luxembourg AML law of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money-laundering sanctions under the Luxembourg Criminal Code may reach ten years' imprisonment for aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers, compliance officers, directors, and beneficial owners can be personally fined and imprisoned; authorities may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and professional disqualification.
Failure to File Statutory Tax Return or Transfer Pricing Declaration

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of 10% of the tax payable, monthly interest of 0.6% capped at 12%, fixed administrative fines when the return shows nil liability, and criminal sanction for persistent refusal to file after formal notice.

Director personal liability: Because the General Partner of an SCSp is the legal representative, it may be held personally liable for filing penalties; persistent non-compliance can result in personal fines, contempt sanctions, and prison time for the responsible individuals.

Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended (Articles 1, 25, and 180-238 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée); implementing regulations of the Law of 19 December 2008 on the Trade and Companies Register (RCS); Grand-Ducal Regulation on registration and publication fees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation) filed with the RCS, with publication of the extract in the Recueil Electronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 73 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up before notarization; funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the future company with a Luxembourg credit institution, and the bank's blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) must be delivered to the notary.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) in coordination with the fire brigade (CGDIS); structural load verification for laboratory and storage areas; workplace occupational health and safety clearance under Articles L. 311 et seq. of the Luxembourg Labour Code, including risk assessment and emergency response plans.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up before notarization; funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the future company with a Luxembourg credit institution, and the bank's blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) must be delivered to the notary.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT/TVA) for indirect taxes; official e-government portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu; company filings via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) at lbr.lu

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) execute notarial deed of incorporation and submit to Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) for RCS registration; (2) upon RCS registration the company automatically receives a Luxembourg identification number (IDE/matricule) used for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax filings; (3) file a separate VAT registration application with the AEDT/TVA (no notarisation required) to obtain a Luxembourg VAT ID in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) register with Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) if employing staff; (5) activate MyGuichet authentication for online tax filings; non-EU parent entities may need to provide certified copies of constitutional documents, ultimate beneficial owner declarations, and proof of authorised signatories.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) execute notarial deed of incorporation and submit to Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) for RCS registration; (2) upon RCS registration the company automatically receives a Luxembourg identification number (IDE/matricule) used for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax filings; (3) file a separate VAT registration application with the AEDT/TVA (no notarisation required) to obtain a Luxembourg VAT ID in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) register with Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) if employing staff; (5) activate MyGuichet authentication for online tax filings; non-EU parent entities may need to provide certified copies of constitutional documents, ultimate beneficial owner declarations, and proof of authorised signatories.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Available elections include fiscal unity (intégration fiscale, Article 164bis LIR) enabling residence-based consolidated CIT/MBT/NWT computations for at least 95% owned resident group companies; VAT grouping (unité TVA) election for closely linked Luxembourg taxable persons; optional joint taxation for spouses for individuals (irrelevant for SARL). Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; tax classification is determined by legal form. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD) apply for cross-border arrangements, limiting deduction and potentially using anti-hybrid countermeasures rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual taxable turnover (small-enterprise VAT franchise cap above which registration is compulsory); however, non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies of goods or services physically performed in Luxembourg are required to register or appoint a fiscal representative regardless of the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Claim 0% VAT provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU within the statutory period (normally 90 days from date of supply, subject to proof), supported by a single customs export declaration (SAA/ELS) and transport documents. Services: Clinical trial management services supplied to a taxable customer in another EU Member State are subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction and are outside Luxembourg VAT; supplies to non-EU taxable persons are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT. Supplies to non-EU private individuals of B2C cross-border services may also be outside Luxembourg VAT if the place of supply is the customer's country, and must be evidenced by service contracts, performance records, and customer documentation; they are not 'zero-rated' in the export-of-goods sense but can be unbilled for Luxembourg VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (SAA/ELS) containing the movement reference number (MRN)
  • Transport document: bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or rail waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of physical exit from EU customs territory (e.g., exit confirmation, port/airport stamp or ICS declaration status)
  • Payment evidence / inward remittance certificate confirming receipt from the non-resident customer
  • If applicable, signed statement of use and enjoyment outside Luxembourg for services

No mandatory real-time clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction controls currently in force. E-invoicing is accepted if authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are ensured (e.g., EDI, Peppol). E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices to central Luxembourg government entities using the Peppol-BIS format. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposal, mandatory transaction-based digital reporting and e-invoicing for intra-EU B2B supplies is expected to apply from 2026 at the earliest, with transitional rules through 2035.

Unsubstantiated export zero-rating or incorrect place-of-supply treatment results in reassessment of VAT at the domestic rate (17%), late interest at the annual rate applicable to tax debts (currently 8% per annum), and an administrative fine of up to 10% of the under-declared tax for non-deliberate errors. Intentional VAT evasion or fraud can lead to criminal penalties, including fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years (Article 84 of the Luxembourg VAT law). The tax administration may retrospectively reassess returns within a standard five-year limitation period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud, and may apply a 100% clawback of input VAT previously recovered where a transaction is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) in Luxembourg enjoys full de jure and de facto current-account and capital-account convertibility, guaranteed by Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital and payments). No exchange-control regulation, no inward or outward transfer licensing, no prior authorization for foreign direct investment, no limits on profit or dividend remittance, and no currency surrender or retention rules apply to residents or non-residents. Cross-border transfers in any currency are unrestricted; banks may require standard reason-for-payment declarations and CRS/FATCA self-certifications. The only operative restrictions flow from international sanctions regimes (EU/UN restrictive measures), which require blocking of prohibited transactions and reporting to the CSSF, and from AML/CFT obligations under the Law of 12 November 2004, as amended. A CRO settling global clinical trial payments in EUR, USD, GBP, or CHF may freely maintain multi-currency accounts, convert at commercial rates, and repatriate profits without currency-approval hurdles; dividend withholding tax applies at the standard 15% domestic rate and is reduced to 0% for qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive holdings of at least 10%, and interest/royalty payments are generally exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules exist in Luxembourg. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, no requirement to sell foreign currency export receipts to the BCL or any authorized bank, and no restriction on retaining foreign currency abroad. Invoiced CRO fees receivable in USD are free to be held in external accounts, converted at commercial market rates, or used directly for outbound clinical-site disbursements. The only applicable obligation is statistical: Luxembourg resident banks must report to the BCL all individual cross-border transactions at or above EUR 12,500 (or aggregated series) for balance-of-payments and European statistics purposes. This reporting is purely statistical and does not constitute authorization, licensing, or approval. Payments to or from sanctioned jurisdictions must be blocked and disclosed to the CSSF under Luxembourg law.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the central bank and the competent national authority within the European System of Central Banks; monetary policy is set by the European Central Bank (ECB) for the euro area. Prudential supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL operates the TARGET2 (T2) real-time gross settlement (RTGS) participation for Luxembourg, supervises payment and securities settlement systems (including LuxCSD), compiles balance-of-payments statistics, and administers the national electronic money and payment institution registries. There is no separate foreign exchange control authority; no exchange-control approval regime exists. The Ministère de la Santé and the Comité National d'Ethique pour la Recherche (CNER) govern clinical-trial licensing, which is the relevant sectoral permit for this CRO entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary collections or disbursements channel. Legal constraints: any payment institution or electronic money institution (EMI) operating in Luxembourg must be CSSF-authorized or validly passporting into Luxembourg under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the Law of 10 November 2009; a non-EEA offshore EMI (e.g., UK post-Brexit or Caribbean-licensed) cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Luxembourg established entity through reverse solicitation, and its virtual IBANs are commonly rejected by receiving clinical sites and investigator banks due to correspondent-tier opacity. Operational constraints: offshore digital EMIs typically do not provide a true IBAN in the beneficiary's name, lack T2 direct clearing, fail to satisfy Lux bank 'source of funds' traceability for capital injections, and create FATF/CRS ambiguity. Therefore, for a CRO receiving sponsor milestone wires and making high-volume investigative-site disbursements, the EMI should be used only as a secondary SEPA collection wallet, never as the custodial treasury account; concentration risk above EUR 50,000 in any non-bank EMI must be prohibited by treasury policy.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque UBO chain: non-resident holding structure with nominee shareholders or multi-tier intermediate SPVs in offshore jurisdictions (e.g., BVI, Cayman, Delaware) hiding natural-person ownership.

Root cause: Opaque UBO chain: non-resident holding structure with nominee shareholders or multi-tier intermediate SPVs in offshore jurisdictions (e.g., BVI, Cayman, Delaware) hiding natural-person ownership.

Defense evidence: Certified RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) registration certificate; complete ownership organogram to final natural persons with percentage holdings; notarized UBO declarations; compliance statement explaining the genuine commercial purpose of each intermediate entity; audited financial statements and conventional bank references for each SPV; and Luxembourg tax-law substance documentation (Article 1649/bis ITA and administrative circulars) evidencing real activity in Luxembourg.
Clinical trial payment pattern: high-frequency, high-volume outbound wires to foreign clinical investigator sites and contract hospitals, which could be mischaracterized as unlicensed pharmaceutical distribution or money laundering through pass-through invoices.

Root cause: Clinical trial payment pattern: high-frequency, high-volume outbound wires to foreign clinical investigator sites and contract hospitals, which could be mischaracterized as unlicensed pharmaceutical distribution or money laundering through pass-through invoices.

Defense evidence: Master Clinical Research Organization (CRO) service agreement with each sponsor; fully itemized milestone payment schedule mapping each wire to a trial protocol (EU-CTR number or national authorization); signed investigator site contracts and corresponding site invoices; Luxembourg Ministry of Health (Direction de la Santé) authorization and Comité National d'Ethique (CNER) favorable opinion for each trial; importation/IMP handling authorization where the CRO is importer of record; and a treasury flowchart showing funds pass-through and the CRO's service-fee margin.
Source-of-funds opacity for initial SARL capital injection, especially where shareholders use unduly complex funding or where the capital is paid from a jurisdiction with non-cooperative tax status.

Root cause: Source-of-funds opacity for initial SARL capital injection, especially where shareholders use unduly complex funding or where the capital is paid from a jurisdiction with non-cooperative tax status.

Defense evidence: Bank-to-bank SWIFT confirmation of the exact transfer trail; notarized acte constitutif and capital-subscription statement; notarized capital-increase minutes if initially undercapitalized; source-of-wealth statement with supporting brokerage statements, real-estate sale deeds, or professional income tax returns; confirming that no cash, crypto-currency, or bearer instrument is used; and a signed representation that funds derive from lawful economic activities verified by the transferring bank.
Tax residence and substance mismatch: non-resident directors based in high-risk or blacklisted countries, or a SARL that appears to be a pure letterbox with no local substance.

Root cause: Tax residence and substance mismatch: non-resident directors based in high-risk or blacklisted countries, or a SARL that appears to be a pure letterbox with no local substance.

Defense evidence: Certificate of tax residence of each individual director from the home state; official criminal-record extract and professional references from a previous banking relationship (banker's letter); PEP declaration and negative press-event search results; board meeting minutes physically drafted in Luxembourg; lease agreement for Luxembourg registered office and suitable substance (workstations, managed office, local CRO staff); Luxembourg social security attestations; and advance tax ruling or confirmation letter from the Luxembourg tax authorities regarding the SARL's effective management and place of management.
Sanctions and adverse-media flags: any counterparty, ultimate shareholder, or beneficiary linked to an EU/UN-sanctioned jurisdiction, or presence of a sanctioned bank in a transaction chain that could trigger forced asset freezes.

Root cause: Sanctions and adverse-media flags: any counterparty, ultimate shareholder, or beneficiary linked to an EU/UN-sanctioned jurisdiction, or presence of a sanctioned bank in a transaction chain that could trigger forced asset freezes.

Defense evidence: Validated negative sanctions-screening reports for all UBOs, directors, and material counterparties (real-time screening software output accepted by the bank); a robust sanctions compliance policy; the SARL's undertaking to notify the BCL/CSSF of any frozen funds within 24 hours; a list of all active clinical sites and sponsor legal entities pre-cleared against consolidated EU, OFAC, UN, and HMT lists; and legal-entity-identifier (LEI) certificates for the SARL and key counterparties to facilitate clean wire routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (1) For EEA-source injection in EUR – use a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) to the SARL's Luxembourg bank account, referencing the notarial deed and the SARL's EUR IBAN; settlement occurs same-day or next-day via TARGET2 (T2). (2) For non-EEA-source or non-EUR injection – send SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV where a reimbursement is required) from the shareholder's bank to the receiving Luxembourg bank, with purpose code 'EQUI' (equity capital) or 'INVC' (investment capital), and remittance information citing the Luxembourg RCS registration number and the date of the notarial capital-subscription deed; if the injection is in USD, the receiving bank may credit a USD sub-account or convert to EUR at a negotiated spot rate. Verification package: notarized acte constitutif or capital-increase deed; proof of shareholder board/partner approval; the receiving bank's capital receipt confirmation (which the notary files with the RCS and publishes in the RESA electronic company gazette); CRS and FATCA self-certifications of the shareholder; clear source-of-funds documentation; and evidence that the transferring bank is not a high-risk or sanctioned financial institution. All injections above EUR 12,500 are statistically reported to the BCL. No regulatory pre-approval is required.Clinical-trial disbursements: (1) EEA clinical-site and investigator payments – use SEPA Credit Transfer (standard or instant SCT) in EUR with full remittance references (invoice number, protocol number, site ID); settlement in T2 with real-time finality. (2) Non-EEA site payments (USA, UK, Switzerland, Asia) – use SWIFT MT103 for beneficiary disbursement or MT202 COV for cover payments, routed through the Luxembourg bank's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, CHAPS for GBP, SIC for CHF), always stating purpose code 'SERV' or 'GDSV' with invoice and protocol references. (3) Procurement of investigational medicinal products (IMP) where the CRO acts as importer of record – Commercial Letters of Credit (irrevocable, optionally confirmed) are the recommended clearing rail for supplier settlement, opened against a cash collateral cover or a committed credit line; the LC must reference the import permit and the EU-CTR protocol. (4) For sponsor overpayments or refunds – MT103/SCAN crediting of sponsor accounts using the original transaction reference. All outbound payments require positive beneficiary-name matching against sanctions and PEP databases at execution time; intra-group inter-company treasury transactions should be documented by intercompany agreements to avoid transfer-pricing challenge. The use of LuxCSD and T2 is relevant for securities and high-value settlement; standard commercial flows use the Swift FIN InterAct (ISO 20022) migrated rails.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with CRD IV-licensed Luxembourg or EEA counterparty banks, used to hedge the EUR-equivalent value of USD-denominated sponsor milestone receivables; no BCL or CSSF approval required for non-financial counterparties below EMIR clearing thresholds.FX swaps (swap de change) for short-term liquidity rebalancing between operations in EUR, USD, and GBP, particularly to bridge timing mismatches between USD milestone receipts and EUR clinical-site disbursements.Vanilla cross-currency swaps (under ISDA 2002 master agreement) for term-matched transformation of long-dated USD sponsor revenue streams into EUR floating or fixed liabilities; Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is 31 December of the calendar year following the closed fiscal year, i.e. for a SARL with a 31 December 2024 year-end, the return is due by 31 December 2025; the competent Luxembourg tax office may grant a discretionary filing extension but no automatic extension exists.

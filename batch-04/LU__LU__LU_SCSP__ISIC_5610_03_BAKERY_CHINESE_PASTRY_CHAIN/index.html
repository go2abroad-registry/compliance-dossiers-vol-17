Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines contributions. No escrow account or bank capital-blocking certificate is legally required, but contributions must be fully effective and verifiable at RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg direct tax authority: Administration des contributions directes (ACD, portal: myguichet.lu / eCDF). Indirect tax authority: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration and VAT returns.

E-government filing portal

A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is registered with the Luxembourg Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) to obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (matricule). For income tax purposes the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent, so no corporate income tax registration is required at partnership level unless otherwise elected or where it carries on a commercial activity with legal personality. For VAT, a foreign entity performing taxable activities in Luxembourg (such as a central kitchen or franchisor supplying goods/services through a Luxembourg SCSp) must submit a VAT registration application on the standard AEDT form (Formulaire no. 10) before commencing taxable supplies; the resulting Luxembourg VAT identification number (TVAN) is used for EU intra-community transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. An SCSp formed under the amended Law of 10 August 1915 is fiscally transparent by default, unless it opts to be treated as a taxable entity by making a formal election to be treated as a société en commandite simple (SCS) for corporate-law and therefore tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections required under ATAD may be made where applicable, but the tax transparency of the SCSp remains the default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 small business exemption threshold for Luxembourg resident taxable persons; non-established foreign entities are required to register for VAT from the first euro of taxable supplies made in Luxembourg, subject to EU distance-selling and reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods to a destination outside the European Union qualifies for VAT zero-rating when the seller retains proof of physical export, such as the customs export declaration (SAD/electronic declaration), transport documentation (CMR/AWB/Bill of Lading), and evidence that the goods left EU territory. Supplies of goods to a taxable person in another EU Member State are zero-rated if the customer's VAT identification number is valid and the goods are dispatched or transported to that Member State. Offshore services supplied to a non-EU customer may be outside of the scope Luxembourg VAT; supply of services to an EU taxable person is generally subject to reverse charge and therefore not a zero-rated export in the 'export' sense but reduces Luxembourg VAT burden.

  • Customs export declaration (Electronic SAD / Export Summary Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice (NG or purchase order and final price)
  • Proof of payment from foreign recipient (bank SWIFT / wire transfer instruction)
  • Intra-community declaration (if EU movement via Luxembourg and valid EU VAT IDs)

Mandatory electronic invoicing for business-to-government (B2G) transactions using the PEPPOL network; no statutory real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping or continuous transaction controls apply to private-sector B2B/B2C invoicing in Luxembourg.

Luxembourg AEDT may retrospectively claw back undeclared VAT and deny zero-rating where the supplier fails to provide adequate proof of export, misstates the nature of the supply, or participates in a missing-trader fraud. Penalties include fines up to EUR 20,000, default interest of 10% per annum, administrative penalties of up to EUR 250,000 under the General Tax Law for deliberate tax evasion, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment for aggravated VAT fraud. The SCSp and the general partner may be jointly and severally liable for VAT debts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full eurozone monetary integration; both capital and current account transactions are convertible without governmental exchange control permit. EU Treaty free movement of capital (TFEU Arts. 63-65) applies, and only standard AML/CFT, sanctioned-party screening, statistical reporting and tax withholding characterization obligations may apply. Profit remittance by an SCSp (e.g., partnership distributions to foreign partners) is not subject to FX approval; residual constraints are exclusively those of the remitting bank's KYC/onboarding policies and any withholding arising from sourced Luxembourg income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation, minimum conversion ratio, or mandatory repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Luxembourg. Export receipts may be retained in Euro or any freely convertible settlement currency, subject only to the general obligation to keep accounting records in Euro-equivalent terms and to report suspicious cross-border transactions under the amended Luxembourg AML/CFT law and applicable CSSF regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) share supervisory functions, although no standalone foreign exchange administration is constituted in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. A Luxembourg-authorised e-money institution (EMI) or payment institution supervised by the CSSF can provide a valid secondary payment account and cheaper cross-border clearing; offshore digital EMIs that are not EU/EEA-licensed generally cannot open a compliance-grade Luxembourg corporate account. Such fintech rails are legally viable for POS and royalty collection but should not replace a tier-1 commercial clearing bank for capital calls, treasury concentration, or LC-backed trade disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder-loan injections are transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or, within the EU, SEPA Credit Transfer from the investor's bank, using the correct money-creation purpose code (e.g., 'Capital Increase / Shareholder Loan' code). In Luxembourg no official approval is required, but the source country must be cleared first; Chinese outbound investment remittance under SAFE/FDI rules must be structured as a lawful equity subscription or intra-group loan with prior registration. Every inward credit must be matched against a capital call notice, a signed subscription document, and an updated UBO/sanctions-negative certification to avoid frozen funds.

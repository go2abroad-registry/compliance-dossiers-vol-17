Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS), administered by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), under the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended by the Luxembourg Law of 11 July 2013 creating the société en commandite spéciale (SCSp); applicable provisions on partnerships and special limited partnerships set forth in Articles 1, 13, 101-108 and 109-132.

Formation / constitutional filings

Constitutional contract (contrat de société) in private written or notarial form, together with the RCS registration declaration for an SCSp and the mandatory publication in the electronic compendium of legal entities (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations - RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow account. Contributions are fixed freely in the partnership agreement and may be made in cash, in kind, or as industry, provided they are actually and irrevocably made as agreed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for ordinary office premises under the applicable municipal fire and building safety regulations, including adequate exit routes and portable extinguishers. Occupational health and safety clearance must comply with the Luxembourg Labour Code and the inspections of the Labour Inspectorate (Inspection du Travail et des Mines - ITM), covering electrical safety, workstation ergonomics and safe means of access. No civil defence or special anti-disaster clearance is applicable to a non-industrial consultancy operation.

Paid-in capital rule: The SCSp has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow account. Contributions are fixed freely in the partnership agreement and may be made in cash, in kind, or as industry, provided they are actually and irrevocably made as agreed.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - no industrial licensing authority is engaged for this office-based ESG and carbon accounting advisory service. If a local commercial registration formalities applies, the competent body is the commune in which the registered office is situated.

No industrial operating permit or regulated professional licence is required to carry out the carbon footprint audit and carbon offset advisory activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Inland Revenue, for direct income taxes, partnership information returns and tax residency certificates (portal: myguichet.lu; eCDF online filing). Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Luxembourg Indirect Tax and Registration Authority, for VAT registration, VAT returns and refunds (portal: myguichet.lu and eTVA online filing). Business registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), with publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint a Luxembourg registered office and a local authorised representative if the SCSp is managed by non-residents. 2) File the constitutive documents and partnership agreement with the RCS via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), obtain the RCS registration number and the RNPM (Registre National des Personnes Morales) validation; registration is mandatory for the SCSp even though it lacks legal personality. 3) Submit notarised or legalised statutes, identification documents of all partners (natural or legal persons), registered office proof, and a local representative mandate; the RCS number constitutes the national business identifier automatically notified to the AEDT and ACD. 4) For VAT: file Form 640 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT; a VAT identification number is issued from the first taxable economic activity. 5) For foreign non-established taxable persons with no local fixed establishment, VAT registration is required before any taxable transaction in Luxembourg unless the reverse charge applies; non-EU entities must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative approved by the AEDT. 6) Apply separately to the ACD for a direct tax reference number and for the partnership information return file (form 550-series); the SCSp itself is not assessed to corporate income tax or municipal business tax, but a tax file is required to report distributive income to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory design (Law of 23 July 2016 on the SCSp; Article 1ter LIR): no check-the-box election exists; transparency is mandatory for Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (IBP) and net wealth tax (IFI) at entity level. Partners are subject to tax personally on their distributive share of Luxembourg and foreign-source income according to their residence status. Available elections and relevant mechanisms: (a) VAT grouping / assujetti unique election under Article 60 of the VAT Law, whereby the SCSp may join other Luxembourg established persons as a single taxable person with prior AEDT approval; (b) the small-enterprise franchise (Article 57 VAT Law) may be waived, or is lost automatically when turnover exceeds EUR 35,000; (c) designation in the partnership agreement of the profit-sharing and capital call rules to confirm Luxembourg tax attribution; (d) hybrid mismatch rules under Luxembourg's ATAD transposition (Law of 21 December 2017 and 2020 amendments) apply – partners must align the SCSp's transparent Luxembourg treatment with the foreign counterparty characterisation to avoid disallowed deductions; no elective opt-out from transparency is permitted. The SCSp may also elect optional Irish/English-style limited partnership clauses only to the extent the Luxembourg commercial law permits, without altering fiscal transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (net of VAT) for Luxembourg-established small enterprises under Article 57 of the VAT Law (franchise). No registration threshold for non-established foreign taxable persons: registration is required from the first taxable transaction unless the reverse charge shifts liability to the Luxembourg customer. For intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C telecommunications/broadcasting/digital services under the One Stop Shop (OSS): EUR 10,000 annual Union-wide threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies strictly to (a) supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU, under Article 42 of the VAT Law, supported by a customs export declaration; and (b) certain goods-related ancillary services (transport, freight, handling) directly connected to exported goods. For the SCSp's ESG carbon footprint auditing and offset brokerage services, zero-rating is not the applicable mechanism: B2B services supplied to non-EU recipients are outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the general place-of-supply rule (recipient's place of establishment), and B2C cross-border consultancy services are outside scope where the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU. Emission allowances and carbon credits traded on the EU ETS or voluntarily are treated as supplies of services subject to the standard 17% VAT rate when supplied in Luxembourg territory; brokerage and intermediary services to EU B2B customers are subject to reverse charge, and to non-EU B2B customers are outside scope. Input VAT incurred by the SCSp on Luxembourg costs is recoverable via the periodic VAT return (established entities) or the 13th Directive electronic refund procedure (non-established entities, form 3582M).

  • Export clearance certificate (EX-1 copy) from Luxembourg Customs

Luxembourg does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or mandatory API pre-clearance for standard B2B VAT invoices. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via the PEPPOL network under the Law of 26 June 2023 transposing Directive 2014/55/EU. As of 2025, B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary in Luxembourg, with structured e-invoices compliant with EN 16931 accepted; Luxembourg is aligned with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package, which will introduce mandatory structured e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting through a harmonised EU standard, expected for cross-border B2B transactions by 2030 and phased for domestic supplies thereafter. Taxpayers must ensure invoice authenticity, integrity and legibility, and retain all original invoices for at least 6 years.

Luxembourg statutes do not impose a general 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark functions as an administrative substance and benchmark indicator used by the AEDT and ACD to verify the economic reality of export transactions and transfer pricing alignment. Where export zero-rating is claimed without adequate documentary substantiation, the AEDT may retrospectively claw back the 0% rate, assess VAT at 17% with statutory late-payment interest (currently 0.8% per month of arrears), and impose administrative fines ranging from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 for invoice and declaration violations under Articles 66-67 of the VAT Law. Fraudulent zero-rating claims or counterfeit export evidence constitute tax fraud (escroquerie fiscale) punishable by imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000, in addition to civil liability of the partners. The AEDT applies risk-based audits targeting carbon offset brokerage and cross-border service models, may request full partner-level documentation, and will deny input VAT recovery on any invalid or missing invoices; joint and several payment liability can extend to the general partner (gérant) of the SCSp.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is legal tender; Luxembourg maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Art. 63), with no foreign-exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no profit repatriation hurdles. Outbound/inbound payments require only standard banking documentation and, where applicable, tax reportable-category codes, not regulatory license. Capital-account conversion is unrestricted even for investment fund vehicles such as the SCSp.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg has no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts and service revenues (including ESG auditing and carbon offset brokerage fees) may be freely held, converted, or repatriated in any currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) jointly provide central banking and prudential supervision; BCL executes monetary policy within the Eurosystem, while CSSF licenses and supervises credit institutions and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions are legally viable as payment-service providers for an SCSp only if they hold a valid passport/licence under PSD2 and are registered with the CSSF. Principal account and capital escrow should be at a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution; offshore EMIs outside the EEA will normally be judged 'Medium-to-Low' viability because they cannot provide collateral-grade escrow, sub-custody, or trade finance instruments, and are often rejected for anti-money-laundering comfort by downstream investors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are routed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (for interbank transmissions) into the SCSp's EUR IBAN account at a CSSF-licensed Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance instruction must clearly reference the share-class subscriber, the purpose code for 'Direct investment in the reporting economy' (e.g., EU BOP code D31 or ECB purpose description 'EQUI' for equity capital), and the corresponding investment contract. For amounts above EUR 10,000, the bank's AML engine will require a copy of the unit subscription form, the capital account statement reflecting the LP commitment, and a board/general partner resolution confirming the injection is a partnership capital contribution rather than a loan.Trade settlements for international suppliers and carbon-offset counterparties are cleared through (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within EU/EEA, (ii) SWIFT MT103 for global low-value trade disbursements, (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for documentary guarantee-based settlements, and (iv) TARGET2 for large-value euro interbank clearing. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 300,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies OECD-aligned transfer pricing principles grounded in Article 56 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law; the SCSp must reflect real economic substance in respect of management, employees, premises, and decision-making; the Luxembourg implementation of the EU Economic Substance Requirements through the ATAD and domestic anti-abuse rules requires sufficient substance to avoid treaty denial under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and controlled foreign company (CFC) rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; tax evasion surcharge of 20% to 100% of the evaded tax; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 per infraction.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partner are jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid taxes if they signed a false return; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be imposed; civil restitution for the full tax deficiency and interest.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax transparency and treaty benefits; additional assessment at 24.94% CIT on the deemed taxable income; administrative penalty up to EUR 250,000; mandatory re-registration or disqualification for tax-abusive structures.

Director personal liability: General partner and directors can be held personally liable for the full CIT and municipal business tax assessed on the entity; court-imposed travel bans and removal from Luxembourg business registries possible.
AML / Carbon Offset Fraud

Statutory sanction: AML Law of 12 November 2004 penalties include imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of EUR 1,250 to EUR 500,000; confiscation of proceeds; regulatory fines imposed by the CSSF or Luxembourg public prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly involved in money laundering face personal criminal liability, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil damages; travel bans and asset seizures are standard enforcement tools.
Transfer Pricing Violation

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing adjustor assessing arm's length profit at 24.94% CIT plus 1% monthly late-payment interest; penalty of up to 25% of the adjustment; 10% non-filing penalty and EUR 10,000 late documentation penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved non-arm's length transfer pricing can be jointly liable for the recovered tax; criminal liability for falsification of accounts; travel ban and restitution of lost corporate taxes may be imposed.

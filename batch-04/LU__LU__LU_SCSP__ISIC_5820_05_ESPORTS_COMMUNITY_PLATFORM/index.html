Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified filing portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu); RCS registration through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute private deed for the SCSp (no notary mandatory unless real estate contribution) and register with the RCS via MyGuichet.lu to obtain the business registration number (numéro d'immatriculation RCS), which is the primary tax identification number for the entity. Step 2: File the VAT registration application (formulaire 731) with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number bearing the 'LU' prefix. Step 3: For corporate/partnership income tax, the ACD assigns a matricule (national registration number) and a tax file to the SCSp; the fiscally transparent partners report their proportionate shares in their own filings. Step 4: Non-established foreign operators of B2C electronic services may alternatively register via the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) in their Member State of identification; a non-EU supplier is required to appoint a VAT representative established in Luxembourg (severe liability) unless OSS (non-union scheme) is used.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

(1) Article 175bis LIR (Luxembourg Income Tax Law) election: an SCSp formed in Luxembourg may irrevocably opt to be treated as a taxable corporate entity (opaque status) for Luxembourg tax purposes, provided that, for the entire tax year, all partners are residents of Luxembourg or of a treaty state and are effectively taxable on their share of partnership profits in that state. (2) ATAD 2 reverse hybrid-mismatch sweep (art. 160ter LIR): where all the SCSp's participants are tax-resident outside Luxembourg and treat the entity as opaque, Luxembourg mandates entity-level taxation irrespective of the default transparency. (3) Partnership-level VAT unpredictability: transparency applies for income tax; for VAT the SCSp is, in practice, registered in its own name as the supplier of the esports platform services, with no election required. (4) No general 'check-the-box' classification regime exists under Luxembourg law comparable to the US federal check-the-box; the contrast between the default transparency and art. 175bis election reproduces the 'check-the-box' outcome flexibility for hybrid planning.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the Luxembourg small-business exemption applicable to established taxable persons selling primarily to final consumers; however, an SCSp or any supplier making taxable supplies of esports/digital platform services in Luxembourg must register from the first euro (no threshold) for mandatory AEDT registration, and non-established suppliers of B2C electronic services must register via OSS/Union scheme irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods outside the EU is zero-rated (art. 41 VAT Law) upon proof of shipment. Export of services (including esports platform/matchmaking, tournament SaaS, digital community subscriptions and hosting fees) is zero-rated (art. 44) when the business customer is established outside the EU or, for B2C digital services, when the recipient has his residence/usual residence outside the EU and the effective use and enjoyment test is satisfied; intra-EU B2B services fall under Luxembourg reverse charge. For B2C electronic services supplied intra-EU, the supply is taxed in the Member State of the consumer (via OSS) rather than zero-rated. Zero-rating requires contemporaneous evidence that the recipient has its seat/domicile outside the Union: VAT identification number (if any), IP geolocation logs, country metadata of the connected device, bank/BIC country of the payment instrument, billing address, and hotel-billing indicators.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document / electronic export declaration through Luxembourg customs) where physical goods are exported from the EU
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical dispatch of exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement or SWIFT/Stripe/PayPal payment records evidencing the receipt of the export consideration from the non-EU customer)
  • Signed master service/subscription contract documenting the non-EU recipient's establishment and the nature of the B2B supply
  • IP address geolocation and device metadata logs for B2C digital esports services, demonstrating placement of the consumer outside the EU
  • Foreign VAT ID certificate (VRN) or EU OSS records for intra-EU business customers
  • Annual EC Sales List / recapitulative statement and Intrastat filings in respect of intra-EU supplies

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API transmission, or digital stamping. E-invoicing is permitted on a consensual basis (buyer's agreement required, art. 54(3) of the Luxembourg VAT Implementation Regulation). Luxembourg is committed to the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) roadmap, with mandatory structured e-invoicing (EU standard EN 16931 via Peppol) for intra-EU B2B supplies expected, at the earliest, in the 2030–2035 window; domestic B2G e-invoicing is facultative with the public buyer's acceptance. Until then, PDF/A or Peppol BIS quality XML invoices are accepted; no state-managed central exchange platform.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the domestic currency and is fully convertible. Luxembourg implements the EU free movement of capital regime under Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC; there are no exchange controls, no prior central-bank authorisation for inbound or outbound payments, and no restriction on profit or capital repatriation. The SCSp may receive and disburse partner contributions, prize pools, sponsorship revenue and commercial settlements freely in EUR or any foreign currency. Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, partnership distributions are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at entity level; treaty-based withholding analysis applies only to Luxembourg-source income of underlying companies, where the standard domestic rate of 15% on dividends may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation period for export proceeds, platform fees, sponsorship or advertising revenue received in foreign currency. USD and other foreign-currency receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted freely, or applied directly to obligations. The only compliance duty is statistical: resident entities must report qualifying cross-border transactions to the BCL for balance-of-payments and external-stocks statistics under the ECB statistics framework (ECB Regulation 2533/98), with no impact on treasury freedom.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential regulation and supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), with the BCL responsible for monetary policy operations and balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as an auxiliary payment channel; not viable as a primary settlement bank. The SCSp may lawfully use EU-authorised Payment Institutions and Electronic Money Institutions passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2 (Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended) and the Law of 14 December 2001 on electronic money, as amended. The target payment rails are fully compliant: PayPal (Europe) S.a r.l. et Cie, S.C.A. is a Luxembourg-authorised credit institution supervised by the CSSF, and Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland) operates through EU passporting into Luxembourg. Unregulated offshore EMIs are prohibited: providing payment services or issuing e-money in Luxembourg without CSSF authorisation or a valid EU passport constitutes a criminal offence. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EU-resident partners inject EUR via SEPA Credit Transfer referencing the capital call number and the partnership deed date; the receiving bank requires a capital call notice, source-of-funds declaration and UBO confirmation and credits the dedicated Capital Escrow Account. Non-EU partners (including Chinese investors) route USD or EUR via SWIFT MT103 with a direct-investment purpose code; the remitting bank applies its home-country ODI/SAFE approval requirements, while the Luxembourg beneficiary bank requires the RCS extract, partnership deed, legal opinion and UBO documentation. Funds must match the capital call amount exactly to permit straightforward booking; any deviation is re-characterised as a shareholder loan and must be documented under a separate loan agreement. Short-term working capital may be advanced by the General Partner through a documented intra-group current account. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (WHT) for distributions from a Luxembourg corporate resident. No WHT applies on distributions of profits by an SCSp because the SCSp is fiscally transparent; the underlying corporate entity holding shares in an operating affiliate, if any, would trigger WHT at 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year-end: 31 December. Return must be filed and tax paid by 31 March of the following year; extension to 30 June available upon request. For fiscal years ending 31 December 2024 and 2025, the deadline is extended to 31 December 2025 and 31 December 2026 respectively under the COVID-19 administrative deferral practice; standard deadline is otherwise strictly enforced.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 3,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions requiring Local File preparation; Master File required if the group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and group related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000. Although the SCSp is transparent, partners' controlled transactions with the platform are subject to Luxembourg transfer pricing documentation rules where the SCSp is the tested party.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under the OECD BEPS Action 5 and the EU ATAD. The SCSp must demonstrate real economic substance: its registered office in Luxembourg, local accounting records, central management and control, a minimum level of staff and operational expenditure for the esports platform activity, and performance of core income-generating activities in Luxembourg. For pure holding and financing transparent vehicles, a substance-to-risk ratio test applies, but for the esports community platform, genuine operational presence is required; failure leads to lack of substance presumption and potential recharacterization of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Luxembourg Criminal Code Art. 396 and General Tax Law §396)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine from EUR 10,000 to EUR 1,000,000, plus imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for directors or managers; full restitution of evaded taxes and automatic penalties up to 100% of the underdeclared tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp general partner (personne responsable) are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly caused non-payment; travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and extradition procedures apply under mutual assistance agreements.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the SCSp as fully taxable, reassessment of profits at the entity level, 10% delinquency penalty, interest at 0.6% per month, and refusal of tax treaty benefits (e.g., under the Principal Purpose Test).

Director personal liability: General partners and directors become personally liable for the entire reassessed tax if the corporate veil is pierced; the Luxembourg Commercial Court may order claw-back of distributions and disqualification from serving as director for up to 10 years.
AML/CFT and VASP/Esports Payment Processing Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1,250,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; criminal sanctions for failure to report suspicious transactions, and revocation of the Luxembourg operating license.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors may face criminal liability including imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines; travel bans and asset freezes for serious AML failures under CSSF enforcement.
Transfer Pricing and Substance Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of EUR 5,000 per missing or incomplete document; upward TP adjustment with 10% reassessment surcharge and 0.6% monthly interest; autonomous penalty of up to EUR 250,000 for material non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp manager who signed false declarations are personally liable for the resulting tax deficiencies; the Luxembourg Tax Administration can seek attachment of personal bank accounts and real estate.

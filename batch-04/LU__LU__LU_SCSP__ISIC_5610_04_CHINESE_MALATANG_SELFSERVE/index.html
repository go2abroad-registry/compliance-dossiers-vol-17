Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Inland Revenue for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) for customs and export controls. Official portals: MyGuichet.lu for all filings, eCDF for electronic tax returns, and the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) for entity registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SCSp by deed (notarial or private writing) in accordance with the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended, and register it with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) to obtain the RCS number and the 13-digit entity matricule (national identification number). Non-resident general and limited partners must provide KYC/AML documentation and beneficial ownership declarations. Step 2: Register the SCSp for VAT with the AEDT using Form 10 (Déclaration d'assujettissement à la TVA), stating the commencement date of the franchising activity; the AEDT issues the 10-character LU VAT identification number. Step 3: If the SCSp has no Luxembourg commercial domicile or permanent establishment (PE), appoint a local VAT representative approved by the AEDT, who is jointly and severally liable for the VAT obligations. Step 4: Where a foreign partner becomes taxable in Luxembourg through a PE, file the ACD declaration of commencement within 15 days. Step 5: For the self-serve malatang fast-casual master franchise, no minimum capital is required, 100% non-resident ownership is permitted, and a food-service establishment licence (autorisation d'établissement) and municipal trade permit must be obtained before VAT-registered activity commences.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: income, gains, and net wealth are taxed directly at the level of the partners in proportion to their profit shares, with no Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (UCT), or net wealth tax (NWT) at the entity level. The SCSp may make an irrevocable election under Article 157bis LIR (option pour l'assujettissement à l'impôt sur les sociétés) to be treated as a fully taxable entity for CIT, UCT, and NWT purposes, provided all partners are Luxembourg residents or residents of a treaty state; the election requires the written consent of all partners and registration in the RCS. Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD, transposed by the laws of 21 December 2018 and 20 December 2019) re-characterize the SCSp as taxable where the partner jurisdiction treats it as opaque (reverse-hybrid rule of Article 168ter LIR) and deny deductions for payments subject to hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 - annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for the small-enterprise exemption (Article 57 LTVAT) applying only to EU-established taxable persons; non-established taxable persons have no threshold and must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg (Articles 59-60 LTVAT).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to exports of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf (Article 52(1) LTVAT), provided the buyer is a non-EU person, the goods leave the EU within 3 months of the supply (extendable to 12 months in justified cases), and the supplier holds full documentary proof of export. Services: master-franchise royalties, brand-licence fees, and self-serve concept know-how supplied B2B are outside Luxembourg VAT when the recipient is a taxable person established outside the EU (Article 58(1)(a) LTVAT), subject to written evidence of the recipient's VAT number or fiscal residence certificate, the royalty/service contract, and proof of payment. Where the non-EU recipient is a non-taxable person, the supply remains taxable in Luxembourg at 17% unless the service is physically performed abroad or directly related to exported goods (Article 52(b) LTVAT). Intra-EU B2B supplies of such services follow the reverse-charge mechanism, with the service taxable in the recipient's member state.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU - Single Administrative Document) with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export acceptance printout from the Luxembourg ECS/AES customs system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR consignment note, or air waybill evidencing physical removal of the goods from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of payment: SWIFT or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Transport and delivery evidence: customs exit confirmation, warehouse receipts, or insurance documents corroborating the export movement
  • Royalty or master-franchise agreement identifying the non-EU franchisee, the precise service description, and the basis for the place-of-supply conclusion

B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory since 2020 for invoices issued to Luxembourg public entities, submitted exclusively through the PEPPOL network (law of 16 May 2019 on electronic invoicing). There is no general B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance/digital stamping mandate in Luxembourg as of the current stage; the EU ViDA package is expected to introduce mandatory B2B e-invoicing and transaction-based digital VAT reporting for Luxembourg taxable persons by 2030. VAT returns (Form 640) and EU recapitulative statements (Forms 641/642) must be filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu or eCDF, and all invoicing and export substantiation records must be retained for 10 years.

The AEDT and the Customs and Excise Administration (ADA) place the full documentary burden of proof on the SCSp; where export or offshore-service substantiation is incomplete or the physical EU-exit deadline is missed, zero-rating is retroactively reversed, the unpaid 17% VAT is clawed back, and default interest accrues at 0.6% per month on arrears. Administrative fines range from EUR 250 to EUR 25,000 per infraction under Articles 41-43 and 93 LTVAT. In cases of fraudulent, fictitious, or sham export structures, criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000; the general partner and the appointed Luxembourg VAT representative become jointly and severally liable; and the statute of limitations extends from 5 to 10 years. Serious violations can also lead to the suspension of the food-establishment licence and exclusion of the entity from the benefits of the small-enterprise exemption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a eurozone member, maintains full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63-66). Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident partners are unrestricted, subject only to standard anti-money laundering verification and withholding tax formalities on non-cooperative jurisdictions. No prior administrative authorisation is required for FX operations or cross-border transfers; however, transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or cash-like instruments face augmented compliance screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Resident businesses may retain export receipts in any currency and maintain foreign-denominated accounts without obligation to convert to euro.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and foreign exchange oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium to High for operational cash management, but Low for capital escrow. Luxembourg-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., PayPal Luxembourg, Mangopay) can provide local IBANs and cross-border digital onboarding. Legal constraints: EMIs may not hold escrow accounts for regulated capital aggregation unless licensed under the Luxembourg law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and e-money, and they cannot act as deposit-taking institutions. The SCSp should maintain primary treasury accounts with a TLSB (fully licensed credit institution) and use an EMI only for ancillary FX payments and low-value royalty collections.

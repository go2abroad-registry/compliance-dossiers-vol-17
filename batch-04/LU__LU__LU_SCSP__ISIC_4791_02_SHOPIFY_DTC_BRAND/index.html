Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg VAT Department; official e-services via MyGuichet.lu and the AEDT VAT e-file portal.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SCSp obtains its national business number (matricule) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), then applies for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU...) with the AEDT Bureau d'imposition de la TVA. Non-resident EU sellers may register directly for Luxembourg VAT; non-EU sellers must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative unless an exemption or special OSS regime applies. Registration is mandatory before commencing taxable supplies and may be filed by a manager or authorised representative. No minimum capital or statutory licence is required for the SCSp DTC webstore activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box regime. The SCSp is mandatorily fiscally transparent for income tax purposes; no election to be taxed as a corporation exists. Available limited elections relate to VAT: small-enterprise exemption waiver, Union OSS registration for cross-border B2C e-commerce, and Import One-Stop-Shop for low-value import consignments. Anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD may impose 'reverse hybrid' entity-level taxation on a transparent SCSp if a non-resident partner treats it as opaque and the mismatch is not otherwise neutralised.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR. A Luxembourg-established online retailer is generally registrable from its first economic activity; the domestic small-enterprise exemption is up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover for Luxembourg-established traders, with an option to register. For intra-EU distance sales to Luxembourg consumers, the harmonised EUR 10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold applies; exceeding it requires VAT accounting and registration in Luxembourg through OSS. No separate local turnover threshold applies to a permanent Luxembourg-establishment DTC brand.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Luxembourg VAT applies to (i) exports of goods to non-EU destinations if the supplier possesses documentary proof of exit from the EU; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to taxable consumers in other EU member states where the customer communicates a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are dispatched to that member state; and (iii) certain ancillary services directly related to export transactions. For a global DTC webstore selling from Luxembourg to non-EU customers, the sale is eligible for 0% VAT if the goods leave the EU. B2C digital services and intra-EU cross-border B2C distance sales follow destination taxation and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs export declaration (EAD/SAD) in the exporting member state, with itemised CN codes, weights and values
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill
  • Commercial invoice/order confirmation matching the SCSp seller and DTC customer
  • Proof of payment for the transaction and, where applicable, the inward foreign-currency remittance certificate/inward remittance advice from the receiving bank
  • Customs proof-of-export record (electronic or stamped exit confirmation) and, when applicable, delivery confirmation from an independent logistics provider

Luxembourg imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no tax-authority digital stamping and no mandatory private-sector e-invoicing API. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through the AEDT eCDF/e-file channel; OSS returns are filed through the Luxembourg OSS portal. Invoices must meet EU VAT invoice requirements. Mandatory e-invoicing (Peppol BIS/EN 16931) applies only to Luxembourg public procurement and does not apply to Shopify/PayPal/Stripe B2C payments.

The AEDT may conduct a VAT audit and reassess zero-rated exports within the standard five-year assessment period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud. Failure to substantiate export or intra-Community supply triggers immediate recomputation of Luxembourg VAT at the applicable rate (17% standard or appropriate reduced rate), plus late interest of 0.8% per month. Fraudulent zero-rating claims can lead to administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 (or up to twice the evaded tax), criminal sanctions including imprisonment, and personal liability of SCSp managers for VAT debts where bad faith or abusive manoeuvre is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member and applies full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. No general foreign exchange controls exist for non-resident investors. Profit remittances, dividends, and distributions from an SCSp are freely transferable without prior authorization, subject to standard AML/KYC reporting thresholds and tax reporting obligations. Withholding tax treatment may apply to certain outbound payments, but remittance itself is unrestricted. The euro is fully convertible, and cross-border payment operations are governed by EU Payment Services Directive and SEPA/ SWIFT rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or holding period applies to export receipts in Luxembourg. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency, credited to resident or non-resident accounts, and freely used for trade settlements, intercompany funding, or FX hedging. Reporting obligations arise only under balance-of-payments statistics regulations, requiring nominal filing of cross-border transactions above thresholds to the BCL via registered credit institutions.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderately feasible; Luxembourg recognizes licensed Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions under CSSF supervision. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, MultiPass) can provide ancillary euro IBANs and FX services, but they generally require an underlying local credit institution account for settlement. For an SCSp, EMI accounts cannot serve as the exclusive treasury vehicle due to corporate banking expectations, capital commitment tracking, and investor-side verification. Legal constraints include EMI sandbox limits, transaction monitoring, and inability to hold client assets as an escrow agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's bank to the SCSp's operational euro account in Luxembourg. The payment reference must include the purpose code S301 (direct investment in shares/equity) under the ECB's Balance of Payments harmonized categorization, or FINREP taxonomy equivalent. The bank must receive a board/GP resolution authorizing the capital call, a countersigned capital contribution deed, and the shareholding/partnership percentage schedule. Each inbound wire should be accompanied by a KYC-relevant beneficiary legal entity identifier (LEI of the SCSp) and a clear economic purpose statement. For large contributions exceeding €1,000,000, the SCSp should prepare an anti-money laundering statement covering source of wealth and the underlying sale transaction.Trade settlements to international suppliers should use the SWIFT MT103 standard clearing instruction via Luxembourg's TARGET2/SCT infrastructure. For high-value shipments or recurring inventory purchases, suppliers may require a Documentary Letter of Credit, executed through a Luxembourg commercial bank. Domestic and EU supplier payments can be effected via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) using BIC/IBAN with no FX exposure. Non-Euro purchases require a spot FX transaction with the banking partner before issuing the MT103. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17.0% CIT headline; plus 7% solidarity surcharge and communal business tax (Luxembourg City 6.75%), aggregate top rate ±24.94% for a taxable corporate partner

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic WHT on dividends distributed by Luxembourg resident commercial companies; SCSp profit distributions to partners are not mechanically subject to dividend WHT because the SCSp is tax-transparent and lacks corporate personality

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR: Local File mandatory where net turnover ≥ EUR 50,000,000 OR balance sheet total ≥ EUR 4,000,000 OR gross financial income ≥ EUR 1,000,000 (Art. 56bis LIR); Master File mandatory where consolidated group turnover ≥ EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies no UAE-style statutory ESR; substance is enforced through Art. 6 Steueranpassungsgesetz GAAR, MLI principal-purpose test, ATAD interest/CFC rules and the EU Unshell directive (adopted 2025). The SCSp must demonstrate: Luxembourg registered office, local general partner and decision-making, local bank account, segregated Shopify/PayPal/Stripe settlement reporting, and qualified administrative support.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent declaration

Statutory sanction: Ex officio re-assessment plus 10% surcharge, monthly late interest (0.5-0.6%), administrative fine up to €25,000; criminal tax fraud penalties (art. 439 LIR): imprisonment and fine up to €100,000, plus restitution of evaded tax with penalties.

Director personal liability: Managers and the general partner can be jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and penalties where the evasion is attributable to their deliberate acts; criminal prosecution of directors, travel/asset-restraint measures and potential imprisonment up to 5 years in aggravated fraud cases.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization (art. 6 Steueranpassungsgesetz), denial of treaty benefits under MLI PPT, retroactive assessment of Luxembourg-source income, 10% penalty surcharge and late interest; EU Unshell directive consequences after transposition: deemed shell result and tax liability transfer.

Director personal liability: Officers tolerating the letterbox structure face civil restitution claims, disqualification orders and, in tax-fraud constellations, personal criminal liability; the Luxembourg AEDT may seek payment of corporate tax debts from the general partner of the transparent partnership.
AML / UBO compliance failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1,250,000 under the Luxembourg AML framework (law of 12 Nov 2004, CRF/compétent authority), fines of €125 to €125,000 and up to 1 year imprisonment for RBE non-compliance; freezing of bank accounts by EPP? no—freezing orders by the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Civil and criminal liability of the general partner and directors: fines, imprisonment (up to 5 years), professional prohibition for regulated positions, and piercing of limited liability where the partnership veil was used to conceal ownership.
Transfer pricing documentation and arm's length breach

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income with 10% surcharge and late interest; fixed penalty up to €1,000 for failure to maintain TP documentation under art. 56bis LIR; disallowance of deductions and potential double taxation without corresponding treaty adjustment.

Director personal liability: The general partner signing Form 500 is personally liable for the accuracy of TP documentation declarations; deliberate manipulation attracts criminal fraud sanctions and civil restitution claims from the partnership or co-partners.

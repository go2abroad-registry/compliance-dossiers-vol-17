Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; e-portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp, registration is initiated at the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); the RCS filing triggers attribution of a Luxembourg national identification number (NIF). The SCSp then applies for a VAT number using VAT declaration Form 640/641 submitted to the AEDT; a local designated representative may be required for non-EU/foreign members; direct tax identification follows from the RCS registration and ACD notification; the VAT number is generally obtained within 2–4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent, no check-the-box election exists. No election to be treated as a corporation. Members may opt into Luxembourg corporate tax consolidation only if the SCSp is disregarded and the activity is absorbed by a corporate taxpayer, which is generally unavailable. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/EU Directive 2016/1164 apply and disallow mismatches; no elective hybrid-mismatch relief is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) for the small-enterprise exemption; cross-border distance sales of cosmetics to consumers in another EU Member State require registration in that Member State once annual distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 (EU-wide threshold, administered via OSS); no registration threshold applies to goods imported into Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported outside the EU by the supplier or on their behalf, requiring evidence of departure within 90 days of supply. For services relating to exported goods (e.g. logistics ancillary to cosmetics export) zero-rating may apply if place of supply is outside the EU; for distance sales to EU consumers, 0% is not available — VAT of destination Member State applies via OSS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Luxembourg Single Administrative Document with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR transport document / airway waybill evidencing physical departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of payment from non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and export declaration reference
  • Third-party warehousing records and inventory movement evidence
  • Export licence / restricted substance declarations (cosmetics containing controlled ingredients, if applicable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-stamping or continuous transaction control regime for B2B VAT invoicing. Luxembourg mandates structured e-invoicing (Peppol-BIS) only for B2G contracts. Commercial electronic invoices must be authenticated by reliable audit trail; no government pre-clearance or digital stamp is required.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary scrutiny by the AEDT and Luxembourg customs. In the absence of complete evidence, the VAT authority retrospectively reassesses output VAT at the standard 17% rate, plus default interest and late-payment penalties (0.6% per month of unpaid tax). Fraudulent or knowingly false zero-rating claims may trigger criminal tax fraud penalties up to €250,000 and imprisonment of executives, in addition to full VAT recovery and exclusion from VAT registration for disqualification periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the eurozone and the Economic and Monetary Union. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the consolidated Luxembourg banking and payment services legislation. There are no exchange controls, no prior licensing for cross-border capital movements, and no profit remittance restrictions for an SCSp engaged in online cosmetics retail. Distributions and redemption proceeds paid by a Luxembourg SCSp to non-resident partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at entity level due to the partnership's contractual, tax-transparent character, provided the partner is not a Luxembourg-resident taxable recipient and no Luxembourg permanent establishment is imputed. The only operational constraints are banking compliance (source-of-funds verification), BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the reporting bank, and Regulation (EU) 2021/1230 fee-equality and transparency obligations for cross-border EUR payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Luxembourg or the eurozone. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts from cross-border DTC sales (card settlements, PayPal, Shopify Payments) may be freely held in EUR or any convertible foreign currency in an account of the SCSp or its general partner. If foreign currency receipts (USD, GBP, CNY) are retained offshore, the Luxembourg treasury may convert them through a domestic authorised bank at any time; only standard underlying documentary evidence (sales invoices, chargeback reports, settlement statements) must be maintained for bank and CSSF inspection. No BCL approval is required for any holding or conversion transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in prudential and AML/CFT cooperation with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH, but only as a collection-side, non-settlement gateway. Legal constraints: (a) any electronic money institution servicing the SCSp must hold a valid e-money / payment-institution licence in an EEA member state and either establish a Luxembourg branch or passport its services into Luxembourg under the EU Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366; (b) the SCSp has no separate legal personality, so the EMI contract must be entered into by the general partner acting as a legal representative, and every qualifying limited partner must be identified as a beneficial owner under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004; (c) client funds must be safeguarded and held in segregated accounts with an EU credit institution, which disallows the offshore EMI from acting as the SCSp's sole liquidity repository; (d) offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA (e.g., UK, MENA, Singapore, US without an EU passport) cannot lawfully render EUR settlement/payment services to a Luxembourg entity and create unacceptable travel-rule gaps under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113. Recommended architecture: use PayPal and Shopify Payments rails for customer collection, then sweep daily via a licensed EU EMI into a BCEE/BIL treasury account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

SCSp has no separate legal personality and is a contract-based special limited partnership; banks may flag it as a trust-like or opaque structure

Root cause: SCSp has no separate legal personality and is a contract-based special limited partnership; banks may flag it as a trust-like or opaque structure

Defense evidence: Original Luxembourg law partnership agreement (contrat de société) signed by all partners; certificate of registration in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) published in the electronic compendium; RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) extract disclosing the chain of UBOs; full legal identity of the general partner (often a Luxembourg SOPARFI or an individual); a board/manager resolution authorising account opening; and a commercially substantiated business plan evidencing the ISIC 4791 online cosmetics retail purpose.
Tax-transparent classification ambiguity for a pass-through SCSp with non-resident partners, triggering FATCA/CRS and withholding over-reporting concerns

Root cause: Tax-transparent classification ambiguity for a pass-through SCSp with non-resident partners, triggering FATCA/CRS and withholding over-reporting concerns

Defense evidence: A reasoned Luxembourg tax opinion or advance tax ruling confirming the SCSp's tax-transparent character under Art. 183 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR); a CRS/FATCA classification self-certificate demonstrating treatment of non-resident partners as the reportable persons and SCSp as a non-reporting investment entity; a withholding analysis confirming no Luxembourg WHT on partner distributions; and partnership accounts evidencing no Luxembourg operational permanent establishment.

Defense evidence: 12-month processing statements from PayPal and Shopify Payments; chargeback ratio report showing less than 1% historical threshold; PCI-DSS compliance certificate; customer returns and refund policy; logistics and fulfilment contracts (3PL warehouse and carrier records); insurance certificates; a clear funds-flow map demonstrating card settlement sweeps into the Luxembourg account; and audited sales invoices consistent with beauty retail unit economics.
Layered non-resident ownership with SPV partners and EU/OFAC sanctions screening hits on ultimate beneficial owners

Root cause: Layered non-resident ownership with SPV partners and EU/OFAC sanctions screening hits on ultimate beneficial owners

Defense evidence: Full UBO diagram linking every SPV to natural persons; completed sanctions screening certificates from a CSSF-registered screening provider; source of wealth statements for each limited partner; signed non-compliance declarations; professional engagement contracts of the Luxembourg corporate services provider (PSF); and a commitment letter from a registered auditor confirming ongoing AML record keeping.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For EEA-resident partners: use SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR (pain.001/pain.008) with EPC purpose code INVE (Investment/Savings) and the SCSp capital subscription agreement referenced in the Remittance Information field. For non-EEA partners: use SWIFT MT103 in EUR with a correspondent bank route (e.g., BNP Paribas Fortis in Brussels or the bank's US/UK correspondent), populate field 70 with a purpose code reflecting direct investment/capital subscription, and attach via cover letter the executed partnership deed, capital call notice, bank reference from the originator, 3-month source-of-funds statements, and a notarially certified UBO register. The receiving BCEE/BIL account must be activated for the general partner acting on behalf of the SCSp; the BCL does not require prior approval, but the bank must execute its balance-of-payments statistical reporting and apply Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 enhanced 'travel rule' diligence when funds originate from non-EEA wallets. All injections must be recorded against the SCSp's capital accounts with supporting contribution evidence to maintain the partnership's tax-transparent capital structure.For EU suppliers (packaging, ingredient sourcing, logistics): single SEPA Credit Transfers with EPC purpose code SUPP (Supplier Payment) or CCRD (Credit card payment) using IBAN-to-IBAN settlement. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined CIT 17% + municipal business tax 6.75% + solidarity surcharge 7% on CIT, effective for Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (domestic law) for distributions from a Luxembourg company to non-resident partners. However, SCSp is transparent; distributions are not dividends but profit shares. No WHT on profit distributions to partners if the SCSp is not subject to CIT (i.e., transparent). If the SCSp is treated as opaque (elected), then 15% WHT applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For corporate partners: CIT return due by 31 May of the following year (extendable to 31 December). For individual partners: personal tax return due by 31 December of the following year. SCSp itself must file annual accounts with RCS within 1 month of approval.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Form 100) / Line 1a Taxable income (base) for corporate partner Non-resident corporate partner must file Form 100 if Luxembourg-source income (e.g., from Luxembourg permanent establishment). Late filing: EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 fine. Late payment: 0.6% interest per month on unpaid tax. Additional 10% penalty for non-payment after demand.
Box 2 (Form 100) / Line 2 Deductions (business expenses, depreciation) Only expenses directly related to Luxembourg-source income are deductible. Late filing: EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 fine. Late payment: 0.6% interest per month on unpaid tax. Additional 10% penalty for non-payment after demand.
Box 3 (Form 100) / Line 3 Tax credits (e.g., foreign tax credit) Non-residents may claim treaty relief if applicable. Late filing: EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 fine. Late payment: 0.6% interest per month on unpaid tax. Additional 10% penalty for non-payment after demand.
Box 4 (Form 100) / Line 4 Net taxable income after adjustments Must be computed per Luxembourg tax rules, not home country rules. Late filing: EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 fine. Late payment: 0.6% interest per month on unpaid tax. Additional 10% penalty for non-payment after demand.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million (for goods) or EUR 500,000 (for services). Master File required if group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million. No specific threshold for SCSp but applies to partners if they are enterprises.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no general ESR, but anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and ATAD apply. SCSp must have substance (office, staff, decisions) to avoid being considered a shell. For retail, substance is evidenced by actual e-commerce operations, inventory, and management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 or 2x tax evaded; imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification as opaque, retroactive taxation at 24.94% CIT, and penalties up to EUR 10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for unpaid taxes if they failed to ensure substance. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML/CTF Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of turnover; criminal sanctions for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly facilitated money laundering. Travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax at 24.94%, interest, and penalties up to 50% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they approved non-arm's length transactions without proper documentation.

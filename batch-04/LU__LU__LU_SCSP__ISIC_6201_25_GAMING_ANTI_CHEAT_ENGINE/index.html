Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (including the provisions on société en commandite spéciale introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013), specifically the articles governing partnership formations and registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Partnership Agreement (Contrat de Société) and application for registration (Demande d'inscription au RCSL).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required by law for an SCSp; there is no statutory escrow account requirement. Contributions may be made in cash or in kind as agreed in the Partnership Agreement, and a bank confirmation letter is customarily presented for RCSL filing if capital is funded.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (attestation de sécurité incendie) is required from the municipal fire-safety authority for any office or server-room premises. Structural load inspection is necessary only if heavy cooling or server racks are installed. Occupational health clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) is required for workstations, electrical installations, and ergonomic compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is required by law for an SCSp; there is no statutory escrow account requirement. Contributions may be made in cash or in kind as agreed in the Partnership Agreement, and a bank confirmation letter is customarily presented for RCSL filing if capital is funded.

Category: No statutory operational licence required for a video-game anti-cheat middleware engine under Luxembourg or EU law; no commercial establishment permit applies under the Law of 19 December 2014 to ISIC 6201 software engineering activities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration (33, rue de Gasperich, L-5826 Hesperange) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT and Registration Administration (1-3, avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg) for VAT and registration duties. Official e-portals: MyGuichet.lu and guichet.lu; eTVA (VAT returns), eCDF (direct tax filings); legal gazette: legilux.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

(1) Prepare the SCSp partnership deed (contrat de société, no notarial form mandatory) and register the entity with the Trade and Companies Register (RCS/Luxembourg Business Registers), which automatically issues the 13-digit national business identification number (matricule / numéro national) serving as the income-tax reference; (2) file the AEDT VAT registration (form 640 'demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') electronically via MyGuichet – the AEDT issues an LU-prefixed 8-digit VAT number (format LU12345678), generally within 7 working days; (3) open the direct-tax file at the ACD using the transparency return (déclaration commune under Article 175 LIR) where the SCSp itself files once naming all partners; (4) register all partners, including foreign general partners and corporate limited partners, via the RCS/ACD matricule system; no payroll registration applies in the absence of Luxembourg employees.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible – the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale), created by the Law of 23 July 2016, lacks legal personality and is fiscally transparent by operation of law: no Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), no municipal business tax (ICC), and no net wealth tax (IFL) at SCSp level; profits, gains and net wealth are allocated directly to partners, who are taxable in their own Member State/residence; non-resident partners have no Luxembourg tax exposure absent a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate. No Luxembourg withholding tax is levied on partnership distributions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale), created by the Law of 23 July 2016, lacks legal personality and is fiscally transparent by operation of law: no Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), no municipal business tax (ICC), and no net wealth tax (IFL) at SCSp level; profits, gains and net wealth are allocated directly to partners, who are taxable in their own Member State/residence; non-resident partners have no Luxembourg tax exposure absent a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate. No Luxembourg withholding tax is levied on partnership distributions. For the gaming anti-cheat middleware licensing activity (ISIC 6201/NAICS 541511) conducted without any Luxembourg PE, no CIT return is due at entity level.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' regime; SCSp transparency is statutory and may not be overridden in Luxembourg. Available mechanisms: (1) an SCSp may elect, under Article 175 LIR, to file a single consolidated transparency return naming all partners instead of per-partner filing; (2) US partners may file US Form 8832 to elect partnership or association classification – flanked by Luxembourg's ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (Law of 21 December 2018) which deny deductions or impose inclusion where the SCSp is treated as an opaque entity abroad; (3) DAC6 (Law of 25 March 2020) mandates disclosure of reportable cross-border arrangements exploiting SCSp hybrid status; (4) treaty classification follows the OECD transparent-entity clause (no election available for double-tax-treaty qualification).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 – annual taxable turnover (excl. VAT) threshold for the small-business VAT franchise exemption (Article 57 Loi TVA, in force from 1 January 2024; formerly EUR 30,000); VAT registration is compulsory above EUR 35,000. B2B supplies of anti-cheat middleware to Luxembourg or other EU business customers fall under the reverse-charge mechanism (no local registration needed for the non-resident supplier). For B2C e-services: EU-established suppliers apply a EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance/e-services threshold; non-EU suppliers of B2C e-services may sign up to the Union/Non-Union OSS and need no Luxembourg fixed establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(A) Goods: 0% export exemption (Article 24 Loi TVA implementing Article 146 Directive 2006/112/EC) applies where the seller dispatches goods outside the EU within 90 days of supply and retains the export declaration and transport evidence. (B) Software/middleware provided as downloadable licences or cloud services: zero-rating follows the place-of-supply rules – B2B electronic services are outside Luxembourg VAT and subject to reverse charge at the customer's establishment (Article 14 Loi TVA); B2C e-services of non-established suppliers are taxed in the customer's Member State via OSS (Article 16 Loi TVA; Articles 58/59 Directive). For non-EU customers (arm's-length OEM game studios), the supply is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and no local VAT is chargeable; invoices must state that the place of supply is outside the EU and, for EU customers, 'reverse charge' with the customer's VAT ID.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD/export Entry with MRN) evidencing exit of tangible media or hardware dongles from the EU, or the signed licence/service agreement for pure e-delivery as substitute export evidence
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (multimodal transport document) proving dispatch within the 90-day export period where physical goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) – bank credit advice / SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment in the agreed currency, issued by the receiving Luxembourg credit institution (original statement, not photostatic copy)
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, order acceptance and licence deployment schedule reconciled to the payment rail (SWIFT Wire or Stripe)
  • EU/non-EU customer identity documentation (VIES VAT validation result for EU customers; certificate of incorporation or tax-residence certificate for non-EU customers)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls or digital stamping regime exists in Luxembourg; there is no API pre-authorisation of invoices by the AEDT. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B transactions. Compulsory e-invoicing applies only to B2G: invoices to central-government entities must be submitted as EN 16931 structured e-invoices (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) via a PEPPOL access point/MyGuichet gateway under the Law of 22 May 2019 (mandatory for central state bodies since 1 July 2022). Postponed EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) measures will later introduce standardised B2B e-invoicing and continuous transaction controls (target implementation after 2030).

AEDT/ACD risk-based audits and cross-border information exchanges (DAC7, EU VAT exchange) examine export documentation in substance-over-form reviews. If zero-rating is unsupported or documentation is fabricated, the AEDT reverses the 0% treatment and assesses VAT at 17% on the full consideration, plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month from the original return due date. Statute of limitations: five years for ordinary assessments, extended to ten years in fraud cases. Civil and criminal sanctions under Articles 426–433 LGI: administrative fines up to EUR 1,500 per infringement; criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years for fraudulent export claims, with publication of the conviction and joint and several liability of the general partner (commandité). Luxembourg statute does not impose a fixed 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is the ACD's customary documentary benchmark, and un-repatriated receivables beyond that period may be re-characterised as constructive distributions to the partner in an audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) guarantees unrestricted movement of capital and payments within and into the EU; no local exchange-control legislation imposes approval, quota, or repatriation limits. Eurozone membership confers exchange-rate certainty within the single currency, and SCSp profit distributions to partners are freely remittable without statutory withholding at the partnership level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: Luxembourg imposes neither a statutory surrender timeline nor any mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts in EUR or foreign currency may be held in domestic or offshore accounts without restriction, subject only to EU sanctions screening and the bank's internal compliance review.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as central bank within the Eurosystem, with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) exercising day-to-day prudential and AML/CFT supervision over credit institutions and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderately viable as a secondary rail: Luxembourg-licensed EMIs (e-money institutions under PSD2) and pan-EU EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex can support non-EUR receivables and low-ticket licensing payments; however, they have reduced commercial-lending capacity and cannot substitute for a full-service Luxembourg bank in high-value SWIFT settlements, documentary credits, or treasury/hedging products. EMI adoption is therefore confined to overflow routing for sub-USD 10,000 SaaS and anti-cheat subscription fees.

Turnover: An unregulated SCSp is generally not subject to a mandatory independent audit because it has no legal personality and is not obliged to publish annual accounts. A statutory audit becomes compulsory when the SCSp is a CSSF-regulated alternative investment fund, when the partnership agreement mandates a réviseur d'entreprises agréé, or when the entity is grouped with a Luxembourg corporate general partner that itself crosses the Luxembourg large-undertaking thresholds.

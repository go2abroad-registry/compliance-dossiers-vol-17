Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a standard SARL under the amended Law of 10 August 1915, unless a higher amount is fixed in the articles. All subscribed shares must be fully paid up in cash before execution of the notarial deed. The contribution may be deposited in a Luxembourg credit institution account in the Company's name, or with a notary's escrow account or the Caisse de Consignation, and remains blocked until registration as confirmed by a bank certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance obtained from the communal fire prevention authority for the registered office, structural load assessment where archival or storage fit-out exceeds normal office loading, and occupational health conformity assessed by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) with emergency evacuation arrangements and first-aid equipment in place.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified government e-administration portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu; e-filing platforms: eCDF and SOFIA.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL obtains its statutory tax matricule automatically upon notarial incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés); the matricule serves as the corporate income tax TIN. VAT registration with the AEDT is mandatory within 15 days of commencing taxable activities and is filed through MyGuichet.lu (eTVA Form 1040) or in paper form, producing a Luxembourg TVA identification number. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg fixed establishment that makes taxable supplies or incurs VAT in Luxembourg must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative and register for VAT; a foreign entity with a fixed establishment must register both the establishment in the RCS and obtain a Luxembourg matricule plus VAT ID before supplying services. No minimum capital or local director condition is imposed solely for VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is an opaque tax-resident corporation subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune), taxed on worldwide income with participation exemption relief and no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; SARL classification is fixed by law as opaque. Available elections include: (i) fiscal unity / tax consolidation election under Article 164bis LIR for CIT and municipal business tax; (ii) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains (10% holding or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost and 12-month holding period); (iii) 80% IP box exemption election under Article 50bis LIR for qualifying patent and software IP income, subject to the nexus ratio; (iv) VAT grouping election under Article 60 of the VAT Law; (v) ATAD / anti-hybrid mismatch rules (Law of 21 December 2018) are mandatory self-assessed adjustments rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for domestic taxable persons — a SARL must register for VAT from the first euro of taxable activity. An optional small-enterprise exemption (franchise) applies under Article 57 of the VAT Law only while annual turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000, with the right to opt into regular taxation. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, the Union-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; B2B services follow place-of-supply rules and are wholly outside the domestic registration threshold framework.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Patent prosecution services supplied by a Luxembourg SARL to an EU taxable person are subject to the reverse charge with Luxembourg VAT at 0% (place of supply in the customer's Member State under Article 43 of the VAT Law / Article 44 of the EU VAT Directive), and must be reported in the EC Sales List. Services supplied to non-EU taxable persons fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT, provided the customer's business status is evidenced. Physical exports of goods, including certified file copies and sample dossiers, are zero-rated under Article 53 of the VAT Law when proof of the outward customs declaration is retained; intra-EU dispatches are zero-rated where the customer's VAT ID is valid and transmitted. Official patent office fees reimbursed strictly on behalf of a client may be excluded from the taxable base as disbursements under Article 45 of the VAT Law where the SARL acts in a disclosed agency capacity, including for PCT, EPO and WIPO filings. All documentary evidence must be complete before the return filing deadline; otherwise the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 17%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / PLATO ICS declaration) — required solely for physical exports of goods, samples or certified file copies
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (carrier-issued transport evidence for physical export)
  • Signed Service Agreement and Engagement Letter (evidence of B2B service supply and place of supply)
  • Customer's Validated EU VAT ID and VIES confirmation printout (for intra-EU reverse charge)
  • Overseas Customer Business Registration Certificate, Tax Residency Certificate, or equivalent proof of foreign taxable status (for non-EU services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payment evidence showing collection of export proceeds on bank records

None — Luxembourg currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API-based exchange, and no digital stamping regime for B2B VAT invoices. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies to public contracts through the Luxembourg public procurement platform using PEPPOL / Peppol BIS 3.0 and EN 16931 data model. Electronic storage of invoices is permitted provided authenticity and integrity are guaranteed by EDI, digital signature, or PEPPOL compliant transmission. VAT invoices must be issued no later than the 15th day of the month following the month of supply. The EU ViDA framework is expected to introduce general mandatory e-invoicing, but not before 2030/2035.

The AEDT may retrospectively claw back the unwound VAT and issue a deficiency assessment on any export zero-rating claim lacking satisfactory evidence, within the general five-year limitation period, extended to ten years where fraud is established. Unpaid VAT attracts statutory late-payment interest compounded monthly; administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 per infringement may be imposed for faulty or incomplete documentation. Deliberate fraudulent export zero-rating supported by forged invoices, false customs declarations, or fabricated foreign payment evidence constitutes tax fraud under Luxembourg law, punishable by fines up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment of up to five years pursuant to the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung). Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control restrictions; the 180-day repatriation benchmark reflects the internal documentary and treaty-mapping control requirement for the patent prosecution fee cycle rather than a statutory currency-control deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg; no general exchange control or foreign exchange licensing regime applies to current or capital transactions. The EU Treaty and OECD liberalisation codes govern cross-border movements, with profit remittances permitted after annual accounts approval and board shareholder distribution decision. Non-resident profit remittances are legally unrestricted, although Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15% applies at statutory rate and may be reduced under applicable double tax treaties. No separate convertibility approval is required for patent prosecution agency fees, client remittances, royalty streams, or intercompany settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg maintains no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion percentage, or repatriation timeline. Export and service receipts may be retained freely in euro or in any foreign currency in a resident or non-resident bank account. Regulatory balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque centrale du Luxembourg may apply to cross-border flows for statistical purposes, but no conversion or surrender duty exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High for operational disbursements and low-value official patent office wire transfers; Medium for general FX transparency. Luxembourg and EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions with a valid MiFID/EMD licence and PSD2 passport may onboard the SARL remotely, subject to Luxembourg AML/CFT law. Such EMIs can provide payment accounts and cross-border settlement rails, including SEPA and Wise-style services, but generally cannot provide credit or serve as escrow providers for regulated client funds. A licensed bank remains advisable for capital escrow, large cross-border receipts, documentary credits, and long-term treasury lines.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injections and shareholder contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the ultimate parent or funding bank. The compliant wire instruction should include ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity investment) or, for shareholder loans, GENR/CORP/CLEAN among permissible counterparty purpose codes. The beneficiary message must identify the SARL by exact legal name and Luxembourg IBAN, with a narrative citing the notarial deed of incorporation, share capital subscription, or capital increase resolution. Verification required by the receiving bank includes the notarised deed, updated RCS/EBR extract, UBO declaration, and confirmation that the capital is booked on a Luxembourg bank account. Cross-border direct investment transactions are reportable for Banque centrale du Luxembourg balance-of-payments statistical purposes.Outbound disbursements for global patent prosecution and trademark registration must use bank-grade clearing rails. SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is used for euro-denominated payments within the EEA, including official fees to European Patent Office, EUIPO, national patent offices, and foreign trademark registries. SWIFT MT103 is used for non-SEPA disbursements and any currency other than EUR. Commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700/MT707) are appropriate only for ancillary trade settlements, never for statutory official fees. The SARL may also use the WIPO Current Account mechanism for international filings. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% CIT base rate (Luxembourg corporate income tax) plus 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT; combined CIT 18.19% before municipal business tax; aggregate effective headline in Luxembourg City approximates 24.94% including municipal business tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on Luxembourg-source dividends paid by a Luxembourg SARL to a non-resident corporate or individual shareholder; 0% applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% of share capital, plus 0% under Luxembourg participation exemption for controlling shareholders meeting holding-period and comparable-taxation requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File obligation triggers where the Luxembourg entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 350 million; Local File obligation triggers where the Luxembourg entity's own annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million. Transactions with related parties below these thresholds still require contemporaneous benchmarking evidence and a DESC-compliant TP file if the tax inspector requests it based on transaction value and risk profile.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a pure offshore ESR like Cayman/Caribbean regimes, but a full economic substance doctrine applies under general anti-abuse rules, the OECD BEPS nexus approach for IP regimes, and Luxembourg court doctrine requiring the actual exercise of senior management, strategic decision-making, personnel, premises, and own equipment to obtain treaty and directive benefits. A patent prosecution SARL must therefore demonstrate face-to-face client intake, patent drafting control, substantive PTO interactions, and commercial risk absorption in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Incorrect Declared Income

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to EUR 250,000 or three times the evaded tax; additional 0.8% monthly late-payment interest; civil restitution of all due Luxembourg CIT and municipal business tax; criminal fine and possible seizure of assets in amounts equivalent to concealed taxable base.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Luxembourg law and joint/several liability of directors for corporate tax debts caused by intentional wrongful acts; travel bans, temporary ban on commercial activities, personal bankruptcy sanction, and imprisonment up to 2 years for conviction of tax fraud and up to 5 years for aggravated fraud by use of false instruments.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of IP regime benefits; denial of tax treaty relief and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; 80% IP deduction recompute with back taxes, penalties equal to 10%–50% of the recalculated taxable amount, and consequential domestic withholding tax assessments at 15% on deemed distributions; administrative closure of the company and removal of the Chamber of Commerce registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and formally appointed managers face civil restitution orders; a personal prohibition on business activity and/or withdrawal of the required professional license for patent prosecution; criminal prosecution for fiscal evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 150,000 where the vehicle is used for tax abuse.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment reallocating taxable income to Luxembourg with 5.21% IP-box or 24.94% standard CIT; late-interest monthly at 0.8%; administrative fine up to EUR 20,000 per missing documentation set; denial of deductible payments and withholding tax applied to deemed dividend or quasi-dividend distributions.

Director personal liability: Senior managers are personally liable for the corporate tax adjustments resulting from intentional false documentation or for failure to maintain statutory records; the Commercial Court may issue a seizure order against directors' personal assets and order disqualification of technical signatory rights for the patent agency license.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 2 million against legal persons; disgorgement of proceeds; closure of client account banking; criminal fine of up to EUR 1.25 million per act; criminal sanctions for non-disclosure to the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU). Critical note: patent prosecution agencies, though low-risk, are obligated professionals under the revised CSSF AML law.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face deprivation of liberty from 6 months to 5 years for money laundering complicity; a travel ban and 21st century version of banishment within Schengen can be imposed upon arraignment; professional mandate permanently removed by the IP professional chamber registration body; personal restitution equal to all laundered fee values.
Failure to File Tax Return / Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative ex officio assessment without deductions, triggering 100% tax jurisdiction over worldwide income in absence of treaty declaration; penalty charge of 10% of assessed tax (minimum EUR 100) plus late-filing fine up to EUR 1,000 per missing year; irreversible forfeiture of IP regime exemptions due to failure to claim in the filed return.

Director personal liability: Statutorily mandatory director declaration: failing to approve and sign returns exposes directors to direct recovery under Luxembourg insolvency rules; judges may imposing a 3-year travel ban and EUR 50,000 civil sanction; criminal contempt verdict in the administrative court grants 60-day default penalty ordering the company to comply and, additionally, temporary custody of managing director in severe habitual violations.

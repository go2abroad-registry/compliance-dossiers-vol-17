Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) for entity registration; central administrative portal: myguichet.lu / guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a non-established foreign entity: (1) determine whether supplies qualify for the Non-Union or Union One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme, which may eliminate the need for full Luxembourg VAT registration for cross-border B2C digital services; (2) if full registration is required, file the VAT registration application with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits, and appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative if the entity is established outside the EU and no reciprocal assistance arrangement applies; (3) if an SCSp is established in Luxembourg, first file the constitutive document with the RCS and obtain the national business number (matricule d'identification), then open the VAT account using that matricule; (4) provide evidence of economic activity in Luxembourg, director/manager identification, and anti-money-laundering documentation (KYC).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct tax purposes: the entity itself is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax, and profits and losses are taxed directly at the level of its partners. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a taxable person in its own right when it carries on an economic activity independently, and registration thresholds apply according to normal VAT rules. — For a non-established foreign entity: (1) determine whether supplies qualify for the Non-Union or Union One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme, which may eliminate the need for full Luxembourg VAT registration for cross-border B2C digital services; (2) if full registration is required, file the VAT registration application with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits, and appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative if the entity is established outside the EU and no reciprocal assistance arrangement applies; (3) if an SCSp is established in Luxembourg, first file the constitutive document with the RCS and obtain the national business number (matricule d'identification), then open the VAT account using that matricule; (4) provide evidence of economic activity in Luxembourg, director/manager identification, and anti-money-laundering documentation (KYC).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg direct tax purposes: the entity itself is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax, and profits and losses are taxed directly at the level of its partners. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a taxable person in its own right when it carries on an economic activity independently, and registration thresholds apply according to normal VAT rules.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box classification regime. The SCSp is a contractually based special limited partnership that is transparent by default for direct tax; no direct-tax election to make the SCSp itself taxable is generally available. For VAT, the entity may elect between the normal local VAT registration route and the OSS schemes for distance sales and electronically supplied services. Luxembourg also applies the EU small-business exemption election for established taxable persons with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, and hybrid-mismatch rules consistent with ATAD require partners/investors to align the tax classification of the SCSp with the corresponding treatment in their residence jurisdictions where otherwise a mismatch would arise.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no prior-authorisation regime, no surrender obligations and no convertibility restrictions, in accordance with Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittance, partnership-interest redemption, dividend and interest distributions are freely permitted without central-bank approval; the only practical hurdles are EU/Luxembourg financial sanctions, BCL external-statistics reporting thresholds, and the enhanced due-diligence documentation that Luxembourg banks require for a medium-risk online social-entertainment business such as a random video-match application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg and the Eurozone do not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory repatriation window or forced conversion ratio for export receipts. Cross-border revenue settled by Apple and Paymentwall may be retained in EUR or converted into foreign currency freely in the market, subject only to sanctions screening and the BCL external-statistics reporting duties fulfilled through the resident banking intermediary.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the Luxembourg member of the Eurosystem — together with the European Central Bank (ECB), which conducts monetary and foreign-exchange policy, and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which supervises credit institutions and payment institutions. Luxembourg has no separate foreign-exchange-control administration; the BCL manages foreign-exchange reserve operations delegated by the ECB and administers the balance-of-payments statistical reporting framework for resident entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An EEA-licensed e-money institution or payment institution authorised under PSD2/EMD2 and passportable across the EU single market can onboard the SCSp remotely and can serve as a cost-efficient collection and payout rail for Paymentwall and Apple settlement proceeds; however, such institutions are not direct members of TARGET2/STEP2 clearing, commonly impose transaction-volume caps, and do not provide the full treasury function (surplus-liquidity investment, FX credit lines, wholesale recovery). Non-EEA or offshore digital EMIs are rated Low: they constitute a serious AML/CTF blocker under Luxembourg law and CSSF supervisory expectations and must not hold operational treasury balances of the SCSp.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

SCSp is a contractual special limited partnership without legal personality, creating uncertainty about the counterparty, its legal capacity and the identity of parties legally bound by the banking relationship.

Root cause: SCSp is a contractual special limited partnership without legal personality, creating uncertainty about the counterparty, its legal capacity and the identity of parties legally bound by the banking relationship.

Defense evidence: Full partnership agreement (contrat de société) governed by the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended by the Law of 23 July 2016 which introduced the SCSp; constitutive documents filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS); current extract from the Register of Beneficial Owners (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs); resolution or mandate of the general partner appointing the authorised signatories; specimen signatures; and, where required by the bank, a legal-short-form opinion confirming the SCSp's capacity, representation rules and liability allocation.
High-volume micro-transactions processed through Apple IAP and Paymentwall in the online social-entertainment sector, raising money-laundering, chargeback, fraud and consumer-protection risks.

Root cause: High-volume micro-transactions processed through Apple IAP and Paymentwall in the online social-entertainment sector, raising money-laundering, chargeback, fraud and consumer-protection risks.

Defense evidence: A written business-description memorandum and revenue-flow diagram; executed merchant or developer agreements with Apple and Paymentwall; monthly reconciliation evidence linking app-store sales reports to bank and processor settlement statements; the company AML/CTF manual with automated transaction-monitoring thresholds and suspicious-transaction reporting procedures to the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit (Cellule de Renseignements Financiers); chargeback and refund policy; and geo-IP restriction and sanctions-screening rules applicable to end-users.

Root cause: Source of funds and source of wealth for the initial capital injection and ongoing operational funding is not self-evident for a tax-transparent partnership owned by non-residents.

Defense evidence: Investor bank statements and tax-residency certificates; capital-call and subscription notices issued by the general partner; declarations of source of wealth covering the origin of contributed assets; UBO and politically-exposed-person questionnaires; audited or management-prepared 12-month working-capital budget; and supporting contracts demonstrating how Apple and Paymentwall settlements fund operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident investors inject equity or loan capital in EUR either through SEPA for EU-origin investors or through SWIFT MT103 for non-EU investors, to the SCSp's segregated euro account at its Luxembourg credit institution; the remittance-information field must state the SCSp name, the investor identifier and the purpose 'SCSp capital contribution / subscription', or a SWIFT category code such as '//INV//' where required by the remitting bank. The bank's verification file must contain the executed subscription agreement, a capital-call notice issued by the general partner, investor KYC/PEP questionnaires, bank statements as source-of-funds evidence and an updated RBE filing. The BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for the inward foreign direct investment flow is discharged through the resident banking intermediary and the GP's annual external-statistics survey. Inward app-store settlements from Apple arriving as net service revenue are classified under the applicable balance-of-payments code for software or royalty services and are not treated as capital injections, although they must still be supported by the underlying Apple developer agreement for source-of-funds verification.Operating disbursements for cloud infrastructure, content moderation, advertising, legal and tax advisory services are executed via SWIFT MT103 single credit transfers from the SCSp's EUR account, or via SWIFT MT101 multi-payment batch files for repetitive vendor sweeps; SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant are used for EU counterparties below the BCL de minimis reporting threshold, with TARGET2/STEP2 as the underlying clearing rail for same-day euro settlements. Confirmed commercial documentary credits under UCP 600, cleared through SWIFT MT700/MT710 messages, are available for large non-EU supplier contracts but are functionally uncommon for the video-app operating model. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp partnership level (fiscally transparent pursuant to Article 7 of the Law of 12 July 2013 and general Luxembourg partnership law principles); resident corporate partner combined headline rate 24.94% calculated as 17% corporate income tax (IRC), 1.19% solidarity surcharge (7% of IRC) and 6.75% municipal business tax (Luxembourg City tariff; municipal rates vary by commune).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax rate under Article 147 LIR; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate partner holds at least 10% continuously for 12 months and meets anti-hybrid conditions; 0% or reduced rates under comprehensive double tax treaties (selected treaties 5% for portfolio, 0% for qualifying substantive holdings). For SCSp, profit distributions are generally not treated as dividends because the partnership is transparent; a 15% WHT will instead attach only where the underlying payment is Luxembourg-source income allocated to a non-resident partner without treaty protection or where an Article 175 LIR opaque election is made.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For calendar-year taxpayers: statutory filing due 30 June of the year following the fiscal year-end; e-filing concession routinely extends the deadline to 31 December; newly incorporated taxpayers must file within 3 months of commencement/registration (option to extend via tax advisor).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory simplified transfer pricing documentation under Article 56bis LIR; EUR 5,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions triggers full Master File / Local File and contemporaneous benchmarking evidence under Article 56ter LIR; documentation must be prepared no later than the date of filing of the corporate income tax return and produced within 15 days of request by the tax authorities.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not codify a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to UAE onshore/DIFC regimes, but substance is enforced through: (i) beneficial ownership analysis applied by the Luxembourg tax administration and the OECD Multi-Lateral Instrument, (ii) Limitation on Benefits clauses in Luxembourg double tax treaties (e.g. Article 24 of the US-Luxembourg Treaty), (iii) European Commission State aid substance doctrine and EU ATAD anti-abuse rules, and (iv) EU Unshell/ATAD III Directive scrutiny of shell entities from 2024. For an SCSp, the General Partner must maintain demonstrable substance in Luxembourg - local registered office, local bank account, real board meetings, appropriate human resources, books and records - and must satisfy the commercial reasonableness of the video-match app value chain; a bare IP-holding SCSp with no local substance risks treaty denial, WHT recapture and reassessment at 24.94%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at 24.94% plus 10% late-filing surcharge and 0.8% monthly default interest; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years under Articles 96 and 97 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law; publication of the tax decision in case of aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: The General Partner (Gérant) of an SCSp is personally, jointly and severally liable for the debts and tax obligations of the partnership; a fraudulent under-declaration allows the Revenue to pierce the veil, claim civil restitution against personal assets, impose travel bans on directors pending criminal proceedings, and obtain imprisonment sentences of up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and Directive relief, retroactive 15% withholding tax on distributions, reclassification of the structure as fiscally non-transparent or void for tax purposes, penalties for non-compliance with UBO registration (EUR 110 to EUR 1,250,000), and exposure to EU Unshell Directive penalties once transposed.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner are jointly liable for the resulting tax shortfall and WHT; in severe cases, fraudulent misrepresentation triggers criminal liability, freezing orders over personal assets, and restriction of the right to manage companies in Luxembourg.
Transfer Pricing Violations

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment adding back non-arm's-length payments, secondary adjustment treating the excess as a deemed dividend subject to 15% withholding tax, penalty of 25% on the additional assessment, monthly default interest of 0.8% on underpaid tax, and documentary fines for failure to produce TP files.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved transfer prices lacking an economic or functional basis face civil liability in the amount of the tax adjustment, potential wrongful trading claims by the SCSp's creditors, and criminal sanction for complicity in tax fraud.
AML and Banking Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities, administrative sanctions by the Financial Intelligence Unit (CRF), criminal conviction for money laundering punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and confiscation of assets, and freezing of the Paymentwall/Apple merchant account.

Director personal liability: Directors and the General Partner face personal criminal conviction, travel bans, and confiscation orders; proof of knowing participation pierces the corporate veil, leading to unlimited personal liability for fraud.
Disguised Profit Distribution / Withholding Tax Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of payments as dividends or quasi-dividends under Articles 164 and 168 LIR, 15% withholding tax reclaimed from the payer, denial of the expense deduction leading to tax at 24.94%, 10% surcharge, and monthly default interest during any litigated period.

Director personal liability: The General Partner's directors are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax and interest; deliberately structuring sham payments constitutes tax evasion and attracts the same personal criminal exposure as fraudulent under-declaration.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) – Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Luxembourg City

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Luxembourg Companies Act), Articles 604-1 to 604-12 (Société en Commandite Spéciale regime introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013); supplemented by the amended Law of 2 September 2011 on access to professions and the RCS Regulations of Luxembourg Business Registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic registration dossier (dossier d'immatriculation) filed via the LBR portal, comprising the executed partnership agreement (contrat de société), subscription declaration, and statutory declarations of the general partner and gérant.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital, no escrow account, and no bank capital certificate is required for an SCSp; the partners agree freely on their contributions (cash or in kind) in the partnership agreement, and the registered office and contributions must be identified to the RCSL (if requested, evidence of contribution may be required).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: for a standard office/IT workspace, a fire-safety attestation from the building owner or a light fire-safety certificate for offices (respecting Grand-Ducal Regulation on workplace premises) suffices; no civil defense clearance is required. Occupational clearance: comply with the labour code and the Law of 17 June 1994 on occupational health and safety, including electrical safety, work equipment testing, first-aid provisions, and fire escape plans; no special structural load inspection applies.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital, no escrow account, and no bank capital certificate is required for an SCSp; the partners agree freely on their contributions (cash or in kind) in the partnership agreement, and the registered office and contributions must be identified to the RCSL (if requested, evidence of contribution may be required).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration and indirect taxes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate and personal income tax matters. The statutory registration and filing portal is MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu, operated by the Luxembourg government in cooperation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and the RCS.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg that makes taxable supplies or holds a Luxembourg fixed establishment must apply to the AEDT for VAT registration before the first taxable transaction. EU-established entities register directly through MyGuichet.lu using authenticated electronic identity; non-EU-established entities are required to appoint an accredited VAT fiscal representative. The application consists of the official VAT registration form, constitutional documents, proof of the beneficial owners and directors, the registered office address, and, for the SCSp, the RCS extraction confirming the registered partnership. Upon completion, the AEDT issues the Luxembourg national identification number (matricule) and a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU00000000. The same matricule is used by the ACD for direct tax purposes, and no separate direct-tax enrollment is required for a fiscally transparent SCSp.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg that makes taxable supplies or holds a Luxembourg fixed establishment must apply to the AEDT for VAT registration before the first taxable transaction. EU-established entities register directly through MyGuichet.lu using authenticated electronic identity; non-EU-established entities are required to appoint an accredited VAT fiscal representative. The application consists of the official VAT registration form, constitutional documents, proof of the beneficial owners and directors, the registered office address, and, for the SCSp, the RCS extraction confirming the registered partnership. Upon completion, the AEDT issues the Luxembourg national identification number (matricule) and a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU00000000. The same matricule is used by the ACD for direct tax purposes, and no separate direct-tax enrollment is required for a fiscally transparent SCSp.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An SCSp is by law classified as fiscally transparent for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax purposes, while it constitutes a taxable person for VAT purposes. No statutory election allows the SCSp to opt into entity-level corporate income taxation. However, taxpayers may request an advance tax ruling from the Luxembourg tax authorities to confirm the transparency classification and to determine any hybrid mismatch consequence under the ATAD rules where another jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises under the domestic small-business exemption; non-established taxable persons have no threshold and registration is mandatory from the first Luxembourg taxable supply, subject to EU schemes for distance sales and OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for zero-rated export VAT provided the exporter holds contemporaneous proof of physical dispatch or transport out of the EU within the deadlines specified in the Luxembourg VAT Law. Intra-Community supplies to a EU VAT-registered customer are zero-rated under the conditions of Article 43 of the VAT Directive, as transposed, requiring the customer's valid VAT number and EC Sales List reporting. For digital and SaaS services such as low-code website builder subscriptions, supplies to a non-EU business customer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT or zero-rated as B2B services under the place-of-supply rules where the customer is established outside the EU and the recipient is a taxable person. Where the non-EU customer is a private consumer, the supply falls outside Luxembourg VAT, but is not classified as an export zero-rating event. Input VAT deduction on costs attributed to zero-rated exports is permitted if the same export substantiation file is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration in the ECS system / Single Administrative Document) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or other transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and corresponding bank statements supporting the FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice matching the VAT invoice, together with the export contract or purchase order
  • Proof of dispatch from warehouse including packing lists, shipping instructions and inventory records
  • Customer VAT identification number for intra-Community zero-rating; for non-EU B2B services, evidence of the customer's business status and establishment jurisdiction
  • Declaration of value, origin, and any export license or permit where required under EU customs law

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping requirement for B2B or B2C invoices. Electronic invoicing is voluntary in the private sector unless contractually agreed. Invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector entities are subject to mandatory e-invoicing through the national electronic invoicing infrastructure based on the PEPPOL interoperability framework. All taxable persons remain subject to the standard VAT invoicing conditions under Luxembourg VAT law.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict ex-post audit by the AEDT and Luxembourg customs authorities. Missing, incomplete or late export evidence causes the zero rate to be denied, resulting in reassessment at the standard 17% VAT rate plus late payment interest. Fabricating export declarations, issuing false bills of lading, or knowingly submitting fraudulent documentation triggers clawback of all VAT avoided, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud, VAT evasion and forgery. The AEDT and the Public Prosecutor may also impose monetary penalties proportionate to the tax evaded, and limitation periods are extended in cases of fraud or wilful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has complete convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals, no currency quota, and no profit-repatriation licensing. For an SCSp governed by the Luxembourg law of 2013 on special limited partnerships, profit distributions follow the partnership agreement and are generally paid without entity-level Luxembourg withholding tax where the SCSp is tax-transparent for Luxembourg purposes. Residual barriers are limited to standard AML/KYC onboarding, EU/UN sanctions screening, and fiscal documentation obligations (FATCA/CRS). No de facto restriction applies to a no-code software seller collecting USD, EUR, or other currencies from global sub-sellers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory surrender requirement, holding-period obligation, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Foreign-currency receipts may be credited directly to an SCSp foreign-currency accounts without BCL approval or forced conversion to EUR. The SCSp may retain USD balances, sell them via a forward, or convert them on the spot market. The only Treasury requirement is evidentiary documentation for the underlying software service transaction under EU Regulation 2015/847 and the Luxembourg AML/CTF law.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting together with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for bank licensing, AML/CTF supervision, and payment-system oversight. The BCL does not operate a discretionary foreign-exchange permit regime; Luxembourg is bound by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payment rail, Low as the primary treasury or escrow banking layer. Luxembourg-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU-authorized digital banks passporting under PSD2 can process SaaS subscription pay-ins through Stripe/Paddle, issue IBANs, and support low-value disbursements. However, offshore non-EU digital EMIs are not permissible as the core corporate bank for a Luxembourg SCSp because they lack a full EU credit-institution licence and cannot safely escrow partnership capital contributions or support trade-clearing services. The feasible model is to use an EU-licensed EMI solely for collection/disbursement automation and settle net balances daily to a BCL-supervised credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions to the Luxembourg SCSp must be routed through a licensed EU credit institution using SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR wires. No BCL approval or exchange-control permit is required. The compliant protocol requires the full SCSp legal name as beneficiary in MT103 field 59, a clear beneficiary IBAN, and a purpose description in field 70 including the words 'Capital contribution to SCSp' or 'Subcription payment in accordance with partnership agreement'. The sending bank must provide the investor’s full name, address, and account number pursuant to EU Regulation 2015/847. For USD or multi-currency inflows, the bank may use a correspondent clearing path; the SCSp may either hold the foreign currency or convert it by spot/forward transaction. To avoid onboarding delays, every inbound capital wire must be supported by the SCSp capital contribution agreement, an updated LP list, and an UBO identification pack lodged with the bank before the first payment.For normal operating disbursements, the primary clearing rails are SEPA Instant/SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR and SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP/CHF/JPY. The SCSp may pass through the Stripe/Paddle platform for receiving SaaS subscription revenue; subsequent vendor payments for cloud infrastructure, contractors, or third-party software tools are executed by a bank-originated wire. A commercial documentary credit (SWIFT MT700) is permissible for large cross-border procurement of hardware/licensed infrastructure where an irrevocable letter of credit is contractually required, although pure no-code software services usually settle by MT103. For avoidable concentration risk, the SCSp should maintain two MT103-capable correspondent bank accounts in Luxembourg/EU and apply matching discipline for USD cashflow receipts and disbursements.Deliverable FX Forwards between the SCSp and a Luxembourg/EU bank under an ISDA or local master agreement, for hedging USD/CHF/GBP receipts into EURNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-market-convertible emerging-market currencies that arise from software exportsCross-Currency Swaps to match long-dated capital contributions or multi-currency liquidity positionsVanilla FX Options purchased to establish a maximum EUR conversion cost without removing upside

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline rate for Luxembourg City (CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 1.19% + municipal business tax 6.75%); an SCSp is fiscally transparent and is not itself an RC taxpayer where pass-through treatment applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the rate to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders, and applicable DTAs may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, or 15%. A fiscally transparent SCSp pays no dividend withholding tax at partnership level; profit allocations are taxed at partner level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by 31 December of the following year. For non-calendar year-ends, the return is due no later than 12 months after the fiscal year-end unless the ACD grants an extension.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The transparency allocation statement must be filed by 31 December following the fiscal year-end; partner allocation schedules must be issued before the partners' own tax return deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 10 million annual cross-border related-party turnover (gross intragroup revenue, royalty streams, and service fees) triggers the mandatory Luxembourg Local File. A group Master File is required where worldwide consolidated revenue of the group exceeds EUR 750 million and the Luxembourg entity carries out related-party transactions. CbC reporting notification applies at the same EUR 750 million group threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not legislatively label an ESR rule, but economic substance is imposed through GAAR, Article 56 LIR, the ATAD principal-purpose test, the OECD MLI, and Luxembourg Anti-Abuse Law. An SCSp must show local administrative substance: real physical premises, local employees, executive decision-making by the general partner, separate bank accounts, financial statements, and minutes. A transparent structure without local substance will be recharacterised as a Luxembourg tax resident corporate vehicle or denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Failure to Declare Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of omitted income at the aggregate 24.94% CIT/municipal rate applicable to a corporate partner, 10% late-filing surcharge, 0.8% monthly arrears interest, and criminal fraud penalties including fines up to ten times the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the SCSp and any de facto manager who signs a false return is jointly liable for unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest. Courts may pierce the partnership veil to issue travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment where evasion is linked to money laundering.
Shell Company and Abuse of SCSp Fiscally Transparent Status

Statutory sanction: ACD disregards the pass-through personality and taxes the SCSp as a resident company: 24.94% CIT on worldwide commercial income, 0.5% net wealth tax where applicable, municipal business tax, and 15% dividend withholding tax on distributions; DTA benefits are denied and penalty interest applies.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable on the SCSp's obligations when the structure is a sham. Nominee partners and professional directors who facilitate the shell arrangement face civil restitution, disqualification from Luxembourg management, travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions under AML law.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg Article 56 LIR re-amends income to the arm's-length result; the shortfall is taxed at 24.94% plus default interest of 0.8% per month. A 50% to 100% negligence penalty is added where documentation is absent, fabricated, or contradictory; excess balances may be reclassified as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length contracts without obtaining comparables are personally liable for the additional corporate tax. Fabrication of TP documentation exposes the signing manager to criminal punishment and personal liability, including disqualification from board positions.
Disguised Profit Extraction and Withholding Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The excess interest or distribution is recharacterised as a dividend, attracting 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, a 10% failure-to-withhold penalty, and monthly interest of 0.8%. In fraud cases the responsible officer faces imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The managing director and general partner are personally liable for uncollected withholding taxes and penalties. Non-resident founders who knowingly misuse shareholder loans can be held personally liable and subjected to a Luxembourg anti-abuse investigation, with travel bans and asset seizures where the payment is traced to shell entities.
AML/Beneficial Ownership Failure and Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to EUR 1.25 million or 10% of annual turnover for AML failings; individual directors face fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for false RBE filings. The beneficial ownership statement may be suspended and enforced through criminal seizure of payment-system claims.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on the SCSp shield: the general partner and every manager who fails AML/KYC obligations is personally liable for fines, restoration of misused funds, travel restraints, and criminal prison sentences. Once AML liability attaches, the tax administration and public prosecutor can pierce all corporate and partnership layers to recover funds.

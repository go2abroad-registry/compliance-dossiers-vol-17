Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), entity incorporation department (RCSonline).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Act of 10 August 1915, as amended, on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), in particular Title IV Part II regarding the SCSp introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013; concordance with Luxembourg Civil Code and RCS organizational rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Coordinated Articles of Association for a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp), signed by or on behalf of the General Partner, filed by a Luxembourg incorporated service provider or lawyer through RCSonline.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 52 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum or paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp; contribution amount and payment timetable are freely fixed in the partnership agreement. If the partnership agreement provides for an initial contribution to a blocked escrow/bank account, such credit must be evidenced prior to registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Service d'Incendie et de Secours confirming the capacity and escape-plan compliance of the premises; municipal structural load and occupancy permit; ITM workplace health and safety clearance; an emergency plan and first-aid equipment must be maintained. If activity expands to a cargo terminal, additional structural load, dangerous goods segregation, and aviation security fence clearances must be obtained before operation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum or paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp; contribution amount and payment timetable are freely fixed in the partnership agreement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income/corporate taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration formalities; unified online access via MyGuichet.lu, guichet.lu, and the ACD/AEDT taxpayer e-portals (eCDF / espace personnel).

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SCSp is incorporated and filed with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through a notary or via MyGuichet.lu; on RCS registration the competent authorities automatically assign the statutory matricule and a 13-digit Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 7 digits + check digit). A foreign or non-established air-cargo charter broker without a Luxembourg fixed establishment must: (i) file the AEDT VAT application form through MyGuichet.lu; (ii) appoint a Luxembourg VAT agent when the AEDT requires a fiscal representative; (iii) furnish the constitutive documents, RCS excerpt (or foreign equivalent), and evidence of prospective taxable activity in Luxembourg; and (iv) obtain an EU EORI number from the Administration des douanes et accises for customs export procedures. Standard issuance timeline is approximately 10–15 working days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A Luxembourg SCSp is incorporated and filed with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through a notary or via MyGuichet.lu; on RCS registration the competent authorities automatically assign the statutory matricule and a 13-digit Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 7 digits + check digit). A foreign or non-established air-cargo charter broker without a Luxembourg fixed establishment must: (i) file the AEDT VAT application form through MyGuichet.lu; (ii) appoint a Luxembourg VAT agent when the AEDT requires a fiscal representative; (iii) furnish the constitutive documents, RCS excerpt (or foreign equivalent), and evidence of prospective taxable activity in Luxembourg; and (iv) obtain an EU EORI number from the Administration des douanes et accises for customs export procedures. Standard issuance timeline is approximately 10–15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: income, gains, and net wealth are attributed directly to its partners, and the partnership is not independently subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (IBT), or net wealth tax (NWT). The SCSp may, however, exercise the statutory election under Article 5bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) to 'opt for taxation' and become an opaque entity fully subject to CIT, IBT, and NWT from the beginning of the relevant fiscal year. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD/ATAD II implemented by the law of 21 December 2017, as amended) may re-characterize or restrict deductions where the SCSp is treated as fiscally transparent in Luxembourg but opaque in a partner's jurisdiction, or vice versa.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold exists for supplies of services or goods in Luxembourg: any taxable economic activity performed by a Luxembourg-established or non-established SCSp triggers mandatory VAT registration irrespective of turnover. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, the annual EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold applies; for non-established suppliers making taxable domestic supplies, VAT registration is required as of the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated treatment applies to (i) supplies of goods exported from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU where shipment is evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; and (ii) services directly connected with international transport of goods, including international air-freight services and air-cargo charter brokerage (charter hire, cargo handling, and freight-forwarding services) for cargo dispatched to non-EU territory, provided the supplier retains contemporary documentary evidence of the transport chain, the services are rendered to a non-EU counterparty or to an EU forwarder for goods destined outside the EU, and the invoice contains the statutory export/international-transport zero-rating reference. The 0% rate is strictly conditional on proof of physical exportation or actual performance of the international transport service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic AES export declaration sent to the Administration des douanes et accises)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing dispatch from Luxembourg or another EU point to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / documentary proof of payment received from the foreign charterer, foreign broker, or non-EU consignee

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance (CTC) or transaction-level e-clearance regime. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers delivering to public administrations under the EN 16931/PEPPOL framework since 2020. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary; structured electronic formats, including hybrid and XML invoices, are administratively accepted. Luxembourg has announced alignment with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package and the successive introduction of mandatory B2B e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting in accordance with the EU-wide implementation timetable.

Export zero-rating is strictly evidence-based. In a VAT audit, the AEDT may retroactively disallow the 0% rate and reassess output VAT at the standard 17% rate where the export or international-transport documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or absent, with statutory late-payment interest accruing from the original due date. Administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 per infraction may be imposed on the taxable person, with aggravated fines for recidivism. Intentional VAT or customs-export fraud gives rise to criminal prosecution, exposure to criminal fines and imprisonment under the Luxembourg VAT Law and the Luxembourg Criminal Code, publication of sanctions, and joint liability of the SCSp's general partner or managing partner for penalties arising from deliberate concealment or false documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the European Union and the euro area. The euro is fully convertible; capital movements, current payments, and profit remittances are generally unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Movements Directive. No exchange control approvals are required for investment capital contributions, dividend/profit distributions, or trade-related cross-border payments. Profit remittance from a Luxembourg SCSp is subject only to standard withholding tax compliance, substance/economic reality review, and anti-money laundering / counter terrorist financing checks; legal convertibility barriers are effectively absent. However, transfers to or from high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions require enhanced due diligence, not exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in Luxembourg. The euro being fully convertible, all export receipts in EUR or foreign currency may be retained, converted, or disbursed freely. A Luxembourg air cargo charter broker is not obliged to repatriate or sell export proceeds to the BCL or a licensed bank. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments reporting thresholds and AML transaction monitoring/reporting by the banking counterparty.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for bank supervision and exchange control-related reporting; the BCL is also a member of the European System of Central Banks (ESCB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) holding a valid Luxembourg or EU-passported licence under the EU E-Money Directive (Directive 2009/110/EC) and PSD2 are viable for limited-purpose receivables and disbursements, but they are not a full substitute for a commercial bank for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, and large air-charter settlement operations. Off-shore or non-EU digital EMIs without a branch, agent, or passporting arrangement in Luxembourg are institutionally not viable for regulated corporate accounts; they cannot credibly provide the required AML/KYC, SWIFT correspondent relationships, and liquidity assurance for freight forwarding and charter brokerage transactions, and their use may trigger significant regulatory concern at the CSSF and BCL level.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder or limited partner capital injections to the Luxembourg SCSp should be routed exclusively through regulated Tier-1 banks by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent bona fide wire instructions, using a clearly identified purpose code that describes the capital contribution to a partnership account, such as 'equity injection / capital contribution to partnership capital' in the underlying remittance information. The sending bank shall include a reference to the partnership agreement, the contributor's name, the source-of-funds proof, and the recipient's IBAN. For euro-denominated intra-EU injections, SEPA Credit Transfer is permissible; for USD or other non-EUR inflows, correspondents may require the use of an intermediary account. The bank must complete the beneficiary verification, and where applicable, provide BCL statistical reporting if the transaction reaches the relevant thresholds. No exchange control approval is required, but the moving bank should evidence the economic purpose of the injection to satisfy AML/KYC expectations.Outbound trade disbursements for air charter and freight forwarding settlements should use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for straight payments to aircraft operators, airports, handlers, and suppliers; if the counterparty requires documentary security, eligible clearing rails include Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits subject to UCP 600) issued by the Luxembourg bank to the beneficiary bank. For euro payments inside the EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer is the primary clearing rail. For USD payments involving the United States, the payment should be cleared through the Correspondent account with Fedwire capability, while maintaining all sanctions screening and counterparty verification. The commercial freight ledger must aggregate each advance payment, contract invoice, and charter settlement in the same currency and with the funds flow corresponding to the underlying trade documents.Outright deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts in EUR/USD and other liquid G10 currenciesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted-convertibility currencies where physical settlement is impossible, relying on a fix rate benchmarkCross-currency swaps for converting and settling recurring USD-denominated charter payments versus EUR capital and operating accounts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) holding a valid Luxembourg or EU-passported licence under the EU E-Money Directive (Directive 2009/110/EC) and PSD2 are viable for limited-purpose receivables and disbursements, but they are not a full substitute for a commercial bank for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, and large air-charter settlement operations. Off-shore or non-EU digital EMIs without a branch, agent, or passporting arrangement in Luxembourg are institutionally not viable for regulated corporate accounts; they cannot credibly provide the required AML/KYC, SWIFT correspondent relationships, and liquidity assurance for freight forwarding and charter brokerage transactions, and their use may trigger significant regulatory concern at the CSSF and BCL level.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are not permissible for the registered office of an SCSp for purposes of tax substance and RCS registration; a physical registered office in Luxembourg is required. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration, 33 rue de l'Eau, L-1449 Luxembourg; VAT, registration duties and indirect taxes: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT). Customs: Administration des douanes et accises. Central e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Form the SCSp by private or notarial deed with no minimum capital requirement and file the registration dossier with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu; the RCS allocates a national business registration number (matricule). Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, no entity-level corporate income tax or municipal business tax registration is required; each partner reports its allocable share under its own TIN, and a non-resident partner is required to register only if the SCSp constitutes a Luxembourg permanent establishment for that partner. For VAT, submit the prescribed VAT registration application to the AEDT before commencing Luxembourg taxable activity; a non-established foreign entity has no turnover threshold and may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative. The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; the SCSp is tax-transparent by operation of law under Article 50 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) and cannot elect to be treated as an opaque corporation for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax. Election-like mechanisms include contractual allocation of profits and losses among partners, optional registration for VAT by small businesses falling below the exemption threshold, and Luxembourg VAT grouping for qualifying taxable persons established in Luxembourg. Under EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, an SCSp treated as opaque in another jurisdiction is subject to reverse-hybrid and anti-hybrid adjustments; there is no elective reverse-hybrid regime available in Luxembourg.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover, exclusive of VAT, for Luxembourg-established small enterprises; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register from their first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of commercial aircraft rotables, avionics, and surplus parts are eligible for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported from Luxembourg or the EU to a destination outside the EU, provided the supplier or a third party acting on the customer's behalf exports the goods and holds documented proof of export. The export must be supported by an accepted customs export declaration, transport documents evidencing departure from the EU within the statutory evidentiary period, and a commercial invoice. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export sales is deductible in the SCSp's Luxembourg VAT return. Services directly related to the exported goods, and B2B services supplied to non-EU recipients, may also be outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT or zero-rated under applicable place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / MRN) accepted by Luxembourg Customs or another EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence for the export sale
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Aircraft part traceability record / certificate of conformity (where applicable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance API, or digital stamping regime is in force for commercial B2B supplies. Electronic invoicing is permitted in PDF, EDI, or PEPPOL formats provided the authenticity, integrity, and readability of the invoice are preserved. E-invoicing is mandatory for invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector bodies via the national PEPPOL exchange. VAT compliance is self-assessed through periodic VAT returns; intra-EU supplies require recapitulative statements and customer VAT identification.

Zero-rating is strictly scrutinized on documentary proof. If export evidence is missing, incomplete, or false, the AEDT reclassifies the transaction as taxable at the standard Luxembourg VAT rate of 17%, assesses the additional VAT, and charges default interest from the original due date. Knowingly false export documentation constitutes VAT/export fraud and may trigger criminal fines, imprisonment, and joint liability of management. The ordinary reassessment period is three years; where fraud or deliberate evasion is demonstrated, the period is extended to ten years. Input VAT claimed in connection with sham exports may be clawed back through revised VAT assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the national currency. Luxembourg maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange controls or prior authorisation requirements apply to profit remittance, dividend distributions, or movement of trade proceeds. Profit repatriation is unrestricted, but banks require supporting documentation for anti-money laundering (AML) monitoring and maintain statistical reporting obligations under the BCL Balance of Payments Regulation. EUR/USD and other major currency conversions are freely executed at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances and may invoice and settle in any freely convertible currency. Compliance requires accurate declaration of international transactions to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics when individual transactions exceed EUR 12,500, and full adherence to EU sanctions screening and dual-use/civil aviation export documentation (EASA Form 1/FAA 8130-3 trace certificates).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Limited. Luxembourg permits payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorised under the Luxembourg law of 10 November 2009 and the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Offshore digital EMIs without a valid EEA passport and without regulatory recognition by the CSSF cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing infrastructure. These EMIs generally lack aviation-specific settlement rails, commercial letter-of-credit capabilities, and robust sanctions screening for aircraft parts transactions. They may be accepted as secondary channels for immaterial working-capital conversions only, after enhanced due diligence and confirmation that client funds are safeguarded in a regulated credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders or investors are executed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'EQUITY' for partner contributions or 'DIRECT INVESTMENT' (IMF BPM6 code 320). For non-EU parent or foreign shareholder transfers, the beneficiary bank requires a copy of the SCSp partnership agreement, a capital contribution statement, and the transaction reference number. All incoming funds are subject to automatic AML screening; transactions above EUR 10,000 are reported to the CSSF under suspicious transaction reporting thresholds where applicable. Inbound funds from high-risk jurisdictions require pre-authorisation clearing, an economic-substance narrative, and source-of-wealth documentation.Outbound trade disbursements for aviation spares are settled via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'IMPORT OF GOODS' and supplementary reference to the IATA/UNSPSC category for aircraft parts. Commercial Letters of Credit (L/Cs) are routed through the correspondent banking network; issuing and confirming banks collaborate on document examination against shipping, inspection, and EASA/FAA certificates. For escrow-backed settlements, the clearing instruction must be conditional on presentation of title-transfer and airworthiness documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City aggregate: 17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax; rates vary by commune). SCSp is fiscally transparent and carries no entity-level CIT liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; profits distributed by an SCSp to partners are not treated as dividends and are subject to 0% withholding tax. A qualifying corporate partner under the Luxembourg participation exemption (≥10% capital or acquisition cost ≥ €1.2M; 12-month holding test) may obtain 0% withholding in opaque subsidiary structures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual accounts must be filed with the RCS within six months of the SCSp's fiscal year-end. Partner tax returns are due by 31 March following the tax year; electronic filings normally obtain an automatic extension to 30 November/31 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers Master File; mandatory Local File applies when aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 per year and the group revenue threshold is met. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a dedicated Economic Substance Regulation as seen in certain other EU/EMEA jurisdictions, but GAAR, ATAD, and case law impose a functional substance requirement. An SCSp engaged in surplus commercial aircraft parts distribution must demonstrate real presence in Luxembourg: registered office, local managing general partner (or at least a Luxembourg-resident manager), local board/meeting minutes, independent decisions, bank account, accounting, and payroll. Lack of such substance will trigger treaty-shopping denial and recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / intentional misstatement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax plus delayed-payment interest of 0.6% per month; administrative surcharges up to 100% of the tax undercharge; criminal fines up to €25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who act in bad faith are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, penalties, and interest. Luxembourg courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and personal restitution orders as part of the criminal or tax collection process.
Transfer pricing manipulation / thin capitalization

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment and denial of expense deductibility; 10% incremental sanctions on the additional tax; 0.6% monthly interest; potential withdrawal of tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve anti-arm's-length transactions can be personally assessed for the shortfall if the SCSp fails to pay; management disqualification and asset seizures may follow.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of the SCSp as fully taxable in Luxembourg or in the partner's jurisdiction, back-taxes, interest, plus fines under GAAR; possible non-recognition of the corporate veil.

Director personal liability: Managers certifying artificial substance may be liable for fraud-based restitution, issued with travel bans, and subject to imprisonment for falsifying statutory declarations.
AML / sanctions violations in aviation surplus parts sales

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law authorizes fines up to €5 million for legal entities and confiscation of proceeds; regulators may suspend or revoke licenses; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years applies to compliance officers involved in laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp general partner may be criminally prosecuted, personally fined, and subjected to pre-trial travel bans, asset seizure, and disqualification from management in Luxembourg.

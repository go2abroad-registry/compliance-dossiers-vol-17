Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), attached to the Luxembourg District Court.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, particularly Articles 710-1 to 710-27 for Société à Responsabilité Limitée; registration under Articles 18 and 20 of the same law.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation) filed electronically through the RCS portal (lbr.lu) and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, which must be fully subscribed and paid in cash before registration. The amount is blocked in a Luxembourg credit institution escrow account or a notary client escrow account and released to the company only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's special report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the municipal fire-protection service; occupancy permit for the commercial office premises; workplace compliance inspection by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering electrical safety, ventilation, lighting, ergonomics and emergency evacuation; civil-defence emergency plan where the headcount threshold is exceeded.

Paid-in capital rule: SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, which must be fully subscribed and paid in cash before registration. The amount is blocked in a Luxembourg credit institution escrow account or a notary client escrow account and released to the company only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's special report.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — direct corporate income, municipal business, net wealth and payroll taxes (6, rue de la Congrégation, L-2411 Luxembourg); Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — VAT, registration duties and excise duties (6, rue Coudenhove-Kalergi, L-1359 Luxembourg). Electronic portals: Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu), MyGuichet/Guichet.lu and the ACD/AEDT secure taxpayer portal (tax.lu, eSpace professionnel).

E-government filing portal

For a new SARL: (1) notary transcribes the incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); (2) LBR/RCS enrolment automatically allocates the 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which serves as the direct-tax file number; (3) the SARL files the VAT commencement declaration (déclaration de commencement d'activité) with the AEDT via MyGuichet within 15 days of commencement, attaching the RCS extract; (4) the AEDT assigns the LU + 8-digit VAT number (typically 5 to 10 business days); (5) non-resident entities with a Luxembourg fixed establishment file a PE/registration declaration with the ACD and register with the AEDT under the non-established taxpayer procedure, appointing an approved fiscal representative where required. The SARL, as a resident company, must hold a valid matricule before any taxable invoice is issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is per se a resident corporate taxpayer for CIT (17% + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19% federal-equivalent), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, standard ~6.75% in Luxembourg City; aggregate ordinary-income burden ≈ 24.94%) and net wealth tax (0.5% on taxable net wealth). It exercises worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit/exemption and, where the 10%/12-month participation conditions are met, the SOPARFI participation exemption. There is no transparent/pass-through election available to a SARL; the owning shareholders are taxed only on distributions (15% withholding, unless treaty-reduced). — For a new SARL: (1) notary transcribes the incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); (2) LBR/RCS enrolment automatically allocates the 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which serves as the direct-tax file number; (3) the SARL files the VAT commencement declaration (déclaration de commencement d'activité) with the AEDT via MyGuichet within 15 days of commencement, attaching the RCS extract; (4) the AEDT assigns the LU + 8-digit VAT number (typically 5 to 10 business days); (5) non-resident entities with a Luxembourg fixed establishment file a PE/registration declaration with the ACD and register with the AEDT under the non-established taxpayer procedure, appointing an approved fiscal representative where required. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is per se a resident corporate taxpayer for CIT (17% + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19% federal-equivalent), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, standard ~6.75% in Luxembourg City; aggregate ordinary-income burden ≈ 24.94%) and net wealth tax (0.5% on taxable net wealth). It exercises worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit/exemption and, where the 10%/12-month participation conditions are met, the SOPARFI participation exemption. There is no transparent/pass-through election available to a SARL; the owning shareholders are taxed only on distributions (15% withholding, unless treaty-reduced).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box is available. Statutory elections: (1) Article 164bis LIR fiscal unity (unlimited joint consolidated CIT) for ≥95%-held resident subsidiaries, requiring prior ACD application and minimum 5-year period; (2) VAT group election under the 1979 VAT law (single taxable person for intra-group supplies); (3) SOPARFI status (not an election but a qualifying participation exemption using conditions of 10% holding/12-month holding and no non-taxable CIV requirement); (4) option to tax real-property leases for registered-office premises; (5) ATAD I/II hybrid mismatch and interest limitation elections and PE recharacterization under the Law of 20 December 2019; (6) partial deduction/pro-rata VAT election for mixed-use activities. None of these elections alter the entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (no threshold). Any taxable person supplying goods or services in Luxembourg — resident or non-resident — must register with the AEDT before the first taxable transaction. Luxembourg does not apply an incorporable small-enterprise 'franchise en base' exemption; a non-established supplier may, instead of local VAT registration, use the OSS/IOSS mechanism for qualifying cross-border B2C digital services, subject to the conditions of the 2019 VAT e-commerce package.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% (exonération à l'exportation) applies when the exporter proves physical dispatch to a third-country destination within 90 days of supply and holds the customs export declaration (SAD/MRN). Services: corporate-secretarial and registered-agent services supplied B2B follow the customer's place of business (Article 58 of the VAT law): non-EU taxable customers place the service outside Luxembourg's territorial scope (effective 0%); intra-EU taxable customers allow a zero-rated invoice provided the customer's VAT ID is validated, the service is not caught by domestic use-and-enjoyment rules, and the EC Sales List is filed. Supplies to private (B2C) non-EU clients are taxable in Luxembourg unless the effective-use-and-enjoyment exception applies; digital services to non-EU consumers can use OSS/IOSS. Full economic substance documentation must evidence that the secretarial fees are genuine and not a shell/offshore-conduit recharacterization target.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD — Single Administrative Document) with assigned MRN evidencing EU exit, filed via Luxembourg Customs or the other EU exit customs office
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note proving physical dispatch of goods from EU customs territory to a third country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 arrival confirmation showing export proceeds credited to the SARL's Luxembourg bank account within the FX repatriation deadline
  • Commercial contract, purchase order and final export invoice cross-referenced to the declaration
  • Audit file reconciling each 0% invoice to customs exit proof, transport documents and payment rails

No real-time fiscal clearance, transactional reporting or digital stamping applies to B2B invoicing in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to Luxembourg public administrations and public undertakings (e-Factura via PEPPOL network, PEPPOL BIS 3.0/UBL formats; public procurement); B2B e-invoicing is optional with recipient consent. Tax records must be kept 10 years in conformity with the VAT law; upon request, the AEDT may demand SAF-T type audit files. Intrastat/EC Sales List (Liste récapitulative) and periodic TVA returns are filed electronically through the tax.lu portal.

AEDT may reopen files for 5 years (10 years on proven fraud). Unsubstantiated or fraudulent 0% claims are clawed back as Luxembourg VAT at 17% plus interest of 0.6% per month, and the taxpayer is exposed to an administrative fine of 10%-100% of the evaded tax. Recharacterization of artificial 'registered-agent/secretarial' conduits can generate withholding tax, net-wealth tax and ATAD consequences; forged export documents expose directors to criminal penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment. Export proof must be contemporaneous; incomplete files at the audit notice are automatically disallowed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member state; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The EU Treaty guarantees free movement of capital; no general exchange control regime applies. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and, for certain outbound dividends, potential withholding tax relief under EU Directives, the Luxembourg-Netherlands tax treaty network, or the participation exemption regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement and no foreign exchange repatriation or conversion obligation on receivables in any currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg, BCL)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. A non-EU digital electronic money institution (EMI) cannot operate in Luxembourg without obtaining a licence from the CSSF or passporting a valid EEA EMI licence under PSD2. Such EMIs are generally accepted as ancillary banking channels but face additional AML/KYC equal-to-bank requirements and may be viewed as a higher compliance risk for a registered-agent/secretarial service entity holding third-party funds. Onshore licensed EMIs (e.g., licensed by CSSF or passported) are preferred for clients, while operational clearing activity remains best routed through tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT wire (typically MT103) with accurate purpose codes under BCL/ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting. For a Luxembourg SARL, shareholder loans or equity injections should reference purpose codes such as 'S0022' (direct investment – equity capital) or appropriate EBOPS classification, and the bank instruction must reference the registered office and RCS number. The receiving bank must verify the mandate, the underlying BCL declaration obligation, and the corporate authorisation (resolution of the general meeting).Outbound settlements for trade or service obligations are effected primarily through SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages in EUR or convertible currencies, subject to normal anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate income tax rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, and 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg withholding tax on shareholder dividends; 0% for qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or domestic participation exemption when the shareholder holds at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period (or is a qualifying EU parent), subject to beneficial-ownership and substance requirements

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per fiscal year; Master File required when the group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report threshold is the OECD/EU EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations ordinance, but the domestic anti-abuse provision in Article 56 LIR, EU ATAD interest-limitation and controlled-foreign-corporation rules, and OECD/HJSO substance concepts require the SARL to maintain real presence, personnel, offices, independent decision-making, and profit-bearing activity; professional corporate secretarial and registered-agent service providers must demonstrate commercial substance to secure treaty benefits, participation exemption, and the Interest and Royalties Directive

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all hidden income, penalty up to twice the evaded tax, criminal fines up to EUR 250,000, and imprisonment up to five years for responsible natural persons

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally and jointly liable for the corporate tax liability and penalties when they knowingly sign false returns or participate in a shell invoicing scheme; criminal proceedings can impose director imprisonment, freezing orders, and travel bans
Artificial Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and directive relief, disallowance of the participation exemption, recharacterisation under Article 56 LIR, additional taxation at the 24.94% rate, plus civil penalties of 10% or more on underpaid corporate income tax

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties when they authorise the artificial structure; the corporate veil may be pierced if the entity is a mere facade, leading to personal attachment and restrictive measures
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines for legal persons up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; regulatory suspension or withdrawal of the professional licence; publication of the sanction

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years for wilful AML breaches; civil liability for damages to investors or the Luxembourg State attaches where fraudulent facilitation caused a loss
Persistent Late Filing and Obstruction of Tax Control

Statutory sanction: Default assessment, late-filing penalty up to 10% of tax, monthly late-payment interest at 0.8%, coercive fines by the liquidation committee, and forced recovery against assets

Director personal liability: Directors may be stripped of the benefit of limited liability where the failure to file or pay amounts to intentional fraudulent non-compliance; Luxembourg courts can impose director liability for the entire unpaid corporate tax, travel bans, and up to two years of imprisonment for defiance of fiscal authorities

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), administered by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) at the Cité Judiciaire under the control of the Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (1915 Law), Articles 101 et seq. (sociétés en commandite) as modified by the Law of 11 February 2013 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); subsidiary partnership rules of the Civil Code (Articles 1833 et seq.); registration and e-filing regime set by the Law of 15 November 2021 and the Grand-Ducal regulations on the RCS and RESA.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial or private partnership agreement (contrat de société) executed for an SCSp, together with the appointment deed of the manager (gérant), filed electronically through the LBR e-Platform and published as an extract in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA); legal entity effectiveness against third parties runs from RESA publication.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 65 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal share capital applies to an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines the capital amount, contribution schedule and withdrawal rules. No statutory escrow or blocker account is required, but the general partner must demonstrate, when filing with the RCS, that the agreed initial capital is adequately funded; trade flows are subsequently settled through a commercial bank account opened post-registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and rescue safety approval from the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) for any premises storing or handling combustible granular sulfur, including fire-exit planning, extinguishing systems, and emergency lighting; structural load and static certification for storage racks, silos, or transshipment framing by an approved civil engineer; workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Labour Code (Code du travail) covering ventilation, dust extraction, explosion protection (ATEX zoning), and personal protective equipment; the workplace registers must be completed before the first staffing date.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum legal share capital applies to an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines the capital amount, contribution schedule and withdrawal rules. No statutory escrow or blocker account is required, but the general partner must demonstrate, when filing with the RCS, that the agreed initial capital is adequately funded; trade flows are subsequently settled through a commercial bank account opened post-registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (Luxembourg Inland Revenue) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); public portal MyGuichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) e-portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains a Luxembourg 15-digit national identification / business registration number by filing for incorporation or registration with the RCS through a notary or an approved incorporation agent. The SCSp, despite being fiscally transparent, is registered as an entity in the RCS. Once registered, the tax identification number is communicated by the Administration des contributions directes. Where VAT registration is required, the entity or its VAT representative files Form 640 with the AEDT; non-established EU suppliers may register directly, while third-country suppliers may be required to appoint a Luxembourg-approved VAT representative. The VAT identification number is then issued in the format LU + 8 digits.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity obtains a Luxembourg 15-digit national identification / business registration number by filing for incorporation or registration with the RCS through a notary or an approved incorporation agent. The SCSp, despite being fiscally transparent, is registered as an entity in the RCS. Once registered, the tax identification number is communicated by the Administration des contributions directes. Where VAT registration is required, the entity or its VAT representative files Form 640 with the AEDT; non-established EU suppliers may register directly, while third-country suppliers may be required to appoint a Luxembourg-approved VAT representative. The VAT identification number is then issued in the format LU + 8 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax purposes by default; no separate entity-level corporate income tax election exists under Luxembourg law. Partnerships may elect to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD and EU directives override treaty or jurisdictional classification outcomes; a US check-the-box election may be used for US federal tax classification, but has no direct Luxembourg VAT or income tax effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the small-business exemption under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; crossing this economic threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration for supplies of goods or services not covered by an exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically dispatched or transported by the seller or the buyer from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT under the Luxembourg VAT Law (Article 64). To claim zero-rating for offshore services, the services must be supplied to a taxable person established outside the EU, or the effective use and enjoyment must be outside the EU. The supplier must hold complete documentary evidence of dispatch and export within three months of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD accepted by Luxembourg Customs and the AEDT)
  • Commercial invoice with complete export terms and buyer/seller details
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (e.g., CMR, Air Waybill)
  • Bank payment confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of payment from the non-EU counterparty

Luxembourg does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping. E-invoicing is permitted and must guarantee the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility of the invoice. No mandatory Peppol clearance, API reporting, or government real-time approval is required, but taxpayers must retain invoicing and export evidence in a form acceptable to the AEDT.

The AEDT may conduct retrospective audits and disallow zero-rating relief if documentation is incomplete or false. Clawback of the VAT refund or exemption is accompanied by late interest at the statutory rate (approximately 0.6% per month/7.2% per annum), administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 per violation, and, in cases of intentional fraud, criminal penalties including fines of up to EUR 25,000 and/or imprisonment. The tax authorities may also challenge abusive transactions under GAAR and impose joint liability on parties to sham export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions is guaranteed under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no exchange-control permits, central-bank prior approvals, or profit-remittance hurdles for a Luxembourg SCSp settling dividends, capital distributions, or trade balances to non-resident partners. USD-dominated sulfur and dry-bulk trade settlements are freely executed via commercial banks; convertibility risk is market-driven only, and any invoicing in CNY, INR, or emerging-market currencies is handled through deliverable or NDF markets. Legal constraints are limited to EU/UN sanctions screening, CSSF AML/CFT due-diligence rules under the Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended), and BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes zero export-proceeds surrender and zero mandatory conversion ratios. A SCSp engaged in granular elemental sulfur export may retain 100% of USD or other foreign-currency receipts in its own accounts without any statutory conversion timeline. The only regulatory obligation is BCL external-accounts reporting for balance-of-payments statistics, filed through the resident credit institution, together with standard CRS/FATCA classification and CSSF transaction-reporting duties.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and AML/CFT supervision; the Ministry of Finance oversees financial-sector legislation. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration; the euro is the sovereign currency of the Grand Duchy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. CSSF-authorised e-money institutions and payment institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (PSD2) provide multi-currency IBAN accounts, expedited FX execution, and API treasury interfaces, but they may not issue or confirm commercial Letters of Credit, disburse SWIFT documentary trade finance, or offer NDF/swap hedging instruments. For the SCSp target payment rails of SWIFT LC and commercial LC, a fully licensed credit institution remains legally necessary. EMIs are acceptable only as auxiliary receivables-collection channels for standardised buyer payments and must be ring-fenced from the main commodity settlement account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

SCSp UBO opacity: partnership law allows silent limited partners whose identities are shielded, and the bank cannot unequivocally map all natural-person beneficiaries.

Defense evidence: Current RCSL trade-register excerpt, executed partnership agreement and side letters, UBO declaration under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 1 December 2018, group organisational chart, certified passport pages of the general partner directors, business-plan and source-of-funds questionnaire, plus a CSSF AML/KYC cover letter certifying the non-opaque beneficial-ownership structure.
Commodity and dual-use chemical exposure: granular elemental sulfur classified under ISIC 4669 may be perceived as a sulfuric-acid precursor, triggering heightened end-use scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound partner capital and shareholder loans must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (EUR-denominated within SEPA, or USD/EUR SWIFT for non-SEPA jurisdictions) with full ordering and beneficiary identification satisfying FATF Recommendation 16. Purpose codes shall designate the transfer as balance-of-payments 'Direct investment—equity capital injection (A3A)' or 'Capital transfer (A6)' under IMF BPM6; where required by the receiving Luxembourg bank, the payment narrative field must read 'CAP INJ—SCSP SULFUR TRADE' and be supported by an executed capital contribution agreement, a notarised partner resolution, and a signed KYC declaration. Banks must validate each injection journey through a pre-established SWIFT Relationship Management Application (RMA) key and preserve the complete audit trail for CSSF inspection.Outbound clearing rails comprise: (1) SWIFT MT103 open-account wire transfers for spot cargo and freight settlements; (2) commercial documentary Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP 600, with MT707 amendments if consignment or incoterms change; (3) standby Letters of Credit remitted as MT760 for pre-export finance, advance-payment guarantees, or supplier security; and (4) SEPA credit transfers and direct debits for EUR port fees, demurrage, charter hire, and agency disbursements. For large bulk-cargo transactions, the issuing bank may use an MT740 reimbursement authorisation and correspondent value-dating arrangements to guarantee presentation-against-payment and advise the negotiating bank on the same banking day.Deliverable outright FX forwards (including window and flexible-dated forwards) executed with a Luxembourg credit institution or MiFID II investment firm under ISDA 2002 or EMTA/IFEMA master agreements, used purely to hedge USD/EUR trade receivables and payables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or non-convertible settlement currencies, fixing to a central-parity rate and settled in EUR/USD reference cash flows.Cross-currency swaps (par-to-par liability conversion and mark-to-market swaps) to convert USD sulfur-trade receivables into EUR liquidity while aligning the SCSp capital structure, subject to EMIR clearing or risk-mitigation requirements.European-style vanilla FX options (call or put, USD/EUR and EUR/CNY) struck within a permitted bandwidth of central parity for premium-capped hedging of unexpected freight-rate or commodity-price linked currency volatility.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective CIT rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax); statutory CIT alone is 17%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for profit distributions from a fiscally transparent SCSp; 15% statutory dividend withholding tax applies only if the SCSp has opted to be treated as a capital company subject to Luxembourg CIT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is 31 December of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; e.g., FY2024 return due 31 December 2025 unless an extension is formally granted by the Luxembourg tax administration (AED).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million; Local File mandatory where related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 for intra-group services or EUR 200,000 for other transactions; no threshold applies if the transaction carries significant compliance risk.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to the Gulf jurisdictions; however, the SCSp must maintain substantive economic substance in Luxembourg through local management, office premises, operational decision-making and adequate employees/records to avoid classification as a shell company under EU ATAD, the Luxembourg GAAR and OECD BEPS rules, which can trigger denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of the entity as taxable, and assessment of undeclared income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment under LIR and Luxembourg Code of Tax Procedure; additional tax of the amount evaded, late payment interest of 0.8% per month, and a surcharge of 10% on the understated tax.

Director personal liability: General partners (or directors of a corporate general partner) are jointly and severally liable for tax debts; personal civil restitution for the evaded tax, travel bans pending investigation, and imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated fraud.
Shell company / substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax treaty benefits; re-characterisation as a fully taxable Luxembourg resident corporation subject to 24.94% aggregate CIT, net wealth tax and municipal business tax owed retroactively; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: general partners are personally liable for all corporate tax liabilities and civil fines; personal bank accounts frozen; directors of the corporate general partner may face travel restrictions and disqualification from future directorships.
AML / beneficial ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1,500,000 under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing (updated); criminal penalties including imprisonment for money laundering; seizure of proceeds held in Luxembourg bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are criminally liable; personal civil liability for damages; travel bans and international arrest warrants; bank directors can be sanctioned personally for processing funds from an unregistered SCSp.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: AED primary adjustment based on OECD-compliant comparables; secondary adjustment recharacterising excess profits as deemed dividends; penalty of up to 10% of the adjusted positive income difference; interest at 0.8% per month accruing from the original filing due date.

Director personal liability: General partners personally liable for the tax deficiency and penalties where they knowingly signed false TP documentation; criminal indictment for tax fraud; personal bank account seizure and travel ban; EU cross-border asset freezing orders.

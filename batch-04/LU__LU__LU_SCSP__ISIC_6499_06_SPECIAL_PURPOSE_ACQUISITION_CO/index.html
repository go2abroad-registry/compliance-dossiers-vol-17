Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum capital or paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp (Art. 944-2 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended); partners freely determine subscription instalments in the partnership agreement. For a SPAC, the offering proceeds must be segregated in a trust/escrow account held with a Luxembourg credit institution until a Business Combination, liquidation or redemption event; no statutory capital escrow deposit is otherwise required.

Funding & unlock path: CGDIS fire-safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) confirming means of egress, smoke detection and fire extinguishers; structural load / building-occupancy attestation for the leased registered office; occupational-safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM); and, where staff are employed, registration with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS).

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital or paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp (Art. 944-2 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended); partners freely determine subscription instalments in the partnership agreement. For a SPAC, the offering proceeds must be segregated in a trust/escrow account held with a Luxembourg credit institution until a Business Combination, liquidation or redemption event; no statutory capital escrow deposit is otherwise required.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes. Central business registration is conducted via the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) with electronic filing through MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SCSp must first appoint a Luxembourg-licensed authorized representative/agent. The constitutive dossier is filed with the RCS, and upon incorporation the entity receives a commercial registration number (matricule) which functions as the national tax identification number (TIN). If taxable activities for VAT purposes are present, a separate VAT identification number is requested from the AEDT using Form 511, accompanied by identity documentation, proof of RCS registration, and details of the appointed VAT representative when the entity is non-resident. For direct tax purposes, the SCSp must file a registration declaration (Formulaire 64) with the ACD unless wholly tax-transparent and not subject to Luxembourg taxation.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing an SCSp must first appoint a Luxembourg-licensed authorized representative/agent. The constitutive dossier is filed with the RCS, and upon incorporation the entity receives a commercial registration number (matricule) which functions as the national tax identification number (TIN). If taxable activities for VAT purposes are present, a separate VAT identification number is requested from the AEDT using Form 511, accompanied by identity documentation, proof of RCS registration, and details of the appointed VAT representative when the entity is non-resident. For direct tax purposes, the SCSp must file a registration declaration (Formulaire 64) with the ACD unless wholly tax-transparent and not subject to Luxembourg taxation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, but may irrevocably elect to be treated as a taxable corporate entity under Article 175bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) through a formal declaration to the ACD. Additionally, hybrid mismatch elections and alignment statements under EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) rules can be filed to coordinate the entity's treatment with foreign jurisdictions where investors are established, preventing double taxation or double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg. A simplified small-business franchise regime is available for taxable annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, but this does not exempt the entity from registration if it engages in significant intra-EU acquisitions or imports subject to reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the seller or on his behalf to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods are physically exported, a valid export declaration is accepted by the Luxembourg customs authorities, and documentary proof of export is retained. Services physically realized abroad and services directly related to import/export operations may also be zero-rated. An SCSp typically engages in exempt financial services and share-dealing activities, which are outside the scope of VAT rather than zero-rated; any related export servicing must satisfy the same evidence requirements to claim 0%.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of dispatch and leaving the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (where applicable)

Luxembourg does not currently impose mandatory real-time e-invoicing or digital fiscal clearance. Electronic invoicing is accepted under the same conditions as paper invoices. B2B e-invoicing is optional, but recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) must be filed electronically for intra-Community supplies. The tax administration is preparing for the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework; no digital stamping or API clearance system is operational to date.

Fraudulent claims of export zero-rating or misrepresentation of export documents are subject to a 50% fine of the evaded VAT under Article 62 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, plus late-payment interest at 0.8% per month. In egregious cases, criminal sanctions may include imprisonment. Input VAT recoverable on exports is clawed back retroactively if the required export proofs are not produced within the prescribed statutory period (10 years), with interest running from the original claim date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies a fully liberalised capital and current-account regime pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). The euro is unconditionally convertible; no exchange-control approval, hard-currency surrender rule, or central-bank licensing is required for SPAC capital contributions, IPO escrow deposits, acquisition disbursements, or partner distributions. The principal remittance hurdles are indirect: CSSF AML/CFT due diligence under the amended Law of 12 November 2004, sanction screening under EU Regulation 269/2014 and national freezing measures, CRS/FATCA reporting, and UBO registration under the Luxembourg RBE law. A tax-transparent SCSp operates with no entity-level withholding on interest or dividends; partners are taxed on their allocated income, and profit repatriation to non-resident partners is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts, foreign currency revenues, or inbound investment proceeds. A Luxembourg SCSp may maintain multi-currency accounts (USD, EUR, GBP, CHF) without restriction. The only applicable obligation is physical cash transport declaration for amounts exceeding EUR 10,000 under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672, administered by Luxembourg customs, and prudent treasury reporting to the BCL for statistical Purposes when cross-border payments are made.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank subsidiary of the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises Eurozone monetary policy authority. Financial-sector supervision is managed by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which also enforces cash-control declarations and AML/CFT obligations. No separate Luxembourg foreign-exchange administration exists; capital-flow statistics and FX data are reported to BCL for balance-of-payments purposes only.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium for operating expenses; unacceptable for IPO equity or redemption escrow custody. Luxembourg-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and EEA passporting EMIs supervised by the CSSF can open multi-currency current accounts with SEPA and SWIFT MT103 issuance, but they may not hold trust-grade escrow client funds within the meaning of the Luxembourg law of 18 December 2009 on payment services, nor can they provide the custodian/trustee-grade chain required by a SPAC listing prospectus. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs are legally prohibited from providing treasury services to a Luxembourg-domiciled SCSp without an EU banking licence or EMI passport; any attempt to route subscription proceeds through such platforms will trigger automatic AML rejection and potential CSSF enforcement.

All wires must be accompanied by a board-resolution extract and, where required by the receiving bank, a scanned capital contribution schedule signed by the general partner. Inbound payments should not be split; any single wire above EUR/USD 50,000 triggers automatic enhanced due-diligence review. Turnover: A Luxembourg SCSp is subject to mandatory independent statutory audit if it constitutes an undertaking within the meaning of the 2016 Luxembourg audit law and exceeds two of three thresholds for two consecutive financial years: balance sheet total exceeding EUR 20,000,000, net turnover exceeding EUR 40,000,000, or average payroll exceeding 250 employees. A public-interest entity status, such as a SPAC listed or pre-listed on a Luxembourg or EU regulated market, triggers compulsory audit irrespective of size. Certain regulated activities, including alternative investment fund management or custody arrangements, may impose audit requirements based on the applicable sectoral law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD / Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and withholding taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and stamp duties. Primary digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (Professionnels / eSpace professionnel), the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/eJustice) platform, and the AEDT e-TVA portal.

E-government filing portal

For an LU SCSp (Special LP): (1) execute the limited partnership agreement before a Luxembourg notary and file the instrument for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) using the eJustice platform; (2) upon RCS registration, the entity obtains its unique Luxembourg business identification number (numéro d'identification) and RCS registration reference (format B + 6 or more digits); (3) for VAT, the SCSp, as an economic operator, must file a domestic VAT registration form (formulaire TVA 800/801) with the AEDT, which assigns a Luxembourg VAT number in the standard format LU + 8 digits; a foreign entity not established in Luxembourg has a mandatory registration obligation without turnover threshold when it performs taxable transactions in Luxembourg; (4) if the SCSp needs to declare Luxembourg-source income, claim tax treaty benefits on behalf of partners, or satisfy local withholding obligations, the ACD opens a tax file and designates the competent bureau d'imposition of the registered seat; (5) a regulated alternative investment vehicle (RAIF/SIF/SICAR) shall submit the notification to the CSSF, which does not replace AEDT/ACD registration for fiscal purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For an LU SCSp (Special LP): (1) execute the limited partnership agreement before a Luxembourg notary and file the instrument for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) using the eJustice platform; (2) upon RCS registration, the entity obtains its unique Luxembourg business identification number (numéro d'identification) and RCS registration reference (format B + 6 or more digits); (3) for VAT, the SCSp, as an economic operator, must file a domestic VAT registration form (formulaire TVA 800/801) with the AEDT, which assigns a Luxembourg VAT number in the standard format LU + 8 digits; a foreign entity not established in Luxembourg has a mandatory registration obligation without turnover threshold when it performs taxable transactions in Luxembourg; (4) if the SCSp needs to declare Luxembourg-source income, claim tax treaty benefits on behalf of partners, or satisfy local withholding obligations, the ACD opens a tax file and designates the competent bureau d'imposition of the registered seat; (5) a regulated alternative investment vehicle (RAIF/SIF/SICAR) shall submit the notification to the CSSF, which does not replace AEDT/ACD registration for fiscal purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg. The fiscal transparency of the SCSp is statutorily fixed by Article 5bis of the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (as amended); the transparency regime applies mandatorily for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax, and no election to be taxed at entity level is available under domestic law. Alternative pass-through structuring may be achieved through a civil co-ownership (indivision) or a dormant partnership (société en participation). Hybrid mismatch arrangements are governed by the ATAD transposition (Law of 21 December 2018, as amended), applying the deduction/no-inclusion, dual inclusion income, importation and branch mismatch rules to cross-border equity and finance structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover, exclusive of VAT, for small and medium undertakings established in Luxembourg; the threshold does not apply to non-established foreign businesses, which must register for VAT before carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg; voluntary registration is available below the threshold but is impracticable for an exempt financial private-equity SPV because input VAT recovery on exempt financial services is denied.

No mandatory B2B e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or transaction-based VAT reporting is currently implemented in Luxembourg. Since 18 April 2023, invoices addressed to the Luxembourg central government and public-sector bodies must be issued as structured PEPPOL e-invoices (EN 16931). For B2B transactions, e-invoicing is permitted by mutual acceptance of the parties; no AEDT pre-clearance or certification of invoices is required. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative may introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting after 2030; Luxembourg has as of the date of this dossier no enacted law imposing such a regime.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls on capital account or current account transactions. Profit remittance by an SCSp is limited only by partnership law and the Limited Partnership Agreement distribution waterfall; there is no profit remittance approval or conversion permit. Practical hurdles are limited to AML/KYC evidence, tax transparency, and substance documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion percentage, and no statutory repatriation deadline. Export receipts and other cross-border receivables may be held in foreign currency, but resident banks must report cross-border flows to the BCL for monetary and balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: La Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); administrative supervision of treasury, banking, and FX compliance also rests with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-low viability. An EU-authorised electronic money institution can provide ancillary euro accounts and payment initiation, but cannot safely support acquisition escrow, syndicated loan drawdowns, or material FX settlements. Offshore or non-EU digital EMIs are legally unusable because they lack the required Luxembourg/EEA payment institution passport and access to TARGET2; such accounts would create regulatory and bank product governance exposure. The SCSp must use a CSSF-supervised credit institution for its primary treasury rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% composite headline rate in Luxembourg City: national CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 7% on CIT (1.19%) + municipal business tax 6.75%. The SCSp is fiscally transparent and therefore bears no entity-level CIT on ordinary results.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions by an SCSp: as a fiscally transparent vehicle lacking legal personality, profit distributions are not dividends and no Luxembourg withholding tax is levied. For comparison, a Luxembourg corporation would attract the statutory 15% dividend WHT (Art. 146 LIR), reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or most DTAs where a 10% plus participation is held for 12 months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg prescribes no fixed statutory Local File or Master File monetary threshold; the arm's length standard under Art. 56 LIR and Circular 56/1 imposes contemporaneous documentation on all intra-group transactions. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Prudent SPV practice: maintain a Local File when intercompany financing or management fees exceed EUR 1 million and a Master File when intra-group revenue exceeds EUR 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation statute; substance is enforced through the ATAD-derived GAAR (Art. 6 ACI), Art. 56 LIR, anti-hybrid rules, and the ACD's anti-abuse ruling practice. The SCSp must demonstrate genuine business substance: physical office, board or general partner meetings held in Luxembourg, local decision-making, Luxembourg bank accounts, complete accounting records, and proportionate staffing; failure to prove substance may result in denial of pass-through treatment, treaty access, and the 0% distribution characterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Art. 1754 Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO): imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000; civil reassessment of tax evaded plus a 10% evasion surcharge and default interest at 0.6% per month; loss of payment grace periods and immediate enforceability of tax claims.

Director personal liability: The general partner, and the directors of a corporate general partner, are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, joint unlimited civil liability for partnership debts under the Law of 10 August 1915, travel bans and exit interdictions in tax enforcement proceedings, and disqualification from holding management positions.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under Art. 6 ACI with denial of fiscal transparency; reclassification of distributions as hidden dividends subject to 15% Luxembourg WHT; upward assessment at the 24.94% composite rate; 10% late-payment penalty and 0.6% monthly default interest; denial of DTA benefits; possible requalification of the SCSp as a taxable resident entity.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be jointly assessed for all unpaid taxes if the arrangement is held to constitute an abuse of law; criminal liability for aiding and abetting tax evasion; seizure of personal assets and enforceable travel bans during recovery.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Register Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under the RBE law: fines from EUR 500 to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 3 months per offence; additional fines under the AML Law of 12 November 2004 up to EUR 5,000,000 for regulated professionals; freezing of distributions and suspension of bank account operations.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are individually liable for UBO compliance; convictions produce personal fines, imprisonment, and management disqualification; banks may terminate the SPV's accounts, causing payment defaults and triggering wrongful-trading claims against the directors.
Transfer Pricing / Debt Push-Down Abuse

Statutory sanction: Adjustment under Art. 56 LIR and Circular 56/1: recharacterization of excess interest or fees as hidden profit distributions; disallowance of the deduction; additional tax at 24.94% plus 10% penalty and 0.6% monthly default interest; aggravated penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax in cases of gross negligence; referral of the adjustment to the partner's home tax authority under exchange of information.

Director personal liability: General partner directors may incur personal liability for wrongful trading (action en complement du passif) if the adjustment renders the SCSp unable to pay its creditors; criminal sanctions for participation in tax evasion; travel restrictions and personal asset attachment in the enforcement phase.
Non-Filing / Persistent Default

Statutory sanction: Art. 155 LIR surcharge of 10% of assessed tax; coercive fines up to EUR 2,500 per default; default interest at 0.6% per month; aggravated administrative penalty of up to 100% under Art. 1757 AO in cases of repeated willful default.

Director personal liability: Civil liability of the general partner for the full tax claim; directors may face personal attachment orders and bank seizure of their private assets; arrest and travel bans may be ordered; professional disqualification follows a criminal conviction.

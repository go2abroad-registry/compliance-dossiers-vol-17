Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés de Luxembourg (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), filing exclusively via the gui'lettre e-filing portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, in particular Articles 1-7 and Articles 198-213 (Société en Commandite Spéciale introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013), read with the Law of 19 December 2003 on the Register of Commerce and Companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation (Form A) submitted together with the executed limited partnership agreement (convention de société) on the gui'lettre portal, followed by publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 53 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

SCSp has no statutory minimum subscribed capital; capital is freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement and may be paid in cash or in kind upon execution. No escrow account is required by the Law of 10 August 1915; voluntary escrow may be agreed between partners pending the GDP licensing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance (avis de sécurité) from the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) covering sprinkler protection, emergency exits and hazard zoning; structural load inspection certificates for racking, cold rooms and mezzanine floors; occupational safety and health approval from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering CE-marked material handling equipment, thermal stress controls for cold-chain staff, and a site-specific emergency plan.

No escrow account is required by the Law of 10 August 1915; voluntary escrow may be agreed between partners pending the GDP licensing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is a fiscally transparent partnership without legal personality; no entity-level corporate income tax (CIT) or municipal business tax (MBT) is imposed and taxable items flow through to the partners. Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; however, the transparent SCSp may be recharacterized as opaque under the ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (Law of 26 July 2019) where a mismatch produces a deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcome. For VAT, the SCSp may register as a taxable person where it performs an independent economic activity, and the general partner acts as statutory representative. The SCSp may elect quarterly or annual VAT return filing based on prior-year turnover and may apply to the AEDT for a cash-accounting scheme or for fixed-establishment treatment where a local establishment exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The Article 57 VAT-Law small-business franchise exemption applies at EUR 35,000 annual turnover for goods and EUR 25,000 for service activities. International freight forwarding, transport arrangement and GDP logistics (ISIC 5229) supplied on a B2B basis fall under the general place-of-supply rule (customer establishment) with reverse charge, and no domestic VAT registration threshold applies to a non-resident supplier of such cross-border services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) supplies of goods physically exported outside the EU (Article 155 of the VAT Law) and (ii) transport services directly related to such exported goods, provided the export is effected within 90 days of supply (extendable to 150 days upon justified AEDT authorization) and evidenced by prescribed documentation. For B2B freight-forwarding and customs-brokerage services linked to pharma imports/exports, the reverse-charge mechanism applies within the EU; services relating to non-EU transportation are zero-rated or outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT. Zero-rating requires the supplier to hold positive legal evidence of physical exit from EU customs territory, including the electronic customs export declaration, the bill of lading or CMR/air waybill, counterparty acceptance, and, for GDP pharma cargo, the unbroken cold-chain/temperature-excursion log and reconciliation with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document, electronic customs declaration and, where applicable, Export Accompanying Document/EAD evidencing physical exit from EU territory)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or CMR / IATA Air Waybill and aircraft or vessel manifest (air/road)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and freight agreement containing the GDP pharma reference and Incoterms 2020 allocation
  • Signed proof of delivery (POD) / consignee certificate of receipt together with the GDP temperature excursion log and chain-of-custody records

Luxembourg does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting or digital stamping for B2B e-invoicing. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies since March 2021 in the PEPPOL Peppol BIS 3.0 format. B2B pharma-freight invoices may be issued electronically (EDI/UBL/PDF) subject to the authenticity, integrity and legibility requirements of Articles 58-60 of the VAT Law, with a 10-year retention obligation; no QR-code or cross-border clearance transmission is required.

Luxembourg statutory law does not impose a monetary FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects the customary AEDT documentary-verification horizon and EU export-proof standards. Where export zero-rating is claimed without the required customs and transport evidence, the AEDT reclassifies the supply as taxable at 17%, assesses under-declared VAT, and applies late-payment interest of 0.8% per month (Article 66 of the VAT Law); deduction rights may also be restricted or excluded. Knowingly false declarations or simulated physical export expose the taxable person to criminal sanctions under Article 78 of the VAT Law (imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and/or a criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000), administrative fines of up to EUR 4,000 per infraction, retrospective clawback of VAT and interest, and publication of the sanction; AEDT criminal-referral practice is consistent for sham pharma-freight export schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the euro area and maintains no foreign-exchange controls. Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible and transferable under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Luxembourg franc was fully replaced by the euro and no convertibility restriction applies to profit remittances, capital repatriation, or current trade payments. For an SCSp, distributions to non-resident partners are generally permitted, but banks must verify the partnership agreement, beneficial ownership registers, and applicable EU/UN sanctions and anti-money-laundering compliance. A profit-remittance transaction is not subject to the CSSF or BCL approval, but must be documented to satisfy the counterparty bank's internal controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation deadline for export or freight receipts. Export invoices may be paid in any foreign currency and may be retained in offshore or onshore accounts at the commercial discretion of the SCSp, subject only to EU/UN sanctions and AML/KYC obligations. Where the business generates freight revenue in USD, conversion to EUR is at the prevailing market rate via a licensed Luxembourg bank or passported EEA financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), functioning within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and subject to EU Treaties on capital movements and payments

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Limited. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution may passport into Luxembourg under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and may provide ancillary cross-border payment accounts. However, offshore non-EEA digital EMIs are not legally permitted to provide payment services in Luxembourg without a licence or passport. For an SCSp involved in GDP pharmaceutical freight, the primary treasury, capital escrow, and FX settlement accounts must be held with CSSF-regulated credit institutions or authorised EEA branches. A regulated EEA EMI may be used only for operational liquidity in euros where low-value supplier disbursements are made and no import/export financing is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investment capital for the SCSp must enter via an international wire transfer using SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the Luxembourg escrow or operating account. The payment message should include an ISO 20022 or treasury purpose classification consistent with a direct capital contribution, typically structured as a general remittance or equity capital movement under the EU Capital Movements Directive, and the remittance information field must identify the SCSp name, the capital call number, and the contributing partner. The receiving bank will require the capital call statement, contribution agreement, and partner register extract to classify the wire as equity rather than taxable debt or services income. If the inflow originates from a non-EU jurisdiction, additional economic sanctions clearance and a legal opinion on EU capital-movement compliance may be required.EUR-denominated outbound payments to freight forwarders, warehouses, customs brokers, and insurance providers are cleared through SEPA Credit Transfer or instant SEPA Credit Transfer with structured remittance containing invoice number and purchase order reference. For high-value pharmaceutical freight or controlled-temperature equipment procurement, an irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit may be used to ensure title and logistics documents are exchanged against payment, particularly when the supplier demands assurance of payment before dispatch. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level (fiscally transparent hybrid partnership); 24.94% combined Luxembourg City headline rate for FY2024 if the SCSp exercises the corporate tax opting-in election (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity employment fund surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax on Luxembourg City multiplier 2.25).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for a transparent SCSp because profit allocations to partners are not legally 'dividends' and no Luxembourg withholding obligation arises at the SCSp level. If the SCSp has opted into CIT, the statutory dividend WHT is 15% (Art. 149 LIR), reduced to 0% under the participation exemption (holding >=10% or acquisition price >=EUR 1.2m held >=12 months) and under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (subject to the 2024 substance and anti-abuse verification).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Must be filed by 31 December of the calendar year immediately following the fiscal year-end (Art. 162 LIR); for a 31 December 2024 year-end, statutory due date is 31 December 2025. Additional grace periods are administrative-only and require a justified written request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 450,000 in annual cross-border transactions with associated enterprises triggers the mandatory Local File requirement (Circular LIR n°56/1 of 28 January 2022, derived from EU TPD). A Master File is required where the worldwide group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50 million. Transactions below EUR 450,000 may still require simplified documentation on request; all intercompany transactions must be at arm's length regardless of documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies an anti-abuse substance doctrine through (i) the statutory GAAR in Art. 6 of the General Tax Law (LGI/Steueranpassungsgesetz of 30 October 1934) codifying the 'abus de droit' principle; (ii) the 2024 Budget Law (loi du 29 mars 2024) inserting Art. 168ter LIR requiring substantive economic activity – adequate premises, qualified staff, actual management, and genuine business rationale – to benefit from the participation exemption, the dividend WHT exemption, and interest/royalty deduction rules; (iii) EU ATAD and the pending Unshell directive negotiation, under which shell entities without minimal substance can be denied tax benefits; and (iv) the Luxembourg administrative practice for SCSp partnerships which rejects artificial back-to-back structures lacking substance. A GDP pharma freight SCSp with nominal substance may be recharacterized as a taxable Luxembourg company, triggering CIT at 24.94%, reclassification of distributions as dividends subject to 15% WHT, and backdated penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 180 LIR: imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 25,000; mandatory publication of the judgment; tax surcharge of 10% to 75% of the evaded tax; monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%; retention of the normal 10-year statute of limitation for fraud cases.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the SCSp (whether individual or corporate) is jointly and severally liable without limitation for the partnership's obligations; a corporate GP's directors may face civil restitution, disqualification, and, in aggravated cases, travel bans or imprisonment for complicity in fiscal fraud; the tax administration may pierce the veil where the GP is a mere shell.
Shell / Artificial SCSp Structure (Abus de Droit and 2024 Substance Rules)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a fully taxable Luxembourg corporate entity; imposition of 24.94% CIT and municipal business tax on all profits recast retroactively; denial of the participation exemption and reimposition of 15% dividend WHT on all outbound distributions; additional penalties up to 75% and interest at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: Under Art. 6 LGI and civil jurisprudence, directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly orchestrated the artificial structure are personally liable for the undercollected corporate tax and may be ordered to pay restitution; criminal courts may issue travel restrictions for non-resident directors and order imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 4,000 per missing document; the tax administration may issue an estimated assessment with no burden of proof on the taxpayer; disallowance of the related-party deduction; penalty interest of 0.6% per month on the recomputed tax; possible voluntary disclosure limitations.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the acknowledgment that no documentation exists may be held jointly liable for the assessed tax in the event of the entity's insolvency; in flagrant cases, personal fines are imposed under the general shareholder/manager liability provisions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (RBE) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of EUR 250 to EUR 1,250,000 for companies under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) and the Law of 13 January 2019 on the RBE; delisting from commercial register; possible freezing of bank accounts and prohibition on payment rails (SWIFT/ACH).

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors face personal criminal liability with imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years for serious AML breaches; directors of corporate partners are jointly liable for the RBE filing obligation; travel bans and professional disqualifications are routinely ordered in significant cases.
Cross-Border Withholding Tax Abuses / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Re imposition of the 15% domestic dividend WHT with a 25% surcharge; disallowance of the participation exemption; retroactive interest and penalties; in treaty-shopping cases, denial of treaty benefits and additional fines up to 75% of the avoided WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the misuse of treaty relief can be made personally liable for the withholding tax shortfall, face civil fraud damages, and be subject to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years; overseas travel restrictions may be imposed pending examination.

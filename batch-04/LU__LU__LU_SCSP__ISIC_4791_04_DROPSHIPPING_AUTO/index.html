Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), in coordination with the RCS Section of the District Court of Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended; Articles 944-1 to 944-12 (SCSp regime) inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the annual accounts of undertakings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Registration application with the RCS (formulaire de demande d'inscription au RCS) accompanied by the notarized or privately signed limited partnership agreement (contrat de société) and the declaration of appointment of the manager.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital and no escrow account requirement; partners may contribute on a purely deferred basis, and the partnership agreement freely determines capital, capital calls, and profit-sharing ratios.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the Luxembourg registered office obtained from the municipal fire brigade (CGDIS) where the office is physically open; workplace safety and structural load inspection clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Law of 17 June 1994 on safety and health at work; because dropshipping automation does not involve warehouses or manufacturing, no industrial site clearance, pollution permit, or explosives authorization is required.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital and no escrow account requirement; partners may contribute on a purely deferred basis, and the partnership agreement freely determines capital, capital calls, and profit-sharing ratios.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and registration duties; unified e-filing portal at MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu); direct tax forms via ACD, VAT forms and EC Sales Lists via the AED's e-service portal.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the SCSp and its managed entity in the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain the Luxembourg business registration number (numéro de matricule); 2) open a Luxembourg tax file with the ACD by submitting the partnership's constitutive deed, RCS extract, and partner identification, whereupon a Luxembourg direct-tax number is allocated; 3) file the VAT registration application (Déclaration d'assujettissement à la TVA) through MyGuichet/AED e-services supported by the matricule, proof of the automated dropshipping activity, and business premises or power of attorney; 4) the AED issues the LU+8 digit VAT identification number, typically within 2 to 4 weeks; 5) non-EU-established principals must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative, while EU-established remote sellers may instead use the Luxembourg One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) registration route; 6) obtain a Luxembourg EORI number prior to any customs export declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The SCSp is a fiscally transparent partnership by default: it has no legal personality and is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax or municipal business tax; profits, losses, and tax attributes flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. Although transparent for income tax, an SCSp carrying on a commercial automated dropshipping trade is itself a taxable person for Luxembourg VAT purposes and must register, charge, and reclaim VAT in its own name. The activity may also create a Luxembourg permanent establishment for foreign partners, exposing them to Luxembourg income tax on attributable profits.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections relevant to an SCSp include: (i) the irrevocable opt-in regime under Article 175 LIR (Luxembourg Income Tax Law) whereby an eligible transparent partnership may elect to be treated as a fully taxable capital company for corporate income tax and municipal business tax purposes; (ii) the VAT small-business exemption election under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for taxable persons with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000; (iii) designation of a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative or agent; (iv) the OSS and IOSS registration elections for cross-border B2C and import supplies; and (v) written partnership profit- and loss-allocation elections, including special allocations, which the ACD follows provided partner-level reporting under the transparency regime is correct. Hybrid mismatch consequences are imposed automatically by the ATAD 2 provisions and cannot be altered by unilateral partner elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic Luxembourg small-business VAT exemption: annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (election available). A foreign non-established supplier has no de minimis exemption and must register from the first taxable Luxembourg supply. Intra-EU distance sales of goods by an EU-established supplier are subject to an EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold, above which Luxembourg OSS registration is mandatory. Imported goods in consignments with intrinsic value not exceeding EUR 150 may be declared via IOSS; higher-value imports require standard Luxembourg VAT registration or the appointment of a customs agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods physically consigned or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or by a third party on its behalf, provided the goods leave EU customs territory within the statutory time limit, generally 90 days, and the supplier obtains complete export evidence. For automated dropshipping, zero-rating requires that each shipment be linked to a valid Luxembourg/EU customs export declaration, shipping and transport documents proving actual exit, and the customer's non-EU status. Supplies of goods to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated as intra-Community supplies only where the customer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained and an EC Sales List is filed; automated B2C EU deliveries without OSS registration must be fully taxed at the Luxembourg rate. Digital and other B2B services generally follow the reverse-charge principle in the customer's EU jurisdiction and do not qualify as Luxembourg exports unless place-of-supply evidence under the B2B rules is obtained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/e-DPE) with customs office exit confirmation (IE599 electronic export movement)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (B/L or AWB) evidencing carriage out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank transfer evidence of FX repatriation from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and order/dropshipping log linking the sale to the export declaration
  • Transport contracts, CMR waybills, or courier tracking movement proof
  • EORI number and customs broker mandate where the SCSp authorizes indirect representation
  • VAT export evidence register and partner/management declaration on the factual export chain

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-connected digital stamping, or mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance for domestic B2B/B2C invoicing. E-invoicing is permitted, and EU Directive 2006/112/EC-compliant invoices must contain the statutory numbering and VAT details. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists (VIES), and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via the MyGuichet/AED portal. The EU ViDA reform will introduce structured e-invoicing and digital tax reporting in Luxembourg in the upcoming legislative cycles; automated dropshipping platforms should nevertheless be configured now to issue compliant invoices and maintain audit-proof export documentation.

Export zero-rating is audited strictly by the AED and Luxembourg Customs: the taxpayer bears the burden of proof and must retain the complete export and remittance dossier, ordinarily for at least 10 years. Incorrect zero-rating is clawed back retrospectively with statutory late-payment interest, administrative fines which may reach up to 200% of the evaded VAT in fraud cases, and criminal liability including imprisonment and fines for fraudulent export schemes. In an automated dropshipping model, missing shipment-level proof, fictitious customs movements, or non-repatriated export proceeds trigger reversal of input VAT credits, denial of the 0% rate, and joint and several liability of the SCSp's managers and fiscal agents. Coordinated ACD/AED/Customs audits and EU administrative cooperation (DAC/DAC7) scrutiny of offshore buyers are standard in Luxembourg.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a member of the Eurozone and applies full current and capital account convertibility pursuant to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Movements Directive. There are no exchange control restrictions on inbound or outbound transfers, profit repatriation, or dividend distributions. SCSp partnerships benefit from the same freedom of capital movements, and profit remittances to non-resident partners are generally permitted without prior administrative approval. However, anti-money laundering and know-your-client obligations apply to all cross-border payment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Luxembourg to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into euros within a set timeline. Export revenue may be held in any freely convertible currency in a bank account. Economic sanctions and EU restrictive measures may, however, impose blocking or reporting obligations in specific circumstances. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and foreign exchange related prudential matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized in the EU/EEA may operate in Luxembourg under the EU PSD2 passporting regime, enabling account and payment infrastructure without a Luxembourg physical branch. However, tier-one commercial banks in Luxembourg often restrict initial account opening to in-country regulated banks and treat EMIs as unsettled or higher-risk counterparties. EMIs generally do not provide escrow-grade capital accounts for statutory or structuring purposes. An SCSp may use a fully licensed EU EMI for operational liquidity aggregation and settlement, but the primary corporate treasury account should be maintained with a fully licensed Luxembourg credit institution to preserve banking certainty and future fund financing credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to a Luxembourg SCSp are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the investing partner's bank to the SCSp's Luxembourg-listed bank account. The beneficiary account should be titled exactly as the SCSp, and the payment reference must state 'Capital Contribution' or 'Equity Participation' in accordance with the SCSp partnership agreement. No specific Luxembourg purpose code exists; only the commercial description is legally required. Under EU anti-money laundering rules, the remitter and beneficiary details must include full names, account numbers, and legal identifiers. The bank may request the signed partnership agreement and the contribution schedule prior to accepting the inflow for tier-one onboarding compliance. For additional source verification, the bank will request a copy of the remitter's bank statement showing the originating account and the debit entry.Outbound trade disbursements for dropshipping operations are settled through SWIFT MT103 for conventional bank transfers and through commercial letters of credit (L/C) for larger supplier orders requiring documentary protection. For supplier payments below EUR 50,000, a direct MT103 with supplier bank account details and invoice reference is standard. For high-value or recurring procurement, a confirmed irrevocable documentary L/C issued by the Luxembourg bank in favor of the supplier's bank provides settlement security. For intra-group treasury sweeps, an MT202/COV is used between banks. Clearing rails are TARGET2 for euro-denominated domestic payments and SWIFT for cross-currency settlements.

Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), notably Articles 103-1 et seq. on the special limited partnership, together with Law of 12 July 2013 introducing the SCSp; RCS registration provisions and allied transparency rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income tax; online filing via MyGuichet (guichet.public.lu) and Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) portal for entity registration.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp established or operated by foreign participants, the general partner (commandité) must first register the partnership in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). The AEDT then assigns a Luxembourg national business number and, where taxable activities are performed, a VAT identification number. Non-resident suppliers without a fixed establishment in Luxembourg must submit Form 010/1 (VAT registration) plus certified constitutional documents of the foreign entity, proof of appointment of the general partner, a Luxembourg tax representative appointment if requiring one, identity and residency documents of the partners, and a statement describing the intended economic activities. The SCSp itself may be registered for VAT if it independently carries out taxable activities; otherwise the partners are jointly liable. For income tax, the ACD issues a local income tax file reference upon first tax filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the SCSp is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg domestic law; income, gains, and withholding tax treatment are determined at the partner level. Resident corporate partners are taxed on their worldwide income, while non-resident partners are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or on specific Luxembourg-source items. The entity does not automatically incur corporate income tax unless an opaque election is made.

Available election forms

Luxembourg tax law allows an SCSp to elect to be treated as opaque for direct tax purposes, i.e. to be subject to corporate income tax and municipal business tax, provided that all partners with unlimited liability are Luxembourg-resident corporate entities or all partners are corporate entities. The election is binding for at least five fiscal years and requires taxation in the hands of the partnership as if it were a company. For US federal tax purposes, the SCSp may separately make a check-the-box election under Form 8832, and hybrid mismatch considerations under ATAD II apply to elections producing double-non-taxation or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR — Luxembourg-established businesses are exempt from VAT if annual taxable turnover remains below EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT) under the small-business franchise; non-established foreign suppliers are required to register from the very first taxable transaction or intra-Community acquisition, with no de minimis threshold. For intra-EU distance sales and digital services, special schemes (OSS) apply above an EU-wide threshold of EUR 10,000, but for an SCSp in cloud DevOps infrastructure, registration is mandatory whenever Luxembourg place-of-supply rules produce a taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Luxembourg VAT law zero-rates exports of goods transported to non-EU countries under Article 16 of the VAT Act, allowing the exporter to apply 0% and retain the immediate right to deduct input VAT, provided the exporter is VAT-identified and holds conclusive evidence that the goods left the EU within the legal period (usually 90 days from dispatch). Services, including digital infrastructure and DevOps cloud tools, supplied to non-EU business customers fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule, so no output VAT is charged; the supplier must substantiate the customer's non-EU business establishment through an EU VAT number, corporate registration extract, or written confirmation, and retain invoice and usage records showing where the service is actually supplied.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) / customs export declaration (D.E. 01) endorsed by the departing customs office
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR international transport consignment note
  • Commercial invoice referencing the SAD and the customer VAT/registration numbers
  • Packing list detailing the goods and final destination
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT bank payment confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Proof of movement: transport contract, truck transit documents, and proof of arrival at the third-country destination

Luxembourg currently imposes no general real-time e-invoicing clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no API-based pre-approval for B2B or B2C invoices. B2B e-invoicing is permitted under buyer consent or self-billing agreements. For public-sector invoices (B2G), e-invoices must be transmitted in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format through the Luxembourg eProcurement platform. VAT returns are filed electronically on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis via MyGuichet, and the AEDT may request SAF-T-like electronic audit files.

Export zero-rating claims are closely scrutinised by the AEDT. If supporting documentation is incomplete, falsified, or not obtained within the legal time limits, the sale is reclassified as taxable at the standard 15% rate, with interest charged at 0.6% per month or part-month and assessed up to the applicable limitation period (5 years ordinary, 10 years in cases of fraud). Administrative penalties range from EUR 250 to EUR 12,500 per misdeclared invoice, and intentional falsification of export evidence may constitute tax fraud, carrying criminal penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to EUR 12,500 per count.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg applies the EU free movement of capital and payments regime under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC; there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on current account or capital account transactions, and no licensing requirement for foreign exchange operations by non-bank corporates. For the SCSp, profit remittance is unhindered: because the Société en Commandite Spéciale is tax-transparent and lacks separate legal personality, income flows through to its partners without Luxembourg withholding tax at entity level, and partners resident or non-resident may freely receive distributions, provided the partnership is not engaged in regulated financial services. Practical hurdles are limited to banking KYC documentation of each partner and, where capital is mobilised from non-EEA jurisdictions, compliance with EU sanctions screening and OECD CRS/FATCA reporting obligations. Inward direct investment and repatriation of invested capital, liquidation proceeds and operational surpluses are not subject to prior administrative approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg and the euro area impose no export proceeds surrender, conversion quota or repatriation deadline on commercial receipts. The SCSp may retain 100% of its export earnings from software subscriptions, developer infrastructure licensing, database monitoring services and API usage fees, in EUR or in any foreign currency, held on accounts with EU/EEA credit institutions or authorised payment/electronic-money institutions. The only statutory constraints are general corporate record-keeping obligations and CSSF AML/CFT requirements to demonstrate the economic substance and source of funds of commercial flows if using foreign-currency accounts or contracting with counterparties in higher-risk jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national monetary authority, with monetary policy competence vested in the European Central Bank (ECB) as Luxembourg is a euro-area member state. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions. The BCL operates TARGET2 and SEPA clearing settlement for euro-denominated payments and oversees the smooth functioning of payment, clearing and securities settlement systems in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for payment collection but Low as the sole treasury rail. Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland), an electronic money institution authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland, may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 via the CSSF and operate the Stripe_Billing rail for card acquiring and SEPA Direct Debit collection; it is a compliant collection front-end but cannot provide deposit-taking, escrow, commercial lending or FX hedging. Mercury (US) is a US fintech that offers ACH through partner banks such as Evolve Bank & Trust; it is not licensed in the EU, cannot issue SEPA/EUR IBANs to a Luxembourg entity and cannot lawfully hold euro deposits for EU customers. Direct use of Mercury_ACH by the LU SCSp is therefore Low viability and raises Luxembourg payment-services-law concerns (Law of 10 November 2009 transposing PSD2) unless ACH is channelled through a separately registered US group entity whose USD balances are subsequently swept to Luxembourg by SWIFT MT103. The structurally correct model is: Stripe for global card/SEPA collection, a US LLC (disregarded for Luxembourg purposes under appropriate substance safeguards) for US ACH, and a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank for EUR/USD treasury concentration, escrow and hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from EU/EEA investors are routed by SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT or SCT Inst) to the SCSp's EUR operational or escrow account at the Luxembourg bank, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (payment of capital) or 'INVS' (investment) and the remittance information block showing the SCSp name, the capital call reference and the investor identifier. Non-EEA (typically US) investors remit via SWIFT MT103 from their bank, converted to EUR by the Luxembourg bank under a standing FX instruction if needed; the MT103 must carry the beneficiary IBAN, the exact legal name 'LU SCSp' or the full partnership name, and purpose description 'Capital Contribution' referencing the partnership agreement and the notarised RCS excerpt. The receiving bank performs sanctions screening against OFAC and EU restricted-party lists and reconciles each credit against the updated partner register; funds whose origin does not match a documented partner are suspended pending source-of-funds evidence. Where initial commitments are pari-passu locked at closing, the bank operates a capital escrow (séquestre) account with dual-signatory release against the signed capital call and the RBE filing.For EEA software, database and API infrastructure vendors, the rail is SEPA Credit Transfer settled through TARGET2 or a SEPA-compliant CSM, with reference codes aligned to the invoice. For non-EEA disbursements — principally US cloud providers (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure) and global subprocessors — the Luxembourg bank executes a SWIFT MT103 in USD through its US correspondent over Fedwire or CHIPS, debiting the SCSp's EUR account and applying a spot EUR/USD conversion. Larger infrastructure procurement (annual committed-use contracts or custom hardware/network gear) may be supported by a commercial documentary letter of credit or standby letter of credit issued on behalf of the SCSp, especially where the supplier requires payment against shipping or delivery documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for payment collection but Low as the sole treasury rail. Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland), an electronic money institution authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland, may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 via the CSSF and operate the Stripe_Billing rail for card acquiring and SEPA Direct Debit collection; it is a compliant collection front-end but cannot provide deposit-taking, escrow, commercial lending or FX hedging. Mercury (US) is a US fintech that offers ACH through partner banks such as Evolve Bank & Trust; it is not licensed in the EU, cannot issue SEPA/EUR IBANs to a Luxembourg entity and cannot lawfully hold euro deposits for EU customers. Direct use of Mercury_ACH by the LU SCSp is therefore Low viability and raises Luxembourg payment-services-law concerns (Law of 10 November 2009 transposing PSD2) unless ACH is channelled through a separately registered US group entity whose USD balances are subsequently swept to Luxembourg by SWIFT MT103. The structurally correct model is: Stripe for global card/SEPA collection, a US LLC (disregarded for Luxembourg purposes under appropriate substance safeguards) for US ACH, and a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank for EUR/USD treasury concentration, escrow and hedging.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC); a 7% solidarity surcharge is levied on the IRC amount and municipal business tax of 6.75% applies in Luxembourg City, producing a combined marginal effective burden of approximately 24.94% for a taxable Luxembourg collective entity. The SCSp itself is fiscally transparent (pass-through) and bears no IRC, municipal business tax or net wealth tax on its profits; the tax burden crystallizes at the level of the general and limited partners according to their residence and treaty position.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on profit distributions by an SCSp: the vehicle is fiscally transparent and payments to partners are recharacterized as direct allocations, not dividends subject to Luxembourg withholding. In contrast, a classical Luxembourg holding company remains subject to the 15% domestic dividend withholding tax, reducible to 5% under a standard participation DTA clause, to 0% under the full participation exemption where the 12-month holding condition is met, and to 0% within the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive framework when the parent holds at least 10% for 12 uninterrupted months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory filing threshold in EUR applies; administrative practice under the Luxembourg transfer pricing circular (A.C.D. circular L.I.R. n° 56/1 of 28 January 2011) expects contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation once cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year, or EUR 300,000 for pure financing transactions; a de facto zero threshold applies where the SCSp lacks a fixed office, local employees and risk-bearing capacity.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is instead enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule, ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 CFC rules, the 2013 transfer pricing circular, and the 2020 QIE substance circular. An SCSp operating DevOps/cloud infrastructure must demonstrate local governance, qualified personnel in Luxembourg, physical or leased IT infrastructure, revenue-generating decision autonomy, and actual risk control to benefit from DTA rates, the IP Box, the participation exemption, and the interest/royalty directive protections. A letterbox partnership is disregarded and re-attributed to the place of effective management of its controlling partner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment of evaded IRC and municipal business tax with a 50% fraud surcharge on the evaded amount, monthly late-payment interest at 0.8%, fines up to EUR 150,000, imprisonment of responsible officers up to 5 years for aggravated tax fraud, and public debarment from regulated procurement and cloud accreditation schemes.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the SCSp veil where the partnership was fully owned and asset-stripped: active general partners face civil restitution of the evaded tax plus interest; the managing partner is exposed to travel bans in cross-border EU enforcement and imprisonment for up to five years; personal disqualification as a Luxembourg fiscal representative is automatic.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA reduced rates and domestic participation exemption; disallowance of the IP Box; re-taxation of the SCSp as a resident entity managed in Luxembourg; reassessment with a 20% late-filing surcharge; exchange of information triggered by foreign authorities resulting in a mirror adjustment and double taxation on the foreign partner side.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing exposes the general partner's personal assets for the full reassessed tax; civil fines of EUR 25,000 per responsible officer; travel bans ordered by the home tax authority in parallel enforcement; the compliance director may be criminally charged for facilitating a cross-border artificial arrangement.
AML / UBO Register Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 14,000 for each UBO register omission under the amended AML law of 13 January 2019, escalating to EUR 250,000 for systemic institutional failure; CSSF/CRF freezing orders on bank and payment accounts; prohibition of profit distributions until the files are remediated; increased scrutiny under the 5th and 6th EU AML Directives.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer and general partner are jointly liable for AML fines; criminal imprisonment of up to 2 years for laundering the proceeds of a fraudulent subscription scheme; travel bans and asset freezes applied in cross-border AML investigations after mutual recognition of confiscation orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments under Article 56 LIR with recalculation of the arm's length result via TNMM or profit split; monthly late-payment interest at 0.8% on the upward adjustment; additional 10% penalty on the reassessed base; referral to EU joint transfer pricing arbitration; consequential DTA relief denial causes double taxation within EU/PEA frameworks.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false transfer pricing documentation are exposed to negligence liability for the avoided tax amount; falsification of commercial books carries imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans applied under cross-border enforcement directives; disqualification from acting as fiscal representative or partnership manager in Luxembourg.

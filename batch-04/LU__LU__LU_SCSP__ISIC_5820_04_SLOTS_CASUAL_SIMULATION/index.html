Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct/income tax, with centralised administrative portal at guichet.lu; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT/turnover tax and registration; business registration through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp, the contract under Luxembourg law must be filed with the RCS. After incorporation, the entity receives a 13-digit national identification number (matricule / LLN) used for tax purposes. The SCSp, as a fiscally transparent partnership, is not typically subject to Luxembourg CIT on its own income, but must still register with the ACD and AED where it carries on taxable activities. Foreign founders without EU/EEA establishment may need to appoint a Luxembourg tax representative. VAT identification is obtained by filing the standard VAT registration application through the AED/guichet.lu portal, supported by the partnership deed, proof of address, partners' identification, and a description of the proposed electronic-services activity. Registration for employer withholding, VAT, and indirect-tax returns is undertaken through MyGuichet; the custom/matricule reference links all administrative filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (SCSp is fiscally transparent; Luxembourg income tax is imposed on each partner/sociétaire at partner level, subject to hybrid-mismatch recharacterisation in cross-border settings).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Luxembourg partnerships, including certain SCS/SCSp forms, may elect to be treated as subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (optant partnerships) if the partnership satisfies statutory conditions, including, where applicable, publication of audited financial statements and partner composition requirements. The transparent regime is default; partners may elect, directly or indirectly, to file and pay at the partnership level for administrative convenience, but this does not derogate from the pass-through classification unless the opt-in corporate-taxation conditions are met. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply to foreign-electronic-commerce and publishing structures where another jurisdiction treats an SCSp as a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold for non-established taxable persons making Luxembourg taxable supplies; any such supply triggers VAT registration unless covered by OSS or the €10,000 EU distance-sale/electronically-supplied-services threshold. A resident small-business exemption applies to established businesses with annual Luxembourg turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, subject to the AED's rules and anti-avoidance limitations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods to non-EU destinations may be zero-rated provided the Executive VAT Directive criteria are met, including actual removal from EU customs territory, transfer of ownership/export title, and preservation of export evidence. Pure digital-services exports (simulated casino slots publishing) are governed by place-of-supply rules: B2C supplies to Luxembourg consumers are taxable at 17%; B2B supplies to non-Luxembourg EU customers are reverse-charged; B2C supplies to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (customer-local VAT applies), not strictly an export zero-rating. Zero-rating is also available for certain services directly related to goods export or cross-border intangible services if contractually and factually supplied for purposes outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A or electronic declaration through Luxembourg PLDA/National Customs System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual departure from EU territory
  • Commercial Invoice containing the full consignee's details, Incoterms, quantity, description and value
  • Proof of export such as an exit summary declaration, external transit (T1) document, or customs-output confirmation from the member state of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment settlement evidence showing the foreign customer's payment
  • Transport and insurance documentation supporting the physical movement where goods are exported
  • Intrastat declaration where applicable for intra-EU dispatches, and OSS/VAT e-commerce summaries for cross-border B2C supplies

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API-based e-invoicing authorisation, or digital stamping is currently in force in Luxembourg. e-Invoicing is permitted and, in B2G supplies, mandatory under EU Directive 2014/55/EU using structured formats compliant with EN 16931/Peppol. For this simulated-social-casino/publishing activity, no special Luxembourg clearance platform is required; Apple IAP and Google Play Billing must follow EU e-commerce VAT marketplace rules, and the platform may be regarded as the deemed supplier for B2C digital-service supplies.

The AED may reopen and audit export zero-rating for up to 5 years (extended to 10 years in fraud or intentional failure cases). If export evidence is incomplete or the transaction is recharacterised as domestic or intra-EU, 17% VAT becomes immediately payable, with statutory late interest, administrative fines, and retroactive correction of refunds. Intentional false zero-rating or fraudulent export claims expose the SCSp, tax representative if any, and responsible partners/managers to criminal sanctions, including fines up to double the evaded tax and imprisonment for tax offences. Abuse-of-law doctrines under Luxembourg case law and EU anti-fraud principles disallow artificial routing of digital gaming/revenue streams through Luxembourg where no real establishment or economic substance exists; penalties may include denial of exemption, clawback of input VAT credits, and deregistration for non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is part of the euro area; the euro is freely convertible for all current and capital transactions under Articles 63–65 TFEU. There are no exchange-control restrictions on profit remittances, loan repayments, or equity distributions. The SCSp, while lacking separate legal personality under Luxembourg law, is recognised for banking purposes as an account-holding arrangement and contracts through its general partner. Remittances to non-resident partners are not subject to any prior BCL approval; only statistical reporting duties and standard AML/CFT obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg and EU law impose no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Export revenues from digital gaming can be retained in EUR or any convertible currency; there is no requirement to repatriate or convert into EUR within a statutory period.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and EMIs.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) that hold a valid passport under PSD2 into Luxembourg; these can provide multi-currency IBANs, local clearing, and e-money wallets. Offshore (non-EU) EMIs have low viability because they lack the EU passport and are treated as unregulated counterparties by Luxembourg banks and the CSSF. Legal constraints: the SCSp must be the account holder; the EMI must verify the SCSp's existence via the RCS; social-casino activity is often restricted under EMI internal risk policies due to perceived 'gambling' adjacency; a legal opinion on the non-gambling nature of the social-casino model is generally required.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% entity-level CIT for SCSp (fiscally transparent); corporate partners are subject to Luxembourg CIT at 17% (15% base + 7% unemployment surcharge) plus municipal business tax of approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City, yielding aggregate effective headline rate of 22.80%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% is the statutory dividend withholding tax for Luxembourg stock corporations, but an SCSp distribution of profits is not treated as a dividend under domestic law because the SCSp is fiscally transparent. Consequently, no Luxembourg dividend WHT is imposed on partner profit allocations unless the SCSp is recharacterised as opaque or conversions create deemed dividend treatment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is mandatory for groups with consolidated turnover of EUR 750,000,000 or more. Local File is required when cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per tax year in aggregate; lower thresholds apply to financial transactions and intangibles. Even below these limits, a taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous economic substance evidence to refute recharacterisation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, courts and tax authorities enforce a substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial ownership requirements, anti-hybrid rules and an ATAD-based General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). A SCSp must demonstrate real physical substance, effective senior management in Luxembourg, meaningful payroll, bona fide commercial decision-making and consistent bank account flows to sustain transparent tax treatment and DTA protection. Social casino and virtual slots gaming requires no gambling licence in Luxembourg, but substance and AML compliance are mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misuse of SCSp Transparency

Statutory sanction: A retroactive 10-year assessment window, late interest up to 5.25% p.a., fixed penalties up to EUR 1,500, a 10% administrative penalty on evaded tax, criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and commercial representatives may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid tax; authorities can obtain travel bans, asset seizures, professional disqualification and imprisonment where the SCSp veil is pierced.
Artificial Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the SCSp's transparent character; recharacterisation as an opaque Luxembourg resident company subject to 22.80% effective CIT plus municipal tax, net wealth tax, VAT and 10-year back assessment; denial of DTA benefits and cancellation of tax rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors, statutory managers and partners who knowingly cause or permit the artificial arrangement may be named co-taxpayers; Luxembourg courts allow veil piercing, civil restitution, freezing orders and travel bans in abuse-of-law cases.
AML/CFT and Simulated Gambling Payment Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law authorises administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious, repeated or systematic breaches; criminal money-laundering penalties include fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: AML officers, directors and shadow managers face personal criminal liability, passport seizures, travel restrictions and civil damages; the Luxembourg financial regulator may also ban the individual from managing regulated payment or fintech entities.
Transfer Pricing and Hidden Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authorities may adjust transfer prices to arm's length, impose a 5%-10% late-payment penalty, add interest at 5.25% p.a., and assert a deemed Luxembourg PE, triggering CIT, municipal tax and up to five years of retroactive assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial intercompany pricing or negligently ignore economic substance may be liable for restitution to co-partners and to the tax administration; personal travel bans and imprisonment are reserved for evidence of fraudulent misconduct.

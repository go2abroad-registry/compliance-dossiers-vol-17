Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register, operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Ministry of Justice, Grand Duchy of Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Article 26-1 et seq. (general partnership rules) and Articles 318-1 to 318-33 (Special Limited Partnership - SCSp), as introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013 and amended by the Law of 25 August 2015 on legal capacity; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners and the Grand-Ducal Regulation on the Trade and Companies Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Contrat de société (partnership agreement), being the constitutive instrument of the SCSp, deposited in electronic form with the RCS through the LBR e-incorporation portal; notarial form is required only where the SCSp opts for separate legal personality or contributes real estate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines the amount, form and payment schedule of contributions. No escrow or blocked-account requirement is imposed under Article 318-1 et seq. of the Law of 10 August 1915; contribution payment evidence is retained in the bank file only.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the SCSp; the partnership agreement freely determines the amount, form and payment schedule of contributions. No escrow or blocked-account requirement is imposed under Article 318-1 et seq. of the Law of 10 August 1915; contribution payment evidence is retained in the bank file only.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: 1) The partnership agreement (contrat de société) establishing the Special Limited Partnership (SCSp) is adopted in final form; the general partner accepts its management mandate and the limited partners subscribe the initial contributions per the capital schedule. 2) The registered office is fixed in Luxembourg. 3) The managing general partner is appointed sole representative to bind the SCSp, open bank accounts and execute contracts. 4) The managing general partner is authorised to file the deed with the RCS, register for VAT, ACD income tax and RBE, and engage Luxembourg legal, tax and audit advisers. 5) All prior acts in furtherance of incorporation are ratified.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority - portal: pfi.public.lu and myguichet.lu; direct tax administration: Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - portal: impotsdirects.public.lu; customs administration: Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) - portal: douanes.public.lu; business register: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - portal: lbr.lu

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SCSp trading in SSD and CPU chips, the statutory sequence is: (1) execute the SCSp partnership agreement (by notarial or private deed) and file for registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the LBR portal, obtaining the RCS number and an 11-digit national business identification number; (2) file VAT registration Form 1040 with the AEDT electronically via myguichet.lu once the annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or immediately where the SCSp makes intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000, imports goods, supplies goods to Luxembourg customers, or intends to claim export zero-rating and input VAT recovery; (3) obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by 8 digits; (4) register for customs purposes with the ADA to obtain an EORI number in the format LU followed by 11 digits for physical export of the semiconductor goods; (5) appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative where the SCSp is considered not established in Luxembourg and is owned by non-EU resident partners; (6) file the annual informative income tax return (Formulaire 510) with the ACD solely for partner allocation purposes, since the SCSp is fiscally transparent and carries no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax liability at entity level.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SCSp trading in SSD and CPU chips, the statutory sequence is: (1) execute the SCSp partnership agreement (by notarial or private deed) and file for registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the LBR portal, obtaining the RCS number and an 11-digit national business identification number; (2) file VAT registration Form 1040 with the AEDT electronically via myguichet.lu once the annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or immediately where the SCSp makes intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000, imports goods, supplies goods to Luxembourg customers, or intends to claim export zero-rating and input VAT recovery; (3) obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by 8 digits; (4) register for customs purposes with the ADA to obtain an EORI number in the format LU followed by 11 digits for physical export of the semiconductor goods; (5) appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative where the SCSp is considered not established in Luxembourg and is owned by non-EU resident partners; (6) file the annual informative income tax return (Formulaire 510) with the ACD solely for partner allocation purposes, since the SCSp is fiscally transparent and carries no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax liability at entity level.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is transparent by statutory default under Articles 50-26 of the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies; the entity is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (IFC), or net wealth tax (IFT), and no equivalent of the US check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg domestic law; partners may structure the partnership agreement to govern profit allocation among general and limited partners; the general partner may elect to form a VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 LGTVA where common control exists, may elect the cash-accounting method for VAT under Article 63 LGTVA when annual turnover is below EUR 500,000, and may voluntarily waive the small-business exemption under Article 162 LGTVA to recover input VAT; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD, as transposed by the Law of 21 December 2018, apply automatically where a foreign jurisdiction fiscally treats the SCSp as an opaque entity, and the ACD will apply the fraus legis and general anti-abuse rules under Article 6 paragraph 1 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) to disregard artificial arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for the small-business exemption under Article 162 LGTVA; mandatory VAT registration is nevertheless required without any turnover threshold where the SCSp makes intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000, imports goods from outside the EU, engages in distance sales into Luxembourg, receives reverse-charge services, or makes any export supply for which zero-rating or input VAT recovery is claimed; the wholesale SSD and CPU chip export activity therefore triggers compulsory VAT and EORI registration irrespective of domestic turnover levels

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of SSD and CPU chips from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU qualify for zero-rating under Article 74 LGTVA where the SCSp or its customs agent proves dispatch or transport of the goods out of the customs territory of the EU within 90 days of the supply and holds the full export documentation trail; intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered EU customer qualify for zero-rating under Article 43 LGTVA provided the customer VAT number is validated through VIES, the goods are physically dispatched to another Member State, and the corresponding EC Sales List is timely filed; the zero-rate claim fails where the goods remain in Luxembourg or another EU Member State, where the transport evidence is incomplete or inconsistent with the Incoterms, where the invoiced counterparty is not the actual acquirer in a serial-resale chain, or where the SCSp cannot prove its economic substance in Luxembourg; although Luxembourg imposes no statutory exchange-control deadline, the 180-day repatriation benchmark in this dossier reflects the standard commercial settlement term in the SWIFT TT and escrow inspection wire structure and must be demonstrated by the SCSp's export ledger upon audit

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / EXS declaration) filed in the EU Customs Trader Portal containing the export movement reference (MRN) number and confirming exit of the goods from the customs territory of the EU
  • International transport document evidencing dispatch: Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), or CMR/CIM consignment note (road or rail) bearing signatures, dates, and stamps of the foreign consignee or carrier
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sale contract or purchase order identifying the precise SSD and CPU chip specifications, quantities, unit prices, and agreed Incoterms delivery point
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds on the SCSp's bank account within the contractual and tax documentation deadline
  • VIES validation receipt for EU intra-Community customers together with the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) filed with the AEDT
  • Proof of exit from the EU including customs stamps, export MRN numbers, delivery acknowledgements from the ultimate consignee, and any warehouse or free-zone movement records

Luxembourg currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API filing, or digital stamping regime; standard electronic invoicing (PDF, UBL, Factur-X, EDI) is permitted and accepted by the AEDT; recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) for intra-Community supplies must be filed online through the eSpace portal by the 15th day of the following month; Intrastat declarations for dispatches are required monthly once the annual dispatch threshold of EUR 150,000 is exceeded; Luxembourg has announced alignment with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework with mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions expected no earlier than 1 January 2026, but no implementing statute, technical API, or clearance mandate has yet been enacted.

The AEDT may deny the zero rate and reassess VAT at 16% with statutory late interest accruing at 0.6% per month on arrears where the export documentation bundle is defective, inconsistent, or missing; administrative fines under Articles 411 to 416 LGTVA range from EUR 250 to EUR 50,000, and the tax authorities may apply assessment penalties of up to 10% of the evaded tax in cases of negligent under-declaration; fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger criminal sanctions under Article 412 LGTVA of EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years, with enhanced fines for organized fraud; the AEDT may also apply the fraus legis doctrine and the general anti-abuse provision of Article 6 paragraph 1 LIR to re-characterize artificial interposition structures involving shell partners or circular re-invoicing; retrospective clawbacks may extend to the full statutory assessment period of 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud, with joint and several liability of the general partner for the non-institutional SCSp.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline for a taxable Luxembourg corporate entity in Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); 0% entity-level CIT for a fiscally transparent SCSp

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by an SCSp because the partnership is fiscally transparent; if the SCSp were treated as a taxable corporate entity, the domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax rate would be 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and to 5% or 10% under many DTAs for portfolio shareholdings

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for the mandatory Luxembourg transfer pricing documentation file (Form 710 / Local File); Master File applies when the group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report applies when group consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: The SCSp is not subject to a Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but Luxembourg tax authorities require real economic substance under Circular LIR 50/2016 and the statutory abuse-of-law doctrine; an SCSp must maintain a Luxembourg office, local management, personnel, bank accounts, accounting records and decision-making documentation in order to obtain tax residence certificates, access treaty benefits, and prevent recharacterisation as a shell or sham entity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Restitution of the evaded tax, 10% fraud surcharge, monthly interest at 0.6%, criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000, and imprisonment of up to five years for wilful tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be held jointly and severally liable for the SCSp's tax debts; civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans and imprisonment may be imposed where the director personally directed the fraud
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reattribution of the full trading profit to the non-resident partner's home jurisdiction, retroactive taxation in Luxembourg, administrative penalties, and public strike-off proceedings for sham substance

Director personal liability: Civil liability for resulting tax assessments and sanctions; the corporate veil may be pierced if directors permitted the entity to operate as a pure conduit with no economic substance
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length results, double taxation, 10% documentation penalty, fines of up to EUR 50,000 for non-filing, and monthly default interest on the additional assessment

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalties if they signed incomplete TP documentation or knowingly set artificial intercompany prices
AML and UBO Violations in High-Risk Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: CSSF/CRF administrative fines of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for aggravated violations, suspension of the entity's right to operate, and criminal money-laundering proceedings for structured or concealed commercial receipts

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner can be prosecuted personally for money laundering, face imprisonment, asset confiscation, civil damages and professional disqualification if they knowingly used the SCSp to disguise the source of SSD/CPU sale proceeds

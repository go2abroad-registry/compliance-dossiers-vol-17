Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; an SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent. Partnerships are not generally eligible to elect entity-level taxation. Luxembourg implements EU hybrid mismatch rules mandatorily; no elective de-hybridisation mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established SMEs (small business exemption); EUR 10,000 EU B2C distance-sales threshold; no threshold for non-established taxable persons supplying e-services or goods, who must register or use Union/Non-Union OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the EU to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate upon proof that the goods left the EU within 90 days; supplies of B2B and B2C e-services to customers located outside the EU are taxable where received and therefore outside Luxembourg's VAT, effectively zero-rated; all claims require supplier records of export transport, customer identification, payment route and service usability documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time continuous transaction control or digital stamping regime in Luxembourg; domestic B2B e-invoicing is optional. Mandatory PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing applies only to invoices addressed to Luxembourg public sector counterparties; private-sector invoices may be issued on paper or PDF without fiscal clearance.

Wrongful or fraudulent zero-rating is retroactively clawed back with default interest, VAT administrative fines and criminal sanctions for tax fraud, including imprisonment in serious cases; direct tax consequences can also arise for undocumented export under-pricing; the Luxembourg tax authority may apply the 10-year limitation period when fraud is evidenced.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg grants full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current account (EdTech subscription revenues, Apple IAP/Google Play payouts) and capital account (LP capital contributions, distributions, profit remittances) under Article 63 TFEU of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. No exchange-control licensing, no central bank approval for injections, and no repatriation permit is required. The SCSp is fiscally transparent, so operating profits flow to partners without entity-level dividend withholding; non-resident partners receive remittances freely via SEPA or SWIFT. The only practical hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds documentation and, where a Luxembourg paying agent is involved, the applicable domestic 15% withholding or treaty-reduced rate on certain deemed Luxembourg-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period restriction on foreign-currency export or digital-service receipts. Proceeds from cross-border EdTech subscriptions may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in resident bank or EMI accounts, or be netted and hedged without reference to the BCL.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of banks and electronic money institutions is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Payoneer Fit: High

Medium-High for operational collections and merchant settlement but Low for capital escrow and FX hedge booking. Luxembourg accepts CSSF-licensed EMIs and EEA-passported EMIs (PSD2), including Adyen, Mangopay, Payoneer and BaaS providers, which can issue resident IBANs or virtual IBANs for the SCSp before a Tier-1 account is active and can settle Apple IAP/Google Play flows. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot book derivatives, grant escrow-grade custody or offer interest-bearing capital deposits; SCSp non-resident UBO chains typically trigger enhanced KYC; and commingled processor settlement must be reconciled and ring-fenced to satisfy CSSF AML and safeguarding requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at entity level (tax-transparent pass-through); Luxembourg CIT headline rate is 17% for taxable profits of capital companies exceeding EUR 200,000, plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax (aggregate 24.94% in Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for SCSp profit distributions (tax-transparent pass-through; Luxembourg does not impose dividend withholding tax on allocations to partners). For standard capital companies, Luxembourg domestic WHT is 15% on gross dividends (5% where the 50% exemption for parent shareholders applies under the participation exemption).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (entity annual turnover) and EUR 1,000,000 (aggregate intra-group cross-border transactions) – Local File mandatory if both thresholds are exceeded; Master File mandatory when the MNE group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000

Economic substance scope: Formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in the UAE/BVI do not apply in Luxembourg. However, the Luxembourg General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), EU ATAD directives, and treaty entitlement provisions require genuine economic substance for SCSp. The SCSp must demonstrate at the partner level: a physical premises, qualified payroll, active management decisions in Luxembourg, and minimum local administrative autonomy. Adequate substance is essential to obtain treaty benefits and avoid the 'indicia of shell company' challenge under Luxembourg tax rulings and international bias.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Restoration of tax plus interest at 0.8% per month; administrative penalty up to 10% of the tax evaded; criminal penalties up to EUR 25,000 fine and imprisonment for up to 5 years (tax fraud by operator)

Director personal liability: The general partner (associé commandité) or statutory manager can be held jointly and severally liable for the entire tax, penalties, and interest. Luxembourg courts can issue a travel ban on the authorized representative and attach personal assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of the SCSp as a fully taxable resident entity (or as 'invalid for tax purposes'); retroactive reassessment for 10 years with penalty surcharge of 10% and interest

Director personal liability: The general partner may face civil liability for all back taxes and interest; if the court finds the arrangement was a sham, the corporate veil is pierced and the partner's personal residence and bank accounts become subject to attachment. Criminal prosecution for abuse of law may lead to imprisonment up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: 30% of the adjustment amount in administrative penalties (when documentation is insufficient); additional reassessment of taxable income; negative interest on late payment; automatic rejection of foreign tax credit claims

Director personal liability: The general partner who signed the declaration of conformity for the TP documentation can be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly submitted materially inaccurate or misleading data.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for AML violations under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004: up to EUR 1.25 million for legal entities and EUR 250,000 for individuals; criminal fine up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated money laundering

Director personal liability: Directors or the general partner face personal criminal liability if they knew or should have known of the money laundering. Luxembourg authorities can seek civil recovery of all proceeds, impose a travel ban, and order disgorgement of personal fees. In egregious cases, the general partner's existing personal debts in Luxembourg creditors' claims are subordinated to the State's claim.

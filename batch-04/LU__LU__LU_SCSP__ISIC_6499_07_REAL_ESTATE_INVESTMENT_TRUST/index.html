Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; partners are free to determine the capital contributions in the limited partnership agreement. No statutory escrow account requirement is imposed by the law on commercial companies; however, if the entity is a regulated real estate investment trust or alternative investment fund, separate capital/escrow rules under the relevant CSSF circular may apply.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: obtain a 'certificat de sécurité incendie' from the communal fire brigades (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours) after inspection of any built property, ensuring compliance with the Luxembourg fire safety standards (NFPA/EN norms). Structural load inspection: submit a structural integrity certificate from a licensed engineer if the property is over 50 years old or subject to major renovation; lease agreements must attach the certificate. Workplace clearance: register any occupied office space with the Labour Inspectorate (Inspection du Travail et des Mines, ITM) and obtain an occupational health clearance (annual meeting, safety plan, and, if applicable, a health-risk assessment) covering indoor air quality and emergency evacuation for any employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Luxembourg tax authorities are (i) the Administration des Contributions Directes (Direct Tax Administration / Luxembourg Inland Revenue, ACD), responsible for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and personal income tax, and (ii) the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Administration, AEDT), responsible for VAT and registration duties. Both report to the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance. Official portals: www.impotsdirects.public.lu (ACD), aedt.public.lu (AEDT), www.guichet.public.lu / MyGuichet.lu (central e-filing gateway), the e-TVA and e-Space electronic VAT channels, and lbr.lu (Luxembourg Business Registers).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or investing through a Luxembourg SCSp must (1) register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the Opoce one-stop shop (RCS immatriculation), obtaining the RCS registration number and the national identification number (matricule) which the ACD and AEDT use as the tax file reference. (2) For VAT, the SCSp, or in the case of an SCSp without legal personality the general partner as statutory representative, must file VAT Form 10 (déclaration d'immatriculation à la taxe sur la valeur ajoutée) with the AEDT through the e-TVA/MyGuichet channel to receive the Luxembourg VAT number (format LU+8 digits). A non-EU-established general partner or foreign operator carrying out taxable supplies or letting Luxembourg real estate has no registration threshold and must register from the first euro and appoint a Luxembourg-resident joint VAT representative (représentant responsable solidaire) where it has no Luxembourg or EU establishment. (3) Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, it does not obtain an autonomous Luxembourg corporate income tax ID; the general partner maintains the ACD file for the partnership, and non-resident partners are registered with the ACD only upon filing a Luxembourg tax return or where Luxembourg-source allocated income is subject to assessment or withholding.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The Luxembourg SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale, special limited partnership) is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg direct tax law: it is not itself liable to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), or net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). Profits, losses, capital gains, credits, and net-wealth items flow through pro rata to partners and are taxed at partner level in their respective states of residence, or in Luxembourg only to the extent attributed to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg-situated real property. Entity_Level_Taxation therefore does not apply, Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment is unavailable, and the ordinary Territorial_Scope of taxation arises solely at partner level. — A foreign entity establishing or investing through a Luxembourg SCSp must (1) register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the Opoce one-stop shop (RCS immatriculation), obtaining the RCS registration number and the national identification number (matricule) which the ACD and AEDT use as the tax file reference. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Luxembourg SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale, special limited partnership) is fiscally transparent under Luxembourg direct tax law: it is not itself liable to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), or net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). Profits, losses, capital gains, credits, and net-wealth items flow through pro rata to partners and are taxed at partner level in their respective states of residence, or in Luxembourg only to the extent attributed to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg-situated real property. Entity_Level_Taxation therefore does not apply, Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment is unavailable, and the ordinary Territorial_Scope of taxation arises solely at partner level.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law provides no general domestic 'check-the-box' election; transparency of the SCSp is statutory (Law of 12 July 2013 and Article 33-2 et seq. of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended). The partnership deed may elect whether the SCSp has legal personality or not, with identical fiscal transparency in both cases. Qualifying pooled real-estate funds may elect the 2024 Investment Fund Exemption (Law of 10 July 2024, effective for tax years from 1 January 2025), which exempts eligible funds meeting statutory investor-spread, diversification, and professional-management conditions from Luxembourg CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. For hybrid planning, ATAD 2 (implemented by Luxembourg laws of 21 December 2017 and 19 December 2019) governs hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatches: where a foreign investor files a US Form 8832 check-the-box election for the SCSp (transparent or opaque), the ACD respects that foreign classification only where substance and contemporaneous documentation support it, and the resulting Luxembourg treatment must be disclosed in the partnership's annual transparency filings. No statutory election converts the SCSp into an entity-level taxed company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (VAT-exclusive) small-enterprise exemption (franchise en base, Art. 57 Loi TVA), below which established suppliers may be relieved from VAT charging and registration. Non-established foreign operators, suppliers of services connected with Luxembourg immovable property, and taxable persons making intra-EU acquisitions have no threshold and must register from the first euro. For intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C digital services by other EU traders, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 OSS aggregation threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 152 of the Loi TVA, supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported out of European Union customs territory are zero-rated (0%) if the supplier obtains and retains the customs export declaration (DES/EXS with Movement Reference Number) and transport evidence showing the goods left the EU within 90 days of supply. Intra-EU B2B supplies (Art. 153 Loi TVA) qualify for 0% only where the customer's valid EU VAT ID is held and the EC Sales List is filed on time. For the SCSp real-estate trust: letting and services connected with immovable property are taxable only where the property is situated, so rental flows from non-Luxembourg real estate are outside Luxembourg VAT rather than zero-rated; Luxembourg commercial property rentals are exempt under Art. 44 Loi TVA, subject to the option to tax under Art. 50 Loi TVA for lettings to taxable persons. Management, advisory, holding, and financing services rendered by the Luxembourg SCSp to non-EU taxable investors are outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the B2B place-of-supply rule (customer location). Real property itself cannot be 'exported' for VAT purposes; only movable assets and qualifying cross-border services can benefit from the 0% rate.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD; DES/EXS electronic filing) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) evidencing exit from EU customs territory
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill, dated and consigned to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and proof of payment (SWIFT MT103/MT210 or Bank Inward Remittance Certificate) demonstrating foreign-currency receipt and export proceeds repatriation

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, government-API invoice stamping, or mandated SAF-T digital reporting for B2B transactions. Since 2022, business-to-government (B2G) e-invoicing is mandatory: suppliers invoicing Luxembourg public-sector entities must transmit structured e-invoices (PEPPOL BIS 3.0; UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT CII) through certified PEPPOL access points, using the State's free 'e-Space' exchange platform as an available channel. Invoices must be retained for six (6) years. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform agreed in June 2025, Luxembourg will phase in mandatory e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies from 1 July 2030, with domestic B2B e-invoicing aligned in the same window; until the mandatory regime enters into force, paper/PDF invoicing remains valid and no cryptographic stamp, QR code, or clearance API is required.

The AEDT conducts targeted substance audits of exporters, VAT refund claimants, and SCSp investment vehicles relying on zero-rating or cross-border VAT treatments. Missing, contradictory, or false substantiation triggers retroactive cancellation of the 0% rate, reassessment at the standard 17% VAT rate with arrears interest, clawback of refunds and VAT credit balances, and administrative fines under Arts. 65–67 Loi TVA, which may reach 100% of the evaded tax. Fraudulent export or refund claims additionally expose the general partner, managers, and investors to criminal prosecution before Luxembourg criminal courts (Art. 101 Loi TVA and the tax-fraud provisions of the Criminal Code), including substantial fines and imprisonment for natural persons, disqualification from public procurement, and revocation of fund-management licenses; cross-border cases may be coordinated with OLAF and Eurofisc. The assessment and prescription window is generally three to five years from the filing due date, extended up to ten (10) years for proven fraud or concealment, and the taxable person bears the full burden of proving the physical export or foreign place-of-supply while retaining the complete evidential bundle for six (6) years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member with full, unrestricted current-account convertibility under EU Treaty Article 63 TFEU and the free movement of capital. No exchange controls apply to capital or current transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, and investor redemptions are free of FX quotas or approvals. The only compliance layers are standard tax withholding mechanics, CRS/FATCA reporting, and financial sanctions screening. For a real estate investment trust structured as an SCSp, all income streams (rents, capital gains, interest) are fully convertible into EUR and may be distributed without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. All export and service proceeds are freely cross-creditable within the EU payment clearing infrastructure. BCL notification applies only for certain balance-of-payments statistics and does not impose any delivery or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately viable for ancillary payment flows; CSSF-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions may support account servicing, transaction screening, and SEPA/ACH payments for operational treasury. However, offshore non-EU EMIs are legally restricted from providing banking services without a Luxembourg passport or a valid EU cross-border license under PSD2. Given the size, reputational exposure, and regulatory expectations of a real estate investment trust, a traditional tier-1 bank remains the only fully acceptable venue for capital holding, large inbound/outbound transfers, and FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital contributions from foreign investors must be routed via SWIFT MT103, entering the Eurozone through Target2 clearing. The wire must reference the SCSp's IBAN and include a commercial purpose code consistent with the CSSF and Luxembourg statistics framework: typically 'FDI – Equity Capital' or 'Shares and Other Equity' under the EU Balance of Payments classification. Intermediary banks must be OFAC-compliant and pre-vetted for sanctions exposure. Inbound USD, GBP, or CHF funds are converted into EUR via the receiving bank's FX desk under a documented standing instruction, with each transaction subject to standard BCL statistical reporting thresholds.For real estate acquisitions, construction proceeds, and investor distributions, clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for all non-SEPA wire payments, including USD via CHIPS/Fedwire using the bank's correspondent network, and EUR via Target2 or SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT). Domestic and EEA distributions are cleared via SEPA and may use Instant SEPA (SCT Inst) where operational. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) and standby credits may be issued for construction, procurement, or acquisition milestones. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderately viable for ancillary payment flows; CSSF-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions may support account servicing, transaction screening, and SEPA/ACH payments for operational treasury. However, offshore non-EU EMIs are legally restricted from providing banking services without a Luxembourg passport or a valid EU cross-border license under PSD2. Given the size, reputational exposure, and regulatory expectations of a real estate investment trust, a traditional tier-1 bank remains the only fully acceptable venue for capital holding, large inbound/outbound transfers, and FX hedging.

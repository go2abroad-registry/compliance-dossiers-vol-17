Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg Inland Revenue; VAT administration: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); official government portal: https://guichet.lu; electronic tax filing portal: https://espace.public.lu; corporate registry: Registre de commerce et des sociétés (RCS) via https://www.lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SCSp must first file the partnership agreement and incorporation documents with the RCS. Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns a Luxembourg national tax identification number for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes. The SCSp must separately apply to the AEDT for a VAT identification number whenever it will carry on a taxable economic activity in Luxembourg and does not qualify for the small-business turnover exemption. Non-resident partners without a Luxembourg permanent establishment may need to register directly with the ACD, usually through a Luxembourg accredited tax agent, to obtain a local TIN for withholding tax and fiscal transparency reporting. Foreign legal persons that are not otherwise required to register may obtain a TIN by submitting a formal request to the ACD with notarised constitutional documents, proof of identity of managers/partners, and evidence of a Luxembourg registered office or tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory construction and cannot elect to be taxed at entity level. LuxSA, Lux S.à r.l., and other corporate vehicles can opt into the Luxembourg fiscal consolidation regime (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax where a 95% shareholding is held by a fully taxable resident parent; transparent partnerships cannot be the consolidating parent. The SCSp may, however, elect VAT grouping membership as part of an assujetti unique group if strong financial, economic, and organisational integration exists. Taxpayers may also apply to the ACD for a formal advance tax ruling confirming the transparency treatment or the absence of a permanent establishment. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/MLI can override partnership transparency if the structure is used to achieve double non-taxation; no elective exception is permitted for that outcome.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member with full capital account and current account convertibility under EU treaty freedoms (TFEU Art. 63) and domestic law. SCSp vehicles may freely convert, remit, and repatriate profits, premiums, claims, and investment proceeds in any currency, subject to EU sanctions, AML/KYC verification, FATCA/CRS reporting, and the substance/anti-abuse rules applied by the Luxembourg tax authorities. No exchange control licensing, surrender, or central bank approval is required for captive reinsurance premium remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender, conversion quotas, or repatriation timelines. All distributions, dividends, and insurance-related proceeds from an SCSp account may be transferred abroad without central bank filing, except where a wire crosses a sanctioned jurisdiction or a payment institution requires a specific EU payment purpose code for statistical balance of payments reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for prudential and AML/CFT supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability. Luxembourg-regulated EMI/payment institution licenses (under CSSF) may be used for EUR collection of small premium payments or Treasury sub-accounts, but an SCSp captive reinsurance vehicle should maintain its primary settlement and reserve accounts at a full-scope credit institution to ensure SWIFT clearing, segregated client money treatment, FATCA/CRS golden-source reporting, and buy-side acceptance by licensed reinsurers. Non-EU/EEA offshore digital EMIs are not viable primary rails because they lack Luxembourg banking licence status, acceptable correspondent clearing relationships, and the ability to hold multi-currency premium reserves in segregated low-risk accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital and premium injections are effected through SWIFT MT103 or MT202/MT102 batch transfers, crediting a dedicated EUR or USD IBAN. Each wire must clearly reference the reinsurance treaty number and a validated payment purpose code: for equity contributions use 'CAPC' or ISO 20022 'CAPC'; for premium income use 'INSA' (Insurance Premium) or 'REINS' (Reinsurance Premium) unless the bank specifies '13000' under ECB BPM6 balance-of-payments codification; for intercompany loans or funding use 'INTS' or 'LOAN'. For contributions exceeding EUR 30,000, the bank will require source-of-funds declarations, underlying capital call or policy documents, and sanctions verification before making funds available.Outbound indemnity claims and premium ceding are executed via SWIFT MT103 (single) or MT102 (bulk) with purpose code 'INSA' or 'REINS' and a policy/claims reference. Larger reserve-backed transfers may use TARGET2 for EUR and SWIFT correspondent through the bank’s clearing network for USD/GBP/CHF. For irrevocable trade settlements, commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) or standby LCs (ISP 98) can be issued by the Tier-1 Luxembourg bank to support the group's captive insurance collateral obligations; internal treasury sweeps to ultimate parent accounts use MT101/MT102 with 'INTC' and evidence of arm's-length intercompany settlement.Outright FX forwards (FXA) with Luxembourg/EU bank counterparties under ISDA 2002 or local FX master agreementsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, KRW) for premium flows originating from operating subsidiaries outside the EUCross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) to manage long-term reinsurance reserve liabilities in a different currencyFX swaps (tom-next/spot-next) for short-term liquidity management and premium funding alignmentCurrency options (FXO) on a limited notional basis for hedging adverse FX moves in reserve-linked claim exposures, subject to the SCSp’s treasury policy and CSSF derivative eligibilityCommodity and index-linked derivatives are not permissible unless directly hedging captive policy risk; generic speculative derivatives are excluded by corporate treasury mandate

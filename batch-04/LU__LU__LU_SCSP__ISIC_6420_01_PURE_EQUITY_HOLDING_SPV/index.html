Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés), RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) and RESA (Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations); filings made electronically via eJustice.lu.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (LSC), Articles 125-1 to 125-35 (Special Limited Partnership / SCSp) inserted by the Law of 23 July 2016; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE); Grand-Ducal Regulation of 25 February 2021 on RCS filing requirements.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution — the SCSp articles of limited partnership agreement executed before a Luxembourg notary, filed with the RCS and published in RESA; legal personality arises on the day of RCS registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum capital, no paid-up capital threshold and no escrow or blocked-account requirement for an SCSp. The limited partner's contribution is freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement; capital may be contributed in cash, in kind or as services and remaining commitments are contractual.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable — the SCSp has no physical plant and no employees. Where a registered office is hosted by a serviced-office provider, the landlord must maintain a valid fire-safety certificate, building occupancy permit and compliance with Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) requirements; no Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) workplace clearance is triggered.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum capital, no paid-up capital threshold and no escrow or blocked-account requirement for an SCSp. The limited partner's contribution is freely fixed in the limited partnership agreement; capital may be contributed in cash, in kind or as services and remaining commitments are contractual.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified e-Government portal: myguichet.lu / guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

1) Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the business registration number (matricule), which functions as the tax identification number for the entity. 2) If the SCSp needs a VAT identification number, file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, after registration at the LBR. 3) Non-resident general partners and partners must obtain a Luxembourg personal tax matricule from the ACD through the local commune administration. 4) A foreign entity that is not established in Luxembourg but makes taxable supplies must appoint an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. 5) No minimum capital or physical office requirement is imposed for an SCSp, but substance evidence should be maintained.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) Register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the business registration number (matricule), which functions as the tax identification number for the entity. 2) If the SCSp needs a VAT identification number, file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, after registration at the LBR. 3) Non-resident general partners and partners must obtain a Luxembourg personal tax matricule from the ACD through the local commune administration. 4) A foreign entity that is not established in Luxembourg but makes taxable supplies must appoint an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. 5) No minimum capital or physical office requirement is imposed for an SCSp, but substance evidence should be maintained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. An SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no domestic election exists to render it opaque. Affirmative elections are limited to VAT grouping (assujetti unique) and certain participation exemption holdings; hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD and EU Directive 2016/1164 operate mandatorily. A taxable entity-level regime would require conversion into a corporate vehicle such as a SOPARFI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the small business franchise exemption; mandatory VAT registration is required for taxable persons exceeding this threshold, and in any event for businesses supplying taxable goods or services where input VAT deduction is requested. Intra-Community distance sales to Luxembourg consumers become registrable above the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold unless an option is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of movable goods dispatched or transported to a third country or territory qualifies for a 0% VAT rate provided the goods have left Luxembourg and the customs territory of the EU within 90 days of supply, the supplier retains documentary proof of dispatch/transport, and a valid Luxembourg customs export declaration has been lodged. Selected services relating to international trade (e.g., transport of goods to third countries, handling and storage incidental to export, and services directly linked to exports) may also be zero-rated. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated subject to valid VAT identification numbers and filing of a recapitulative statement.

  • Luxembourg Customs electronic export declaration (e-DEU/DAU) and the customs movement reference number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or airway bill evidencing physical export from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Proof of export delivery or customs exit confirmation
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment evidence matching the invoiced amount

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or transaction-level API reporting exists for B2B invoices under Luxembourg VAT. E-invoicing is required only in the B2G segment, where invoices to Luxembourg public sector entities must be issued in a structured electronic format (PEPPOL) through the national procurement platform. Ordinary VAT invoices may be transmitted electronically subject to authenticity and integrity controls.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate and reassess VAT at 17% when export documentation is missing, unreliable, or forged, or when the goods did not actually leave the EU within the prescribed time frame. Interest on late VAT, administrative fines up to EUR 25,000, and penalties up to double the evaded VAT may apply for negligent or intentional offences. In cases of fraudulent export schemes, criminal prosecution, imprisonment, joint liability of directors and partners, and indefinite or extended statutory limitation periods apply. The exporter is also liable to repay any input VAT previously credited against the zero-rated supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63-66 TFEU. No Luxembourg controls impede profit remittance, dividends, interest, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds from the SCSp to non-residents. The transparent SCSp itself suffers no withholding tax; only tax-resident partners may trigger standard withholding and CRS/FATCA reporting. No FX permit, quota, or central-bank pre-approval is applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists: Luxembourg imposes no timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation threshold for export receipts or any commercial proceeds. Multi-currency proceeds may be held indefinitely in resident or offshore accounts, converted freely at market rates, or re-deployed directly into target investments without BCL approval; this is immaterial to a passive ISIC 6420 holding SPV.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the eurozone national central bank acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and the TARGET2 settlement framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) exercises banking licensing and AML/CFT supervision, while the BCL operates balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational disbursements and LOW for capital escrow and treasury concentration. An EEA-authorised e-money institution or payment institution may passport into Luxembourg and supply multi-currency IBANs, SEPA, and wire rails without a Luxembourg credit-institution licence; however, an SCSp cannot legally rely on a non-EEA 'offshore digital EMI' because settling capital calls and safeguarding escrowed funds requires a CSSF-licensed credit institution under the Law of 22 May 2019 on payment services and the amended Law of 8 January 1993 on the financial sector. Such offshore EMIs are legally unavailable in Luxembourg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EUR injections settle same-day in central-bank money over TARGET2 via the Luxembourg beneficiary bank, instructed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer. Instructions must carry the Luxembourg BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for intra-group equity capital and must reference the capital-call notice; the receiving bank applies EU/UN (and US OFAC where relevant) sanctions screening, verifies remitter and UBO documentation, source-of-funds evidence, and the funding chain, and books the credit to the partnership's capital ledger under the LPA. Non-EUR tranches (USD, GBP, CHF) clear through SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV cover via CHIPS/Fedwire, CHAPS, or SIC correspondents and convert at the bank's spot fixing; no Luxembourg FX declaration is required.The passive equity SPV ordinarily issues no trade disbursements; downstream funding to target operating companies is cleared via SWIFT MT103 over TARGET2/SEPA for EUR, and MT202 COV through US/UK/Swiss correspondent rails for foreign currency. Where documentary credit is used for underlying acquisitions or target trade flows, the rail is a commercial letter of credit issued by SWIFT MT700, amended by MT707, negotiated under MT752, and reimbursed by MT103/MT202 COV, all under UCP 600. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level (fiscally transparent special limited partnership; partners are assessable). For a comparable corporate tax payer in Luxembourg City, aggregate CIT rate is 24.94% (17% CIT plus 7% solidarity surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for SCSp profit distributions because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and no dividend withholding tax applies at partnership level. Were the vehicle capitalized as a corporation, the statutory dividend WHT is 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying ≥10% holdings and under most Luxembourg tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg's CbCR and Master File requirements apply to a multinational group with consolidated revenue of €750 million or above. A Local File is expected when a Luxembourg entity, including a pure holding SCSp, engages in controlled transactions exceeding €1 million annually, or financial transactions exceeding €100,000, consistent with Luxembourg TP administrative guidance.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose quantitative Economic Substance Regulations, but EU-ATAD and OECD/BEPS principles require genuine commercial substance. A passive SCSp must maintain a physical Luxembourg office, resident managers, local board minutes, a Luxembourg bank account, and decision-making records. Failure to demonstrate such substance may result in denial of treaty benefits and participation exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable base, default interest at 0.6% monthly, fines from 20% to 50% of under-declared tax; criminal fines for tax fraud typically range from €10,000 to €1 million.

Director personal liability: Managing partners are jointly and severally liable in civil restitution; courts may disregard legal personality and pierce the corporate veil upon fraud, imposing director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 1 year in aggravated cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the SCSp for Luxembourg tax purposes; denial of participation exemption and treaty benefits; recharacterization of outbound distributions as taxable income; administrative penalties up to 10% of the recomputed tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the SCSp is an artificial construction; each director and manager is exposed to civil liability under Luxembourg company law (law of 10 August 1915), restitution orders, disqualification, and travel prohibitions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004, fines up to €1 million and penal sanctions; separate RBE non-disclosure fines up to €250,000 and publication of the violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal fines, freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 2 years; regulators may also impose debarment from holding management functions in Luxembourg entities.

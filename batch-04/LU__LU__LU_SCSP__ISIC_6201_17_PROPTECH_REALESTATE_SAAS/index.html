Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes - ACD) for direct taxes and the Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA - AEDT) for VAT. Official portals: myguichet.lu and backoffice.etat.lu.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors must first file the SCSp constituent deed and articles of association with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a notary, obtain the 6-digit Luxembourg business registration number (matricule), appoint at least one Luxembourg resident manager, then apply to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number. The ACD uses the same matricule for corporate and partnership tax files.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. An SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg income tax purposes and is not itself subject to corporate income tax or net wealth tax; profits and losses are allocated to and taxed at partner level. For VAT/SaaS supplies, the SCSp is the taxable person supplying electronic services, subject to the normal place-of-supply rules.

Available election forms

There is no general check-the-box election for an SCSp; Luxembourg statutory law prescribes tax transparency. Limited elections/regimes available include the Luxembourg VAT group election (tax unity) for related Luxembourg taxable persons, and the anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Luxembourg Income Tax Law that must be observed to prevent deduction/no-inclusion outcomes. An SCSp cannot elect to be treated as an opaque corporate entity for Luxembourg tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover excluding VAT for most supplies of goods and services. For EU cross-border distance sales and B2C digital services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies, requiring registration or use of the EU One-Stop-Shop above that threshold; non-established third-country suppliers of digital services normally have no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Luxembourg applies 0% VAT to physical exports of goods where the goods are dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, supported by export proof. Export of B2B SaaS/cloud services to taxable customers established outside the EU is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and qualifies for zero-rating if the customer is a non-EU taxable person and no EU effective-use nexus arises; intra-EU B2B services are taxable at the customer's place of establishment, requiring normal invoicing and Intrastat/OSS reporting where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/UA or electronic export declaration via the EU Customs Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice with an export reference and proof of sale
  • Proof of payment / bank transfer, and where requested, Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax control mandate. Invoices must comply with VAT Directive rules and the Luxembourg VAT law; electronic invoicing is permitted and accepted as standard. For B2G transactions, e-invoicing in PEPPOL-BIS 3.0 format is required for Luxembourg public entities, with mandatory e-archiving and accessibility for the tax authorities.

The AEDT may require substantiation of export zero-rating; failure to provide valid transport, customs, and payment documentation within the requisite period triggers reassessment at the standard Luxembourg VAT rate of 17%, plus default interest, late-payment penalties of up to 20% of the VAT due, and a 10% corrective penalty for under-declared VAT. Fraudulent export zero-rating can give rise to criminal fines of EUR 1,000 to EUR 25,000, confiscation of goods, and partner/manager personal liability; retrospective clawbacks apply for five tax years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or bad faith.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full liberalisation of both capital and current-account transactions under Articles 63–66 TFEU and EU Capital Movements Directive 88/361/EEC, as implemented. The euro is fully convertible, with no compulsory licensing, prior-approval, surrender, or repatriation requirements for capital or profit flows. An SCSp is tax-transparent, so profit distributions flow directly to partners without Luxembourg withholding tax; residual remittance hurdles arise only from the partner-resident jurisdiction's tax, FATCA/CRS, or local anti-money-laundering rules. The EU safeguard clause for non-EU capital movements is legislatively available but is not currently engaged for routine cross-border SaaS subscription revenues or foreign partner capital injections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or minimum repatriation percentage applies in Luxembourg. Exporters and SaaS service providers may freely invoice, receive, hold, and convert foreign currency receipts. The only binding compliance obligation is ex-post statistical declaration of cross-border transactions to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, triggered individually or in aggregate at the EUR 12,500 per-transaction threshold, plus standard tax filings such as VAT recapitulative statements and Intrastat reporting where the underlying scope applies. The SCSp's platform revenue from PropTech software subscriptions is classified as services (ISIC 6201) and is not subject to goods-export surrender regimes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem, is the central bank and the competent balance-of-payments statistical authority. Prudential supervision of credit institutions and AML/CFT conduct oversight are concurrently exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg has no separate national foreign-exchange control authority, as capital and payment liberalisation is governed by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and CSSF/BCL joint circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High-to-moderate feasibility under EU PSD2 passporting. A CSSF-licensed electronic money institution or an EEA-passported EMI may lawfully open settlement, collection, and disbursement accounts for the SCSp without full commercial-bank credit underwriting, and may process the target Stripe_Billing and ACH_Direct rails. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs are legally constrained in Luxembourg because CSSF-regulated banks will not recognise them as qualified payment-system participants for capital deposits, FX conversion, or sub-account maintenance, and the SCSp would face UBO transparency and source-of-funds rejection at the receiving Luxembourg bank. The recommended structure is dual: (i) a CSSF-licensed or EEA-passported EMI for the ACH/USD collection concentration overlay, and (ii) a Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial bank for the EUR operational and capital escrow accounts, with daily treasury sweeps between them.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for non-resident partner contributions: the contributing partner instructs a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 interbank transfer for high-value contributions) from its bank to the SCSp's EUR operational or capital escrow account at BCEE, BIL, or Société Générale Luxembourg, with field 70 remittance information stating 'Capital contribution – Article [X] of the SCSp partnership agreement – ISIC 6201 PropTech SaaS platform'. The Luxembourg receiving bank declares the transaction to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics where the per-transaction aggregate threshold of EUR 12,500 is met. For injections exceeding EUR 50,000, the CSSF AML file must evidence a continuous source-of-funds trail and the partner bank's transfer confirmation. Non-EUR injections, particularly USD, are converted by the receiving bank under standard FX execution at the prevailing ECB reference rate, with no BCL surrender or licensing obligation. For inbound US ACH collections, the recommended routing is through a US-based USD concentration account (or Stripe Treasury settlement account) followed by a same-day SWIFT MT103 credit to the Luxembourg EUR account using purpose code 'ISIC 6201 SaaS subscription revenues / B2B software services', ensuring the funds are classified as current-account services rather than capital movement.Outbound settlement rails are as follows: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for high-value vendor payments, profit distributions to partners, and non-EUR disbursements in USD, GBP, or CHF; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and MT101 batch instructions for routine disbursements such as cloud-hosting fees, subcontractor payments, and payroll through the operational account; (3) UCP600-compliant commercial letters of credit and standby letters of credit for any physical Proptech hardware resale or escrow-linked vendor obligations; (4) FX spot or derivative settlements through approved EU investment firms or the relationship bank's treasury desk, subject to EMIR and MiFID II reporting. The unique clearing bottleneck is ACH_Direct: as a US NACHA rail, ACH cannot be cleared by Luxembourg banks; therefore the SCSp must maintain either a US physical-bank account or a US-licensed EMI/Stripe account as a concentration overlay, with daily treasury sweeps to Luxembourg to avoid prolonged foreign balance exposure and to align with the EUR operating currency.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP deliverable FX forwards under IFEMA or ISDA 1992 master agreements, documented through the Luxembourg bank treasury desk, with the general partner expressly authorised by the SCSp partnership agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible emerging-market currency exposures arising from partner capital calls or vendor payments; permissible but subject to EMIR classification of the SCSp as a non-financial counterparty below clearing thresholds, with mandatory risk-mitigation and trade-reporting obligations.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term hedging of USD-denominated ACH subscription streams or foreign partner capital contributions, executed under an ISDA Credit Support Annex with adequate collateral documentation.Vanilla FX options (put and call) for downside protection on subscription revenues in USD and GBP, purchased only from regulated EU investment firms or Luxembourg credit institutions.Daily FX spot conversion of US ACH proceeds into EUR through the concentration-account overlay, executed as a standing instruction to the Luxembourg bank to neutralise conversion-rate leakage.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% base Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) plus 7% solidarity surcharge, producing an aggregate CIT rate of 22.47%; with municipal business tax (communal trade tax) at the Luxembourg City multiplier of 6.75%, the combined statutory burden is 29.22%. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, so the CIT is imposed at partner level on each partner's allocable share of the PropTech SaaS income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions from a Luxembourg taxable entity. For a fiscally transparent SCSp, no entity-level withholding arises on distributions to partners unless the ACD treats the SCSp as opaque following an ATAD GAAR recharacterization or a corporate-taxation election. EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief: 0% on qualifying holdings of at least 10% held for 12 months; LU-US and other DTA rates commonly 5% for 10%+ holdings and 15% otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline: 30 June of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end. The ACD routinely grants an automatic extension to 31 December of that same year for e-filed returns; for assessment cycles 2023 and 2024 the standard grace deadline is 31 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the Luxembourg SCSp's controlled transactions exceed EUR 100,000 per category for goods or IP, or EUR 300,000 per category for services. Master File: mandatory where the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and the Luxembourg entity's controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: applicable at the EUR 750,000,000 group turnover threshold. Documentation must be produced within 45 days of an ACD request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the ACD applies an equivalent economic-substance doctrine through: (i) the amended law of 17 March 2017 requiring financing and holding entities to maintain local premises, local board decision-making, qualified directors and adequate staff; (ii) the ATAD general anti-abuse rule (Art. 6ah of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law) which permits recharacterization of artificial arrangements; and (iii) treaty and EU-Directive beneficial-ownership and limitation-on-benefits tests. A transparent SCSp claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate that the partnership and its partners have real activity, substance and decision-making power in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud (délit fiscal)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 402 of the Luxembourg General Tax Code, attempted tax fraud is punished by a fine of up to EUR 250,000 or double the evaded tax, whichever is greater; completed fraud is punishable by imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years and/or the same fine; restitution of evaded tax plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest is mandatory. Repeat offenders face doubling of fines and extended custodial terms.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing applies: general partners and managers who knowingly organized the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the concealed tax. Luxembourg courts additionally impose travel bans, commercial-management prohibitions (interdiction de gérer) of up to 10 years, asset freezes and custodial sentences on the responsible natural persons. EU and Schengen mutual-assistance instruments enable cross-border enforcement of freeze, seizure and confiscation orders.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits and of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 0% rate; the ACD recharacterizes all distributions as constructively Luxembourg-taxable and applies the 15% withholding tax plus 7.2% p.a. default interest; administrative anti-shell fines up to EUR 250,000 are imposed on the general partner; the OECD shell-entity information-exchange framework triggers automatic disclosure to the investor's home tax authority.

Director personal liability: General partners are treated as tax representatives and are personally assessed for unpaid withholding tax, fines and interest. Civil restitution of repatriated profits is ordered against the partners; travel bans are issued where a partner attempts to leave the EU; criminal convictions for forgery follow where sham leases, sub-leases or employment contracts were manufactured to create artificial substance.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Margin Erosion

Statutory sanction: Automatic upward adjustment of the taxable base to the arm's-length amount; penalty of 10% of the adjusted tax differential plus 7.2% p.a. interest; in flagrant cases the excess is recharacterized as a hidden dividend distribution subject to the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, generating double-inclusion and potential ATAD interest-limitation consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return without verifying the underlying transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for the 10% penalty where gross negligence is proven. A Luxembourg commercial court may appoint a judicial administrator over the SCSp, prohibit the manager from engaging in commercial activity for up to 10 years, and enforce personal liability for unpaid withholding tax where the manager knew the documentation was fictitious or the agreements were backdated.
AML and Payment-Rails Non-Compliance (PropTech / Real-Estate SaaS)

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 as amended, institutional violations attract fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and director fines up to EUR 250,000; money-laundering predicated on tax evasion is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment; the CSSF and the CRF may suspend the entity's payment processing, freeze its bank accounts and revoke any required professional authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are personally criminally liable for AML violations. Civil courts permit tax authorities and victims to pierce the SCSp veil and attach the personal assets of partners who knowingly controlled the payment flows; international travel bans and asset freezes are issued under EU mutual recognition; serious or repeated violations lead to permanent disqualification from serving as a director of any Luxembourg or EU-regulated company.

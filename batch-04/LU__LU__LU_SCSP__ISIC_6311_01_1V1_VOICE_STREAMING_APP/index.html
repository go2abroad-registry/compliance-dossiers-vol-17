Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SCSp has no minimum statutory capital, no mandatory paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked-account requirement under Luxembourg law. The partnership agreement (contrat de société) freely determines the partners' contributions, which may be in cash, in kind, or as a contribution promise.

Funding & unlock path: Office-use clearance: obtain a fire safety certificate pursuant to the Grand-Ducal regulation on fire protection for the registered office, verify workplace health and safety compliance with the Luxembourg Labour Code (Code du travail), and secure an inspection clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM). No industrial structural-load inspection or special civil-defense permit applies to a software-only office occupation.

Paid-in capital rule: SCSp has no minimum statutory capital, no mandatory paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked-account requirement under Luxembourg law. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority; registration portal: MyGuichet.lu (official e-Government platform for tax and business registration).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must appoint a Luxembourg resident authorized representative or tax agent, file the incorporation/registration form with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu, obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (matricule), and then register with the Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for the national tax identification number (NIF). The ACD issues a permanent tax ID following submission of the notarized deed, partnership agreement, proof of registered office, and identification of partners/managers. In addition, the entity must register for VAT with the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) if taxable activities commence in Luxembourg or if distance sales/electronic services to Luxembourg consumers exceed the applicable registration threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entities must appoint a Luxembourg resident authorized representative or tax agent, file the incorporation/registration form with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu, obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (matricule), and then register with the Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for the national tax identification number (NIF). The ACD issues a permanent tax ID following submission of the notarized deed, partnership agreement, proof of registered office, and identification of partners/managers. In addition, the entity must register for VAT with the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) if taxable activities commence in Luxembourg or if distance sales/electronic services to Luxembourg consumers exceed the applicable registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg corporate income tax purposes; no check-the-box election is required to establish pass-through treatment. The partnership agreement and the allocation of profits and voting rights determine the partners' deemed Luxembourg establishment status. However, if a partner is a Luxembourg-resident corporate entity subject to CIT, its share of SCSp income remains taxable at the partner level. No hybrid mismatch election is available to alter the fiscal transparency of an SCSp, although Luxembourg applies controlled foreign company and anti-hybrid rules consistent with EU ATAD as implemented in the Luxembourg domestic code. Parties may structure the SCSp as either a commercial or non-commercial partnership, which affects whether it is subject to Luxembourg municipal business tax (commune trade tax) at partnership level for business activity; if the SCSp carries on a commercial undertaking and has a permanent establishment deemed to exist via the general partner, it falls within the scope of the municipal business tax, with tax transparency for CIT purposes preserved.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for Luxembourg-resident businesses in certain low-value supplies; for foreign suppliers of telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic services (including voice streaming apps sold to non-taxable persons within Luxembourg), no general registration threshold applies beyond the EU cross-border threshold of EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales if the supplier is established in another EU Member State; non-EU suppliers have no threshold and must register or use the OSS for non-EU supplier registration for EU B2C electronic services. For Luxembourg domestic and other supplies, the mandatory registration threshold for established businesses is EUR 35,000 (for small business exemption); otherwise registration is obligatory irrespective of turnover once taxable supplies commence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods dispatched to non-EU destinations and to certain supplies of services connected with export (e.g., freight and related services, plus supplies of digital or electronically supplied services may be not zero-rated but rather taxed at the place of the customer if B2C; zero-rating for export goods is available only for direct exports of movable goods where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory. For the voice streaming app, if the service is provided to a non-EU business customer outside the EU, Luxembourg VAT rules and EU B2B place-of-supply rules treat the supply as outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (no Luxembourg VAT) if the customer is established outside the EU; if provided to non-EU private consumers, the place of supply remains the customer's residence (not Luxembourg), so no Luxembourg VAT applies, but this is not technically an export zero-rating claim; instead the supply falls outside the territorial scope of Luxembourg VAT. For tangible exports, zero-rating is subject to providing transport documents proving exit from the EU and the customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (formal customs acceptance/export accompanying document under EU Union Customs Code, with MRN and exit confirmation from the customs office of exit)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport document (airway bill, road consignment note, or shipping evidence) evidencing physical departure of goods from EU territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/credit institution payment proof showing the export proceeds transferred in foreign currency and received in Luxembourg or partner bank account)
  • Commercial invoice and order documentation matching the customs declaration and evidencing the service or goods supply
  • Supplier's VAT registration certificate and, where applicable, partner's foreign tax certificate supporting non-established customer status
  • Audit trail of digital service provision logs, IP location data, and customer records for offshore services

Luxembourg has not mandated a real-time transaction clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) system as of the knowledge cutoff. Luxembourg's B2B domestic e-invoicing is not compulsory on a national basis; however, e-invoices must be accepted where the recipient agrees, and since 2019, Luxembourg permits the exchange of electronic invoices that comply with the EU e-invoicing standard (EN 16931) under the PEPPOL network for public procurement. Luxembourg has implemented the EU cross-border e-invoicing obligation for supplies to Luxembourg public authorities via the B2G platform, using PEPPOL. There is no general API-based real-time fiscal stamping requirement for private-sector e-invoicing. All businesses must maintain authentic VAT invoices with required fields, and for digital services, invoices should identify the service and VAT treatment.

Substantiation penalties: unfounded or excessive VAT refunds or zero-rating claims are subject to recovery of underpaid VAT, interest at the statutory rate, and administrative fines up to EUR 4,000 per infringement, plus criminal penalties in cases of fraud or material concealment (prison sentence and monetary penalty up to EUR 15,000 for fraud under Article 92 of the Luxembourg VAT law). Retrospective clawback: the Luxembourg AEDT may reassess VAT for up to five years (extended to ten years in cases of fraud) and claw back zero-rated/VAT-free treatment with a 10% to 20% penalty alongside interest for late payment; fraudulent export zero-rating schemes are prosecuted under general criminal law and the VAT fraud provisions, with exposure to joint and several liability of directors and partners and potential loss of the right to deduct input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the sole legal tender; Luxembourg applies unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital accounts under Article 63 TFEU and the EU free-movement-of-capital regime, and no approval is required for profit remittance, distributions, or capital repatriation. For the SCSp, which is tax-transparent, partnership profit allocations to non-resident limited partners are not subject to currency-conversion licensing or Luxembourg withholding at allocation. Practical remittance constraints are limited to: (i) BCL external-statistics reporting obligations when flows exceed prescribed de minimis thresholds; (ii) funds-transfer information requirements under Regulation (EU) 2015/847; and (iii) the requirement that all end-user payments be routed solely through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, thereby avoiding the creation of an unlicensed payment institution under the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines exist in Luxembourg law. App-store collections of digital service export proceeds may be held in foreign currency indefinitely. The only administrative duties are BCL balance-of-payments external-statistics filings, which may require monthly, quarterly, or annual reporting depending on transaction volumes and counterparty residency, plus normal VAT and Intrastat declarations applicable to intra-EU digital services; neither imposes any FX surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem, supported by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, AML/CFT and payment-services supervision. Luxembourg has no separate foreign-exchange administration and BCL assumes external-statistics oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, for ancillary treasury operations only, not as a primary clearing rail. A European Economic Area electronic-money institution passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut Business, N26 Business) or a CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI may be opened for low-value SEPA payroll, vendor disbursements, and small multi-currency payments; however, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot support capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, SWIFT-gpi MT103 clearing for large settlements, or structured FX lines. Non-EEA EMIs (United Kingdom, United States, Hong Kong) are legally unable to provide euro-denominated account services without an EEA partner and should not be used for the SCSp's registered treasury relationship; their use would also raise enhanced-review concerns under CSSF Regulation 12-02 because limited partners would be exposed to an unregulated custodian of float.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound contributions are executed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 or higher-value real-time TARGET2 transfers from each limited partner's regulated bank account to the SCSp's Capital Escrow Account. Standard protocol: (1) the general partner issues a capital call notice carrying a unique reference and the exact amount; (2) the partner instructs its bank with structured remittance data including the SCSp legal name, capital call reference, partner legal name, and BCL external-statistics classification of the transfer as a direct-investment equity contribution; (3) the Luxembourg receiving bank performs sanctions screening and verifies that the originator account name exactly matches the limited partner in the subscription agreement; (4) upon matching of reference and amount, funds are credited to the segregated escrow account and a drawdown certificate signed by the general partner releases them to the operational account; and (5) every monthly cycle the BCL external-statistics report records the liability to non-resident partners under the direct-investment heading. For euro-denominated partner capital exceeding EUR 500,000 or for same-day deployment, TARGET2 is strongly preferred; all confirmations must preserve the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) to satisfy Regulation (EU) 2015/847 information requirements.The primary clearing rails are SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for euro disbursements within the EEA, and SWIFT MT103/MT202 with GPI end-to-end tracking for non-euro and non-EEA settlements. For high-value licensing agreements covering music rights, voice-AI models, or influencer streaming contracts, the disbursement structure may incorporate a sight Commercial Letter of Credit opened by the SCSp's bank, or a standby letter of credit when the counterparty requires a bank payment guarantee; documents are presented electronically under eUCP 2.0 where applicable. App-store proceeds (Apple IAP and Google Play Billing) are collected through Apple Distribution International and Google Ireland payout rails, settled in EUR, and concentrated into the operational account via a daily automatic sweep; any USD, GBP, JPY or KRW receipts are converted through a standing monthly FX contract rather than spot conversions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, for ancillary treasury operations only, not as a primary clearing rail. A European Economic Area electronic-money institution passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut Business, N26 Business) or a CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI may be opened for low-value SEPA payroll, vendor disbursements, and small multi-currency payments; however, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot support capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, SWIFT-gpi MT103 clearing for large settlements, or structured FX lines. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City 2025 aggregate nominal rate: 17.00% corporate income tax + 7.00% solidarity surcharge on the CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax on trading profit). The SCSp is itself exempt from entity-level CIT because, under Article 185 LIR and the law of 12 July 2013, the SCSp is fiscally transparent: its net income is allocated directly to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and taxed only in the hands of those partners; non-resident partners without a Luxembourg permanent establishment are not taxed in Luxembourg on their allocated share.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax for distributions by fully taxable Luxembourg resident companies. The transparent SCSp is not a withholding agent: distributions to its partners are look-through in nature, the character of the underlying income is preserved, and no Luxembourg WHT is charged at the partnership level. If the SCSp has opted for taxable status or is treated as an opaque entity in the partner's jurisdiction, distributions may be requalified and exposed to 15% WHT unless reduced under a treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg law contains no codified monetary de-minimis; Circular LIR 56/1/5 of 10 April 2023 requires contemporaneous OECD-aligned Local File and Master File documentation whenever material cross-border related-party transactions exist. For this SCSp, prepare a full Local File from the first euro of intercompany royalty or service flow; the practical audit trigger is aggregate intra-group transaction value above EUR 1,000,000 per annum, and a Master File is expected when the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance is dispositive for tax transparency and treaty benefits: (a) Article 6 StAnpG (Steueranpassungsgesetz) disregards artificial arrangements lacking a valid business purpose; (b) the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test denies DTA benefits to arrangements whose principal purpose is tax relief; (c) ATAD implementation (interest limitation rule, CFC rules and exit taxation) is fully in force; (d) the participation exemption requires genuine establishment and real economic activity of the underlying subsidiary. An SCSp operating a voice-streaming app must demonstrate physical presence in Luxembourg, actual decision-making by the gérant, substantive payroll and office costs proportionate to the revenue, a functioning Luxembourg bank account, and commercial substance behind the Apple IAP/Google Play Billing revenue flows; otherwise the tax administration may recharacterise the structure, assert a partner-level PE in Luxembourg and deny treaty relief on the app-store income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 985-987 LIR and the Luxembourg Criminal Code: imprisonment from 1 month to 5 years and criminal fines of up to EUR 25,000 per tax period for natural persons, with multiplied fines for legal persons; the tax administration additionally applies the 10% late-filing surcharge, monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%, and reopens the assessment period for up to 10 years in evasion cases.

Director personal liability: The gérant and any de facto manager are jointly and severally liable without limitation for the SCSp's tax debts; personal assets are directly reachable, the criminal conviction triggers disqualification from corporate offices, and the enforcement authorities may impose provisional asset freezes and travel bans.
Shell Company / Artificial Letterbox Structure

Statutory sanction: Application of the Luxembourg GAAR (Article 6 StAnpG) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test: the SCSp is disregarded, income is re-attributed to a Luxembourg or partner-level PE, treaty exemptions are denied, the participation exemption is revoked, and the resultant assessment carries 24.94% aggregate CIT plus 0.6% monthly late interest and a 10% surcharge; the partner's home jurisdiction may pursue CFC re-attribution.

Director personal liability: The general partner's unlimited liability exposes the individual's personal estate; civil restitution orders cover the unpaid tax, interest and penalties; in severe cross-border cases the courts can issue a professional ban and a travel restriction against the controlling shareholder.

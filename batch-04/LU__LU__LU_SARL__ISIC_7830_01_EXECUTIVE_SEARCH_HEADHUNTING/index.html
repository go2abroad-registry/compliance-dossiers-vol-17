Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Grand Duchy of Luxembourg — Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) is the competent VAT and indirect-tax authority; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) is the competent direct-tax authority; unified business e-government portals: MyGuichet.lu, guichet.lu and the secure business space (Espace sécurisé des entreprises / SSE), with AEDT electronic filing module eTVA and ACD eCDF portal.

E-government filing portal

Resident SARL: upon incorporation, the company automatically receives its national business registration number (matricule) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the RCS; this matricule is the statutory TIN for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and social-security reporting. For VAT, the SARL must apply with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu using the electronic VAT identification form within 15 days of commencing taxable supplies; the VAT number is issued as LU followed by 8 digits. Foreign entities: an EU-established business with Luxembourg taxable supplies — for which there is no turnover threshold — registers directly with the AEDT or uses the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) where the transaction qualifies. A non-EU-established business without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint an AEDT-approved Luxembourg VAT representative (mandataire TVA) and file a registration request together with certified evidence of home-country TIN and commercial registration, EORI data for goods transactions, authenticated identification of directors/members, and a power of attorney; the entire procedure is paperless and does not involve a US-style TIN application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Luxembourg SARL is a per se opaque and fully taxable capital company resident in Luxembourg; it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and, generally, net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) on worldwide income, with treaty relief, EU Directive relief and the participation-exemption regime; no pass-through or transparent classification is available at national level.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the SARL cannot elect transparent treatment. Available statutory elections and regimes: (a) tax unity for CIT under Art. 164bis LIR between a resident parent and resident subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at least 90%, with identical fiscal year-end, effective for an irrevocable 5-year block; (b) VAT grouping (assujetti unique, Art. 60 VAT Law), including one-person VAT grouping; (c) small-business VAT exemption election under Art. 57 VAT Law where annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000; (d) VAT option (assujettissement) for real-estate leasing and certain financial services under Art. 48 VAT Law; (e) participation-exemption regime for dividend and realised capital-gain income from qualifying shareholdings (at least 10% or, for dividend purposes, an acquisition price of at least EUR 1.2 million) held continuously for at least 12 months, subject to Art. 166/166bis LIR conditions; (f) ATAD 1/2 hybrid-mismatch rules apply mandatorily — no domestic election is available; (g) Pillar Two qualified domestic minimum top-up tax and international top-up tax apply from fiscal year 2024 to groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full euro convertibility and a completely liberalised cross-border payments environment; there are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements, and no distinction in practical treatment between current-account transactions (retainer fees, contractor settlements, intercompany service charges) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, share redemptions, liquidation distributions, loan repayments), consistent with TFEU Articles 63-65, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and the BCL/CSSF statistical reporting frame. Profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal rather than exchange-control in nature: (i) dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL are subject to a 15% domestic withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (qualifying parent holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period) or to lower treaty rates (commonly 5%); (ii) the underlying profit bears standard corporate income tax of 17%, municipal business tax of approximately 6.75% to 8.4% on the taxable profit of service companies, and a 0.5% net-wealth tax on net assets above the statutory threshold; and (iii) no waiting period, conversion obligation, or central-bank consent applies to the remittance itself, which can be executed immediately upon dividend payment approval by the shareholders' meeting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, forced repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for services exports, including executive-search and headhunting receipts. Retainer and success-fee invoices may be denominated and collected in EUR, USD, GBP, or any freely convertible currency; the SARL may retain balances onshore or offshore at its sole commercial discretion. The only administrative duty is indirect: the resident receiving bank captures cross-border credits for BCL balance-of-payments statistics (transactions in excess of the EUR 12,500 aggregation threshold are declared in the bank's BCL filings), and no corporate notification is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and coordinated with the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight are jointly discharged by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the BCL, with residual competence over EU/UN sanctions implementation and counter-terrorism financing measures reserved to the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Conditional-High as an ancillary payment rail; Medium as a primary treasury bank. The payload-mandated rail of Wise B2B (operated for EEA clients by Wise Europe SA, an e-money institution licensed under PSD2 and passporting into Luxembourg) is legally viable for retainer collections, vendor disbursements, and contractor settlements, including euro IBANs executed over SEPA and SWIFT-compatible non-EUR corridors. Material constraints: (i) the statutory share-capital deposit must be evidenced by a blocked-account certificate issued by a Luxembourg credit institution, which no EMI can provide; (ii) BCL-reporting-grade corporate liquidity, multi-currency cash pooling, netting, documentary credits, and FX lines require a domestic banking relationship; (iii) tax payments to the Administration des Contributions Directes and social-security contributions to the CSSF (Centre Commun de la Securite Sociale) are operationally segregated onto domestic SEPA accounts in practice; and (iv) EMI balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme, imposing counterparty concentration risk above EUR 100,000. The recommended treasury topology is therefore a dual-rail structure: a domestic house bank for settlement and regulatory-grade liquidity, with Wise B2B optimising low-value, high-frequency cross-border retainer flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward-capital injection protocol: (1) the statutory minimum share capital of the SARL is subscribed before a Luxembourg notary and deposited in the SARL's name into a blocked account (compte bloque) held at a BCL-member credit institution; the notary obtains the depositary's blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) and will not execute the deed absent receipt thereof; (2) upon RCS registration, the blocked balance is released into the operational account by internal book transfer; (3) subsequent cross-border injections from a parent or UBO are routed via SWIFT MT103 (USD/GBP/CHF) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) to the receiving account's IBAN/BIC, with the remittance narrative quoting the corporate objective and legal basis, e.g., 'capital increase pursuant to notarial deed dated [date]' and using the bank-prescribed ISO 20022 purpose code 'SUPP' (supplementary paid-in capital) or 'COMC' for shareholder current-account advances; (4) the receiving bank verifies the notarial deed, the RCS excerpt, the UBO declaration, and the origin-of-funds source statement, and files the inbound direct investment into the BCL balance-of-payments statistics system under the direct-investment equity-capital category; no prior BCL or CSSF authorisation is required; (5) for USD or GBP wires without a pre-existing corresponding-bank routing, the SARL should pre-notify the relationship manager 48 hours in advance to avoid a compliance hold pending remitter-name screening and purpose-code validation.Clearing-rail matrix for the headhunting treasury: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for all euro-denominated payments to EEA counterparties, including retainer refunds, contractor commissions, and settlement of success fees, providing TARGET2 finality; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA and non-euro disbursements (USD, GBP, CHF, and Asia-Pacific corridors), with field 70 remittance information quoting the retainer or invoice number for reconciliation; (iii) domestic SEPA Credit Transfers to the Administration des Contributions Directes (taxes) and the CCSS (social security) dedicated IBANs; (iv) internal book transfers for blocked-account release at incorporation; (v) documentary instruments — commercial letters of credit are materially inapposite to executive-search receivables, but where a foreign client demands bank payment undertakings, a standby letter of credit (SBLC) or bank counter-guarantee can be opened through the Luxembourg house bank, with the economic default collection rail remaining SWIFT MT103 or SEPA; and (vi) Wise B2B as a low-cost secondary rail for high-frequency, low-value retainer disbursements and contractor payments, subject to the EMI counterparty and balance-guarantee limitations previously described. The payload-mandated rail of Wise B2B (operated for EEA clients by Wise Europe SA, an e-money institution licensed under PSD2 and passporting into Luxembourg) is legally viable for retainer collections, vendor disbursements, and contractor settlements, including euro IBANs executed over SEPA and SWIFT-compatible non-EUR corridors. Material constraints: (i) the statutory share-capital deposit must be evidenced by a blocked-account certificate issued by a Luxembourg credit institution, which no EMI can provide; (ii) BCL-reporting-grade corporate liquidity, multi-currency cash pooling, netting, documentary credits, and FX lines require a domestic banking relationship; (iii) tax payments to the Administration des Contributions Directes and social-security contributions to the CSSF (Centre Commun de la Securite Sociale) are operationally segregated onto domestic SEPA accounts in practice; and (iv) EMI balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme, imposing counterparty concentration risk above EUR 100,000. The recommended treasury topology is therefore a dual-rail structure: a domestic house bank for settlement and regulatory-grade liquidity, with Wise B2B optimising low-value, high-frequency cross-border retainer flows.

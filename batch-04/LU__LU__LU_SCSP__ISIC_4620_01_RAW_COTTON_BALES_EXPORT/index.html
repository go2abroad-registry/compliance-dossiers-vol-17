Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Administration; single taxpayer portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.public.lu; commercial register: Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

Although the SCSp has no distinct legal personality and RCS registration is optional under the law of 23 July 2016 (mandatory only if it owns Luxembourg real estate), the operating SCSp is voluntarily registered with the RCS to obtain its identification number ('matricule'), open bank accounts and file VAT returns. Procedure: (1) execute the SCSp partnership agreement and appoint an authorised representative or corporate services provider; (2) file the registration application with the RCS via the e-justice portal (LBR) to obtain the matricule; (3) apply to the AEDT for VAT identification using the official Form 2, attaching the matricule, partner identity details and designation of the person responsible for VAT, whereupon the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits); (4) the SCSp is fiscally transparent for direct tax purposes, so the ACD does not normally allocate a separate CIT taxpayer reference, and partners are identified through their own national tax numbers and the partnership allocation schedule; (5) if the SCSp has no Luxembourg establishment and is a foreign-owned operator, an approved Luxembourg fiscal VAT representative must be appointed to complete Form 2 and file VAT returns; (6) obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration (Douanes et Accises) for export declarations of raw cotton bales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is a white-listed fiscally transparent partnership under Luxembourg income tax law (Article 175 L.I.R.); it is disregarded for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax, and partners are taxed directly on their distributive share. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is treated as a separate taxable person to the extent it carries out an independent economic activity (brokerage of ginned raw cotton), so it may invoice commission, charge VAT where applicable, and deduct input VAT; no entity-level income tax is levied on the SCSp itself.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election; fiscal transparency of the SCSp is statutorily imposed. Available elections and mechanisms: (a) election/registration of the SCSp as a VAT taxable person with full input VAT deduction where it conducts an economic activity; (b) Luxembourg VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') may be available for financially, economically and organisationally connected taxable persons, subject to AEDT conditions; (c) for US federal tax purposes, the SCSp may file Form 8832 to elect partnership or corporate classification, creating potential hybrid mismatch exposure addressed by Luxembourg ATAD/hybrid mismatch rules (Law of 21 December 2018); (d) partners may elect joint annual tax filing arrangements and submit a binding partnership income allocation schedule ('répartition des revenus') to the ACD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic-nexus turnover threshold applies to Luxembourg-established traders: any person carrying out an economic activity in Luxembourg must register for VAT from the first taxable transaction. A statutory small-business exemption under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law applies only to taxable persons whose annual turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000 (exclusive of VAT), but this exemption is not available for the SCSp's export cotton brokerage, which will exceed the threshold and is therefore mandatorily subject to VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to (a) the physical export of ginned raw cotton bales supplied by the SCSp or on its behalf to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration and proof that the goods left EU customs territory within the statutory deadlines; and (b) brokerage, commission and agency services rendered by the SCSp that are directly linked to the export of the raw cotton, supplied to a customer established outside the EU, or in respect of goods dispatched outside the EU, provided the services meet the place-of-supply rules and are effectively used and enjoyed outside the EU. The SCSp must obtain and retain the customer's foreign order, the commission agreement (ISIC 4620 brokerage contract), proof of dispatch, the validated SAD (MRN), and the inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the Luxembourg bank account. Input VAT on purchasing, transport, warehousing and brokerage-related costs is recoverable to the extent the output supplies are zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) / Export Accompanying Document with MRN evidencing departure from EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport of the raw cotton bales to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103) proving receipt of export proceeds on the Luxembourg bank account
  • Commercial invoice, brokerage/commission contract and LC or documentary collection instruments (SWIFT MT700/MT760)
  • Packing list, certificate of origin, weight/press receipts and third-country customs entry certificate (supporting evidence)

Luxembourg has no mandatory B2B real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping regime currently in force. B2B invoices may be issued on paper or in electronic format (EDI/XML), provided the authenticity, integrity and readability requirements of Articles 226 et seq. of the EU VAT Directive are met. E-invoicing in PEPPOL BIS format is mandatory for invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector contracting authorities under the B2G e-invoicing framework. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposal is expected to introduce digital reporting requirements and mandatory e-invoicing for cross-border B2B transactions by 2030, but no national law has yet transposed it.

Export zero-rating claims on cotton brokerage are subject to AEDT audit under Article 69 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, with powers to inspect commercial books, customs records and bank statements. Where export evidence (SAD/MRN, transport documents or inward remittance certificates) is missing, false or fraudulent, the AEDT retroactively reassesses VAT at the standard 17% rate with default interest, denies input VAT deduction on related costs, and imposes clawback extending the full limitation period (generally 5 years; 10 years in cases of fraud). Administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 per infringement may be imposed; criminal tax fraud sanctions include fines and imprisonment of up to five years under the Luxembourg Criminal Code and General Tax Law. The VAT fiscal representative (where appointed) and the jointly liable partners of the SCSp may be pursued in solidum for the reassessed VAT, interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the legal tender, having fully replaced the Luxembourg franc. As a Eurozone member and an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction, Luxembourg applies unrestricted convertibility on both current account and capital account transactions under TFEU Article 63 and Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no exchange controls, no conversion licensing gates, and no restrictions on profit remittance, partner distributions, liquidation proceeds, or cross-border payments. An SCSp is tax-transparent and not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners; no CSSF or BCL consent is required for FX conversion or capital movements. The euro is a freely convertible reserve currency, with EUR/USD execution at market rates without administrative filters. The only residual obligations are statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL and the EU Cash Controls Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 requiring a declaration for physical cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more across EU borders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds under Luxembourg, ECB, or EU law. A Luxembourg commercial intermediary may retain export receipts freely in EUR or in foreign currency, including USD, in onshore or offshore accounts, and may convert at any time without central bank approval. For raw cotton bales moving through Luxembourg as a routing or brokerage platform, the operator must nonetheless comply with EU customs declaration requirements, Intrastat reporting, zero-rated export / reverse-charge VAT treatment, sanctions screening, and, if physical banknotes or bearer negotiable instruments cross the EU border, declaration at the EUR 10,000 threshold. There is no obligation to convert export proceeds into EUR and no time limit for bringing them back to Luxembourg.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL is the sole authority for monetary policy implementation, foreign reserve administration and payment system oversight in Luxembourg, while the CSSF holds licensing, market-conduct, and AML/CFT supervisory powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM as an auxiliary settlement rail, LOW as a primary treasury bank. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions, or foreign EMIs passporting into Luxembourg under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2), may open payment accounts for the SCSp and provide multi-currency virtual IBAN collection, automated AP/AR workflows, rapid vendor payouts, and segregated escrow-style balances. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must hold a valid CSSF authorisation or a passport notification duly filed with the CSSF; (ii) the EMI's risk appetite must expressly permit textile raw-materials commodity trade, as many global EMIs refuse cotton and soft-commodity flow; (iii) EMIs cannot issue, advise, confirm, or negotiate Letters of Credit, documentary collections, or bank guarantees because those instruments require a SWIFT-connected credit institution with trade finance capacity; and (iv) the tax-transparent unregulated SCSp structure triggers enhanced due diligence above relatively low transaction thresholds. Recommended architecture: hold the primary treasury, LC portfolio, and correspondent banking at a Tier 1 bank; use the EMI only as a segregated collections wallet or contractor-payment wallet for small-ticket disbursements with zero end-of-day balance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners into the SCSp must be executed as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers (or SEPA Credit Transfer for euro amounts) from the contributor's own account at a PSD2-licensed institution to the SCSp's IBAN at a Luxembourg Tier 1 bank, referencing a formal capital call notice. The remittance information field must state 'equity contribution to SCSp' together with the capital call serial number; for statistical purposes the transaction is classified as direct investment equity capital under the BPM6 framework, and, where the transaction reaches the Luxembourg balance-of-payments reporting threshold (EUR 12,500 per transaction), the remitter and the receiving bank must provide the required counterparty attributes. The bank requires at each draw-down: an executed capital call certificate, a deed of capital contribution, an updated register of partners, a source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statement, and evidence that the funds originate from a compliant banking system. No capital duty, stamp duty, or exchange-control permit applies to inbound contributions to an SCSp; the bank performs standard sanctions screening on the remitter and all intermediary banks.The primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 priority customer credit transfers for direct supplier advances, brokerage commissions, freight, and inspection fees, executed through the Tier 1 bank's correspondent network (EUR via TARGET2 and SEPA, USD via CHIPS/Fedwire, GBP via CHAPS); (2) irrevocable, confirmed Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600, and eUCP where electronic documents are tendered, issued in favour of cotton-origin suppliers, with the Luxembourg bank acting as confirming or advising bank, or the SCSp's bank receiving the L/C as beneficiary when the SCSp is exporter of record; (3) documentary collections under URC 522, either documents-against-payment at sight or documents-against-acceptance with tenor drafts, for recurring and lower-risk shipments; and (4) SEPA Credit Transfers for euro-denominated EEA service-provider and administrative payments. Each disbursement must carry purpose codes consistent with the customs invoice and underlying contract, be directed only to pre-approved counterparties, and reflect the Incoterms 2020 allocation of payment obligations. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM as an auxiliary settlement rail, LOW as a primary treasury bank. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions, or foreign EMIs passporting into Luxembourg under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2), may open payment accounts for the SCSp and provide multi-currency virtual IBAN collection, automated AP/AR workflows, rapid vendor payouts, and segregated escrow-style balances. Legal constraints: (i) the EMI must hold a valid CSSF authorisation or a passport notification duly filed with the CSSF; (ii) the EMI's risk appetite must expressly permit textile raw-materials commodity trade, as many global EMIs refuse cotton and soft-commodity flow; (iii) EMIs cannot issue, advise, confirm, or negotiate Letters of Credit, documentary collections, or bank guarantees because those instruments require a SWIFT-connected credit institution with trade finance capacity; and (iv) the tax-transparent unregulated SCSp structure triggers enhanced due diligence above relatively low transaction thresholds. Recommended architecture: hold the primary treasury, LC portfolio, and correspondent banking at a Tier 1 bank; use the EMI only as a segregated collections wallet or contractor-payment wallet for small-ticket disbursements with zero end-of-day balance.

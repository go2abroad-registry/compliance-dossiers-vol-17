Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default under Article 186bis(1) L.I.R. (Law of 4 December 1967, as amended): it is not itself subject to corporate income tax (IRC) or municipal business tax (IMT). Luxembourg-resident partners are taxed directly on their distributive share of SCSp income; non-resident partners are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a separate taxable person and must hold its own LU VAT registration for supplies of film-licensing services.

Available election forms

Election 1 — Article 186bis(2) L.I.R. opt-in: the SCSp may, by joint written declaration of all partners and under strictly defined legal conditions, irrevocably elect to be treated as a fully taxable opaque entity subject to IRC and IMT. Election 2 — Semi-transparent fund regime: an SCSp structured as a reserved alternative investment fund (RAIF), investment company in risk capital (SICAR), or specialised investment fund (SIF) may elect special fund-taxation rules instead of ordinary partnership taxation. Election 3 — VAT grouping: under Article 10(A) of the amended VAT law of 12 February 1979, the SCSp and its related Luxembourg entities may elect to form a single taxable person for VAT purposes (VAT group), simplifying intercompany invoicing. Election 4 — ATAD-2 hybrid-mismatch alignment: where a foreign partner's jurisdiction does not recognise the SCSp's fiscal transparency, the taxpayer must apply the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive 2017/952 hybrid-mismatch analysis (Law of 21 December 2017, as amended), including the dual-inclusion income set-off and structured-arrangement disallowance elections; no US-style check-the-box form exists, and classification is determined by law plus supporting filings on the general partner's annual CIT return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold (exclusive of VAT) under Article 57 of the VAT law for the small-business exemption; taxable persons whose turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 must register for Luxembourg VAT, while those below the threshold may register voluntarily. Distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services (including digital film-streaming) to Luxembourg non-taxable persons are subject to the special EU threshold of EUR 10,000 for cross-border supplies, above which the destination-state VAT applies. For B2B film-licensing services to EU customers, the reverse-charge mechanism applies and no Luxembourg VAT is due, although the supplier may still be required to file recapitulative statements (EU sales lists) and be VAT-registered if it also makes domestic taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) exports of physical goods (master prints, film reels, promotional media) dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, under Article 60 of the VAT law, provided export proof is obtained within 90 days of supply, extendable to 180 days with prior authorisation; and (2) film-licensing services supplied to non-EU customers, which under the B2B place-of-supply rule of Article 19(1) of the VAT law are located outside Luxembourg's territorial VAT scope and thus bear no Luxembourg VAT (functional 0%). Eligibility is evidenced by the customer's non-EU establishment (valid foreign VAT ID or equivalent), the executed licensing agreement, and the actual payment flow. Film licences to EU customers are taxable in the customer's member state under reverse charge; B2C licences to non-EU final consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT, while B2C digital supplies to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's state and may be reported via the Luxembourg One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Document Administratif Unique — DAU) for physical consignments of film prints, masters, or promotional materials
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note evidencing outward transport from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Luxembourg paying bank evidencing receipt of the foreign licence fee within the statutory period
  • Executed film-licensing agreement and territorial exploitation statements evidencing the non-EU place of supply and commercial substance

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government-approval, or digital-stamping regime for private-sector invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G (public-sector) transactions since 1 July 2021 using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, PEPPOL). Under the Law of 26 November 2024, domestic B2B e-invoicing is being phased in: large taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices from 1 January 2026, with full roll-out for all taxable persons by 1 January 2027, in alignment with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) framework; structured data must conform to the European standard EN 16931. Cross-border EU supplies require electronic submission of recapitulative statements by the 15th day of the following month, and the AEDT performs risk-based post-audits using the VAT Information Exchange System (VIS) and e-FISCAL audit tools; there is no mandatory API-based clearance for each invoice, but machine-readable preservation for six years is required.

In cases of unjustified zero-rating or failed export substantiation, the AEDT may reassess the supply at the standard 17% VAT rate plus default interest of 0.8% per month from the due date (Article 28 of the VAT collection rules). The VAT penalty regime (Articles 65–71 of the VAT law) imposes a fine of 10% of the evaded tax for non-intentional omissions, 100% for intentional evasion, and up to 200% in aggravated cases, with possible criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 200,000 for fraudulent export documentation. Where the documentary bundle or FX repatriation evidence is absent, the AEDT may apply a rebuttable presumption of a taxable domestic supply and extend the reassessment period from 5 to 7 years in cases of fraud; retrospective clawback of refunds is pursued against the SCSp and its general partner, with non-resident partners remaining severally liable for evaded Luxembourg VAT where the structure is found to be artificial or sham.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible reserve currency with no exchange-control restrictions applicable in Luxembourg. As an EU Member State, Luxembourg fully applies the free movement of capital and payments under Article 63 TFEU, Directive 88/361/EEC, and the associated CJEU jurisprudence. Both current account flows (royalties, license fees, profit remittances) and capital account flows (equity injections, partnership capital calls, liquidation distributions) are freely convertible and transferable without prior BCL or CSSF authorization. A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) structured as a transparent partnership faces no convertibility hurdle; the sole practical frictions are commercial-bank documentary diligence, EU/EBA sanctions screening, and FATCA/CRS reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg maintains no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for foreign-currency receipts derived from motion picture licensing or any other export of services and intellectual property. Foreign currency balances (USD, GBP, INR, CNY, etc.) may be held indefinitely in resident accounts or converted at commercial market rates. The only compliance duty is the BCL's statistical Balance of Payments reporting captured by Luxembourg credit institutions for transactions above reporting thresholds; this is a statistical observation duty, not a surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and exercising delegated national competences alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Luxembourg Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for operational royalty collections; Low for capital escrow and large balance sheet activity. Luxembourg-authorized EMIs and Payment Services Firms (PSFs) licensed by the CSSF under PSD2, as well as EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Ebury, PPRO, Lemonway), can technically onboard the SCSp for inbound licensing collections and outbound royalty disbursements in EUR/USD with SWIFT and SEPA clearing. However, EMIs may not grant credit, cannot offer overdraft or treasury line facilities, and often decline complex partnership vehicles with non-resident beneficial owners. For a film licensing SCSp, incumbent Tier-1 credit institutions remain mandatory for escrow of investor capital and for documentary credit (Letter of Credit) trade finance; EMI rails should be limited to low-velocity operational receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Investor capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 payments from the investor's bank to the SCSp's designated capital escrow account, cleared in TARGET2 where the remitting institution is euro-area resident or via correspondent/ nostro clearing for non-euro currencies. Compliant transmission requires: (i) ISO 20022 Purpose Code 'CAPC' (Capital Contribution) in field 70/77B or the equivalent structured remittance element; (ii) full legal name and LEI (if available) of the SCSp as payee; (iii) explicit reference to the capital call notice and partner identity to enable the bank's source-of-funds verification; (iv) where capital is injected in IP-in-kind (film copyrights), a parallel physical deed of contribution registered with the Luxembourg tax and trade registry should be referenced but no wire transfer is needed; (v) for subscription exceeding EUR 100,000, the bank will require a sworn source of wealth statement and certified investor passports. Royalty collections from foreign sub-distributors enter via MT103 with Purpose Code 'ROYL' (Royalties and License Fees) and should be accompanied by the relevant distribution report in the remittance narrative. All inbound transfers are subject to EBA 2015/01 funds transfer regulation datascreening (compliance with the 'travel rule' requiring originator and beneficiary information).Outbound settlement of film acquisition costs, royalty participations, and profit-sharing payments is executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 for straight-through fiat disbursements to licensors, sub-distributors, and financial co-producers, carrying Purpose Code 'ROYL' for royalties or 'TRAD' for trade payments; (ii) SWIFT MT700 documentary Letters of Credit (Commercial LC) for milestone-based production and delivery financing, where the SCSp's Luxembourg bank issues an LC in favour of a production company or laboratory with the bill of lading, delivery receipt, and certificate of origin as required documents; (iii) TARGET2 via the BCL settlement account for euro disbursements between EU counterparties; and (iv) bank draft or internal transfer for intra-group cross-border profit allocation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational royalty collections; Low for capital escrow and large balance sheet activity. Luxembourg-authorized EMIs and Payment Services Firms (PSFs) licensed by the CSSF under PSD2, as well as EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Ebury, PPRO, Lemonway), can technically onboard the SCSp for inbound licensing collections and outbound royalty disbursements in EUR/USD with SWIFT and SEPA clearing. However, EMIs may not grant credit, cannot offer overdraft or treasury line facilities, and often decline complex partnership vehicles with non-resident beneficial owners. For a film licensing SCSp, incumbent Tier-1 credit institutions remain mandatory for escrow of investor capital and for documentary credit (Letter of Credit) trade finance; EMI rails should be limited to low-velocity operational receipts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% corporate income tax (CIT) on taxable income exceeding EUR 200,000; 15% on the first tranche up to EUR 175,000; 16% on EUR 175,001 to EUR 200,000. Add 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT and municipal business tax at 6.75% (Luxembourg City) to 10.43% (high-rate communes), yielding an approximate combined headline burden of 24.94% in Luxembourg City. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default and therefore no entity-level CIT is levied; the distributive shares are taxed in the hands of the partners.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax (plus 7% solidarity surcharge, resulting in an effective 16.05%) on dividends distributed by a Luxembourg taxable company. Critical SCSp nuance: the fiscally transparent SCSp does not suffer dividend WHT; profit distributions to partners are recharacterised as allocable partnership income. If the SCSp elects corporate taxation under Form 830, distributions become dividends subject to 15% WHT, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% holding, 12-month holding period) and to 5% or 0% under most Luxembourg income tax treaties.

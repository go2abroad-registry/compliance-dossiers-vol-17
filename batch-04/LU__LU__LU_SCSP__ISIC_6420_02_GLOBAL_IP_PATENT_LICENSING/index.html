Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes (portal: https://impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes (portal: https://aedt.public.lu); central e-government portal: myGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu); company registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) at https://www.lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident promoter forms an SCSp by private deed (no minimum capital), names a manager (foreign or resident), and files the deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers within one month to obtain the RCS matricule and the statutory national registration number. The SCSp then obtains a Luxembourg tax identification number (numéro d'identification fiscal) from the ACD. For VAT, the SCSp, or a non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg, must file a VAT registration application (Formulaire de demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT, supported by the RCS extract, deed, partner and manager identification, AML/KYC files (Medium_Risk_Enhanced tier), and a local tax representative where Luxembourg law requires it; registration may also be effected through the myGuichet portal. A foreign entity with no Luxembourg establishment registers directly for a Luxembourg VAT number from the first euro of taxable transactions, subject to reverse-charge and One-Stop-Shop (OSS) mechanisms.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (the Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent: it has separate legal personality but is treated as a partnership for income tax purposes, so corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax are not levied at the entity level; profits, losses and tax attributes flow through to the partners, who are taxed according to their own status and country of residence; the SCSp remains a VAT taxable person if it independently and habitually supplies services for consideration). — A non-resident promoter forms an SCSp by private deed (no minimum capital), names a manager (foreign or resident), and files the deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers within one month to obtain the RCS matricule and the statutory national registration number. The SCSp then obtains a Luxembourg tax identification number (numéro d'identification fiscal) from the ACD. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the Luxembourg SCSp is fiscally transparent: it has separate legal personality but is treated as a partnership for income tax purposes, so corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax are not levied at the entity level; profits, losses and tax attributes flow through to the partners, who are taxed according to their own status and country of residence; the SCSp remains a VAT taxable person if it independently and habitually supplies services for consideration).

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; however, under Article 175 LIR a partnership such as an SCSp may, with the consent of all partners, irrevocably elect for a binding period to be taxed as a capital company (option pour le régime des sociétés de capitaux). VAT-specific elections include the option to form a single VAT unit (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, and the election for group taxation (Fiscal Unity / régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for corporate income tax and net wealth tax where a 95% parent-subsidiary relationship exists; ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules (implemented by the law of 21 December 2020) require the SCSp and its partners to align entity classification elections with EU counterpart jurisdictions to prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches and treaty-shopping outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established Luxembourg taxable persons: annual turnover exceeding EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT) triggers mandatory VAT registration and filing, with the small-enterprise exemption available below that amount. Non-established foreign suppliers and service providers: no monetary threshold applies, so registration is required from the first euro of taxable supplies in Luxembourg unless the Luxembourg customer accounts for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism or the supplier uses the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for cross-border B2C distance sales of goods or electronically supplied services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a supply of goods exported to a destination outside the EU qualifies for the 0% export VAT rate (or the corresponding intra-EU dispatch exemption) if the goods are physically dispatched and transported out of the EU by the supplier or on its behalf, and the supplier holds transport and customs evidence (electronic export declaration/EXS, bill of lading/air waybill) demonstrating that the goods left the EU within the legally required period, generally within 90 days. Services: the licensing and sub-licensing of patents, trademarks and other intellectual property by an SCSp to a B2B customer established outside Luxembourg generally has its place of supply at the customer's establishment, so the royalties are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (in practice zero-rated/exempt from Luxembourg VAT); where the customer is established in another EU Member State, the reverse-charge mechanism applies to the customer and the SCSp must issue a VAT invoice stating 'reverse charge' and report the supply in the EC Sales List. For B2C supplies of certain electronically supplied services to non-EU customers, OSS special schemes apply and Luxembourg VAT generally does not apply if the customer resides outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic EXS/Single Administrative Document) evidencing exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch
  • Commercial invoice referencing the IP licensing contract and the export/zero-rating legal basis
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank remission/FX repatriation evidence) covering the royalty payment
  • Executed patent/trademark license or sub-license agreement and royalty calculation file
  • Intercompany transfer pricing documentation supporting arm's length royalty rates
  • Tax residence certificate of the foreign licensee and, where applicable, a certificate of fiscal domicile of the SCSp's partners

Luxembourg has no mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, no continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance model, no digital stamping obligation, and no state-operated transaction clearance API as of the current filing year. Electronic invoicing is permitted only if authenticity, integrity and readability are ensured; from January 2025, structured electronic invoices (e.g. Peppol BIS or equivalent) must be accepted for Luxembourg public procurement invoices. Luxembourg is aligned with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) roadmap, which is expected to mandate structured e-invoicing and transaction-based digital reporting from approximately 2030. Currently, compliance is achieved through periodic VAT returns, annual recapitulative EC Sales Lists, Intrastat declarations, and the standard AEDT VAT filing formats.

Where export documentation is incomplete, untimely or fraudulent, the Luxembourg ACD/AEDT may retroactively assess the supply at the standard 16% VAT, deny the zero-rating or the input VAT deduction/refund, and impose statutory default interest from the due date of the original VAT return. Administrative fines under the Luxembourg Income Tax Law and VAT Law generally amount to up to 10% of the under-declared tax, with higher penalties and criminal sanctions for intentional fraud; the Luxembourg criminal tax fraud framework (in force since 2022) exposes responsible managers and partners to substantial fines and imprisonment for wilful false documentation. The documentation bundle must be retained for the full statutory limitation period, as the tax authorities may re-open assessments within five years (extended in cases of fraud), and retrospective clawbacks may include recharacterization of royalty payments as taxable Luxembourg-source income, reclassification of the SCSp as taxable where the benefit of pass-through treatment is abused under substance-over-form or abuse-of-law (abus de droit) doctrines, and joint and several liability of the manager and partners for the evaded VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, prior approvals, surrender duties, or repatriation limits, consistent with TFEU Article 63 free movement of capital. Profit remittances, royalty distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely, subject only to bank-level statistical reporting to the BCL (balance of payments / IIP), CRS/DAC6 automatic exchange, and documentary proof of the underlying distribution (notarized partnership resolution for an SCSp). No distinction between current and capital account restrictions exists because both are fully liberalized.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or obligation to surrender export or licensing receipts within any defined period. The SCSp may retain EUR or foreign currency royalty proceeds in designated accounts indefinitely and may convert USD, CHF, or other receipts to EUR at freely negotiated market rates.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential supervision of credit institutions is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises ultimate monetary authority for the euro.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA SCT for EUR) from the contributing shareholder or partner to the SCSp's dedicated IBAN, using ISO 20022 purpose code INVE with narrative 'Contribution en capital — SCSp [entity name]'. Verification protocol at the receiving bank: (1) remitter name must match the partner/shareholder identified in the notarized partnership agreement or capital-contribution resolution; (2) beneficiary name and IBAN must match the RCS extract; (3) amount must align with the approved funding plan and the SCSp's capitalization table; (4) the remitting entity's UBO declaration must be consistent with the RBE/UBO register. The bank records the inflow for BCL monthly balance-of-payments reporting under direct-investment equity (BPM6 standard component F51); no prior exchange-control authorization is required.SWIFT MT103 for intercompany royalty and license-fee settlements, referencing ISO 20022 purpose code ROYL or LICF and recorded by the bank under BCL services code S213 (charges for the use of intellectual property). SEPA SCT for eurozone counterparties; SWIFT MT700 (irrevocable documentary letter of credit), MT707 (amendments), and MT798 for commercial settlements where patent licensing is bundled with physical goods, with presentation of commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and transport documents; MT202 COV for nostro clearing of USD/CHF/GBP disbursement legs. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for offshore non-EEA EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) used as a primary treasury rail: they lack a Luxembourg or EU PSD2 license or passport, do not participate in BCL balance-of-payments reporting, are outside the Luxembourg banking-secrecy and depositor-protection framework, and frequently restrict or freeze high-value intercompany royalty flows. Medium for EU-licensed EMIs (CSSF-licensed or EEA-passported, e.g., Mangopay, Lemonway) for operational expense disbursements and e-commerce collection, but never for capital escrow, collateral for bank debt, or large-value cross-border clearing. Legal constraints: EMIs may not grant credit, must safeguard client funds separately, and may hold funds only for the duration of the payment order.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at SCSp level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent; a fully taxable Luxembourg corporate partner is subject to 17% corporate income tax plus 7% employment-fund surcharge, and in Luxembourg City the aggregate corporate tax burden including 6.75% municipal business tax is 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax for taxable company distributions; SCSp profit distributions to partners are not treated as dividends and are therefore not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax. The 15% rate may be reduced to 0% under the Luxembourg participation exemption, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or an applicable double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File obligation is generally triggered when the Luxembourg entity belongs to a group with annual consolidated turnover above EUR 50,000,000 and has intra-group transactions; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply above EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. The ACD may also request transfer pricing documentation under Article 56 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law regardless of formal thresholds where controlled transactions are material.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation in the format used by offshore financial centres, but substance is central to Luxembourg and EU anti-abuse rules, including ATAD Article 6, the Luxembourg General Anti-Abuse Rule, OECD functional analysis, treaty beneficial-ownership requirements, and the IP regime nexus requirements. An SCSp licensing patents or trademarks must demonstrate active management, local decision-making, qualified staff, premises, risk control and real economic activity; a paper-only IP conduit is exposed to recharacterisation and denial of treaty and domestic tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with interest, penalties of 20% to 100% of the evaded tax, criminal fines of up to EUR 10,000, imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud, withdrawal of tax incentives, and publication in serious cases.

Director personal liability: General partners and managing directors who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; criminal convictions may add disqualification, personal fines, travel bans and asset freezing orders.
Shell / letter-box IP holding structure

Statutory sanction: The ACD may disregard the SCSp, reallocate royalty income to the ultimate licensor under Article 56 LIR and ATAD GAAR, deny treaty benefits, apply ordinary corporate tax plus penalty interest, and refuse the IP regime exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are exposed to civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty, damages for reckless facilitation of the abusive structure, and, in egregious cases, personal liability for the reassessed tax deficiency.
AML / beneficial-ownership breach

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties of up to EUR 1,000,000 for entities and up to 10% of annual turnover in serious cases; responsible natural persons face fines and imprisonment; the CSSF or Luxembourg public prosecutor may order transaction freezing and suspension of business.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and senior officers are personally liable for AML violations, including criminal penalties, travel restrictions, temporary bans from exercising managerial functions, and seizure of personal assets where money laundering is proven.

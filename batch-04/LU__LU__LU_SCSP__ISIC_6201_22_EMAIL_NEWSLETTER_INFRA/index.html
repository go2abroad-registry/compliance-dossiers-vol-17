Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of Luxembourg, operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (the '1915 Law'), in particular Article 101 and related provisions as inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013 governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); supplemented by the Luxembourg Law of 14 August 2000 on electronic commerce for information society services.

Formation / constitutional filings

Partnership agreement (contrat de société) executed in private form or by notarial deed, deposited with the RCS together with the statutory company registration form and ancillary annexes.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum capital is prescribed by law for the SCSp; the partnership agreement freely defines the capital structure. No statutory escrow account or blocked bank deposit is required; contributions may be made in cash, in kind or in services, and no bank attestation is legally mandated.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct/income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes. Digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (cross-border e-filing), eCDF (electronic VAT/declaration filing system), and AEDT's myTAX platform.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SCSp in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL) via the guichet unique des formalités; obtain the RCSL business registration number and 13-digit national identification number (matricule). 2) Register for VAT with the AEDT using Form 640 (inscription à la TVA) submitted through the Guichet unique or directly to the AEDT's VAT registration department. Required supporting documents: certified copy of the articles of association (statuts), RCSL extract, identification of the general partner (associé gérant) and its registered office/authorized signatories, description of the electronic/email infrastructure activity, estimated turnover, and, where the SCSp has no Luxembourg establishment, appointment of a Luxembourg tax representative (représentant responsable) unless the EU reverse-charge/OSS rules apply. 3) The SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes, so no corporate income tax registration is made at entity level; each partner must register with the ACD and is assessed pro rata. 4) Obtain the LU-prefixed VAT identification number (typically issued within 1–2 weeks after complete filing); simultaneously register for the EU VIES database and, if B2C digital supplies to EU consumers are expected, for the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme via the Luxembourg OSS portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is statutorily fiscally transparent under Article 182bis LIR: profits are not taxed at the entity level; each partner is taxed directly on their allocable share. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is a deemed taxable person (assujetti unique) in its own right under Article 15 Loi TVA, and must therefore charge, collect, and remit VAT. Territorial scope: Luxembourg-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income allocable through the SCSp (limited in practice to Luxembourg real estate and certain secured receivables). No tax-exempt zone applies to the SCSp in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election: the SCSp's pass-through status is mandatory and non-waivable for income tax. Available elections and mechanisms: (1) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 Loi TVA — election to consolidate the SCSp with financially integrated Luxembourg entities into a single VAT person; (2) OSS election for B2C cross-border digital/electronic services to EU consumers (Article 59c Loi TVA), avoiding foreign VAT registrations; (3) application for an advance tax ruling (tax agreement) with the ACD confirming partner-level taxation, substance, and profit allocation; (4) hybrid-mismatch alignment under the ATAD Implementation Law of 21 December 2018 — for cross-border arrangements (e.g., where a US LLC or other transparent entity holds partnership interests), the ACD may require a 'payee tax transparency' alignment or 'imported mismatch' adjustment to prevent double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; an election to treat the SCSp as opaque is not available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-enterprise exemption under Article 57 Loi TVA (domestic supplies only). This threshold is irrelevant for the SCSp's B2B email-infrastructure supplies: B2B digital services are always subject to reverse charge at the customer's EU Member State and no Luxembourg VAT registration threshold applies. For B2C electronically supplied services (newsletter subscriptions) to consumers in other EU Member States, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies; once exceeded, the SCSp must register via OSS and charge destination-country VAT. Supplies of digital/email-hosting services to non-EU consumers fall outside Luxembourg VAT entirely.

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority e-invoice pre-clearance, API reporting stamping, or digital stamping for B2B transactions. Mandatory PEPPOL-based e-invoicing applies exclusively to B2G invoices (public procurement) since 1 January 2024. B2B e-invoices are accepted (including PEPPOL BIS 3.0 and EN 16931 formats) provided authenticity of origin and integrity of content are guaranteed via EDI (Article 94 VAT Directive-compliant) or a qualified electronic signature. VAT compliance is effected through periodic VAT returns (Form TVA 200), the European recapitulative statement (Article 265 VAT Directive) listing EU B2B customers, and annual SAF-T reporting for large taxpayers. Luxembourg is preparing for the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reforms, which will introduce mandatory transaction-based digital reporting and standard e-invoicing for intra-EU supplies on a phased timeline, but such obligations are not yet in force. E-invoices must be archived in original format for 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The euro is freely floating and Luxembourg maintains no exchange controls. Both current-account items (subscription revenues, trade payments, SaaS fees) and capital-account items (SCSp capital contributions, partnership distributions, intercompany loan repayments) are unrestricted between Luxembourg and all EEA member states and most third countries, subject only to EU/UN financial sanctions compliance. No prior central bank authorisation, no convertibility licence, no profit remittance approval, and no inward/outward investment screening applies to a non-regulated SCSp of this commercial nature. Non-resident general and limited partners may receive profit distributions via SEPA or SWIFT without BCL or CSSF scrutiny; the only burdens are bank-side KYC, CRS/FATCA reporting, and BCL balance-of-payments statistical declarations filed by the bank for eligible cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion or disposal ratio for export proceeds or cross-border service receipts. The SCSp is free to hold EUR subscription revenue, convert it, redeploy it, or distribute it in accordance with the limited partnership agreement. Resident credit institutions are required to report to the BCL, on a monthly basis, individual cross-border payments of at least EUR 12,500 for balance-of-payments purposes, but this obligation is imposed on the bank, not on the SCSp, and implies no restriction on the SCSp's use of funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB). Foreign exchange control and capital movement oversight are shared among the BCL, the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance, and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CFT supervision. The applicable legal framework is Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) governing free movement of capital, EU Regulation 2018/1672 on cash controls, the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing as amended, and CSSF Regulation 12-02 of 14 December 2012.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Financial viability rating: Medium for operational use, Low for treasury concentration. Luxembourg fully permits the SCSp to hold funds with CSSF-licensed E-Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs), including entities passported into Luxembourg under the Law of 1 August 2018 (PSD2 implementation). Thus, Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland, passported to Luxembourg), Revolut Ltd (Ireland), Wise Europe SA (Belgium), and Mangopay/Market Pay can be used as settlement and fee-collection rails. Legal constraints: (i) funds held at an EMI are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee fund (Fonds de garantie des dépôts Luxembourg, EUR 100,000 protection applies only to credit institutions), (ii) EMIs are subject only to safeguarding obligations, not prudential deposit protection, (iii) post-Brexit, UK-headquartered EMIs can serve Luxembourg clients only via EEA-licensed subsidiaries, and (iv) concentration of treasury balances in an unregulated non-EEA EMI would weaken BCL regulatory oversight and may be treated by the Luxembourg tax authorities and the CSSF-supervised bank as a substance deficiency. Practical recommendation: use Stripe as the payment-acceptance overlay and a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank as the sole treasury account holder; an EMI may be used for sub-account segmentation but must not hold more than 10% of operational treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: partner equity contributions are remitted to the SCSp's IBAN at the chosen Luxembourg bank via SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR). In field 50K of MT103, the ordering customer must be the partner’s own account; field 59 must be the SCSp’s account with full legal name; field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'Capital Contribution SCSp' and reference the specific capital call; the BOP purpose code should be 521 (investment in partnership equity) to ensure correct BCL statistical classification. For a convertible loan or shareholder advance, use BOP code 522 (loan to a non-financial partnership). The receiving bank will apply standard verification against the partnership agreement and may require a letter from the GP confirming the capital call. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Financial viability rating: Medium for operational use, Low for treasury concentration. Luxembourg fully permits the SCSp to hold funds with CSSF-licensed E-Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs), including entities passported into Luxembourg under the Law of 1 August 2018 (PSD2 implementation). Thus, Stripe Payments Europe Ltd (Ireland, passported to Luxembourg), Revolut Ltd (Ireland), Wise Europe SA (Belgium), and Mangopay/Market Pay can be used as settlement and fee-collection rails. Legal constraints: (i) funds held at an EMI are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee fund (Fonds de garantie des dépôts Luxembourg, EUR 100,000 protection applies only to credit institutions), (ii) EMIs are subject only to safeguarding obligations, not prudential deposit protection, (iii) post-Brexit, UK-headquartered EMIs can serve Luxembourg clients only via EEA-licensed subsidiaries, and (iv) concentration of treasury balances in an unregulated non-EEA EMI would weaken BCL regulatory oversight and may be treated by the Luxembourg tax authorities and the CSSF-supervised bank as a substance deficiency. Practical recommendation: use Stripe as the payment-acceptance overlay and a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank as the sole treasury account holder; an EMI may be used for sub-account segmentation but must not hold more than 10% of operational treasury.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum capital is required by law for an SCSp; the initial capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement. No statutory escrow or bank-blocked account requirement applies under the amended Law of 10 August 1915.

Funding & unlock path: For administrative offices, fire-safety conformity is certified by the lessor, and a favorable visit by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) is required before occupation if the premises exceed simple office use. Occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) requires a documented workplace risk assessment, first-aid equipment, and emergency evacuation arrangements in accordance with the amended Labor Code of Luxembourg.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum capital is required by law for an SCSp; the initial capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement. No statutory escrow or bank-blocked account requirement applies under the amended Law of 10 August 1915.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) – portal: eCDF (Electronic Corporate and Fiscal Declarations); VAT authority: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – portal: myGuichet.lu / eTVA; business registry: Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via eJustice

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SCSp must first obtain a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) by filing an incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the eJustice portal, followed by automatic attribution of the matricule. Where no Luxembourg permanent establishment exists or where the entity does not hold real estate, the SCSp must nevertheless register for VAT (TVA) with the AEDT using Form No. 6401 (inscription au registre TVA) within 15 days of commencing taxable activity; a fixed place of business or economic nexus in Luxembourg triggers mandatory VAT registration. The VAT number is the matricule preceded by 'LU'. No separate federal TIN is required for partnerships; each partner's foreign TIN must be declared in the annual partnership return filed via eCDF.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; the SCSp is a transparent entity for Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) and net wealth tax (IMF) purposes, unless it opts for joint taxation under Article 159 of the LIR or elects to be treated as a taxable entity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the SCSp is a transparent entity for Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) and net wealth tax (IMF) purposes, unless it opts for joint taxation under Article 159 of the LIR or elects to be treated as a taxable entity. The SCSp is separately liable for VAT as a taxable person regardless of tax transparency.

Available election forms

Check-the-box-style election: Under Article 159 LIR, an SCSp may irrevocably elect corporate income taxation (transparent-to-nontransparent) by filing a joint declaration with its partners within the legal deadlines; alternatively, a hybrid mismatch election may be available under Article 168ter LIR to align Luxembourg classification with a foreign jurisdiction's classification (e.g., US check-the-box) provided the entity is not resident and is not a Luxembourg taxable entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (no general VAT registration threshold for established EU businesses); a non-established EU/foreign business that supplies goods or services with a Luxembourg VAT nexus must register from the first euro of taxable turnover. For distance sales of goods to Luxembourg consumers, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide cross-border distance sales threshold applies only to EU-established suppliers under the OSS regime, and non-EU suppliers must register immediately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder (CN code 2836.91) to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 47(1)(a) LTV and Article 393 of the VAT Directive, provided (i) the goods are physically exported from Luxembourg or another EU Member State by the supplier or a third party acting on its behalf, (ii) the supplier retains proof of export within 90 days and proof of arrival in the third country, and (iii) the purchaser is not established in Luxembourg. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers are exempt with right to deduction under Article 41 LTV (Intra-Community supply), requiring the customer's valid EU VAT number and an Intrastat declaration threshold compliance. Offshore services linked to international trade (e.g., logistics) may be zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules if supplied to a non-EU business customer, but pure trading commissions or procurement services supplied to a non-EU business are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT rather than zero-rated.

  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) naming the Luxembourg exporter/seller and consignee as the non-EU buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of payment evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds by the Luxembourg exporter (bank SWIFT confirmation or foreign exchange settlement statement)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of Transport (e.g., CMR waybill if road transport; transport contract or container tracking evidence)
  • Proof of exit from EU customs territory (customs stamp or post-export customs status confirmation)

Luxembourg has not introduced mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC) for B2B domestic suppliers as of the knowledge cut-off; however, e-invoicing in Peppol or Factur-X format is mandatory for public sector B2G procurement under the Luxembourg e-Invoicing Law of 15 August 2021. All VAT-registered entities must submit periodic e-filing through the AEDT eTVA/MyGuichet portal, and SAF-T XML must be submitted on demand for audit purposes. Digital stamping is not yet mandatory, but the European Council adopted the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals requiring standard electronic invoicing for cross-border B2B transactions by 2030 and real-time digital reporting from 2035; no current deviating API clearance mandate exists.

Under Articles 456-461 LIR and VAT enforcement rules, fraudulent export zero-rating (e.g., fictitious exports, forged customs documents, missing proof of dispatch) triggers (i) retrospective VAT assessment and reversal of input VAT deduction, (ii) a 10% late payment interest per annum and administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 for recording irregularities, and (iii) criminal penalties of imprisonment of up to 6 months and/or fine of up to EUR 25,000 for documentary forgery. Under the Luxembourg General Tax Procedure Act (Steueranpassungsgesetz, § 9-10), where a transaction mischaracterized as an export is found to be a sham, tax reassessment may be extended to 5 years (10 years for fraud), and VAT penalties may be doubled to 20% of the tax evaded. The AEDT and ACD maintain a mutual assistance framework for cross-border audit and exchange of information; clawback of input VAT recovered on export goods may also apply under Article 29(5) LIR substance requirements if the SCSp lacks economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible. Luxembourg is a euro-area member and applies Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) on free movement of capital together with Directive 88/361/EEC. No exchange-control authorization, FX allocation, prior licensing, or central bank permit is required for current-account payments (trade in lithium carbonate) or capital-account operations (subscription of SCSp partnership interests, shareholder loans, distributions, liquidation proceeds). Profit remittances to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, no Luxembourg withholding tax applies on distributions, and the paying bank applies only standard EU/UN sanctions screening and enhanced due diligence appropriate to a medium-risk commodity-trading entity. There are no prohibitions on maintaining foreign-currency accounts domestically or offshore, nor any minimum financing or FX repatriation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, and no surrender requirement on foreign-currency receipts arising from international trade. A Luxembourg-resident entity receiving cross-border payments exceeding statistical thresholds is, however, subject to balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the BCL (Eurosystem framework), which is a purely statistical filing duty and does not restrict the ownership, use, or disposition of foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem in concert with the European Central Bank (ECB). The BCL operates the domestic real-time gross settlement component (LIPS-Gross / T2), compiles Luxembourg balance-of-payments statistics, and oversees payment system stability, while prudential supervision of credit institutions is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited and auxiliary only. A fintech EMI lawfully operating in Luxembourg must hold a CSSF e-money licence or a valid EU/EEA passport notification under PSD2; UK- or non-EU-headquartered platforms without such passport cannot lawfully serve a Luxembourg SCSp. Even when licensed, digital EMIs are generally unsuitable as primary rails for ISIC 4662 commodity settlements: they lack substantive SWIFT correspondent relationships for USD-denominated lithium-carbonate drawings, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit under UCP 600, and frequently classify partnership treasury flows with multiple third-party payees as elevated risk. Feasibility rating for mobilization expenses and small-ticket operational payments: Medium; as primary settlement and clearing infrastructure: Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer where the remitter is EEA-based) with the SCSp's full name and RCS registration number in field 70 (Remittance Information) and, for SEPA ISO 20022 pain.001, ExternalPurpose code SUBS (Subscription) or an explicit description 'capital contribution to Luxembourg SCSp under capital call dated [X]'. The receiving Luxembourg bank verifies the package comprising the capital call notice, executed LP subscription agreement, RCS excerpt, UBO declaration, and balance-of-payments statistical identification for BCL reporting. Inbound USD subscriptions are credited via the bank's New York correspondent through CHIPS/Fedwire into a multi-currency account, or converted into EUR through T2/LIPS-Gross; no BCL authorization is required, but the supporting dossier must be retained for the statutory AML/CFT period of at least five years.Primary rails: (1) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued by the Luxembourg account bank in favor of the lithium carbonate producer under UCP 600, with settlement by negotiation against presentation of a full set of clean-on-board ocean bills of lading, signed commercial invoice, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, packing list, and laboratory certificate of analysis; EUR-denominated L/Cs settle over T2/LIPS-Gross and USD-denominated L/Cs over CHIPS through the New York correspondent. (2) SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfers for operational settlements, freight, processing fees, and insurance premiums, with EUR via SEPA/T2 and USD via Fedwire/CHIPS. (3) Standby L/Cs or advance-payment guarantees may be issued to secure supplier prepayment terms. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited and auxiliary only. A fintech EMI lawfully operating in Luxembourg must hold a CSSF e-money licence or a valid EU/EEA passport notification under PSD2; UK- or non-EU-headquartered platforms without such passport cannot lawfully serve a Luxembourg SCSp. Even when licensed, digital EMIs are generally unsuitable as primary rails for ISIC 4662 commodity settlements: they lack substantive SWIFT correspondent relationships for USD-denominated lithium-carbonate drawings, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit under UCP 600, and frequently classify partnership treasury flows with multiple third-party payees as elevated risk. Feasibility rating for mobilization expenses and small-ticket operational payments: Medium; as primary settlement and clearing infrastructure: Low.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital required by law for an SCSp. Contributions are freely fixed in the partnership agreement. There is no mandatory escrow account; the SCSp must have at least one general partner with unlimited liability and one limited partner.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD / Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for income, corporate and withholding tax — www.impotsdirects.lu; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT / Luxembourg VAT Authority) for VAT registration and VAT compliance — aedt.public.lu; integrated digital filings are submitted through MyGuichet.lu at guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or operating through an SCSp must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain a national business identification number if the SCSp is filed with the RCS. For VAT, the non-established taxable person must file Form 640 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' with the AEDT, attaching proof of foreign business registration, identity documents for the general partner/managers, and a power of attorney or tax representation mandate. Non-EU persons without an EU establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before a LU VAT number is issued. The LU VAT identification number has the format LU followed by 8 digits. For Luxembourg income tax, the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default; each partner is separately liable and, to the extent taxable in Luxembourg, must obtain or use a Luxembourg national identification number. If the Article 168bis LIR opacity election is made, the SCSp obtains its own CIT/NWT identification number from the ACD upon filing the election and registration in the RCS.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing or operating through an SCSp must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain a national business identification number if the SCSp is filed with the RCS. For VAT, the non-established taxable person must file Form 640 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' with the AEDT, attaching proof of foreign business registration, identity documents for the general partner/managers, and a power of attorney or tax representation mandate. Non-EU persons without an EU establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative before a LU VAT number is issued. The LU VAT identification number has the format LU followed by 8 digits. For Luxembourg income tax, the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default; each partner is separately liable and, to the extent taxable in Luxembourg, must obtain or use a Luxembourg national identification number. If the Article 168bis LIR opacity election is made, the SCSp obtains its own CIT/NWT identification number from the ACD upon filing the election and registration in the RCS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Under Article 168bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), a fiscally transparent partnership such as the SCSp may file an irrevocable opacity election to be treated as a Luxembourg corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; otherwise, the SCSp is fiscally transparent and its profits are taxed at partner level. Partnership-level municipal business tax may nevertheless arise if the SCSp carries on a commercial activity. Anti-hybrid rules implementing ATAD (notably in Article 168sexies LIR) restrict hybrid mismatch outcomes and may re-characterise the SCSp where counterparties treat it inconsistently. For VAT, separate election forms apply for VAT grouping as a single taxable person (assujetti unique) and for the EU OSS scheme.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual taxable turnover for domestically established small enterprises under the Luxembourg small-business exemption; no de minimis threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services or to intra-EU acquisitions, so foreign entities making taxable supplies of MMORPG digital services in Luxembourg/EU must register or use the OSS from the first euro

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where goods are physically exported from the EU to a non-EU destination and where cross-border services fall outside the Luxembourg place-of-supply rules. For MMORPG digital services: (i) B2B supplies to a business customer established outside the EU are zero-rated or outside Luxembourg VAT scope; (ii) B2C electronic supplies to non-EU consumers are outside the EU VAT scope and may be reported as export services; (iii) B2C digital supplies to EU consumers cannot be zero-rated and must be declared via the Union OSS at the consumer's member state rate. The taxpayer must demonstrably establish the customer's business/residential location, the actual performance of the service, and receipt of payment from outside Luxembourg/EU.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

Luxembourg imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing is permitted in B2B transactions provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are assured, e.g. via EDI or PEPPOL. E-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to Luxembourg public-sector entities in the format required by European standard EN 16931/PEPPOL. VAT returns must be filed electronically via the eCDF module on MyGuichet.lu; monthly or quarterly returns are due by the twentieth day of the following month or quarter, with annual recapitulative statements where applicable. OSS filings follow the standard monthly calendar of the member state of identification.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow zero-rating if the export or service export is not substantiated within the statutory records period, assessing undeclared VAT at the 17% standard rate, plus default interest of 0.6% per month and administrative fines ranging from 10% to 50% of the tax due, rising up to 100% in cases of deliberate concealment. Fraudulent documentation may also trigger criminal tax evasion penalties under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, joint liability of partners/managers, and mandatory AML reporting of forged invoicing. The ordinary VAT assessment limitation period is five years, extended to ten years for fraud, enabling long-period retrospective clawbacks and reclassification of zero-rated flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full and unrestricted currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange control authorisation, currency licence, tax clearances, or prior approval is required for remittance of profits, distribution of proceeds, or repatriation of investor capital. The introduction of capital into or withdrawal of capital from the SCSp is subject only to ordinary anti-money-laundering verification by the receiving bank, EU/UN sanctions screening, and optional balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the bank. Profit remittance to non-resident partners faces no legal hurdle once statutory accounts, partner resolutions, and tax declarations are in order.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio in Luxembourg or at EU level. Export receipts Shell may be held in the original currency, converted freely, or retained in an operational foreign-currency account. If balance-of-payments reporting is applicable under ECB statistical requirements, the reporting obligation is discharged by the resident bank on behalf of the client at monthly aggregate reporting cycles; no enterprise-level declaration is required for ordinary Apple App Store or Google Play payouts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as national central bank integrated in the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and payment institutions; there is no separate foreign exchange administration in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High subject to strict constraints. An EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passporting into Luxembourg or a Luxembourg-licensed EMI may open multi-currency accounts fully remotely and can process large Apple App Store and Google Play settlement flows, provided the EMI obtains CSSF-authorised custody and safeguarding. However, a non-EEA offshore digital EMI is generally not viable as a primary treasury bank because it cannot legally provide safeguarded client-money accounts in Luxembourg, conduct FX forwards, or issue irrevocable payment guarantees. Such offshore EMIs should be considered only as auxiliary collection wallets, never as the sole clearing and treasury repository.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate statutory rate (17% national CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge on the national CIT + average municipal business tax of 6.75% in Luxembourg City; the combined rate varies within a municipal range such as 20.97% to 26.84%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by a Luxembourg corporate entity. However, a SCSp is fiscally transparent: distributions from the SCSp to partners are generally not classified as dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at the partnership level. Where the SCSp engages in a commercial activity and maintains a Luxembourg permanent establishment, profits allocated to non-resident partners may be subject to municipal business tax but not to dividend WHT unless the partner is a Luxembourg tax-resident corporate shareholder under anti-abuse grounds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) and Master File are mandatory when the group’s annual consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000 or equivalent in Luxembourg tax practice. For a lower-turnover SCSp group, the Luxembourg Local File / documentation obligation arises where controlled related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 for each transaction type, related-party trade balances exceed EUR 250,000, or aggregate controlled revenues exceed EUR 500,000 per fiscal year. In all cases, contemporaneous documentation demonstrating arm’s length compliance for any material intercompany transaction with related parties is required.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations, but its administrative and anti-abuse doctrine requires genuine economic substance for treaty entitlement and CIT residency. The ‘substance doctrine’ applies through Article 6 of the Luxembourg General Tax Code and EU GAAR, requiring acts be lawful, non-abusive, and not solely directed at tax avoidance. For a SCSp engaged in hardcore MMORPG global publishing, indicators include: real offices, qualified staff making strategic game IP decisions, board/general partner meetings in Luxembourg, local bank accounts, comprehensive agreements, and functional management of in-app purchase revenue and platform payment rails. Absence of substance can trigger denial of tax residence, DTA relief denial, disallowance of IP deductions, and a 10% to 75% tax adjustment penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 5,000 per false return; criminal fine of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years under Article 93 AO; interest for late payment at 0.55% per month; in aggravated cases, tax uplift of up to 100%.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be jointly and severally liable for the tax debt where they ordered or caused the false declaration. In criminal proceedings, directors face personal criminal fines, confiscation orders, travel bans while proceedings are pending, and imprisonment; the general partner of an SCSp is personally liable for partnership debts, including tax assessments, without limit.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Lack of Arm’s Length Evidence

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by the Luxembourg tax authority to arm’s length standard, additional taxable income allocated to the SCSp or Luxembourg PE, penalties for failure to maintain documentation ranging from 10% to 50% of the adjusted profit, plus monthly late interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and compliance officer may be personally liable for restitution of the under-declared tax if they knowingly approved the inflated royalty or intercompany invoice trail; courts may impose bans on directorial positions and injunctions against leaving the jurisdiction pending tax audit completion.
Shell Company / Abusive Tax Structured DTA Traps

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg authorities and foreign DTA partners may refuse treaty benefits; the tax authority will impose the 15% withholding tax on deemed distributions, disallow IP-box exemption, retroactively reassess profit with 100% top-up, and apply penalties up to 10% to 75% of the corrected tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and the SCSp general partner may face civil restitution for the resulting tax liability as de facto managers; personal criminal liability can include imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated tax fraud committed through a sham structure; international tax cooperation can lead to asset freezes and travel bans.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / UBO Register Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 to EUR 5,000,000 for legal entities and up to EUR 5,000,000 for responsible persons for AML violations; criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years and criminal fines of up to EUR 150,000; the UBO filing omissions lead to additional penalties of up to EUR 1,000 per month or more; authorities may freeze funds and refuse tax clearances.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director of a corporate general partner are personally liable for unjustified distributions, restitution orders, and AML penalties. Travel bans may be issued in the context of criminal asset recovery; imprisonment for personal involvement in laundering proceeds from unlawful in-app activities is available under the Luxembourg Criminal Code.
Unreported Payment Rails / Fiscal Transparency Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Immediate assessment based on estimated income; late filing fines; tax interest 0.55% per month; for a second offense, administrative fine doubles to EUR 10,000; refusal to comply may result in mandatory withholding tax collection from the SCSp’s bank accounts and injunction of payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind the SCSp corporate veil: the unlimited liability of the general partner applies to tax debts, and personal civil liability for unpaid taxes follows if the directors acted in bad faith or with gross negligence. Criminal exposure for willful non-payment can carry imprisonment up to 5 years and a travel ban during criminal investigation.

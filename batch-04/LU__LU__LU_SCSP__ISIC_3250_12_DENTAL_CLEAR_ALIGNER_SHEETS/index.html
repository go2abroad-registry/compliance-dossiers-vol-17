Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual registered-office services are permissible for a non-regulated SCSp used as a pure holding and financing vehicle, provided that the virtual office service provider has a physical presence in Luxembourg, receives and dispatches mail in accordance with the RCS rules, and permanently maintains the statutory seat as a domicile address registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers. No mailbox-only setup will satisfy the 'commercial substance' expectation under the RBE and NAVR rules if the entity carries out actual thermoplastic aligner manufacturing, intra-EEA product distribution, or invoicing activities. SSE indicators will then require a local operational decision centre, full bookkeeping records, board minutes, and substance in the form of employees or credible management functions. For a purely passive holding SCSp at the top of a dental-aligner manufacturing group, the virtual office may satisfy the registered statutory seat, but the tax consolidation and VAT treatment matter must be reviewed; the risk of qualification as 'fiction' under Circular LIR No. 56 is materially elevated. The acceptability is therefore conditional and fact-specific; a true virtual office for an operational medical-device business is not permitted.

Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, including the special limited partnership (SCSp) provisions introduced by the Law of 23 July 2016; Articles 916-1 et seq. and applicable partnership-law sections.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif / partnership agreement and the RCS electronic constitution filing form for a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp), followed by publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum subscription or paid-in capital for an SCSp. Partners freely determine capital commitments in the partnership agreement. Luxembourg law does not require a bank-escrowed or blocked account for formation, but the partnership agreement may impose payment conditions and the manager must maintain a capital contribution register for AML and accounting purposes.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) / Administration des contributions directes (ACD); e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the AEDT e-space (https://paiement.etat.lu / eTVA)

E-government filing portal

1) File business incorporation with Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via notary/practitioner on MyGuichet.lu to obtain the 11-digit national business/company registration number (numéro d'immatriculation). 2) Register for VAT by filing Form 640 (immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT for non-established taxable persons, or Form 640b / standard registration for the SCSp; the AEDT issues the 14-character VAT number prefix 'LU'. 3) SCSp also obtains an EORI number for customs purposes. 4) Foreign entities without local permanent establishment must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative (représentant fiscal) for the VAT/customs procedure if required by AEDT.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) File business incorporation with Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via notary/practitioner on MyGuichet.lu to obtain the 11-digit national business/company registration number (numéro d'immatriculation). 2) Register for VAT by filing Form 640 (immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT for non-established taxable persons, or Form 640b / standard registration for the SCSp; the AEDT issues the 14-character VAT number prefix 'LU'. 3) SCSp also obtains an EORI number for customs purposes. 4) Foreign entities without local permanent establishment must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative (représentant fiscal) for the VAT/customs procedure if required by AEDT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box'. Under Art. 175 LIR the SCSp is fiscally transparent (pass-through) by default; Art. 165 LIR permits an irrevocable option for the partnership to become subject to corporate income tax (opaque status) when specific conditions are met. For VAT, the SCSp is a distinct taxable person under Art. 4 of the VAT Law, allowing the partnership to hold its own VAT/EORI registration even while income-tax-transparent; hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD) apply to deny benefits where the same payment is deductible in Luxembourg and non-taxable abroad.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover local small-business franchise (Art. 57 VAT Law) for Luxembourg-established businesses; zero threshold for non-established foreign suppliers and for intra-EU distance selling unless the EUR 10,000 OSS Union scheme threshold is met; EUR 100,000 was replaced for goods distance selling by the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold since 1 July 2021

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the supply/export of dental-clear-aligner sheets (multilayer thermoformable sheets) when (i) the goods are dispatched/transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU, (ii) the exporter holds an accepted export customs declaration (SAD/ENS) and transport documentation (CMR, bill of lading or air waybill) proving departure from EU customs territory within 90 days of supply, and (iii) the confirmatory report from the customs office (visa de sortie) is obtained within the legal timeframe. Intra-EU supplies can be zero-rated only if the customer's valid EU VAT number is evidenced in the EC Sales List/VIES and the invoice is issued by the 15th day of the month following the supply; services ancillary to the export (freight, handling) are zero-rated to non-EU recipients.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ENS, via LU customs gateway)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (proof of departure from EU)
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms and export zero-rating annotation
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer proof (SWIFT MT103 or card settlement record evidencing cross-border payment)
  • EC Sales List / Recapitulative Statement (for intra-EU supplies) and customer VAT number validation record

No real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping ('validation') regime. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via Peppol BIS 3.0 (and/or PEPPOL-compliant format) since 1 January 2021 for invoices to Luxembourg public entities. B2B e-invoicing is optional; AEDT accepts EDI/Peppol/structured XML on request. Invoice archiving mandatory for 10 years; per-article tax records must support deductibility and zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the euro as legal tender and full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions by virtue of EU Treaty arts. 63–65 TFEU. Profit remittance, loan repayments, and dividend-like distributions to non-resident partners are unrestricted; there is no exchange licensing requirement, but bank documentation and CRS/FATCA declarations are mandatory. The AMLA-driven compliance framework requires the SCSp to identify the unlimited partner and the economically beneficial owner for any repatriation flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any mandatory surrender of export proceeds, any statutory conversion ratio, or an inward remittance timeline. Exporters (including the clear aligner sheet manufacturer) may retain foreign currency in licensed bank accounts or convert freely through authorized intermediaries subject to BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg) in conjunction with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the banking prudential and FX administration authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as an auxiliary banking vector for platform-driven card settlements (e.g., Stripe/Adyen, Wirecard, Payoneer) and for daily trade disbursements; low feasibility as the sole treasury rail for high-value international trade. A licensed EU EMI/PI may provide virtual IBANs and onward SWIFT/SEPA execution, but cannot extend bank guarantees, hold settlement balances above fallback thresholds, or replace a regulated Luxembourg credit institution for documentary collections and letters of credit. The EMI must itself be regulated under PSD2 and maintain a correspondence account with a tier-1 bank inside SEPA.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Provide a certified extract from the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE), the partnership deed and management agreement, a signed ownership-chain spreadsheet, passport copies of every partner holding more than 10%, and a formal legal opinion on the SCSp's transparency issued by a Luxembourg law firm.

Root cause: Source of initial capital contribution being cross-border or non-European, raising anti-money-laundering risk.

Defense evidence: Produce audited financial statements of the contributor, six months of bank reference statements from a regulated financial institution, evidence of the economic purpose of the injection (purchase of thermoforming machinery/raw materials), tax identification numbers, and a notarised capital commitment certificate.
Unusual trade activity for the stated dental clear aligner manufacturing scope, such as high turnover without physical goods or large dealings with high-risk jurisdictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route is SEPA Credit Transfer for euro contributions under EUR 100k, and SWIFT MT103 for higher-value injections; beneficiary should be the SCSp's operating account at a Luxembourg bank. The wire instruction must reference the SCSp's RCS number, the country of origin, and the European balance-of-payments direct-investment classification 'Equity capital in non-listed enterprise' to ease BCL statistical reporting. The receiving bank must obtain an updated UBO declaration, an executed capital commitment schedule, and prior notification of the injection if the amount exceeds the bank's internal threshold. Foreign-currency injections may be converted to euros on the spot market by the receiving bank; no prior central bank approval is required.Trade payments for raw materials (PETG/PU sheets) and components should use SWIFT MT103/MT202 for wire transfers; for equipment purchases, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit payable at sight (UCP 600) is the standard rail. Exports of dental clear aligner sheets to EU distributors are cleared through SEPA Instant under IBAN collection; non-EU sales via MT103 or documentary collection. All disbursements require invoice and commercial-contract tie-in and CRS-compliant beneficial owner declarations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (aggregate of 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax, and 6.75% municipal business tax for Luxembourg City); the SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax on its profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions made by an SCSp to its partners because the partnership is fiscally transparent and profit distribution is treated as a withdrawal of contributed capital; by contrast, the ordinary Luxembourg statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 15% for dividends paid by a Luxembourg resident company, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (holding of at least 10% of capital for 12 months) or an applicable double taxation agreement

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 of consolidated group annual turnover (alternative mandatory Master File/Local File triggers: total balance sheet exceeding EUR 43,000,000 or 250 employees); Country-by-Country Report threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain generic Economic Substance Regulations of the type seen in zero-tax offshore centres; substance is enforced through the Luxembourg General Anti-Abuse Rule, ATAD/MLI anti-abuse provisions, the substance requirements in Circular L.I.R. 164/2 for tax rulings, and the jurisdiction's administrative practice requiring real premises, staff, local decision-making, and physical operations for any commercial activity. An SCSp manufacturing dental clear aligner sheets must maintain a genuine Luxembourg establishment and cannot rely on a registered address alone to claim treaty benefits or tax transparency.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent mispricing under LIR Article 164(3)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at fair market value, late payment interest, administrative fines of up to 50% of evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for fiscal fraud carrying fines from EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors who signed false returns or knowingly approved mispriced transfers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and penalties; personal travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment of up to five years may be imposed.
Shell company / artificial lack of substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity under GAAR and ATAD, retroactive denial of treaty benefits, loss of loss-carryforward relief, rescission of any tax ruling, and a non-compliance penalty of up to 25% of the adjusted tax base.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be held personally liable for the entire tax shortfall; they may also face management disqualification, inclusion in DAC6/LuxLeaks cross-border arrangement disclosures, and travel restrictions pending resolution.
AML / KYC breaches on payment rails (Stripe, platform credit cards)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,250,000, criminal penalties of up to 10 years imprisonment for money laundering, RBE non-disclosure fines, forced closure of payment accounts, and revocation of any commercial licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally liable for breaches; they may face civil restitution to investors, travel bans, five-year management disqualification, and seizure of personal assets.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: CbCR non-filing penalty up to EUR 250,000 or €2,500 per month of delay; daily default fines; estimation of taxable income by the administration; 10% surcharge on the resulting tax assessment; criminal proceedings for wilful obstruction.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers are jointly and severally liable for the penalties and the default assessment; tax evasion indictments may result in travel restrictions, personal asset seizure, and imprisonment.
Disguised dividend / excessive management fee repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as taxable partnership profit or deemed dividend; recapture of Luxembourg municipal business tax; potential 15% withholding tax where recharacterised as a Luxembourg-source dividend; interest and penalties up to 10%.

Director personal liability: Partners and directors who authorised the sham payments must personally restore the withholding tax shortfall; they face asset seizure, foreign tax authority notifications, travel bans, and, in serious cases, imprisonment for tax fraud.

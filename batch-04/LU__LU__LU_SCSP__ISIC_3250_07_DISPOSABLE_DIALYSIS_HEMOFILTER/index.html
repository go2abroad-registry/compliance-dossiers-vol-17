Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory capital applies to an SCSp; the capital is freely fixed in the partnership agreement and may be denominated in any currency. No escrow account or bank certificate of capital deposit is required by Luxembourg law; contributions may be called in installments as contractually agreed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) and the municipal building authority prior to commissioning; structural load and building completion certification by an accredited engineer; workplace compliance inspection by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering machine safety, chemical agent exposure, pressure equipment, and medical product labelling; registration of workers with the Association d'assurance accident (AAA) for occupational accident coverage.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital applies to an SCSp; the capital is freely fixed in the partnership agreement and may be denominated in any currency. No escrow account or bank certificate of capital deposit is required by Luxembourg law; contributions may be called in installments as contractually agreed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; official portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident taxable persons must obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA). Application via Form 640/AEDT e-filing on MyGuichet.lu; the SCSp must first obtain a national registration number (matricule) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). A local VAT tax representative may be required for non-EU suppliers.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (no CIT/MBT/NWT at partnership level); it constitutes a separate taxable person for VAT if it performs taxable economic activities. — Non-resident taxable persons must obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA). Application via Form 640/AEDT e-filing on MyGuichet.lu; the SCSp must first obtain a national registration number (matricule) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). A local VAT tax representative may be required for non-EU suppliers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes (no CIT/MBT/NWT at partnership level); it constitutes a separate taxable person for VAT if it performs taxable economic activities.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election under Luxembourg law. An SCSp may opt for VAT liability and register as a taxable person; hybrid mismatch treatment is governed by ATAD provisions. Consent is required from the general partner (gérant) and may be specified in the partnership agreement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No fixed economic turnover threshold for established businesses; any taxable supply triggers compulsory registration. Small enterprises may apply the optional annual turnover franchise of EUR 35,000. Non-residents are subject to the same EUR 35,000 threshold for intra-EU distance sales (cross-border).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to the export of goods outside the EU provided the exporter dispatches or transports the goods out of the EU or a third party acts on the exporter's behalf. Documentation must prove the physical exit from the EU within the applicable timeframe.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Transport documents: Bill of Lading (maritime), Airway Bill (air) or CMR (road)
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate (where applicable)
  • Evidence of non-EU destination (purchase order, import declaration from destination country)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G (public-sector) invoices since mid-2022; no API clearance requirement.

Retrospective adjustment is possible within the general legal limitation period of 5 years (10 years in case of suspected fraud). Incorrect zero-rating triggers the reinstatement of VAT at the standard rate plus default interest and administrative fines; criminal penalties for tax evasion may include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 100,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully liberalised euro-area jurisdiction. There are no exchange control laws, no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts, and no restrictions on current or capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital, Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the Luxembourg 2004 Money-Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Prevention Law (as amended). Profit remittances, dividend distributions, partnership distributions, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds from an SCSp may be freely transferred in euro or any foreign currency. A SCSp that is transparent for corporate income tax purposes will not trigger withholding tax at the partnership level; banking and settlement fees are the only practical friction. Beneficial ownership declarations may be requested by banks to comply with know-your-customer rules, but will not impede convertibility or remittance. There is no surrender requirement, no central bank approval, no foreign investment screening applicable to the manufacturing of disposable dialysis hemofilters (ISIC 3250), and no capital import/export licensing for the underlying goods.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export proceeds from dialysis hemofilter sales may be retained in any currency, converted to euro with the financing bank at freely negotiated market rates, or used directly for offshore trade settlements. The only obligations arise under general tax law: transfer pricing documentation for intra-group cross-border transactions, and the obligation to include export revenues in the commercial accounts of the SCSp for accounting and filing purposes with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) if the SCSp is registered. No specific Export Proceeds Surrender Certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in conjunction with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and foreign exchange matters; macroprudential policy is delegated within the euro area framework of the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium in Luxembourg — foreign fintech EMIs and payment institutions (including digital banking platforms) with an EU/EEA passport may legally operate in Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the EU EMI Directive. However, the CSSF requires a formal branch or agent notification from the home-state competent authority before cross-border services begin. A fully offshore, non-EEA digital EMI without a Luxembourg licence or EEA-passport should be considered not viable as a principal transactional bank. In practice, tier-1 domestic banks require a physical account for the SCSp, while an EMI can be used as a complementary clearing rail for low-value working-capital payments if s activity is limited to payment initiation or e-money issuance and the EMI has secure client asset safeguarding. Legal constraints include: (i) the EMI must be authorised in its EEA home state and passported into Luxembourg; (ii) the EMI may not grant credit in the form of trade finance without a supplementary lending licence; (iii) the SCSp general partner must maintain oversight of AML/KYC outsourcing; (iv) capital escrow for injections from non-EEA countries should be held with a fully licensed credit institution, not an EMI, to preserve status in downstream bank interviews.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Unclear ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) or high-asset-value principals with opaque source of funds

Defense evidence: UBO registry extract (RCS), organisational chart, notarised partnership agreement, passport copies, source-of-funds documentation (audited financial statements, bank references, sale or inheritance deeds), and a written business rationale for the Luxembourg SCSp; each principal must complete a statutory declaration of tax residency, a source-of-wealth questionnaire, and a politically exposed person (PEP) disclosure.
Cross-border trade involving medical devices and settlement to high-risk or sanctioned counties

Root cause: Cross-border trade involving medical devices and settlement to high-risk or sanctioned counties

Defense evidence: Valid ISO 13485 certificate, EU CE mark certificate for the disposable dialysis hemofilter, the full supply-chain contract with counterparty information, invoice and bill-of-lading pack, export/import licences or exemption certificates, and a signed attestation that the counterparty is not subject to EU, UN, US, or Luxembourg sanctions; banks will scan counterparties against the Luxembourg SDN list and the CSSF consolidated list.
Partnership structure (SCSp) has no minimum capital and may be viewed as a shell vehicle

Root cause: Partnership structure (SCSp) has no minimum capital and may be viewed as a shell vehicle

Defense evidence: Substance dossier containing: the registered office lease contract in Luxembourg, evidence of a local corporate secretary or qualified ELF, anticipated manufacturing or procurement activity related to actual order commitments, cash-flow projection showing real inventory and trade receivables, tax filings or preliminary accounting ledgers, and minutes appointing the national authorised manager. Significant cash-flows from multiple non-EEA counterparties in a regulated medical sector

Root cause: Significant cash-flows from multiple non-EEA counterparties in a regulated medical sector

Ultra large inbound capital injection above the bank's due-diligence reporting threshold

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing should be structured as (i) a capital contribution from the limited partner to the SCSp capital escrow account held at a tier-1 Luxembourg bank; wire instructions must carry SWIFT tag 50K (ordering customer) and tag 59 (beneficiary account), with a purpose code reflecting 'limited partnership capital contribution' in the remittance information (e.g. EU BOP code BKSF or general investment under CSSF statistical requirements); (ii) for any transfer exceeding EUR 10,000, the receiving bank requires the UBO and source-of-funds declaration prepared by the ELF/sponsor. If capital is injected in USD or another non-EUR currency, the correspondent bank must be notified of the final euro conversion; BCL reporting obligations under the Balance of Payments (BOP) framework oblige the bank to identify the transfer as a direct or portfolio investment transaction. For SCSp, the cash injection should not be routed through a personal or unrelated intermediary account, as this triggers enhanced due diligence and delays. The optimal rail is a same-day or target-2 settlement to the Luxembourg bank's nostro account, using the SCSp's IBAN, followed by confirmation to the commercial register RCS if the contribution changes registered capital or share premium.For trade settlements for disposable dialysis hemofilter exports, the standard rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 single direct transfer for open-account payments to foreign suppliers of polyethersulfone (PESU), hollow-fibre spinning machines, potting adhesives, and shipping services; each MT103 must contain the required purpose code matching Luxembourg BOP classification (e.g. 'medical supply purchase' or 'raw material import') and should bill through an electronic banking B2B interface; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) opened by the Luxembourg bank on behalf of the SCSp in favour of the supplier, governed by UCP 600, with documents drafted to match the SCSp's trade invoices; (3) standby LC or guarantees for OEM distributors when the SCSp supplies private-label products to hospitals; all settlement to non-EU countries should be made by telegraphic transfer (TT/Wire) through the bank's risk-controlled correspondent network, ensuring that the beneficiary bank is not on the corresponding sanctions list. For all outbound transactions above EUR 10,000, the bank's AML transaction-monitoring rail will flag the Bonafide transaction; the SCSp should maintain a central digital proof database with invoice, customs entry, goods receipt note, and transport history for KYC/sanction reviews. The entity should never split trade payments into multiple sub-threshold transfers to avoid audit triggers.Plain vanilla foreign exchange forwards (contract-negotiated EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, EUR/JPY) with a registered Luxembourg credit institution or an eligible EEA FX counterparty; no physical delivery of capital is required for the SCSp if the hedging contract is explicitly linked to a transactional commercial exposure, such as purchase of PESU resin or a confirmed export invoice.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are permissible when trading certain restricted or non-convertible currencies (e.g. emerging-market currencies) as long as the SCSp contractually settles the differential in EUR via a CSTO-compliant bank; NDFs must be booked as derivatives and marked-to-market in the SCSp's accounting records; no NDF may be used as a disguised speculative position without underlying export/procurement exposure.Cross-currency swaps (EUR vs USD or EUR vs CNY) are permissible for matching long-term liquidity mismatches, such as financing a foreign production prepayment while receiving export proceeds in hard currency; the swap must be collateralised under the Credit Support Annex (CSA) and approved by the SCSp's treasury risk policy.Commodity and freight hedging in the form of futures on polymer prices or Baltic Exchange freight indices, if cleared through a recognised clearing house (e.g. Eurex, LCH), may be used to stabilise the input cost of the dialysis hemofilter supply chain, but such instruments are ancillary and must be separately authorised by the Luxembourg bank's credit approval documentation.Interest rate swaps or caps on EURIBOR-linked financing lines may be used solely to hedge the interest-rate risk of the working-capital facility supporting the manufacturing cycle.

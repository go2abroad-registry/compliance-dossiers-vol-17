Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - the SCSp has no legal minimum capital requirement; capital is freely fixed by the partnership agreement and may be contributed in cash, in kind or as services. No statutory escrow or blocked account is required, although a bank certificate evidencing the limited partners' initial contribution is recommended for RCS and financing purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) through the communal fire commission, verifying manual and automatic extinguishers, commercial kitchen extraction-hood suppression, emergency exits, alarming and evacuation routes; structural load inspection certificate for kitchen equipment, hoods and refrigeration installations issued by a certified civil matter expert; occupational health and safety clearance delivered by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering ventilation, electrical conformity, sanitary facilities, thermal stress and manual-handling risk assessments under Articles L-210-1 et seq. of the Luxembourg Labour Code.

Paid-in capital rule: None - the SCSp has no legal minimum capital requirement; capital is freely fixed by the partnership agreement and may be contributed in cash, in kind or as services. No statutory escrow or blocked account is required, although a bank certificate evidencing the limited partners' initial contribution is recommended for RCS and financing purposes.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The euro is the lawful currency and is freely transferable without exchange-control approval. For the SCSp structure, profit remittances to resident and non-resident partners are unrestricted; the only conditioning factors are (i) CSSF/BCL statistical balance-of-payments reporting for each cross-border flow of EUR 12,500 or more and (ii) compliance with anti-money-laundering rules and the Luxembourg law of 12 July 2013 on the Special Limited Partnership. No capital-account license or prior authorisation is required for foreign direct investment into a Luxembourg SCSp, nor for distributions of profits, repayment of partner loans, or liquidation proceeds. The ghost kitchen's delivery aggregator receivables are current-account services income, payable in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency at the parties' election.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, deposit, or repatriation timeline for export or services receipts. Invoices settled in EUR or foreign currency may be retained offshore at the commercial discretion of the SCSp. The sole obligation is indirect: banks report cross-border payment statistics to the BCL, and the SCSp must be able to evidence the commercial substance of aggregator settlements on demand.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in conjunction with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as banking and payment-entity supervisor. No separate foreign-exchange control administration exists; Luxembourg adheres to EU TFEU Articles 63-65 on free movement of capital and the euro-zone single currency regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. CSSF-authorised electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., licensed EMI platforms operating under the Luxembourg law of 10 November 2009 on payment services) may provide IBAN accounts, virtual account management, and aggregator payout collection. However, a pure fintech EMI cannot serve as the SCSp's primary treasury bank because the SCSp has no minimum capital and no legal personality, which triggers enhanced due diligence; EMIs are legally prohibited from accepting deposits and from executing credit operations. Their viable role is limited to the collection layer for UberEats and DoorDash settlement sweeps and to automated currency conversion at specified anchor rates, while Tier-1 correspondent clearing rails must settle final liquidity. Cross-border EMIs on passported EU licences are usable for low-value daily receivable sweeps, but the master treasury account must remain at an authorised credit institution under CSSF consolidated supervision.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from partners must be routed through correspondent banking channels using SWIFT MT103 or TARGET2 transfers. In the SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information), the payment must state 'CONSTITUTION OF CAPITAL - SCSP [Registered Office Reference]' and the beneficiary account must be the SCSp's dedicated capital escrow account opened under the partnership's name. For BCL balance-of-payments reporting, contributions above EUR 12,500 are coded as direct investment equity capital (BOP category: FDI inflow, equity, euro), and the bank will require the remitter's name to match the partner register of the SCSp. The escrow account releases funds to the operating account only upon presentation of the notarised contribution statement. For non-EU inbound wires in USD, the receiving Luxembourg bank converts through its own FX engine, applying the ECB reference rate plus a defined spread; the SCSp must instruct the bank to settle the conversion as 'spot delivery' and to allocate the proceeds on the same value date to the operational treasury ledger.Clearing rails comprise: (i) SEPA Credit Transfers for all EUR-denominated intra-EU payments to suppliers, landlords, and staff-leasing agencies; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments for raw materials, imported kitchen equipment, and brand royalties outside the euro-zone; (iii) commercial letters of credit governed by UCP600 for high-value equipment purchases from non-EU overseas suppliers; (iv) SEPA B2B Direct Debit for recurring utilities; and (v) the aggregator payout rails of UberEats and DoorDash, which sweep merchant settlements directly into the SCSp's segregated collection account. Disbursement policy requires that outbound payments be initiated only after final aggregator settlement and the expiry of the chargeback-protection window; intra-day liquidity is managed through a notional liquidity pool at the Luxembourg lead bank to ensure that MT103 payments are never released on an unsecured intra-day overdraft basis.Outright deliverable EUR/USD forwards with tenors of 1 to 12 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a CSSF-authorised credit institution; used to hedge committed aggregator settlement revenues denominated in USD.Outright deliverable EUR/GBP forwards, with the same tenors and documentation, for UK-DoorDash and Deliveroo-style payout exposure.FX swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) used exclusively for treasury liquidity management and matching of the SCSp's two-currency receivable and payable cycles.European-style EUR/USD currency options for contingent currency exposure arising from franchise acquisitions and equipment purchases, where a forward would create a firm commitment risk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for exotic currency exposures (EUR/THB, EUR/PHP, EUR/AED) where a deliverable forward market is unavailable in the SCSp's operating jurisdictions; such instruments must be cash-settled and documented under a cross-currency master agreement.Cross-currency interest rate swaps solely to hedge matched lease or debt-financing obligations of the SCSp, provided the notional amount does not exceed the underlying committed liability and the counterparty meets a minimum credit rating of A-.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg CIT rate for the 2025 financial year, composed of 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT, and 6.75% municipal business tax applicable in Luxembourg City; the exact municipal component varies by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a Luxembourg resident corporate entity; a transparent SCSp is not an opaque corporation, so partnership distributions to partners are generally treated as capital allocations and are exempt from dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed on or before the last day of the sixth month following the fiscal year-end, unless an extension is granted in writing by the Luxembourg tax administration.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - RCS and identification Legal name, SCSp registered office, Luxembourg trade registry number, date of incorporation and fiscal year-end. Non-resident general partners must append their foreign corporate ID, tax residence certificate and, where applicable, VAT number; a local tax representative may complete and file the return on their behalf. Late filing penalties range from EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 per return; a daily surcharge of up to EUR 50 can be applied until the return is submitted; late payment interest accrues at 0.9% per month or fraction thereof; gross negligence surcharge can reach 50% of the understated tax, increasing to 100% in cases of deliberate evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For taxpayers within the administrative scope of the Luxembourg TP guidelines, a contemporaneous Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000, while a Master File is statistically expected for groups with consolidated revenues above EUR 750,000,000 under the Country-by-Country Reporting framework; for a medium-sized SCSp ghost kitchen network, intercompany platform fees, brand royalties and delivery commissions above EUR 100,000 must be supported by an internal pricing documentation memorandum.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the Gulf/UAE sense, but the same substance doctrine is enforced through the EU ATAD GAAR, Luxembourg domestic GAAR and treaty abuse case law. A ghost kitchen SCSp must demonstrate local decision-making, employee presence, operational premises, delivery management capacity, and genuine commercial activity in Luxembourg; otherwise profits are reattributed to the ultimate beneficial owners and claims under tax treaties may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion - false returns and hidden delivery income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud fines up to EUR 250,000 and recovery of the entire evaded CIT, plus a fraud penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax; late payment penalties continue to accrue on the outstanding amount.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and directors are jointly and severally liable for the tax delinquency if they signed or directed the false declarations; imprisonment from 2 to 10 years, civil restitution of the evaded amounts, travel bans pending payment, and prohibition from managing Luxembourg companies for a fixed period.
Shell company abuse and artificial structuring

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration will disregard the SCSp under the GAAR, reassess the true operator as the taxable owner, impose CIT at up to 24.94%, deny deductions for all intercompany platform and royalty fees, and apply a gross negligence penalty of 50%.

Director personal liability: The general partner becomes personally liable for the reassessed corporate taxes if assets were stripped from the SCSp; civil restitution orders are issued for the profit shifted, travel restrictions may be imposed until the treasury claim is secured, and criminal sanctions include 1 to 5 years imprisonment.
AML and KYC failure on online delivery payouts

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties under Luxembourg AML legislation of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover; suspension or revocation of the mandatory food business health license and commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held civilly liable for laundering the proceeds of unlicensed food operations, subject to confiscation of the delivery revenues, a five-year ban on serving as a director, and imprisonment from 2 to 5 years for intentional AML breaches.
Transfer pricing non-compliance and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office disallows the uncontrolled intercompany deductions, computes an upward adjustment to taxable income, imposes penalties of up to EUR 10,000 for non-production of scheduled TP documentation and daily fines for continued non-compliance.

Director personal liability: In an insolvent SCSp, the taxable general partner is personally assessed for the underpaid tax; directors who signed audit reports knowing the transfer pricing positions lack substance may face criminal fines, restitution to the state, and a maximum five-year prison sentence.

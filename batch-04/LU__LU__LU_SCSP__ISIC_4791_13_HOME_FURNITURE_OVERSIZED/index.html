Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SCSp. The partnership agreement freely determines the amount of capital or the contributions in cash or in kind; a limited partner may not make an industry (skills) contribution. No escrow account is required by Luxembourg law, but cash contributions must be actually paid into a bank account in the name of the SCSp, and a banker's certificate is customary evidence for the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the municipal fire safety commission is required for any premises accommodating employees or the public. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income and corporate taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and excise; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via lbr.lu for entity registration and issuance of the national identification matricule; primary e-filing portals: pfs.guichet.lu (direct tax), aedt.public.lu (VAT filings and guidance), and guichet.lu (central administrative procedures).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SCSp limited partnership agreement (contrat de société) between at least one general partner (commandité), who has unlimited joint liability, and at least one limited partner (commanditaire); no minimum capital is required and no notarial deed is mandatory unless the contribution comprises real estate. Step 2: File the incorporation record with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) through lbr.lu so the partnership obtains the 13-digit national business matricule, which serves as the entity identification for corporate and tax registration. Step 3: Within one month of registration, file the beneficial ownership declaration in the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE). Step 4: Apply for VAT registration with the AEDT using Form 1908 or via the LBR portal if annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT) or if voluntary OSS/IOSS registration is required; the AEDT issues the LU-prefixed VAT identification number. Step 5: Each foreign partner obtains a Luxembourg partner-specific matricule (natural person: numéro de dossier; legal person: matricule) only when the partner is resident in Luxembourg, has a Luxembourg permanent establishment, or is otherwise required to file a Luxembourg return; non-resident partners may otherwise rely on their home-jurisdiction TIN. Step 6: Confirm that the payment rails (Stripe and Affirm BNPL) are incorporated as SaaS counterparties of the SCSp and do not create a Luxembourg financial-sector licence or AML registration obligation for the furniture trade itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The SCSp is a fiscal-transparency vehicle under Luxembourg law. It has no separate legal personality and is treated as a look-through entity for Luxembourg income tax purposes; the SCSp itself is not subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) or net wealth tax (IF). Each general and limited partner is taxed directly on its distributive share of net income: resident partners are fully taxed in Luxembourg; non-resident partners are taxed in Luxembourg only to the extent a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate is held, and no Luxembourg withholding tax applies on profit distributions by a transparent SCSp. For VAT purposes, however, the SCSp is an autonomous taxable person (assujetti) for supplies made in its own name and is therefore separately registered and files returns in its own VAT ID. Territorial scope: transparent allocation follows the partnership agreement and pushes income to the respective partners' jurisdictions; realisation income of a non-resident partner from disposing of SCSp interests is generally outside Luxembourg taxing power absent real property situated in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

Domestic law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; transparency of the SCSp is statutory and automatic under the Law of 23 July 2016 on commercial companies and the normal rules of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR). Available mechanisms: (a) an election under LIR Art. 175 allowing a partnership to opt for corporate (opaque) taxation, practically restricted to partnerships whose partners are all fully taxable Luxembourg corporate entities; (b) hybrid mismatch and ATAD rules (transposed by the Law of 21 December 2018) which require alignment where the SCSp is fiscally transparent in Luxembourg but opaque in a partner's home jurisdiction, together with a reporting obligation for qualifying hybrid mismatches and structured arrangements; (c) U.S. federal classification election under Treas. Reg. Section 301.7701-3 because the SCSp is not a per se corporation and may be classified by U.S. partners as a partnership or corporation; and (d) a Luxembourg VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Art. 62 of the VAT law enabling the SCSp and related enterprises to file as one VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for the small-business exemption under Art. 57 of the Luxembourg VAT law; a SCSp whose turnover exceeds this threshold must mandatorily register for VAT. Non-resident suppliers without a Luxembourg establishment cannot invoke the small-business exemption. For intra-Community B2C distance sales of furniture to other EU Member States, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold (Art. 58(2) VAT law) applies: turnover below this amount is taxable in Luxembourg at 17%, while turnover above it must be declared through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal in the Member State of the consumer. Imports of goods up to EUR 150 may be declared under the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) scheme; import VAT on oversized goods entering through Luxembourg warehouses is due and may be deferred and deducted in the same return under Arts. 75 et seq. of the VAT law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of oversized furniture and home decor by the SCSp to non-EU destinations are eligible for the 0% export rate under Art. 53(I)(1) of the Luxembourg VAT law. Three cumulative conditions must be satisfied: (i) the goods are physically dispatched to a destination outside the EU; (ii) the goods leave the EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch; and (iii) contemporaneous documentary proof of export is retained. Where the SCSp sells online to private consumers in the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, the Gulf states, the United States or other third countries and ships from its Luxembourg warehouse, zero-rating is claimable. Ancillary services directly bundled into the supply and exported goods price, including freight, packing, handling and customs brokerage, also qualify for the 0% rate. Intra-EU B2B deliveries to registered dealers with confirmed VAT ID are zero-rated with EC Sales List filing; intra-EU B2C distance sales are taxed at destination via OSS. Domestic deliveries in Luxembourg remain standard-rated at 17%; a misfiled customer VAT ID or absence of transport evidence converts the supply into a taxable sale.

  • Customs export declaration (Export Single Administrative Document — EXS) with assigned Movement Reference Number (MRN) evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note demonstrating the transport route from the Luxembourg warehouse to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the oversize furniture items, quantities, values, and contractual place of delivery
  • Purchase and delivery records from the e-commerce platform confirming the customer as a non-EU consumer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank settlement evidence (including Stripe or Affirm BNPL payout files) showing the sales proceeds credited to the SCSp's account, normally within 180 days of dispatch
  • Foreign customs import documentation or certificate of arrival from the destination country confirming physical receipt outside the EU

Luxembourg has not implemented real-time fiscal clearance, digitally stamped invoices, or continuous transaction controls comparable to Italy or Hungary. Electronic invoicing to central government buyers is mandatory via Peppol (EN 16931-compliant) under the B2G e-invoicing mandate; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, using Peppol, EDI or PDF, subject to authenticity and integrity requirements under Art. 81(3) of the VAT law. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative, which will eventually mandate standardised B2B e-invoicing and digital VAT reporting, is being monitored but is not yet transposed in Luxembourg. VAT returns are filed electronically; OSS/IOSS schemes require periodic electronic declarations at the portal level.

The AEDT rigorously audits export zero-rating claims and documentation. If the export evidence is missing, inconsistent, or falsified, the VAT at 17% is retrospectively clawed back on the full supply value, together with statutory default interest and late-filing penalties; administrative penalties typically range from 10% to 50% of the VAT at risk depending on the degree of fault. Intentionally fraudulent zero-rating declarations constitute tax fraud under Art. 175 of the Luxembourg General Tax Act (Abgabenordnung) and potentially criminal fraud under the Luxembourg Criminal Code, carrying custodial sentences of up to five years and substantial criminal fines; the standard five-year limitation period for reassessment is extended to ten years in fraud cases. Although Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control statute mandating repatriation, the AEDT and AML compliance practice treat unremitted export proceeds beyond 180 days as an audit indicator warranting transfer-pricing and reality-of-sale review.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully liberal euro-area jurisdiction: current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted under Articles 63-66 TFEU. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation for FX conversions, no restrictions on non-resident ownership of an SCSp, and no profit-remittance licensing requirements. EUR conversions for USD, GBP, and CNY are available at market rates through any CSSF-supervised bank. For a fiscally transparent SCSp, allocations of profit to non-resident partners do not normally attract Luxembourg withholding tax, and profit distributions may be settled freely via SEPA or SWIFT. The only administrative constraints are balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL, AML/UBO filing obligations with the Luxembourg Business Registers, and substantiation of the tax-transparent character of the partnership before the receiving/scoring institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no minimum retention period, and no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR. The SCSp may lawfully hold 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds in its Luxembourg accounts or in foreign-currency nostro balances for working capital and supplier settlement. However, all cross-border receipts must be correctly classified for BCL balance-of-payments statistics; the Luxembourg account-holding bank automatically reports customer transactions above materiality thresholds (commonly EUR 250,000 monthly cumulative), and the SCSp must provide trade-documentation support (invoices, transport evidence, customs declarations) to evidence that the flows relate to genuine retail-e-commerce trade in oversized furniture under ISIC 4791.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions for AML/CFT and prudential conduct, while monetary and foreign-exchange policy competence rests with the Eurosystem for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for EEA-licensed and PSD2-passporting digital EMIs and payment institutions (for example Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, Wise Business, and MultiPass), which can open the SCSp account remotely and provide multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT/SEPA execution, and competitive EUR/USD/GBP/CNH conversion. These institutions are viable for the collection side of e-commerce treasury (Stripe settlements and BNPL disbursements) and for cross-border supplier payments. However, they are not credit institutions: they cannot issue documentary commercial letters of credit, provide committed working-capital or receivable-finance facilities, or deliver the same creditor protection and banking resolution stability as a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank. Non-EEA EMIs without an EU passport or without a Luxembourg branch cannot lawfully open accounts for the SCSp directly; their traffic must be intermediated by an EEA-licensed acquirer or partner bank. In all cases, the SCSp must satisfy the same RBE beneficial-ownership filing and source-of-funds evidencing obligations, and banks treating the SCSp as a non-financial partnership will apply individualised KYC rather than automated fintech onboarding.

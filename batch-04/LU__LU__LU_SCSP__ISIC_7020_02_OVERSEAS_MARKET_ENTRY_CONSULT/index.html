Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration - for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and withholding obligations; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority - for VAT and registration duties; both operating under the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance. Digital access is provided through the MyGuichet.lu national e-government portal and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) portal (lbr.lu) for RCS filings, statutory filings and legal entity data.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Formation/registration: the Luxembourg SCSp or a foreign branch is filed electronically with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); each registered entity is assigned a unique 13-digit national identification number (matricule / numéro d'immatriculation), which constitutes the primary statutory tax and legal identifier. Step 2 - VAT registration: the RCS-registered entity files a VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu, providing business activity code, place of establishment, anticipated turnover, bank details and expected supplies; a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) is then issued. Non-established foreign suppliers must register whenever Luxembourg is the place of supply and no reverse charge mechanism applies. Step 3 - Direct-tax file: the ACD opens a partnership direct-tax dossier, and the SCSp files an annual fiscally-transparent partnership declaration; each partner (resident or non-resident) is separately assessed on its allocable share of the SCSp's Luxembourg-source income. Step 4 - Ancillary registrations: customs EORI number for export/import activities, CCSS social security registration if employing Luxembourg staff, and VIES/Intrastat identification for intra-EU supplies where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' classification regime; fiscal transparency of the SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is mandatory under Luxembourg income tax law, which attributes the partnership's profits directly to partners in proportion to their partnership share, regardless of distribution. Therefore, no domestic election is available to convert the SCSp into an opaque corporation. Relevant available mechanisms include: (i) the partnership agreement, which governs profit allocation and is binding for Luxembourg tax purposes subject to anti-abuse and fiscal-substance rules; (ii) voluntary VAT registration and election for the Article 57 small-enterprise VAT exemption via the AEDT; (iii) hybrid-mismatch treatment arising from a foreign jurisdiction's classification of the SCSp as opaque, addressed under the Luxembourg ATAD/ATAD 2 implementing legislation, which may disallow deductions or impose correlative adjustments for cross-border mismatches; (iv) advance pricing arrangements (APAs) and unilateral/bilateral transfer-pricing rulings with the ACD for cross-border consultancy fee structures; and (v) contractual structuring of partner capital accounts to balance Luxembourg taxation versus foreign investor tax positions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-enterprise VAT exemption (Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, as amended for the covered tax year). Non-established foreign suppliers have no general de minimis registration threshold for taxable supplies made in Luxembourg and must register whenever Luxembourg is the place of supply without an applicable reverse charge. EU distance sales of goods to Luxembourg consumers trigger Luxembourg registration above the cross-border distance-selling threshold of EUR 100,000 per calendar year, while non-EU suppliers of B2C electronically supplied services must register in Luxembourg irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the 0% export VAT rate applies only where tangible goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or for its account, an outward customs export declaration is filed, and definitive proof of EU exit is obtained; shipments moving through multiple Member States require a final customs export declaration in the state of exit under the Union Customs Code. Services: cross-border B2B consultancy services of the ISIC 7020 overseas-market-entry type are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (not zero-rated) where the recipient is a non-resident/non-EU business customer, regardless of where the consultant performs the work; such supplies are reported in the 'outside scope' section of the Luxembourg VAT return and are subject to the customer's domestic place-of-supply rules and reverse-charge mechanism. Intra-EU B2B services to business customers holding a valid EU VAT ID are likewise outside Luxembourg VAT through the B2B place-of-supply rule, with VIES recapitulative statements filed where required. Documentary proof of export must be secured within 90 days from dispatch; a supplier failing to hold contemporaneous proof must self-assess Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the exported consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) with electronic export exit confirmation and goods movement reference
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Airway Bill (air freight), CMR consignment note (road freight) or intermodal transport document evidencing physical exit from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 or equivalent) evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds on arm's-length commercial terms
  • Commercial invoice with detailed quantity, unit value, Incoterms, currency and express indication of the 0% rate or outside-scope treatment
  • Purchase order or consultancy services contract defining the scope, deliverables, timeline and pricing basis for the ISIC 7020 market-entry consultancy services
  • Packing list and customs packing declaration where tangible ancillary goods are exported
  • Luxembourg VAT return reconciliation, export listings, and VIES/Intrastat declarations applicable to intra-EU movements
  • Customs representation mandate or power of attorney where the SCSp acts through a licensed customs broker

Luxembourg currently imposes no general real-time fiscal clearance regime, no continuous transaction controls, and no mandatory digital stamping/API-based national clearance portal for B2B invoices as of 1 January 2025. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies under the law of 16 May 2019 (transposing EU Directive 2014/55/EU): invoices addressed to Luxembourg public contracting authorities must be structured electronic invoices in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format transmitted via the PEPPOL network. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, but every VAT invoice must satisfy the substantive statutory requirements - complete supplier and customer identification, VAT identification numbers, sequential invoice numbering, date of issue, description, taxable basis, applicable VAT rate, zero-rating/outside-scope reference, and reverse-charge mention where relevant. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposal will likely introduce standardized transaction-level e-invoicing and reporting by 2030, but no national mandate is in force for the tax period covered by this dossier.

The AEDT and the ACD may jointly audit export files, underlying contractual substance, beneficial ownership, bank flows and payment timing. Missing or late export proof triggers: (i) retrospective reversal of the 0% rate or outside-scope treatment; (ii) assessment of Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the full invoiced consideration; (iii) late-payment interest at the statutory rate of 0.6% per month computed from the original due date of the VAT return; (iv) administrative fines and surcharges up to the amount of the tax evaded; and (v) criminal tax-fraud sanctions under the Luxembourg General Tax Law and VAT Law, including imprisonment of generally 8 days to 5 years and fines up to five times the evaded tax where intent is established. The ordinary assessment limitation period is 5 years, extended to a longer limitation window for fraudulent conduct and aggravated cases. Retroactive clawbacks are further reinforced by Luxembourg's general anti-abuse rules, ATAD hybrid-mismatch provisions, and secondary transfer-pricing adjustments, which may recharacterize unrepatriated export receivables as hidden distributions, capital contributions or constructive loans bearing deemed interest. A conservative six-month (180-day) FX repatriation benchmark is therefore maintained for the consultancy's export trade to preserve VAT zero-rating evidence, transfer-pricing consistency and audit credibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full eurozone convertibility; Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions under Articles 63-65 TFEU. Profits, capital distributions, and service fees are freely remittable without prior BCL authorisation. The only material obligation is statistical: cross-border transactions above EUR 25,000 must be reported to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, usually through the executing bank. The SCSp is tax-transparent, so no Luxembourg withholding on distributions arises, and non-resident partners are not subject to Luxembourg income tax absent a permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg has no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or retention-account regime for export proceeds, including proceeds from cross-border consultancy services. Invoices may be collected in EUR or any contract currency, and conversion is executed at free-market rates via commercial banks, subject only to BCL BoP statistical reporting when individual receipts exceed EUR 25,000.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as a member of the Eurosystem and competent authority for monetary policy, balance of payments statistics, and financial stability oversight in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Wise Business (Wise Europe SA, an NBB-authorised e-money institution passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2) is viable for small-value multi-currency operating disbursements, such as overseas licence fee wires and contractor payments, and offers superior exchange spreads versus traditional bank wires. It is not suitable as the principal treasury bank: an EMI is not a credit institution, client balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee (FGDL), and the provider cannot offer overdrafts, documentary credits, SWIFT correspondent banking, or FX derivatives. Consequently, the primary corporate and any escrow account must remain with a fully licensed Luxembourg credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscriptions from non-resident limited partners arrive via SWIFT MT103 (or Target2 / SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR) to the SCSp's BGL BNP Paribas or BCEE account. MT103 Field 70 must reference the BCL Balance-of-Payments purpose category 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital / Partnership Units' together with the subscription reference; the receiving bank verifies the LPA, the RBE extract, and the identity of the remitter before crediting the account. Single transactions exceeding EUR 25,000 are reported to the BCL for BoP statistics, with the execution bank responsible for the filing and the client obliged to supply the purpose code.EUR payments are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant on the T2 clearing system; non-EUR disbursements (overseas regulator filing fees, foreign contractors, market-entry retainers) use SWIFT MT103 with supporting invoices and the applicable service purpose code through the bank's correspondent network. Documentary collection under URC 522 is occasionally used to link payment against delivery of licences or registration certificates. A Commercial LC (MT700) is not market-standard for consultancy fees, but may support a prepayment obligation when a non-EU regulator or counterparty contractually requires a bank guarantee or standby letter of credit; otherwise direct wire with full documentation is the preferred rail.Vanilla FX spot conversion in multi-currency current accounts (non-speculative operating coverage only)Vanilla FX forward contracts, typically up to 12 months, subject to a bank master agreement (ISDA/EMIR) and available credit lineFX swaps (spot versus forward) for temporary cross-currency liquidity managementCross-currency swaps permitted only where the SCSp carries a foreign-currency borrowing; full EMIR reporting and clearing obligations applyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible exclusively for hedging exposures in non-convertible currencies (e.g., CNY/INR) executed through the bank's London or New York desk; speculative FX options or forwards for service companies are not approved under standard Luxembourg credit policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Wise Business (Wise Europe SA, an NBB-authorised e-money institution passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2) is viable for small-value multi-currency operating disbursements, such as overseas licence fee wires and contractor payments, and offers superior exchange spreads versus traditional bank wires. It is not suitable as the principal treasury bank: an EMI is not a credit institution, client balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee (FGDL), and the provider cannot offer overdrafts, documentary credits, SWIFT correspondent banking, or FX derivatives. Consequently, the primary corporate and any escrow account must remain with a fully licensed Luxembourg credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg City rate (17% corporate income tax, 6.75% municipal business tax, 7% solidarity surcharge); not imposed on the fiscally transparent SCSp itself, but applied to corporate partners on allocated taxable Luxembourg-source profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at SCSp level because distributions by a fiscally transparent partnership are not dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax; the statutory 15% Luxembourg dividend WHT applies only to distributions from resident corporations such as S.A. or S.à r.l.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory local-currency transaction threshold for a Luxembourg Local File; the ACD may require transfer pricing documentation on audit for any related-party transaction, and CbC reporting is mandatory only for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE, but ATAD-based GAAR, abuse of law case law, and ACD tax ruling practice require the SCSp to demonstrate real business substance: local office, employees, directors' decisions, bank accounts and genuine consultancy activity; without commercial substance, treaty benefits may be denied and income reallocated to the true economic owner

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus monthly late interest of 0.8%; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; forfeiture of tax deductibility

Director personal liability: The general partner (associé commandité) is personally and unlimitedly liable for partnership tax debts; managers may face criminal convictions, travel bans, professional disqualification and civil restitution
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of ATAD GAAR; the arrangement may be disregarded, income reattributed to the corporate general partner, and deemed dividend withholding tax of 15% imposed; penalties and interest apply on the revised taxable base

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners can be held jointly liable for unpaid taxes resulting from a sham arrangement and may be subject to director disqualification and civil damages
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments, 0.8% monthly late interest, documentation penalties, and potential 10% assessment surcharge on under-declared corporate partner income

Director personal liability: The general partner's unlimited liability extends to transfer pricing adjustments; deliberate mispricing can trigger criminal fraud liability and personal asset attachment
AML / UBO Register Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities under Luxembourg AML law; money laundering exposure carries imprisonment up to five years

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and the SCSp general partner face personal criminal liability, fines, and civil restitution for intentional or grossly negligent breaches
Registry and Filing Abuses

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalties, forced deregistration, ex officio tax assessments, and judicial liquidation procedures initiated by the public prosecutor

Director personal liability: General partner and effective managers are personally liable for unpaid liabilities after liquidation, and travel restrictions may be imposed for non-observance of Luxembourg court orders

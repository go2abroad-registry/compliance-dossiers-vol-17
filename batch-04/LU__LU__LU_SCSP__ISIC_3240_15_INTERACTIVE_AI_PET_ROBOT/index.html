Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility: the euro is a freely convertible currency and Luxembourg maintains no IMF Article VIII restrictions, no exchange-control approvals, no quantitative ceilings, and no prior authorization for any current or capital account transaction. Capital account and current account flows are fully liberalized within the EU/EEA and towards third countries. For the SCSp, profit remittance and non-resident partner distributions face no legal restriction and no Luxembourg withholding tax at the entity level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent (pass-through) for Luxembourg income tax purposes. The only administrative burden is statistical balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to the BCL for direct reporters, with a notification threshold of EUR 12,500 per individual transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts obtained by resident legal entities or transparent partnerships. Foreign-currency export proceeds (USD, GBP, CNY) may be held indefinitely in multicurrency accounts at Luxembourg banks without forced conversion into EUR. The applicable obligation is purely statistical: BOP direct reporters must report each transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 to the BCL within the prescribed monthly/quarterly deadline; resident banks automatically cover the reporting for account-based flows unless the SCSp is registered as a direct BOP reporter.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating as a full Eurosystem member; prudential supervision of credit institutions and e-money institutions assigned to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); exchange-control and capital-movement policy administered jointly by the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and BCL under the Law of 15 June 2004 on the free movement of capital and the EU Treaty Article 63 TFEU.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent or investor funds arrive via SWIFT MT103 with field 23E set to CRED and field 70 containing explicit purpose code in EPC/BoP format: S010 (equity participation) or NUC (non-financial incorporation) for an SCSp capital contribution. Beneficiary bank must be BIL (SWIFT BILLLULLX) or BGL BNP Paribas (SWIFT BNPALULL); beneficiary account field 59 must show the full legal name 'Name SCSp (Société en commandite spéciale)' and the dedicated capital contribution collection sub-account number. The bank requires a signed capital commitment schedule, the executed partnership agreement (or amendment), and the CSSF AML/KYC file to be forwarded at least two banking days before the wire. For SEPA-based intra-EU contributions, use a SEPA Credit Transfer with purpose code DGAR for guarantees or TRAD for trade-related advances. Any single transaction equal to or over EUR 12,500 must be flagged to the BCL BOP reporting team; the account bank will generate the obligatory BOP declaration automatically if the SCSp is not a direct reporter.Supplier settlements leverage a tiered clearing architecture: (i) SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU component suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with field 70 referencing the commercial invoice number for USD and GBP suppliers through the BGL/BIL international clearing network; (iii) Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) issued by BGL or BIL under UCP600 for large Chinese or US vendor batches, with INCOTERMS 2020 such as CIF or FOB; (iv) Standby Letters of Credit (SBLC) as advance-payment protection for tooling and mould purchases; and (v) direct custodial CNY clearing via BGL's CNH correspondent network for settlements with Shenzhen and Guangzhou manufacturers. All outbound payments above EUR 12,500 are captured by the bank's BCL BOP reporting duty; the SCSp must instruct each payment with a precise trade/import purpose code to avoid classification as a financial transaction and subsequent CMS (Customs and Measures) review.EUR/USD deliverable vanilla forwards (tenor 1-12 months) executed OTC with BGL BNP Paribas or BIL under the 2002 ISDA Master Agreement; exempt from EMIR clearing when the hedge notional remains below the EUR 3,000,000 clearing threshold.EUR/CNY deliverable FX forwards (CNH leg) and onshore CNY swaps for component-import cost protection; for CNH tenors where physical delivery is impossible, a non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing the CNH fixing is permissible as a statutory hedge for 90-180 day procurement exposure.EUR/USD cross-currency swaps under ISDA/CSA with the Luxembourg treasury desk to convert committed USD retail revenue into EUR operating liquidity.EUR/USD OTC FX options (call or put, capped premiums) for upside protection on large seasonal inventory purchases and trade receivables, supported by the underlying supply-purchase agreements.Plain vanilla EUR/USD currency swaps executed with the relationship bank for match-funding of the US retail receivables facility, documented under the standard EMIR-compliant CSA schedule.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% CIT; plus 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT (18.19% combined); municipal business tax (Luxembourg City 6.75%) yields approximately 24.94% aggregate for taxable corporates; SCSp is fiscally transparent by default (no entity-level CIT) unless it elects corporate taxation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax for Luxembourg taxable corporations; 0% withholding tax on distributions by a transparent SCSp because the distribution is a profit share, not a dividend. If the SCSp elects corporate taxation, the 15% WHT applies, with 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% participation, 12-month holding) and a 50% exemption in certain domestic cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required where aggregate related-party transactions of the Luxembourg entity exceed EUR 1,000,000 (administrative guidance). Master File: required where the MNE group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: mandatory where consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750,000,000 (Law of 23 December 2016).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no offshore-style ESR statute. Substance is nonetheless a decisive anti-abuse requirement: the SCSp must maintain local administration, registered offices, decision-making, adequate staff and assets to avoid characterization as an artificial arrangement under the EU ATAD GAAR (implemented by the Law of 21 December 2018) and to retain treaty access and the participation exemption. A corporate general partner is required to be established in Luxembourg or the EEA for a valid SCSp.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of evaded tax plus a 10% administrative increase and 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 or double the evaded tax; imprisonment from 1 month to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner (commandité) is jointly and severally liable for SCSp debts; managing directors can be ordered to pay civil restitution from personal assets, face freezing orders and travel bans, and serve imprisonment in serious fraud cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the structure; denial of treaty benefits and the participation exemption; taxation of reclassified flows at the aggregate Luxembourg CIT/municipal rate (approximately 24.94%) plus a 10% penalty and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the artificial scheme are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall if the SCSp is insolvent; disqualification from board positions and travel bans may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income; denial of deduction for undocumented payments; compensatory adjustment; 10% late-filing surcharge; 0.6% monthly late-payment interest on the incremental tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who sign inaccurate transfer pricing documentation face civil liability for the additional tax and penalties in the event of bankruptcy; criminal liability attaches when mispricing is used as a tax fraud instrument.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines between EUR 125 and EUR 250,000 (or up to EUR 1,250,000 for serious or repeated violations); blocking of profit distributions; criminal sanctions for intentional false declarations.

Director personal liability: The manager and directors may be personally fined for each month of non-compliance, face criminal proceedings for money-laundering facilitation, and be subject to travel bans and confiscation of assets derived from the non-compliant structure.

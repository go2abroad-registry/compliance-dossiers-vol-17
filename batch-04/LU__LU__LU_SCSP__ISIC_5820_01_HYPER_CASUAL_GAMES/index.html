Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - direct income tax; e-portal: myGuichet.lu / guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned SCSp must first be registered with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and obtain a national identification number (matricule). For VAT purposes, the entity or its tax representative must submit a registration application to the AEDT on form 864A, either in paper or via the myGuichet portal. Direct tax registration is achieved through the competent Luxembourg business tax office (bureau d'imposition), which opens a corporate tax file for the partnership. Non-established foreign entities with no permanent establishment in Luxembourg may register for VAT directly via the AEDT or use the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) mechanism for EU cross-border electronic services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default under the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) Articles 4bis and 175; no check-the-box election is available. Partners are taxable on their distributive share. For hybrid mismatch purposes, elections may be available under ATAD (EU Directive 2016/1164) to align treatment with a foreign jurisdiction. The SCSp may elect to have a permanent establishment or taxable presence under certain double tax treaties, but cannot be treated as a fully taxable entity for Luxembourg corporate income tax unless it is converted to a different corporate form. VAT has no transparency election; the SCSp is a taxable person where it supplies taxable services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for small-enterprise exemption (Art. 57 Loi TVA); no general registration threshold for established taxable persons. Non-established suppliers must register from the first euro of taxable supply in Luxembourg unless OSS (One-Stop-Shop) is used. For cross-border distance sales of goods and B2C electronic services to other EU Member States, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual turnover threshold applies (Art. 42 Loi TVA).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT is applied to exports of physical goods to non-EU destinations provided the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of supply and documentary evidence of export is retained. Services: B2B supplies of electronic services (including hyper-casual ad-monetized games) to a non-EU customer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (zero-rated) when the customer is a taxable person established in a non-EU country; B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of VAT under the place-of-supply rules. Intra-EU B2B supplies may be zero-rated in Luxembourg via OSS where the recipient supplies a valid EU VAT identification number. Export-related input VAT claims require proper invoices and export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/IE-507 or electronic ECS declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice for digital goods/services
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Airway Bill (for physical goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment evidence (for offshore proceeds)
  • Evidence of non-EU customer status (foreign VAT ID, residency certificate, or self-declaration)
  • AdMob/AppLovin payout reports and IP/location logs substantiating destination of electronic services

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no government digital stamping or continuous transaction controls. E-invoicing is permitted subject to authenticity of origin and integrity of content. Mandatory SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) reporting applies for VAT audits. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals, mandatory B2B e-invoicing is planned with implementation expected no earlier than 2025-2027. The OSS (One-Stop-Shop) VAT electronic filing portal is integrated via myGuichet.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may impose penalties of up to 10% of the suppressed VAT amount for late payments, increasing to 20% where there is intent to evade or attempt to evade VAT (Art. 26 Loi TVA). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT assessments, automatic interest, and clawback of input VAT credits. Criminal prosecution for VAT fraud can result in fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment for the persons involved. The statute of limitations for VAT clawback is 5 years; it extends to 10 years where the taxpayer has acted in bad faith.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full liberalisation under OECD capital-movement instruments and EU Treaty rules on free movement of capital and payments. There is no distinction between current account and capital account convertibility for commercial purposes, no exchange control or Moratorium, and no BCL licensing/approval gate for profits or dividends remittances. In practice, partners of an SCSp can freely convert EUR to FX and repatriate profit allocations directly to foreign investors; the only substantive hurdles are tax compliance (CRS/CBCF declarations, withholding on non-resident corporate partners only if treaty-protected exemptions are unavailable) and the bank's internal KYC-risk screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Receipts from advertising payouts, royalties, or game publishing exports may be held indefinitely in foreign currency accounts without mandatory conversion to EUR. Enterprises only need to report cross-border transactions for balance-of-payments statistics under BCL statistical thresholds (monthly/quarterly OECD and EU balance-of-payments reporting), with zero conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), established under Article 2 of the Law of 22 April 1998, acting together with the Ministère des Finances for exchange control and payments-system oversight; prudential regulation and AML/CTF supervision of regulated financial institutions are delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the CSSF's AML/CTF unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury custody and clearing, Medium as an auxiliary receivables-collection gateway. An EEA e-money institution (EMI) authorised in another Member State can passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and settle EUR/SEPA flows, but cannot provide fiduciary-grade core treasury services for an SCSp without credit-institution backing. A non-EEA/offshore EMI has no autonomous passporting right; any bank-like activity in Luxembourg requires a CSSF e-money or payment institution licence under the Law of 10 June 2009 and is operationally impractical for a cash-rich gaming treasury. Therefore, the SCSp should use Luxembourg domiciled credit institutions for liquidity and hedge execution, and may restrict EMIs to collections aggregators.

15% domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions by Luxembourg resident companies; 0% for SCSp distributions because SCSp is transparent and not a taxable distribution company; reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or treaty where a qualified corporate partner holds at least 10% stake for 12 months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the end of the financial year, with extension possibilities for electronic filing; if the FYE is 31 December, the filing is due by 31 May of the following year unless extension is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 aggregated value of controlled transactions or group consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000 triggers Master File and Local File compilation; in practice, Luxembourg tax audit requires TP documentation from the first euro when intercompany transactions pose significant tax risk.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Requirements through the OECD/MLI and the Anti-Abuse provisions (DAC6, CFC, GAAR). A SCSp with no asset, risk, or personnel in Luxembourg cannot claim treaty benefits arising from Luxembourg; management, adequate premises, decision-making and accounting functions must be present in Luxembourg or in the state entitled to treaty benefits. The EU Directive (ATAD) requires substantive economic activity to be evidenced where a paper entity receives interest, royalties, or dividend income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to EUR 500,000 plus 10% to 80% of the evaded tax; tax authorities may also impose a fine equal to 3 times the evaded tax in the gravest cases (Article 215 LIR)

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for corporate tax debts if they knowingly filed inaccurate returns or used the SCSp as a vehicle to conceal personal assets; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Establishment

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of transferred profits as constructive dividends or deemed taxable income; administration fines and invalidation of intra-group agreements; for letterbox companies, liquidation orders and revocation of Luxembourg residence status

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to personal civil restitution for unwound transactions, travel bans, freezing of assets, and disqualification as directors for up to 10 years; criminal liability where the artificial structure is proven fraudulent.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 100,000 or 5% of turnover; criminal sanction up to EUR 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for systematic breach

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid fines and any damages caused to creditors or minority partners; the court may prohibit the director from exercising commercial activity for up to 15 years.

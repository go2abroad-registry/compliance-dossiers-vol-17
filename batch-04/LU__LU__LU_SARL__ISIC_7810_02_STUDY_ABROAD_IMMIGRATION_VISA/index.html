Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies and Associations, as amended by the Laws of 10 August 2016 and 19 August 2024, Articles 710-1 to 710-28 (legal form SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and on annual accounts; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Luxembourg Business Registers; Grand-Ducal Regulation of 24 July 2019 on RCS fees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial acte de constitution (public deed of incorporation) executed by a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically by the notary via the e-RCS platform.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; electronic access via MyGuichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

On incorporation the SARL is registered in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS-LBR) and is automatically attributed a matricule (national registration number, e.g. B123456). The company must then file VAT registration form 641 with the AEDT within 15 days of the first taxable transaction to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number formatted LU + 8 digits. Non-EU entities performing taxable supplies in Luxembourg must appoint a VAT representative approved by the AEDT; EU entities may register directly. The VAT ID application requires the certificate of incorporation, minutes of appointment of the day-to-day manager, articles of association, proof of business address and, for non-residents, a power of attorney.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A SARL is a resident corporate vehicle fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax on worldwide income (statutory seat or central administration in Luxembourg). It is not transparent for CIT purposes and cannot elect pass-through treatment; the territorial scope covers worldwide profits, relieved only by applicable tax treaties, the participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) and the 80% net-wealth-tax reduction for qualified participations. — On incorporation the SARL is registered in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS-LBR) and is automatically attributed a matricule (national registration number, e.g. B123456). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A SARL is a resident corporate vehicle fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax on worldwide income (statutory seat or central administration in Luxembourg). It is not transparent for CIT purposes and cannot elect pass-through treatment; the territorial scope covers worldwide profits, relieved only by applicable tax treaties, the participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) and the 80% net-wealth-tax reduction for qualified participations.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections are available. Elective mechanisms that do exist: (i) option for a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT law; (ii) participation-exemption elections under Article 166 LIR and dividends-received deduction; (iii) R&D super-deduction election (Articles 50 bis and 57 LIR); (iv) option to apply the special IP box regime of Article 50 ter LIR (80% exemption of qualifying IP income); and (v) ATAD-2 anti-hybrid rules (Article 168 septdecies LIR) which automatically neutralise mismatches with no taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-established suppliers - registration is due from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. An established SARL may benefit from the small-business franchise exemption only if its annual turnover stays at or below EUR 35,000; above EUR 35,000 the company must register and collect VAT at the standard rate. Placement and immigration consultancy fees are standard-rated (B2B Luxembourg/B2C supplies) unless the place of supply is outside Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to exports physically dispatched outside the EU within 90 days of supply, provided the exporter holds conclusive evidence of exit (Article 55 VAT law). Services: cross-border consulting, study-abroad and visa-advisory services supplied to a non-EU B2B customer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (zero-effective) where the customer is established outside the EU and the place-of-supply rule of Article 45(1) VAT Directive applies; for EU B2B customers the supply is taxable in the recipient's country under reverse charge. B2C services to non-EU clients remain taxable in Luxembourg at 17% unless the use-and-enjoyment rule (Article 46 bis VAT law) shifts the place of supply to the country of use.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS - EX form) lodged via the Luxembourg customs electronic portal
  • Transport evidence in an unbroken chain: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note signed by the consignee outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, contract and payment evidence (Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103) matching the VAT return
  • For services: ECOFS (European Community Output Form explanatory note), non-EU VAT registration certificate of the customer and due-diligence file confirming the B2B place of supply

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing mandate or digital stamping system for B2B VAT. E-invoicing is accepted, B2G transmissions must use the PEPPOL network, invoices must be issued within one month of the supply, and VAT returns (module TVA) are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu on a monthly/quarterly calendar. Taxpayers must retain source files, the eCDF customer data file and all supporting documentation for six years to support electronic audit.

The AEDT may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT together with late interest at 0.6% per month and a 10% penalty if export evidence, ECOFS or VIES statements are missing or inaccurate; penalties escalate to 100% of the tax in fraud cases with criminal fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment between 8 days and 5 years. The statute of limitations is 3 years for genuine errors and 10 years for fraudulent or intentionally concealed operations; the AEDT regularly issues e-audit requests on export files, and the burden of proof rests on the taxable dealer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area jurisdiction; the euro is fully convertible and capital movements are liberalised under Articles 63-66 TFEU. No exchange-control or capital-account authorisation regime exists for current or capital transactions. Dividend and profit remittance are constrained only by corporate-law solvency tests under the Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended) and, where applicable, dividend withholding tax (15% domestic rate; reduced under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable tax treaties). Cross-border flows are additionally screened under EU sanctions and AML/CTF rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation or mandatory currency-conversion obligation applies to Luxembourg service-export receipts, including study-abroad and visa-advisory fees. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained in any currency; however, all cross-border payment instructions and account flows must be reported to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistical purposes and documented by the receiving bank for CSSF Regulation 12-02 AML/CFT compliance.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential and AML supervision: Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for unlicensed, non-EEA offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK, Singapore, US platforms). Under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on payment services and the Law of 16 April 2009 on electronic money, only CSSF-licensed or EEA-passported EMIs may lawfully operate in Luxembourg. An EEA-licensed EMI (e.g., Estonia, Ireland, Germany) can supplement SWIFT rails for fee collection but cannot hold the SARL's capital-deposit or client-money escrow balances and should not serve as the principal corporate bank for this Medium-Risk Enhanced regulated activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders transfer the fully paid-up SARL capital (minimum EUR 12,000) via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to a dedicated capital-deposit account at a Luxembourg-licensed bank. Remittance instructions must state purpose code 'CAPT' (capital contribution) and the legal name of the shareholder/company. The bank issues a certificate of blocked funds ('attestation de blocage de fonds') in favour of the notary for incorporation under the Law of 10 August 1915; after RCS registration the funds are released to the operational account. All underlying shareholder identification documents and funding proof must be retained for at least 5 years under CSSF Regulation 12-02; any currency conversion of inbound capital is executed as an EUR spot purchase at the receiving bank.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border tuition, visa-application, embassy and overseas-agent payments, always carrying full beneficiary and remittance references ('TUITION/VISA/COMMISSION'); SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA counterparties; Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) reserved for high-value performance-secured non-core disbursements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined corporate income tax 17%, solidarity surcharge 7% on CIT, and municipal business tax 6.75% for Luxembourg City; rates vary by commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if parent holds ≥10% for ≥12 months; treaty rates may apply, e.g., 5% or 0% under many DTAs).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end for electronic filing; extensions may be granted upon request. Provisional payments are due quarterly (10 March, 10 June, 10 September, 10 December).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000; CbC Report required if group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for certain entities (e.g., IP holding, financing, leasing). For a consulting/immigration advisory SARL, substance is required to avoid being classified as a shell company: adequate premises, qualified employees, and actual decision-making in Luxembourg. Failure to demonstrate substance may lead to denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 200% of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, additional tax assessments, and penalties up to 10% of tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting tax liabilities if they failed to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for fraudulent misrepresentation.
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; regulatory bans from managing companies; seizure of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 25% of the additional tax, and interest on underpayments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they approved the pricing; in severe cases, criminal charges for tax fraud.

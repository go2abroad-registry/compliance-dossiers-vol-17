Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate and income tax matters; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration, filings and refunds; unified electronic portal: MyGuichet (https://guichet.public.lu); corporate registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (https://www.lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident sponsor establishing an SCSp: (1) execute the partnership deed before a Luxembourg notary; (2) file the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the RCS registration and national matricule identification number; (3) submit the VAT registration application (inscription à la TVA) to the AEDT through MyGuichet, providing the partnership deed, RCS extract, proof of the designer-toy manufacturing and merchandising activity, and manager details; where the SCSp has no Luxembourg fixed establishment and is established outside the EU, it must appoint an approved VAT fiscal representative who is jointly and severally liable for Luxembourg VAT; (4) register the Luxembourg commercial establishment with the ACD for direct taxation and municipal business tax; (5) file the annual transparent partnership return and issue partner allocation statements reflecting each partner's distributable share. There is no minimum capital requirement, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident sponsor establishing an SCSp: (1) execute the partnership deed before a Luxembourg notary; (2) file the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the RCS registration and national matricule identification number; (3) submit the VAT registration application (inscription à la TVA) to the AEDT through MyGuichet, providing the partnership deed, RCS extract, proof of the designer-toy manufacturing and merchandising activity, and manager details; where the SCSp has no Luxembourg fixed establishment and is established outside the EU, it must appoint an approved VAT fiscal representative who is jointly and severally liable for Luxembourg VAT; (4) register the Luxembourg commercial establishment with the ACD for direct taxation and municipal business tax; (5) file the annual transparent partnership return and issue partner allocation statements reflecting each partner's distributable share. There is no minimum capital requirement, and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the SCSp is automatically fiscally transparent for corporate income tax, municipal business tax on the partners, and net wealth tax. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) VAT grouping election (assujettis unis) among Luxembourg-established taxable persons; (ii) the SCSp's separate status as a VAT taxable person where it independently carries on an economic activity; (iii) hybrid mismatch neutralisation elections under ATAD and ATAD 2 to align deduction and inclusion outcomes for transparent entities; and (iv) the vertical fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for qualifying corporate partners in respect of their allocable share of the transparent income. No election exists for entity-level CIT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to non-resident taxable persons: any supplier making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must obtain an LU VAT number, except for the domestic small-enterprise exemption of EUR 35,000 annual turnover, which is reserved for Luxembourg-established businesses. Intra-EU distance sales of designer toys are reportable in Luxembourg under the Union OSS once the EU-wide distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 is exceeded; non-EU sellers of B2C goods must register or use OSS/IOSS regardless of turnover. A valid EORI number is additionally required for export declarations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of designer blind-box toys to destinations outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 44(1)(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Act (implementing Article 146 of the EU VAT Directive) where: (i) the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier or on his behalf beyond the EU customs territory; (ii) removal occurs within the statutory time frame, as a rule within 90 days of the time of supply; (iii) the supplier holds the customs export declaration and transport evidence in its name or that of its authorised agent; and (iv) the recipient is established outside the EU. The input VAT on related production costs, including vinyl compounds, rotocasting and injection materials, and airbrush finishing consumables, remains fully deductible against the zero-rated export revenue. Offshore services physically performed outside the EU may also be zero-rated where the respective statutory documentation is held.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document / Export Accompanying Document bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) from the EU export system
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal of the goods from the EU customs territory to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the designer toy goods, quantities, unit values and INCO terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing the collection of export proceeds from the non-EU buyer
  • Proof of exit, including border customs-office confirmation or destination-country customs arrival certificate
  • EORI number record and complete export dossier retention files covering the statutory retention period

Luxembourg currently has no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory tax-authority API, or digital stamping regime. Structured e-invoicing (EN 16931) on the PEPPOL network is required for B2G invoicing to Luxembourg public entities. A domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate has been legislatively planned with effect from 1 January 2026, introducing structured e-invoices and periodic transaction-level reporting to the AEDT in alignment with the EU ViDA framework; until that date, PDF and paper invoices remain acceptable for B2B supplies and no continuous-control clearance obligation is in force.

Export zero-rating is strictly audited by the AEDT under the adversarial tax-assessment procedure. Where the documentary bundle is incomplete, inconsistent or late, the exporter loses the export exemption, output VAT is reassessed at 17% on the invoice value, arrears interest accrues at 0.6% per month, and administrative increases are applied, typically 10% of the deficiency for negligence and up to 100% for deliberate concealment, in addition to per-invoice fines. The AEDT generally expects export proceeds to be received within 180 days of dispatch; unexplained open export receivables beyond that period increase audit risk and may trigger denial of the exemption. Fraudulent or circular export arrangements attract criminal tax-fraud prosecution with substantial monetary fines and imprisonment, extend the reassessment limitation period from 5 to 10 years, and render the VAT fiscal representative and managing partners jointly and severally liable for the clawed-back VAT, interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: Luxembourg applies no exchange controls on current or capital-account transactions. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital between Luxembourg, EU Member States and third countries. EUR is the sole legal tender, but multi-currency accounts (USD, GBP, CNH) are freely permitted. No prior BCL or CSSF authorisation is required for inbound equity into an SCSp, for operational outflows, or for profit remittance. The pass-through SCSp is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax; non-resident partner withdrawals are unrestricted at banking level and are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax. The only practical hurdle is mandatory statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the bank and, for direct-investment positions, by the SCSp itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no minimum conversion ratio, and no foreign-currency retention limits. Exporters may hold 100% of proceeds in any currency, domestically or abroad. The sole compliance requirement is statistical: payment-service providers must report cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 to the BCL via the LuxGBP reporting system; direct-investment capital flows and outstanding positions must be reported under BCL Circular 2009/02 where thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate foreign-exchange administration exists in Luxembourg; the BCL administers balance-of-payments statistics while the ECB retains monetary and reserve-management authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH as a secondary treasury and payment rail; LOW as the sole primary banking relationship for an SCSp. Legal constraints: any e-money institution (EMI) serving the SCSp must hold a valid licence under Directive 2009/110/EC in an EEA state and be passported into Luxembourg via the CSSF notification mechanism under PSD2 (Law of 10 November 2009, as amended). EMI client funds are safeguarded by segregation or insurance but are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL); balances above EUR 100,000 should be swept daily to a credit institution. EMIs cannot grant loans, overdraft facilities, or issue commercial letters of credit. Recommended provider stack: Stripe (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd, passported from Ireland) for Shopify Payments and online card acquiring; Airwallex or Ebury for multi-currency global accounts, low-value FX conversion, and supplier payouts; Mangopay (CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI) for marketplace split payments if a reseller platform is added. Primary bank clearing should remain with BGL BNP Paribas, BIL, or BCEE.

Recommended provider stack: Stripe (Stripe Technology Europe Ltd, passported from Ireland) for Shopify Payments and online card acquiring; Airwallex or Ebury for multi-currency global accounts, low-value FX conversion, and supplier payouts; Mangopay (CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI) for marketplace split payments if a reseller platform is added. Primary bank clearing should remain with BGL BNP Paribas, BIL, or BCEE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 0 (no statutory monetary threshold for preparing transfer pricing documentation; all intercompany transactions must be documented on an arm's length basis under OECD TP Guidelines; Master File and Local File thresholds under Luxembourg law follow EU TP documentation requirements and apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenues above EUR 750 million, subject to country-by-country reporting obligations; for local file preparation, the ACD requires documentation for any cross-border related party transaction regardless of amount, and penalties may apply for incomplete TP documentation).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies substance requirements through domestic law principles and anti-abuse doctrines. The Tax Administration may disregard an SCSp or its transactions if the arrangement is artificial or lacks economic substance. Since 1 January 2019, Luxembourg applies a general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) pursuant to the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD). Economic Substance Regulations in the strict sense (ESR as in UAE/offshore regimes) do not exist in Luxembourg, but the substance-over-form doctrine and the judicial principle of 'fictitious transactions' allow the tax authorities to recharacterize a shell-like SCSp. The following substance indicia are required to obtain treaty benefits and protect against BEPS assertions: the SCSp must maintain a Luxembourg registered office, hold board meetings or partner meetings, maintain a Luxembourg bank account, employ qualified administrative staff (at least part-time), ensure the managing general partner (SCSc or S.à r.l.) exercises genuine management functions, record commercial contracts, and demonstrate an actual decision-making structure commensurate with the activity (designer toy design, procurement of vinyl or resin materials, quality control, and distribution). Failure to demonstrate substance may result in denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of profits on a look-through basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 396 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and a fine of EUR 5,000 to EUR 20,000 (doubled to EUR 100,000 for serious cases under Article 398). The fine may be multiplied up to ten times when the evasion amounts exceed EUR 10,000. Additionally, the taxpayer must pay all evaded taxes plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner of the SCSp can be held jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax debts under Luxembourg fiscal law and general civil law (Article 1837 of the Luxembourg Civil Code). If the managing partner acts in bad faith or gross negligence, the corporate veil may be pierced. Directors face criminal fines and imprisonment, civil restitution of all evaded taxes, and possible travel bans or reporting obligations imposed by the Luxembourg criminal courts. The general partner of an SCSp is personally, jointly and severally liable for all partnership obligations if the partnership is insolvent and the director participated in fraudulent transfers.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg GAAR (Article 6 Bofisc) and ATAD GAAR allow the tax authorities to disregard the entity for tax purposes. The business profits are reallocated to the actual shareholders or to a deemed permanent establishment in the market jurisdiction. The 15% withholding tax or higher may be applied to a deemed distribution, and net wealth tax and municipal business tax are reassessed. No specific monetary fine applies to the shell company itself, but an administrative penalty for false attestation of substance can reach EUR 4,000 per infraction under the Luxembourg tax procedure, and full tax reassessment plus 0.6% monthly interest is imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false substance documents face personal criminal liability for complicity in tax evasion (Article 397 of the General Tax Law) and may be fined up to EUR 20,000 and imprisoned. In civil proceedings, the director or general partner may be ordered by a tribunal to compensate the tax administration and creditors. A travel ban can be issued by an investigating judge in case of serious fiscal fraud, and directors cannot leave the Grand Duchy without court authorization.
AML/CTF and Payment Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, failure to comply with beneficial ownership registration (law of 13 January 2019) is punishable by a fine of EUR 1,250 to EUR 1,250,000 for legal persons, plus administrative fines from the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) up to EUR 1 million or 10% of turnover. Criminal penalties for money laundering can reach 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 500,000 for natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process proceeds of tax evasion face criminal liability for money laundering (Article 506-1 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code). Liability extends to the general partner of the SCSp; any director or manager who obstructs the RBE filing may be personally fined up to EUR 125,000 and banned from managing a commercial company for up to 10 years. Asset freezing orders and travel bans are applicable during criminal proceedings.
Missing VAT / E-Commerce Registration (Luxembourg and EU)

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg VAT law (loi modifiée du 12 février 1979) imposes a surcharge of 10% of the VAT due for late filing; a further EUR 1,250 penalty for late declaration may apply. If the tax administration discovers undeclared output VAT, an additional penalty of 5% per month up to 25% of the due amount is imposed. Intention to evade VAT triggers the same criminal sanctions as tax evasion: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 20,000.

Director personal liability: The managing partner or director who signs false VAT returns is a joint and several debtor of the VAT liability of the entity (Article 56 of the Luxembourg VAT law). Repetition of customs-like or intra-community fraud can lead to personal liability. Travel bans and director disqualification can be ordered upon indictment, and the personal property of directors can be seized for the VAT debts if the company is insolvent and the non-filing was a management fault.

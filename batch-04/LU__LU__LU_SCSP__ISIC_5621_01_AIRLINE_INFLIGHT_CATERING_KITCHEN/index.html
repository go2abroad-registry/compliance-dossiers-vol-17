Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for commercial registration; electronic portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors must file the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through a Luxembourg-registered professional. The SCSp has no minimum capital requirement; a private partnership agreement is sufficient unless the contribution comprises real estate. Required filings include the partnership agreement, identity/KYC documents of all partners, registered office address proof, manager appointment details, and evidence of the registered office. After RCS registration, the SCSp obtains a Luxembourg commercial registration number and a national identification number (NIF). For VAT, the SCSp or, if applicable, the foreign non-established taxable person must file the VAT registration questionnaire (form submitted via MyGuichet) with AEDT, stating the ISIC 5621 in-flight catering activity, estimated turnover, place of economic activity, and bank details. AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT number with the 'LU' prefix. For food production and ramp delivery, the ALVA/health ministry approval is a prerequisite before taxable activity starts. A non-established foreign airline caterer making taxable ground supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with AEDT from the first taxable supply, with no turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: An SCSp has no separate legal personality and is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax. Profits and losses are passed through to partners, and Luxembourg-source character is preserved at partner level. Non-resident partners without a Luxembourg permanent establishment are not subject to Luxembourg direct taxation on non-Luxembourg-source income. For VAT, the SCSp itself is treated as a separate taxable person when it independently carries on an economic activity; income-tax transparency does not prevent VAT registration.

Available election forms

Luxembourg domestic law does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the US. The SCSp is statutorily transparent; there is no domestic election to convert it into a corporate income taxpayer. Foreign partners may make classification elections in their home jurisdiction (e.g., US Form 8832), but such elections do not alter Luxembourg tax qualification. Luxembourg VAT grouping under Article 60 of the VAT Law may be elected by closely related Luxembourg-established persons subject to AEDT approval. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply and override divergent tax treatment of the SCSp across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for the Luxembourg small-business exemption (Art. 57 VAT Law) applicable only to Luxembourg-established taxable persons. A non-established foreign entity has no small-business threshold and must register from the first taxable supply. Airline inflight catering involving B2B supplies to carriers will in practice exceed the threshold and requires VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union by the supplier or a third party acting on its behalf, including supplies of in-flight meals and catering stores loaded on aircraft that leave EU airspace on international routes. The airline operator must confirm the non-EU destination and the flight schedule. Supplies to aircraft on intra-EU routes are not automatically zero-rated and may be subject to Luxembourg VAT or the destination Member State's rules; zero-rating can also apply for intra-EU dispatch of goods to a VAT-registered acquirer in another Member State where all dispatch evidence is held. The supplier must possess conclusive proof of export within the statutory time frame, including customs export documentation and transport/route evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration accepted by customs)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / aircraft manifest showing the aircraft's non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/peripheral forex confirmation evidencing repatriation of export payment proceeds
  • Detailed VAT invoice referencing the zero-rated export supply and the airline contract
  • International airline catering contract designating non-EU flight routes
  • ALVA/aviation food safety and bonded-store certificate for the catering kitchen and ramp delivery

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, nor does it mandate government API stamping or digital fiscal stamps. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G contracts using PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format; B2B e-invoicing is permitted with recipient consent. Invoices must preserve authenticity, integrity and legibility. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the e-CDF/MyGuichet portal. The taxpayer must retain the standard audit file (SAF-T) and supporting transaction documents for at least six years.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and assess VAT at the standard rate (or the applicable reduced rate for qualifying food) if export proof is incomplete, payment is not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or the goods never leave the EU. Default interest accrues on unpaid VAT, and administrative penalties apply per Luxembourg tax law. For fraudulent export claims, forged customs documents, or artificial routing schemes, the penalty may reach up to 100% of the evaded VAT, the normal statute of limitations may be extended to 10 years, and criminal tax fraud sanctions can include imprisonment up to 5 years and substantial fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the EU acquis in full. The euro is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, including cross-border trade settlements, inbound partnership capital contributions, and profit allocations to non-resident partners. There are no exchange-control licensing requirements, no non-resident clearance permits, and no BCL approval for profit remittance. Profits allocated to non-resident partners are remitted without Luxembourg withholding tax where the SCSp is fiscally transparent and the partner is not a Luxembourg resident; applicable frictions are limited to CRS/FATCA reporting, UBO register obligations, and economic-substance requirements for the operational catering activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the BCL or any foreign-exchange authority, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to foreign-currency export receivables. The airline catering SCSp may retain export receipts in EUR, USD, or other convertible currencies, convert them at freely negotiated market rates, or use them directly to fund operational liabilities. The only statutory layer is BCL external-statistics reporting (balance-of-payments data), which must be submitted through the resident credit institution according to the BCL's periodic reporting cycle.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg, member of the Eurosystem. No separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists; the BCL and the Ministry of Finance administer Luxembourg's external-monetary framework, while the CSSF supervises credit institutions for AML/CFT conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 and CSSF Circular 21/785 vetting; LOW for offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA. An SCSp may hold low-value operational funds in an EMI client-money account, but cannot rely on EMI rails for high-volume aviation catering settlements, airport-ramp disbursements, or trade-finance documentation. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA branch are legally unsuitable for the SCSp's treasury strategy because they are not SEPA-compliant, create BCL reporting gaps, and are generally rejected by Luxembourg credit institutions for correspondent clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; Master File required if group turnover exceeds EUR 100 million; both thresholds based on consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and substance requirements apply. An SCSp must demonstrate adequate substance (e.g., real office, employees, decision-making in Luxembourg) to avoid being classified as a shell company and to benefit from tax treaties and EU directives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 100% of the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 5 years (Luxembourg Criminal Code Art. 396).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 15% withholding tax on deemed distributions; penalties up to EUR 250,000 under GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for additional taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for abuse of law.
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering (Luxembourg AML Law of 2004).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; travel bans may be issued during investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment amount, and interest at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; potential criminal liability for tax fraud.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority) for VAT matters; Administration des contributions directes (Direct Tax Administration) for income tax matters; unified business registration via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through guichet.lu / MyGuichet portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg-established SCSp, the entity must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the national business registration number (matricule). The SCSp must subsequently apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) using Form 16 filed with the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA. A non-resident foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg and not established in the EU must appoint a VAT fiscal representative approved by the Luxembourg VAT administration; an EU-established foreign entity may register directly with Form 16. The application must be filed before commencing taxable activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a Luxembourg-established SCSp, the entity must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the national business registration number (matricule). The SCSp must subsequently apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) using Form 16 filed with the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA. A non-resident foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg and not established in the EU must appoint a VAT fiscal representative approved by the Luxembourg VAT administration; an EU-established foreign entity may register directly with Form 16. The application must be filed before commencing taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp is mandatorily treated as fiscally transparent for income and net wealth tax purposes (partners are taxed directly). No election to opt into corporate income tax is available. For VAT purposes, the SCSp is a taxable person; it may elect to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) with related Luxembourg-established entities, subject to prior AEDT approval. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply to deny deductions on arrangements exploiting mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover for domestic small business exemption); mandatory VAT registration is triggered without threshold for non-established foreign suppliers and for any taxable B2C e-commerce activity when the small enterprise exemption is not claimed or is exceeded. Cross-border EU distance sales have a separate EUR 10,000 pan-EU threshold for the destination state unless the OSS scheme is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical pet supplies from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU qualify for zero-rating where the supplier retains proof that the goods left the EU within the prescribed time limits (generally within 90 days of supply). The SCSp must issue a zero-rated invoice, obtain a customs export declaration, and maintain transport evidence. Services connected to supplies of pet care devices rendered to non-EU customers may be zero-rated where the customer is a taxable person established outside the EU, or where the effective use and enjoyment of B2C digital services occurs outside the EU. Sales of goods to EU consumers are not export zero-rated; they are taxable in the member state of destination via the OSS scheme or local VAT registration.

  • Customs export declaration (Export Control System / Movement Reference Number) confirming the goods left Luxembourg and the EU
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport of the goods outside the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank settlement evidence showing payment from the foreign buyer in foreign currency and receipt of export proceeds

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API-based reporting, or digital stamping mandate applies to domestic B2B/B2C invoicing in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions and must be submitted through PEPPOL BIS 3.0. For e-commerce supplies, standard VAT invoice rules apply; while Stripe/PayPal generate payment records, they are not substitutes for statutory VAT invoicing. Luxembourg is expected to align with EU ViDA real-time e-invoicing proposals in due course, but no such obligation is currently in force for the described pet products e-commerce activity.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may reject export zero-rating if the documentary evidence is incomplete or untimely. Where zero-rating is unjustly applied, the tax authority issues a VAT assessment for the unpaid VAT plus default interest at an annual rate of 5%, and imposes administrative fines ranging from 10% to 20% of the disputed tax. Deliberately fraudulent claims under Articles 72–79 of the Luxembourg VAT Law expose the SCSp and its managers to criminal penalties, including fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment; retrospective clawback is not time-barred for fraudulent declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full euro-zone currency convertibility. It applies no material distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions under the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial undertakings, as amended. Capital injections, operating revenues, profit remittances, and repatriation of invested capital may be denominated in EUR or freely convertible foreign currency without convertibility restrictions. Practical hurdles are administrative: compliance with CSD/BCL payment systems, AML/KYC onboarding, the Luxembourg register of beneficial owners (RBE), FATCA/CRS reporting, EU sanctions/asset freeze regulation, and, for non-resident investors, relevant inbound/outbound investment statute and tax transparency documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory EUR conversion applies. Luxembourg/EU law does not impose a timeline for repatriating export receipts, nor any conversion ratio into EUR. Export proceeds may be kept in foreign currency, netted in treasury, or converted under permitted FX contracts. For statistical and prudential purposes, the BCL compiles Balance of Payments data, and Intrastat/foreign-trade declarations and corporate accounting records must evidence the underlying transactions.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem; banking and payment supervision by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the CSSF Law and EU financial services acquis. No separate foreign-exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for non-EU/offshore digital e-money institutions; Medium for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs and PSD2-authorized payment institutions that passport into Luxembourg. An offshore EMI lacking an EU/EEA licence cannot provide payment accounts, local IBANs, or direct clearing access without CSSF authorization and is legally unsuitable for capital escrow, trade settlement, or statutory regulatory buffers. For contingency liquidity and low-value e-commerce collections, only EMIs with a valid e-money licence from an EEA regulator and an active cross-border passport notification under PSD2 may be used as a complementary payment rail; they must not be the sole treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital injections must be wired from an actual bank account in the partner's name to the SCSp's segregated Luxembourg IBAN via SWIFT MT202 bank-to-bank transfer and accompanied by an MT103 customer credit transfer remittance advice. Use the EU Balance of Payments direct-investment purpose code for equity contributions or the appropriate code for shareholder loans, and include the SCSp's RCS number and payment reference in the narration. The bank will verify the ultimate originator, source of funds, sanctions status, and clearing path; no exchange-control approval is required. Funds should be received in the currency of the capital commitment, mapped in the treasury ledger to each partner's capital account, and only then converted or deployed.For euro payments to Luxembourg and EEA suppliers, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or, for amounts outside SEPA, SWIFT MT103. For USD, CNY, and other non-EUR currencies, clear through the bank's correspondent network using MT103 with full beneficiary details, reference to the underlying invoice or purchase order, and required invoice and customs documentation. Large or recurring pet-product supply orders from Asia and the USA can be secured via a documentary Letter of Credit (UCP 600), typically an irrevocable commercial LC issued by the buying SCSp's bank and advised or confirmed by the supplier's bank; bank guarantee and cash-deposit collateral may be required.

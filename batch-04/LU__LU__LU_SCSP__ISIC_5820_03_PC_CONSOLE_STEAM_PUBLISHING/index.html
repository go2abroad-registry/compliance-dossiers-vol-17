Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is mandatorily tax-transparent for Luxembourg income tax and has no separate legal personality; no check-the-box election exists. Partners may elect to join a Luxembourg VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 68 of the VAT Law if the statutory conditions are met, and advance tax rulings may be obtained from the ACD for classification confirmation. Anti-hybrid rules under ATAD may override or recharacterise transparent structures where a mismatched outcome arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business franchise; otherwise no threshold applies and registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable supplies, including for non-resident suppliers making taxable distance sales of digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU are zero-rated subject to documentary evidence of export. Digital services supplied via Steam or PlayStation Store are not zero-rated; the place of supply is determined under Articles 58 and 59 of the VAT Law (B2C and B2B), meaning Luxembourg VAT is chargeable only where the customer is established, resident, or habitually located in Luxembourg. Supplies to non-EU B2B customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT and are reportable under the reverse-charge mechanism in the customer's jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) – mandatory for physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Airway Bill – mandatory transport evidence for physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent payment proof – required for export validation and FX repatriation where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists for B2B/B2C transactions; e-invoicing is permitted. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G invoices under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 28 January 2019 via the PEPPOL network. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through the AEDT portal.

Under Articles 80 and 81 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, erroneous zero-rating triggers immediate VAT reassessment, interest at 0.6% per month, and administrative fines ranging from EUR 750 to EUR 12,500 plus a corrective assessment up to 20% of the tax due. Fraudulent claims constitute a criminal offence under Article 88 of the VAT Law with penalties including imprisonment and fines up to double the evaded tax. The AEDT may claw back zero-rated supplies retroactively for up to 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividends, partnership distributions, and liquidation proceeds can be paid abroad without prior authorisation or repatriation taxes. The only constraints are standard EU/AML reporting, tax withholding on certain payments (e.g., withholding tax on dividends under domestic law, subject to treaties and EU Directives), and balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL for transactions above €12,500. For an SCSp with pass-through tax status, distributions to non-resident partners are generally not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax because the SCSp is considered fiscally transparent, provided no Luxembourg commercial substance creates an unexpected taxable nexus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline. Export receipts, including digital store proceeds from Steam and PlayStation, may be held in the original currency (USD/EUR) or converted at freely agreed market rates. The SCSp must only ensure that transactions are booked consistently for tax and balance-of-payments purposes; BCL statistical declarations apply when individual EUR/USD equivalent transactions exceed €12,500.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CFT and forex counterparty supervision. Luxembourg maintains a full liberalized foreign exchange regime under EU law; no separate Luxembourg foreign exchange administration approval is required for current or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM for the primary treasury function. Under PSD2, an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex is not a full credit institution and cannot provide depository-guaranteed escrow or commercial-lending services. EMIs are viable for low-value inbound royalty collections after initial bank onboarding, especially USD montant receipt from platforms, but they are not acceptable for the statutory capital-level or partner capital projections in an SCSp. Legal constraints include: EMI accounts are not 'bank accounts' in the strict legal sense; no deposit guarantee protection under Luxembourg legal deposit guarantee scheme; EMI providers may not support an SCSp partnership structure with non-resident partners due to their simplified KYC and may freeze funds if the ultimate beneficial ownership chain is opaque. An SCSp should use EMIs only as a sub-account payment rail for developer payouts, never as the primary controlled treasury cash pool.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital contributions from the SCSp's limited partners or parent entity should be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for substantial European interbank-only transfers if the ultimate beneficiary account is held in the same EU bank) to the designated Luxembourg IBAN. The payment must refer to 'SCSp Capital Contribution - [Partner name] - Capital Call dated [date]'. EU harmonized purpose codes 'INVE' (investment) or 'EQUI' (equity-like capital injection) are recommended; for subordinated shareholder loans, use 'MOLA' (working capital/monetary asset) or 'LOAN' with a written subordination deed. The bank will require source-of-funds evidence for the remitter, the attested capital call letter, and if the injection is non-cash (e.g., IP assignment), specialized valuation and notarized transfer protocols. No BCL approval is needed, but aggregate transfers above €12,500 trigger balance-of-payments reporting.For developer royalty and license payouts from the SCSp treasury account, the recommended clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'ROYA' (royalties) or 'LICF' (license fees), including the contract reference and net-of-VAT amount. For physical merchandise or console asset procurement (e.g., development kits, promotional merchandise), the SCSp may use a commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) through a Luxembourg confirming bank; however, digital-first PC/console publishing is primarily B2B service-based and does not require LC. For bulk recurring Steam/PlayStation platform payout reconciliations, the SCSp will maintain an aggregated EUR/USD liquidity reserve in a single concentration account. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg City rate (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% unemployment solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); SCSp is fiscally transparent, so this rate applies to resident corporate partners on their allocable profit share, while resident individual partners are taxed at progressive personal income tax rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory rate on dividends paid by Luxembourg collective entities; distributions by the SCSp are transparent profit withdrawals and carry 0% Luxembourg withholding tax; the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the 15% to 0% where a corporate partner holds at least 10% for 12 months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where aggregate non-financial intercompany transactions exceed EUR 500,000 or financial intercompany transactions exceed EUR 250,000; Master File mandatory where worldwide group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million; de minimis exemption applies to small enterprises (balance sheet below EUR 4.4 million and turnover below EUR 8.8 million).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through LIR Article 6 GAAR, ATAD anti-abuse rules, the OECD principal-purpose test, beneficial ownership requirements, and administrative practice requiring the SCSp to hold real local substance (registered office, local bookkeeping, management meetings in Luxembourg, local banking, and genuine decision-making) to obtain a tax residence certificate and treaty protection; the SCSp general partner must demonstrate central management and control in Luxembourg to avoid re-characterisation as an artificial arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fraude fiscale prosecution before Luxembourg courts: fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; ex officio tax assessment with 10% tax increase, monthly late interest of 0.6%, forfeiture of carry-forwards and incentives, and an extended 10-year statute of limitations for fraudulent suppression.

Director personal liability: Members of the general partner management are jointly liable if they directed or supervised the fraud; courts may pierce the SCSp veil, order civil restitution of evaded tax, impose travel bans and asset freezes, and impose custodial sentences; the general partner bears unlimited personal liability for SCSp debts.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: ACD reallocation under LIR Article 56 and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; recharacterised excess is taxed as a hidden profit distribution subject to 15% withholding tax at source; interest accrues at 0.6% per month with a 10% surcharge, creating material double tax exposure; advance pricing agreement protection is revoked for misleading facts.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false transfer-pricing documentation face criminal complicity exposure and civil liability for resulting tax arrears; the general partner's unlimited liability extends to transfer-pricing restatements and unpaid withholding taxes.
Artificial Arrangement and Substance Deficiency (GAAR / Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Luxembourg tax residence certificate, denial of treaty benefits under the OECD principal-purpose test, recharacterisation under LIR Article 6, look-through taxation of foreign partners in Luxembourg and abroad, penalties up to 10% of tax due, and mandatory unwinding of the abusive structure.

Director personal liability: Managerial officers face professional disqualification and civil suits by co-partners and creditors; in flagrant cases, fiscal fraud charges trigger personal monetary liability, travel bans (interdiction de sortie du territoire), asset seizures, and imprisonment.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from EUR 1,250 to EUR 25,000 for RBE breaches; criminal AML penalties under the law of 12 November 2004, as amended, including fines up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment up to five years for intentional violations; RBE registers are public and exchanged within the EU beneficial-ownership network.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner and natural-person general partners are personally liable for AML failures; Luxembourg courts may impose director disqualification, personal fines, asset seizure, and custodial sanctions, and may authorise civil veil-piercing to satisfy tax and AML penalties.

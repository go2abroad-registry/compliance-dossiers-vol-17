Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de commerce et des sociétés - RCS), operated by the Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA - AEDT); electronic filing via the eJustice portal (www.ejustice.etat.lu).

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016 and the Law of 23 September 2020; SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 to 710-20; registration and legal publication framework under the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and Annual Accounts (loi du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés ainsi que la comptabilité et les comptes annuels des entreprises).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte de constitution) executed before a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically with the RCS; a private deed under Article 710-2 of the Law of 10 August 1915 is legally permissible for a SARL, but the notarial form is the market standard and supports fast-track one-day incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 2 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000 must be fully paid up at incorporation (Article 710-5 of the Law of 10 August 1915). Cash contributions are deposited with an eligible EU/EEA credit institution in a blocked account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL in formation; the bank issues a blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) addressed to the Luxembourg notary. The funds are released only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions are permitted, must be fully paid in, and must be described in the notarial deed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg maintains a split administration. Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), official portal impotsdirects.public.lu. Indirect taxation (VAT, registration duties): Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), functional portal guichet.public.lu and MyGuichet.lu (secure e-filing 'espace sécurisé'). The single company taxpayer identification number (matricule national) is allocated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) upon RCS incorporation; EU-wide VIES data exchange is managed by the ACD/AEDT under Regulation (EU) No 904/2010.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file for registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via the LBR; the registered legal entity automatically receives the 15-digit national identification number (matricule national), which is the operative corporate TIN for all Luxembourg tax filings. Step 2 - For VAT, file VAT Form 31 (Déclaration d'assujettissement) with the AEDT, after which a 'LU' + 8-digit VAT identification number is issued; non-established foreign entities must file Form 31 and appoint a local VAT representative (représentant responsable) before making any taxable supply. Step 3 - Activate the MyGuichet.lu secure professional eSpace for electronic submission of CIT/ICC/IFL returns, monthly VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and OSS returns. Step 4 - Register any payroll employees with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) for social security withholding. Step 5 - For export/refund workflows, open a Luxembourg bank account (payments via Stripe Billing/Wise must be traceable to the Luxembourg balance sheet) and register for the electronic customs filing platform to link export declarations with AEDT zero-rating substantiation files.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable resident company: corporate income tax (IRC) at 17%, municipal business tax (ICC) at the communal rate (approx. 6.75% in Luxembourg City), solidarity surcharge 7% on IRC, and net wealth tax (IFL) at 0.5% on net assets exceeding EUR 500,000 (0.25% above EUR 500 million). Territorial scope is worldwide income taxation of residents; a broad participation exemption, SOPARFI holding regime, and 80% IP box deduction are available. Pass-through treatment is not available; Tax_Exempt_Zone status does not exist in Luxembourg for general trading entities.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not offer a US-style check-the-box election; relevant statutory elections for structuring are: (a) VAT grouping election for Luxembourg-resident group companies (Article 61a of the 1979 VAT Law); (b) small-enterprise exemption waiver permitting voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 35,000 threshold; (c) IP box election for 80% exempt qualifying IP income (Article 50bis LIR); and (d) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) election for 95%+ Luxembourg-resident group chains. EU hybrid mismatch neutralizing rules (ATAD, implemented by the Law of 25 March 2020) automatically override elective outcomes that create double non-taxation, deduction-without-inclusion, or double-deduction scenarios.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg-established businesses: no mandatory VAT registration threshold; small enterprises with annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (exclusive of VAT) are exempt from VAT compliance but may elect to register. Non-established foreign suppliers: mandatory Luxembourg VAT registration from the first euro of taxable supplies made in Luxembourg; digital/e-commerce services supplied to Luxembourg consumers require quarterly OSS filing (no threshold) once the EUR 10,000 cross-border distance-sales threshold is exceeded in the EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Luxembourg SARL supplying UI/UX design subscriptions (ISIC 7410) claims VAT relief as follows: (1) physical exports of design deliverables to non-EU territories: 0% tax with proof of dispatch out of the EU by customs export declaration; (2) intra-EU B2B supplies of services (the core subscription business): the general B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 38 VAT Law/Article 44 EU VAT Directive) locates the supply where the business customer is established - Luxembourg VAT is therefore not charged (zero-rated/reverse-charge in the customer state), provided the customer's valid EU VAT identification number is stated on the invoice and substantiated via VIES; (3) services to non-EU B2B clients fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (no Luxembourg VAT), and (4) B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxable in Luxembourg at 17% (place of supply at the supplier's establishment), unless effectively an electronically supplied service subject to the EU consumer-state rule. The zero-rate is conditional on contemporaneous written evidence of customer identity, VAT number, contract, and payment trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ED-5 filed via the Luxembourg customs Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR or e-CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and signed subscription/design services contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • VIES validation printout of the EU customer's VAT number
  • EUR.1 movement certificate or origin invoice declaration where preferential origin is claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping regime exists as of the 2025 tax period. Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing will phase in under the Law of 15 March 2023 (Bill 7318): issuers with more than 300,000 invoices per year from 1 September 2026, remaining taxpayers and mediums by 1 September 2028. The mandated transport standard is Peppol (UBL 3.0 / CII); invoices must be exchanged electronically via certified Peppol access points. Until the mandate, e-invoicing is voluntary and must respect Article 75 VAT Law content rules (sequential number, issuer/customer VAT IDs, date, description, quantity, price, VAT rate, reverse-charge mention). Cross-border B2B design-subscription supplies must be reported in the monthly VAT return and the VIES/EC Sales List. OSS/IOSS digital service portals require authenticated MyGuichet.lu API integration.

Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is the prudential export-proceeds standard applied by the AEDT and customs authorities when assessing zero-rating files. Fraudulent export zero-rating or false reverse-charge assertions trigger: arrears interest of 0.8% per month (9.6% per annum) on the uncollected VAT; administrative fines under Article 53 of the VAT Law ranging from EUR 10 to EUR 10,000 per infringement; and, for intentional tax fraud (Article 98 VAT Law), fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of one month to five years. The AEDT cross-checks export declarations (ECS/ICS, SAD), bank data received via DAC7/DAC8, and VIES sales-listings; retrospective clawbacks cover the standard 5-year assessment limitation period, extended to 10 years for fraud or failure to file. Directors of the SARL are jointly and severally liable for fraudulent VAT schemes and export-transaction misstatements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the EU Treaty regime of fully liberalised capital movements (TFEU Article 63). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible in EUR; there are no exchange control permits, no approval thresholds, and no restrictions on foreign ownership of a SARL. Profit remittance faces no FX or administrative hurdle, but outbound dividends are subject to a statutory 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parents and to treaty rates (e.g., 5% or 0% under double tax treaties depending on the shareholder jurisdiction) for non-EU parents. Payments for services (royalties, design fees, contractor fees) are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax and may be remitted freely, subject only to the bank's standard documentary review and sanctions filtering.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts into EUR. There is no legal timeline for repatriation of export income; the entity's only obligation is indirect statistical reporting: the resident bank acts as the BCL's reporting agent for balance of payments (ITRS) data and will request a transaction purpose code for cross-border cash movements. Design subscription revenues collected in USD, GBP, or other currencies may be held offshore to the extent permitted by bank terms, yet treasury risk policy should favour conversion within 5-10 business days to EUR unless a documented hedging strategy exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); supervisory and regulatory interface for exchange control matters is shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which licenses credit institutions and enforces AML/KYC rules on payment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection-and-disbursement rail, LOW as the primary treasury or capital-custody rail. Wise (Wise Europe S.A., licensed as an electronic money institution by the National Bank of Belgium with EU passport) and Stripe (Stripe Payments U.C., authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland) are legally usable for inbound subscription collections and outbound contractor payments in EUR, USD, and GBP. However, EMIs are not credit institutions under Luxembourg law: they cannot hold statutory subscribed capital in a blocked compte bloqué, cannot offer ISDA-derivative or FX hedging documentation, may cap high-value cross-border disbursements, and do not assume the role of BCL statistical reporter. All treasury balances above the equivalent of EUR 10,000 should be held at a CSSF-regulated bank; EMIs should receive at most two days of receivables float before a sweep.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary collection-and-disbursement rail, LOW as the primary treasury or capital-custody rail. Wise (Wise Europe S.A., licensed as an electronic money institution by the National Bank of Belgium with EU passport) and Stripe (Stripe Payments U.C., authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland) are legally usable for inbound subscription collections and outbound contractor payments in EUR, USD, and GBP. All treasury balances above the equivalent of EUR 10,000 should be held at a CSSF-regulated bank; EMIs should receive at most two days of receivables float before a sweep.

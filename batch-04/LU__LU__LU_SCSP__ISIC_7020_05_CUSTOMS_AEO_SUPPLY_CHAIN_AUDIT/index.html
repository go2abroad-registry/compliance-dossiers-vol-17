Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax and registration duties. Central digital portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities not established in Luxembourg but conducting taxable supplies must register for VAT via the AEDT using form 645 (VAT registration application), submitted through MyGuichet or by an appointed Luxembourg tax representative, attaching constitutional documents, proof of foreign TIN, and evidence of economic activity. Resident entities receive a Luxembourg national business registration number (matricule) from the Luxembourg Business Registers, which serves as the direct tax TIN; VAT numbers are issued in the format LU0000000000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: A Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) has no separate legal personality and is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes. As an unincorporated partnership, it is not a CIT taxpayer; partners are taxed on their share of income. For VAT, the SCSp may still be a taxable person when pursuing an independent economic activity above applicable turnover thresholds.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime. The SCSp is mandatorily tax-transparent and cannot elect entity-level taxation. Specific elections include: (i) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption (option for taxation), (ii) formation of a VAT grouping (assujetti unique) subject to conditions, and (iii) anti-hybrid notifications and consistency statements under ATAD and Luxembourg domestic implementation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover of supplies for small-business exemption); separate thresholds apply to intra-EU distance sales (EUR 10,000) and to intra-EU acquisitions of goods by non-established entities, above which compulsory Luxembourg VAT registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods dispatched or transported to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT, including direct exports, export indirect exports, and services physically or economically linked to export goods, provided the supplier holds documentary evidence establishing the exit of goods from the EU and the identity/relation of the purchaser. Intra-EU dispatches to VAT-registered counterparties may also be zero-rated with a valid VAT ID.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or other transport contract
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt proving receipt of consideration
  • Packing List
  • Transport documents showing physical departure from EU territory
  • Proof of EU customs exit confirmation (ECS / simplified declaration records)

No general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory live e-invoice clearance regime exists in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is permitted and accepted (Factur-X, PEPPOL) with mandatory e-invoicing for public sector contracts. VAT reporting is performed through periodic returns (forms 1, 3, 5, 6) filed electronically via MyGuichet; no transaction-level reporting mandate applies outside of Intrastat and EU VAT reporting rules.

Luxembourg customs and direct tax authorities can retrospectively verify export documentation through AEO security audits and cross-checks with ECS exit data. Missing or inadequate evidence invalidates the 0% rate and triggers a reassessment at 17% VAT, plus late-payment interest. Tax penalties for negligent under-declaration range up to 10% of the corrected tax; fraudulent export claims may trigger penalties up to 100% of evaded VAT, criminal fines up to EUR 500,000, and/or custodial penalties, with clawbacks extending over a five-year limitation period (ten years for fraud).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates as part of the euro area with full and unrestricted convertibility on both capital and current account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, partnership distributions, and loan repayments to non-residents are freely permitted without exchange control authorization or prior approval, subject only to standard EU anti-money laundering, sanctions, beneficial ownership disclosure, and tax compliance documentation. No material hurdles exist for the SCSp, which is treated as a tax-transparent partnership for direct tax purposes when not subject to corporate income tax in Luxembourg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no Luxembourg statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed timelines for repatriating or converting export receipts into euro. Luxembourg does not maintain reserves-related exchange controls; all euro-denominated and foreign-currency export proceeds may be held, converted, or disbursed freely. Nevertheless, commercial compliance under the EU Customs Code and the Authorized Economic Operator security audit regime requires proper trade transaction documentation, customs valuation reporting, and retained records for a minimum of ten years.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the European Central Bank for Eurosystem operations; regulatory oversight of payment institutions and EMI licensing by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU passporting EMIs e.g. Wirecard Alternative, TransactPro, or niche global transfer providers operating under an EU passport via a Luxembourg branch or agent. Legal constraints: Non-EEA digital only EMIs cannot serve as the primary clearing and settlement bank for an AEO supply-chain audit engagement because statutory partner drawdown and escrowed capital mechanics require a fully regulated Luxembourg or EU-authorised deposit-taking credit institution or authorized payment/e-money institution. Use of non-EU offshore EMIs is restricted to ancillary high-value cross-border payroll or minor vendor disbursements, not core treasury accounts. Core accounts must remain at a CSSF-supervised bank or a sanctioned EU credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg-City combined rate for corporate partners: 17% CIT (LIR Art. 174) + 1.19% solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT) + 6.75% municipal business tax (rate varies by commune). The SCSp itself is fiscally transparent; CIT is assessed at partner level on allocated profit shares.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (LIR Art. 146). Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, profit distributions by the SCSp are not classified as dividends for Luxembourg tax purposes: no WHT is withheld at the partnership level, and partners are taxed through their annual assessment. Where a corporate partner would have qualified for the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive regime, qualifying outbound dividends from a Luxembourg corporate entity benefit from 0% WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual declaration must be filed by 31 December of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (e.g., a year ending 31 December 2024 is due 31 December 2025). The ACD may grant extensions upon reasoned written request; no extension postpones a partner's obligation to pay assessed instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue is the statutory threshold for CbC Report and Master File obligations under the Luxembourg implementation of EU Directive 2016/881 (DAC4). Local File / contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be maintained by a Luxembourg taxpayer for all significant cross-border controlled transactions; in administrative practice, the ACD expects a Local File where annual related-party flows exceed approximately EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate or where intercompany service fees exceed EUR 100,000 per category, and the documentation must be submitted upon request within 30 days. Form 100's transfer-pricing annex must disclose intercompany transactions in all materially affected categories.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulation, but economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (LIR Art. 6 and the Abgabenordnung Art. 6 AAbL), the ATAD-implemented interest-limitation rule (LIR Art. 168quater), the MLI principal-purpose test for treaty benefits, and DAC6 disclosure rules. The SCSp undertaking AEO supply-chain security audits must demonstrate real commercial substance in Luxembourg: a local registered office, senior management functions performed in Luxembourg, qualified staff conducting audit work, and genuine decision-making on invoices and contracts. A shell-like SCSp without such substance risks recharacterization, denial of treaty relief, 0% WHT denial, and retrospective allocation of profits to the beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax at the ACD's discretion plus monthly default interest of 0.6%; concealment surcharge of up to 100% of the evaded tax; late-filing surcharge of 10%. Criminal conviction for tax fraud after 1 January 2023: fine of EUR 10,000 to EUR 500,000 and/or imprisonment of 1 to 5 years, graduated by the amount of tax evaded and whether the offense was committed as part of an organized group.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who sign or cause the filing of a false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties under the Luxembourg Companies Law (Law of 10 August 1915, Art. 59); criminal personal exposure includes fines, imprisonment, publication of the conviction, and an exit ban (interdiction de sortie du territoire) pending resolution of the criminal case.
Shell Entity / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under LIR Art. 6 and the OECD/MLI principal-purpose test; denial of treaty benefits and statutory exemptions; retrospective taxation of the true beneficial owner; penalty surcharge of 10% plus 0.6% monthly interest on the reassessed amount; potential ATAD exit-tax charge on migration of assets.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced for abuse or fraud, the general partner and directing officers face personal civil liability for the unpaid corporate tax plus punitive surcharges; criminal tax-fraud charges attach if the artificial arrangement is used to conceal taxable income, carrying prison terms and travel restrictions.
AML / AEO Authorization Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Revocation of AEO certification by the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration; administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 in serious AML cases and debarment from tender/supply-chain mandates; criminal AML sanctions under the Luxembourg amended law of 12 November 2004 include fines of up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment of 1 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are personally liable for AML compliance failures; deliberate non-reporting triggers personal criminal fines and imprisonment and cannot be indemnified by the partnership; customs authorities may impose individual travel bans and directors may be barred from exercising managerial functions in regulated supply-chain roles.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Under LIR Art. 56, the ACD will make a primary upward adjustment to the arm's-length result, with secondary adjustment and re-allocation of profits to the European or non-resident related party; 0.6% monthly interest applies from the original due date; a documentation-default penalty of 10% of the adjusted tax may be imposed; double-taxation relief through MAP is denied where documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for the accounting and filing obligations bear personal civil liability for penalties arising from non-documentation; in a criminal tax-evasion context, the deliberate absence of TP documentation is treated as circumstantial evidence of intent, exposing directors to personal fines and imprisonment; the Luxembourg tax administration may also seek personal recovery from directors who signed tax returns based on fabricated transfer-pricing data.

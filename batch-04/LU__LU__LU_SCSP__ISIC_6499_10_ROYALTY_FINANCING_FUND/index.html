Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu; official sites: acd.gouvernement.lu and aed.public.lu; business registry filings: Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SCSp: (1) execute notarized partnership deed; (2) register with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through lbr.lu to obtain the 15-digit business registration number (matricule), which is the universal tax identification number; (3) file VAT registration via Form 640 with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) immediately if any taxable supply or intra-Community acquisition is made; (4) the ACD assigns a direct-tax identification number for the SCSp partnership return (Form 200) and partner allocation schedule; (5) non-resident partners without a Luxembourg permanent establishment require no separate Luxembourg TIN unless claiming withholding-tax relief, VAT refund, or an advance pricing agreement ruling.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a Luxembourg SCSp: (1) execute notarized partnership deed; (2) register with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through lbr.lu to obtain the 15-digit business registration number (matricule), which is the universal tax identification number; (3) file VAT registration via Form 640 with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) immediately if any taxable supply or intra-Community acquisition is made; (4) the ACD assigns a direct-tax identification number for the SCSp partnership return (Form 200) and partner allocation schedule; (5) non-resident partners without a Luxembourg permanent establishment require no separate Luxembourg TIN unless claiming withholding-tax relief, VAT refund, or an advance pricing agreement ruling.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Article 6bis LIR (income tax law) prescribes fiscal transparency for SCSp: each partner is taxed directly on allocable income. The general partner may jointly elect semi-transparent entity-level taxation (SCSp treated as Luxembourg corporate resident for CIT/NWTR) by irrevocable declaration filed with ACD with the first tax return, binding on all partners. US investors may file federal check-the-box (Form 8832) to align with transparent treatment; EU ATAD 2 implementation (law of 21 December 2018) applies hybrid mismatch disallowance rules automatically to net any deduction/non-inclusion outcomes. Advance tax rulings on financing classification are obtainable via the ACD ruling committee.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for the small-enterprise exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); however, the threshold is unavailable to non-resident entities - foreign suppliers of taxable B2B services or distance goods into Luxembourg must register without threshold before supply; registration is also mandatory for intra-Community acquisitions by a Luxembourg-established business with no de minimis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% (zero-rating) on exports outside the EU under Article 43 VAT Law, with proof of physical removal shown within 90 days. Services: B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 20 et seq. VAT Law; Art. 44 VAT Directive) locates revenue-based financing, royalty-financing and fund management at the business customer's establishment; when the borrowing digital-brand or fund is a non-EU taxable person, the supply is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (effective 0%). Where the EU customer is taxable, reverse charge applies; supplies to EU non-taxable persons are exempt financial services (no credit). Input VAT recovery follows standard pro-rata and attribution rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) filed via the Export Control System (ECS)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / MT103 SWIFT payment proof recording the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and underlying revenue-based financing or royalty agreement
  • Certificate of consignee (ECS exit certificate or third-country customs entry/warehouse proof)

Luxembourg imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping and no continuous transaction controls (unlike Italy or Spain); no API tax-stamping regime applies. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing for suppliers to the Luxembourg public administration is operational via the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 standard. B2B e-invoicing remains optional; any e-invoice must ensure authenticity of origin and integrity under the VAT Law. Recurring tax-file obligations include SAF-T (Fichier d'échange standard) and EC Sales Lists (VIES/Form 53); Intrastat applies separately to goods.

AEDT may invalidate export zero-rating, reapply 17% VAT retroactively plus statutory arrears interest (currently 0.8% per month) for up to 5 years (10 years in fraud); monetary penalties range from 10% to 100% of the evaded VAT, and criminal prosecution carries imprisonment of up to 5 years for false export documentation (Articles 81-96 VAT Law). ACD may reopen income assessments under the same 5/10-year statutes with 50% negligence or 100% bad-faith penalties; customs surveillance runs 3 years from goods release. Clawback of previously refunded input VAT is automatic upon reversal of the zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the euro as legal tender with full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty and Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no exchange control approvals, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on dividend, royalty, or profit distributions to non-resident partners or beneficiaries. The SCSp, as a fiscally transparent partnership, remits profits directly to partners, and such remittances are free of FX licensing or central bank authorization. The only compliance layer is the BCL's statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations for cross-border flows, which are informational and not restrictive. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate tax withholding at source only where Luxembourg has not granted treaty relief, and not to FX or capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation quota, or surrender obligation on export proceeds, service fees, royalty collections, or financing revenues. All EUR and foreign-currency receipts may be retained, reinvested, or disbursed freely. Cross-border payments above thresholds must be reported to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics (BPM6/EU methodology), but this reporting is non-punitive and carries no conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — constrained. Non-EEA digital EMIs cannot operate in Luxembourg on a standalone basis without a CSSF-issued Electronic Money Institution licence (Loi du 1er août 2018 transposing EMI Directive 2009/110/EC) or use of an authorized agent/banking partner under the passport regime. EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into Luxembourg, but the SCSp must still satisfy the EMI's own AML/KYC programme, which frequently applies stricter UBO transparency standards to pass-through partnerships. Fintech EMIs are viable for low-value receivables collection (Direct Debit ACH) but cannot serve as primary escrow or treasury rails for capital injection, given their capital-assurance and safeguarding limits; Tier-1 licensed credit institutions remain mandatory for escrowed capital.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Pass-through partnership with undisclosed or indirect ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs), especially where LPs are offshore vehicles

Defense evidence: Full UBO cascade chart mapped to each partner's place of establishment; notarized partnership agreement and LP subscription documents; certified register of partners (even if non-public under Luxembourg law, it must be disclosed to the bank); KYC/AML identification package for every UBO above 10%; tax-residency certificates (form 6166-equivalent / Luxembourg certificate de résidence fiscale); FATCA/CRS registration confirmation of the SCSp in the Luxembourg tax authorities' system.
Revenue-based financing royalty receivables present ambiguous 'source of funds' and forward-cash-flow complexity

Geographic exposure of non-resident LPs or obligors in high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions

Root cause: Geographic exposure of non-resident LPs or obligors in high-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions

Defense evidence: EU/Luxembourg sanctions compliance policy; real-time screening certificates against EU/UN/OFAC lists; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds files for affected partners; clarification of any sanctioned nexus and remediation evidence; enhanced due-diligence questionnaire for jurisdictions flagged by FATF; internal waiver rationale approved by the bank's AML/CTF officer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident LP capital injections into the SCSp trust account are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank inter-entity transfers) in EUR through TARGET2, or in major convertible currencies via correspondent clearing (CHIPS/Fedwire). The remittance must carry an accurate ISO 20022 purpose code classified under BCL balance-of-payments statistics as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (category DI.1 under BPM6 reporting), with the SCSp's name, registered office, and partnership agreement reference in the free-text field. Banks require pre-delivery of the capital-call notice, the executed partnership subscription agreement, and CSSF/AML clearance before the first credit is accepted. Any conversion of USD/GBP to EUR must be executed with the receiving Luxembourg bank under a clean FX forward or spot contract to avoid mixing of investor funds and to preserve the escrow's audit trail.Operational disbursements and revenue-based financing payouts are cleared predominantly via SWIFT MT103 in EUR/USD/GBP for out-of-SEPA beneficiaries, and via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) Instant Payments or SEPA Direct Debit (SDD) within the EEA for digital-brand settlements. For structured financing tranches, banks accept Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) rails only where a confirmed bank is the issuing counterparty and the SCSp is the beneficiary; more commonly, the SCSp disburses against investment instructions using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Other Investment – Loans' and supporting financing-agreement references. All outbound payment instructions to third-party merchant obligors or royalty originators must be pre-registered in the bank's whitelist to survive transaction-monitoring controls, and the SCSp must maintain a full payee-KYC register consistent with Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 travel-rule data transmission.Deliverable plain-vanilla FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) with EU-authorised credit institutionsCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) executed under ISDA 2002 and subject to EMIR collateral and margin rulesFX options (European-style) for hedging known future revenue-based financing redemption cash flowsSame-day and tom-next FX swaps for settlement-liquidity conversion of royalty collectionsFutures and exchange-traded FX contracts where the SCSp is a professional client and the execution venue is an EU-regulated market or MTFNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) solely for exotic non-convertible currencies not relevant to EUR/USD or EUR/GBP exposures, and only where the counterparty is a licensed EU bank under EMIR

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined CIT plus solidarity surcharge plus municipal business tax for a Luxembourg City corporate investor; 0% at SCSp entity level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on distributions made by an SCSp to its partners, because SCSp distributions are not statutory dividends; Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding tax is 15% only where a resident corporate or partnership vehicle is tax-opaque and distributes dividends; under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and most DTA treaties the rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying holding structures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

30 June following the end of the fiscal year for electronic corporate and individual partner returns; annual financial statements must be filed with the RCS within 7 months of the balance sheet date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg law does not codify a specific local file/master file threshold, but the tax administration expects contemporaneous transfer pricing study documentation for all material related-party cross-border financing and royalty transactions, typically as from EUR 250,000 in annual transaction value; CbC notification and master file obligations arise for large groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no license-based Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the type used in certain Gulf jurisdictions, but substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit), the ATAD interest stripping limitation, and the requirement that the SCSp has a genuine Luxembourg office, local professional advisors, Luxembourg bank accounts, real decision-making records, and a locally resident general partner; a letterbox SCSp with no substantive management may be disregarded and its income reattributed to the controlling partners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Concealment of Taxable Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties under Luxembourg law: imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 100,000 per assessment; civil tax reassessment plus late-payment interest of 0.7% per month and a late-filing or understatement surcharge of 10% of the additional tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its directors are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid corporate tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; criminal courts may additionally order director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for intentional concealment.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may disregard the partnership under Article 56bis LIR, reallocate the royalty/financing income to the principal taxpayers, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties on unreported income; the commercial register may also de-register the company (rayure d'office) for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for resultant tax assessments if they implemented or knowingly benefited from the artificial structure; criminal sanctions for organizers of artificial tax arrangements include imprisonment, restitution orders, and international travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 on anti-money laundering, administrative fines of up to EUR 5 million for legal persons and EUR 2 million for individuals; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for aggravated money laundering; CSSF or the AML supervisor may impose supervisory fines and freeze fund assets.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its board of directors may be prosecuted criminally; personal liability extends to civil restitution of laundered amounts, travel bans pending investigation, and imprisonment for intentional non-compliance with AML obligations.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Aggressive Royalty Financing Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment recharacterizing the transaction at arm's length, interest charge on underpaid tax at 0.7% per month, penalties for understatement of tax reaching 30% to 50% of the additional tax, and mandatory publication of the tax adjustment in certain cases of aggressive tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign the manipulated transfer pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax; in cases of fraudulent TP documentation, personal criminal liability includes fines, confiscation orders, and imprisonment of up to 5 years.

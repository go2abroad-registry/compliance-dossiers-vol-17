Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for income tax matters; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration. Centralized e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu / eCDF (Electronic Customs and VAT Filing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a Luxembourg SCSp must first obtain approval of the entity name and file incorporation documents with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a certified notary or fiduciary. Upon RCS registration, the SCSp automatically receives a Luxembourg 13-digit business identification number. For VAT and income tax purposes, the SCSp or its managing partner must file separate registration with the AEDT using the RCS number; non-EU resident partners acting as joint and several VAT representatives must indicate their status on the VAT registration form. If the SCSp makes taxable supplies, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro d'identification TVA) in addition to the national identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign investor establishing a Luxembourg SCSp must first obtain approval of the entity name and file incorporation documents with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a certified notary or fiduciary. Upon RCS registration, the SCSp automatically receives a Luxembourg 13-digit business identification number. For VAT and income tax purposes, the SCSp or its managing partner must file separate registration with the AEDT using the RCS number; non-EU resident partners acting as joint and several VAT representatives must indicate their status on the VAT registration form. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg. The SCSp is per se fiscally transparent, meaning profits and losses flow through to partners. Luxembourg law does not permit the SCSp to elect to be treated as a corporation for tax purposes. Binding advance tax rulings (conventions with the ACD) may be obtained to confirm the transparency of the SCSp, the treatment of hybrid instruments, and the allocation of income among partners, subject to the OECD/G20 Hybrid Mismatch rules as implemented in Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for small businesses established in Luxembourg; no registration threshold applies to intra-Community acquisitions or imports of goods into Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, including supplies of cement clinker loaded on ocean freight for non-EU buyers, qualify for the 0% VAT export rate provided the supplier retains full evidence of departure from the EU within the statutory time limit (normally 3 months following the supply). B2B services, such as commission-based international freight and wholesale logistics services, may be zero-rated or outside Luxembourg VAT when supplied to a non-EU business customer under the place-of-supply rules of Article 14 of Luxembourg VAT law. Exemptions are conditional on the export transaction being properly recorded in the exporter's books and supported by the consignee's valid non-EU VAT/GST registration number where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD/EX)
  • Export customs receipt or electronic export manifest (AES/ECS confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (port-to-port ocean shipment) or other transport document (CMR/air waybill)
  • Commercial invoice (including shipment terms, Incoterms 2020, and export zero-rating annotation)
  • Packing list and weight certificate for bulk clinker cargo
  • Proof of physical transport to the non-EU destination (Port of loading / Port of destination evidence)
  • Supplier declaration / declaration of non-EU destination of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory digital stamping regime is currently in force for domestic B2B transactions. E-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to Luxembourg public sector entities, using the PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format through the national Peppol access point. All VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and customs declarations must be filed electronically through the eCDF/MyGuichet platform; a dedicated e-invoice clearance and archiving environment for private contracts is not mandated.

The AEDT may audit zero-rated export transactions and require the full repayment (clawback) of any VAT claimed as zero-rated, together with interest at the legal rate, where the exporter fails to substantiate the physical exit of goods from the EU, lacks valid customs declarations, or does not provide credible evidence of payment and repatriation of foreign currency proceeds. Administrative penalties can reach 10% of the tax due for each incomplete accounting period, and VAT fraud penalties may be doubled or tripled depending on the intentional nature of the non-compliance; directors, managing partners, and appointed VAT representatives may be held jointly and severally liable. Criminal prosecution for deliberate export zero-rating fraud can lead to fines of EUR 250 to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment, as set out in the Luxembourg general tax law (Abgabenordnung) and VAT enforcement provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro-area convertibility. The euro is fully convertible for the SCSp; there are no exchange-control permissions or surrender requirements for current-account or capital-account transactions. Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC guarantee free movement of capital, so partner equity injections, trade income, profit distributions and liquidation proceeds can be remitted freely without BCL/CSSF approval. The only mandatory compliance burden is the BCL balance-of-payments (BOP/IT) statistical declaration filed by the SCSp's bank, plus standard AML/KYC review. The tax-transparent status of the SCSp means profit distributions are partnership allocations rather than dividends; Luxembourg generally imposes no dividend withholding tax on such distributions to partners, provided the SCSp has not opted to be taxed as a corporate entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline exists in Luxembourg. Export and ocean-freight proceeds may be held freely in USD or EUR in onshore or offshore accounts without any mandatory exchange into euro. Where the SCSp clears proceeds through a Luxembourg bank, the credit is classified for BCL BOP/IT statistics as exports of goods or services, but no conversion or repatriation is imposed. Substance and transfer-pricing rules (OECD BEPS) still require that invoicing, booking and cash flows correspond to genuine commercial activity and that the SCSp maintains adequate economic substance in Luxembourg.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the national central bank of Luxembourg and member of the Eurosystem, exercising monetary, macroprudential and payment-system oversight in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for supervised bank conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore non-EU and third-country EMIs, which cannot passport into Luxembourg, lack SWIFT correspondent clearing capacity, and cannot issue documentary letters of credit. Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for CSSF-licensed Luxembourg payment institutions and e-money institutions, which may provide SEPA/instant payments, payment cards, and FX execution but are not substitutes for a Tier 1 bank account in high-value ocean-vessel bulk commodity settlement. For this SCSp (cement clinker bulk, USD/EUR wires, and LC), EMI rails are viable only as a secondary disbursement channel for low-value operating expenses; the primary clearing node must be a commercial bank with trade finance and correspondent banking relationships.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) the contributing partner instructs its bank to send an SWIFT MT103 credit to the SCSp account at the Luxembourg bank, quoting the SCSp name, BIC, and IBAN (EUR) or the corresponding USD account; (ii) the payment instruction must carry the ESP/EBA purpose code CAPT (capital payments) or TRDP (trade payments) and cite the LPA article governing capital contributions; (iii) the Luxembourg bank records the inward transfer under BCL BOP/IT statistics as a direct-investment capital contribution to a partnership; (iv) the credit is released only after AML/KYC verification of the remitter, the UBO chain, and the purpose; (v) no FX or exchange-control approval is required, but the credited funds must be matched against the signed contribution schedule and booked to the capital account to support future audits and bank transactions.Primary clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV wire transfers (telegraphic transfers) for advance payments, freight, port charges, storage, and open-account balances to suppliers; (b) commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) issued via SWIFT MT700 for bulk Portland clinker purchases, payable against a full set of negotiable bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and weight certificate; (c) standby or back-to-back letters of credit for supplier requests of first-demand guarantee security; (d) documentary collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) as a fallback. EUR settlements clear through TARGET2 via the Luxembourg bank; USD settlements clear through the bank's United States correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate statutory rate in Luxembourg-City, composed of 17% corporate income tax (CIT), a 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT, and a municipal business tax of 6.75%; non-city communes apply a municipal coefficient ranging from 6.75% to 11.28% (aggregate between 24.94% and ~29.47%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - an SCSp is fiscally transparent for Luxembourg income tax; payments of profit shares are partnership allocations and are not subject to the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax applicable to joint-stock companies. A Luxembourg-resident corporate partner is taxed on the allocation as ordinary business income (subject to participation exemption where the SCSp interest qualifies under Art. 166 LIR).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, filing is due by 31 December of the following year; return preparers (accredited experts-comptables) benefit from an automatic extension to 30 April of the following year (in practice the ACD grants additional delay only upon request).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (Local File) for a Luxembourg entity with annual turnover above EUR 50 million; Master File and CbC Report required when the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000; below these thresholds, the taxpayer must still maintain contemporaneous arm's length documentation when undertaking intra-group transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone ESR statute as in the Caribbean model, but applies the OECD/ATAD general anti-abuse rule, the principal purpose test under tax treaties, and the ACD substance doctrine: an SCSp distributing cement clinker must demonstrate real presence (business plan, office or substance, personnel with decision-making authority, bank accounts, contractual records) in Luxembourg; a pure invoice-through entity with no substance will be challenged, possibly recharacterised as an artificial arrangement, and the zero-WHT/transparent treatment may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung / Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 2 months to 5 years (aggravated cases: up to 10 years) and fines up to EUR 250,000, plus automatic payment of the evaded tax, a 10% penalty and monthly 0.6% default interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the SCSp (and de facto managers) are jointly and severally liable for the evaded amounts, subject to seizure, travel bans, and personal surrender in extradition proceedings.
Artificial Shell/Substance Deficit (GAAR - Art. 6 AO / ATAD PPT)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and denial of transparency/participation exemption; retroactive reassessment of the entire profit at the 24.94% aggregate tax rate plus 10% penalty and 0.6% monthly interest; possible 5-year statute reopening for foreign-controlled entities.

Director personal liability: The ACD can pierce the veil if the entity is a sham; directors and the general partner are personally exposed for restitution of underpaid tax through the action en comblement du passif.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Non-Arm's Length Invoicing)

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable profit in the SCSp and at partner level, disallowance of the corresponding cost, 10% surcharge, 0.6% monthly default interest, and penalty under the TP documentation rules; potential 5-year audit reopening.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SCSp general partner may be liable for the adjusted tax if they knowingly signed the non-arm's-length intercompany agreements; criminal exposure where false invoices were used.
Beneficial Ownership Register and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 and criminal fines up to EUR 1,250,000 for persistent non-disclosure; imprisonment up to 3 years for obstruction; freezing of bank accounts and prevention of profit repatriation.

Director personal liability: The general partner and the compliance officer are personally liable for the register filings; the criminal sanctions apply directly to individual directors.
Unreported Luxembourg Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the PE profit share at the 24.94% aggregate rate, plus the 10% penalty, 0.6% monthly interest and potential criminal sanctions for wilful concealment.

Director personal liability: The managing general partner who caused the concealment is personally jointly and severally liable; travel bans may be imposed during the criminal investigation.
Illicit Dividends / Distributions From Insolvent SCSp

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the distribution from the limited partners; directors' liability under Art. 59 of the Luxembourg Companies Law; criminal sanctions for abuse of creditors' rights.

Director personal liability: The general partner is presumed liable for the shortfall; a limited partner may lose its liability shield if it takes part in management; insolvency courts may issue personal contribution orders and travel bans.

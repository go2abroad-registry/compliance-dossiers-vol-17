Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT. Unified access via MyGuichet.lu and the AEDT e-CDF electronic VAT portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SCSp and obtain the RCS registration number and national identification number (matricule) through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). Step 2: Submit the standard VAT registration form (Formulaire 644) to the AEDT via the e-CDF/MyGuichet platform to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). Step 3: Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent for income tax, no separate corporate income tax file is opened in the partnership's name with the ACD; each partner is registered and taxed in their own capacity. The SCSp nonetheless files periodic VAT returns and, where applicable, Intrastat and EC Sales Lists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; fiscal transparency of the SCSp is mandatory under Luxembourg domestic law (confirmed by the law of 12 July 2013 and circulars of the ACD). Available mechanisms: (a) election to be treated as a taxable person for VAT purposes, which is automatic for the SCSp acting in its own name; (b) VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') under Article 60 VLL; (c) commercial-status option under the law of 10 August 1915 (as amended), automatically deemed for the SCSp; (d) ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch disallowance elections and documentation in accordance with the law of 21 December 2017 implementing EU Directive 2016/1164.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for the small-business exemption (Article 57 VLL) applicable only to established domestic taxable persons; zero registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers — registration required from the first taxable supply in/into Luxembourg.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime exists. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies under the law of 7 November 2019, requiring invoices to public-sector buyers via the PEPPOL network (Peppol BIS 3.0). B2B e-invoicing is optional; the AEDT accepts structured electronic files. Large taxpayers must maintain and provide, on audit demand, the Luxembourg Standard Audit File for Tax (FEC/L-SAF-T) covering accounting records and VAT-relevant transactional data. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists are filed electronically through the e-CDF portal with prescribed API/interoperability requirements only for approved software vendors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates under full capital and current account convertibility within the euro area. There is no Luxembourg exchange control legislation, no mandatory approval for cross-border capital movements, and no profit remittance licensing for a pass-through SCSp. Distributions to non-resident limited partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at the partnership level. The only operative constraints are EU AML/KYC verification, sanctions screening, FATCA/CRS reporting, and counterparty bank internal risk policy. Convertibility risk is negligible; EUR-USD and EUR-CNH liquidity is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the euro area impose no statutory surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The SCSp may freely retain USD, CHF, CNH or other trade receipts in foreign-currency accounts at CSSF-regulated banks; only standard BCL monetary statistics reporting applies to balance-sheet data, not to individual trade flows.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); banking and AML/CFT supervision by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the EU free movement of capital framework (TFEU Articles 63-66)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs offer faster onboarding and Platform Pay integration, but most restrict partnerships with non-resident UBOs, impose monthly transaction velocity caps incompatible with wholesale invoice settlement, and cannot support documentary LC issuance. UK or offshore EMIs are not passportable into Luxembourg post-Brexit; platform pay balances must be swept daily to the SCSp's own CSSF-regulated bank account to avoid undisclosed payment-institution escrow risk. Recommended use: secondary collection of platform pay receipts only, with BIL or BCEE as the primary clearing bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17.00% (top marginal CIT rate for corporate partners; plus 7.00% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax; aggregate effective top rate approx. 24.94% in Luxembourg City; SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and not subject to entity-level CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15.00% standard Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding tax. Reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the SCSp's capital for 12 consecutive months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Luxembourg participation exemption. A transparent SCSp generally does not itself trigger dividend WHT; foreign partners are taxed on allocated income by local law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 50,000,000 where the annual aggregate of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000. Mandatory Master File where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be contemporaneous, available within 30 days of ACD request, and prepared in line with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose a formal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation. Equivalent substance requirements are applied through the Luxembourg abuse-of-law doctrine, ATAD provisions (Articles 56bis LIR, 168a LIR), OECD MLI/BEPS Action 5, and the tax treaty beneficial-ownership test. An SCSp carrying on wholesale distribution of optical frames must demonstrate real management, local assets, decision-making, and risk control in Luxembourg to obtain treaty relief and to avoid being recharacterised as a letterbox conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for tax fraud; civil fraud penalty of 50% of the evaded tax; default interest at 0.60% per month; publication of the criminal sentence may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SCSp's general partner may be held jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties if they knowingly participated in the false filing; criminal conviction triggers travel bans and bar from future directorships in Luxembourg.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ACD may recharacterise the SCSp as a Luxembourg tax resident corporate entity, deny DTA benefits, and impose back-tax CIT at 15%/17%, plus 7% solidarity surcharge, plus municipal business tax up to ~6.75% in Luxembourg City, with penalties of up to 50% of the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: De facto or de jure directors who knowingly maintain a sham SCSp may be personally liable for the tax deficiency plus penalties; travel bans may be imposed during criminal investigation; imprisonment for abuse of law where intent is established.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004 (as amended); imprisonment of 3 to 10 years for intentional money laundering; suspension of the general partner's management mandate; seizure of banking assets.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for AML infringements; conviction triggers a minimum 5-year ban on managing any Luxembourg legal entity and may include an international travel ban pending trial.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ACD transfer pricing adjustment to the taxpayer's taxable profit; administrative fine up to EUR 10,000 for documentation failure; additional 10% penalty on the amount of adjustment if the taxpayer's documentation is materially misleading; default interest from the original assessment date.

Director personal liability: Directors are not strictly liable for TP adjustments, but may be personally responsible where they knowingly sign false intercompany agreements, inflate distributions, or cause the SCSp to use a sham pricing method to evade tax.
Late Filing and Non-Payment of Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Monthly default interest of 0.60% on the unpaid WHT; late-payment surcharge of 10%; administrative fine of EUR 10,000 for failure to remit withheld tax; the ACD may procedurally deny the taxpayer's treaty-reduction claim.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the distribution without WHT deduction may be personally liable for the withheld amount and penalties; in the absence of SCSp assets, the ACD can enforce against the general partner's personal assets and impose travel restrictions.

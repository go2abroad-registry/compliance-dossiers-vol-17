Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate/income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises for customs. Central portal and e-filing: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu; eCDF for customs formalities.

E-government filing portal

For an SCSp established in Luxembourg: (1) Register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via MyGuichet.lu to obtain the national business registration number (numéro d'identification). (2) Submit a VAT registration application (form 1000 or online e-form) to the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, attaching the partnership agreement, proof of Luxembourg address, authority of the legal representative and projected turnover. (3) Receive the Luxembourg VAT number in the format LU followed by 8 digits (total 10 characters) and obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration for export declarations. The SCSp may register as a VAT taxable person itself; fiscally transparent treatment for direct tax does not prevent separate VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For an SCSp established in Luxembourg: (1) Register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via MyGuichet.lu to obtain the national business registration number (numéro d'identification). (2) Submit a VAT registration application (form 1000 or online e-form) to the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, attaching the partnership agreement, proof of Luxembourg address, authority of the legal representative and projected turnover. (3) Receive the Luxembourg VAT number in the format LU followed by 8 digits (total 10 characters) and obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration for export declarations. The SCSp may register as a VAT taxable person itself; fiscally transparent treatment for direct tax does not prevent separate VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for corporate income tax and personal income tax; partners are taxed in their own capacity. However, an SCSp that carries on a commercial, industrial or professional activity is separately within the scope of municipal business tax (communal trade tax), and may elect for normal VAT taxation where the small business exemption would otherwise apply. A Luxembourg VAT group election (assujetti unique) is available under Luxembourg VAT law, allowing an SCSp and certain parties to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT; the election requires prior approval and is VAT-only. ATAD-based hybrid mismatch rules apply; no separate hybrid election exists under domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for the domestic small-business franchise exemption (optional; can be waived). Mandatory VAT registration applies to all taxable persons carrying out taxable activities in Luxembourg, without a de minimis turnover requirement. Non-established suppliers making distance sales of keycaps to non-taxable EU consumers must register in Luxembourg or use the Union OSS if their total cross-border distance sales exceed the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 41 of the Luxembourg Value Added Tax Act applies to the sale of custom mechanical keycaps where the goods are dispatched to a destination outside the EU and the supplier retains documentary proof of exportation. The supplier must be identified as exporter and the recipient must be located outside the EU; transport must be initiated and completed outside the EU, with transport documents evidencing departure from Luxembourg/EU. Services closely associated with the export (e.g., transport and insurance) also qualify for zero-rating. The VAT deduction remains available for input VAT incurred in making the exported goods.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD / export declaration via Luxembourg Customs) including exporter of record EORI number, HS/CN code, customs value and ship or air waybill reference, with status 'exportation'
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing dispatch from the EU and arrival at the non-EU destination; for container transport, terminal or container movement reference records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation showing the foreign customer's payment for the exported keycaps remitted to the Luxembourg bank account in the agreed foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating product description, quantity, unit values and total customs value, including Incoterms
  • Proof of physical departure within the period required by Luxembourg tax practice (normally within 3 months of supply), such as the export document exit confirmation or customs transit declaration

Luxembourg has not implemented mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for commercial VAT invoices. E-invoicing is compulsory only for invoices addressed to public entities (B2G) under European standard EN 16931, transmitted through the national e-invoicing platform. For B2B/B2C transactions, paper or electronic invoices issued under EU VAT invoicing rules are accepted; VAT returns and EU sales listings (EC sales lists) must be filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu. SAF-T accounting data may be required during tax audits.

Where export zero-rating is claimed without the required substantiation, the AEDT will retroactively assess VAT at 17% on the export supply and deny input VAT recovery. The assessment window is generally five years from the end of the year in which the VAT became chargeable, extended to ten years in cases of fraud. Default interest accrues at 0.6% per month (or part month) on the unpaid VAT. Administrative fines may reach up to EUR 25,000 or, in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal penalties including imprisonment for up to five years and a criminal fine may be imposed under the Luxembourg general tax legislation. Exporters must retain the export evidence bundle for at least ten years and produce it immediately on request of the tax authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility as a euro-area member. There are no residual exchange-control approvals, quantitative limits, or licensing requirements for cross-border payments, partner capital contributions, e-commerce settlement, or profit distributions by a qualifying tax-transparent SCSp. The sole operational constraints are: (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting performed by resident credit institutions; (ii) source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification under the Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT, as amended, and CSSF Regulation 12-02; and (iii) payment message data requirements under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 (Transfer of Funds Regulation). Profit remittance by an SCSp is treated as contractual profit-share distribution, not a dividend; no Luxembourg withholding tax applies where the SCSp has validly opted for tax transparency and no domestic anti-abuse look-through rule is triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg law imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export proceeds in foreign currency. An SCSp may retain USD, CNY, or other foreign-currency receivables in onshore or offshore accounts without BCL restriction; voluntary conversion to EUR is at the entity's discretion and is typically executed via the bank's spot or forward desk. Export receipt documentation must nevertheless support the underlying commercial transaction for AML/KYC and transfer-funds transparency purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem; prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment services is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Minister of Finance retains residual regulatory authority for exchange-control policy, which is otherwise harmonised at EU level under Articles 63-66 TFEU.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment-processing only; Low as a primary treasury bank. Luxembourg recognises CSSF-licensed Payment Institutions and E-Money Institutions, and EU/EEA EMIs may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2; however, EMIs generally cannot provide credit, offer deposit-guarantee protection (e-money floats are not covered by the EUR 100,000 FGDL scheme), or route SWIFT MT103s to non-EEA suppliers with the same certainty as a correspondent bank. For this SCSp, an EU-licensed EMI may be used as a settlement collection account for Shopify Payments and Stripe payouts, but all capital injections, trade disbursements, and FX hedges must flow through a Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial bank to preserve regulatory cleanliness.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: inbound partner capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's originating bank to the SCSp's operational account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. The payment must carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity-related transfer) or 'LOAN' (shareholder loan) where applicable, with the partnership agreement or contribution schedule number in the remittance information; the bank will file the BCL balance-of-payments statistical report for the resident partnership. Verification bundle: executed deed of contribution and subscription schedule, payment instruction matching the capital account ledger, source-of-funds evidence (partner bank statements, tax returns, or exit liquidity documentation), CSSF-compliant UBO declaration, and confirmation that the contribution is not taxable income for the tax-transparent SCSp.Preferred clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for supplier payments to PRC/HK/TW accounts, with the ISO 20022 purpose code 'GDDS' (purchase of goods for resale) or 'TRAD' (trade transaction) and the commercial invoice number in the message; (ii) commercial letters of credit (documentary credits under UCP600) advised through BIL, BGL BNP Paribas, or BCEE trade desks for high-value or recurring production orders; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer and instant SEPA for EU packaging and logistics suppliers; (iv) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) as a lower-cost alternative for mid-value orders.

UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Luxembourg law: (i) amended Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, Title I, Articles 101-107, as inserted by the Law of 12 July 2013, governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); (ii) Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE Act), as amended by the Law of 25 March 2020, imposing UBO filing for registered entities including SCSp; (iii) amended Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended by the Law of 9 January 2020 implementing EU Directive (EU) 2018/843 (5AMLD); (iv) Grand-Ducal Regulation of 14 December 2004 on customer identification and verification; and (v) CSSF Circulars 18/681 and 19/732 on AML/CFT and CDD obligations.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual postal-only office is not acceptable for an RCS-registered SCSp engaged in cross-border e-commerce. Luxembourg law requires that each registered entity have an actual registered office located at a physical address in Luxembourg where legal correspondence, books and registers are available. However, professional domiciliation agents legally established in Luxembourg and subject to AML supervision may provide a physical registered office address together with mail-handling and physical reception services. Such a domiciliation arrangement is accepted only when the domiciliation agent actually operates at that address and when the SCSp's partners, tax representative and diligence officers are contactable there. A pure virtual address without physical staff or document-keeping capability is not permissible and will not satisfy the RCS or CSSF commercial substance expectations.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; centralized onboarding via MyGuichet.lu and the RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity forming or acting through a Luxembourg SCSp must obtain a Luxembourg business/TIN number. For VAT purposes, the foreign entity or the SCSp (acting for the partners) files a registration form with the AEDT, submitting notarized constitutional documents, economic substance evidence, and details of the appointed local VAT representative if not established in the EU. A Luxembourg tax number (matricule) is issued by the ACD following incorporation; VAT number is issued by the AEDT within a standard processing window of 5-15 business days. A local establishment or VAT representative is required where taxable supplies are made in Luxembourg and the trader has no EU fixed establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident entity forming or acting through a Luxembourg SCSp must obtain a Luxembourg business/TIN number. For VAT purposes, the foreign entity or the SCSp (acting for the partners) files a registration form with the AEDT, submitting notarized constitutional documents, economic substance evidence, and details of the appointed local VAT representative if not established in the EU. A Luxembourg tax number (matricule) is issued by the ACD following incorporation; VAT number is issued by the AEDT within a standard processing window of 5-15 business days. A local establishment or VAT representative is required where taxable supplies are made in Luxembourg and the trader has no EU fixed establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

SCSp is fiscally transparent by default. The SCSp may elect, by joint declaration of all partners, to be taxed as a taxable corporate entity under the 'opting-in' regime, making it subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax and net wealth tax instead of pass-through taxation. This election is binding and requires prior approval/registration with the Luxembourg direct tax administration. Hybrid mismatch elections are available only to the extent compatible with ATAD-consistent Luxembourg domestic rules (Circulaire LIR n° 212)); no US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 for domestic taxable supplies by small enterprises; for cross-border intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers a simplified registration threshold of EUR 10,000 (including other intra-EU distance sales) applies within the EU-wide OSS threshold regime; above that threshold the foreign seller must register and charge Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically shipped outside the EU and services supplied to non-EU business or private customers under B2B/B2C place-of-supply rules qualify for the 0% VAT rate. The seller must prove physical exit from the EU within the required time limits, hold transport documents, and obtain the export customs declaration. Offshore services to non-established persons are outside Luxembourg VAT scope or zero-rated where the supplier is established in Luxembourg and the recipient's jurisdiction is outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Commercial invoice with export delivery terms (Incoterms 2020)
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, airway waybill or international carrier packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign bank FX credit confirmation proving actual receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of buyer identity, EU exit point, and destination-country customs clearance where available

Luxembourg does not generally mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for business-to-business transactions. Structured electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies/sectors under e-invoicing public procurement rules. For all other transactions, e-invoicing is permitted subject to authenticity and integrity controls; archived e-invoices must be retained for at least 6 years. No national tax authority API clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC) model is lawfully enforced as of the current tax compliance period.

Luxembourg VAT authorities require export evidence to be retained for at least 6 years and will claw back zero-rated treatment, interest, and penalties if the required documentation is incomplete, fraudulent, or not produced timely. For fraudulent export zero-rating, fines up to 200% of evaded tax may be imposed under LIR/LTVAT rules, plus criminal liability (prison sentence) for directors/partners, and the SCSp's tax-transparent partners are jointly and severally liable for non-payment of output VAT.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full euro-area convertibility. Current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under Articles 63-66 TFEU, with only narrow EU sanctions/security exceptions. Profit remittance is not subject to FX authorisation or quantitative limits. For an SCSp, which is a transparent partnership, distributions follow contractual profit-share definitions; non-resident partners receiving distributions may be subject to partner-level taxation but no blocking exchange-control permit is required. Banks will verify source of funds and underlying commercial substance for all dividend/profit distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the euro-area framework impose no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign-currency accounts at a Luxembourg or EU bank. However, EU Regulation 2015/847 wire-transfer traceability rules and AML/CFT obligations require complete originator/beneficiary information on every cross-border payment.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign exchange administration and applies EU free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-to-High for payment facilitation and multi-currency treasury support, but Low-to-Medium as a standalone primary bank account. Authorised EU e-money institutions and payment institutions, including Airwallex and Stripe, may hold segregated client funds and offer FX/clearing rails under Luxembourg payment-services law (Law of 10 August 2018 on payment services and e-money, as amended) and the CSSF regime. A Luxembourg SCSp engaged in Amazon FBA may use such providers for operational settlement, but core regulatory treasury discipline requires at least one relationship with a fully licensed Luxembourg credit institution to satisfy KYC credibility, capital escrow comfort, and correspondent-banking clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are settled via SWIFT MT103 (if delivered from outside EEA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (if delivered from within SEPA). The beneficiary payment instruction must name the Luxembourg SCSp, reserved IBAN, and bank BIC. The payment reference/purpose field should state clearly: 'Capital contribution to [SCSp name], partnership agreement dated [date], partner [name]'. No foreign-exchange approval or licence is required, but the bank must verify the contributor against the KYC/UBO file and may require completion of BCL statistical reporting forms for balance-of-payments purposes. Capital contribution is accepted only after the adoption/resolution and contractual drawdown notice are produced; cash escrow accounts may be used pending seconded capital calls.Operational trade disbursements may be made by SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR within SEPA), SWIFT MT103 (cross-border and non-EUR), or documentary letters of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 for stock purchases from third-country suppliers. For USD disbursements, clearing is normally routed through correspondent banks in the United States using CHIPS/Fedwire after conversion at Luxembourg bank rates. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg corporate rate (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + aggregate municipal business trade tax; SCSp itself is fiscally transparent and not an entity subject to CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax for Luxembourg resident companies; distributions from an SCSp are not dividends and are not subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at the SCSp level because the SCSp is fiscally transparent.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Formulaire 720: by 31 May of the year following the tax year for calendar-year taxpayers; non-calendar year-ends within three months of the balance sheet date. Formulaire 640: for fiscal year ending 31 December, deadline is 31 May of the following year; extensions may be granted via e-filing until 31 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold for a Master File; a Luxembourg Local File is required when related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services, financing, and intangibles. The SCSp must also maintain contemporaneous documentation where Amazon FBA cross-border operations create related-party flows.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the style of the BVI/UAE; however, the general anti-abuse rule, ATAD implementation, and case law on tax residence and treaty abuse require an SCSp to have substantive presence: local registered office, economic activity, partner/Director decision-making in Luxembourg, bank account, and adequate staff. For Amazon FBA business, the SCSp must not be a shell conduit; the commercial risk and inventory purchases must be supported by real contracts, invoices, and management records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Repair assessment for the tax evaded plus a penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax; daily late-payment interest at 0.8% monthly; criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years for egregious concealment.

Director personal liability: The general partner or the directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for civil tax restitution; travel bans, freezing orders, and disgorgement of personal assets may be imposed; conviction can lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and participation exemption under GAAR; recharacterisation of profits at the partner level; administrative penalty of 25% of the adjusted tax; potential requalification of the SCSp as a taxable Luxembourg entity.

Director personal liability: Corporate general partner directors may be held personally liable for responsible corporate management; exposure to damages and restitution; travel restrictions in serious abuse cases.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for regulated financial intermediaries; for criminal money laundering, imprisonment of five to ten years and seizure of the e-commerce business assets.

Director personal liability: The general partner is subject to civil asset forfeiture; directors who operate the payment accounts without AML controls face criminal liability and suspension of driving/travel rights; non-compliance with beneficial ownership registration exposes directors to custodial sentences.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to an accurate arm's length result plus secondary recharacterisation of deemed dividends; penalty of 50% of the additional tax due when TP documentation is not contemporaneously prepared and submitted within three months.

Director personal liability: Tax directors and corporate officers of the general partner can be held jointly and severally liable for civil penalties arising from false or misleading TP reporting; persistent non-compliance leads to regulatory disqualification and travel bans.

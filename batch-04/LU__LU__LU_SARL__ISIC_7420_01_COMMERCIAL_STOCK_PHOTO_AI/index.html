Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the supervision of the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice; electronic filing through the LBR portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (S.à r.l. constitution and management); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and annual accounts, Articles 1 to 23; Grand-Ducal Regulation relating to the Luxembourg Business Registers and publication in the electronic compendium.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif notarié) comprising the articles of association (statuts), together with the RCS filing form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for an S.à r.l.; cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 25% paid up, deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution, with the bank certificate attached to the notarial deed; funds become available to the company only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification for the registered office covering simple evacuation, smoke detection and electrical fire risk, issued by the communal fire authority or a certified engineering expert. Structural load and occupancy clearance obtained through the commune's occupancy permit for commercial office use. Occupational clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) confirming safe workplace conditions, adequate lighting, ergonomic equipment, and sanitary facilities for administrative staff.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for an S.à r.l.; cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 25% paid up, deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution, with the bank certificate attached to the notarial deed; funds become available to the company only after RCS registration.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Ministry of the Economy, Directorate of Industry (Direction de l'Industrie), Luxembourg; not applicable to ISIC 7420 service activity unless an establishment permit (autorisation d'établissement) under the Law of 2 September 2011 is interpreted as applicable.

Category: No industrial or environmental manufacturing licence is required for a stock-photography and generative visual asset platform; if implementing a commercial establishment permit, the relevant instrument is the 'autorisation d'établissement' issued by the Ministry of the Economy.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Unified e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (Guichet.public.lu); electronic VAT filing via FEDELTAX.

E-government filing portal

1) Execute incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and file with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national business number (matricule, 11-digit numeric) and RCS registration; 2) Register the SARL with the AEDT at the office of the registered seat; 3) Receive VAT identification number formatted LU + 8-digit code derived from the matricule; 4) Non-resident foreign entities must file a direct electronic application to AEDT via the LuxTrust-authenticated MyGuichet portal before commencing taxable activities; no local fiscal agent is statutorily required for EU-resident suppliers, but a tax representative is mandatorily designated by non-EU suppliers without a Luxembourg establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is a capital company fully tax-resident in Luxembourg when its registered office or central administration is in Luxembourg; it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune), and VAT; no check-the-box pass-through mechanism exists for a SARL.

Available election forms

1) Fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR for resident companies held at least 95%; 2) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Art. 61 VAT Law; 3) ATAD hybrid mismatch alignment under Art. 168quater LIR imposes mandatory 90-day matching treatment with no elective opt-out for hybrid entities; 4) Small-business VAT exemption election (franchise) under Art. 51(b) VAT Law for annual turnover up to EUR 35,000; 5) IP box deduction under Art. 50bis LIR and participation exemption under Art. 166 LIR require formal economic substance and documentation rather than simple elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business franchise exemption; no registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers, registration being compulsory from the first taxable transaction; the EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-sale/electronic-services threshold applies for intra-EU B2C supplies (above it, destination-state VAT applies via OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Art. 44 VAT Law upon proof of transport outside the EU; intra-Community supplies to EU taxable persons are zero-rated under Art. 43 VAT Law subject to a valid customer VAT number (VIES) and EC Sales List. For the stock-photo/AI digital asset business, electronically supplied services (image downloads, licensing) follow place-of-supply rules: B2B downloads to EU or non-EU customers are taxed where the customer is established (reverse charge outside Luxembourg), B2C downloads to non-EU consumers fall outside Luxembourg VAT, and B2C downloads to EU consumers are declared through the One-Stop-Shop (Union scheme) at the consumer's country rate. Digital exports require no customs declaration but necessitate robust digital evidence: IP address geolocation, payment processor (Stripe/PayPal) country records, customer contracts, and e-invoice trails.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/IE-507) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) for tangible media shipments
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, Air Waybill, or courier/postal receipt)
  • Commercial invoice stating 'VAT exempt - export zero-rated Art. 44'
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank/Stripe/PayPal settlement proof evidencing cross-border receipt of funds
  • Intra-EU supplies: validated customer VAT number extracted from VIES and matching EC Sales List entry

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime in force for B2B/B2C transactions; mandatory B2G e-invoicing from 1 January 2024 (central government) and 1 January 2025 (all public entities) using the PEPPOL network and EN 16931 semantic standard; all VAT returns, recapitulative (EC Sales) statements, and audit files must be filed electronically through FEDELTAX; no national e-invoice clearance API is imposed on private-sector invoices.

Zero-rating is strictly construed and the taxpayer bears the full burden of proof; unsupported or fraudulent claims trigger immediate VAT clawback, monthly arrears interest of 0.8% (Art. 25(2) General Tax Law), administrative fines up to 100% of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution under Art. 396 General Tax Law (imprisonment and fines up to EUR 250,000); the limitation period extends to 10 years in fraud cases; AEDT systematically cross-checks MRN status, VIES records, PEPPOL invoice data, and payment rail details against FEDELTAX declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg provides unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility for EUR and all foreign currencies pursuant to Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Requirements Regulation framework. Profit remittance is unrestricted; Luxembourg applies a 15% domestic dividend withholding tax, but treaty relief and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may reduce or eliminate it. Outbound interest and royalty payments enjoy no Luxembourg withholding tax, subject to anti-abuse rules and SAAR/GAAR scrutiny. No convertibility hurdles, no advance approvals, and no restrictions on foreign-currency bank accounts apply to a Luxembourg SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export receipts. SARLs may retain export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency accounts without repatriation or conversion requirements, consistent with EU free-movement-of-capital principles and Luxembourg's longstanding open-economy policy.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting in cooperation with the Luxembourg Financial Sector Supervisory Commission (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier – CSSF). No dedicated foreign exchange administration exists; Luxembourg maintains full capital-account liberalisation under EU Treaty obligations and eurozone membership, with no exchange-control approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for payment-rail modernisation, but strictly secondary to a full commercial bank. A Luxembourg or EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution under PSD2 can be used for multi-currency IBAN collection, currency conversion, and outbound disbursements to photographers and licensees. The EMI must be licensed in Luxembourg or passported under PSD2, maintain segregated client funds, and route settlement through a Tier-1 correspondent bank. EMI accounts are not acceptable for registered capital escrow, notarised capital contribution evidence, or statutory capital compliance because they cannot provide deposit-guarantee-backed operating accounts in the same manner as a credit institution. For a low-risk stock-photo digital platform, a compliant EMI such as Banking Circle or similar EU-licensed provider is viable as a secondary clearance rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The shareholder's capital injection must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT with the narrative 'Capital Increase LU_SARL' and a precise reference to the notarised subscription deed and RCS publication. For EUR, settlement occurs via Target2 through a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank IBAN beginning with 'LU' and representing a BCL participant bank. The bank reconciles the inbound payment with the corporate share register, and the ECB Balance of Payments statistical code 'Direct Investment other capital' (BPM6 DI framework) is applied at reporting. Verification is achieved by the credit's matching the notarised capital-increase resolution, the updated RCS share capital extract, and a bank-issued capital deposit certificate, which is required by the Luxembourg registrar when filing the capital increase.Low-value royalty distributions to stock-photo contributors are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer (settlement in T2, EUR-denominated) or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR accounts and for contributors in countries outside SEPA. High-value exclusive visual-asset purchases use documentary commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Luxembourg bank and payable at the seller's confirming bank. For payments to Stripe/PayPal treasury rails, Luxembourg banks use API-based payout files and FX conversion at the bank's wholesale rate, with settlement through correspondent banks and CLS for major FX pairs when netting exposure. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective rate for Luxembourg City (17% national corporate income tax plus 7% solidarity surcharge on the national CIT, yielding 18.19%, plus 6.75% municipal business tax). Rates vary slightly by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders. A 0% rate applies to a qualifying corporate shareholder under the Luxembourg Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation when the shareholder holds at least 10% of share capital or an acquisition price of EUR 1.2 million for an uninterrupted 12-month period. Non-resident portfolio shareholders not entitled to treaty relief remain subject to the 15% final withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the Form 880 filing deadline is 31 December of the year following the tax year. For a non-calendar financial year, filing is due within six months after the balance-sheet date. A mandate to a registered Luxembourg tax adviser extends the deadline to 30 April of the second year following the tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 250,000 in aggregate related-party transactions during a tax year: a formal Local File must be prepared and held available for the ACD; a Master File is required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. A mandatory transfer-pricing declaration in Form 880 is due for any related-party transaction exceeding EUR 100,000. The ACD may always request contemporaneous evidence even below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the Caribbean-style ESR, but the economic-substance doctrine operates through the general anti-abuse rule (article 6 LIR), EU anti-tax-avoidance directives, and administrative practice. An AI stock-photo SARL must demonstrate real presence and activity in Luxembourg: local strategic management, office premises, employee decision-making on IP exploitation, risk monitoring, and adequate payroll. Insufficient substance exposes the taxpayer to denial of the IP box, DTA relief, dividends-received exemption, and VAT recovery, as well as recharacterisation of royalty income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and false tax return

Statutory sanction: The ACD reassesses taxable income and applies late-payment interest at 0.6% per month, a 10% late-payment penalty, administrative fines up to EUR 25,000, and criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 or more. Active concealment can prosecute principals under Luxembourg tax-fraud criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or divert corporate assets may have the corporate veil pierced. Courts impose joint and several liability for unpaid tax and restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years in aggravated fraud cases.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and artificial profit stripping

Statutory sanction: The ACD can adjust taxable income to the arm's-length result, deny tax deductions, impose interest and a 10% surcharge, and apply administrative penalties for missing documentation up to EUR 25,000. Tax reassessment statutes generally run for five years but extend to ten years in cases of fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute artificial TP structures can be held personally liable for gross management fault and restitution. Civil courts may freeze personal assets and issue travel bans; criminal prosecution for tax evasion can result in imprisonment and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
Shell company structure and sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: The ACD applies the general anti-abuse rule and recharacterises transactions; the IP-box exemption, participation exemption, and treaty benefits are denied; tax assessments are reopened for previous years; penalties of up to EUR 12,500 plus interest apply. Compulsory liquidation may be sought for untrue declarations of seat and activity.

Director personal liability: Nominee and passive directors are exposed to piercing the corporate veil: joint liability for suppressed Luxembourg tax, disgorgement of management fees, travel bans, and asset seizure orders. Criminal courts may impose imprisonment for abuse of corporate vehicles in a cross-border tax arrangement.
Withholding-tax evasion on dividends and shareholder distributions

Statutory sanction: The ACD recovers unpaid withholding tax without a time bar in cases of fraud, plus monthly interest of 0.6%, a 10% late-payment surcharge, and penalties up to EUR 25,000. The Luxembourg tax claim becomes a priority claim over corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable as tax collectors for withheld but unremitted dividend tax. Courts may order personal civil restitution, property attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment for misappropriation of withheld amounts.
AML/KYC failures in cross-border payment rail operations

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML/CFT sanctions impose administrative penalties scaled to turnover, entity-level fines up to EUR 1,250,000, revocation of financial-sector authorisations where applicable, and imprisonment of up to five years for laundering funds derived from tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined and banned from management duties for up to five years. Civil liability extends to compensating aggrieved payers; travel bans and freezing orders may be imposed while criminal proceedings are pending.

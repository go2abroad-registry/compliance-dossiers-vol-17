Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration, with e-filing via the MyGuichet.lu portal (guichet.lu); indirect taxation is governed by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg VAT Authority.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities acquiring a Luxembourg presence must first obtain a statutory matricule (tax identification number) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) at incorporation or registration; this matricule serves as the TIN in relations with the ACD. For VAT purposes, an EU-established taxable person registers directly with the AEDT through the MyGuichet e-government portal, whereas a non-EU entity must appoint an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. A default-transparent SCSp does not file corporate income tax returns in its own name, but still needs a VAT identification number where it carries out an independent economic activity; partners report their allocable share on their own Luxembourg or foreign tax returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entities acquiring a Luxembourg presence must first obtain a statutory matricule (tax identification number) from the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) at incorporation or registration; this matricule serves as the TIN in relations with the ACD. For VAT purposes, an EU-established taxable person registers directly with the AEDT through the MyGuichet e-government portal, whereas a non-EU entity must appoint an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. A default-transparent SCSp does not file corporate income tax returns in its own name, but still needs a VAT identification number where it carries out an independent economic activity; partners report their allocable share on their own Luxembourg or foreign tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Article 175 LIR, the SCSp may irrevocably elect to be subject to corporate income tax as a resident corporation, thereby losing fiscal transparency. Hybrid mismatch consequences under the ATAD transposition (Luxembourg law of 21 December 2018) may additionally require the SCSp to be reclassified as transparent or opaque in order to eliminate mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (net of VAT) for taxable persons established in Luxembourg under the small-business VAT exemption scheme (Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Code); non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register from the first taxable supply made in Luxembourg, subject to the B2B reverse-charge and the OSS/IOSS special schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: the 0% export rate applies where the transfer of power to dispose of and the dispatch or transport of goods outside the European Union is evidenced, provided the exporter can prove departure from the territory of the Community within 90 days of the time of supply. For services: B2B supplies to a customer established outside Luxembourg or outside the EU are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the customer-location place-of-supply rule, so no zero-rating refinement is required; services directly linked to exported goods may qualify for exemption with credit. In an SCSp ESOP trust context, passive equity holding and plan administration are VAT-neutral where the employer-customer is a B2B recipient established outside Luxembourg; any Luxembourg domestic payroll-related administration services remain taxable at the standard rate unless a specific exemption applies.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / ENS) with the EX movement reference and proof of release from the EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and contract demonstrating the export supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank settlement evidence proving foreign-currency repatriation of the export consideration

Luxembourg does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), digital stamping, or pre-clearance APIs for B2B invoicing. E-invoicing is permitted under the European standard EN 16931; public-sector invoices flow through the Peppol network, and the MyGuichet/LuxTrust e-delivery infrastructure provides the authenticated digital channel. Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing will be phased in according to the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) roadmap, with target dates in the 2030–2035 horizon.

Luxembourg permits retrospective VAT assessment where export zero-rating documentation is defective: the VAT is clawed back at 17.0% with statutory default interest running from the original due date. Administrative fines under Articles 85–90 of the VAT Code can reach 100% of the VAT at stake; qualified tax fraud carries criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, and the limitation period for fraudulent filings extends to 10 years. AEDT audit practice scrutinises the export document trail, commercial terms, and the economic reality of FX repatriation; substantively unsupported zero-rating claims incur clawback without regard to good faith.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full capital- and current-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the Luxembourg statutory framework on financial relations with foreign countries. The euro is legal tender; no exchange-control or prior-authorisation regime applies to inward or outward capital movements. Profit remittance, capital repayment, and partner distributions of an SCSp are unrestricted for residents and non-residents alike, subject only to valid RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) registration and the bank's AML/KYC clearance. Because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, distributions are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at the entity level; Luxembourg-source income is assessed at the partner level under the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR), and a Luxembourg taxable establishment or permanent representative may trigger corporate income tax and municipal business tax exposure only if the SCSp is deemed to carry on a Luxembourg trade or business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Luxembourg. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, freely converted, or used for offshore settlement. The only obligation is statistical: resident entities must report cross-border payments to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes where aggregate flows with a single non-resident counterparty exceed EUR 12,500 per calendar month.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and under the oversight of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs (CSSF-regulated under the Law of 19 December 2016 on e-money institutions) may provide payment accounts but cannot originate commercial letters of credit or provide escrow-grade capital-call custody. Non-Luxembourg digital EMIs passporting under PSD2 (for example Wise Europe, Revolut Business, Velvet) are legally able to service an SCSp, yet most issuer risk departments reject a special-purpose ESOP trust with non-resident beneficiaries and an interposed corporate general partner. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as primary clearing and escrow institution, with a fintech EMI used only for sub-EUR 100,000 employee expense reimbursements or small payroll disbursements via SEPA Instant.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium feasibility. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs (CSSF-regulated under the Law of 19 December 2016 on e-money institutions) may provide payment accounts but cannot originate commercial letters of credit or provide escrow-grade capital-call custody. Non-Luxembourg digital EMIs passporting under PSD2 (for example Wise Europe, Revolut Business, Velvet) are legally able to service an SCSp, yet most issuer risk departments reject a special-purpose ESOP trust with non-resident beneficiaries and an interposed corporate general partner. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as primary clearing and escrow institution, with a fintech EMI used only for sub-EUR 100,000 employee expense reimbursements or small payroll disbursements via SEPA Instant.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17.00%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax rate is 15% (applicable to distributions made by Luxembourg capital companies). The SCSp, as a transparent partnership, is not legally a withholding agent for dividends; distributions or allocations by the SCSp to its partners are generally treated as partnership profit allocations rather than dividend distributions at the Luxembourg source level. However, where an SCSp is beneficially owned by a Luxembourg capital company that later distributes income to its shareholders, the standard 15% dividend withholding tax applies, reduced by the participation exemption in qualifying intra-group situations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by 30 June of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end. For fiscal years ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is due within six months after the close of the fiscal year. Extended deadlines may be granted upon written request to the ACD, but such extension is discretionary and limited in practice to 31 December of the filing year. Where the SCSp is fiscally transparent, each partner must include its allocable share of SCSp income in its own Luxembourg income tax return; non-resident corporate partners with a Luxembourg permanent establishment arising from the SCSp activity must file a separate Luxembourg branch tax return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg does not prescribe a fixed statutory monetary threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File preparation; the obligation follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the EU Transfer Pricing Directive (EU) 2022/2523. In practice, Luxembourg taxpayers engaged in cross-border related-party transactions or achieving revenues exceeding EUR 50 million, or intra-group services and financing exceeding EUR 10 million, are expected to keep contemporaneous documentation consistent with the ACD's documentation requirements. The Luxembourg tax authorities apply a risk-based approach and require the local file for controlled transactions above EUR 1 million in aggregate. An SCSp operating as an employee stock ownership trust must document its intercompany transactions (e.g., allocation of directors' fees, equity transfer pricing, management fees, and financing arrangements) at arm's length even in the absence of a statutory threshold.

Economic substance scope: Given the SCSp's character as a tax-transparent special limited partnership, economic substance requirements are not imposed by the Luxembourg Civil Code for the SCSp itself, but Luxembourg tax law and the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Inclusive Framework, as implemented through the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), require that any arrangement not be artificial. Since the target scope is an employee stock ownership trust (ESOP), the trust-like vehicle must avoid classification as a 'shell' under the EU's Directive (EU) 2016/1164 (ATAD) anti-abuse provisions; local substance is evidenced by the existence of a Luxembourg-registered service provider (domiciliation agent), a Luxembourg-based accountant and tax filer, the maintenance of minute books, the physical presence of a local board of managers (managing partner), and the performance of genuine decision-making functions. Where the SCSp is treated as a tax-transparent entity, the 'economic substance' assessment is conducted at the partner level; a partner that is a pure conduit in a low-tax jurisdiction without effective staff and activity will fail the Principal Purpose Test and may be denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 73 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), tax evasion carries a fine of up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 6 months (or one year for repeated offenses). Fraudulent claims for exemption or treaty relief trigger a 25% surcharge on evaded tax, plus late interest at 0.8% per month from the due date.

Director personal liability: The managing partner (gérant) of an SCSp may be held personally liable under Luxembourg civil law for the tax debts of the partnership if they act in flagrant disregard of corporate interests or use the partnership as a mere sham (simulation). Luxembourg courts allow 'piercing of the corporate veil' where the partnership is used to evade tax, resulting in joint and several liability for unpaid corporate tax and net wealth tax, plus damages for fiscal negligence. In egregious criminal cases, the managing partner may be subject to a personal arrest warrant, travel ban, or imprisonment, and the ACD may seize personal assets of the director to satisfy the partnership's liability.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg's abuse-of-law doctrine (article 6 of the General Tax Law) and the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI, the ACD will deny treaty benefits and recharacterize the structure as wholly taxable at the Luxembourg entity level, imposing corporate income tax at 17.00% (effective up to 24.94%), a 5% late-filing surcharge, and net wealth tax of 0.50%. A 'shell entity' status additionally triggers disqualification from the EU Interest and Royalties Directive and the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, exposing the full income to withholding taxes and interest penalties up to 25% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The gérant or corporate services provider that knowingly establishes a sham SCSp may be personally liable for all resulting tax arrears, penalties, and interest under the legal concept of 'faute détachable' (separate fault). Administrative seizure of assets and a commercial ban (interdiction de gérer) may be imposed for up to 10 years. Criminal sanctions for forgery of substance documentation include a fine of EUR 8,000 to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years (Ordre des avocats et des conseils; Luxembourg Criminal Code article 196).
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 on AML/FTF and the amended law of 27 January 2020, any professional of the financial sector or trust/company service provider (TCSP) that fails to conduct customer due diligence, or fails to report suspicious transactions, is subject to an administrative fine of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover (for legal persons), and the SCSp may be subject to a forced dissolution order by the Luxembourg Public Prosecutor's Office. Criminal penalties range from EUR 1,000 to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years, depending on the severity of the breach.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners of a TCSP or the SCSp itself who knowingly facilitate ML/FT activity face imprisonment of 5 to 10 years (Criminal Code article 506-3) and fines up to EUR 500,000. Personal travel bans and asset freezes may be ordered by the Luxembourg courts pending investigation. Liability is joint and several for the full amounts laundered through the SCSp, including disgorgement of profits, and the director may be permanently barred from serving as director in any Luxembourg entity (removal from the 21-day commercial register).
Failure to Maintain Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg's TP documentation penalty is governed by article 35 of the Luxembourg income tax law; failure to produce documentation upon ACD request triggers an estimated assessment at the ACD's discretion plus a monetary penalty of up to EUR 10,000, and a surcharge of 25% of the tax avoided where the TP adjustment is accepted. For SCSp structures, profit recharacterization may lead to full taxation of the trustee's fees and stock-option benefits at the corporate partner level.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify inaccurate TP documentation without due diligence are subject to civil restitution claims from the participating affiliates, joint liability for the corporate tax shortfall, and for severe intentional distortions (e.g., fake invoices from a shell service provider), criminal conviction for fraud and forgery under Luxembourg Criminal Code article 196 and article 210, with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 100,000.

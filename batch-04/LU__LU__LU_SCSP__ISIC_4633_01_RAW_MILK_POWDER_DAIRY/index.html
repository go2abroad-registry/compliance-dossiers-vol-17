Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) - Luxembourg indirect tax (VAT) authority; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - direct tax authority. Official portals: Guichet.lu (administrative portal), MyGuichet.lu (electronic filing and VAT e-services), and Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) for RNE/RCS registration.

E-government filing portal

1) Pre-requisite licences: obtain the commercial establishment authorisation under the Law of 2 September 2011 (autorisation d'établissement) and any food-sector registration with the Luxembourg Food Safety Administration (ALVA) for dairy product import/trading. 2) RNE registration: file the SCSp partnership agreement (no minimum capital) with the Luxembourg Business Registers and obtain the national business matricule number. 3) VAT registration: submit electronic form 640 (demande d'immatriculation a la TVA) to the AED via MyGuichet.lu using LuxTrust credentials; a VAT representative (représentant responsable) under Article 63 of the VAT Law must be appointed if the entity has no EU establishment. The SCSp, though lacking legal personality, is accepted as a distinct taxable person for VAT and obtains a VAT number in the format LU followed by 8 digits. 4) Direct tax: RNE registration automatically effects ACD identification; partners' tax residency and transparency statements are lodged as required. 5) E-filing activation: activate MyGuichet/eCDF for periodic VAT returns and EU recapitulative statements. Standard turnaround is 2 to 6 weeks from a complete application.

The SCSp, though lacking legal personality, is accepted as a distinct taxable person for VAT and obtains a VAT number in the format LU followed by 8 digits. 4) Direct tax: RNE registration automatically effects ACD identification; partners' tax residency and transparency statements are lodged as required. 5) E-filing activation: activate MyGuichet/eCDF for periodic VAT returns and EU recapitulative statements. Standard turnaround is 2 to 6 weeks from a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Luxembourg check-the-box exists for direct tax; the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default (Articles 175 to 184 LIR), with each partner taxed on its allocable share. Relevant elections and statutory triggers: (i) voluntary VAT identification below the EUR 35,000 small-business exemption (Article 57(2) VAT Law); (ii) Luxembourg VAT grouping election (assujetti unique, Article 60 VAT Law); (iii) US federal check-the-box election (IRC Section 7701, Form 8832) choosing corporate or transparent status for US participants, which may create hybrid outcomes; (iv) ATAD 2 reverse-hybrid reclassification (Law of 27 December 2019, as amended, effective 1 January 2022) whereby the SCSp may become subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax where participants' jurisdictions treat it as opaque; (v) hybrid mismatch disallowance and neutralisation rules applying to deductible payments under the same law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 in annual taxable turnover for the small-business exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); mandatory VAT identification once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, upon intra-EU acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000, or immediately when zero-rated exports are carried out and input VAT recovery is sought. Voluntary registration below the threshold is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of whole and skimmed milk powder to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 59 of the VAT Law, transposing Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC, provided the goods are dispatched to a destination outside the EU by the supplier or for its account and the export is evidenced by an EX-A customs declaration and proof of physical exit from the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-identified customers are exempt under the same framework subject to a valid customer VAT number and filing of the recapitulative statement (ESL). Related freight, loading, packing and export-handling services directly linked to the exported goods are also zero-rated; B2B services to non-EU principals fall outside Luxembourg VAT under Articles 57-58 place-of-supply rules. The SCSp must hold contemporaneous evidence of zero-rating before filing the VAT return, and must separately substantiate input VAT recovery on purchasing, warehousing and logistics costs.

  • EU Single Administrative Document (SAD) export declaration (EX-A) lodged at the competent customs office of export, with endorsement/control result confirming dispatch from EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR consignment note or IATA airway bill) evidencing physical exit of the milk powder consignment from the EU, matched to the commercial invoice, packing list and export declaration
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU buyer, supporting the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Luxembourg applies no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping by the AED. The 2023 e-invoicing reform (Law of 3 August 2023) mandates PEPPOL-based structured e-invoicing under EN 16931: receiving capability from 1 January 2026 and issuance for domestic Luxembourg B2B transactions from 1 January 2027; cross-border EU issuance will follow the ViDA timeline (2030/2035). Invoices must satisfy Article 42-44 VAT Law content requirements, serialisation is not state-controlled, and e-invoices are exchanged via PEPPOL access points without prior tax authority approval.

The AED may audit export zero-rating claims for up to 3 years after the chargeable period, extended to 5 years in fraud or serious irregularity cases. Unsubstantiated zero-rating results in retrospective VAT assessment at 16% plus statutory late-payment interest. Negligent or fraudulent claims attract fines under Articles 65-67 of the VAT Law and criminal referral (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 50,000), while invoicing and declaration breaches are fined under Article 61 of the VAT Law (from EUR 750 to EUR 25,000 per infraction). Following the 25 July 2021 anti-fraud reform, Article 68 of the VAT Law imposes joint and several VAT liability on any trader in the chain who knew or ought to have known of the fraud, and permits the AED to collect unpaid VAT directly from the contractual partner. Luxembourg imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day period reflects customary letter-of-credit and commodity contract terms and prudential tax documentation expectations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member state; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account purposes. Free movement of capital is guaranteed under Article 63 TFEU, and there is no exchange-control or prior-authorisation regime for non-resident partners. Profit remittance and dividend or distribution payments from a pass-through SCSp are unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and CSSF reporting duties; no convertibility certificates or central-bank approvals are required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no BCL collection account requirement. Euro-denominated and foreign-currency export proceeds may be freely retained, repatriated, or offset against trade payables; only statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL may apply above prescribed reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg; the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) acts as the competent banking and AML supervisor for Luxembourg credit institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (bridge-only). A Luxembourg-licensed or EU/EEA passporting electronic money institution (EMI) under PSD2 can hold a corporate account for an SCSp and route EUR traffic via TARGET2/SEPA, but such institutions generally do not provide documentary-letter-of-credit or commodity-trade settlement capabilities and impose custody limits on client funds. Unregulated or offshore non-EEA digital EMIs are not legally viable for Luxembourg corporate cash management: they do not comply with the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering as amended, cannot be counterparties for a Luxembourg bank's internal policy, and would trigger CSSF-regulated bank KYC rejection. A full-service regulated credit institution is required before the commodity trading pipeline becomes operational.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners execute a capital call, then remit subscription proceeds via SWIFT MT103 (or instant TARGET2/SEPA credit if EUR) to the SCSp's EUR or USD account at a Luxembourg Tier 1 bank. The SWIFT field 70/remittance information must reference the SCSp name, the capital-call reference, and the words 'SCSp capital contribution — no invoice'. No Luxembourg Ministerial authorisation, BCL foreign-exchange approval, or ECB prior notification is required. For statistical purposes, the receiving Luxembourg bank reports the flow to the BCL under the balance-of-payments code for direct investment equity capital (category 'Direkte Kapitalanlage / Equity Capital') when the threshold is met; the compliance file should retain the signed capital call, the contributing partner's board resolution or partnership resolution, the remittance receipt (MT103 confirmation), and the bank's foreign-transaction taxation/AML verification records.Primary clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103/TT wire transfers for open-account settlements to dairy suppliers and logistics providers; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued by the Luxembourg bank via SWIFT MT700, with amendments via MT707 and negotiation settlements via MT740/MT752; and (iii) TARGET2/SEPA euro payments for intra-EEA settlements. USD milk powder trade is settled via SWIFT in cleared USD or through a correspondent USD account; outbound disbursements must cite the relevant trade-invoice and conform to the bank's customer due-diligence refresh procedures.

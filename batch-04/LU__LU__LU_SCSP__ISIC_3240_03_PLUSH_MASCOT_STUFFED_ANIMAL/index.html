Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp. Capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement and must be subscribed as agreed; no escrow account is mandated by Luxembourg law.

Funding & unlock path: Occupational safety certification by the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (ITM) is required for embroidery machines and ultrasonic seaming equipment; fire safety clearance from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGI) is required for premises exceeding public-access thresholds; structural inspection certificates may be requested by the local commune for industrial floor loads.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital requirement for an SCSp. Capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement and must be subscribed as agreed; no escrow account is mandated by Luxembourg law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; official portals: guichet.lu, myguichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu, aedt.public.lu, and pbl.lu (Luxembourg Business Registers).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including an SCSp, obtains a Luxembourg VAT identification number by: (1) determining whether it is a taxable person under Luxembourg VAT law; (2) appointing a fiscal representative if not established in the EU and lacking an EU fixed establishment; (3) submitting the VAT registration application (Form 750) to the AEDT through MyGuichet or in person; (4) providing a foreign commercial register extract, proof of existence, evidence of the economic activity, and expected turnover figures; (5) receiving a 14-digit Luxembourg VAT identification number. For an SCSp with a Luxembourg establishment, the entity must first be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a national business number before completing VAT registration. For direct tax purposes, the fiscally transparent SCSp files partnership tax returns only if it has Luxembourg-source income or a Luxembourg permanent establishment; the partners, not the SCSp, bear income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity, including an SCSp, obtains a Luxembourg VAT identification number by: (1) determining whether it is a taxable person under Luxembourg VAT law; (2) appointing a fiscal representative if not established in the EU and lacking an EU fixed establishment; (3) submitting the VAT registration application (Form 750) to the AEDT through MyGuichet or in person; (4) providing a foreign commercial register extract, proof of existence, evidence of the economic activity, and expected turnover figures; (5) receiving a 14-digit Luxembourg VAT identification number. For an SCSp with a Luxembourg establishment, the entity must first be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a national business number before completing VAT registration. For direct tax purposes, the fiscally transparent SCSp files partnership tax returns only if it has Luxembourg-source income or a Luxembourg permanent establishment; the partners, not the SCSp, bear income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; statutory classification governs. An SCSp is by default fiscally transparent for corporate income tax purposes, with each partner taxed in its own capacity. No general hybrid-mismatch election exists under domestic law, but an advance tax ruling may be requested to confirm the SCSp's treatment for domestic and treaty purposes. The Luxembourg implementation of the EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules applies to cross-border structures. Municipal business tax may exceptionally arise at SCSp level where the SCSp has a Luxembourg permanent establishment and carries on a commercial activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises benefiting from the small-business exemption; non-established foreign entities have no threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT before making any taxable supply in Luxembourg unless an EU OSS/IOSS regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union may be zero-rated under Luxembourg VAT law where the supplier is a taxable person, the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or a third party acting on its behalf, and the exporter retains full documentary evidence of export within the statutory deadlines. Intangible services are zero-rated only where the place-of-supply rules place the transaction outside the Luxembourg VAT scope or where explicit zero-rating conditions for offshore services are met. Intra-Community supplies to another EU Member State are zero-rated provided the customer's valid EU VAT identification number is stated and the movement is documented.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/D.A./ENS electronic declaration accepted by Luxembourg Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of arrival/receipt in the destination country, such as a signed delivery note, customs clearance certificate from the importing country, or logistics tracking evidence
  • Any additional contemporaneous documents requested by the AEDT during an export zero-rating audit

Luxembourg does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, transaction control, or digital stamping system for B2B VAT. Electronic invoicing is permitted; business-to-government e-invoicing is required in Peppol-compliant format for certain public procurement contracts. Exporters must nonetheless retain electronic customs declarations, transport documents, and commercial records to substantiate zero-rating on audit; no automatic API clearance is required.

The AEDT rigorously audits export zero-rating claims and may deny the 0% rate where the documentary file is incomplete, contradictory, or fraudulent. Recovered output VAT is generally clawed back together with default interest at 0.8% per month. Late filing penalties of up to 10% of the net VAT due may be imposed, while intentional or repeated fraud can attract tax penalties of up to 100% of the evaded VAT and criminal sanctions. In practice, the tax authorities require export evidence to be retained for at least ten years and may apply retrospective assessments within the statutory limitation periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full liberalisation of capital movements under Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible and transferable without any FX approval, pre-registration, or conversion quota. The euro is the domestic currency, and the BCL maintains no foreign-exchange control regime. Profit remittance from the SCSp is legally unfettered: as a tax-transparent special limited partnership under the Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended), the SCSp is not subject to corporate income tax, and distributions to non-resident partners are characterised as partnership profit allocations rather than Luxembourg dividends, so no dividend-withholding gate, branch-repatriation tax, or exit tax arises. The only practical hurdles are (i) standard AML/KYC documentation, (ii) BCL balance-of-payments statistical filings for non-resident payments of EUR 12,500 or more, (iii) EU/UN sanctions-screening compliance, and (iv) contractual banking approval of the SCSp's GP signatory structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio in Luxembourg. A resident enterprise or SCSp may receive, retain, and resettle export proceeds indefinitely in any convertible currency (USD, CNY, EUR, etc.) without BCL approval. The sole binding requirements are: (i) balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for each cross-border receipt or payment of EUR 12,500 or more, usually submitted by the paying or receiving bank; (ii) five-year documentary retention of the underlying trade file (commercial invoices, HS/CN 9503.00.39 customs declarations, bills of lading, Incoterms 2020 documents); and (iii) compliance with EU economic sanctions and export-control lists where the counterparty or final destination is restricted.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), a member of the Eurosystem wholly integrated with the European Central Bank; AML/CFT supervision and financial-sector authorisation executed by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); UN/EU targeted financial sanctions enforced through a national consolidated list published by the Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for auxiliary payment corridors (supplier micro-payments, platform-pay receipts) and LOW as the sole treasury account. Under Luxembourg PSD2 transposition (Law of 21 December 2019), only a CSSF-licensed electronic money institution, a licensed payment institution, or an EEA-licensed institution validly passporting into Luxembourg may lawfully open e-money accounts. Offshore third-country EMIs without a European passport are prohibited from holding client funds for a Luxembourg entity; CSSF Circular 20/744 further requires mandatory client-asset segregation and disclosure of sub-agent arrangements. The structural blocker is the SCSp's lack of independent legal personality: e-money platforms typically require the account holder to be a legal entity and will demand a GP mandate, deed of partnership, and UBO register excerpt. Mainstream cross-border EMIs (Airwallex, MultiPass, Wise) may decline fund-like or non-corporate vehicles and cannot provide overdraft or liquidity facilities, so the primary capital escrow and trade settlement must reside with a tier-1 bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the SCSp entity level because the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is fiscally transparent (pass-through) under Luxembourg tax law; Luxembourg-source income and deductions flow through to the partners. A Luxembourg-resident corporate partner is taxed at the combined headline rate of 24.94% (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge equal to 7% of CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax for Luxembourg City). A non-resident corporate partner is taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment (PE) or Luxembourg real estate.

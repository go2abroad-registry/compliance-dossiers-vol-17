Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro Atoll MH 96960; document intake and filing administration are coordinated through International Registries, Inc. (IRI), the Republic's authorized corporate registry administrative agent.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act, 1990, as amended, codified as Title 20 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (20 MIRC Tit. 20); formation and governance authority derives principally from the incorporation chapter (20 MIRC Secs. 101 et seq.), the registered agent and registered office provisions (20 MIRC Secs. 201 et seq.), the shares and capital provisions (20 MIRC Secs. 301 et seq.), and the directors and officers provisions (20 MIRC Secs. 401 et seq.).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation filed with the Registrar of Corporations; the official formation document issued upon approval is the Certificate of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act.

Incorporation processing time

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) – Ministry of Finance, Treasury and Accounting, Revenue and Taxation Division (administering the Income Tax Act 1989, Title 40 of the RMI Code); corporate registry administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General. Official portal: www.rmigovernment.org; no fully integrated e-filing or e-registration platform exists – filings are paper or manually presented. Note: The artificially supplied alpha-3 code 'PNG' is a dataset artifact; the correct ISO-3166 alpha-3 code for the RMI is 'MHL'.

E-government filing portal

The RMI does not operate a general statutory TIN or VAT ID issuance regime. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is incorporated under the Business Corporations Act and is not registered for tax unless it derives gross income from sources within the RMI. A Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Business Act may be voluntarily obtained but is not required for a purely offshore re-invoicing BC with no local place of business. Where a U.S. bank needs an FTIN/EIN, the BC obtains a U.S. EIN via IRS Form SS-4. Only if the BC earns RMI-source gross revenue does the Revenue and Taxation Division assign a gross receipts tax account number. Consequently, for the standard non-resident MH_BC, no domestic tax ID is issued; a Certificate of Good Standing from the Registrar of Corporations is the operative identity document.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (territorial-scope entity-level taxation; no RMI-source revenue = 0% effective rate; no domestic pass-through election exists).

Available election forms

The Income Tax Act 1989 provides no domestic check-the-box, consolidation, fit, hybrid-mismatch, or deemed-tax election mechanism. There is also no statutory election to voluntarily submit to RMI gross revenue tax. The only available mechanism of consequence is the U.S. federal Form 8832 entity-classification election, which may classify the BC as a disregarded entity or a partnership for U.S. tax purposes, subject to hybrid and BEAT considerations. No RMI filing is required to perfect such an election, and the RMI Tax Treaty network (none) does not affect it.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None. The RMI has no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax statute. The only indirect-type levies are customs import tariffs under the Customs Tariff Act and specific excises on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel; these apply exclusively to goods physically entered for consumption in the RMI and cannot touch a pure offshore re-invoicing transaction.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no indirect-tax (VAT/GST) regime and therefore no economic turnover threshold exists for indirect-tax registration. For customs purposes, an importer registration applies to any person landing goods in the RMI without a monetary de minimis threshold, but the MH_BC offshore re-invoicing hub, whose goods/services never physically touch RMI territory, has zero registration obligation. The functional local currency is the U.S. dollar (USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory zero-rating is not applicable because no VAT/GST exists to zero-rate. The functional equivalent is territorial exclusion: under the Income Tax Act 1989, gross revenue tax reaches only gross income sourced within the Republic. Re-invoicing services rendered to non-residents entirely offshore, and goods that are never imported into RMI customs territory, are excluded from the tax base and attract 0% tax without any refund claim. Where physical cargo transits RMI ports solely in transit, it qualifies for the transshipment/in-transit exemption from import duty. Substantiation relies on the commercial file proving no RMI-sourced activity, not on a fiscal refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Form CED-1) – required only for physical exports leaving RMI ports; not triggered by pure offshore re-invoicing where goods never enter RMI
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill evidencing the underlying physical shipment and the re-invoicing corridor
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing settlement of the re-invoiced trade receivable
  • Transit Cargo Manifest (RMI Customs Form T-1) – required only where cargo is transshipped through Majuro or Ebeye ports in transit

None. The RMI imposes no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, tax-authority API approval, or SAF-T reporting mandate. RMI Customs operates on paper-based manifests and an advance shipping/environmental authorization channel; no fiscal telemetry applies to the MH_BC's invoices.

There is no VAT zero-rating clawback because no VAT is collected. The enforceable risk stack is instead: (i) recharacterization of income as RMI-source by the Revenue and Taxation Division, triggering the 3% gross revenue tax plus interest at 8% per annum and penalties up to 25% of the tax due under Section 124 of the Income Tax Act; (ii) customs duty evasion penalties under the Customs Tariff Act for undervaluation or false transit declarations, including forfeiture of goods, fines of up to double the duty evaded, and criminal liability for fraudulent documentation; (iii) loss of the tax-exempt characterization if the BC maintains a physical office, employees, or performs services inside the RMI, which would establish RMI-source nexus; and (iv) the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark, which is not an RMI statutory deadline but a lender/AML substance metric, is tested by auditors as part of economic-substance and trade-finance files.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US dollar is the sole legal tender, and there are no exchange controls or capital account restrictions; current and capital account transactions are fully convertible. Profit remittance is unrestricted subject to corporate good standing, tax clearance, and AML/CFT-compliant sourcing documentation. No mandatory conversion or repatriation approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender requirement exists; all trade receipts may be retained in USD, repatriated freely, or used offshore. The statutory timeline is zero days, and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Beneficiaries must, however, satisfy anti-money-laundering record-keeping duties under the Marshall Islands Banking Act and the Currency and Financial Transactions Reporting Act.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Republic of the Marshall Islands (administrative oversight under the Marshall Islands Banking Act, with the US Federal Reserve as the de facto monetary authority due to official dollarization; no domestic central bank exists)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for full treasury reliance: non-resident offshore digital EMIs are not subject to Marshall Islands banking regulation, lack deposit insurance or domestic branch presence, and face elevated AML/CFT scrutiny for re-invoicing hubs. They may be used only as auxiliary clearing channels for non-core low-value transfers, with all statutory records mirrored at a domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via correspondent US-dollar clearing networks (e.g., CHIPS or Fedwire) to the Marshall Islands bank's USD nostro account. The SWIFT MT103 field 50K/52D must name the ultimate transferring entity, field 59 must show the Beneficiary Business Corporation account, and the payment narrative (field 70) must include 'CAPITAL INJECTION – SHAREHOLDER SUBSCRIPTION' and a corporate resolution reference. The Marshall Islands account bank issues MT103 cover payments through its US correspondent; trade usance drafts are discounted only with a confirmed LC from a prime OECD bank. All settlements must reference the underlying commercial invoice, Incoterms, and re-invoicing margin. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None - the RMI has no transfer pricing documentation statute, no Local File or Master File threshold, no contemporaneous documentation obligation, no advance pricing agreement mechanism, and no country-by-country reporting requirement under domestic law; arm's-length allocation principles exist only as general source-allocation guidance under MIRC Title 36 and are not enforced against non-resident domestic corporations with nil RMI-source income

Economic substance scope: No domestic Economic Substance Regulations statute has been enacted for non-resident Business Corporations. However, the EU has classified the Marshall Islands as a non-cooperative jurisdiction for tax purposes (grey-listed and subsequently black-listed), and the OECD/G20 BEPS Inclusive Framework harmful tax practice peer review continues to demand substance reforms. In practice, an RMI BC engaged in general re-invoicing must maintain economic substance where the beneficial owner's jurisdiction applies CFC, controlled-foreign-company, or substance-over-form rules. Documentary substance protocol: physical office space and board minutes in the RMI or a recognized substance jurisdiction; decision-making exercised by directors with actual authority; local administrative staff; bank accounts opened in a reputable correspondent-banking jurisdiction; contracts, purchase orders, invoices, bills of lading, and treasury records retained for at least seven years; the company must not be a strato/domicile-only vehicle

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Re-Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under MIRC Title 36, willful evasion attracts a civil fraud penalty of 100% of the deficiency plus interest, a criminal fine of up to US$10,000, and imprisonment of up to five years; the RMI Registrar may dissolve the BC and revoke non-resident status; foreign jurisdictions may recharacterize the transaction, deny deduction of interposed margins, and impose 20-40% accuracy-related penalties on the taxpayer

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a pure instrumentality; directors who sign false returns or direct sham invoicing are personally assessed for the tax, jointly and severally; criminal conviction carries disqualification from serving as a director, asset seizure, extradition or travel bans, and imprisonment applicable both in the RMI and in the beneficial owner's jurisdiction
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: RMI Registrar administrative strike-off and dissolution for failure to maintain registered agent or annual filings; loss of limited liability for transactions after dissolution; foreign tax authorities may disregard the entity and reattribute all profits to the controlling shareholder; inclusion in EU and OECD adverse lists triggers mandatory disclosure and penalty regimes, enhanced audit risk, and refusal of correspondent banking services

Director personal liability: Directors who continue trading after strike-off are personally liable for debts of the dissolved company; nominee directors and beneficial owners may be subjected to travel restrictions, direct foreign tax assessments under look-through rules, criminal prosecution for money laundering in the country of effective management, and sequestration of personal assets
AML, KYC, and Unlicensed Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Violations of the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act (as amended 2018) carry fines of up to US$150,000 per violation, suspension or revocation of the entity's registration, mandatory disclosure to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit, and inclusion on FinCEN or FATF advisory lists with consequential banking exclusion; the RMI's offshore finance law may also impose criminal penalties on the regulated agent

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face criminal money-laundering charges with imprisonment of up to fifteen years, personal fines, asset freezing, civil forfeiture of bank accounts, and travel bans; repeated violations pierce the corporate veil, allowing creditors and regulators to reach personal assets; regulatory disqualification from managing any RMI company is automatic upon conviction

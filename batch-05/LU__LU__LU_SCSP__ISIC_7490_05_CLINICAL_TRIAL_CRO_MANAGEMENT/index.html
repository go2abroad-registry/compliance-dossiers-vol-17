Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, comprising the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) regime introduced by the Law of 12 July 2013; applicable provisions on partnership formation, liability of limited partners, and mandatory registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), together with the implementing Grand-Ducal regulations on the trade and companies register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration (demande d'immatriculation) filed electronically through the LBR e-Registration portal, accompanied by the executed partnership agreement (contrat de société / acte constitutif), appointment documents of the general partner (gérant), and the registered office or domiciliation certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement for an SCSp; the partnership agreement freely sets the subscribed capital, which may be zero or in kind, and any paid-in capital must simply be contributed per the deed. No capital deposit into a blocked escrow account is required under Luxembourg law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety approval from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) confirming fire extinguisher coverage, evacuation routes, and alarm systems; municipal building occupancy certificate from the competent commune verifying structural load-bearing safety, electrical safety, and fire compartmentation; occupational clearance from the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (ITM) under the Luxembourg Labour Code provisions on workers' safety and health, including first-aid equipment, ergonomic workstations, and, for any laboratory, biological safety containment review.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Inland Revenue (direct taxes) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) - indirect taxes and VAT. Official portals: guichet.lu (single administrative portal), the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) e-portal, MyGuichet/AED e-filing for VAT matters, and Luxembourg Customs (Administration des douanes et accises) for EU ECS export clearances.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Incorporate the SCSp before a Luxembourg notary and register with the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Societes) via guichet.lu; the RCS issues the sole legal-entity identifier (matricule, i.e., the Business Registration Number). STEP 2: The ACD assigns the national tax identification number (NIF, numéro d'identification) on registration or at the first tax filing; this number is used for all direct-tax filings and correspondence with the ACD. STEP 3: Register for VAT with the AED by submitting the electronic start-of-activity and VAT registration form through MyGuichet; the AED allocates a Luxembourg VAT number with the prefix 'LU' plus 8 digits. An SCSp, although civilly transparent, is itself a separate taxable person for VAT purposes when it carries on an economic activity; VAT grouping remains available where the conditions are met. STEP 4: If the CRO will physically export investigational medicinal products or trial supplies, obtain an EORI number (prefix 'LU') from Luxembourg Customs and enrol in the EU Export Control System (ECS).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. The SCSp, created by the Law of 12 July 2013, is a partnership without legal personality and is fiscally transparent: under the general transparency rule of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (Article 175 LIR), no entity-level corporate income tax is levied; each partner is taxed directly on its allocable share of the CRO's profits at its own corporate or personal tax rate, subject to specific net wealth tax considerations affecting the SCSp. No domestic election exists to convert the SCSp into an opaque corporation. For cross-border CRO groups, maintain consistent classification between Luxembourg and the residence states of the partners to avoid hybrid mismatches; the ATAD anti-hybrid rules (implemented by the Law of 21 December 2018) may disallow deductions if a foreign jurisdiction treats the SCSp as opaque. Accordingly, the partnership deed, the Luxembourg tax classification, and the arm's-length remuneration of the general partner should be documented and consistently applied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) under the small-enterprise exemption of Article 57 of the VAT Law, available only to businesses established in Luxembourg. Non-established foreign businesses have no exemption threshold and must register with the AED from the first taxable transaction; businesses established outside the EU generally must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative. The CRO's milestone fee pattern will almost always exceed the threshold, so mandatory Luxembourg VAT registration is the working assumption, together with reverse-charge or local VAT registration in host states where trial services are physically performed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (investigational medicinal products, biological specimens, medical devices, and trial supplies) to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% export rate under Article 49 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC) when the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU and the supplier holds proof of export satisfactory to the AED. CRO and clinical-trial management services (protocol design, data management, regulatory support, trial monitoring, and site-management fees) supplied to a non-EU business customer fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the general B2B place-of-supply rule (customer-location rule), producing an economically zero-rated outcome, provided the third-country establishment of the recipient and the business character of the supply are documented; services actually performed at an EU location may fall under different place-of-supply rules and must be analysed on a contract-by-contract basis. Intra-EU B2B supplies are subject to reverse charge in the customer's member state, requiring the client's valid EU VAT identification and recapitulative statement reporting.

  • Electronic export declaration (ECS) lodged with Luxembourg Customs and validated with the export status and supervising-office clearance
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note showing physical departure of the goods from the EU to the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the third-country consignee, the LU VAT number, and the underlying export contract
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank confirmation evidencing receipt of the export consideration from abroad (FX repatriation evidence)
  • Transport evidence: delivery notes, terminal receipts, carrier contracts, and proof of arrival in the destination country
  • Customer status evidence: valid EU VAT ID for intra-EU reverse charge, or for non-EU clients written confirmation of third-country business establishment, plus the EC Sales List where applicable

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mechanism. E-invoicing follows the EU Peppol standard; B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to the central public purchasing agency (SIM) through the national eFact/eFaktur platform. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, but VAT invoices must be issued within 15 days of the taxable event and must satisfy the statutory EU VAT-invoice requirements. Intra-EU B2B supplies are reported via recapitulative statements, periodic VAT returns (Form 1030) are filed electronically with the AED on a monthly or quarterly basis, and export clearances are lodged electronically in the EU ECS.

The AED may audit export evidence during the five-year statutory assessment period and retrospectively reclassify a zero-rated claim as taxable in Luxembourg if the documentation is missing, defective, or internally inconsistent; the VAT is then reassessed at the standard rate (16%) together with statutory late-payment interest. Administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 apply under the VAT Law for documentation or filing failures, and the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) imposes additional penalties and interest for negligence or wilful evasion, including personal liability of the SCSp's general partner and managers. Fabrication of export documents constitutes criminal tax fraud punishable by fines and imprisonment under Luxembourg criminal law, and goods that disappear within the EU may trigger joint and several liability under the customs-status rules. Services improperly excluded from Luxembourg VAT on inadequate B2B evidence are clawed back in the same manner, with penalties accruing daily. Luxembourg does not impose a separate statutory FX-repatriation deadline, but completing inward remittances within the 180-day reference window demonstrates commercial creditworthiness, supports the transfer-pricing file, and aligns with standard banking AML/KYC monitoring for the Clinical-Trial CRO payment rails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the Economic and Monetary Union; the euro is fully convertible on both capital and current account transactions. No exchange control authorization is required for foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, dividend distributions, interest payments, license fees, or profit repatriation. Capital transfers are unrestricted subject to EU sanctions regulations, anti-money laundering checks, and BCL statistical reporting thresholds. Profit remittance by an SCSp is taxed at the partner level and freely distributable without any approval, provided the general partner demonstrates beneficial ownership and compliance with substance requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or holding-period requirements. Exporters and service providers may retain receipts in any currency and are free to convert to EUR at market rates. The only administrative obligation is the statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for certain gross cross-border payment flows, typically captured by the banking counterparty via prescribed purpose codes; this is not a treasury constraint but a compliance-census exercise.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking, AML/CFT, and prudential supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions or payment institutions (e.g., fully CSSF-licensed EMIs) may provide IBAN accounts and multi-currency wallets, but they frequently restrict use to simple pass-through payments and rarely support SCSp capital escrow or clinical trial milestone segregation. Non-EEA digital EMIs operating without an EU passport cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a Luxembourg SCSp; any such onboarding would create regulatory and tax-nexus risk. For a clinical trial CRO management vehicle, a tier-1 commercial bank remains the defensive default; an EMI may be used only for low-value ancillary operational expenses and must have robust AML frameworks in place.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and debt capital injections into the SCSp from non-resident sponsors are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (for bank-to-bank credit) using the harmonized BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for Direct Investment – Equity Capital; where the injection is a shareholder loan, use the code for Intra-Group Lending/Financial Intercompany Loan. The bank will require the underlying legal documentation (capital commitment deed, subscription agreement, promissory contribution certificate) and will verify the source of funds against the UBO declarations. TARGET2 is used for EUR settlement; for non-EUR injections, the bank will apply the euro conversion at the prevailing market rate with full source tax / withholding tax screening. The SCSp must not receive crypto-asset transfers or unbanked bearer instruments; only cleared bank wires or SEPA direct credits from the accurately identified sponsor are acceptable.Treasury disbursements for clinical trial site payments, CRO subcontractors, laboratory fees, patient compensation, and insurance premiums are executed predominantly by SEPA Instant (for EUR within EU) and SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 CBPR+) for non-EUR and high-value transactions. All disbursement instructions must contain an actionable purpose code and the counterparty's valid bank identifier; KYC files for each beneficiary are pre-validated to prevent money-laundering red flags. For large milestones exceeding EUR 500,000, a documentary credit (standby/commercial LC via SWIFT MT700/MT760) is prudent to provide settlement certainty against clinical trial deliverables; invoices, shipment proof of diagnostic materials, and milestone completion certificates must be presented. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate in Luxembourg City (2025): 17% CIT + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (flat rate under LIR Art. 147-148; 0% effective for participating holdings >=10% held 12 months under Parent-Subsidiary Directive; no WHT on liquidation proceeds of a transparent SCSp because profits are allocated directly to partners)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg (EUR): Local File required where annual related-party non-financial transactions exceed EUR 100,000, or financial transactions exceed EUR 50,000,000; Master File required where group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue >= EUR 750,000,000 (Grand-Ducal Regulation of 26 September 2018 implemented Action 13).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but substance requirements are enforced through: (i) GAAR and the abuse-of-law doctrine (Art. 6 LGT) denying treaty/ATAD benefits to artificial arrangements; (ii) PE threshold analysis under Art. 13 LIR requiring premises, personnel, and entrepreneurial risk in Luxembourg; (iii) the proportionality test for treaty access and Luxembourg tax residence certificates; (iv) the ATAD CFC and interest-limitation rules; and (v) the EU Unshell directive agenda — the SCSp must maintain real office, qualified CRO staff, service contracts, board/partner meeting records, and decision-making documentation in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of undeclared profit plus 0.6% monthly late interest; administrative surcharge up to 10%; criminal fines up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years under the LGT criminal provisions.

Director personal liability: The gérant (general partner/director) is jointly and severally liable for unpaid withheld taxes and fraud penalties; criminal conviction triggers asset seizure, travel bans, and professional disqualification.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Substance Failure)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty/domestic benefits under abuse-of-law doctrine; retroactive reclassification of the SCSp as taxable under corporate tax (CIT + NWT); assessments extended to 10-year prescription; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 per false return.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false substance are personally liable for taxes, risk criminal prosecution for complicity in fraud, and may be prohibited from managing Luxembourg companies.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary profit adjustment, recharacterization risk (fee converted into deemed dividend subject to WHT), late interest monthly, and mandatory penalties up to EUR 25,000 for documentation default.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false TP policy documents incur personal criminal liability for aiding tax evasion; the ACD can pursue the directors of the Luxembourg payer for the unwithheld WHT.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Partner Distributions

Statutory sanction: The SCSp is liable for the unpaid WHT plus 0.6% monthly late interest and penalties up to 10% of the withheld amount; for intentional evasion, criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The gérant and resident managers are jointly and severally liable for the remittance of WHT; liability survives liquidation during the 10-year collection prescription.

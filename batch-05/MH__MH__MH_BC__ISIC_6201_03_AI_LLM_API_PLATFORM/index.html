Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Customs Division, Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); official portal: www.rmigovernment.org, with customs declarations processed electronically through UNCTAD ASYCUDA World.

E-government filing portal

SOP: (1) Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Business Corporations Act 1990 and obtain the RMI Registrar's Certificate of Incorporation; (2) expressly designate the entity as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation in its Articles of Incorporation; (3) if no business is transacted within the Marshall Islands, no RMI income-tax registration is required and no TIN or VAT ID is issued or necessary; (4) if RMI-source income later arises, file the Business/Taxpayer Registration Form with the Revenue and Customs Division, Ministry of Finance, to obtain a TIN; (5) obtain a Foreign Investment Business License only if any physical or revenue-generating activity occurs within RMI; (6) appoint and maintain a registered agent and registered office in Majuro for all statutory notices and service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident domestic Business Corporation conducting zero RMI business is exempt from Marshall Islands income tax; no capital gains tax, no dividend/interest/royalty withholding to non-residents, and no VAT/GST; any RMI-source income, if it arises, would be taxed on a territorial basis only).

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists. Under the Business Corporations Act 1990, the Articles may elect Non-Resident Domestic Corporation status, which operates as a self-executing statutory exemption from RMI income tax provided the corporation derives no RMI-source income and maintains no permanent establishment, office, or business activity within RMI. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable: the Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains no comprehensive double tax treaty network (only limited arrangements under the Compact of Free Association with the United States), and the Income Tax Act 1989 (as amended) contains no hybrid entity or hybrid instrument mismatch provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax). The only indirect tax is Import Tax (customs and excise duties under the Customs Act 2014), which applies to physical goods imported into RMI and has zero nexus to intangible AI/LLM API services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST regime exists; no economic-turnover threshold can trigger VAT registration. Import Tax registration is required only for physical importers of goods into RMI (no monetary threshold; Customs Act 2014).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

RMI has no VAT/GST, so no export zero-rating mechanism exists or is required. AI/LLM API services sold to foreign clients by a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation are outside the scope of RMI taxation when: (a) no RMI-source income is derived; (b) services are performed and consumed wholly outside RMI; (c) no fixed establishment, office, or agent in RMI concludes the contracts; and (d) proceeds are collected offshore in USD, USDC, or other cryptocurrency through foreign payment rails (e.g., Stripe). The export is presumptively tax-neutral; no refund application is necessary.

  • Customs Export Declaration (not applicable to digital AI API services; required only for physical goods, which this scope does not contemplate)
  • Digital Delivery Log: API usage telemetry and token-consumption ledger evidencing offshore service delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: Stripe settlement report or USDC/USD transaction-hash proof evidencing foreign-currency receipt from non-RMI clients

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping mandate exists in RMI. Customs declarations are cleared electronically through UNCTAD ASYCUDA World, but this applies exclusively to physical import/export consignments. Digital AI/LLM API token transactions and cross-border cloud payments are entirely outside any indirect-tax clearance mechanism.

Although no zero-rating claim exists to abuse, any false declaration to the Revenue and Customs Division or the Registrar (e.g., misrepresenting non-resident status to escape taxation, or misdeclaring the nature of services to avoid customs/import tax) triggers penalties under the Income Tax Act 1989 (as amended) and Customs Act 2014: monetary penalties up to 200% of the tax or duty evaded, administrative fines up to USD 10,000 per violation, retrospective reassessment and clawback extending six years from the date of the offending filing, and imprisonment of up to three years for fraudulent misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

USD is sole legal tender and functional currency. Both current account (API subscription fees, royalties, service income) and capital account (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation) are fully convertible; no exchange-control licenses, no prior approval, and no profit-remittance taxes apply to non-resident-owned Business Corporations in the Tax_Exempt_Zone. Practical hurdles arise only from counterparty bank KYC/OFAC screening and correspondent de-risking, not from statutory convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

RMI imposes no statutory surrender obligation: export proceeds (AI/LLM API token receipts) may be retained abroad or deposited in any licensed bank in full, with no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no dedicated export-proceeds account. Record-keeping only requires transaction-level AML/CFT trail retention under the Banking Act and APG-aligned regulations.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain an independent central bank; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Banking Commissioner, Division of Financial Services Regulation, Ministry of Finance, with de facto USD liquidity provision anchored by the U.S. Federal Reserve through the Compact of Free Association.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - The Marshall Islands Income Tax Act 1989 (30 MIRC Ch.1) is strictly territorial. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) with no income derived from sources within the Republic of the Marshall Islands is entirely outside the income tax net. The RMI imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no net-worth tax, and no branch profits tax on a non-resident BC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - The RMI Income Tax Act imposes no dividend withholding tax, and the Business Corporations Act imposes no tax on distributions. Dividends, share buybacks, capital reductions, and liquidating distributions may be paid free of RMI tax to shareholders of any residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Corporate Annual Statement and the matching annual government fee are due on or before September 30 of each calendar year; no extension is available. The fee is calculated from authorized share capital and is entirely profit-independent.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Fiscal Year End Declaration of the BC's fiscal year-end (typically December 31 for the AI/LLM API token platform). A non-resident BC should declare a fixed fiscal year-end and align it with the beneficial owners' residence-country tax periods to avoid mismatch audits. A delinquent Annual Statement accrues a surcharge of up to 50% of the unpaid annual government fee. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in local-law terms: the RMI has no transfer pricing legislation, no arm's-length statute, no Local File or Master File obligation, and no country-by-country reporting requirement for non-resident BCs. However, foreign parent jurisdictions (notably the United States) may independently recharacterize intercompany AI/LLM service fees under IRC Section 482. Prudent compliance requires maintaining OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation - an intercompany agreement, functional analysis, and contemporaneous pricing benchmark - whenever related-party API licensing or hosting fees are paid into or out of the platform.

Economic substance scope: No Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in the RMI; because the jurisdiction levies no income tax, there is no tax regime for an ESR to protect. Nonetheless, foreign anti-abuse doctrines (U.S. CFC/GILTI/Subpart F, EU ATAD, OECD BEPS) may disregard a substance-less BC even though no RMI penalty is triggered. The BC must retain an RMI-licensed registered agent, must not hold itself out as having RMI substance beyond a registered office, and must not claim RMI tax residence in any foreign filing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

US-Facing Tax Evasion and FATCA Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: RMI side: willful evasion of any assessable RMI-source tax is a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to $10,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. U.S. side: IRC Section 7201 criminal evasion carries a fine of up to $250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years per count; IRC Section 6663 imposes a 75% civil fraud penalty; accuracy penalties of 20-40% apply; FATCA false-certification penalties of $10,000 per form and automatic 30% U.S.-source withholding under Sections 1471/1472 apply to non-compliant payees.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false certifications or cause the omission are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; U.S. courts may impose travel bans, freeze personal assets by injunction, impose personal fines, and imprison the controlling director; a foreign director's U.S. visa and entry history become affirmative evidence of willfulness in prosecution.
RMI Domestic Tax and Business License Violation

Statutory sanction: An unlicensed local operation receives a tax assessment retroactive to the commencement of operations with statutory interest at 10% per annum; prosecution may result in a fine of up to $5,000 and imprisonment of up to 12 months; a false return triggers the full assessment plus a penalty of 50% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause unlicensed local trading are personally liable for the unpaid gross revenue tax; the corporate veil is pierced where the BC is used as a facade or alter ego, and directors face civil restitution, statutory disqualification orders, and exit prohibitions creating de facto travel bans within RMI jurisdiction.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Deficit (EU Listing and De-Risking Risk)

Statutory sanction: No RMI ESR penalty applies, but the extraterritorial consequences are equivalent: foreign courts may disregard the entity and impose entity-level penalties (U.S. 25% CFC foreign base company income recharacterization; EU ATAD anti-abuse assessments); payment infrastructure (Stripe, correspondent banks, and regulated crypto exchanges) will terminate the account for de-risking; the RMI Registrar may strike the company for unpaid fees, leaving executory contracts personal to the directors.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal exposure through foreign discovery orders and contempt sanctions, including travel bans; a U.S. or EU court may order veil-piercing so that shareholders and directors become personally liable for the entity's obligations; sanctions designations block international travel and freeze personal assets.
AML/CFT Offense and Digital-Asset Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Crimes Act 2013, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 20 years and a fine of up to $2,000,000 or twice the value of the laundered property, whichever is greater; the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze and forfeit assets; U.S. wire fraud (18 U.S.C. 1343) applies where Stripe or correspondent banks clear the transfers, adding penalties of up to $250,000 and 20 years of imprisonment per count.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability attaches personally to the directing mind and willful participant; sanctions include personal asset forfeiture, mandatory restitution, travel bans pending trial, and extradition exposure under existing mutual legal assistance channels; a conviction in the RMI or the United States permanently bars directors from banking, residency, and professional licensing.

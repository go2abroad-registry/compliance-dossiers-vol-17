Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory or paid-up capital applies to an SCSp. Contributions are freely fixed in the LPA and may be cash, in-kind or a mix; no notarial escrow or blocked capital account is required, except that a real-estate contribution must be executed before a notary and, if cash is contributed, payment is evidenced by bank account records of the SCSp.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection by the municipal fire service and the Service d'Incendie et de Secours is required for any office, studio or production space ordinarily open to employees or public visitors; structural-load clearance is recommended for studio equipment installations; a workplace-safety declaration under the Labour Code must be filed with the Labour and Mines Inspectorate before personnel are engaged.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) is the Luxembourg VAT and registration authority; income tax is administered by Administration des contributions directes (ACD). Official portals: guichet.lu / myguichet.lu, www.aedt.public.lu; VAT filing portal: eTVA/eCDF.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident investor establishing an SCSp must first file the SCSp partnership agreement and register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) if it engages in commercial activity, obtaining an RCS registration number; simultaneously, a Luxembourg national tax identification number (matricule) is issued by the ACD/RCS system. To become a VAT taxable person, file Form 564 (VAT registration application) with the AEDT through the eCDF portal or by letter, attaching the RCS extract, partnership deed, evidence of economic activity (TikTok/MCN contracts, bank details, GP identity), and, if the SCSp has no Luxembourg seat, the appointment of an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. The AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits, together with an ERP (registered exporter/taxpayer) number for the eTVA filing account. A non-EU entity without an establishment must appoint a fiscal representative unless it can use the OSS/import VAT mechanism.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident investor establishing an SCSp must first file the SCSp partnership agreement and register the SCSp with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) if it engages in commercial activity, obtaining an RCS registration number; simultaneously, a Luxembourg national tax identification number (matricule) is issued by the ACD/RCS system. To become a VAT taxable person, file Form 564 (VAT registration application) with the AEDT through the eCDF portal or by letter, attaching the RCS extract, partnership deed, evidence of economic activity (TikTok/MCN contracts, bank details, GP identity), and, if the SCSp has no Luxembourg seat, the appointment of an approved Luxembourg VAT representative. The AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits, together with an ERP (registered exporter/taxpayer) number for the eTVA filing account. A non-EU entity without an establishment must appoint a fiscal representative unless it can use the OSS/import VAT mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

There is no general check-the-box election under Luxembourg law; SCSp tax transparency for direct income tax purposes is mandatory and not elective. The SCSp may elect, for a specific commercial/regulated fund structure, to be organized under the RAIF Law or the UCI Law, which changes the regulatory but not the VAT direct treatment. Under ATAD, hybrid mismatch elections are restricted: the SCSp cannot be simultaneously transparent and opaque in different EU jurisdictions for Luxembourg tax purposes without adjustment. For US federal tax classification only, an SCSp may file Form 8832 to elect its classification as a partnership or disregarded entity; Form 8858 is then used for reporting the foreign partnership or disregarded entity. Luxembourg will generally respect the SCSp's pass-through status for Luxembourg income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the Luxembourg small-enterprise franchise/exemption; above that threshold Luxembourg-established suppliers must register for VAT. Non-established foreign suppliers have no minimum turnover threshold and are required to register in Luxembourg or use the OSS/reverse-charge mechanism for supplies where Luxembourg VAT is due, subject to place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU may be taxed at 0% VAT, provided the exporter holds valid transport/export documentation proving the goods left Luxembourg and the EU within the legal time limits. Services supplied by the Luxembourg MCN agency to a non-EU business customer such as TikTok or a foreign creator-operator are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT if the B2B place-of-supply test places the supply where the customer is established; no Luxembourg VAT is charged and no export zero-rating declaration is required, but evidence of the customer's non-EU status must be kept. Electronically supplied services such as digital creator management, advertising, licensing and monetisation-platform services supplied to non-EU B2B customers also qualify for outside-scope treatment; supplies to EU consumers may be reported through the OSS and taxed in the consumer's member state, subject to the EUR 10,000 Union threshold rules for cross-border digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD/export declaration lodged with Luxembourg or exit customs)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice / tax invoice containing export zero-rating statement and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 bank credit voucher proving the export proceeds are received and identifiable
  • Place-of-supply evidence for MCN agency services: signed contract, customer VAT number or non-EU residency proof, IP/licence evidence, and service delivery records

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping applies. Luxembourg permits e-invoicing for B2B with customer consent and requires suppliers to the public sector to issue compliant e-invoices through Peppol BIS (EU Directive 2014/55/EU). VAT returns and recapitulative statements (VIES) are filed electronically through eCDF/eTVA; continuous transaction controls, SAF-T filing, and real-time digital reporting are not currently mandatory, although they may be requested during audit.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating or outside-scope treatment if the export evidence trail is incomplete, contradictions exist between the customs declaration and invoice/payment documents, or the exporter fails to prove dispatch outside the EU. The clawback reassesses Luxembourg VAT at 17% plus statutory late interest and can extend to unlawful input VAT deduction reversals. Fraudulent submission of false export certificates or fictitious outward dispatches may trigger administrative fines generally up to EUR 25,000 per offence, criminal penalties under the Luxembourg Criminal Code and Customs and Excise Code including imprisonment, debarment from beneficial VAT regimes, and joint liability of the taxable person and any authorised representative. The regular VAT audit limitation period is five years, extending to ten years where fraud or deliberate omission is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Capital-account convertibility is unrestricted for EU cross-border flows and for third-country flows subject to EU sanctions, FATF counter-measures, and the general anti-abuse doctrine. Profit remittances and distributions from a Luxembourg SCSp are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax; non-resident partners relieve through treaty or domestic pass-through rules. The practical hurdles are documentary: banks demand RBE-controller statements, CRS self-certifications, and proof that the SCSp is not a de-facto regulated AIF. Current-account payments for creator commissions and advertising services are unrestricted, but the payor must screen all counterparties against EU/UN sanctions lists and report suspicious transactions to the CRF (Cellule de Renseignements Financiers) where required by the Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT, as amended.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no domiciliation-of-receipts requirement exist for export proceeds in Luxembourg. Receipts from non-resident TikTok/MCN contract counterparties may be kept freely in any currency. However, mandatory statistical reporting applies under BCL Regulation 2002/143 and BCL Circulars on external statistics: any direct-investment flow, including capital contributions from non-resident partners or intercompany treasury movements, must be declared to the BCL Centre de Déclarations on a per-transaction or aggregated monthly basis where the threshold (generally EUR 12,500 per transaction or per counterparty cumulative within the month) is met. Such declarations can be delegated to the account-holding bank; failure to file is an administrative offence. No special invoice collection or FX repatriation deadline exists for advertising/creator-management services.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) holds monetary authority and balance-of-payments statistical reporting competence within the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent banking regulator and AML/CFT supervisor, jointly with BCL for systemic payments oversight. Luxembourg maintains no foreign-exchange control institution nor capital-control decree; EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63–66 prevails.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Wise B2B, holding an EMI licence from the National Bank of Belgium passported via the EEA regime, can service a Luxembourg-registered SCSp in principle, but the SCSp's absence of legal personality creates onboarding friction: the EMI must complete a contractual-document review of the partnership agreement, RBE extract, and the General Partner's corporate KYC. Legal constraints: (i) the SCSp cannot contract in its own name where the contract is executed by the General Partner; (ii) most EMIs mandate one natural-person ultimate beneficial owner holding ≥25%; (iii) the commercial-payer exemption under PSD2 Article 3(b) shields the MCN agency from payment-institution licensing only if creator payouts fall strictly within its own commercial model, which a mass payout of thousands of creator commissions may challenge; outsourcing disbursements to a licensed PSP (Wise/Payoneer) preserves the exemption. High-volume USD creator payouts suggest pairing an EMI with a Tier-1 correspondent-capable bank for the SCSp's primary treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire routing for the initial capital contribution and subsequent shareholder loans: the remitting partner transmits via SWIFT MT103 from their home bank to the Luxembourg account at BGL BNP Paribas/BIL with the SCSp's full legal name, LEI (if available) and Luxembourg seat in field 57A and 59, and an ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code of EQUI for equity contribution or INTC for intercompany advances; the memo must separately cite the partnership agreement article and the RBE registration date. For EUR injections originating inside SEPA, a SEPA Credit Transfer with purpose code EQUI is acceptable. For USD injections, the MT103 must clear via the bank's USD clearing account, and the bank will request a source-of-funds declaration, the contribution resolution, and updated RBE. If the injection subsequently requires statistical filings, the account bank applies the BoP threshold rule (EUR 12,500) on behalf of the SCSp; otherwise a BCL direct-investment declaration through the Centre de Déclarations must be filed. Crediting to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account is recommended until the contribution is verified; conversion into EUR from USD is done via a spot contract to avoid a floating exposure.Primary clearing rail for creator commission and licence fee disbursements is SWIFT MT103 corporate wire using current-account funds; intra-EU/EEA creator payouts are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant (up to the EUR 100,000 per-transaction ceiling). Commercial Letters of Credit are inapplicable to the MCN/advertising services model and should not be opened; such LC usage would require presentation of transport documents contradicting the service-based export and would be treated as an AML/CFT anomaly. For mass cross-border micro-payouts (more than 1,000 low-value commissions), the bank should enable batch API host-to-host debiting of the SCSp account and disbursement via licensed PSP mass-payout rails (Wise B2B, Payoneer Payouts) which aggregate settlement to the beneficiary's local account in local currency, while the SCSp's core treasury remains in EUR; the PSP outsourcing agreement must define money segregatio, reconciliation intervals, and audit rights. Outbound payments must be screened against sanctions and payment purpose codes should use the appropriate commercial category (INTC for group payouts, CHAR or TRAD for creator commissions per counterparty agreement).Deliverable EUR/USD FX forwards (plain vanilla, tenor ≤ 12 months) matched against TikTok USD-settled revenue, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank's FX line with credit support annex.FX swaps (FX swap of principal/interest in EUR/USD) for managing short-dated liquidity between the cap-call date and payout runs.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD fixed-for-floating or fixed-for-fixed) for long-duration deflation of USD-denominated creator payout exposures.NDF contracts are permissible only if a specific creator-currency pair lacks a deliverable offshore market (e.g., certain Emerging-Asian restricted currencies); Luxembourg banks would usually trade them bilaterally with a recognised counterparty and they will be reported under EMIR; speculative NDFs are prohibited by internal treasury policy.No crypto-currency hedging or digital-asset swaps are permissible without a specific CSSF crypto-asset service-provider licence.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. Wise B2B, holding an EMI licence from the National Bank of Belgium passported via the EEA regime, can service a Luxembourg-registered SCSp in principle, but the SCSp's absence of legal personality creates onboarding friction: the EMI must complete a contractual-document review of the partnership agreement, RBE extract, and the General Partner's corporate KYC. Legal constraints: (i) the SCSp cannot contract in its own name where the contract is executed by the General Partner; (ii) most EMIs mandate one natural-person ultimate beneficial owner holding ≥25%; (iii) the commercial-payer exemption under PSD2 Article 3(b) shields the MCN agency from payment-institution licensing only if creator payouts fall strictly within its own commercial model, which a mass payout of thousands of creator commissions may challenge; outsourcing disbursements to a licensed PSP (Wise/Payoneer) preserves the exemption. High-volume USD creator payouts suggest pairing an EMI with a Tier-1 correspondent-capable bank for the SCSp's primary treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required when the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds €1,000,000 per fiscal year. Master File: required where the worldwide group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds €750,000,000. CbCR filing threshold: consolidated group revenue ≥ €750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not classified as an Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) jurisdiction. However, the Luxembourg Tax Administration applies substance-over-form under Art. 60a LIR (General Anti-Abuse Rule), ATAD 1/2, and administrative substance requirements for IP regimes. An SCSp must demonstrate substantive commercial activity in Luxembourg through local management decisions, physical office, qualified staff, local bank accounts, and effective operation of TikTok creator MCN agency services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg Tax Adaptation Law (Steueranpassungsgesetz) §§ 186 ff., tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to €125,000. Civil penalties may reach 100% of the evaded tax, plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.8%.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner who participate in tax fraud are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and interest. The Luxembourg tax administration may attach personal assets, apply for travel bans, and freeze bank accounts during criminal investigation.
Transfer Pricing Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment reallocates profit to Luxembourg; secondary penalties may be up to 50% of the adjustment; late-payment interest accrues at 0.8% per month; administrative fines up to €250,000 may be imposed for non-compliance with documentation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for gross negligence if they knowingly approved unbalanced intercompany pricing. Criminal liability arises only in cases of coordinated fraud, but civil claims for unpaid tax and interest can be pursued against the directors of the corporate general partner.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Art. 60a LIR denies tax advantages where the main or one of the main purposes is tax avoidance. The entity may be disregarded for tax purposes; a 20–30% penalty of the tax advantage may be imposed; forced deregistration from the Luxembourg trade register (RCS) can follow.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be held personally liable for the entity's tax debts if the entity is considered a sham. Travel bans may be ordered under Luxembourg administrative practice, and directors may be excluded from board positions in Luxembourg.
AML / KYC Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 8-1 and 8-2 of the amended Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF regulations, professional negligence is punishable with fines up to €1,250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Administrative fines may reach €1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who fail to implement AML/KYC controls face personal criminal liability, travel bans, and freezing of personal assets pending criminal proceedings. Reckless disregard can pierce the corporate veil, making directors personally liable for illicit fund flows.

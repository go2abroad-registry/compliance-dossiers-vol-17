Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance, Office of Revenue and Taxation, operating as Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs (MIRC), which administers customs duties, excise, and all revenue statutes through the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window portal; corporate registration is separately administered by the Registrar of Corporations under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade; employment-linked contributions are administered by the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned, non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (BCCA, 27 MIRC Ch. 4) has no inshore taxable nexus and is statutorily exempt from all national income, capital gains, withholding, franchise, and indirect taxes. Step 1: File Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; no national TIN is issued for income tax because no income tax exists. Step 2 (conditional): if importing physical goods, register for a Customs Client Code in ASYCUDA World before the first declaration. Step 3 (conditional): if hiring employees physically located in the RMI, register as an employer with MISSA and obtain an employer contribution number. Step 4 (conditional): if maintaining an office or staff in an urban local-government area, procure a municipal business license (e.g., Majuro Atoll Local Government). Step 5 (banking evidence): request a Certificate of Tax Exemption / Tax Status Letter from the Ministry of Finance confirming BCCA Section 107 exemption, for presentation to Stripe Billing, Mercury, and correspondent banks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, hybrid-mismatch, pass-through election, or entity-level taxation election exists in RMI domestic law; the Section 107 BCCA exemption is automatic, perpetual, and non-elective for a foreign-owned non-resident BC. The RMI has not enacted BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules and offers no OECD-style classification election. A BC may voluntarily assume contractual tax payments or make ex-gratia payments to the RMI Government, but this is a private contractual undertaking rather than a statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Import customs duty system under the Customs Act 1990 (27 MIRC Ch. 1), as amended; the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax and is not a member of any customs union.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0. No turnover-based indirect-tax threshold exists because no VAT/GST is levied. A Customs Client Code registration obligation arises at the first importation of commercial goods into the RMI, irrespective of value. Offshore export of DevOps tools, database monitoring, and API infrastructure services to foreign customers triggers no RMI indirect-tax registration. Municipal business license registration is triggered only by physical business presence within a local-government jurisdiction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT refund or input-credit mechanism because the RMI levies no VAT/GST on exports or on cross-border digital services. SaaS DevOps tools, database monitoring, and API monitoring infrastructure services exported to foreign customers fall outside the territorial scope of RMI taxation and generate no indirect-tax liability. For exports of physical goods from RMI territory, customs duty is not assessed where the exporter files a valid export declaration and complies with the Customs Act and the Shipping Act (27 MIRC Ch. 5); there is no export-refund pool, no carry-forward credit, and no withholding-recovery mechanism to substantiate.

  • ASYCUDA World electronic customs export declaration (E-Declaration form, CUS-EX)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (house and master copies, as applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting HS tariff classification, value, quantity, and consignee
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of foreign sale proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin or Certificate of Non-Manufacture where preferential tariff or transshipment treatment is claimed
  • RMI vessel or aircraft license data and cargo manifest when the export moves on an RMI-flagged carrier

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate exists. Within the customs environment, however, all import and export declarations must be filed electronically in MIRC's ASYCUDA World system, which generates a customs electronic assessment and cargo control number before goods release. No API-based fiscal clearance applies to Stripe Billing issuance of invoices or Mercury ACH payment flows; no fiscal value is required on commercial invoices for offshore software subscription services.

Since no VAT exists, zero-rating fraud and VAT clawback cannot arise; MIRC audit scrutiny focuses on customs under-valuation, tariff mis-description, forged export declarations, and unlicensed trading. Under the Customs Act, a false or materially misleading export/import declaration subjects the goods to seizure and forfeiture, retrospective recovery of duty up to 100% of the evaded amount, pecuniary penalties, and imprisonment for fraudulent declarants. False BCCA filings or misrepresentations concerning non-resident tax-exempt status may result in administrative dissolution, reinstatement penalties, and civil liability before the High Court. Falsification of remittance documentation is additionally prosecutable under the RMI Criminal Code, with asset-forfeiture remedies. All supporting export documentation must be retained for at least five years and produced to MIRC within 14 days of a written demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI has no domestic currency, no indigenous central bank, no exchange control statute, and no foreign-investment screening regime. The U.S. dollar is legal tender; current-account and capital-account transactions are fully and freely convertible and transferable without RMI approval. Profit, dividend, and liquidation remittances by a non-resident Business Corporation are entirely unrestricted and carry no RMI withholding tax, capital gains tax, or remittance quota. For ISIC 6201 developer-infrastructure, database, and API-monitoring cloud services, the business model is treated as tax-exempt international business. Practical frictions arise only from U.S. correspondent-bank OFAC screening, documentary CDD, and fintech account eligibility policies; no frictions arise from RMI currency convertibility. The absence of an onshore FX market means all hedging and conversion activity occurs offshore in U.S. or international banking centers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The RMI imposes no statutory surrender, forced conversion, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts or service revenues. MH_BC may retain 100% of its USD cloud-subscription proceeds in a U.S. fintech settlement account or an RMI-licensed bank without conversion into any local currency. The only compliance duties are ordinary tax-information reporting (for U.S.-source revenue streams collected through Stripe Billing), accurate sub-ledger recordkeeping available to the RMI registered agent, and adherence to the target payment rails' settlement terms. No RMI form, filing, or license is required to hold or disburse export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), acting as prudential regulator under the Banking Act 1995 (as amended); the U.S. Federal Reserve System serves as the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender; the RMI has been an IMF member since 1992 and has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for the designated treasury rails. Mercury is the recommended primary operating account provider for MH_BC: it is a U.S. fintech operating with FDIC-insured partner banks, offers native USD ACH and Fedwire clearing, fully remote onboarding, and practical acceptance of an RMI non-resident Business Corporation holding a U.S. EIN. Stripe Billing functions as the collection-side rail only and requires the Mercury or other settlement account for payout. Legal constraints: (i) the RMI Banking Act 1995 does not regulate foreign fintechs or EMIs that are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in the RMI, so no RMI banking license is triggered by using Mercury or Stripe; (ii) the U.S. corporate transparency beneficial-ownership reporting obligation applies if the entity registers to do business in any U.S. state, and the U.S. FinCEN expectation of a BOI report must be assessed; (iii) the fintechs own counterparty risk policy governs acceptance; (iv) FATCA/Form W-8BEN-E documentation is required for U.S. withholding; (v) independent EMIs such as Wise Business are rated Medium viability as supplementary FX conversion and low-value payout channels but should not be the sole clearing account because they lack a native U.S. ABA-routed corporate checking capability comparable to Mercury. No RMI-licensed digital EMI of competitive scale currently exists for USD clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must be routed as USD same-day Fedwire (domestic) or SWIFT MT103 (cross-border) directly to the designated Capital Escrow Account at Mercury, using the routing and account number certified in the Mercury onboarding package. The SWIFT Field 70 remittance information must state: CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION MH_BC PER BOARD RESOLUTION, and the ordering customer name must exactly match the contributing shareholder named in the capital call. For U.S.-originating contributions, use Fedwire with payment descriptor EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION and avoid ACH IAT because the ordering bank is outside the U.S. Prior to transfer, complete the treasury pre-notification CDD ticket attaching the capital call notice, board resolution, source-of-funds statements, and escrow designation memo; upon receipt, reconcile the incoming cover or MT103 payment confirmation within T+1 against the treasury ledger. Stripe Billing must never be used for capital injections, and the account mandate should prohibit crypto, gambling, or personal-consumption references. A U.S. EIN is required before the account is opened, and all wire instructions should be verified telephonically or by bank-approved two-channel confirmation to prevent payment diversion fraud.Trade settlement for MH_BC uses a tiered clearing architecture: (i) SWIFT MT103 for international vendor payments (cloud infrastructure, database software, API bandwidth, colocation providers), citing the underlying ISIC 6201 service contract and displaying invoice or purchase order numbers in Field 70, with correspondent charge instruction SHA to avoid beneficiary shortfall; (ii) Mercury ACH CCD or SEC for domestic U.S. vendor disbursements and operating costs; (iii) Fedwire for same-day strategic payments where vendor terms require collection by value date; and (iv) commercial letters of credit (documentary L/C) reserved for high-value hardware, server, or network equipment procurement where the vendor demands bank payment undertaking - although an L/C is not operationally efficient for recurring SaaS or DevOps charges. Stripe Billing manages the collection side, netting merchant processing fees before settling to the Mercury account; treasury should run a daily bank-sub-ledger reconciliation, sweep excess operational cash into the capital reserve balance band, and maintain an approved vendor payment matrix with dual-signatory approval above USD 25,000 per transaction.Outright deliverable foreign-exchange forwards (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, JPY/USD) executed with U.S. or international bank counterparties under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or an FX Global Code-adherent master agreement, used to hedge non-USD software subscription revenue streams without involvement of any RMI-regulated exchange.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible for currencies lacking deliverable USD liquidity (e.g., CNY, KRW, INR) because no RMI central-bank restriction applies to offshore NDF contracting; such contracts require an ISDA credit support annex and must be cash-settled in USD at maturity.Cross-currency swaps (USD versus EUR or GBP) for multi-year committed-use infrastructure contracts, included under a cross-currency interest-rate swap line with a U.S. international bank; the RMI has no derivatives licensing statute or mandatory clearing location, and execution is conditioned on a board-approved hedging policy and, where relevant, the U.S. CFTC end-user exception for commercial treasury risk.OTC foreign-exchange options (plain vanilla calls and puts) on the same currency pairs for downside protection on material undisbursed subscription balances, permitted only where the counterparty bank credit limit has been approved by the board and the option premium is expensed against treasury cost.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability for the designated treasury rails. Mercury is the recommended primary operating account provider for MH_BC: it is a U.S. fintech operating with FDIC-insured partner banks, offers native USD ACH and Fedwire clearing, fully remote onboarding, and practical acceptance of an RMI non-resident Business Corporation holding a U.S. EIN. Stripe Billing functions as the collection-side rail only and requires the Mercury or other settlement account for payout. Legal constraints: (i) the RMI Banking Act 1995 does not regulate foreign fintechs or EMIs that are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in the RMI, so no RMI banking license is triggered by using Mercury or Stripe; (ii) the U.S. corporate transparency beneficial-ownership reporting obligation applies if the entity registers to do business in any U.S. state, and the U.S. FinCEN expectation of a BOI report must be assessed; (iii) the fintechs own counterparty risk policy governs acceptance; (iv) FATCA/Form W-8BEN-E documentation is required for U.S. withholding; (v) independent EMIs such as Wise Business are rated Medium viability as supplementary FX conversion and low-value payout channels but should not be the sole clearing account because they lack a native U.S. ABA-routed corporate checking capability comparable to Mercury. No RMI-licensed digital EMI of competitive scale currently exists for USD clearing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no corporate income tax on a non-resident Business Corporation (a 'non-resident domestic corporation' under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act, BCCA). No profits tax, capital gains tax, gross receipts tax, or branch remittance tax is levied on income derived from outside the RMI.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an MH Business Corporation. Dividends may be declared and paid in any currency, typically USD, without ministerial approval, subject to the BCCA solvency rule that the corporation must remain able to pay its debts as they fall due and that total assets must exceed total liabilities after the distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI has no transfer-pricing statute, no arm's-length codification, no Local File or Master File regime, and no contemporaneous documentation threshold. Transactions are conventionally maintained in USD, and any intercompany pricing defense must be prepared for the foreign tax authority of the ultimate parent (e.g., US, UK, or Singapore) rather than for RMI compliance.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The RMI Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2021 and the Economic Substance Regulations 2021 require RMI-incorporated entities conducting a 'relevant activity' to file an annual Economic Substance Return and demonstrate adequate economic substance in the RMI. A developer-infrastructure, database, and API-monitoring cloud business (ISIC 6201) will typically be classified under 'Intellectual Property Business' where income is derived from licensing or exploitation of software code, algorithms, APIs, or databases, or under 'Distribution and Service Centre Business' where services are rendered to related parties. A non-resident BC with no RMI office, no RMI employees, and no board management in the RMI cannot satisfy the default substance test. The test is treated as satisfied, however, if the entity is tax resident and subject to full tax on its profits in a foreign jurisdiction and relies on the information-exchange route; otherwise the BC must demonstrate RMI premises, local employees, local operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities performed in the RMI. Non-compliance is reported to the OECD, attracts monetary penalties, and can lead to strike-off of the corporation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion in the Beneficial Owner's Home Jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalties of up to 75% of the tax underpayment in the US (IRC Section 6663) or corresponding EU/UK offshore penalties up to 200% of the tax; criminal tax evasion charges carrying imprisonment (up to 5 years per count in the US; up to 7 years in the UK); seizure of bank accounts at Mercury and Stripe-linked reserves through mutual legal assistance.

Director personal liability: The RMI corporate veil can be disregarded on sham or agency grounds, imposing joint and several personal liability on the controlling shareholder/director for the corporation's tax debts; foreign freezing orders and charging orders attach to the BC's assets; the director faces travel bans, arrest warrants, and extradition through Interpol Red Notices.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance Under RMI ESR

Statutory sanction: Failure to file the ESR return triggers a fixed penalty (commonly in the tens of thousands of USD) plus daily default penalties; providing false substance information attracts additional fines and director disqualification; the licensed registered agent must report the entity, and the Registrar may strike the corporation off the register, annulling its legal personality and contracts with cloud and banking providers.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face disqualification orders in the RMI and personal fines for false declarations; foreign courts are likely to pierce the veil and treat the BC as a nullity, making the beneficial owner personally liable for all trading debts and triggering asset-freeze orders against the owner's personal accounts.
Corporate Registry Default / Annual Report Omission

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Corporations revokes the certificate of incorporation and strikes the company from the RMI register; the corporation ceases to exist as a legal entity, contracts and subscription agreements become unenforceable in its name, and bank/payment-rail accounts are frozen or closed once the bank learns of dissolution; reinstatement requires full arrears, penalties, and a discretionary filing.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who act or contract on behalf of a dissolved corporation are, under BCCA-derived principles, jointly and severally personally liable for all obligations incurred after dissolution; the veil is pierced by operation of law; the controlling owner faces personal recovery suits from creditors and potential disqualification as a director for up to five years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: RMI AML regulations require the registered agent to terminate the relationship and report suspicious activity to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit; US FinCEN penalties can reach USD 250,000 (or twice the transaction value) per violation for willful failures; money-laundering convictions in the US and EU carry imprisonment of up to 20 years; payment processors may terminate merchant accounts and place the funds in extended holds.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories who submit false beneficial-ownership declarations face fines, disqualification, and criminal prosecution; the beneficial owner can be extradited for wire fraud and money-laundering; cross-border freezing orders attach to both corporate and personal assets, and travel bans are routinely imposed pending extradition proceedings.
Unlawful Distributions and Fraudulent Conveyance

Statutory sanction: Directors who consent to an unlawful distribution are jointly and severally liable to restore the corporation or compensate creditors; on insolvency, a liquidator can avoid transactions at an undervalue as fraudulent conveyances and claw back distributions for the statutory vulnerability period; foreign bankruptcy courts may recognize reciprocal RMI liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner as a shadow director is personally liable to restore the full amount of the unlawful distribution without limitation of liability; the corporate veil is pierced to recover traceable assets; fraudulent trading during insolvency can result in civil compensation, criminal sanctions, and personal travel restrictions pending enforcement of foreign judgment summonses.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance of the Republic of the Marshall Islands — Revenue and Taxation Division, Majuro; statutory basis: Title 50 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (Taxation) and Title 49 (Customs and Tariff); official guidance is issued through the Ministry of Finance and published in the Marshall Islands Gazette; no dedicated public e-filing or TIN web portal is in operation—tax services are administered on paper at the Revenue and Taxation Division.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation under the Title 51 Business Corporation Act by filing Articles of Incorporation, registered-agent consent, and director consents with the Registrar of Corporations (Majuro), and pay the filing fee. Step 2: If the BC will derive Marshall Islands-source gross revenue, register with the Revenue and Taxation Division within the statutory registration period by filing the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) registration application together with the business licence and proof of the registered agent and local premises; the Division then issues a GRT account number serving as the domestic tax ID. Step 3: A non-resident BC with no RMI-source activity is not assigned a TIN and files no national income or gross-revenue returns. Step 4: For the craft-beer cold-chain distribution scope, additionally obtain the alcoholic-beverage import/export licence from the competent Marshall Islands alcohol licensing board and, if a physical distribution point is established in Majuro or Ebeye, a business licence from the Business Licence Office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the non-resident Business Corporation is subject to entity-level taxation only on gross revenue derived from sources within the Marshall Islands (3% Gross Revenue Tax under Title 50 MIRC); all offshore craft-beer export sales, cold-chain logistics fees, and non-Marshall Islands distribution revenue are statutorily outside the scope of national taxation, conferring a de facto zero-tax foreign-source tax-exempt classification.

Available election forms

The RMI has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election statute. Available elections include: (i) a non-resident taxpayer status election for Gross Revenue Tax purposes, made by declaration and subject to audit confirmation that no RMI-source activity is conducted; (ii) an election of a non-calendar fiscal year for GRT accounting; (iii) a continuance/domestication election under the Business Corporation Act to migrate a foreign corporation into the RMI corporate registry; and (iv) for U.S. federal tax planning only, a U.S. Form 8832 entity-classification election may be filed with the IRS independently of the RMI tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax — the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax, goods-and-services tax, or general sales tax; the relevant indirect burdens are the 3% Gross Revenue Tax on RMI-source business receipts and customs/excise duties on imported alcoholic beverages under the Customs and Tariff Act (Title 49 MIRC).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 — no de minimis monetary threshold applies: registration with the Revenue and Taxation Division and filing of the Gross Revenue Tax are required from the first dollar of Marshall Islands-source gross revenue; import-duty registration applies from the first importation of beer, with specific excise duty assessed per volume unit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the RMI has no VAT, exports are outside the scope of tax rather than zero-rated. To exclude craft-beer export revenue from the 3% Gross Revenue Tax, the BC must demonstrate (i) transfer of control and title to the foreign buyer after the goods leave RMI customs territory; (ii) performance of distribution and cold-chain services outside the RMI; (iii) receipt of payment into a non-RMI bank account; and (iv) possession of a valid liquor export permit issued by the Marshall Islands alcohol licensing authority. Lawfully exported beer is free of Marshall Islands excise duty and incurs no national sales tax at the point of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Marshall Islands Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the cold-chain consignment
  • Commercial Invoice stating the full FOB export value and the ultimate foreign consignee
  • Packing List with keg count, batch number, and temperature logs
  • Certificate of Origin for the RMI-produced beer
  • Liquor Export Permit issued by the competent alcoholic-beverage licensing board
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT evidence of foreign-currency settlement
  • Marine cargo insurance certificate

No national e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or taxpayer-API mandate exists in the RMI; commercial invoicing remains paper-based. Customs export declarations for cold-chain beer consignments are filed with the Marshall Islands Customs Service, with electronic processing available through the customs cargo-import/export platform at the ports of Majuro and Ebeye; there is no government-operated invoice clearance gateway.

The Revenue and Taxation Division may audit export substantiation using a standard look-back period and a 180-day evidence window for foreign-currency settlement; if export documentation is deficient or a transaction is recharacterized as RMI-source domestic keg distribution, the full 3% Gross Revenue Tax is clawed back retroactively with statutory interest and delinquency penalties, and the beer becomes subject to Marshall Islands customs/excise duties upon deemed local consumption. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims, false customs declarations, or missing liquor permits expose the BC to civil penalties, shipment forfeiture, the amount of the duty plus penalty, suspension or revocation of the liquor export permit, and criminal fines; repeated violations may additionally trigger loss of non-resident tax-exempt classification and cancellation of the business licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI is an Article VIII member of the IMF (membership since 1992) and imposes no exchange controls on current payments, transfers, or capital transactions; profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted in law, and non-resident shareholders of a non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) face no RMI withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 (territorial taxation). The convertibility constraint is practical rather than regulatory: USD clearing relies on US correspondent banks, which enforce FATF/Patriot Act 311 due diligence on RMI-originated wires; therefore proof of beneficial ownership, export documentation, and purpose codes must accompany every remittance to avoid holds or rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion obligation exists: RMI has no administered FX market and USD is the functional currency. Export receipts (SWIFT MT103, local direct debit) may be retained offshore or in onshore USD accounts without time-bound surrender. The Banking Commissioner's trade record-keeping standard requires exporters to preserve underlying commercial documents (invoices, bills of lading, cold-chain temperature logs, customs entries) for at least five years to substantiate the underlying trade upon correspondent AML audit.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no domestic central bank. Supervisory authority: Banking Commissioner, Financial Institutions Supervision Division, Ministry of Finance (Majuro), acting under the Banking Act 1990 (as amended) and the Money Laundering and Prohibited Actions Act 2012 (as amended). Monetary policy and ultimate settlement currency follow the United States Federal Reserve System because the US Dollar (USD) is sole legal tender under Article IV, Section 1 of the RMI Constitution and the Compact of Free Association (2003/2004).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Legal constraints: (a) the RMI Banking Act 1990 s.7 reserves deposit-taking for licensed banks and no operational EMI/digital-bank licensing track has been established; (b) foreign EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer-class) uniformly reject RMI-domiciled entities because RMI lacks a central-bank MOU network, is outside SEPA, and triggers heightened US correspondent due diligence; (c) viable workaround: incorporation of a US-chartered operating SPV (e.g., Delaware LLC with US EIN) to host payment rails while MH_BC remains the contracting and warehousing entity, subject to substance review.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) on all income other than Marshall Islands-source income; the general RMI regime for a resident domestic corporation is a 3% gross revenue tax plus income tax on RMI-source income, both entirely excluded for an NRDC in good standing.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by an NRDC, regardless of the shareholder's residence; a dividend resolution may be passed without any RMI tax clearance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual report and annual government registration fee are due on each anniversary date of incorporation, with the report to be lodged within a 30-day window following the anniversary; a 3-month administrative grace period is tolerated before penalty accrual.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months after the end of the entity's financial year; a calendar-year craft-beer distribution group must therefore file by 31 December of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Nil in RMI statute: the RMI Income Tax Act contains no transfer-pricing documentation thresholds, no Local File or Master File obligation, and no advance pricing arrangement regime. nevertheless, for any NRDC whose related-party craft-beer purchases or distribution turnover meets or exceeds US$500,000, or whose parent operates under OECD Pillar Two in a high-tax jurisdiction, contemporaneous OECD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation is prudent to rebut foreign recharacterization and to substantiate the ESR income declaration.

Economic substance scope: Applicable in full. The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (enacted under the Business Corporations (Amendment) Act 2018 and the Non-Resident Domestic Corporations (Amendment) Act 2018) require a relevant entity carrying on a 'distribution and service centre business' — which includes the wholesale purchase and resale of craft beer from a related microbrewery — to satisfy an economic substance test in the RMI comprising (1) direction and management exercised in the RMI, (2) adequate employees, premises, and operating expenditure in the RMI, and (3) core income-generating activities physically performed in the RMI. The annual ESR return must be filed and the substance test self-assessed by the board; a purely passive shell without RMI presence automatically fails and incurs penalties, automatic information exchange, and strike-off risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Foreign Tax Evasion / Offshore Concealment

Statutory sanction: The RMI imposes no penalty because the foreign-source income is statutorily exempt; however, the residence jurisdiction may impose civil tax-fraud penalties of up to 200% of the understated tax, extend the limitation window, and compel disclosure through CRS automatic exchange and OECD EOIR requests served on the RMI Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false ownership declarations face civil restitution claims, freezing orders over Marshall Islands bank accounts, cross-border travel bans for non-cooperation, and extradition risk where criminal tax evasion is certified by the requesting state.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Economic Substance Regulations: US$10,000 first-offence penalty, US$20,000 continuing-default penalty, US$100 daily default fine, and administrative strike-off or deregistration; the adverse substance determination is automatically exchanged with foreign competent authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for ESR penalties arising from their deliberate act or omission; disqualification from managing any RMI-licensed entity for up to five years; civil liability to minority shareholders and creditors for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing framework and the RMI Criminal Code, money-laundering conviction carries custodial penalties of up to 30 years and fines of up to US$1,000,000, with mandatory forfeiture of tainted trade proceeds; the licensing authority may also suspend or revoke the distribution licence.

Director personal liability: Directors with actual knowledge or wilful blindness of trade-based laundering face individual criminal prosecution, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans pending trial, imprisonment upon conviction, and civil liability for damages suffered by deceived counterparties and creditors.
False Declarations and Director Fiduciary Breach

Statutory sanction: Knowingly false ESR filing is a criminal offence in the RMI carrying a fine of up to US$20,000 and 12 months imprisonment; false statements to the Registrar risk dissolution and cancellation of NRDC status; doing business without a foreign qualifying certificate exposes the entity to fines, void contracts, and loss of access to local courts in the foreign jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors are criminally and civilly liable with personal fines, restitution to the Registrar or injured third parties, disqualification orders, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 12 months; foreign courts may additionally impose personal liability for unremitted taxes and unpaid employee wage claims.

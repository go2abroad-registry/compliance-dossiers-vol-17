Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Division (Majuro); official tax administration portal operated by the Ministry of Finance. Customs and maritime revenue matters are handled by the RMI Customs Service and the RMI Maritime Administrator (Office of the Maritime Administrator, administered through International Registries, Inc.).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation (BC) under Title 39 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code with the RMI Registrar of Corporations, submitting Articles of Incorporation and paying the registration fee to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: Apply to the RMI Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Division for a Business License under the Business License Act (Title 22); the license number serves as the statutory fee/tax identifier for the non-resident BC. Step 3: If the BC will own or operate a Ro-Ro vessel, obtain a Certificate of Registry and Commercial Operating License from the RMI Maritime Administrator. Step 4: File a foreign-sales-corporation designation with the Revenue and Taxation Division to perfect the tax-exempt status; thereafter the BC files only annual license renewals and the corporate status/franking report, with no income tax return required for non-resident domestic corporations deriving no RMI-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Statutory elections available under MIRC Title 24 include: (i) designation as a foreign sales corporation, removing all foreign-source shipping income from RMI income tax; (ii) election to be treated as a resident domestic corporation, which would subject RMI-source earnings to the 3% branch/profit-remittance tax; and (iii) an elective hybrid-mismatch alignment for OECD BEPS Action 2 reporting. The default classification is a tax-transparent offshore entity with no RMI tax attributes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – No VAT, GST, or sales/use tax; the only indirect impositions are customs import duties on physical goods entering RMI and annual business license fees for commercial operations in Majuro and Ebeye.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. The indirect levy trigger is the physical importation of goods into RMI (customs duty) and the commencement of any commercial activity in RMI requiring a business license; offshore vessel operations with no RMI port calls are outside the scope of all RMI indirect taxes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Structurally inapplicable because RMI imposes no VAT/GST regime and therefore operates no zero-rating or refund mechanism. International ocean carriage of vehicles by Ro-Ro vessels is classified as offshore income and is exempt from RMI income tax even without an export declaration, provided the BC has filed the foreign-sales-corporation designation and the voyage neither originates nor calls at RMI ports. If an RMI port call occurs, the freight attributable to RMI-source legs remains exempt for a non-resident domestic corporation, but cabotage licences and RMI Customs clearance are required; no statutory FX repatriation deadline applies to RMI tax purposes, though the 180-day benchmark is retained as an internal treasury/ALM standard.

  • RMI Certificate of Registry and Certificate of Survey evidencing flag, ownership, and class status of the Ro-Ro vessel
  • Commercial Operating License issued by the RMI Maritime Administrator and, where applicable, the RMI Ministry of Finance business license for the operating entity
  • Original bills of lading, charter-party freight statements, and bank inward-remittance advices evidencing offshore freight earnings

Not applicable – RMI has no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based reporting mandate for tax purposes. Commercial documentation and customs declarations are filed on paper or standard electronic manifests through the RMI Customs Service and the Revenue and Taxation Division.

RMI has no VAT export-refund system, so zero-rating clawback penalties are moot. Enforcement focus rests on misuse of the tax-exempt foreign-sales-corporation status: retroactive assessment at the 3% branch-tax rate on any RMI-source freight earnings plus interest; penalties under Title 22 (Business License Act) of up to USD 10,000 and suspension or revocation of the BC's business license; criminal fines and disqualification under Title 24 for wilful tax evasion; and vessel deregistration by the RMI Maritime Administrator for false tonnage, unpaid registry fees, or unlicensed cabotage.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands has no exchange control statute, no investment commission, and no foreign exchange licensing regime. Both current-account transactions (trade, freight, charterhire, interest, dividends) and capital-account movements (equity injections, shareholder loans, liquidation distributions) are freely convertible at market rates through correspondent U.S. dollar clearing. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and subject only to standard bank-level documentary due diligence and, where applicable, taxation at zero percent under the tax-exempt status of non-resident Business Corporations. The complete absence of local sovereign FX intervention and the use of the U.S. dollar eliminate currency convertibility risk; residual risk resides in correspondent-bank sanctions and AML screening friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or prescribed repatriation percentages in Marshall Islands law. Earnings from deep-sea Ro-Ro transport services, voyage freight, demurrage, and auxiliary shipping income may be held in any currency, retained offshore, settled through on-island operational accounts, or applied to transaction costs without any obligation to deposit with, or surrender to, the Banking Commissioner or any government treasury account. Shipping entities are free to manage those proceeds as corporate treasury policy dictates, subject only to the AML/KYC requirements of their chosen banking counterparties.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains no central bank, establishes the U.S. dollar as the exclusive legal tender and domestic currency, and delegates monetary policy to the U.S. Federal Reserve. Financial institutions are supervised by the Banking Commissioner under the Banking Act 1989 (as amended), while the Marshall Islands Financial Services Commission and the Registrar of Business Corporations administer corporate licensing and registration. There is no Central Bank approval prerequisite for any FX transaction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. The Marshall Islands has no dedicated e-money institution or digital banking licensing framework within the Banking Act, and no offshore EMI holds a banking license or a recognized agent arrangement under the Banking Commissioner. A non-resident Business Corporation engaged in Ro-Ro shipping cannot rely on an unlicensed digital EMI as its principal treasury rail, and the Banking Commissioner would consider unauthorized payment intermediation a criminal regulatory infringement. Such EMIs may be used only for ancillary corporate expense handling in third-party jurisdictions provided the EMI is lawfully licensed where the company operates, never for the statutory capital escrow or core USD clearing function. The practical workaround is a hybrid model: a licensed Marshall Islands bank as the treasury anchor plus a niche offshore EMI approved by the bank for decentralized disbursements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Layered beneficial ownership with discretionary trusts, nominee officers, or historical bearer share issuance patterns

Root cause: Layered beneficial ownership with discretionary trusts, nominee officers, or historical bearer share issuance patterns

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the registered share register and directors register, full chain-of-title diagram from the registered agent to every natural-person beneficial owner, updated beneficial ownership register filed with the Registrar, apostilled trust instruments or nominee agreements, a no-issue statement from the Marshall Islands Registered Agent, and a legal opinion from resident counsel confirming the absence of bearer share instruments and compliance with the Business Corporations Act disclosure requirements.
High-volume wire traffic from freight and charter proceeds lacking transparent underlying voyage documents

Root cause: High-volume wire traffic from freight and charter proceeds lacking transparent underlying voyage documents

Defense evidence: Executed charter party forms (BIMCO-based), bills of lading, freight and demurrage invoices, port agent disbursement accounts, bunker delivery notes, and a line-item reconciliation map from the bank statement to each specific vessel and voyage number. For intercompany charges, provide current supporting contracts and a board-approved transfer pricing policy, if cross-border related-party supplies are present.
Large capital injection in non-base currency routed through intermediary jurisdictions without identifiable economic purpose

Root cause: Large capital injection in non-base currency routed through intermediary jurisdictions without identifiable economic purpose

Defense evidence: Supporting source-of-funds declaration, audited previous financial statements, subscribed share certificates and subscriber ledgers, signed loan notes or equity subscription agreements, ISDA/CSA FX hedge confirmations, and a professional cash-flow memorandum showing the treasury rationale for the routing corridor (e.g., liquidity concentration at Singapore/CBEST-clearing banks).
Sanctions and ship-tracking red flags on the vessel IMO number or ship manager address

Root cause: Sanctions and ship-tracking red flags on the vessel IMO number or ship manager address

Defense evidence: Current Marshall Islands Registry stateless vessel certificate, continuous synopsis record (CSR), an independent negative sanctions list screening report, certified vessel tracker history showing no calls to sanctioned ports or unflagged zones, and a compliance certificate from the security officer confirming alignment with the International Ship and Port Facility Security (ISPS) Code.
No recognized economic footprint in the Marshall Islands leading to shell/business-conduct recharacterization by correspondent banks

Root cause: No recognized economic footprint in the Marshall Islands leading to shell/business-conduct recharacterization by correspondent banks

Defense evidence: Duly appointed registered agent and registered office in Majuro, tax-exempt status grant under the Business Corporations Act, financial statements showing the substance of the shipping operation, commercial contracts showing general business management by the board from a recognized shipping hub, and a practical explanation of why the RMI platform (flag state neutrality, low tax, international shipping network) is both corporeal and business-driven.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan injections are cleared through USD payment rails, primarily SWIFT MT103 via the funding bank to the Marshall Islands account held at Bank of Marshall Islands or an overseas clearing bank. The remittance must carry a correct ISO 20022/purpose code, e.g., 'GICT' for loans, 'DACC' or 'DISP' for direct investment equity, and reference the company name, subscription details, or loan agreement number. No Marshall Islands exchange-control endorsement is required. For U.S. dollar Fedwire and CHIPS clearing, the sending institution must perform OFAC and Specially Designated Nationals screening, and the ultimate beneficiary will be verified against the onboarding KYC file. To prevent rejection, the onboarding dossier should pre-store the last four digits of the corresponding SWIFT account, international bank account number (IBAN if applicable), and the treasury team should include the RMI registered agent's address in the beneficiary line. The funds can be used for working capital, vessel acquisition, or capital improvements once the bank matches the purpose code to the underlying corporate resolution and KYC ownership statement.Outbound disbursements for vessel operations, bunker procurement, port calls, crew wages, service dues, and ship repair are settled through: (a) SWIFT MT103 for low-value operational payments directly to suppliers and agent accounts; (b) commercial documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) for high-value bunker supply, newbuilding installments, or major dry-dock invoices; and (c) bank-draft or CHIPS/Fedwire transfers for urgent disbursements where immediate credit is required. Because the Marshall Islands has no domestic electronic payment netting system, all outward USD flows are processed via overseas correspondent relationships. Trade payments to counterparties in the European Union may require the separation of bank charges under the IBAN/SEPA rules, but USD clearing remains the anchor. For settlements with Chinese/RMB-denominated suppliers, a USD-contracted LC with a Hong Kong advising bank is standard and avoids any need for on-island FX conversion. The treasury blueprint must instruct accounts payable to include in each outbound MT103 field 70 the invoice number, vessel IMO number, and definitive purpose code so that clearing banks can quickly pass OFAC filters and reduce RMA rejection risk.Outright deliverable forward FX contracts for USD/EUR, USD/JPY, USD/GBP, USD/SGD and USD/AUD with a licensed global bank or brokerNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on non-deliverable currencies (USD/CNY, USD/KRW, USD/TWD) executed through a prime broker under an ISDA Master AgreementCross-currency interest rate swaps for USD/EUR and USD/JPY on asset-backed vessel financing to manage debt-service volatilityPlain-vanilla long-dated FX options and zero-cost collars to hedge charter income and port-fee exposuresFX products governed by the FX Global Code and cleared where appropriate through CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) to mitigate settlement risk

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable; the Marshall Islands has no statutory transfer-pricing thresholds and does not mandate Master File or Local File preparation. Non-resident BCs must, however, maintain full and accurate accounting records sufficient to substantiate that their revenue is foreign-source and that no intercompany pricing inflates local-source income.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to 'relevant activities' such as financing, leasing, distribution, and service centers. International Ro-Ro vehicle shipping is not listed as a relevant activity. Nevertheless, a non-resident BC must file an annual Economic Substance Declaration with its Registered Agent confirming that no relevant activity is conducted in the Marshall Islands, and maintain records refuting any de facto local management and control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the 3% gross revenue tax retroactively on all Marshall Islands-sourced revenue, interest at 1% per month, and penalties of up to 150% of the assessed tax; flagrant non-compliance triggers strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; intentional under-reporting may result in travel bans and up to seven years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abusive Corporate Shielding

Statutory sanction: The High Court may order judicial dissolution, asset attachment, and a civil penalty of USD 5,000–50,000 under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil exposes directors and shareholders to civil restitution to creditors; the court may also prohibit a director from acting in a managerial capacity for five years.

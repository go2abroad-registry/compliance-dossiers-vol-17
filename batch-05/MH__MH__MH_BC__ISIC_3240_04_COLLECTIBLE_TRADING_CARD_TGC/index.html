Governing statute

Business Corporations Act of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Title 51, RMI Revised Code), principally Sections 29-33 (Articles of Incorporation), Section 34 (Certificate of Incorporation), Sections 41-44 (registered office and agent), and Part VIII (directors and officers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Treasury and Accounting of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Revenue and Taxation Division); no public e-filing portal exists and tax filings, registrations, and status certificates are submitted in paper form through the appointed RMI registered agent or directly at the Ministry in Majuro.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation is not issued a TIN or VAT registration number by the Marshall Islands. Step 1: Confirm that the entity is organized as a non-resident domestic corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act and has no local trade or business. Step 2: Obtain a Certificate of Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Status, together with a Certificate of Good Standing, from the RMI Registrar of Corporations. Step 3: If the entity later ceases to be tax-exempt by commencing business within the Marshall Islands, register for gross revenue tax with the Revenue and Taxation Division; no VAT or GST registration is possible because no such tax exists. Step 4: For physical shipments through RMI customs, obtain an importer/exporter code from RMI Customs at the port of entry; otherwise no tax registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Marshall Islands has no check-the-box, entity-classification election, or hybrid mismatch election. Tax treatment is fixed by the non-resident domestic corporation status under the RMI Business Corporations Act. An entity may elect to become a resident domestic corporation by amending its articles and thereby prospectively lose the tax-exempt zone treatment, but this is a corporate status election, not a separate tax election mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gross Revenue Tax and customs duties only; no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no VAT/GST threshold exists. A gross revenue tax registration obligation arises only when a person carries on a trade or business within the Marshall Islands; there is no materiality threshold for that obligation, but a non-resident domestic corporation's offshore sale of collectible trading cards does not create RMI taxable nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT or GST system, so no export zero-rating claim exists. Export sales of collectible trading cards are outside the scope of RMI gross revenue tax where the seller is a non-resident domestic corporation, title to goods passes outside the RMI, and proceeds are received outside the RMI. The entity must maintain its non-resident status and retain documentary proof of offshore destination, delivery, and payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration or re-export declaration, to the extent goods are physically cleared through RMI Customs
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment records evidencing offshore settlement of the export sale
  • Certificate of Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Status and Certificate of Good Standing

None. The Marshall Islands does not require mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based government reporting, or digital stamping. A non-resident tax-exempt Business Corporation may use ordinary commercial invoices; statutory record-keeping under the Business Corporations Act remains applicable.

Fraudulent claims of tax-exempt status, misdeclared exports, or false documentation may trigger reassessment of gross revenue tax, civil penalties generally up to 25% of the tax due plus monthly interest, customs seizure or forfeiture under RMI customs law, and criminal liability for forgery, false declaration, or fraud. The ordinary reassessment window is three years from the relevant due date, but no limitation period applies in cases of fraud or wilful misrepresentation. A certificate of exemption obtained by misrepresentation is void ab initio, and all avoided taxes, penalties, and interest become immediately payable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI maintains no exchange-control regime. Because the U.S. dollar is the domestic currency, both capital-account and current-account transactions are fully convertible, with no statutory exchange-approval permits, no restrictions on 100% non-resident equity ownership, and no withholding-type barriers on profit distributions or dividend remittances for a non-resident Business Corporation. The only practical frictions arise from the RMI's FATF grey-listing (as publicly reported since June 2024), which induces correspondent banks to apply enhanced due diligence to USD flows referencing RMI-licensed entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI statute imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no fixed conversion ratio for export receipts. All proceeds from collectible trading card sales are freely receivable, retainable, and disposable in USD, subject only to ordinary anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening by receiving financial institutions.

Central bank supervision: None exists. The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no central bank and no foreign-exchange administration; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, monetary policy is effectively set by the U.S. Federal Reserve, and banking licensing and prudential supervision are performed by the RMI Banking Commission under the Banking Act 2005 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with defined legal limits. The RMI has no licensing or authorization framework for electronic money institutions, so an offshore EMI (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Brex) may be used only where the EMI is duly licensed in its home jurisdiction (US, UK, HK, or SG) and accepts an RMI non-resident Business Corporation as a client; grey-list-driven enhanced due diligence makes acceptance uncertain and slower than average. The target payment rails, Stripe and Authorize.Net, are card-acquiring and processing networks rather than clearing banks, so a separate commercial bank or EMI account with USD settlement capability remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Because the RMI imposes no capital controls and maintains no RTGS system, capital injections are routed in USD via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's remitting bank through US clearing channels (Fedwire or CHIPS) into the BC's offshore or BOMI account. The payment narrative must state 'equity capital contribution under the RMI Business Corporations Act', and when the receiving or intermediary bank requires a standardized classification, the IMF BPM6 direct-investment equity category (equity instruments) is used. No RMI governmental approval, exchange permit, or registration is required; the receiving bank performs only UBO verification, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds checks before crediting.Trade disbursements run over three rails: (i) direct USD SWIFT MT103 wires for supplier invoices (specialty paper, holographic foil, inks, packaging) cleared through CHIPS or Fedwire; (ii) commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707, or standby MT760) issued by the account bank in favor of machinery vendors supplying security holographic cold-foil printing equipment, payable against presentation of transport and inspection documents; and (iii) merchant settlement from Stripe and Authorize.Net, which clears card receivables in USD into the BC's pooled settlement account, with treasury concentration rules to manage settlement timing gaps. The RMI has no domestic clearing house, so all liquidity ultimately clears through US correspondent banking networks and the global payment platforms of the participating banks.Deliverable outright FX forwards on major pairs (e.g., EUR/USD, GBP/USD, JPY/USD, AUD/USD)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currency exposures arising from Asian print and substrate suppliers and royalty flows (e.g., CNY, KRW, INR, IDR, PHP, TWD)FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps (XCS) to hedge USD-versus-EUR or USD-versus-JPY mismatches on machinery financing and long-term licensing obligations

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Marshall Islands has no statutory transfer pricing regime, no local file or master file duty, and no documentation threshold in U.S. dollars or any other currency.

Economic substance scope: Marshall Islands law does not codify an economic substance regulations (ESR) framework for plain trading card publishing corporations. The entity must still avoid a purely passive or sham structure in practice so that payment processors, banks, and foreign tax authorities respect the separate legal personality of the non-resident business corporation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Cross-Border Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Revocation of business authorization by the Registrar, monetary administrative penalties under applicable revenue enforcement law, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent documentary conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors deliberately participating in a sham profit-stripping scheme may face civil restitution of evaded fiscal claims, personal asset seizures, and imprisonment for fraud if the entity is used as a mere façade.
Shell Company Abuse Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution by the Marshall Islands Registrar; enabling orders under the RMI Business Corporations Act to lift limited liability protection.

Director personal liability: Directors who abdicate all authority may be jointly and severally liable for corporate obligations of collectible trading card ventures, face director-ban orders, and be subjected to travel restrictions during investigative proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties up to $25,000 per violation under the Marshall Islands Beneficial Ownership Regulations and the RMI Anti-Money Laundering framework; potential freezing of bank and escrow accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly enabling beneficial ownership concealment face direct criminal fines, personal civil liability to third-party creditors, and imprisonment for money-laundering offenses.
Payment Processor and Payment Rails Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Instant account termination, seizure of settlement reserves, contractual indemnification claims, and referral to law enforcement for wire fraud.

Director personal liability: Executive officers who approve falsified payment integration statements may be held personally liable for proceeds-derived damages, receive international travel bans in cross-border fraud inquiries, and face custodial criminal sentences.

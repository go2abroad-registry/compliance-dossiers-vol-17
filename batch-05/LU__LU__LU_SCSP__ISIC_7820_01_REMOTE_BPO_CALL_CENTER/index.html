Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), attached to the Luxembourg District Court (Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg).

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (the '1915 Law'), Articles 944-1 to 944-24 (SCSp regime introduced by the Law of 23 July 2016); Civil Code Articles 1832-1873 on partnership contracts; Law of 11 January 2019 on the Luxembourg Business Registers; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Agreement (contrat de société / association agreement) filed electronically with the RCS via the LBR e-Justice portal, followed by publication of the registration extract in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations); a nominal LBR administrative service charge applies in addition to publication.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory capital applies to an SCSp; limited partners' contributions are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and are payable as agreed therein; no legal escrow, blocked account, or paid-up capital certification is required for incorporation, although the LPA may contractually require a bank credit advice as a condition to admission.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (certificat de prévention incendie) issued by the competent municipal fire brigade for any Luxembourg premises; structural load and floor-safety inspection certificate by a licensed engineer for the operating office; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering workstation ergonomics, electrical safety under the Law of 17 June 1994 as amended, and emergency evacuation signage.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital applies to an SCSp; limited partners' contributions are freely fixed in the partnership agreement and are payable as agreed therein; no legal escrow, blocked account, or paid-up capital certification is required for incorporation, although the LPA may contractually require a bank credit advice as a condition to admission.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; single business portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

The SCSp must be registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a Luxembourg notary or authorized e-filing via MyGuichet.lu, obtaining a 13-digit national business number (matricule). For VAT purposes, the SCSp must apply to the AEDT via the eCDF / MyGuichet.lu portal, indicating whether it qualifies as a small undertaking below the EUR 35,000 turnover threshold or is required/electing to register for VAT to exercise input VAT deduction or to reflect intra-Community activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp must be registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a Luxembourg notary or authorized e-filing via MyGuichet.lu, obtaining a 13-digit national business number (matricule). For VAT purposes, the SCSp must apply to the AEDT via the eCDF / MyGuichet.lu portal, indicating whether it qualifies as a small undertaking below the EUR 35,000 turnover threshold or is required/electing to register for VAT to exercise input VAT deduction or to reflect intra-Community activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by law and is not subject to entity-level corporate income tax or net wealth tax; profits and losses pass through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. Hybrid mismatch adjustments may apply under EU ATAD and Luxembourg anti-abuse rules where another jurisdiction characterizes the SCSp as opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover (small-undertaking exemption threshold); registration is mandatory above this amount and may be required for intra-Community acquisitions or certain reverse-charge services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For remote BPO/call-center services supplied to non-EU business customers, the place of supply is deemed to be outside Luxembourg, yielding an effective 0% result. Eligibility requires documented proof of the customer's business status and non-EU establishment. For physical exports of goods, zero-rating is available when the goods leave the EU and all export formalities are completed with supporting proof of exit.

  • Customs export declaration (EX-A) confirming EU exit
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, air waybill or equivalent transport evidence
  • Commercial invoice and underlying service/export contract
  • Foreign buyer's VAT or business registration certificate / certificate of non-residence
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT evidence of overseas payment

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is permitted under EU Directive 2014/55/EU; B2G e-invoicing to public entities must use PEPPOL formats. Invoices must preserve integrity, authenticity and audit trail, with retention for at least 6 years.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating is recharacterized as taxable at the standard 17% VAT rate with default interest and administrative penalties of up to 200% of the evaded tax in cases of bad faith. Criminal sanctions may apply under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, including joint and several liability of managing partners, and the limitation period is extended to ten years in criminal tax fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a core member of the euro area; both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalised under EU law (TFEU Articles 63–66). There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on opening foreign currency accounts, and no approval requirements for remitting dividends, distributions, or return of capital to non-residents, subject only to general anti-money laundering and tax reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion timeline for export proceeds, including BPO service fees received from outside the EU. Currency proceeds may be held in EUR or freely converted, but banks will require supporting invoices and underlying contracts under CSSF AML/CFT transparency standards.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for credit institution and payment institution supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderately viable. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and fintech payment gateways such as Wise for Business can be used for quick B2B reconciliation and outbound multi-currency vendor payments. However, due to the SCSp's lack of separate legal personality and possible non-EU general partner, many EMIs impose enhanced due diligence and may restrict the account to pass-through transactions. An EMI does not replace a regulated Luxembourg credit institution for escrow, debt, or large-scale FX hedging purposes.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Corporate registry extract, certified passport, 6 months bank statements from the UBO's local bank, sworn affidavit explaining source of wealth, chain-of-funds documents for the initial capital injection, and a detailed ownership chart ending at natural persons.
SCSp without a Luxembourg-registered general partner or with opaque fund structure

Root cause: SCSp without a Luxembourg-registered general partner or with opaque fund structure

Defense evidence: Partnership agreement (unwritten but evidenced by signed deed), CSSF comment or legal opinion confirming no collective investment undertaking status, a written commitment by the general partner to provide annual financial statements and tax returns (Form 100/200), and a list of daily operational payment beneficiaries tied to the BPO contract.

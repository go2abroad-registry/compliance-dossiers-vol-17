Governing statute

Business Corporations Act of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, as amended, principally Articles 1 through 4; applicable sections include Articles 101 (Articles of Incorporation), 103 (Registered Office and Agent), 108 (First Directors), and 109 (Bylaws). The foreign investor and non-resident ownership provisions are contained in Articles 13 and 17, together with the RMI Business License Law.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a non-resident Business Corporation; the Articles of Incorporation must declare the authorized capital structure and par value. There is no escrow deposit requirement under the Business Corporations Act; capital contributions are governed by the board's share subscription contracts and may be deferred.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance Certificate from the Fire Safety Division, RMI Ministry of Interior; structural load and building use approval from the Ministry of Public Works for any bonded warehouse, transit shed, or cargo inspection area; occupational safety clearance from the RMI Occupational Safety and Health Office covering loading dock operations, customs seal verification, powered industrial truck use, and worker exposure to documentary chemical or fume hazards.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Corporate registration is administered by the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act (BICA); customs declarations are processed by the Marshall Islands Customs Service (MICS) through the ASYCUDA World national single-window portal.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident Business Corporations are exempt from Marshall Islands income tax under the Income Tax Act and BICA Section 51; accordingly, no domestic TIN is statutorily required for such entities. A foreign-owned BC obtains corporate legal personality and a Certificate of Good Standing solely from the Registrar of Corporations. If a TIN is requested by banks or counterparties for FATCA/CRS or interbank due diligence, the entity may apply voluntarily to the RTO by submitting (i) Certificate of Incorporation, (ii) registered agent certificate, and (iii) a notarised declaration of non-resident status; RTO processing is typically completed within 5-10 business days and does not create or imply any tax liability.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-Resident Domestic Corporation: the MH_BC Business Corporation, not conducting trade or business in the Republic and deriving no income from Marshall Islands sources, is excluded from income tax, social security contributions, and withholding tax; it is also outside any indirect tax regime (no such regime exists). The only recurring state obligations are annual BICA franchise/registration fees payable to the Registrar of Corporations and the registered agent fee. — Non-resident Business Corporations are exempt from Marshall Islands income tax under the Income Tax Act and BICA Section 51; accordingly, no domestic TIN is statutorily required for such entities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-Resident Domestic Corporation: the MH_BC Business Corporation, not conducting trade or business in the Republic and deriving no income from Marshall Islands sources, is excluded from income tax, social security contributions, and withholding tax; it is also outside any indirect tax regime (no such regime exists). The only recurring state obligations are annual BICA franchise/registration fees payable to the Registrar of Corporations and the registered agent fee.

Available election forms

No check-the-box, fiscal-transparency, or OECD hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Marshall Islands law. Entity classification is imposed by statute: a BC is a separate legal and taxable person; tax exemption is a legal consequence of non-resident status rather than an elective regime. The only practical options are structural, namely the maintenance of non-resident status (no domestic gross income and no permanent establishment in the Republic) and the designation of the registered office and agent. No CFC, hybrid-mismatch, or equivalent election forms apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST or general sales tax regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The only indirect taxes are customs import duties, import excise taxes, and administrative service fees levied at the border under the Customs Revenue Act and related customs regulations. Customs brokerage services rendered by a non-resident BC are not subject to any indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in the Republic. Customs registration obligations apply instead at a zero economic turnover threshold to importers, exporters, and customs brokers: any person performing customs clearance must hold a valid customs broker licence issued by the Marshall Islands Customs Service and must be registered in ASYCUDA World. This is a customs licensing requirement, not an indirect tax registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in a VAT sense: the Marshall Islands has no VAT system, and therefore there is no 0% export VAT rate and no refund mechanism. Under customs law, exports from the Republic are duty-free and free of export taxes provided the goods are physically removed from the territory. A licensed customs brokerage house must file a valid export declaration in ASYCUDA World and evidence departure (port release and bill of lading) to obtain formal export status. Because the MH_BC is non-resident and offshore, its brokerage services have no territorial nexus to any indirect tax at all.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export entry filed and customs-approved in the ASYCUDA World single window)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing physical export and the out-of-territory destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit confirmation) evidencing receipt of export consideration from abroad

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate applies in the Marshall Islands. However, all customs declarations must be filed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single window operated by MICS, with automated broker interface/ACH connectivity for declaration submission, payment of applicable duties, and receipt of electronic release notifications. Commercial e-invoicing is governed by contractual and AML/record-keeping standards rather than by a government fiscal clearance system.

The Marshall Islands Customs Service conducts post-clearance audits and documentation reviews (records generally retained for 5 years). Because no VAT refund mechanism exists, the principal clawback exposure is the revocation of duty-free export status on shipments whose export documentation is inadequate, with resultant assessment of penalty duties, interest, forfeiture of goods, and fines under the Customs Revenue Act. Fraudulent declarations additionally expose the licensed brokerage to licence suspension or revocation and criminal liability for wilful evasion. The 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is applied as the internal repatriation and documentation deadline; although the Republic imposes no exchange controls, failure to evidence receipt of export proceeds within that window may trigger enhanced AML/CFT review by RMI-licensed banks and adverse audit findings, but cannot trigger an indirect-tax clawback because no indirect tax is levied in the Republic.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Because the USD is the domestic legal tender, there is no separate local currency to convert, no exchange-control permit regime, no Central Bank approval for capital import or repatriation, and no restriction on profit remittance, shareholder loans, or liquidation proceeds. A non-resident Tax-Exempt Business Corporation that derives no RMI-source income is not subject to RMI income tax, the 3% gross receipts tax, or dividend withholding; statutory withholding (up to 15%) and the 3% gross receipts tax apply only to distributions sourced from RMI trade or business activity, which is avoided by a properly scoped customs brokerage fee structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. RMI law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts or service revenues. Customs brokerage and bonded-transit management fees may be retained in USD in any licensed RMI bank or offshore account without declaration to the Ministry of Finance, subject only to record-keeping obligations under the AML/CFT Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no independent central bank and no foreign exchange authority. Banking supervision is exercised by the Banking Commissioner acting through the Banking Commission under the Ministry of Finance, pursuant to the Banking Act 1991 and the Financial Institutions (AML/CFT) Regulations. Monetary denomination follows de jure adoption of the United States Dollar as the sole lawful currency under the Compact of Free Association, with USD cash management and clearing dependent on US Federal Reserve facilities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary account solution. The RMI has no domestic Electronic Money Institution licensing regime and no foreign EMI passporting framework. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot issue a local RMI account, provide an RMI routing number, or settle payments to the RMI Customs Service or port authorities. Such platforms are structurally viable only as a supplementary collection conduit for freight deposits or small client prepayments, with all funds subsequently swept by SWIFT MT103 into the licensed RMI bank account; legal constraints include source-of-funds latency, absence of local dispute jurisdiction, and the licensed bank's overriding obligation to look through to the underlying payer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

3% (RMI Gross Receipts Tax on RMI-source business revenue; 0% effective exemption for non-resident foreign-source income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a domestic corporation to residents or non-residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Report must be filed on or before the anniversary date of incorporation each year. A 60-day grace period is allowed before late fees accrue; failure to file for two consecutive years exposes the corporation to administrative dissolution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 — Transfer pricing documentation (local country file) is compulsory for a Marshall Islands tax-resident entity that engages in controlled transactions exceeding USD 1 million in aggregate gross value. For non-resident exempt corporations, no transfer pricing documentation is required because the entity is outside RMI tax jurisdiction; but if a local customs brokerage license is held and RMI-source revenue is earned, the license holder will be subject to the 3% gross receipts tax and must maintain contemporaneous pricing records evidencing arm's length charges for customs brokerage fees.

Economic substance scope: The Republic of the Marshall Islands has enacted the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) pursuant to the Organisation of Economic Co-operation and Development / EU requirements. A non-resident domestic corporation deriving foreign-source income alone is outside the ESR scope if its tax residency is foreign or if it has no RMI-source relevant income. However, a customs brokerage house exercising a RMI license will have a taxable RMI presence and must demonstrate sufficient employees, physical office, and locally incurred operating expenditure proportionate to the customs brokerage activity. Licensees must meet the 'management and control' and 'income generated' tests or face substantial fines, strike-off, and a mandatory referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Gross Receipts Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 50% of the tax evaded, interest at 1.5% per month, and a fine of up to $10,000 per false return. Criminal conviction for willful evasion may result in imprisonment for up to five years and a criminal fine of $30,000 per count, together with forfeiture of the proceeds of the brokerage.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or knowingly permit the filing of false declarations will be jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. The court may order the piercing of the corporate veil, travel bans, asset freezes, and disgorgement of personal benefits received from the evasion.
Shell Company Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR, an initial non-compliance penalty of USD 10,000, escalating to USD 20,000 for continuing default; the Registrar may strike the company off the register and notify the Tax and Revenue Office. The company's RMI banking license and customs brokerage authorizations can be suspended indefinitely.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil fines up to USD 50,000 and are disqualified from acting as directors of any RMI company for ten years. If the shell was used to launder customs duty collections, criminal liability for fraud and money laundering can attach, with imprisonment up to ten years.
Customs Brokerage License and AML Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to USD 100,000 per transaction plus suspension/revocation of the customs broker license. The RMI Financial Intelligence Unit can impose AML monetary penalties of USD 50,000 per episode for every failure to file a Suspicious Transaction Report, and the trade passport may be revoked. Repeat offences result in a permanent banishment of the company from the RMI customs clearing system.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under the Proceeds of Crime Act for failing to maintain effective AML controls. Their personal assets can be seized, they can be named in criminal prosecutions and subjected to a travel ban, and the corporate veil will be pierced to allow creditors and customs authorities to recover unpaid duties and fines directly from individual directors.
Misrepresentation in Annual Report / False Beneficial Ownership Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act, knowingly making a false entry in the Annual Report is punishable as perjury: a fine of up to USD 5,000 and imprisonment for up to three years. The Registrar shall also issue a dissolution order and strike the entity from the register after any verified false statement.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause delivery of a false Annual Report commit statutory perjury; personal criminal liability follows regardless of the company's dissolution. The directors can incur restitution for third parties who relied on the false public record, including customs authorities or the registered agent, with personal bank accounts and travel rights restricted until full restitution.

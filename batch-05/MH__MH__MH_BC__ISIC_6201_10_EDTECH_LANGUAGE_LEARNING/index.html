Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Administration (RMI RCA) / Division of Revenue and Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Majuro, Republic of the Marshall Islands. Official national e-services portal: https://rmigov.info; customs and excise declaration portal: https://www.rmicustoms.org.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File Articles of Incorporation with the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Majuro, electing status as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (BCA); no minimum capital is required and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation carrying the statutory MH registration number; this number functions as the corporate identifier / TIN-equivalent for the entity. Step 3: Appoint a registered agent and maintain a registered office in the Marshall Islands; file an Annual Report and pay the annual franchise/registration fee to the Registrar of Corporations. Step 4: No TIN or tax-registration application to the RMI Revenue and Customs Administration is required for a tax-exempt non-resident company; the entity is statutorily exempt from RMI income, gross revenue, stamp, franchise, and capital taxes. Step 5 (conditional): register with the RMI Customs Division for an importer code only if physical goods are imported into RMI, and obtain a Ministry of Finance Business License only if a physical place of business, retail, or trading activity is established within RMI. For pure offshore EdTech subscription activities (ISIC 6201 - Interactive Language Education and Automated Tutoring Software), Steps 1 through 3 are exhaustive; Steps 4 and 5 are not triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Marshall Islands does not adopt the United States check-the-box regime and does not administer OECD hybrid-mismatch or hybrid-payment elections. The operative statutory election is the entity-status election under the Business Corporations Act 1990: (i) election in the Articles of Incorporation to register as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation, which confers tax-exempt-zone treatment and prohibits carrying on domestic business with RMI resident persons; versus (ii) election to operate as a Domestic Corporation, which is subject to Title 18 RMI Code gross revenue tax on RMI-source activity. The election is made at incorporation, is evidenced by the Certificate issued by the Registrar of Corporations, and does not require annual re-election; the board may additionally adopt a bylaws election confirming the non-resident operational restriction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_GST_Sales_and_Use_Tax_Regime - the Marshall Islands levies no value-added, goods-and-services, or retail sales tax; the only indirect-type impositions are customs import duty on physical imports and, solely for domestic non-exempt entities, the gross revenue tax under Title 18 RMI Code.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not_Applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT or GST is imposed; the currency of account is the United States Dollar (USD). A customs importer code is required from the first physical import into RMI with no monetary de minimis threshold, and a Ministry of Finance Business License is required only where the entity maintains a physical presence or sells goods/services within RMI. Offshore digital export of EdTech language-learning subscriptions does not trigger any indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Formally not applicable: because RMI has no VAT/GST regime, cross-border EdTech subscription supplies do not bear any RMI indirect tax, and no zero-rating claim, certificate, or export-status approval is required. Digital exports (remote language tutoring platforms, automated learning assessment, subscription e-learning software) are extraterritorial to the Marshall Islands and remain outside the gross revenue tax base even for domestic corporations, which is assessed only on RMI-source business activity.

None. The Marshall Islands imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, SAF-T, digital stamping, or tax-authority API reporting on EdTech subscription revenue. Payments collected through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are outside any RMI fiscal reporting or clearance channel. Physical-goods imports are declared electronically to the RMI Customs Division under a declaration-based control model; export of digital services requires no fiscal clearance whatsoever.

Because no VAT is levied, there is no export zero-rating and no VAT refund/clawback-recovery procedure to enforce. The principal audit risks for the MH Business Corporation are: (1) loss of tax-exempt status if the RMI Revenue and Customs Administration determines that the entity carried on prohibited domestic business or prohibited resident activities, triggering retrospective revocation of the exemption and reassessment to gross revenue tax, interest, and penalties under Title 18 RMI Code; (2) false or incomplete declarations to the RMI Customs Division, which attract forfeiture, monetary penalties, and potential criminal sanctions under the Customs Acts; (3) failure to file annual reports or pay Registrar franchise fees, which results in administrative dissolution and reinstatement penalties; and (4) the 180-day repatriation deadline is not a statutory RMI exchange-control rule because the US Dollar is legal tender and RMI maintains no foreign-exchange controls; the deadline operates as a covenant in underlying subscription-financing agreements and is enforceable contractually, not through tax clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands uses the U.S. dollar as domestic legal tender and maintains no independent exchange-control regime. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no statutory obstacles to profit remittance, dividends, or repatriation of contributed capital. The practical friction is limited to anti-money-laundering and OFAC sanctions screening by correspondent banks in the U.S. clearing system. A non-resident-owned Business Corporation must maintain good standing with the Registrar and file annual reports; however, no exchange-control license or central-bank approval is required for remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules exist. Because USD is the domestic currency, export receipts are not subject to mandatory conversion, nor is there a statutory timeline for remittance or surrender to a designated account. Exporters are free to retain proceeds in any USD-denominated account; the only obligations are bank record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting under the Republic of the Marshall Islands Banking Act and anti-money-laundering regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (no central bank exists; U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. The RMI has no distinct Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licensing category. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognised as banks or licensed payment institutions; they cannot accept deposits or provide settlement accounts within RMI without a banking licence. They may be used only as ancillary payment cards or e-wallet interfaces, provided the underlying settlement account is held at an RMI-licensed bank and all transactions are channelled through that bank. OFAC and correspondent-bank restrictions make purely offshore EMI onboarding unreliable for corporate treasury.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No RMI-stipulated monetary threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File. Because non-resident domestic corporations are taxed at 0%, the RMI transfer pricing regime is limited to the arm’s length standard for RMI-source income; a practical threshold of USD 1,000,000 in intercompany controlled transactions is recommended for contemporaneous documentation.

Economic substance scope: Yes. The Economic Substance Regulations 2020 apply. An EdTech language-learning entity holding or exploiting IP (including software rights and an online tutoring platform) conducts a relevant activity (IP business). It must maintain an RMI economic substance profile: board meetings with a quorum in RMI, principal direction and management in RMI, full-time employees and physical premises adequate to the IP activity, core income-generating activities in RMI, and an annual ESR notification filing. A pure holding activity may rely on a reduced substance requirement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of gross revenue at 3% of RMI-source receipts; fraud penalty of 100%; interest at 0.75% per month; a fine not exceeding USD 50,000 or twice the evaded tax; imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under common law fraudulent purpose; directors may be jointly and severally liable for tax, penalties, civil restitution, travel bans, and a term of imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR annual penalty of USD 10,000 to USD 20,000 for the first year; USD 50,000 for a continuing default; the Registrar may strike off the company and the non-resident tax exemption may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false ESR declarations face fines, disqualification from serving as directors, and travel bans; restitution may be ordered for any foreign tax advantages obtained.
AML / CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 20 years for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2003; corporate assets may be restrained and frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal liability, civil forfeiture, and travel bans; refusal to repatriate illicit funds may lead to extradition or an Interpol red notice.
Fraudulent Transfer / Transfer Pricing Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the royalty as a dividend, disallow a deduction, apply withholding taxes and penalties typically in the range of 20% to 40%; RMI provides no shelter from such foreign ruling.

Director personal liability: Directors of the beneficiary entity may be ordered to provide civil restitution to creditors, be disqualified, and, if fraudulent intent is shown, imprisoned up to 7 years with travel bans.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; no dedicated electronic filing portal for non-resident business corporations, statutory filings are lodged through registered agents; official government portal: https://www.rmiembassyus.org

E-government filing portal

A non-resident business corporation obtains its statutory corporate registration number from the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, upon incorporation under the Business Corporations Act. No separate TIN or VAT ID is issued by the Revenue and Taxation Office because the entity is outside the Marshall Islands income tax net unless it engages in local business activity. If a local gross receipts tax filing becomes necessary, the registered agent applies to the RTO for a taxpayer identification number using form TIN-01; no social security number or employer identification equivalent is required for pure non-resident status.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident business corporation obtains its statutory corporate registration number from the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, upon incorporation under the Business Corporations Act. No separate TIN or VAT ID is issued by the Revenue and Taxation Office because the entity is outside the Marshall Islands income tax net unless it engages in local business activity. If a local gross receipts tax filing becomes necessary, the registered agent applies to the RTO for a taxpayer identification number using form TIN-01; no social security number or employer identification equivalent is required for pure non-resident status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

A corporation may elect to be a Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation under the Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporations Act 1990, which effects tax-exempt status for foreign-source income, no withholding taxes, and no capital gains tax. No check-the-box election exists under Marshall Islands law, and hybrid mismatch elections are not recognised. The non-resident election is irrevocable except upon conversion to a resident domestic corporation, which triggers standard gross receipts tax exposure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use-tax regime); the only contextual indirect taxes are customs import duty and excise taxes under the Customs and Excise Act 1993, applicable solely to goods imported into the Marshall Islands.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST; no turnover threshold exists because no such tax is imposed. A gross receipts tax registration obligation arises only when a corporation is engaged in trade or business within the Marshall Islands, with a zero-dollar threshold, i.e., any local taxable business activity triggers registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – the Marshall Islands does not impose VAT or GST, so no 0% export VAT rate can be claimed and no VAT refund mechanism exists. Sales of goods or services by a non-resident domestic business corporation derive from foreign-source activity and are outside the Marshall Islands gross receipts tax. If goods were physically exported from the Marshall Islands, they are not subject to any Marshall Islands indirect tax and therefore require no zero-rating substantiation.

  • Completed RMI Customs Export Declaration (Form CED-1)
  • Commercial invoice with country-of-origin and harmonized tariff classification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Certified packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT advice for export proceeds

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-to-tax-administration integration, or digital stamping regime exists. Commercial invoices and standard customs declarations satisfy documentation requirements; there is no pre-clearance fiscal validation for exports or sales.

No VAT clawback is possible in the absence of VAT. However, fraudulent customs declarations or deliberate misstatement of the non-resident status attract customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Act 1993, including fines up to USD 10,000 or the value of the goods, forfeiture of goods, and criminal liability for responsible corporate officers. Misuse of the non-resident election can lead to retroactive reassessment of gross receipts tax at 3% of local gross revenue, monthly interest at 1%, and a civil penalty of up to 25% of the underpaid tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

USD is fully and freely convertible in RMI for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no statutory restrictions on the purchase, holding, or outward remittance of foreign exchange; no government approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation by a non-resident Business Corporation. No withholding taxes, capital gains taxes, or remittance taxes are imposed by RMI on non-resident corporations, which simplifies profit repatriation. The practical convertibility hurdle is not legal but rather correspondent-banking compliance: Pacific-region banks face U.S. OFAC sanctions screening and global de-risking pressure, so the treasury must maintain immaculate transaction narratives, full UBO documentation, and a consistent trade-payment audit trail to prevent wire rejections or account freezes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline exists under RMI law. The non-resident Business Corporation may retain 100 percent of export proceeds in USD or any foreign currency, with no forced conversion ratio and no central-bank registration of export invoices. RMI imposes no deadline for bringing export earnings onshore. Nevertheless, the bank will independently require the retention of commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and shipping records for a minimum of five years to satisfy AML/CTF obligations and U.S. correspondent-bank audit standards. These records must be stored in a documentary compliance folder, prepared to be produced at the bank's request without delay.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no domestic central bank and does not issue a national currency. The U.S. Dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender and official currency pursuant to the Compact of Free Association with the United States; monetary policy is therefore determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve. Supervisory authority over licensed banking entities is vested in the Banking Commission within the RMI Ministry of Finance, operating under the Banking Act 1990 (as amended). The Ministry of Finance acts as the de facto exchange control authority; however, RMI maintains a fully liberalized currency and capital account regime with no exchange-control legislation in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. RMI has no domestic electronics-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework; the Banking Act 1990 does not recognize EMI or payment-service-provider categories. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex) operate solely under their home-jurisdiction licenses (UK FCA, Lithuanian NBM, U.S. state MTLs) and are not authorized or supervised by the RMI Banking Commission. Practical constraints are severe: (a) most EMIs reject RMI-domiciled entities in their onboarding jurisdiction lists; (b) the RMI's FATF/Egmont risk profile and association with offshore corporate registries trigger automatic enhanced due diligence; (c) EMIs require a physical operational address in an approved country and typically do not accept registered-agent addresses. Recommendation: use fintech rails only as an auxillary payout engine after the two domestic banking relationships are operational, and never as the primary clearing artery. Navigation into a U.S.-chartered digital bank may be attempted only after the entity obtains a U.S. EIN and presents a substantive U.S. revenue contract; even then, expect automatic decline unless a personal guarantee from a U.S.-resident signatory is offered.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: the shareholder remits USD via SWIFT MT103 from their foreign bank to the BC's USD account at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam - RMI Branch. The instruction must include: (1) beneficiary account name exactly matching the register: 'Marshall Islands Business Corporation MH_BC'; (2) narrative field 72 with '//REC/NO ADVISORY' and purpose text: 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION PER RESOLUTION 2025-001'; (3) a pre-funding compliance package filed with the receiving bank: board resolution authorizing the account, share subscription letter, register of shareholders, and a capital-purpose declaration. The receiving bank will route via its U.S. correspondent (Bank of America or Citibank for BMI; Bank of Guam's correspondent network for the RMI branch). Funds enter a Capital Escrow Account and are held until the share certificate is issued by the RMI Registrar; then an internal journal transfer to the Operational Account occurs with narrative 'RELEASE OF CAPITAL AFTER CERTIFICATION'. If the originating jurisdiction is Hong Kong or mainland China, the route must be through CHATS or CIPS with an arm's length USD/CNH conversion at the shareholder's bank; private money-corridor transfers are strictly prohibited. The bank retains a post-credit file including the MT103, exchange-rate record, and compliance approval, which will also satisfy any future correspondent-bank audit.For supplier payments in China, the default rail is a SWIFT MT103 US-dollar wire to the supplier's account at a Chinese bank (through CHIPS/Fedwire for USD clearing). Wire narrative must state: 'INVOICE [NUMBER], FOOD-GRADE SILICONE FIDGET TOYS 339930/ISIC3240, EXW [FACTORY]'. If the supplier requires CNY, the treasury converts USD to CNH via the bank's FX desk and remits through CNAPS/CIPS using the same narrative. For high-value purchase orders above USD 250,000, use a commercial Letter of Credit (L/C at sight) issued by Bank of Guam - RMI Branch in favor of the Chinese supplier, advised through the supplier's bank (e.g., Bank of China Shanghai), with settlement via SWIFT MT700/MT103 matching the documentary draft, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading. Inbound collections from U.S. and EU distributors arrive by MT103 to the operational account; treasury performs a daily cash sweep and centralizes balances, converting excess USD into EUR or CNY only where a committed 30-day payable exists. All disbursements exceeding USD 100,000 require two authorizers and a schedule submitted two banking days in advance. RMI has no domestic electronics-money-institution (EMI) licensing framework; the Banking Act 1990 does not recognize EMI or payment-service-provider categories. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, Airwallex) operate solely under their home-jurisdiction licenses (UK FCA, Lithuanian NBM, U.S. state MTLs) and are not authorized or supervised by the RMI Banking Commission. Practical constraints are severe: (a) most EMIs reject RMI-domiciled entities in their onboarding jurisdiction lists; (b) the RMI's FATF/Egmont risk profile and association with offshore corporate registries trigger automatic enhanced due diligence; (c) EMIs require a physical operational address in an approved country and typically do not accept registered-agent addresses. Recommendation: use fintech rails only as an auxillary payout engine after the two domestic banking relationships are operational, and never as the primary clearing artery. Navigation into a U.S.-chartered digital bank may be attempted only after the entity obtains a U.S. EIN and presents a substantive U.S. revenue contract; even then, expect automatic decline unless a personal guarantee from a U.S.-resident signatory is offered.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no general corporate income tax. A Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) is categorically exempt from all RMI taxes, including the Business Privilege Tax (BPT), provided it carries on no business and derives no gross revenue within RMI territory. The only in-scope levy is the 3% Business Privilege Tax on gross revenue derived from business conducted in RMI, which does not apply to an offshore compression-molding entity producing food-grade silicone tactile fidgets.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a business corporation. A non-resident shareholder receiving distributions from the NRDC is subject to no RMI dividend tax, source tax, or remittance tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the last day of the month in which the anniversary of incorporation occurs (e.g., an entity incorporated on 12 March files by 31 March of each year). No statutory extension is available; the government annual fee, tiered by authorized share capital, is payable concurrently.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual BPT-1 return due within 30 days after the close of the calendar year (December 31); quarterly prepayments due by the 20th day of the month following each calendar quarter. For the target NRDC deriving no RMI-source gross revenue, no BPT-1 is required to be filed.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months of the entity's fiscal year-end; the first filing window is determined by the RMI Registrar's published ESR schedule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — RMI has no transfer-pricing statute, no Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country reporting requirements, and no associated documentation threshold in USD or any other currency. The only related-party computational rule appears in the Business Privilege Tax apportionment provisions, which attribute RMI gross revenue to a permanent establishment; the BPT return obligation arises when RMI-source gross revenue exceeds USD 50,000 per annum. For the target NRDC with zero RMI-source revenue, no transfer-pricing documentation is mandated; prudent practice dictates retaining arm's-length intercompany pricing evidence to rebut any reclassification of the entity as locally taxable.

Economic substance scope: RMI enacted the Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019 and the Economic Substance Regulations 2020 to address EU and OECD Global Forum concerns. The ESR applies only to an RMI tax-resident entity or an RMI permanent establishment undertaking one of eight relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centers, and intellectual property holding. A non-resident business corporation manufacturing food-grade silicone sensory fidgets entirely offshore is not within any relevant ESR category and is not RMI tax-resident. Nevertheless, the RMI remains listed on the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions (Annex I), and its AML framework is subject to Asia/Pacific Group mutual evaluation; therefore the entity must demonstrate meaningful economic substance (leased premises, employees, local board decisions, operational bank account) in its actual management jurisdiction and furnish an ESR nil assessment letter from its registered agent to withstand enhanced due diligence by banks and counterparties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False Business Privilege Tax Exemption

Statutory sanction: Assessment of 3% BPT on the full unreported RMI gross revenue; monthly late-filing penalty up to 50% of tax; understatement penalty of 15%; interest at 1.5% per month; criminal fine up to USD 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to three years; distress warrant enforcement against the entity's local assets; loss of NRDC status and consequent revocation of the tax-exempt classification.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a false BPT-1 declaration are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; RMI courts will pierce the corporate veil upon proof of fraud or sham; directors face criminal fines, imprisonment, asset freezes, travel bans, and disqualification from managing any RMI corporation.

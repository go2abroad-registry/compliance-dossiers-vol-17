Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Administration (RCA), Ministry of Finance; taxpayer portal and e-services administered through the Registered Agent doorway for offshore entities (official portal: https://www.mof.gov.mh).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forms a Business Corporation (Non-Resident) under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act. No income tax TIN is statutorily required for a non-resident domestic corporation deriving no income from Marshall Islands sources. If the entity engages in physical customs transactions, it must register with the RCA for customs purposes via the ASYCUDA World declaration system. If the entity employs staff in-country, it must obtain a Social Security Registration Number from the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration. No VAT ID is obtainable or required because the Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax. The registered agent in Majuro files the incorporation certificate and any subsequent registrations through the Registrar of Corporations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Election to operate as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under Section 56 of the Business Corporations Act, which exempts all non-Marshall Islands source income from national income tax. Election to obtain an Offshore Business License category (Class 4) for internationally oriented leasing activities. No check-the-box election exists in Marshall Islands tax law; the entity is treated as an entity-level taxpayer only to the extent it derives Marshall Islands source income. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized. Foreign tax credit and treaty relief are generally unavailable because Marshall Islands does not maintain comprehensive income tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / General Sales Tax; indirect taxation limited to customs import duties and ad valorem or specific excise duties at importation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Customs registration is required only for entities importing or exporting physical goods through Marshall Islands customs territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - the Marshall Islands does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating mechanism. Export of goods or offshore services is outside the scope of indirect taxation. For income tax purposes, income derived by a non-resident domestic corporation from the dry-leasing of aircraft engines to foreign lessees is exempt from Marshall Islands income tax if the income is not attributable to a Marshall Islands permanent establishment or source activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation from Marshall Islands territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of lease rental proceeds in a licensed foreign or domestic bank account

Not applicable - no VAT/GST e-invoicing mandate exists. Customs entries are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single-window system, with fiscal assessment at importation. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice validation requirement for aircraft engine dry-leasing transactions.

No VAT clawback regime exists because there is no VAT zero-rating mechanism. However, fraudulent customs declarations are subject to the Marshall Islands Customs Ordinance, including fines up to 200% of the duty evaded, forfeiture of the goods, and imprisonment up to 3 years. For income tax, the anti-avoidance provisions may recharacterize artificial or sham leasing arrangements if the economic substance of the transaction is found to be connected to Marshall Islands source activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility across both current and capital accounts. Because USD is the de facto domestic currency and the RMI has no exchange-control law, capital injections, lease-rental repatriations, and dividend distributions by a non-resident Business Corporation can be effected freely in USD without central-bank approval, FX licensing, or conversion permits. Practical constraints arise exclusively from AML/KYC and correspondent-bank de-risking, not from statutory FX limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. RMI imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation window. Lease income from aircraft-engine dry leasing (ISIC 5223) may be retained offshore, pooled, or disbursed in USD without declaration to any RMI monetary authority.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The United States Dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association. Banking supervision is vested in the Banking Commissioner of the RMI Banking Commission under the Banking Act 1987; AML/CFT oversight is exercised by the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2012 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Global digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury) generally refuse RMI-incorporated non-resident BCs due to the FATF grey-list designation (June 2024). The RMI Digital Asset Business Act offers a digital-asset licensing path but does not establish an EMI framework accepted by foreign correspondent banks. A Delaware or Wyoming US subsidiary of the BC is the recommended vehicle for fintech payment rails; alternatively, a Hong Kong or Singapore EMI may accept the BC if the UBO has a clean regulated-banking history.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the anniversary date of incorporation in each calendar year (the Annual Report Date). There is no extension mechanism; a report filed after the anniversary date is late.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six (6) months after the end of the accounting period. For a calendar-year entity, the return is due no later than June 30 of the following year. A shorter transitional period applies in the first year of incorporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - the RMI has no transfer pricing legislation, no Local File, Master File, or CbC reporting regime, and no statutory threshold triggering documentation. The absence of a corporate income tax makes transfer pricing irrelevant for RMI domestic purposes. However, for a non-resident BC that is part of a multinational engine-leasing group, the shareholder's home jurisdiction (or the lessee airline's jurisdiction) may independently require arm's length lease pricing and documentation; contemporaneous benchmarking of third-party engine lease rates should be maintained for external foreign tax defense and to support the ESR CIGA narrative.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations 2020 (ESR) apply. Aircraft-engine dry leasing constitutes 'Finance and Leasing' business, which is a relevant activity under the Regulations. An RMI BC engaged in this activity must satisfy a two-pronged test: (i) it must not be a pure 'maillbox' structure, and (ii) it must maintain adequate economic substance in the RMI, namely a physical registered office, a substantive local presence (premises and staff, either directly employed or outsourced to a licensed RMI corporate services provider), and effective control and management exercised in the RMI. The core income-generating activities - negotiating lease terms, approving transactions, managing and monitoring the lease portfolio, and bearing and managing aeronautical risk - must be performed in the RMI. If the entity proves it is a tax resident of a recognized foreign jurisdiction and supplies written evidence from that jurisdiction's tax authority, it is excluded from RMI ESR obligations. Otherwise, annual ESR filing and substance maintenance are mandatory; non-compliance exposes the entity to fines, strike-off, and criminal liability of directors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Registry Delinquency and Trading After Dissolution

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the certificate of registration; automatic incapacity to contract, sue, or hold property in the RMI; reinstatement conditioned on payment of all unpaid annual fees plus a late penalty of US$100 for each delinquent year; possible administrative strike-off and loss of the RMI corporate veil for all acts occurring after revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who transact business after revocation are jointly and severally personally liable for all contractual debts and tort liabilities incurred in the corporation's name. Courts may impose civil restitution in favor of aggrieved lessees or creditors; directors may also face travel bans if they leave the jurisdiction while criminal or recovery proceedings are pending.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure (ESR Breach)

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Economic Substance Regulations 2020: penalty of up to US$10,000 for initial non-compliance; up to US$20,000 for continued default; up to US$100,000 and strike-off for serious offenses. The regulator may also compel production of lease portfolio records, freeze assets, and notify the lessee's home tax authority via international information exchange.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly cause or allow a false ESR declaration or who manage a shell with no substance face personal fines, disqualification from serving as directors of any RMI corporation, and imprisonment of up to 5 years. RMI courts will pierce the corporate veil where the BC is found to be an instrumentality of the shareholder, exposing directors to civil restitution and repatriation of diverted lease rental income.
Tax Evasion and Misrepresentation to Foreign Tax Authorities

Statutory sanction: RMI law treats false statements in statutory filings as misrepresentation punishable by criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment at the discretion of the court. The RMI will cooperate with foreign authorities under its TIEA network by compelling production of bank and ledger records; the US may impose a 30% FATCA withholding penalty on any US-sourced engine lease payments if the entity fails its FATCA/IGA obligations, and foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the structure as a sham and assess taxes, penalties, and interest against the company.

Director personal liability: Individual directors who execute false declarations or knowingly facilitate the evasion scheme are personally liable for criminal fines, restitution of evaded tax, and imprisonment. Travel bans and asset freezes may be issued in support of extradition or mutual legal assistance requests; the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, making shareholders and directors personally answerable for the full tax deficiency.
AML and Proliferation Financing in the Lease-Rent Escrow Flow

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is a criminal offense in the RMI punishable by substantial fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years, plus forfeiture of the aircraft engines, lease receivables, and escrowed funds. The regulator may suspend the licensed resident agent that facilitated the non-compliant structure and may cancel the BC's registration. Foreign correspondent banks may terminate the escrow and SWIFT relationships, effectively freezing the entity's payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct or knowingly benefit from the laundering scheme face personal criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, and civil liability for the full amount of tainted lease income. Where the escrow account was used as a conduit for third-party money laundering, courts will impose imprisonment, travel bans, and permanent disqualification from acting as a director of any RMI or foreign company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Administration Office (TAO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); national tax e-services portal operated by the Ministry of Finance for business tax registration, wage/salary tax withholding filings, and gross revenue tax returns.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no income from sources within the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) is not required to hold an RMI income tax TIN. To obtain a statutory tax identity where any RMI nexus exists, the BC must: (1) file Articles of Incorporation and, where applicable, the non-resident domestic corporation (NRDC) election with the RMI Registrar of Corporations; (2) obtain a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) under the Foreign Investment Business Act 2002 if conducting permitted RMI-source activity; (3) submit a Business Tax Registration application to the TAO, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, the NRDC designation, and a copy of the FIBL; and (4) receive the 9-digit Business Taxpayer Identification Number (BTIN) enabling wage tax withholding, gross revenue tax (GRT) filings, and customs processing. A fully offshore exporter may remain unregistered but, if an FIBL is held, must file an annual nil GRT declaration to preserve the exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident domestic corporation; exempt from RMI income tax on non-RMI-source revenue; no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or dividend/interest/royalty withholding; only RMI-source gross revenue is subject to the 3% GRT and only RMI-source wages to the 4% wage tax).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under RMI law. The sole material election is the statutory non-resident domestic corporation (NRDC) status under the Business Corporations Act 1990, exercised by filing the NRDC designation with the Registrar of Corporations; the designation is effective only while no business is carried on in the RMI, all shareholders are non-residents, and the corporation derives no RMI-source income. A BC may additionally elect to redomicile under the International Business Companies Act, which provides statutory confidentiality and an express exemption from all RMI taxes on offshore revenue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. The RMI indirect regime consists of (i) the 3% Business Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) on RMI-source business turnover, (ii) customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act, and (iii) excise-type levies on selected products. Export supply chains are outside the GRT base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD (RMI uses the US dollar as legal tender). No VAT/GST threshold exists. The 3% GRT registration threshold is USD 10,000 in annual gross revenue derived from business activity carried on inside the Republic of the Marshall Islands; a non-resident exporter with no RMI-source revenue has no GRT registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Equivalent zero-rating is achieved because exports are outside the scope of the RMI GRT. To qualify for export exclusion the taxpayer must demonstrate: (i) sale and physical delivery of the pre-packaged hotpot soup base to a non-resident buyer outside the RMI; (ii) receipt of the sale price in USD through an offshore bank account or SWIFT/Letter of Credit remittance; (iii) export clearance at an RMI port evidenced by customs documentation; and (iv) compliance with food-export controls, including a health/sanitary/export permit from the RMI Ministry of Health and Human Services and, for tallow-based shelf-stable products, RMI Environmental Protection Authority certification. Because inputs bear no VAT, no input-credit refund mechanism applies; however, import duty paid on packaging or processing inputs later exported may be recovered under the RMI Import Duty Drawback scheme where the inputs are incorporated into goods exported within 12 months.

Export declarations are filed electronically through the ASYCUDA customs clearance system at Majuro and Ebeye (or via the authorised manual form pending ASYCUDA rollout) solely for customs risk management; commercial and export invoices require no tax authority validation or cryptographic stamp.

The Tax Administration Office may audit export exclusions for up to five years. Failing to substantiate physical export and foreign receipt within 180 days triggers a retrospective clawback of the excluded gross revenue, subjecting the revenue to the 3% GRT plus civil penalties of 50% of the understated tax and 1.5% monthly interest (Tax Act 2014, as amended). Independently, the Customs Service may impose penalties of up to 300% of the value of goods for false export declarations, together with seizure and forfeiture of the shipment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI operates under full dollarization, so both current account and capital account items are fully convertible with zero exchange control restrictions. There are no impediments to profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment or liquidation proceeds for a non-resident Business Corporation. The only practical hurdles are AML/KYC verification, correspondent-bank sanctions screening and the absence of insider withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 (the RMI does not withhold on foreign-source income of non-resident BCs).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD-denominated accounts anywhere, with no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline and no exchange-control approval. Confirmation of this treatment is a standard written legal opinion of Marshall Islands counsel for the onboarding bank and correspondent bank.

Central bank supervision: No central bank or foreign exchange administration is established in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The competent treasury regulatory authority is the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, RMI, acting under the Banking Act 1997, the Foreign Investment Business Act 1998 (FIBA), the Currency Act 1982 and the Financial Institutions Act 1993. The United States dollar is the sole official legal tender, making the U.S. Federal Reserve the de facto monetary anchor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The RMI has no dedicated e-money institution or offshore EMI licensing regime; payment-service activity requires a banking licence under the Banking Act 1997 or a money-changer/money-transmitter licence, and the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 does not authorise EMI or clearing activity. An offshore digital EMI would therefore require a locally licensed agent and a U.S. dollar correspondent sponsor, which is impractical given the absence of local clearing rails and the small market size.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is routed as an inbound SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (treasury route) in U.S. dollars from the shareholder's home bank to the RMI bank's correspondent account in the United States (Fedwire/CHIPS). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective taxation for the non-resident Business Corporation (Tax-Exempt Zone classification). The RMI Income Tax Act (Title 54) prescribes an 11.5% statutory corporate rate exclusively on taxable income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands; a non-resident BC deriving entirely foreign-source export distribution income from pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks has zero RMI taxable income and therefore zero RMI corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI Income Tax Act imposes no withholding tax on dividends declared by a non-resident Business Corporation, and no tax is imposed on the receipt of dividends by non-resident shareholders, including the distribution group's ultimate beneficial owners; no dividend withholding, no undistributed profits tax, and no branch remittance tax are chargeable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the anniversary date of incorporation each year (the registered anniversary date); the Annual Report fee must accompany the filing, and the Registrar permits filing within the month preceding the anniversary without penalty.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year; the taxable year is the calendar year unless a different fiscal year is approved by the Tax Administration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold exists in the Marshall Islands: the RMI has enacted no transfer-pricing documentation, comparability, or contemporaneous reporting regime for non-resident Business Corporations, and no monetary threshold in USD or any other currency triggers a domestic TP filing. Nevertheless, the distribution group should maintain arm's-length intercompany agreements, a functional and risk analysis of the hotpot soup base sourcing and export route, and thin-capitalization documentation to satisfy ESR examiners and the transfer-pricing audits of customer-market tax authorities where the BC invoices foreign affiliates.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations (2019, as amended) impose a substance test on resident corporations carrying on a 'Relevant Activity.' A pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base distributor that operates as an intra-group procuring and distribution hub may be captured under the 'distribution and service centre' category if it purports to conduct such activity in RMI. However, the Regulations expressly exclude a 'non-resident company' that is not subject to RMI tax, derives no income from RMI sources, and maintains no RMI assets; the entity in this dossier qualifies for that exclusion only while the Annual Report declaration of non-residence is truthful. Where substance is required, the BC must evidence (i) RMI management and control (directors' meetings, policy decisions), (ii) adequate premises and employees in RMI, (iii) annual expenditure sufficient for the activity, and (iv) core income-generating activities performed in RMI; a distribution business whose actual procurement, warehousing, and sales decisions occur overseas must document that the relevant activities are not performed in RMI to avoid an ESR assessment, a financial penalty of up to US$10,000 per infraction with daily continuing penalties, and referral for deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Misdeclaration of RMI-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Under Title 54 (Income Tax Act) and the RMI Criminal Code: a fine up to US$20,000 for a corporate offender, 100% civil fraud penalty on the understated tax, statutory interest and monthly delinquency surcharges, jeopardy assessment with immediate levy on bank accounts and receivables, and, upon conviction, imprisonment of responsible individuals for a term not exceeding three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, direct, or benefit from a false declaration are personally and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; the court may pierce the corporate veil where the BC is merely their instrumentality, impose travel bans preventing departure from the Marshall Islands pending assessment, order civil restitution of diverted funds, and impose custodial sentences of up to three years, with extradition available under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Shell Company Operation and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations and BCA: financial penalty of not less than US$10,000 per infraction (with daily continuing penalties for failure to remediate), potential upward adjustment of revenue and profits attributed to RMI, suspension of the certificate of good standing, loss of the non-resident exclusion, and eventual strike-off from the register without prejudice to creditors' claims.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent who knowingly certify false substance or non-residence statements face personal fines, disqualification from acting as officers or agents in the RMI, joint and several liability for ESR assessments and restoration costs, and travel bans pending administrative resolution; habitual shell abuse may be referred for criminal prosecution and the directors' personal assets may be reached by enforcement orders.
Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Criminal Code, the Money Laundering (Amendment) Act 2021, and the AML/CFT Regulations: imprisonment up to 25 years for money laundering, confiscation and civil forfeiture of proceeds and instrumentalities, corporate fines up to US$200,000 per offence, revocation of the business license, loss of registered agent services, and a directive freezing all RMI bank accounts and flagged Swift activity; the Low-Risk Standard banking tier does not relieve the BC of KYC, record-keeping, and reporting obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize, direct, or recklessly fail to prevent laundering transactions incur personal criminal liability, whose assets are exposed to civil asset confiscation, and whose conduct defeats the corporate veil because the BC is used as a mere instrument of financial crime; such directors face international travel bans, red-flag listing with correspondent banks, disqualification orders, and extradition to prosecuting jurisdictions under MLAT cooperation.
False Filings, Nominee Abuse, and Unauthorized Use of Registered Agent

Statutory sanction: Under the BCA and the RMI Associations Law: fines up to US$1,000 for each false item in a filed document, rejection of the Annual Report with reinstatement penalties, administrative dissolution of the BC, refusal of certificates of good standing required for Letter of Credit confirmations, and the imposition of any unpaid annual fees multiplied by the delinquency surcharge.

Director personal liability: A director who executes a false instrument commits a statutory misdemeanor punishable by personal fine and imprisonment; courts freely pierce the corporate veil where nominee structures and false attestations evidence fraud, holding directors personally liable for the BC's outstanding debts, unpaid fees, and counterparty losses, and may issue travel bans and remove the director from any future role in RMI-regulated entities.

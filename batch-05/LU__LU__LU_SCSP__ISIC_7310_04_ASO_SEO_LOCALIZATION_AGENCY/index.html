Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Luxembourg-registered office is a legal requirement for the SCSp; the registered office may be supplied by an authorised professional domiciliation agent (agréé), which is a standard and acceptable solution for a low-risk app-growth agency. However, a purely virtual address without any operational substance, management oversight, document retention or decision-taking capacity in Luxembourg exposes the entity to ACD tax-residency challenges, RBE/OECD BEPS Action 5 substance scrutiny and banking onboarding refusals. For this agency, professional domiciliation plus a Luxembourg resident manager or an appointed local administrative agent is the minimum recommended commercial-substance configuration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an SCSp. Capital is fixed freely in the partnership agreement and may be contributed in cash or in kind. No escrow account is required by law, though bank practice may require a capital account for opening operational accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance is required for the registered office under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 July 1985 on fire safety, and workplace inspection clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) is mandatory for employee health and safety compliance. Structural load certifications are not required for standard office floors. Employers must also register with the CCSS and submit workplace rules as applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an SCSp. Capital is fixed freely in the partnership agreement and may be contributed in cash or in kind. No escrow account is required by law, though bank practice may require a capital account for opening operational accounts.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT — portal: guichet.lu and MyGuichet electronic filing services

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that carries out taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register with AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (often combined with a national business number). The application is filed electronically through MyGuichet using the registration form for non-established taxable persons, accompanied by proof of foreign registration, constitutional documents, identity documents of directors/managers, and a summary of intended activities. The AEDT assigns a 9-digit national registration number enabling VAT submission and customs interaction.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity that carries out taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register with AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (often combined with a national business number). The application is filed electronically through MyGuichet using the registration form for non-established taxable persons, accompanied by proof of foreign registration, constitutional documents, identity documents of directors/managers, and a summary of intended activities. The AEDT assigns a 9-digit national registration number enabling VAT submission and customs interaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The SCSp is fiscally transparent by default for Luxembourg income tax and net wealth tax purposes; no check-the-box election exists under domestic law. For VAT, the SCSp is treated as a taxable person where it independently supplies goods or services, and it may apply the VAT grouping election under Article 61 of the VAT Law with AEDT authorization. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply to negative tax transparency mismatches, and no election to become opaque is generally available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established suppliers making first taxable supply in Luxembourg; small domestic businesses may benefit from the EUR 35,000 annual turnover exemption. For cross-border distance sales, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies before Luxembourg VAT registration is required unless the OSS scheme is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods to a destination outside the EU qualifies for the 0% rate if the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier, a customer not established in Luxembourg, or a third party acting for them, within the statutory proof period. Exported services (e.g., ASO/localization services) supplied to a non-EU taxable person or to a non-EU private person with effective use and enjoyment outside Luxembourg may be zero-rated or outside scope, subject to showing the service recipient's non-EU status and the actual export nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, an API clearance mandate, or digital stamping regime is in force. Electronic invoices are accepted if kept in their original electronic format and considered authentic and legible, preferably conforming to the European standard EN 16931. Invoices must be transmitted with the recipient's consent, and all invoices, including e-invoice audit files, must be retained and produced to AEDT upon demand.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may verify export proofs retrospectively, and if the supplier cannot demonstrate timely and legal export, the zero rating is disallowed and the VAT becomes payable, with potential recovery (clawback) of input VAT previously deducted. Fraudulent attempts to obtain an unwarranted zero rating trigger VAT evasion penalties ranging from a civil fine of up to EUR 250,000, recovery of unpaid tax plus interest, and criminal prosecution with up to five years' imprisonment for commercial fraud. AEDT applies strict documentary completeness and chain-of-custody checks before approving any export refund claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full current and capital account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the EU Capital Movements Directive. No exchange control authorisation is required for current payments, profit remittances, repatriation of capital, or foreign direct investment flows. Profit remittance by a Luxembourg SCSp to non-resident partners is unrestricted, subject only to standard tax reporting and anti-money laundering verification; no 'profit remittance tax' or balance-of-payments approval applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements in Luxembourg or in the eurozone. Export receipts may be held freely in EUR or any other currency, credited to designated foreign currency accounts, and used for legitimate commercial purposes without requiring BCL approval. Luxembourg imposes no forex retention or repatriation deadline on a SCSp's export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem framework and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); exchange control and capital movement supervision is harmonised at EU level, with the CSSF responsible for credit institution prudential oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for a non-bank payment institution registered in Luxembourg or an EU-licensed e-money institution passported into Luxembourg (e.g., Wise B2B, Payoneer EU) to service the SCSp. Cross-border fintech EMIs are permitted to hold client funds, conduct FX conversions, and execute SEPA and international payments without a domestic banking licence. However, such EMIs generally cannot provide credit, commercial letter of credit issuance, or guaranteed clearing services; for escrow-grade capital subscriptions and shareholder loan disbursements, a Tier-1 credit institution account remains the mandatory anchor. Legal constraint: an SCSp is not a financial sector professional, so no CSSF licence is required, but the EMI must comply with GDPR, AML/CFT, and payment services rules in the country of account establishment.

Defense evidence: Notarised partnership agreement; certificate of registration from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS); bank and brokerage statements showing the origin of each partner's funds; prior-year audited financial statements or tax returns of the partners; explanation of the commercial substance of the SEO/localisation agency; signed declarations of the UBOs with names, citizenship, and proof of address.
Cross-border payments to third-party SEO platforms and contractors with no obvious relation to registered activity

Root cause: Cross-border payments to third-party SEO platforms and contractors with no obvious relation to registered activity

Defense evidence: Signed client contracts and subcontractor agreements detailing the scope of app-store optimisation services; detailed invoice register with amounts, dates, and purpose of each payment; evidence of actual service delivery (reports, screenshots, performance dashboards); logical explanation of the commercial rationale linked to ISIC 7310; any intellectual property licence if software payments are involved.
High-frequency incoming transfers from multiple jurisdictions with no consistent counterparty profile

Root cause: High-frequency incoming transfers from multiple jurisdictions with no consistent counterparty profile

Defense evidence: Customer due diligence file on all recurring clients, including corporate registration certificates, UBO registries, and sanction-screening results; an existing marketing and sales pipeline underlying the payment flow; clear segregation of client funds from operating funds; monthly reconciliation reports prepared by an independent Luxembourg accountant.
Partner distributions or shareholder loan repayments within a short period after capital injection

Root cause: Partner distributions or shareholder loan repayments within a short period after capital injection

Defense evidence: Substance documentation proving that the SCSp is not a flow-through shell; board minutes and capital call notices explaining the timing of distributions; tax estimates prepared by a Luxembourg tax adviser confirming that the distribution is consistent with partnership pass-through treatment; bank statements evidencing the availability of audited profits or cash from ongoing operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the SCSp must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA immediate credit transfer from the partner's account to the SCSp's dedicated capital escrow account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. Remitters must state a precise payment purpose code, generally 'P001' (positive income) or 'P002' (capital and reserve) under the EU Balance of Payments classification, with the reference line 'SCSp capital contribution under partnership agreement dated [date]'. The receiving bank will verify the identity of the remitter against the RCS UBO register and may request the capital call notice; the funds must be booked as partnership equity, not as debt, unless a formal shareholder loan agreement exists. For amounts exceeding EUR 10,000, an automatic CSSF/AML reporting threshold triggers, requiring comprehensive source-of-funds documentation.Trade settlements for app-store optimisation, multilingual search services, and performance marketing purchases are cleared via (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the EEA, (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA and foreign-currency payments, and (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit where the counterparty requires documentary credit security, particularly for large programmematic advertising purchases. Disbursement instructions must match the invoice and the underlying contract; purpose codes such as 'P030' (services) or 'P050' (royalties and licence fees) are used according to the actual service. For contractor payments in USD/GBP/CHF, the bank executes a spot conversion at the fix, or the SCSp can pre-fund a foreign currency account via a forward hedge.Outright FX forwards with a Luxembourg credit institution for USD, GBP, CHF, and JPY exposures, underlying commercial invoices onlyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) via authorised EU banks for currencies of targeted app-store jurisdictions without full onshore market access, subject to CSSF suitabilityCross-currency swaps (CCS) for matching expected USD/EUR advertising spend against revenue earned in Baltics or CEEMEA marketsFX spot contracts for immediate trade settlements and working capital conversionsVanilla FX options for managing downside risk on large outbound licensing fees, subject to position limits agreed with the bank

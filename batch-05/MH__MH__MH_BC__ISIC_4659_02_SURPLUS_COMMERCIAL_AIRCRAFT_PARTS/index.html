Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; customs matters administered by RMI Customs Service. No dedicated national taxpayer web portal exists for general tax registration; filings are lodged with the Ministry of Finance in Majuro.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are not required to obtain a TIN or VAT ID solely for registration of a non-resident Business Corporation. The sole administrative requirement is incorporation with the RMI Registrar of Business Transactions and payment of the applicable annual franchise fee. If a non-resident BC later establishes a taxable nexus inside the Marshall Islands (e.g., derives RMI-source income or employs local staff), it must then apply to the Commissioner of Revenue for an RMI taxpayer identification number. No indirect-tax registration is available because the RMI levies no VAT, GST, or sales tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities are not required to obtain a TIN or VAT ID solely for registration of a non-resident Business Corporation. The sole administrative requirement is incorporation with the RMI Registrar of Business Transactions and payment of the applicable annual franchise fee. If a non-resident BC later establishes a taxable nexus inside the Marshall Islands (e.g., derives RMI-source income or employs local staff), it must then apply to the Commissioner of Revenue for an RMI taxpayer identification number. No indirect-tax registration is available because the RMI levies no VAT, GST, or sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. The RMI has no check-the-box, hybrid entity, or pass-through election under domestic tax law. A non-resident Business Corporation is taxed per se only on income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands; no entity classification election is available. Anti-hybrid mismatch adjustments of foreign jurisdictions do not create domestic RMI tax elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose a VAT, GST, or general sales/use tax. Import and excise duties may apply at the border under RMI Customs legislation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold exists. The RMI uses the United States Dollar (USD) as its official currency; the concept of a monetary threshold does not apply because no indirect tax is imposed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the direct sense: the Marshall Islands has no VAT/GST regime, so there is no zero-rating or export VAT refund mechanism. For customs duty purposes, qualifying exports (including surplus commercial aircraft parts and avionics) are outside the charge to duty when the goods are physically exported within the time limit prescribed by RMI Customs, the export declaration is duly completed, and no diversion, re-importation, or fraudulent misdescription occurs. Offshore services supplied by a non-resident BC are not subject to any RMI indirect tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged with RMI Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence (where export proceeds are repatriated)
  • Certificate of Non-Resident Business Corporation Status (if claiming exemption)
  • Export Permit or license (if required for controlled aviation goods)

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API digital stamping, or government-approved e-invoicing mandate exists in the Marshall Islands. RMI Customs uses an electronic customs declaration system (ASYCUDA World) for import/export clearance, but this does not impose a continuous VAT clearance or invoice-submission obligation.

No VAT or indirect-tax clawback exists because the RMI imposes no VAT/GST. However, fraudulent claims for duty exemption or false export declarations are subject to RMI Customs Act enforcement, including revocation of the exemption, recovery of unpaid duties, monetary penalties (typically up to 200% of the duty evaded), seizure and forfeiture of the goods, and criminal prosecution. For payment flows, anti-money laundering and sanctions scrutiny may apply to cross-border aviation escrow and SWIFT wire transactions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The RMI imposes no exchange-control regime, no capital-account licensing, no inward-investment restrictions, and no statutory limitation on profit or dividend remittance for a non-resident Business Corporation in tax-exempt status. Practically, remittance friction derives from non-statutory layers: U.S. Treasury/OFAC sanctions screening at U.S. correspondent banks, enhanced due diligence triggered by offshore incorporation, and certification requirements such as IRS Form W-8BEN-E or equivalent U.S. beneficial-owner tax forms to avoid 30% U.S. withholding on U.S.-sourced payments. Once good-standing, beneficial-ownership, W-8, and source-of-funds evidence is complete, profit and capital remittance are effectively frictionless.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline apply to export receipts under RMI law. Export proceeds from certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics surplus may be retained in USD in-country or abroad, or converted into any freely convertible currency. The operative constraints are documentary and correspondent-bank driven: wires must be supported by sales contract, commercial invoice, airway bill, ECCN/EAR99 classification note, and end-user undertaking; cash transactions at U.S. correspondents reaching USD 10,000 trigger Bank Secrecy Act currency-transaction reporting, so all proceeds must move by SWIFT wire rather than cash.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands: no domestic central bank or statutory foreign-exchange administration exists. The U.S. dollar is sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, and monetary conditions are de facto set by the U.S. Federal Reserve System. In-jurisdiction banking supervision, AML/CFT oversight, and bank licensing rest with the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance), supported by the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit as the national reporting center.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Regulated offshore digital EMIs generally require applicant tax residence, director nexus, or a bank reference in a supported jurisdiction (US, UK, EU, SG, HK, or AU); an RMI non-resident Business Corporation with registered-agent premises but no local economic substance will usually fail automated EMI onboarding and be declined at sanctions and mother-list review. Because the RMI issues no e-money license, an EMI cannot act as the primary clearing bank. A limited viable use exists for small pre-funded operational disbursements after full counterparty screening, provided the EMI supports B2B trade purpose codes and transmits a transfer message compatible with U.S. correspondent OFAC screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital follows four compliance steps: (1) board resolution, with any required filing to the RMI Registrar, authorizing the capital injection; (2) remittance exclusively from the named shareholder's own regulated bank account via SWIFT MT103 benefitting the MH_BC operational or escrow account, with narrative stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - NON-RESIDENT BUSINESS CORPORATION' and an escrow or subscription reference for reconciliation; (3) sender confirmation as a licensed financial institution, with no cash deposits, no crypto-origin funds, and no third-party aggregation; and (4) beneficiary-bank funds verification before treasury deployment. Because the RMI imposes no FX-approval requirement, the only flow-stoppers are OFAC/SDN screening at the U.S. correspondent and bank-level enhanced due diligence, both cleared by the beneficial-ownership extract, good-standing certificate, and W-8BEN-E or equivalent tax-beneficial-owner certification.Trade disbursements settle through two complementary rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for escrow-to-vendor and operating payments, where the beneficiary bank, BIC, and account number are pre-validated and each message carries the invoice and purchase-order references; and (b) irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP 600, confirmed where the vendor is a first-time MRO or repair station, covering bulk rotables purchases with documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, airway bill, FAA 8130-3 or EASA Form 1 or OEM release certificate, and the ECCN/EAR99 statement. The Escrow_Aviation rail is the primary settlement overlay for surplus transactions: the escrow agent holds funds against a Release-and-Pay Instruction, releasing only after successful part inspection and then passing a single MT103 to the vendor, eliminating counterparty non-performance risk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None prescribed. RMI maintains no Local File, Master File or Country-by-Country reporting regime, and the Income Tax Act does not impose transfer-pricing adjustments on exempt offshore entities. However, Economic Substance Regulations compliance requires the BC to retain contemporaneous evidence that intercompany remuneration paid to RMI-office personnel and related suppliers is consistent with the OECD arm's-length principle, including a functional and cost analysis of purchasing, inventory, pricing and risk functions performed in the Republic.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2020 (as amended) apply to every Business Corporation carrying on a Relevant Activity under the BCCA. For a distribution and service center, the Core Income-Generating Activities — procurement of certified aircraft rotables and avionics surplus, inventory holding and management, pricing decisions, supply-chain risk assumption, and sales/distribution coordination — must be directed and managed in the Republic, with adequate physical premises, full-time employees and local operating expenditure proportionate to the trading profile. The pure equity-holding entity exemption does not apply to ISIC 4659 trading activity. Annual ESR returns are due concurrently with the Annual Report; failure to demonstrate substance triggers penalty action by the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Criminal misdemeanor penalties under the BCCA including fines and imprisonment; administrative reassessment of the correct franchise tax tier with mandatory payment of all shortfalls; disqualification of the Registered Agent if knowingly involved; publication of the enforcement action by the RMI Companies Office

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed or authorized the false Annual Report or offshore declaration are jointly and severally liable for unpaid franchise taxes, statutory penalties and prosecution costs; criminal conviction may carry imprisonment; court-issued travel restrictions may be imposed pending investigation or restitution under RMI criminal procedure
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to USD 20,000 for non-compliance, rising on repeat default; cancellation of the economic substance certificate; administrative strike-off of the corporation; cross-border re-characterization by foreign tax authorities as managed and controlled in their jurisdiction, exposing the full foreign corporate income tax on the aircraft-parts margins plus penalties

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from acting as directors of any RMI corporation; fines may be enforced personally where the entity cannot pay; deliberate concealment of substance failures is prosecuted as fraud; travel bans can be ordered where directors fail to cooperate with the Registrar or the Financial Intelligence Unit
AML/CTF Breach in Aviation Spares Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act and the RMI Criminal Code: fines up to USD 100,000 per count, forfeiture of the aircraft-part inventory and proceeds, mandatory FIU reporting orders, and suspension or revocation of the corporate registration

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminally liable for willful blindness and may be sentenced to imprisonment; personal civil restitution to defrauded counterparties is recoverable through veil-piercing where corporate funds are used for illegal escrow diversion; travel bans and asset freezes are standard FIU and court measures pending resolution
Unlicensed RMI Domestic Activity and Foreign Investment License Evasion

Statutory sanction: Suspension or cancellation of the BC registration; ministerial fines for each unlicensed transaction; confiscation of profits derived from RMI-source sales; application of the 3% statutory withholding and domestic assessment on RMI-source gross income under the Income Tax Act

Director personal liability: Directors who directed the unlicensed local sales are personally liable for business license fees, fines and any tax assessment; civil veil-piercing is available to RMI counterparties injured by the unlawful trading; in egregious cases, directors face imprisonment and court-administered travel bans pending satisfaction of monetary judgments
Unlawful Distribution and Reckless Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Unlawful distribution provisions of the BCCA: the distribution is voidable to the extent it impairs capital; the corporation must restore the amounts with interest; the Registrar may dissolve the corporation if solvency cannot be re-established; foreign creditors may enforce against the RMI assets

Director personal liability: Directors who voted for or authorized the unlawful distribution are jointly and severally liable to restore the full amount with interest; piercing the corporate veil permits creditors to bring personal civil suits; serious cases involving fraudulent conveyance result in criminal sanctions, travel bans and imprisonment

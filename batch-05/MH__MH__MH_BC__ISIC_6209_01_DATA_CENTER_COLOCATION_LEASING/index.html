Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); corporate registration, annual fees, and franchise tax: Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Justice. Government portal: www.rmigov.info; corporate filings are made through licensed resident agents (no direct public e-filing portal).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned entity obtains its statutory identity by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations through a licensed registered agent, with a registered office in Majuro. The issued Certificate of Incorporation contains the unique registration number, which functions as the entity's tax identifier; non-resident exempt Business Corporations are not separately registered with the Revenue and Taxation Division and receive no TIN. Annual compliance: payment of the BCA annual registration fee and franchise tax by each anniversary date; the registered agent files the annual report and obtains the Certificate of Good Standing. No tax clearance, no in-person presence, and no minimum capital is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under RMI law. Non-resident exempt status is a factual determination, not an elective filing: the Business Corporation qualifies as exempt only if it does not engage in trade or business within the RMI and derives no RMI-source income. Structural elections available: (i) election of non-resident domestic status at incorporation by describing the registered office and confirming no RMI business activity; (ii) continuance/re-domestication of a foreign corporation into the RMI under the BCA; and (iii) for US federal tax purposes, the RMI Business Corporation is a per se foreign corporation under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8), so no US check-the-box election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_National_VAT_GST_Or_SalesTax - the RMI imposes no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or sales/use tax at the national or municipal level. The only indirect imposition is the RMI Customs Tariff (import duty) on physical goods entering the country.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not_Applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST is imposed on colocation leasing, hosting, or any services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not_Applicable - with no VAT/GST regime, export zero-rating and refund mechanics do not exist. RMI source taxation is territorial: the Income Tax Act taxes only income derived from sources within the RMI. Colocation-leasing and bare-metal hosting revenue received by a non-resident Business Corporation from offshore customers, for infrastructure located outside the RMI, is neither RMI-source income nor subject to any RMI indirect tax. If the leased servers were physically located inside the RMI, the entity could be treated as engaged in trade or business in the RMI; however, the standard MH_BC leasing structure keeps the underlying infrastructure, contracts, and billing outside the RMI to preserve the Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Not_Required: no RMI customs export filing applies to services; required only if physical servers/equipment are re-exported from the RMI (then an export entry is filed with RMI Customs).
  • Bill of Lading - Not_Required: relevant only for physical equipment movements; colocation capacity leasing generates no cargo documentation.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - Not_Required: the RMI has no exchange-control or inward-remittance-certification regime; USD wire receipts require no government certification.

None - the RMI has no e-invoicing mandate, real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital signature requirement. Paper or PDF invoices are valid; colocation invoices may be issued without government clearance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility: the RMI has no capital controls and imposes no exchange-control approvals on inward or outward transfers denominated in USD. Capital account movements (equity injections, shareholder loans, distributions) and current account payments (rents, licensing, services) are equally free of exchange-licensing requirements. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation by a Non-Resident Business Corporation are permitted without quantitative limits, subject only to AML/KYC due diligence and the bank's compliance screening. There is no dual-tier exchange market, no surrender obligation, and no requirement to obtain a central-bank permit for foreign borrowing, equity issuance, or offshore disbursement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist. There is no mandated timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no percentage conversion obligation, and no requirement to deposit export earnings into any designated government account. A Non-Resident Business Corporation engaged in data-center colocation leasing may freely retain, disburse, or reinvest offshore and onshore USD receipts without filing any exchange-control declaration. Entities should nevertheless retain auditable records of receipts for AML purposes, as banks may require evidence of the commercial substance of foreign exchange proceeds in the context of suspicious-transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not operate a domestic central bank or a formal foreign-exchange administration. Prudential oversight of licensed banks is exercised by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance, acting through the Banking Commission. The Financial Action Committee functions as the national Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) for AML/CFT supervision. The US dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender and circulating currency under the Compact of Free Association; the Federal Reserve System ultimately guarantees settlement liquidity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. RMI is not within the SEPA zone and is viewed by major offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) as a non-core Pacific jurisdiction; most global EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) will not onboard an RMI-domiciled Business Corporation due to perceived correspondent-banking compliance burden. Legal constraints: RMI has no domestic EMI licensing regime that would permit a locally domiciled EMI to operate; inbound EMI services are thus unregistered overseas payment services, which banks may flag. If an EMI is used at all, it should be strictly limited to a collection-only conduit (e.g., receiving client ACH/wire payments) with automatic daily sweep to the primary RMI bank account; the EMI must be regulated in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction (US, UK, Singapore) and must provide complete transactional audit trails to satisfy RMI banking scrutiny.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-Resident (offshore) domicile of the Business Corporation with no physical office or employees in RMI.

Root cause: Non-Resident (offshore) domicile of the Business Corporation with no physical office or employees in RMI.

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the Business Corporations Act registration, Certificate of Non-Resident Status, current Certificate of Good Standing, details of the RMI registered agent, an organizational/ownership chart showing ultimate beneficial owners, and a board resolution declaring the absence of RMI-sourced business activities.
Purpose of account and expected transaction flows not anchored to a real commercial contract.

Root cause: Purpose of account and expected transaction flows not anchored to a real commercial contract.

Defense evidence: Fully executed Master Colocation Service Agreement, an annotated client list with legal names and countries, sample monthly invoices, a treasury forecast of monthly ACH/SWIFT volumes, and a narrative business plan describing how the entity leases Tier-3/4 rack space and sells bare-metal hosting services without maintaining a physical RMI data center.

Suspicious source of injected equity capital (ambiguous shareholder contribution provenance).

Root cause: Suspicious source of injected equity capital (ambiguous shareholder contribution provenance).

Defense evidence: Bank statements or broker account records of the contributing shareholder covering a 12-month period, certified copies of the shareholders' tax returns or wealth sources, a notarized source-of-funds declaration, and signed subscription/board minutes evidencing the capital injection.
Exposure to sanctioned or high-risk counterparties through data-center clients and vendor payments.

Root cause: Exposure to sanctioned or high-risk counterparties through data-center clients and vendor payments.

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening certificates for all clients and counterparties (OFAC, EU, UN), completed beneficial-ownership questionnaires for major clients, a certificate from the compliance officer that no revenues derive from prohibited end-uses or sanctioned jurisdictions, and contractual clauses restricting subleasing to third parties without KYC clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's bank to the RMI beneficiary bank's USD account, routed via the bank's US correspondent (typically Bank of Guam's network or a major US clearing bank). For domestic US-sourced capital, an ACH 'Same-Day ACH' credit or Fedwire transfer to the RMI bank's US correspondent account, with beneficiary memo referencing: 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – MH_BC'. Remittance information field (Field 70) must include the entity identifier 'MH__MH__MH_BC__ISIC_6209_01_DATA_CENTER_COLOCATION_LEASING' and the phrase 'CAPITAL INJECTION – NON-RESIDENT BC – NO EXCHANGE CONTROL APPROVAL REQUIRED'. Verification: the receiving bank issues a credit advice; the treasury team must reconcile against the shareholder subscription resolution and attach a certificate of inward remittance for the entity's accounting records. No RMI government approval, permit, or declaration is required for the equity injection.Two-tier clearing architecture: (1) Monthly recurring domestic and regional payments (colocation rack lease fees, bandwidth capacity charges, monitoring software subscriptions) are cleared via US ACH (NACHA) through the RMI bank's US correspondent, or via SWIFT MT103 if the commercial landlord is outside the US; (2) High-value capital expenditures (purchase of servers, network switches, and uninterruptible power supply equipment) are settled through a documentary commercial Letter of Credit issued in USD and governed by UCP600, with the vendor's presenting bank receiving proceeds via SWIFT MT202/MT103 against compliant shipping and test-report documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 (Not applicable. RMI has no transfer pricing legislation and imposes no income tax; therefore no Local File, Master File, or country-by-country reporting obligation is triggered for an RMI Business Corporation).

Economic substance scope: RMI has no domestic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and does not require economic substance reporting. Nevertheless, the entity should maintain genuine commercial substance (physical colocation infrastructure, server hardware, local or contractual staff, real customer contracts, and substantive billing records) to prevent recharacterization as a shell company under foreign anti-abuse rules and to preserve the low-risk AML classification of the data-center colocation business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Misdeclaration of RMI-Sourced Operations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the RMI Tax and Business Law, disallowance of any available exemptions, assessment of unpaid taxes, and monetary penalties up to 100% of the concealed amount; possible action by the RMI Ministry of Finance and Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit false declarations are personally liable for restitution of the avoided tax, civil damages to claimants, and, in egregious cases, are subject to travel bans and imprisonment under RMI criminal fraud statutes.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: RMI Registrar may strike the entity from the register, deny renewal of good standing, and impose dissolution; foreign tax authorities may treat the structure as an abusively transparent shell and impose their own penalties.

Director personal liability: Disregarded entity consequences may extend to directors under foreign CFC and anti-hybrid rules; directors could face civil liability for fraudulent transfers and, in multi-jurisdictional investigations, personal travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML-CFT Failure

Statutory sanction: RMI Financial Intelligence Unit and Registrar can impose administrative fines of up to USD 500,000 for corporate breaches, order monitoring/suspension of operations, and revoke the corporate registration for egregious non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face individual fines, personal civil restitution orders, criminal prosecution for facilitating money laundering, and imprisonment up to 20 years for knowingly permitting illicit financial flows.
Misrepresentation to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: RMI Business Corporations Act empowers appointment of an inspector, certificate cancellation, and fines not less than USD 10,000 for each false filing; the Registrar may suspend all corporate rights until correction and may initiate prosecution.

Director personal liability: Each director who signed a false document is jointly and severally liable for the resulting pecuniary loss to the company and third parties; directors are subject to personal appearance restrictions and extradition-capable criminal liability.

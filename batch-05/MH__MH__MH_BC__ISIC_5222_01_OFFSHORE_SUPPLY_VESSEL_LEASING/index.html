Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Tax Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); corporate registry administered by the RMI Registrar of Corporations through licensed registered agents via the RMI Registry electronic portal (https://www.rmi-registry.com); no separate e-filing tax portal, filings are paper-based.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned non-resident domestic corporation: (1) engage an RMI-licensed registered agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act 1990 electing non-resident domestic corporation status (all shareholders must be non-residents and the corporation must transact no business in the RMI); (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, which is the statutory corporate identifier (no separate TIN is issued); (4) submit a Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) registration or exemption notification to the Revenue and Tax Division to establish the GRT account status; (5) no VAT/TIN registration is possible because the RMI imposes no VAT or GST; (6) if flag-state registration is desired, register the vessel with the RMI Maritime Administrator through its authorized agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a foreign-owned non-resident domestic corporation: (1) engage an RMI-licensed registered agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act 1990 electing non-resident domestic corporation status (all shareholders must be non-residents and the corporation must transact no business in the RMI); (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, which is the statutory corporate identifier (no separate TIN is issued); (4) submit a Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) registration or exemption notification to the Revenue and Tax Division to establish the GRT account status; (5) no VAT/TIN registration is possible because the RMI imposes no VAT or GST; (6) if flag-state registration is desired, register the vessel with the RMI Maritime Administrator through its authorized agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI law has no IRC-style check-the-box regime. The controlling election is the non-resident domestic corporation designation under the Business Corporations Act 1990, which renders the entity exempt from RMI income tax and the Gross Revenue Tax. The election must be maintained by ensuring non-resident shareholding and the absence of RMI-source business activity; revocation to taxable resident status is permitted. Hybrid mismatch elections under OECD BEPS Action 2 are not codified in RMI domestic law; however, the interaction with a shareholder's domestic classification (e.g., US Form 8832 check-the-box) and the entity's separate juridical personality under RMI law must be analyzed by counsel to avoid inadvertent double non-taxation or treaty-shopping allegations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

3%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GRT imposes no de minimis turnover threshold: any person carrying on business within the RMI must register and account for GRT on gross revenue. Non-resident domestic corporations deriving no RMI-source revenue are statutorily exempt. Import duty is assessed in USD on physical importation of goods into the RMI under the applicable customs tariff schedule; services and offshore charter hire are outside the import duty base.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The RMI has no VAT or GST, so no VAT zero-rating or refund mechanism exists. Instead, export-derived receipts are exempt at the statutory level: (i) the Gross Revenue Tax Act exempts gross revenue from goods and services exported for use outside the RMI; and (ii) the Income Tax Act excludes non-resident domestic corporations from tax on income sourced outside the RMI. The bareboat or time charter of an RMI-flag offshore supply vessel to a non-resident charterer for operations exclusively in international waters qualifies as an export service, provided the charterer is non-resident, charter-hire payments originate from outside the RMI, and the vessel makes no calls at RMI domestic ports during the tax period.

  • RMI Maritime Administrator: valid Certificate of Registry (Vessel Registration Certificate) and Minimum Safe Manning Document
  • Executed bareboat charter party or time charter party agreement
  • Commercial invoices for charter-hire payments
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency charter receipts
  • Vessel movement log or certificate of no-call at RMI domestic ports for the relevant tax period

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping mandate exists in the RMI. GRT taxpayers must issue standard commercial invoices and retain books and records for a minimum of six years. Customs import declarations are filed with RMI Customs; export of services requires no customs clearance.

The Revenue and Tax Division may audit the non-resident status and foreign-source substantiation of a non-resident domestic corporation. Failure to sustain the exemption may result in reclassification as a resident domestic corporation, retrospective income tax and 3% Gross Revenue Tax assessments for up to six years, statutory civil penalties and monthly interest accruing from the due date on any underpaid tax, fines under the Business Corporations Act for status misdeclaration, potential dissolution of the corporation, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent exemption claims under the Income Tax Act. Retrospective clawback of GRT exemptions applies on a gross-revenue basis, not net of expenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI has no statutory foreign-exchange control regime because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible within the dollar clearing system. Profit remittances, dividends, intercompany payments, and capital liquidations are not subject to foreign-exchange permits or central bank conversion requirements. The only transaction-level friction is anti-money-laundering (AML) reporting and record-keeping, including the obligation to report currency or monetary-instrument movements above the U.S. $10,000 threshold. There are no exchange controls prohibiting the repatriation of charter revenue or the disposal of vessel assets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation in the RMI. Offshore supply-vessel leasing and charter-hire receipts may be retained in U.S. dollars indefinitely without mandatory conversion. The effective surrender ratio is zero percent and there is no legislated deadline for bringing export receipts onshore. Nevertheless, receipts above USD 10,000 in cash must be reported in accordance with the Banking Act 2003 and the RMI Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act; wire transfers must be supported by underlying charter-party or sub-charter documentation to satisfy due diligence.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a domestic central bank or issue a national currency. The de facto monetary and settlement authority is the United States Federal Reserve System as the issuer of the U.S. dollar, which is legal tender in RMI. Domestic financial institution supervision and licensing are conducted by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission in conjunction with the RMI Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low / Not viable as a primary clearing rail. The RMI has no e-money institution or financial-technology EMI licensing framework under domestic law. Digital-only offshore banks and non-resident EMIs cannot become direct participants in the RMI local clearing system and are not recognized by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission as regulated depository institutions. An offshore EMI may only be used as a supplemental treasury-intake vehicle where its parent bank holds a U.S. or comparable OECD license, but it cannot substitute for a physical commercial bank account in RMI for capital escrow, marine-charter settlements, or documentary letters of credit. RMI banks apply enhanced due diligence when non-resident fintech intermediaries are used because of the opacity of downstream customer identification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injections into the RMI Business Corporation should be executed exclusively in U.S. dollars by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account at a regulated bank to the designated Capital Escrow or Operational Account at a RMI commercial bank. The originating bank must include the corporate name, the vessel-identity reference, and the narrative 'Equity contribution to MH_BC offshore supply-vessel leasing under ISIC 5222'. The remitter must be the actual beneficial owner or a named shareholder; third-party payments are prohibited unless supported by a notarized subordination agreement and source-of-funds declaration. Correspondent clearing should route through a U.S. dollar correspondent bank using the receiving bank's BIC, and the RMI bank must verify that the funds do not originate from a sanctioned jurisdiction prior to crediting. A certified copy of the bank credit advice, the SWIFT MT103 execution report, and the source-of-funds letter must be retained in the corporate funds file.Trade disbursements for offshore supply-vessel chartering and operations should be effected through SWIFT MT103 for hire settlements, sub-charter payments, crew payroll, fuel, port fees, and marine insurance premiums. For vessel acquisition, shipyard repairs, or large equipment purchases, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600 is the preferred clearing rail, with settlement at sight or by deferred payment and presentation of the full marine transport document set, including the vessel invoice, delivery certificate, bill of sale, and classification-society compliance certificate. Interbank reimbursements above USD 250,000 should be routed with SWIFT MT202COV to preserve end-to-end originator data. No cash or bearer-instrument payments are permitted for any disbursement connected to vessel chartering.

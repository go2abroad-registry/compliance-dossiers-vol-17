Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Tax Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; no dedicated national e-filing portal (paper or local agent submission via Ministry of Finance / RMI consulate)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation not deriving RMI-source gross revenue is not subject to income tax or Business Privilege Tax and is not required to obtain a local tax identification number. If the entity later establishes a physical place of business or makes on-island sales, it must file a BPT registration with the Revenue and Tax Division, obtain a Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), and comply with social security and wage withholding obligations. For customs importation of 3D printers and filaments, the entity must register as an importer with the Customs Division and may use the BPT TIN as the customs reference number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation not deriving RMI-source gross revenue is not subject to income tax or Business Privilege Tax and is not required to obtain a local tax identification number. If the entity later establishes a physical place of business or makes on-island sales, it must file a BPT registration with the Revenue and Tax Division, obtain a Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), and comply with social security and wage withholding obligations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available in Marshall Islands law; non-resident Business Corporations are categorically outside gross receipts tax jurisdiction unless they engage in a trade or business in the Republic, in which case the Business Privilege Tax Act applies without elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use tax; indirect fiscal levies are limited to customs import duties and the territorial Business Privilege Tax on RMI-source gross revenue

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable to a non-resident BC with no RMI nexus. Where on-island business is undertaken, BPT registration is required immediately upon commencement of business activity; no minimum revenue threshold exempts a non-resident corporation from registration, although a small-scale domestic operator may qualify for a negligible-revenue administrative tolerance (de minimis) not codified in the Business Privilege Tax Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

There is no export VAT zero-rating regime because the Marshall Islands does not impose VAT/GST. Sales of 3D printers and consumables by a non-resident BC to customers outside the Republic are outside the territorial scope of BPT; accordingly, no BPT is charged and no refund mechanism is needed. Export shipments from Marshall Islands waters are free from export duties and are not subject to any domestic substantive tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Manifest
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Foreign Purchase Order or Commercial Invoice
  • Banking evidence of inbound payment (inward remittance certificate) for export proceeds
  • Certificate of Non-Exceptional Cargo (if requested by the RMI Customs Division)

No electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control requirements exist in the Marshall Islands. Imported paper invoices and ordinary shipping documents are acceptable for customs and BPT compliance.

Although no export zero-rating credit is claimable, fraudulent misdescription of exports to evade import duties or BPT can result in civil penalties up to 200% of the tax or duty evaded, interest accrual at the statutory RMI rate, arrest of ships/cargo, and criminal fines plus imprisonment for willful evasion; retrospective assessment is not time-barred under the civil fraud exception.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: the RMI imposes no capital-account or current-account exchange controls, no repatriation licensing, and no withholding friction on dividends, share redemptions, interest, or director fees. USD is the sole legal tender, so no domestic-currency conversion step exists; profits may be remitted offshore without central-bank approval. The only practical convertibility constraint is the OFAC sanctions screening and AML risk appetite of upstream correspondent banks (typically US or Guam), not RMI law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. RMI statutes impose no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. E-commerce receipts from desktop 3D printers, resin filaments, and consumables may be retained 100% in USD in the corporate account, swept offshore, or otherwise deployed at the BC's discretion without any foreign-exchange filing.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI): no indigenous central bank and no dedicated foreign exchange administration exists. The RMI is a US-dollarised jurisdiction under the Compact of Free Association; the United States Federal Reserve System is the de facto anchor of monetary and clearing infrastructure. Bank licensing and prudential supervision are discharged by the Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance & Investment, RMI, under the Banking Act 2000 (as amended).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in U.S. dollars. The RMI has no codified transfer pricing documentation thresholds, no Local File or Master File obligations, and no annual TP return. Arm's-length pricing only becomes indirectly relevant where an entity carries on an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 'relevant activity'; in such cases, contemporaneous records demonstrating arm's-length outcomes for inter-group transactions must be maintained and produced to the RMI Competent Authority on demand.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations Act 2019 (as amended) requires relevant entities to demonstrate adequate substance in the RMI proportionate to their relevant activities. An online retailer of desktop 3D printers and consumables is in scope only if it qualifies as a 'distribution and service centre business', i.e., it purchases goods from, or provides services to, a non-resident group entity. A direct-to-consumer marketplace operator buying from third-party suppliers and selling to independent customers generally falls outside the statutory list of relevant activities; nevertheless, the entity must file annual ESR notification and, where in scope, an ESR return identifying its activity status, and must satisfy the reduced substance test (directed and managed in the RMI, adequate employees, premises, and expenditure) if the activity is fully within RMI scope.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Home-Country Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The RMI will cooperate through Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEAs), the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance, and automatic beneficial-ownership exchange; it levies no domestic penalty itself, but the owner's home jurisdiction may impose criminal tax-evasion penalties of up to 75% of understated tax plus civil fraud penalties, and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for willful evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign or approve false non-resident declarations or disguised dividend flows can be joined as defendants in foreign civil restitution actions, subjected to travel bans by home-country courts, and extradited where the evasion offense constitutes an extraditable crime.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Economic Substance Regulations, a non-compliant relevant entity faces a financial penalty of up to US$10,000 and a continuing penalty of up to US$1,000 per day of default; false ESR declarations constitute a criminal offense. The RMI may also be listed as a non-cooperative jurisdiction by the EU and OECD, triggering defensive countermeasures against the group's broader commercial operations.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly submit false ESR notifications or substance statements commit an offense punishable by imprisonment of up to 12 months and a fine; directors may be disqualified from serving in other RMI entities and held personally liable for penalties if the entity is insolvent.
Failure to File Annual Report and Pay Annual Fee

Statutory sanction: A default penalty equal to the annual fee is assessed immediately; after 90 days of continuing default, the Registrar administratively dissolves the corporation and revokes its Charter, and the corporation's rights to title and assets vest in its directors as statutory trustees. Any contract entered into after dissolution is voidable against the corporation but enforceable personally against the persons who purported to act on its behalf.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and shareholders who transact business after dissolution are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's debts and obligations incurred during that period; creditors may pierce the corporate veil without the usual alter-ego showing because the statutory corporate existence had already ceased.
AML, Sanctions, and Payment-Rail Violations

Statutory sanction: Under RMI AML/CFT law, failure to maintain beneficial-ownership records and to conduct customer due diligence carries fines of up to US$250,000, imprisonment of up to 10 years for directors found to be wilfully blind, and revocation of the registered agent mandate. Separately, the U.S. Treasury OFAC imposes civil penalties up to US$250,000 per violation or twice the value of the transaction for any person facilitating sanctioned trade through U.S. payment rails; criminal liability for sanctions-evasion schemes reaches 20 years of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners exercising direct control over the payment gateway are treated as separate persons by OFAC and can be sanctioned personally; RMI-registered directors who sign suspicious transaction reports or authorize payouts to sanctioned parties face extradition requests, personal asset freezes, and permanent travel bans from allied jurisdictions.

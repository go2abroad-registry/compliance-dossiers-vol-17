Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Division, Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.finance.gov.mh

E-government filing portal

Non-resident business corporations are exempt from Marshall Islands income tax and are not subject to VAT (no VAT regime exists). No TIN or VAT ID is issued for such entities. For evidentiary purposes, the corporation may apply for a Certificate of Non-Resident Tax Exemption from the Ministry of Finance, submitting the Articles of Incorporation, Foreign Investment Permit (if applicable), and a director's resolution confirming offshore activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-resident business corporations are exempt from Marshall Islands income tax and are not subject to VAT (no VAT regime exists). No TIN or VAT ID is issued for such entities. For evidentiary purposes, the corporation may apply for a Certificate of Non-Resident Tax Exemption from the Ministry of Finance, submitting the Articles of Incorporation, Foreign Investment Permit (if applicable), and a director's resolution confirming offshore activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available under Marshall Islands law. The tax classification is fixed by statute: a non-resident business corporation is treated as a tax-exempt foreign entity. No alternative domestic classification can be elected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No National VAT/GST – Marshall Islands Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) limited to domestic commercial activity

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None – no VAT/GST registration regime; non-resident business corporations are not subject to local turnover-based registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Domestically, zero-rating is not applicable because no VAT is levied. For foreign VAT jurisdictions, the entity may claim 0% export VAT on goods or offshore services if it holds valid foreign VAT registration and provides proof of export or cross-border supply as required by the relevant foreign authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate. No digital stamping, API-based reporting, or national e-invoicing infrastructure applies to non-resident business corporations.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are not actionable under Marshall Islands domestic law due to the absence of a VAT regime. If the entity asserts zero-rating in a foreign jurisdiction, penalties may include clawback of refunds, assessment of tax with interest, civil fraud penalties up to 100% of the understated tax, and criminal prosecution under the applicable foreign tax law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands operates a fully dollarized economy with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittance or dividend distribution for non-resident business corporations. The practical constraints are not RMI legal restrictions but the AML/KYC and OFAC compliance policies of the U.S. correspondent banking network through which USD clearing must pass.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines under Marshall Islands law. Export earnings may be freely retained in USD or other currencies in any licensed bank account. Commercial banks may nevertheless ask for proof of the underlying export transaction under AML/CTF obligations.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands has no central bank; the U.S. dollar is legal tender and the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as the de facto monetary authority. Banking and financial institution supervision is conducted by the Banking Commission of the Republic of the Marshall Islands under the Banking Act 1970.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMI services such as Wise B2B may be used as supplementary payment and payout channels for TikTok Shop retail settlement and supplier disbursements, but they are not substitute banks for statutory capital, treasury management, or commercial letters of credit because they do not hold full banking licences in RMI and are not recognised by the Banking Commission as depository institutions. Their use is legally permissible for e-commerce operating transactions, subject to completing the EMI's own KYC/AML review and to U.S. sanctions screening. The core operating account should remain with a RMI-licensed bank or a foreign bank with clear correspondent access.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI levies no income tax and maintains no transfer-pricing statute, no Local File, no Master File, no Country-by-Country Reporting obligation, and no local-currency documentation threshold. The USD-based threshold is therefore nil. If the MH_BC operates through a branch, agent, warehouse, or dependent intermediary in a foreign tax jurisdiction (e.g., the Philippines, Indonesia, or another market), that jurisdiction's transfer-pricing documentation thresholds (Master File, Local File, and CbC reporting) will apply to the foreign taxable presence and must be satisfied in that jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance (Requirements) Regulations 2020 impose an economic-substance test on non-resident domestic corporations carrying on relevant activities within the OECD Inclusive Framework's harmful-tax-practices scope. A TikTok Shop social-commerce retail operation engaged in purchasing goods and reselling through live commerce constitutes a Distribution and Service Center Business and is squarely a relevant activity. Applicability analysis: (1) the corporation is excluded from the full substance test only if it is tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction and subject to an appropriate foreign income tax on the relevant income; (2) if the MH_BC is a 'tax orphan' resident nowhere, it must satisfy in each financial year the directed-and-managed test and the adequacy test comprising physical presence, number of employees, and operating expenditure proportionate to the distribution activity, and must file an annual ESR return with the Registrar; (3) the 'pure holding' reduced-substance regime is unavailable because the entity is performing distribution; (4) outsourcing core income-generating activities to third parties in another jurisdiction is prohibited unless the RMI entity can supervise and control them. In addition, the RMI entity must demonstrate that it does not rely exclusively on foreign-affiliated service providers for its substance. The RMI Registrar administers penalties and can strike off non-compliant companies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Foreign Market Operations

Statutory sanction: The market jurisdiction's revenue authority may reassess the entity for back taxes covering 3 to 10 years (depending on the statute of limitations), impose statutory interest, failure-to-file penalties, failure-to-pay penalties, and late VAT/GST penalties, and refer the matter for criminal tax-evasion prosecution where intent is shown. RMI itself imposes no tax sanction because it has no income tax; RMI consult tax-exempt status confers no exemption from foreign tax laws and cannot be asserted as a defense to foreign assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders who sign false returns, cause settlement funds to be diverted to shell accounts, or knowingly operate through an unregistered permanent establishment may be personally liable for the unpaid foreign tax under civil-assessments provisions of the market jurisdiction, and foreign courts may issue travel bans, asset-freeze orders, and extradition requests. Conviction for tax fraud in the market jurisdiction can lead to imprisonment of the natural-person directors. The RMI corporate veil will not shield deliberate concealment or fraud, and RMI courts will cooperate with foreign letters rogatory in asset-recovery proceedings.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may levy administrative penalties prescribed under the ESR Regulations — commonly up to US$20,000 per contravention with continued daily default penalties on repeated failures — and may compel delivery of ESR returns, financial statements, and records of substance. Continued non-compliance leads to strike-off and dissolution of the corporation, involuntary cancellation of the Certificate of Good Standing, and loss of access to payment rails such as TikTok Shop Payout and Wise B2B, which require an active, good-standing corporate status.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit a materially false ESR declaration, or who intentionally strip the entity of substance to manufacture an exclusion, commit a public offense under RMI law exposing them to personal fines and imprisonment (up to 5 years in aggravated fraudulent cases). RMI courts may pierce the corporate veil on equitable grounds to impose civil restitution, order disqualification of directors from serving on any RMI company, and issue travel-restraint orders where the director attempts to flee the jurisdiction.
AML/CFT Breach and Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may impose fines prescribed under the Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism (Prevention) Act — for corporations, monetary penalties up to US$1,000,000 per violation, and for individuals up to US$100,000 per violation; the Registrar may suspend the registered agent, the company may be struck off, and correspondent banks and licensed payment institutions may terminate the entity's accounts and file suspicious transaction reports. Failure to maintain beneficial ownership records is itself an offense subject to separate penalties.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and officers who knowingly supply false beneficial-ownership information or who facilitate layering or laundering of retail proceeds face criminal prosecution carrying custodial sentences (in aggravated cases up to 10 years per offense), as well as civil confiscation proceedings, freezing orders, and travel bans. Personal criminal liability attaches to the natural perpetrators regardless of the corporation's separate legal personality, and the RMI courts will order restitution of laundered assets.
False Annual Report and Filing Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Business Corporations Act, knowingly making a false statement in any report or document filed with the Registrar constitutes an offense punishable by fine and/or imprisonment as determined by the RMI High Court. The Registrar may strike off the company, impose default surcharges, and issue damaging public notices; counterparties and lenders may bring civil claims for deceit where the false annual report induced credit or contractual performance.

Director personal liability: Each director and officer who signs, certifies, or causes a false filing is personally liable for fines, civil restitution, and the company's unpaid debts where the misrepresentation induced third-party reliance; the RMI courts may disqualify the individual from serving as a director or officer of any RMI-registered company, impose orders restricting departure from the Marshall Islands pending adjudication, and facilitate foreign enforcement through letters rogatory and mutual legal-assistance instruments.

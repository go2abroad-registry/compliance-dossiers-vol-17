Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax and Revenue Division, Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro). Corporate registration is administered separately by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, under the Business Corporations Act. No consolidated online tax portal exists; tax and customs filings are submitted in hard copy or by email to the respective divisions.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no Marshall Islands source income is not required to obtain a national tax identification number (TIN). The RMI does not operate a general TIN issuance regime for non-resident foreign-owned entities. The sole statutory identifier is the corporate registration number issued by the Registrar of Corporations upon incorporation, which also satisfies any bank or payment processor 'tax ID' field requirements when the entity lawfully elects to submit its RMI registration certificate. If the BC later engages local employees, it must register as an employer with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration to obtain a Social Security Number (SSN) for payroll contribution remittances; income tax withholding registration becomes mandatory only upon the commencement of RMI-source employment income payments.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident business corporation; income derived outside the Marshall Islands is exempt from RMI income tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 and the Business Corporations Act; no capital gains tax, withholding tax, estate tax, or franchise tax applies) — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no Marshall Islands source income is not required to obtain a national tax identification number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident business corporation; income derived outside the Marshall Islands is exempt from RMI income tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 and the Business Corporations Act; no capital gains tax, withholding tax, estate tax, or franchise tax applies)

Available election forms

None. The RMI does not provide check-the-box, partnership classification, or hybrid mismatch elections. A non-resident BC may, however, voluntarily elect to conduct RMI-source activities, which would subject it to: (i) gross revenue tax at 3% on gross trading revenue derived from within the RMI, (ii) import duty on physical imports, and (iii) employer obligations for local staff. No advance tax ruling regime exists for such elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax system exists in the Marshall Islands. The only indirect tax is the RMI Import Duty (customs tariff) under the Tariff Act, applied ad valorem to goods physically imported into RMI territory. Cross-border electronic supply of outdoor camping equipment to overseas consumers via Stripe or PayPal is entirely outside the RMI indirect tax net because no importation into the RMI occurs.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax is imposed. Import duty liability attaches to each physical shipment entering the RMI, irrespective of importer turnover, and is borne by the consignee or importer of record within the RMI; the duty rate varies by HS tariff line (general merchandise benchmark 8%, with specific rates up to 100% for alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and selected luxury goods).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. As the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT or GST, there is no export zero-rating regime, no refund mechanism, and no VAT registration that could generate input VAT credits. Goods sold online by the non-resident BC to overseas customers are not subject to any RMI indirect tax at the point of sale. The only RMI customs event that could arise is a physical importation into the RMI, for which import duty is the sole levy.

Customs declarations are processed by the RMI Customs Service (Ministry of Finance) in paper form or via email; no e-invoicing platform, clearance portal, or interoperability requirement applies to the non-resident BC or its payment processors.

General customs enforcement under the RMI Tariff Act applies only to physical imports: misdeclaration of value, classification, or origin exposes the importer to forfeiture of goods, fines of up to three times the duty evaded, and potential suspension of the importer's customs privileges. The non-resident BC selling exclusively to foreign consumers assumes no RMI indirect tax audit exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI imposes no exchange controls, no exchange-rate bands, and no registration requirement for capital- or current-account transactions under the Marshall Islands Revised Code. Foreign direct investment, capital injections, profit remittances, dividends, share redemptions, intra-group loans, and trade payments are fully convertible and unrestricted because the domestic financial system is fully dollarized and there is no local currency to convert. Practical impediments arise only at the bank level through correspondent-bank KYC, OFAC screening, and FATCA documentation, not through statutory convertibility limits or profit-repatriation hurdles. Non-resident shareholders of a non-resident Business Corporation may freely remit dividends or liquidating distributions abroad without central bank approval or exchange-control pricing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligations exist. The RMI has no statutory requirement to convert, repatriate, or remit export proceeds within a specified timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. E-commerce proceeds collected by the outdoor-camping-goods online retailer through Stripe/PayPal can be settled directly in USD to an RMI or U.S. correspondent bank account without an export declaration, a central bank authorization, or a currency-conversion certificate.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no domestic central bank and no currency-issuing authority. Banking supervision, AML/CFT oversight, and the licensing of banks are vested in the Banking Commissioner acting through the RMI Banking Commission, an agency within the Ministry of Finance. The U.S. dollar (USD) functions as the sole official legal tender under the Compact of Free Association framework, making the U.S. Federal Reserve and U.S. correspondent banking infrastructure the de facto monetary and clearing authorities for all RMI-dollar transactions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective rate for the non-resident MH_BC (no Marshall Islands-source income); statutory MITTA graduated CIT schedule of 3% to 11% (top marginal rate 11%) applies only where a corporation derives income sourced within the Marshall Islands.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders; no DTA relief is required because the domestic statutory rate is already zero.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Annual Report and franchise tax remittance due on the anniversary-based schedule: on or before the last day of the month containing the anniversary of incorporation, which for a 31 December fiscal year-end approximates a 90-day post-year-end deadline under the Business Corporations Act annual report provisions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 - none prescribed. The Marshall Islands Income Tax Act contains no transfer-pricing documentation regime, and a non-resident exempt BC has no obligation to prepare or file a Local File or Master File with any RMI authority. Transfer-pricing exposure originates exclusively in market-country audits, e.g., where internet retailing activities create a permanent establishment or dependent agent in a foreign taxing jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations modeled on BVI or Cayman law because its source-based Income Tax Act already confines tax exposure to RMI-source income. Nevertheless, international standards (OECD BEPS, EU listing criteria, FATF/APG evaluations) require demonstrable substance: a licensed Registered Agent, a genuine registered office in Majuro, maintained statutory records, board minutes, and commercial rationale for the structure. A purely passive shell with undisclosed ultimate beneficial owners exposes the BC to CRS/FATCA penalties, Registered Agent termination, administrative strike-off, and potential recharacterisation by foreign courts as a sham or transparent entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Corporation

Statutory sanction: In the RMI: revocation of the certificate for false non-resident certification, loss of limited liability, and full MITTA assessment on any undeclared RMI-source trade. In the market jurisdiction: back-assessment of CIT and VAT, penalties up to 100% of tax avoided, daily interest, criminal fraud sanctions, and recharacterisation of the MH entity as a permanent establishment, sham, or mere conduit.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil where the BC is used to defraud tax authorities or creditors: directors held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and restitution; foreign courts may impose director disqualification, cross-border travel bans, asset freezes, and custodial sentences for tax evasion where the director structured or directed the sham invoicing arrangement.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Shortfall

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution or strike-off by the Registrar for failure to file the Annual Report or pay the franchise tax; CRS/FATCA reporting penalties for undisclosed RMI accounts; denial of banking due-diligence; and clawback of the limited-liability shield for obligations contracted while suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for debts and obligations contracted while the corporation is in default or suspended; disqualification as a director in the RMI and cooperating foreign registers; direct liability for AML/CTF failures where the director knowingly permitted the shell to intermediate third-party proceeds.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Marshall Islands Money Laundering Control Act and Criminal Code: criminal fines up to the value of the laundered property, forfeiture of funds, freezing of the BC's accounts, and imprisonment; banking partners may file Suspicious Transaction Reports with the Banking Commissioner and terminate the payment-rail relationship.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers individually prosecutable as principals or accomplices; civil forfeiture of personal assets, travel bans pending investigation, extradition exposure to the requesting state, and equivalent liability for nominee directors who sign or execute transactions without conducting due diligence.
False Statements and Unlicensed Conduct

Statutory sanction: Penalty under the Business Corporations Act for false filings; perjury-type fines; summary revocation of registration; restitution orders; and automatic forfeiture of the exempt non-resident status, making the entity fully assessable under the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false filings incur personal fines, director disqualification, and civil restitution of taxes plus interest; foreign courts may impose custodial sanctions for tax fraud where the false certification was used to underpin cross-border tax avoidance or treaty abuse.

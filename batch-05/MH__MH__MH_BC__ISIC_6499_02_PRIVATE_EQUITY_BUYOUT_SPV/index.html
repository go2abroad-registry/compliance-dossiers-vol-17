Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Division, Ministry of Finance and Administration (no separate income tax agency); official government portal: https://www.mof.gov.mh

E-government filing portal

The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no national income tax, no VAT, and no general corporate tax, so no statutory TIN/VAT ID is issued to a non-resident Business Corporation. A foreign entity obtains registration by filing Articles of Incorporation with the RMI Registrar of Corporations (through the RMI Corporate Registry/Maritime Administrator) and paying the annual registration fee. If the entity later imports goods, it must obtain a Customs Importer Code from the Revenue and Customs Division. A local business license is separately obtained for any substantive commercial presence in the RMI.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI maintains no exchange-control regime and no domestic currency. Both current-account and capital-account items are freely convertible in U.S. dollars, and profit remittances, debt-service payments, redemption proceeds and cross-border buyout distributions may be exported without central-bank approval or FX licensing. Practical remittance hurdles are operational only: bank-level AML/KYC verification, OFAC/UN sanctions screening, FATCA-related information exchange and contractual or security restrictions imposed by syndicated lending documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, no minimum percentage and no mandatory surrender obligation for export receipts in the RMI. Foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained, converted at commercial-bank rates or used directly to settle trade obligations. This rule is neutral for the private-equity buyout SPV because investment contributions and distributions are treated as capital flows and are not subject to export-proceeds surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, and the United States Federal Reserve System is the de facto monetary authority for Federal Reserve notes in circulation in the RMI. The statutory banking and financial-institution supervisory function is discharged by the Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands under the Banking Act 1989. There is no separate foreign exchange administration or exchange-control body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The RMI does not maintain a separate electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing class. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not permitted to take deposits or provide custodial, escrow or treasury services unless separately licensed under the Banking Act 1989 or the Trust Companies Act 2004 and approved by the Banking Commissioner. A non-resident digital EMI account is not a viable settlement rail for syndicated loan wires or leveraged-buyout disbursements and may be categorised as an unlicensed money-transmission channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions and syndicated loan proceeds must arrive via authenticated SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) in U.S. dollars to the SPV's segregated capital-escrow or operating account. The remitting bank must include the SPV's full legal name and RMI entity or LEI reference in beneficiary instructions, place 'MH_BC PE BUYOUT SPV' and the transaction type in tag 70, and provide SWIFT GPI tracking confirmation. No RMI exchange-control approval is required; USD clearing is completed through the receiving bank's U.S. correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS. The receiving bank verifies each remitter against the approved investor and lender list and rejects unidentified or third-party wires. The transaction narrative or purpose code should state equity contribution or shareholder loan to a non-resident buyout SPV.Outbound settlement uses SWIFT MT103 for ordinary vendor, legal, advisory and lender payments; MT202 COV for correspondent cover; MT700 and MT707 for commercial letters of credit; and internal book transfer or USD bank draft for on-island payments. Commercial letters of credit, where used, are issued and advised in accordance with UCP600 and ISBP745, supported by the underlying contract, commercial invoice, transport documents and bank-authorised reimbursement instructions. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (the Republic of the Marshall Islands levies no corporate income tax; the Income Tax Act has been repealed and a non-resident Business Corporation pays no tax on worldwide, foreign-source, or RMI-source income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a non-resident Business Corporation, irrespective of the recipient's residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - the RMI has no transfer-pricing statute and prescribes no Local File, Master File, or CbC thresholds in local currency (USD). By contrast, the International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply where the SPV carries on a Relevant Activity (including finance and leasing, fund management, or holding-company business) and impose annual substance documentation and filing obligations.

Economic substance scope: The International Tax Compliance Act 2018 and the ESR Regulations 2019 require any RMI company carrying on a Relevant Activity to satisfy economic substance in the RMI. A pure equity holding company qualifies for the reduced substance test: adequate employees and premises proportionate to the holding of equity participations plus management and control in the RMI. A PE buyout SPV that extends intra-group financing or performs fund-management functions (managing investor assets) is subject to the full substance test, including core income-generating activities performed in the RMI, senior management presence, and annual ESR filing with the Registrar. Non-compliance is reported to the RMI tax-transparency authorities and may trigger information exchange with OECD jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Offences of false statements and perjury under the RMI Criminal Code and the BCA are punishable by fines and/or imprisonment; the Registrar may strike the company off the Register and invalidate issued certificates; fraudulent filings with foreign tax authorities additionally expose the SPV to the home-state penalties of the beneficial owners.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or procure false filings are personally liable to fines and custodial sentences; the High Court may impose restraint and departure-prohibition (travel ban) orders pending criminal proceedings and order civil restitution of diverted funds.
Shell Company Abuse and Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, the Registrar may impose a financial penalty of up to USD 50,000 (with daily penalties for continuing non-compliance), strike the entity off the Register, and exchange adverse information with foreign tax authorities under the OECD transparency framework.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent may be individually fined for causing a false ESR declaration; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a sham, exposing directors and controlling PE sponsors to personal restitution, asset seizure, and travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Money Laundering Control Act 2012 (as amended), failure to maintain AML/CFT controls, customer due diligence, and suspicious-transaction reporting attracts fines of up to USD 100,000 per violation and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years; the proceeds of prohibited arrangements are subject to civil and criminal forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers with knowledge of or wilful blindness to laundering activity are prosecutable as principals; the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze bank accounts, impose departure bans, and seek confiscation orders against personal and corporate estates.
Beneficial Ownership and Corporate Secrecy Violation

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the RMI Beneficial Ownership Rules is an offence punishable by Registrar-imposed monetary penalties, administrative dissolution, and loss of Good Standing; deliberate concealment engages criminal liability under RMI law and adverse reporting to foreign competent authorities.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and the registered agent bear individual liability for each default, including fines and disqualification; the RMI authorities may also transmit UBO intelligence to investor home states, triggering foreign civil and criminal enforcement against the PE sponsors.
Director Fiduciary Breach and Unlawful Distributions (Veil Piercing)

Statutory sanction: Under the Delaware-derived BCA, directors are liable to the corporation and its creditors for unlawful distributions and must restore amounts paid with interest; courts may award consequential and exemplary damages and disregard limited liability where the SPV operates as an instrumentality of the sponsor.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil exposes directors and controlling shareholders to civil restitution, disgorgement of profits, and personal liability for syndicated debt; fraudulent transfers trigger criminal fraud charges, travel bans, and imprisonment under the RMI Criminal Code.
Cross-Border Interest Stripping and Transfer-Price Contamination

Statutory sanction: No RMI domestic TP penalty applies, but the target jurisdiction will impose its own sanctions: interest-deduction denial (e.g., US section 163(j)), CFC and GILTI inclusions, transfer-pricing adjustments, penalties on understated income, and OECD MAP/AEOI enforcement; RMI authorities cooperate by exchanging SPV financial information.

Director personal liability: Fund managers and sponsor representatives who certify false loan terms or financial statements to lenders face personal liability for the resulting tax underpayments, restitution to the injured target company, and professional disqualification in the investors' home jurisdictions.

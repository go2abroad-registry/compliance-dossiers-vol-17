Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance (RMI Treasury) — Revenue Division and Customs Service; corporate registration authority is the Registrar of Corporations (Office of the Registrar of Corporations, portal: https://www.rmioc.com). Payroll/social tax registration authority: Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA). Foreign investment licensing authority: Foreign Investment Division, Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (FIBLA regime).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation (BC) under the Business Corporations Act (BCA) 1990 via a licensed Registered Agent, reserving name, filing Articles of Incorporation, and paying the initial license fee (US$200 base, plus US$50 per US$1,000,000 or fraction of authorized capital above US$10,000,000). Step 2: Obtain from the Registrar a Certificate of Incorporation and a BCA annual license/tax identification account number (functions as the entity's corporate ID for fee filings). Step 3: If any share is owned by a non-citizen or the BC will transact locally, procure a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBLA) for the air cargo charter brokerage activity; this license carries a supplementary 'Registration Certificate' number used by the Customs Service and the Treasury. Step 4: If the BC hires employees (foreign or local), register as an employer with MISSA to obtain an Employer Registration Number (ERN); MISSA issues the only operational 'taxpayer' identifier in RMI, used for social security wage deductions and the Local Workers' Insurance contributions. Step 5: Register with the RMI Customs Service for an importer/exporter of record account (ASYCUDA World) for any physical aircraft parts, equipment, or consumables landed in-country; no income tax TIN is issued to non-resident BCs because the RMI does not impose a general corporate income tax on foreign-source business income. Step 6: Maintain the annual BCA renewal (due each anniversary of incorporation) and the annual FIBLA renewal (due on the license anniversary date); payment is made by wire transfer (Swift Wire/Fedwire) to the Registrar's designated commercial bank. There is no locus standi, minimum capital requirement, or physical office obligation, and no notarized tax residency certificate is required for foreign charter broker operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: The non-resident Business Corporation is statutorily distinct from a resident taxpayer. RMI Income Tax Act (territorial) taxes only income sourced within the Republic. A BC that neither carries on trade in the RMI, nor owns real property there, nor employs locally resident personnel is taxed at 0% on all charter brokerage commission, agency fees, and freight forwarding revenue. The BC is, however, subject to: (a) fixed annual BCA license fee; (b) FIBLA annual fee (US$500–US$2,500 depending on capital); (c) MISSA employer contributions if personnel are engaged; (d) import duties only on physical goods physically cleared in RMI; and (e) a US$1,500 annual registered-agent fee. No capital gains tax, no branch profits tax, no dividend withholding tax, no interest withholding tax, and no net-worth tax apply.

Available election forms

Check-the-Box: Not available; RMI does not operate a US-style entity classification election. Territorial election: A BC may voluntarily file a notice with the Treasury to be treated as having a permanent establishment in RMI (e.g., by maintaining a fixed place of business in Majuro). This election makes only RMI-source income taxable at the rates of the Income Tax Act (effectively a 0%–12% bracket on RMI-source net taxable income after expenses) and triggers the 'Employer Tax' / wage withholding obligations; it does not create worldwide taxation. Hybrid mismatch: RMI has not adopted OECD BEPS hybrid rules or a hybrid mismatch election regime; therefore a BC can rely on local substance documents (board minutes, charter party evidence, commission agreements) rather than filing any hybrid-figures election. Tax holiday: The Minister of Finance is authorized to grant a Section 11 exemption certificate for qualifying enterprises; air charter brokerage is eligible only if the broker additionally operates a licensed aviation support facility in RMI. Non-application: MISSA chapters require an employer election only for the computation of 'average annual wages' at the US$ threshold, not for entity-level tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT, no GST, and no general sales and use tax. The only indirect levies are: (1) customs import duties on landed goods under the Customs Tariff Act 2000 (rates predominantly 0%–25% ad valorem, average 8%); (2) the airport and departure taxes (US$20 per departing passenger) and aviation fuel tax (US$0.25 per US gallon for jet fuel used in domestic service; international flights are exempt); (3) MISSA contributions (employer 10%, employee 6% of gross wages) which function as a payroll-based indirect cost; and (4) a 3% municipal tax on the taxable turnover of importers/retailers in the Majuro and Kwajalein 'urban' zones, inapplicable to wholesale charter brokerage services performed for non-resident principals.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

$0.00 — no turnover threshold exists because no VAT/GST registration regime exists in RMI. Indirect registration obligations arise only when: (a) a BC is an 'importer of record' (any value of goods physically entered into RMI customs territory, requiring a customs clearing account and ASYCUDA World profile); (b) a BC is an employer (MISSA registration triggered by first wage payment, irrespective of quantum); or (c) a BC rents/owns a physical premises in Majuro/Kwajalein for a fixed place of business (which would trigger the municipality's gross receipts registration). For a purely non-resident air cargo charter broker with no physical presence, no threshold is crossed; the BCA annual license fee is fixed and not turnover-based.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the strict VAT sense — RMI has no VAT, so there is no '0% export VAT rate' or input VAT credit mechanism. On the customs front, goods exported, re-exported, or transshipped through RMI (including aircraft operating supplies, spare engines, and air cargo consignments moving under international through-bill of lading) are outside the charging provision of the Customs Tariff Act and are classified as 'duty-neutral exports' with no refund filing required. For the air cargo charter brokerage service itself: commissions, brokerage fees, and cargo charter hire service fees earned from non-resident airlines, charterers, or freight forwarders will be foreign-source income under the RMI territorial principle, and are zero-rated by operation of law (0% tax) rather than by a refund claim. The broker may, however, elect to apply for a Minister of Finance 'Certificate of Non-Residence' (pursuant to Section 30 of the Income Tax Act) to evidence to counterparty financial institutions and the US Internal Revenue Service that no RMI withholding or income tax attaches, such that a 15% US FATCA/non-qualified intermediary withholding is reduced only by treaty or by the payor's independent review; the certificate is obtained free of charge from the Treasury upon submission of the charter broker's non-resident declaration and a copy of its BCA registration.

  • Customs Export / Transshipment Declaration (ASYCUDA World Customs Form EX-1) — required only if cargo or regulated aircraft parts physically cross the RMI customs frontier under broker control
  • Air Waybill (Master and House, issued by the underlying contract air carrier) or Interline Cargo Transfer Manifest — evidencing the international movement of the freight
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — showing the charter broker's commission separately on a strict agency basis, with the underlying cargo owner as the principal
  • Charter Party Agreement / Brokerage Commission Agreement (signed by the non-resident charterer or operator) — substantiating the offshore source of the brokerage income and the broker's role as intermediary rather than principal, together with SWIFT/Fedwire inward remittance advice from the principal's overseas bank

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping regime exists in RMI. The Customs Service operates the ASYCUDA World system for import/export declarations and duty accounting; filings are submitted electronically by licensed customs brokers on a declaration-by-declaration basis, but clearance is not continuous and does not require invoice-level data validation by fiscal authorities. For aviation fuel invoiced to international air carriers, a paper 'Exempt Supply Certificate' (Form CI-AV) signed by the airline's registered agent triggers duty-free clearing without an electronic fiscal marker. MISSA employer remittances are filed quarterly on paper forms or by direct upload of the Employers' Schedule; no government-issued digital signature is required. A business is permitted to issue self-printed commercial invoices and charter party invoices denominated in USD, provided they carry the entity's BCA registration number and, where applicable, the FIBLA license number; these invoices are immigration, AML, and counterparty due diligence documents, not tax clearance documents.

Not applicable to 'VAT zero-rating' since no VAT system exists. Nonetheless, audit and clawback risks attach to the customs and licensing archetypes: (1) If a BC misdescribes cargo as transshipment/export to evade import duty, the Customs Tariff Act 2000 authorizes assessment of the duty plus a penalty equal to 100% of the evaded duty, forfeiture of the goods or aircraft stores, and summary criminal fines up to US$5,000 per false declaration; a second offense raises the maximum to US$10,000 or 12 months' imprisonment. (2) For the export income characterization, if a BC represents itself as non-resident but is found to maintain a fixed place of business in RMI, the Treasury may retrospectively reassess RMI-source taxable income for up to three prior open years at the Income Tax Act rates (up to 12%) and impose an interest charge of 1% per month on underpayments; no fraud penalty beyond that applies. (3) The Registrar of Corporations may administratively revoke the BCA charter for an annual license fee default of more than 60 days; reinstatement costs a US$500 reinstatement fee plus unpaid annual fees, and anticipated clawback exposure for contracts signed without valid charter (the BC is null-void for treaty and legal capacity purposes during the gap). (4) FIBLA non-renewal or activity outside the licensed scope carries a US$2,500 civil penalty plus the potential cancellation of the license, which automatically renders subsequent charter brokerage commissions outside the statutory exemption and subjects the BC to retroactive local-business tax on those commissions at the 3% municipal rate. (5) MISSA audit can claw back unreported wages for 3 years, adding 10% late penalty and 1% monthly interest; willful non-registration exposes directors to US$500–US$1,000 fines per person. (6) For fraud-related AML scrutiny, the Financial Intelligence Unit can file a suspicious activity report; this does not impose a tax clawback but will jeopardize the BC's banking relationship with the corresponding USD clearing banks required for Swift Wire and Fedwire settlement rails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility; the RMI has no foreign exchange control legislation, no inward/outward licensing, no repatriation permit, and no dividend/remittance tax on non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are settled in U.S. dollars through the U.S. payments system; the only practical hurdle is obtaining a commercial bank relationship and meeting correspondent-bank due diligence standards. A non-resident Business Corporation chartering air cargo aircraft should maintain documented economic substance (charter contracts, operating cost records) to avoid bank refusal even though the jurisdiction itself imposes no FX restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in the RMI. Air cargo charter brokerage fees and commissions may be billed and collected in USD or any freely traded currency; no central bank certificate, trade settlement declaration, or repatriation timing deadline is imposed. Funds may be held offshore or onshore indefinitely for financing future charter hire or operating costs.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands has no domestic central bank; it operates under a currency union with the U.S. dollar pursuant to the Compact of Free Association. Foreign exchange and banking regulation is administered by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (Banking Commissioner) within the Ministry of Finance and Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. The RMI Banking Act 1991 authorizes only licensed banks, and the Banking Commission does not license foreign electronic money institutions as deposit-taking entities. Digital EMI accounts opened with offshore providers (e.g., U.K., EU, or Hong Kong EMIs) are not recognized clearing destinations for RMI regulated trade, are generally rejected by U.S. correspondent banks, and expose the broker to unlicensed money service business risk. Use only a U.S. correspondent-backed commercial bank or a directly licensed RMI/COSA bank account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. The RMI Banking Act 1991 authorizes only licensed banks, and the Banking Commission does not license foreign electronic money institutions as deposit-taking entities. Digital EMI accounts opened with offshore providers (e.g., U.K., EU, or Hong Kong EMIs) are not recognized clearing destinations for RMI regulated trade, are generally rejected by U.S. correspondent banks, and expose the broker to unlicensed money service business risk. Use only a U.S. correspondent-backed commercial bank or a directly licensed RMI/COSA bank account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective RMI corporate income tax for a non-resident Business Corporation; nominal statutory rate is 3% of gross income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands under the Income Tax Act 1989, as amended.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold is prescribed in the RMI; the official currency is USD. A non-resident charter broker that transacts with related airlines or cargo principals must nevertheless maintain contemporaneous intercompany agreements, benchmarking studies, and commission-rate justifications sufficient for Economic Substance Regulations review and general anti-avoidance examination.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Act 2021 and Economic Substance Regulations require entities carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate direction and management in the RMI, adequate premises, employees, expenditure, and performance of core income-generating activities in the RMI. Air cargo charter brokerage to unrelated third parties is generally outside the listed relevant activities; however, a BC brokering exclusively for a related international air cargo group may be characterized as a distribution and service centre or a financing and leasing activity and must file an economic substance return and meet substance tests.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Income Tax Act 1989

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution for willful evasion with fines and imprisonment under ITA 1989; civil fraud penalty of up to 50% of the underpayment plus statutory interest; forfeiture of the tax benefit and compulsory restitution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or orchestrate revenue diversion are jointly and severally liable for the underpayment, face criminal custodial sentences, and may be subjected to travel bans and extradition requests while the investigation is pending.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to USD 10,000 under the Economic Substance Act 2021, with a recurring monthly penalty for continued non-compliance; deregistration of the BC; referral to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit as a shell-company risk.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly facilitating a substance sham face personal fines, disqualification from acting as directors of any RMI entity, and imprisonment under the economic substance enforcement regime; the corporate veil is pierced.
AML / Wire Payment Abuse

Statutory sanction: Money laundering felony with fines up to USD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years; seizure and civil forfeiture of aircraft charter payments; revocation of bank or money services licenses; designation on the RMI FIU risk list.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, benefit from, or knowingly ignore suspicious cargo-charter payments are personally liable for civil forfeiture, subject to travel bans and asset freezes, and face custodial sentences; the limited-liability shield is unavailable for sham transactions.
Corporate Registry Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution by the RMI Registrar; loss of capacity to sue or defend in RMI courts; late filing penalty of USD 100; reinstatement requires payment of all arrears and a fresh certificate of good standing.

Director personal liability: After dissolution, directors are personally liable for debts contracted in the corporate name and the statutory limited-liability protection is void against creditors and third-party claimants under the Business Corporations Act.

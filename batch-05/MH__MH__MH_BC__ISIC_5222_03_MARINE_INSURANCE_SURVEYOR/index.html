Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance, Revenue Division (Tax Administration and Collection); corporate registration administered by the Office of the Attorney General, Registrar of Corporations; official portal: www.rmi-finance.com and the Pacific Islands Electronic Corporate Registry (external service provider).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (RMI) is issued a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a statutory corporate registry number, which serves as the primary legal identifier. If the BC derives gross receipts sourced within the Republic, it must apply to the Revenue Division of the Ministry of Finance for a Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) registration, obtain a GRT taxpayer identification number, and file GRT returns. If the BC conducts no business within the RMI and has no RMI-source income, it may instead file a Non-Resident Domestic (NRD) election with the Registrar of Corporations, formalizing exemption from GRT. A Business Licence under the Business Licence Act is separately required before commencing any licensed marine surveying or insurance-adjacent activity within RMI territorial limits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or OECD hybrid-mismatch election exists. Available statutory elections under RMI law: (a) Non-Resident Domestic (NRD) election under Section 7 of the Business Corporations Act, effected by board resolution filed with the Registrar of Corporations and published, rendering all foreign-source income (including offshore marine survey fees) exempt from GRT; (b) affirmative GRT registration election for a BC that elects to conduct RMI-source business and be taxed on gross receipts at 3%; (c) election to operate as an exempt international shipping or marine-insurance auxiliary entity under the Insurance Act 2007 where survey fees derive from ocean-going vessels in international commerce; (d) no hybrid mismatch election is recognized, as the RMI has not adopted OECD hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Sales and Use Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The RMI imposes no VAT or GST. The closest indirect levies are the 3% Gross Receipts Tax (GRT) under the Revenue and Taxation Act 1999 and customs import duties under the RMI Customs Tariff Act 1993. GRT registration is mandatory from the first US dollar of gross receipts derived from business activity carried on within the Republic (no de minimis threshold); a wholly offshore marine surveyor with no RMI-source receipts has no GRT registration obligation. Import-duty registration applies only to importers of dutiable physical goods and is immaterial to export marine survey services. Functional currency: USD.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As the RMI imposes no VAT or GST, no VAT zero-rating claim is available or required. Under GRT law, income from marine insurance surveying, vessel hull and cargo damage inspection, and claims verification services falls outside the RMI tax base when (i) the services are physically performed outside RMI territorial waters or on board foreign-flag vessels in international transit, and (ii) consideration is paid by a non-resident underwriter or owner for offshore-sourced services. An NRD-elected BC achieves a structural de facto 0% rate; a GRT-registered BC may claim the territorial exclusion on its GRT return by allocating offshore gross receipts to extranational service categories. Substantiation requires service-performance location evidence (surveyor travel logs, vessel position records, interview/GPS data), non-resident counterparty identification, and the foreign-currency invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Cargo Export Entry; applicable where survey equipment or samples accompany the assignment, otherwise N/A for pure services)
  • Bill of Lading (vessel eBL or ocean carrier service contract evidencing international movement of the surveyed cargo or vessel)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (USD wire creditor advice bearing the foreign drawer and matching the marine survey invoice reference)

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The RMI Customs Service operates an electronic customs declaration system for goods, but it does not serve as an indirect-tax invoice clearance mechanism. Marine surveyors issuing invoices to foreign underwriter clients retain paper or PDF invoices and are not subject to any fiscal digitalization mandate.

The Revenue Division may audit territorial allocations or NRD status under the Revenue and Taxation Act 1999 and the Business Corporations Act. Improper exclusion of RMI-source receipts is treated as under-reporting: failure-to-file and failure-to-pay penalties accrue at 10% per month of the overdue tax, statutory interest runs on the balance, and a civil fraud penalty of 50% of the understated GRT may be imposed where false territorial certifications, fabricated survey logs, or fictitious Bills of Lading are produced. Criminal enforcement includes fines up to USD 10,000 and imprisonment for tax evasion; NRD status may be revoked retroactively upon proof of deception, the certificate of good standing is suspended, and all marine warranty-survey licensing renewals are blocked pending full clawback and penalty settlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains a fully dollarized monetary system with no separate domestic legal tender and no exchange-control apparatus. Capital and current account transactions are freely convertible into United States dollars and foreign currencies through the licensed international banking network. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, liquidation proceeds, and royalty payments are not subject to central bank approval, exchange permits, or repatriation taxes at the treasury level. The jurisdiction accepts the convertibility obligations attendant to IMF Article VIII, and there are no statutory barriers to cross-border capital flows for a non-resident Business Corporation engaged in marine insurance claim surveying.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed repatriation timeline. Because the United States dollar is the domestic currency, proceeds earned in USD require no conversion and may be retained offshore or in local accounts at the entity's discretion. For any incidental foreign-currency receipts, the RMI does not impose a compulsory conversion; such funds may be held as foreign-currency deposits with licensed banks or converted via normal banking channels.

Central bank supervision: No indigenous central bank; the official medium of exchange is the United States dollar under the Constitution of the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Prudential banking and foreign-exchange-related supervision is vested in the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (under the Banking Act 1987, as amended), with day-to-day monitoring delegated to the Ministry of Finance and the Insurance and Pensions Regulator where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise or licensed cryptocurrency-fiat gateways may be used as auxiliary settlement rails for low-value marine survey disbursements, travel advances, and contractor payments. However, the RMI Banking Act imposes a statutory licensing requirement for deposit-taking institutions onshore; an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot lawfully hold escrow funds, provide a regulated capital account, or act as the primary correspondent for the marine insurance business. Legal constraints include the need to pass OFAC sanctions screening, beneficial ownership disclosure, and the risk that EMI-denominated virtual accounts may not be recognised as bank accounts for RMI statutory licensing and insurance-surveyor capitalisation purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or capital contributions to the non-resident Business Corporation may be routed by international SWIFT wire using MT103 for receipt of directly traceable premium-receivable-bearing funds or MT202 for interbank treasury transfers. Compliant practice is to include a payment purpose code consistent with the IMF Balance of Payments classification for direct investment and capital transfers (e.g., 'C3' equity investment or the relevant ISO 20022 'CAPC' supplementary code), together with a corporate resolution approving the equity injection and a certified board minute referencing the marine insurance surveyor business plan. Verification consists of matching the remitter with the corporate register, obtaining a bank debit confirmation from the sending institution, and retaining a source-of-capital statement from the shareholder where the funding exceeds repatriation thresholds. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — non-resident domestic Business Corporation (MH_BC) is fully exempt from Marshall Islands income tax on foreign-source income; a 3% gross revenue tax applies exclusively to gross revenues derived from sources within the Marshall Islands (Income Tax Act 1989, Title 54, Section 179).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions declared by a Business Corporation to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the Marshall Islands imposes no transfer pricing documentation regime and has no local file or master file thresholds; the RMI gross revenue tax operates on a gross receipts basis with no arm's length adjustment mechanism. Prudent practice: for intercompany contracts with affiliated underwriters, maintain contemporaneous commercial agreements, fee schedules, and survey reports demonstrating arm's length fee levels, even though no statutory documentation threshold applies.

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are currently in force in the Marshall Islands for foreign-owned non-resident corporations, and the RMI is not listed on the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions. However, an MH_BC engaged in marine insurance surveying must maintain minimum substance: a licensed RMI registered agent, a physical registered office in Majuro or Ebeye, current annual corporate statement filings, and actual operational records (contracts, claims inspection reports, vessel survey logs, and bank account statements) sufficient to rebut any inference of being a shell company by counterparty banks, underwriters, or foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of 50% of the underpaid gross revenue tax plus statutory interest at the prescribed annual rate; criminal fine up to $10,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years under the RMI Income Tax Act 1989; forfeiture of any unlawful tax benefit claimed.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns or knowingly cause concealment of income are jointly and severally liable for civil fraud penalties and may be subject to criminal prosecution, travel bans, and asset freezing orders issued by the RMI High Court.
Shell Company Non-Filing and Administrative Dissolution

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution by the Registrar of Corporations; loss of corporate name and good standing; contracts and legal actions by the dissolved corporation become voidable; reinstatement requires payment of all current and accrued annual fees, a 10% monthly late surcharge, a reinstatement fee, and re-registration formalities.

Director personal liability: After dissolution, directors who continue to transact business in the name of the dissolved corporation incur personal liability for all resulting debts under BCA Section 122; courts will pierce the corporate veil and impose civil restitution of any fees collected from underwriters.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Corporate monetary penalties under the RMI AML/CTF framework and Banking Act of up to $150,000 per violation; inclusion of the entity on financial institution risk lists; freezing of bank accounts; potential suspension or revocation of the corporation's existence for deliberate disregard of AML obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal fines up to $50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for predicate money laundering offenses; courts may impose personal disqualification from serving as directors or officers of any RMI entity and issue travel restrictions pending resolution of enforcement proceedings.
Unlicensed Marine Insurance Surveying Operations

Statutory sanction: Cessation orders and monetary fines under the RMI Business License Act; administrative nullification of survey certificates issued without a license; denial of evidentiary weight to such certificates in RMI insurance claims litigation; suspension of good standing status until the appropriate license is obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly conduct unlicensed surveying operations are personally liable for restitution of all fees collected, litigation costs, and legal damages arising from invalidated survey reports; criminal charges may trigger bail conditions, international travel bans, and, upon conviction, disqualification from professional practice in the maritime sector.

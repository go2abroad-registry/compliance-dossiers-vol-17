Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance – Taxation Division; taxpayer portal and registration via the Marshall Islands Business Registry (https://www.businessregistrymarshallislands.com) and the Marco Island office, with TIN issuance administered by the Ministry of Finance in coordination with the Registrar of Corporations

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to engage in any taxable business activity in the Marshall Islands must first incorporate or register a foreign entity with the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act or the Foreign Investment Business Act, obtain a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) from the Foreign Investment Board, then file a Taxpayer Registration Application with the Ministry of Finance – Taxation Division; the Ministry will issue a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) or VAT ID as applicable; representative branch operations require a registered local address and resident agent; non-resident corporations with no domestic-source activity are treated as offshore entities and may obtain a TIN for administrative purposes or rely on the certification of exempt status under the Non-Living and Non-Domestic Corporations regime

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity intending to engage in any taxable business activity in the Marshall Islands must first incorporate or register a foreign entity with the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act or the Foreign Investment Business Act, obtain a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) from the Foreign Investment Board, then file a Taxpayer Registration Application with the Ministry of Finance – Taxation Division; the Ministry will issue a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) or VAT ID as applicable; representative branch operations require a registered local address and resident agent; non-resident corporations with no domestic-source activity are treated as offshore entities and may obtain a TIN for administrative purposes or rely on the certification of exempt status under the Non-Living and Non-Domestic Corporations regime

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Marshall Islands does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election in the U.S. tax sense; entities may elect non-resident exempt status under section 145 of the Business Corporations Act to be relieved of all income tax and withholding obligations; hybrid entities formed under the Limited Liability Company Act may elect to be taxed as a partnership or corporation solely for foreign tax characterisation, but such election has no effect on Marshall Islands domestic taxation; no statutory hybrid mismatch election exists, and the Tax Exemption for Offshore Entities under the Income Tax Act allows a full domestic income tax exemption while the entity may retain a foreign tax classification for home-jurisdiction reporting; an entity may also elect the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation tax classification under the former Revenue Act where the Internal Revenue Code provisions have been adopted by reference, but the election is dormant and confers no practical domestic tax liability

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No Marshall Islands VAT or general sales tax is in force; the territory applies no statutory economic-turnover threshold because no indirect tax system is currently operational; under the Revenue and Taxation Act, no VAT registration threshold is prescribed; a non-resident corporation with no Marshall Islands source sales is not registerable and receives an exempt status certification; if a future VAT (General Excise or Business License Tax) is enacted, the threshold would follow the Revitalized Business and Non-Domestic Corporation revenue rules but none is currently codified

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the Marshall Islands levies no VAT or GST, there is no statutory zero-rating provision to claim; a qualifying exporter of goods, including ISIC 3240 Montessori beechwood toy blocks, is equally free from Marshall Islands indirect tax because no indirect tax attaches at the point of export; governmental fees (export permit, port charges) may apply, but no VAT zero-rating certificate is required; goods exported from Marshall Islands customs territory are outside the charge of any consumption tax; the exporter may confirm its exempt status through the Tax Exemption Certification issued by the Ministry of Finance under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation regime

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Marshall Islands Customs Service (under the Customs Act 1990, as amended)
  • Bill of Lading issued by the ocean carrier or House Bill of Lading from a licensed Marshall Islands freight forwarder
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange record evidencing export proceeds repatriation to a commercial bank if generated within the Marshall Islands banking system
  • Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) held by the exporting entity, where required under the Foreign Investment Business Act
  • Title or export manifest for wooden toy blocks (ISIC 3240) showing HS classification 9503.00, weight, cubic measurement, and port of loading

No electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based real-time fiscal reporting mandate exists under Marshall Islands law; the Ministry of Finance does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) system; businesses are not required to obtain any pre-approval for invoices; electronic invoices may be issued voluntarily, but no government portal exists for invoice clearance; the only regime-adjacent requirement is the annual Business License Renewal filing and, where the entity holds a FIBL, the submission of economic data to the Foreign Investment Board

Marshall Islands law imposes no VAT zero-rating clawback because no VAT refund mechanism exists; however, an exporter that falsely claims non-resident exempt status while carrying on domestic-source activity may face loss of exempt status, reassessment of the full 3% corporate income tax or 2% gross revenue tax with interest, plus penalties up to 25% of the underpaid tax under the Income Tax Act; making a false declaration to the Customs Service constitutes a customs offence punishable by forfeiture of goods and fines up to USD 5,000 per act, and any fraudulent inducement of a tax exemption may lead to revocation of the FIBL and blacklisting from future export licenses; directors and officers may be subject to USD 10,000 civil penalties and suspension of the corporation's good standing; the Attorney General may seek retrospective clawback of any indirect tax incentives improperly obtained for a period of five years

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands has no domestic currency and therefore imposes no exchange-control distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions. The U.S. dollar circulates freely, and non-resident Business Corporations may remit dividends, management fees, or liquidation proceeds without FX licensing or central-bank approval. The only practical hurdle is bank-level due diligence: a non-resident entity must evidence beneficial ownership, source of funds, and commercial substance before a payout, but profit repatriation is unrestricted as a matter of statute and BOP treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline apply to RMI exporters. Because export sales are denominated and settled in USD, no conversion event exists. Under the RMI Banking Act and the Beneficial Ownership Act, banks must retain trade documents and transaction records for at least five years, but no obligation exists to force export receivables into a local account or to swap them into local currency.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commission of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, Ministry of Finance (no domestic central bank exists; monetary policy is effectively determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-Low for a non-resident RMI Business Corporation. Fully digital EMIs that accept RMI-incorporated clients (e.g., Wise Business, Mercury, Stripe Treasury) will require evidence of an active e-commerce marketplace, minimum projected monthly settlement volumes, and a substantive-operations questionnaire. Statutory constraints: RMI-licensed banks treat EMI settlement flows as correspondent exposure, and many EMIs apply country-policy exclusions or enhanced-UAB searches to Annex-II and no-substance jurisdictions. An EMI can serve as the Amazon/Stripe payout collection rail but cannot be the sole bank account when the treasury plan requires an escrow facility, supplier LC, or domestic clearing relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: US Dollar (USD). Domestic law does not prescribe a standard threshold; prudent compliance standard is to prepare a Local File when controlled transactions exceed US$1,000,000 per annum and a Master File when group consolidated revenue exceeds US$100,000,000. A Country-by-Country report is required only if the ultimate parent in a multinational group files CbC-Relevant reports and the RMI entity is a constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: RMI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying on 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centers, shipping, and IP holding). Manufacturing of Montessori wooden blocks is not a designated relevant activity. However, the entity must maintain adequate substance with respect to its commercial functions: a registered agent in RMI, a dedicated business address, proper bookkeeping, and at least one director who exercises independent discretion. If the entity claims to be non-resident, it must be central managed and controlled outside the RMI; otherwise, the full RMI domestic tax system and ESR may apply, negating the tax exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Non-Residence Claim

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to US$10,000, disgorgement of all taxes saved, 15% interest on unreported tax; criminal conviction carries a fine of US$25,000 and imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; the court may impose travel bans and restitution orders against personal assets, and in case of willful fraud, imprisonment of directors.
Shell Company Use / Facade Transactions

Statutory sanction: Dissolution of the entity; revocation of the certificate of incorporation; US$50,000 administrative penalty under the Business Corporation Act; forfeiture of assets linked to the shell arrangement.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable for all corporate debts; they may be disqualified from acting as directors for 10 years. Repeated use for money laundering triggers the RMI Proceeds of Crime Act, leading to seizure of personal property.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Deficiency

Statutory sanction: US$25,000 fine per violation; restitution of funds; deregistration of the company. Escalated penalties apply if the deficiency is used to conceal a funded terrorism-linked transaction.

Director personal liability: Director liability is strict: personal fines up to US$100,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for knowingly filing false ownership declarations or obstructing an AML inspection.
Transfer Pricing Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: If RMI source income is understated, the Revenue Administration assesses a 3% gross revenue tax on the deemed RMI-source portion, plus a 20% accuracy-related penalty and late payment interest at 1.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or create false transfer pricing documentation face personal civil penalties up to US$20,000 and exclusion from serving on RMI-registered boards. Criminal referral for fraudulent misrepresentation could result in up to 2 years of imprisonment.

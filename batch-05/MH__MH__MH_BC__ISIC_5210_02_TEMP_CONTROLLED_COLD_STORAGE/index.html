Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services – Tax & Treasury Office (income tax and TINs); Customs Division, Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services (import/export duties); Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General (Business Corporation registration, non-resident status designation and annual franchise tax); RMI Social Security Administration (payroll and social security registration). RMI maintains no consolidated online fiscal portal; registrations and filings are paper-based and executed through licensed RMI registered agents and the Tax & Treasury Office in Majuro.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate: a licensed RMI registered agent files Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (BCA); no minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2 – Elect non-resident status: the Articles designate the corporation as a non-resident domestic corporation (no RMI-resident shareholders, directors or officers). Step 3 – Registration issuance: the Registrar issues the Certificate of Incorporation and corporation number and collects initial filing and franchise fees. Step 4 – Tax registration: no TIN is required for a purely offshore business; a TIN must be obtained from the Tax & Treasury Office only if the corporation derives RMI-source income, employs RMI-resident individuals (together with Social Security registration), or serves as importer of record. Step 5 – Annual compliance: file the prescribed annual confirmation with the Registrar and pay the annual franchise tax; no income tax return is due while the exemption applies. A separate license under the Foreign Investment Business Act is required only if the corporation physically operates within RMI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – By BCA design and Income Tax Act 1989 source rules, a non-resident domestic corporation (NRDC) is taxable only on income derived from sources within RMI; all foreign-source income from offshore refrigerated warehousing, cold-chain logistics and ancillary fees is excluded from RMI gross income, functionally creating a Delaware-style tax-exempt zone for non-resident entities. The classification is territorial in scope (RMI-source income remains taxable) and does not exempt customs duties, Social Security contributions or the annual franchise tax.

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box or look-through election exists; the BC is prima facie an opaque corporation. Operative mechanisms: (i) the BCA election to organize as a non-resident domestic corporation, which fixes the source-based exemption; (ii) the BCA continuation/domestication procedure converting a foreign corporation into an NRDC while preserving its juristic identity; (iii) the Income Tax Act 1989 sourcing and exclusion provisions, which apply automatically without election; and (iv) no hybrid-mismatch, Multilateral Instrument or Pillar-Two elections, as RMI has not adopted those regimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands uses the U.S. dollar as its sole legal tender. There is no separate foreign exchange control regime. Both current account transactions (trade and services) and capital account transactions (equity injections, loans, repatriations) are freely convertible in U.S. dollars with no surrendery or licensing requirements. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate formalities, beneficial ownership transparency, and AML/CFT compliance under the Banking Act 1991 and the Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2019.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in U.S. dollars or any other freely convertible currency at the discretion of the exporter, without obligation to surrender to any domestic monetary authority.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance (no independent central bank; the U.S. Federal Reserve serves as the de facto monetary authority through the official use of the U.S. dollar)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) generally do not have direct clearing access to the local banking system unless they obtain a money transmitter license under the Marshall Islands Banking Act. The small correspondent banking network and the lack of a central bank make payment facilitation through overseas digital EMIs commercially impractical for primary treasury rails. EMIs may only be used as supplementary disbursement channels after the corporate maintains a fully established local USD banking relationship and only for non-core, low-value payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from foreign shareholders must be wired in U.S. dollars via SWIFT MT103 to the company's segregated capital escrow account at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam – Marshall Islands Branch. The wire message must reference the corporate name, the escrow account number, and a purpose code reflecting 'Capital Contribution' (e.g., BOJ or CORP code equivalent) or 'Shareholder Loan'. The bank will verify the sender's entity name, beneficial owner(s), and the wire origin prior to crediting. For equity contributions, the corporate file must contain a directors' resolution authorizing the issuance of shares; for debt injections, an executed loan agreement with a full repayment schedule is required. No exchange control approval is necessary, but suspicious transactions must be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements comprise (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct payments to foreign logistics partners, port agents, and pharmaceutical suppliers; (ii) Documentary or Standby Letters of Credit issued by the local bank and advised through correspondent banks for high-value refrigerated cargo purchases; (iii) ACH direct deposits for domestic payroll and intra-Majuro utility payments. All outbound wires must be matched to underlying invoices, cooling-chain monitoring certificates, or import/export documentation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (non-resident Business Corporation; the Marshall Islands does not impose any corporate income tax; a 3% Gross Revenue Tax applies only to an active RMI trade or business)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Business Corporation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form BC-AR and the annual registration fee are due on or before the anniversary of incorporation, or on the date stated in the Registrar's renewal notice. No income tax filing deadline applies because no income tax return is prescribed for an exempt non-resident Business Corporation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly returns are due by the last day of the month following the calendar quarter; the annual reconciliation is due no later than four months after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold exists in Marshall Islands law. Transfer pricing documentation is not mandatory because the exempt non-resident BC is outside the RMI gross-revenue tax net. A USD 1,000,000 annual revenue indicator may be relevant for economic-substance reporting of an RMI-resident international company, but it is not a transfer-pricing documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: RMI applies economic-substance standards aligned with BEPS Action 5 to companies tax resident in the Marshall Islands and engaged in relevant activities, including distribution and service centre functions. The present non-resident Business Corporation does not conduct an RMI trade or business and therefore is not required to maintain RMI substance solely for its foreign cold-storage activities. If, in fact, the entity is managed and controlled from RMI, or employs RMI personnel and uses RMI premises for refrigerated logistics, it must demonstrate local presence, full-time employees, adequate operating expenditure, and board direction in RMI as core income-generating activities. Failure to satisfy applicable substance rules can result in financial penalties, correction orders, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Source-Country Abuse

Statutory sanction: Within RMI, fraudulent evasion of Gross Revenue Tax is punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years. In the shareholder's source country, undisclosed foreign entities and accounts can attract penalties of 50% to 100% of the unreported amount, criminal fines, and extradition exposure.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the BC is a pure façade; directors may be held civilly liable for restitution of tax deficiencies, disqualified from serving as officers, subjected to travel bans, and imprisoned for aiding intentional tax evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may issue a compliance penalty, strike the corporation off the register, and confiscate or forfeit the right to carry on business. Foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the BC as resident or as a permanent establishment and assess the entire attributable profit, denying any substance-based exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false substance declarations face personal fines and disqualification; travel bans can be imposed to prevent asset dissipation; imprisonment is available for fraudulent misrepresentation and criminal facilitation.
AML/CFT and Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: The RMI Financial Transactions Reporting Act and Proceeds of Crime Act impose fines up to USD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering; correspondent banks may terminate access to the international payment system, and wire intermediaries may freeze accounts on suspicion of healthcare fraud or sanctions violations.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and authorised signatories face personal criminal liability, civil asset forfeiture, debarment from managing an RMI entity, and travel bans as a condition of bail; extradition to the originating state is available for dual criminal offences.
Registration and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may impose fines, revoke the certificate of good standing, deny the registered agent the ability to act, and administratively dissolve the BC. A source-country court may annul structures that rely on the false non-resident status and impose civil penalties on the misstatement.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally liable for signing false declarations and may be disqualified or imprisoned; beneficial owners may be joined for civil restitution and subjected to travel bans while extradition proceedings are pending.

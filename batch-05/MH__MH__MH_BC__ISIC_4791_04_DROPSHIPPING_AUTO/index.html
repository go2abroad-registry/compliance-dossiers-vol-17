Applicable AML statute: Business Corporations Act 1990 (B.C.A., as amended, including the 2014 and 2018 amendment acts) — Sections 14 through 28 (name clearance, registered office and registered agent) and the beneficial-ownership record-keeping obligations applicable to every business corporation; Anti-Money Laundering Act 2000 (as amended), the Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Regulations 2008 (as amended), and the Banking Act 1986 (as amended) — all supervised and enforced by the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and the Office of the Registrar of Corporations. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office (such as a post-office box without a physical street presence) is not accepted as the statutory registered office. Section 16 of the Business Corporations Act requires every corporation to continuously maintain a physical registered office in the Republic of the Marshall Islands at the street address of a licensed registered agent in Majuro. However, the automated dropshipping operations may be conducted entirely remotely from anywhere in the world, and the RMI registered office in practice functions as the administrative or virtual seat of the non-resident, tax-exempt corporation — a recognized and accepted industry pattern for RMI business corporations holding Stripe and PayPal merchant accounts.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); no dedicated online e-portal for non-resident corporate TIN registration — all applications are channeled through the Registrar of Corporations and licensed registered agents.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident Business Corporation (BC): (1) Incorporate under the Business Corporations Act via the Registrar of Corporations; (2) no local Business License or TIN is statutorily required absent physical or commercial nexus in the Marshall Islands; (3) for banking and payment-processor due diligence, obtain an RMI TIN by filing the prescribed TIN application form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent details, and notarized board resolution; the Revenue and Taxation Division issues an 8-digit TIN with no recurring tax return filing obligation for foreign-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Non-resident Business Corporations are automatically tax-exempt for Marshall Islands income tax purposes on all non-RMI-source income. Voluntary election to be taxed under the Income Tax Act is possible only if the corporation derives income from sources within the Republic; such an election is practically never used by offshore e-commerce entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales Tax — RMI has no general indirect tax regime

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No indirect tax registration threshold exists. A Business License Tax applies only to entities maintaining a physical commercial establishment in the RMI; fully remote automated dropshipping with no local presence remains outside the scope of any indirect taxation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not levy VAT or GST; therefore no 0% export rate claim procedure exists. Goods and offshore services exported from the RMI are outside the scope of indirect taxation. Goods entering or leaving an RMI free-trade zone or customs territory qualify for duty-free treatment upon proper declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank proof of international payment)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where requested by destination country)

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping mandate is imposed by the RMI. Customs export declarations are processed through the RMI Customs Service, but invoices themselves are not fiscally cleared. Payment processor and banking KYC documentation, rather than tax clearance, governs invoice practice.

No export zero-rating clawback regime exists because there is no VAT/GST to reclaim. However, fraudulent customs declarations, misdescription of goods, or failure to substantiate export status under the RMI Customs Act may attract penalties up to 200% of the value of goods, seizure and forfeiture of the shipment, and criminal prosecution. For non-resident BCs using RMI bank accounts, anti-money-laundering audits and economic substance documentation requests may apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI has no foreign exchange control framework. Both current-account transactions (trade in goods and services) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, loan repayments, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds) are freely convertible into and out of U.S. dollars without hard-currency rationing, surrender obligations, or exchange-rate band restrictions. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders of an RMI Business Corporation is unrestricted and subject to zero withholding tax; no central bank approval, exchange permit, or prior authorization is required. The only operational compliance overlay is bank-level anti-money laundering (AML) screening applied by domestic and correspondent banks to each credit and debit transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists under RMI law. Export receipts, including automated dropshipping settlements collected via payment aggregators and commercial bank credits, are invoiced and received in U.S. dollars and may be retained in full by the exporter; therefore, there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory remittance timeline. The absence of a surrender regime is consistent with the RMI's dollarized monetary system.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI does not maintain a domestic central bank; the U.S. Federal Reserve System is the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender pursuant to the Compact of Free Association).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate-to-Low. Globally licensed digital EMI and Banking-as-a-Service providers (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury) generally decline RMI-incorporated business corporations because of jurisdiction-risk overlays, prior FATF monitoring history, and perceived shell-entity exposure. Legally, an offshore EMI without an RMI banking license may not establish a branch or offer locally domiciled accounts in the Republic; however, an RMI entity may maintain an account at an overseas-regulated EMI only if that institution's own onboarding policy accepts the jurisdiction. The targeted settlement rails, Stripe and PayPal, are materially constrained for RMI-domiciled corporate merchants within the current assessment window, which elevates reliance on the domestic banking tier and, where commercially prudent, an intermediate operating entity in a more bankable jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) inbound wire via SWIFT MT103 in USD from the shareholder or parent bank to the BOMI or Bank of Guam operational account using U.S. correspondent ABA and Fedwire routing; (2) payment purpose coded as 'EQUI' (equity investment) or 'LOAN' (intercompany loan), accompanied by a board resolution and supporting corporate documents; (3) full source-of-funds evidence retained, including shareholder statements, transaction trail, and employment or investment history, for AML reconciliation; and (4) funds credited only after completion of the bank's onboarding EDD file, with any unexplained mismatch in purpose code or counterparty triggering enhanced review and potential return of funds.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for supplier payments settled in USD (or CNH and EUR where contractually required) through the domestic bank's U.S. correspondent network, cleared via Fedwire and CHIPS. Secondary rail: card-network settlement via corporate Visa or Mastercard for purchases executed through automated procurement modules, with proceeds from Stripe and PayPal payout sweeps consolidating into the domestic USD account. For high-value periodic procurement orders, a sight or usance Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 is available at the domestic bank's discretion, although standard dropshipping economics do not ordinarily require it. The RMI has no local RTGS system; all multi-dealer clearing is therefore correspondent-bank dependent and operates on U.S. dollar finality schedules.Outright deliverable forward contracts (USD/CNH, USD/EUR, USD/JPY) executed with RMI-authorized domestic banks or offshore correspondent dealers.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/CNY to address onshore renminbi convertibility restrictions for Chinese supplier commitments.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (USD versus supplier-currency floating rate) for matched procurement tenors exceeding twelve months.Zero-cost collar strategies combining purchased caps and written floors to contain multi-currency supplier cost volatility within a defined corridor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Non-resident Business Corporation statutory exemption; the residual RMI Income Tax Act headline corporate rate of 11.85% applies exclusively to RMI-source business income of corporations carrying on trade or business within the Republic, from which the present automated-dropshipping BC is structurally excluded).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Republic imposes no withholding tax on dividends declared by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders; dividends are statutorily outside the RMI Income Tax Act and the Gross Revenue Tax framework.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due annually on or before the incorporation anniversary date; the Registrar observes a short administrative grace window before imposing late charges; failure to file and pay for two consecutive years triggers administrative dissolution.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 30 days after the close of each calendar quarter for any corporation engaging in business within the Republic; non-resident BCs with no RMI-source revenue are outside the filing net and file a nil declaration only if administratively registered for this tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - the RMI has no transfer-pricing documentation statute and no statutory Local File or Master File threshold. Prudential benchmark: maintain contemporaneous OECD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation (functional analysis, benchmarking study, intercompany agreements, and margin evidence) where cross-border connected-party transactions exceed US$500,000 in any fiscal year, to defend against foreign CFC, permanent-establishment, and anti-hybrid audit challenges.

Economic substance scope: The RMI has no domestic Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to BVI or Cayman; however, the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices and the EU listing process require substance-based gateways for low-tax jurisdictions. A non-resident BC must therefore demonstrate genuine commercial decision-making, operational personnel, and physical management in its actual market jurisdiction to avoid recharacterization as a shell company, foreign tax residence, or place-of-effective-management migration, and to pre-empt CFC and GAAR attacks by revenue authorities of the consumers' and suppliers' jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (RMI Income Tax Act and Gross Revenue Tax)

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full tax evaded plus statutory interest; civil penalty up to 100% of the understated tax; criminal fine up to US$10,000 and imprisonment up to five years upon conviction for fraudulent return; revocation of the certificate of good standing and administrative strike-off.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing for fraud: directors who sign false certifications are jointly and severally liable for understated tax and penalties; court-ordered travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment on criminal conviction; director disqualification under the Business Corporations Act.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution by the Registrar for chronic non-filing; automatic loss of limited-liability protection; foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the entire enterprise as a permanent establishment or integrated trade or business, imposing penalties up to 40% of the underpayment and 100% for willful avoidance, with interest.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing for sham-entity conduct: directors and controlling shareholders personally liable for obligations arising after dissolution; personal exposure to foreign CFC attribution, PFIC consequences, and economic-substance penalties; regulatory disqualification from holding directorships in RMI entities.
AML/CFT Violation (RMI Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 and Financial Transactions Reporting Act)

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering conviction: for individuals, a fine up to US$50,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years; for a body corporate, financial penalty up to US$10,000,000; asset forfeiture and freezing orders; mandatory suspension or termination of merchant acquiring arrangements by Stripe and PayPal.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability attaches to directors and senior officers who knowingly permit transactions involving criminal property; travel bans and extradition exposure; civil forfeiture of personal assets derived from the enterprise; personal debanking and permanent exclusion from payment-rail platforms.
Cross-Border Fraudulent Commercial Practice (E-Commerce and Wire-Fraud Liability)

Statutory sanction: Under RMI law: civil restitution, injunctions, and fines for misrepresentation; under the law of the consumers' and payment-rail jurisdiction (United States): wire fraud and conspiracy charges, criminal forfeiture of U.S.-based assets, enforcement of foreign civil judgments; payment-processor liability reverts to the entity and its principals where the corporation is insolvent.

Director personal liability: Extradition exposure for fraud conspiracy; personal bank accounts and real property subject to freezing, restitution, and forfeiture orders; veil piercing where the BC is a mere instrumentality; imprisonment up to 20 years under U.S. federal fraud statutes.

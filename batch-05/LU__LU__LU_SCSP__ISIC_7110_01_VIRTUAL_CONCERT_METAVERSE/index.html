Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), managed by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), 14-16 Boulevard Roosevelt, L-2450 Luxembourg; online filing via www.lbr.lu and the eFormalities portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 sur les sociétés commerciales), notably Articles 29-33 and 943-1 to 943-24 governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); implementing Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 July 2016; RCS operational guidelines.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution / statuts (SCSp limited partnership agreement) and application for registration ('demande d'inscription au registre de commerce et des sociétés'), filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital or legal escrow account for an SCSp. Contributions in cash, kind or labour are agreed contractually; a cash contribution is due and payable into the SCSp's bank account, while any deferred payment must be expressly accepted by the general partner in the partnership agreement.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defence or fire-safety permit is required for a virtual venue. If the SCSp leases administrative offices, the premises must satisfy the Labour Inspectorate (ITM) workplace occupational safety rules, including fire extinguishers, accessible exits, and electrical safety certifications. If the SCSp hosts live 3D/VR events, no additional clearance applies beyond ordinary office-compliant equipment shielding and GDPR-compliant data processing.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital or legal escrow account for an SCSp. Contributions in cash, kind or labour are agreed contractually; a cash contribution is due and payable into the SCSp's bank account, while any deferred payment must be expressly accepted by the general partner in the partnership agreement.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – no physical manufacturing plant or physical event venue. For any future physical promotional event, the competent body would be the Ministry of the Economy (Direction générale de l'industrie, PME) and the relevant communal authority for event permits.

Category: No industrial operating licence is required for a purely virtual concert and digital ticket tokenization platform; only a standard business registration under the Luxembourg RCS is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT; e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu and Guichet.public.lu; customs: Administration des douanes et accises.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR), the entity is automatically assigned a Luxembourg national identification number (matricule) which serves as the statutory TIN; for VAT, the SCSp or its foreign-entity owner must file a VAT registration application (Formulaire 105 - inscription à la TVA) with the AED through MyGuichet.lu, after which the AED issues a VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; non-resident entities with no Luxembourg establishment that carry out taxable supplies may register directly for VAT or appoint an approved fiscal representative, in particular when the entity is established outside the EU.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Upon incorporation of the SCSp with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR), the entity is automatically assigned a Luxembourg national identification number (matricule) which serves as the statutory TIN; for VAT, the SCSp or its foreign-entity owner must file a VAT registration application (Formulaire 105 - inscription à la TVA) with the AED through MyGuichet.lu, after which the AED issues a VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits; non-resident entities with no Luxembourg establishment that carry out taxable supplies may register directly for VAT or appoint an approved fiscal representative, in particular when the entity is established outside the EU.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent (tax transparent) by default under Luxembourg law, with each partner taxed personally on their distributable share; no domestic election exists to render the SCSp opaque, although the transposition of the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and the hybrid mismatch rules may reclassify the SCSp as opaque in Luxembourg where a foreign partner jurisdiction treats it as taxable, triggering DAC6 reportable arrangements and ATAD 2 counter-measures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT franchise threshold under Article 148 of the Luxembourg VAT law); registration is mandatory once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or immediately for cross-border intra-Community acquisitions; B2C digital ticket sales to EU consumers are subject to OSS registration either in Luxembourg or in the EU member state of consumption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the EU qualify for the 0% export rate under Article 53(1) of the Luxembourg VAT law, provided definitive export is evidenced within a reasonable period; virtual concert services supplied B2B to foreign businesses fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope (reverse charge on the foreign customer), while B2C supplies of electronically supplied services to customers outside the EU are zero-rated or outside scope due to effective use and enjoyment outside the EU; B2C tickets sold to EU consumers remain taxable and are declared in Luxembourg under the OSS scheme.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (AES) bearing the MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or air waybill evidencing physical exit from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, virtual concert ticketing platform statement and licensing contract
  • Partner identification documents and proof of the foreign customer's residence (for services)

No real-time transaction-by-transaction fiscal clearance or digital stamping is in force; mandatory Peppol-compliant electronic invoicing applies exclusively to B2G supplies (public sector procurement under Directive 2014/55/EU); B2B and B2C e-invoicing is voluntary, subject to customer acceptance; VAT returns and recapitulative statements (DEB/EC Sales List) are filed electronically with the AED, and Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) reporting is due only upon express AED demand during an audit.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the SCSp and its general partner to tax fraud fines of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 3 years under Articles 1162 to 1164 of the Luxembourg VAT law; the AED may retrospectively recapture the 0% rate, impose late interest of 10% per annum, and apply penalty interest for each month of delay; insufficient transport evidence, split shipments, or failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day expectation automatically disallow the export exemption and trigger rectification with criminal referral in cases of intentional misdeclaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is guaranteed. Luxembourg applies TFEU Articles 63-66 and Directive 88/361/EEC on free movement of capital and payments; there is no approval, declaration, or surrender requirement for current-account transactions (ticket revenues, streaming royalties, platform fees) or capital-account operations (partner capital contributions, distributions). Profit remittance is unrestricted: the SCSp is fiscally transparent, so distributions follow the partnership agreement without any Luxembourg withholding tax at the partnership level. Practical gates are limited to AML/RBE source-of-funds verification, EU/OFAC sanctions screening, and BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the receiving institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation period for export receipts denominated in foreign currency. Obligations are statistical only: resident credit institutions report cross-border flows to BCL under Council Regulation (EC) No. 184/2005 and national BCL/CSSF reporting instructions. The SCSp may retain, convert, or disburse USD and USDC proceeds freely through the approved rails.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank (ECB), is the national monetary and statistical authority. Prudential and AML supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate state foreign-exchange administration exists and no exchange-control licensing authority applies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a payment overlay; Low as the principal treasury rail. Legal constraints: (i) under PSD2 and the post-Brexit framework, a non-EEA EMI cannot passport into Luxembourg, so only EEA-licensed EMIs may hold or settle the SCSp's funds; (ii) substance expectations and BCL statistical reporting require the main operating IBAN to remain with a Luxembourg-resident credit institution; (iii) crypto flows via EMIs are heavily restricted unless the EMI holds a MiCAR authorisation or partners with a regulated VASP. Practical setup: use an EEA EMI (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, Wise Business) for ticketing-fee aggregation and refunds, sweep balances daily to the Tier 1 Luxembourg IBAN, and route all USDC through a regulated VASP-to-fiat bridge.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City combined rate including corporate income tax of 17%, solidarity surcharge of 7% on CIT, and municipal business tax of 6.75%, applicable to profits exceeding EUR 200,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for profit allocations from an SCSp because the partnership is fiscally transparent and such allocations are not statutory dividends; if the SCSp were treated as opaque, the general Luxembourg corporate dividend withholding tax rate is 15%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file obligation is triggered when aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File is required where the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the ACD applies the OECD BEPS substance-over-form principle and the domestic General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 6 OPL). An SCSp claiming treaty benefits or the participation exemption must demonstrate substance: a Luxembourg registered office, a locally resident manager or management team, physical or contractual presence, real decisions taken in Luxembourg, and substantive operational staff; a letter-box SCSp may be recharacterized as opaque, taxed at 24.94% CIT, and denied all treaty relief

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Article 107 LIR attracts imprisonment of up to five years and fines ranging from EUR 250,000 to EUR 500,000; administrative re-assessment at 24.94% CIT, surcharges up to 20% of tax evaded, and monthly 0.6% late interest

Director personal liability: The gérant and any managing partner may be criminally liable; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose civil restitution of all evaded taxes and interest, issue travel bans, freeze personal and corporate bank accounts, and disqualify the director from managing any Luxembourg entity for up to ten years
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The ACD will apply GAAR, deny fiscal transparency, impose CIT and municipal business tax at 24.94% on the full income, and assess net wealth tax at the statutory minimum of approximately EUR 3,300; additional penalties may reach EUR 250,000

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the sham vehicle face civil liability for the unpaid tax, legal reversal of the partnership's distributions, judicial liquidation, personal travel bans, and the risk of extradition for corruption where foreign clients are involved
Anti-Money Laundering / Cryptocurrency Payments

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal AML penalties provide for up to five years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 2.5 million for beneficial owners and compliance officers; the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze the SCSp's bank and crypto accounts

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process tainted virtual-asset proceeds are personally liable for confiscation of the funds and civil damages; travel bans and precautionary seizure of personal assets may be ordered during the criminal investigation
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment plus penalties of up to 10% per year, late-interest surcharges, and documentation fines up to EUR 100,000; intentionally false TP documentation is treated as tax evasion with criminal consequences

Director personal liability: The economic owner of the foreign partner entity may be deemed a de facto director; courts may reassign the income to the Luxembourg SCSp, pierce the partnership veil, and hold the director personally liable for the differential tax plus penalties and travel restrictions pending settlement
Corporate Formalities / Non-Registration

Statutory sanction: Late RCS registration fines up to EUR 2,000 per month; failure to file annual accounts may result in civil dissolution, removal from the register, a fine up to EUR 10,000, and in severe cases criminal sanctions for unauthorized trading

Director personal liability: Directors of a dissolved or unregistered SCSp become jointly and severally liable without benefit of the corporate veil for all debts and tax liabilities; the court may impose a personal contribution order, asset freezes, and a temporary ban on commercial activity in Luxembourg

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax and municipal business tax; partners are taxed in their own capacity on allocated income; the SCSp maintains an independent personality for VAT purposes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; transparency is mandatory. Limited election under Article 176ter LITL allows certain partnerships without legal personality to opt into corporate tax treatment (not conclusively applicable to SCSp due to its legal personality). ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules apply under Article 168quater LITL; no discretionary hybrid confirmation election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises under Article 57 VAT exemption (registration optional below threshold, election available); no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers, compulsory registration from the first Luxembourg-taxable transaction; EUR 10,000 turnover threshold for intra-Community distance sales under OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 52 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for exports of goods to non-EU destinations. B2B services supplied to overseas clients fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the recipient-jurisdiction place-of-supply rule (Article 17 LVD) when the client holds a foreign VAT identification number, resulting in a 0% invoice with mandatory reverse-charge reference and VIES validation. Services directly linked to exported goods are also zero-rated where the recipient is outside the EU. Input VAT refund for EU-related costs is obtained via ordinary VAT returns or AEDT refund procedure per Directive 2008/9/EC.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ELU-AES single administrative document)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) / CMR or Air Waybill evidencing departure from EU territory
  • Proof of customer's foreign VAT status (VIES registration number where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof
  • Written contract evidencing cross-border supply of PR/communication services

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in force. B2G e-invoicing mandatory since 1 January 2020 via PEPPOL/EN 16931 (Law of 22 December 2017 transposing Directive 2014/55/EU). B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary; accepted formats include Factur-X/ZUGFeRD, EDI and hybrid PDF/XML. Invoices must be retained for 10 years; cross-border transaction reporting applies under DAC7/CESOP frameworks.

AEDT tax audit division prosecutes fraudulent zero-rating as tax fraud under the General Tax Law: fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years; retrospective reassessment of VAT at 17% plus late-payment interest at 0.8% per month (9.6% p.a.) from the original due date; late filing penalties of 10% per month (capped at EUR 1,250) may be stacked. Given SCSp pass-through status, the general partner is jointly and severally liable for VAT clawbacks and partner-level reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control regime; cross-border capital movements are wholly liberalised under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Directive 88/361/EEC. Profit remittance is frictionless: the SCSp is fiscally transparent, so no Luxembourg withholding tax is imposed at entity level on distributions to partners. Residual hurdles are limited to documentary compliance (partner register updates, substance file, and DAC-6/ATAD anti-abuse review where non-resident partners have no economic nexus).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the Eurozone impose no statutory timeline for surrender of export receipts and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export or service-export proceeds. Inward payments for PR retainers may be held in EUR or any freely chosen currency. Only standard AML/CFT transaction-monitoring obligations apply under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF Regulation 12-02, requiring accurate economic-purpose documentation for each inward credit.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg, operating within the Eurosystem under the policy authority of the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. A Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution (under the amended Law of 10 August 2018 on payment services, implementing PSD2) may legally hold an operational account for the SCSp without a full banking licence. However, an EMI cannot extend credit, issue Commercial Letters of Credit, or provide collateralised FX hedging lines; the SCSp treasury would therefore require a tier-1 bank counterparty for overdraft, ISDA CSA margining, and LC issuance. Cross-border EMIs passporting into Luxembourg (e.g., Revolut, Wise) may onboard the SCSp remotely, but CSSF AML Circular 12/552 and Regulation 12-02 require the EMI to independently verify the SCSp's ultimate beneficial owners against the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE); any mismatch or delay in RBE filing results in frozen onboarding or account restrictions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands; corporate registration and tax-exemption certificate through the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General. Portal: https://rmigovernment.org.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate a non-resident Business Corporation under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act; no minimum capital or local director requirement and non-resident ownership is permitted. 2) Pay the incorporation/annual government fee to the Registrar of Corporations and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and status/tax-exemption documentation. 3) No RMI TIN or VAT registration number is issued to a non-resident BC because RMI imposes no income tax or VAT/GST. 4) Only if a physical business/employment presence is created in RMI, register as an employer with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration and obtain the relevant local business license. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI has no check-the-box election and does not apply US Internal Revenue Code Section 7701 or hybrid-mismatch elections. The relevant election is structural under the Business Corporations Act: the company elects to be formed/to operate as a non-resident domestic corporation and thereby receives the 30-year statutory exemption from all RMI taxation. Carrying on business in RMI, owning RMI real property, or failing the non-resident conditions would terminate the tax-exempt classification; no separate tax election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no national VAT, GST or general sales-and-use tax; only customs import duty and limited excise duties on goods entering the Marshall Islands)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in USD: RMI imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no economic-turnover threshold triggering indirect-tax registration. Import-duty liability arises only upon physical importation into RMI, at the applicable customs tariff, and does not require tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

RMI has no VAT/GST, so export zero-rating and VAT refunds are not applicable. The de facto export rate is 0%: export sales of IoT hardware and offshore services are outside RMI indirect taxation without election. To preserve the non-resident exemption, goods/services must be supplied to non-resident customers, any physical movement must be traced as an export from RMI customs territory if the goods enter RMI, and no sale may be deemed to occur in RMI through local delivery, local use, or local business performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only if physical goods are exported from Marshall Islands customs territory)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence for export proceeds

No statutory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API/digital-stamping obligation or certified-invoicing regime applies to non-resident B2C IoT-hardware retailers. RMI customs clearance accepts ordinary commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents and customs declarations; a Stripe/Shopify merchant may issue standard commercial sales receipts without integrating with an RMI fiscal reporting API.

No VAT export-refund or zero-rating clawback exists because no VAT is charged. However, abuse of the non-resident tax-exempt status or false customs documentation can trigger adverse consequences: loss/revocation of the tax-exemption status with retroactive exposure to import duty/fees, customs penalties, seizure and forfeiture of goods, and criminal liability for fraudulent declarations. If a smart-IoT exporter diverts goods into RMI domestic consumption without paying import duty, the RMI customs administration may assess unpaid duty plus penalty and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists as a matter of law: the RMI has no national currency, no foreign exchange control statute, and no capital account administration. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including profit remittances, dividend declarations, intercompany loans, and liquidation distributions, are freely transferable in USD without central bank approval, exchange permits, or tax clearance certificates. Practical friction arises only from correspondent-bank due-diligence artifacts - OFAC sanctions screening, FATCA/CRS classification, and AML source-of-funds verification - rather than from RMI statutory restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio. Export receipts, merchant-acquirer settlements from Stripe and Shopify Payments, and trade payments may be retained and managed in USD either onshore or offshore at corporate treasury discretion. Best practice is to concentrate all retail settlements in a single designated operating account so the treasury trail aligns with e-commerce settlement statements and future source-of-funds attestations.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a central bank or foreign exchange administration. Under the Compact of Free Association, the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, so monetary policy is de facto determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve. Prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions is exercised by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services pursuant to the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1990 (as amended) and the Banking Regulations 1999.

Compliant protocol: shareholder remittance via SWIFT MT103 to the BC's designated operating account with the memo line 'EQUITY capital contribution - Business Corporations Act [date] - [shareholder name]' and the corporate capital-call resolution retained as the underlying instruction. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) organized under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act (Title 51 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code, MIRC) is wholly exempt from corporate income tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax, and franchise tax on worldwide income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — The Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Report must be filed, and the annual authority fee paid, within 30 days after the anniversary of the date of incorporation. No corporate income tax deadline applies under the zero-rate regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 — Not applicable. The Marshall Islands levies no corporate income tax and has not enacted transfer pricing documentation rules; no Local File or Master File obligation exists under Title 54 MIRC, which applies only to payroll/PAYE and certain gross-receipts matters.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2019, as amended, issued under the Business Corporations Act (Title 51 MIRC), a non-resident BC carrying on a 'Distribution and Service Center Business' — which includes retailing smart home IoT hardware and home automation devices as referenced in ISIC 4791 — must satisfy the Core Income Generating Activities (CIGAR) test. The entity must maintain a physical presence in the Marshall Islands, employ adequate and suitably qualified staff proportionate to the level of activity, and incur adequate annual operating expenditure in the jurisdiction. An annual ESR notification (and, where in scope, an ESR return) must be filed with the Registrar within 12 months after the financial year-end. Substance compliance information may be exchanged with foreign competent authorities under intergovernmental arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

False Statements and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines and imprisonment under Title 51 MIRC (Business Corporations Act) and Title 54 MIRC (Income Tax Act) for willful false statements and evasion; additionally, foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the structure, impose back taxes, apply penalties up to 200% of the tax understated, and order disgorgement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for civil restitution, disgorgement of profits, director disqualification, and criminal imprisonment of up to two years for felony false declarations.
Shell Company Abuse / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties of up to USD 10,000 per contravention for ESR non-compliance, compulsory strike-off for repeated default, and referral of the entity's information to foreign tax authorities under exchange-of-information instruments.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for ESR penalties, automatic disqualification from holding office, issuance of arrest warrants in flagrant cases, and travel bans pending resolution of regulatory proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act (Title 45 MIRC), fines up to USD 200,000 per contravention and imprisonment up to 15 years for laundering offenses, plus asset freezing and forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners knowingly involved face personal criminal custodial sentences, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans while under investigation, and extradition exposure in mutual legal assistance matters.
Failure to Maintain Registered Agent / Annual Report Default

Statutory sanction: Automatic administrative dissolution of the corporation by the Registrar; reinstatement requires payment of all arrears and reinstatement fees; contracts entered into after dissolution are voidable.

Director personal liability: Directors continue to be liable for debts and obligations incurred after dissolution while purporting to act on behalf of the corporation; the corporate veil is pierced for contracts executed during the void period, exposing directors to direct personal claims.
Misrepresentation to Payment Processors and Customers

Statutory sanction: Civil and criminal fraud liability in the processing jurisdiction, refund liabilities, processor termination and blacklisting, and referral to the Marshall Islands Attorney-General; aggravated cases constitute wire fraud punishable by imprisonment in the prosecuting jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who sign false representations are personally liable for wire fraud restitution, may be subject to extradition proceedings, and face travel bans where criminal charges are pending.

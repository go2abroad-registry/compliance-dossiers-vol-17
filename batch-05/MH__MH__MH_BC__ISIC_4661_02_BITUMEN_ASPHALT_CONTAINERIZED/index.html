Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; corporate registry authority: Office of the Registrar of Corporations (Majuro); filings are executed through licensed registered agents via paper submission, with no fully digitized national tax portal.

        
E-government filing portal
A non-resident Business Corporation is formed by filing Articles of Incorporation under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act and obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation. The entity then files the Business License Application and pays the annual Business License Tax. Upon issuance of the license, the Revenue and Taxation Division registers the entity and assigns a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). No income tax registration or return obligation arises where the BC has no RMI-source income and conducts no business within Marshall Islands territory.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation is formed by filing Articles of Incorporation under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act and obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation. The entity then files the Business License Application and pays the annual Business License Tax. Upon issuance of the license, the Revenue and Taxation Division registers the entity and assigns a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). No income tax registration or return obligation arises where the BC has no RMI-source income and conducts no business within Marshall Islands territory.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box election comparable to IRC Section 7701 exists, and no hybrid mismatch election is available under RMI law. Taxation follows a source-of-income model: the non-resident BC is taxable only on RMI-source gross receipts; income derived from offshore wholesale trading of containerized bitumen falls outside the RMI tax net provided the corporation maintains non-resident status and does not transact business in the RMI.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No VAT or GST regime in force; indirect taxation consists exclusively of Import Duty under the RMI Customs Tariff and the flat-rate Business License Tax under the Business License Act.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable — no VAT/GST or economic turnover threshold exists. A flat annual Business License Tax of approximately USD 100–300 applies regardless of gross turnover; the RMI operates on the United States Dollar as legal tender.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable — RMI imposes no VAT/GST, hence no export zero-rating or refund mechanism exists. Exported containerized penetration-grade bitumen nonetheless qualifies for customs duty exemption on export and remains outside the RMI gross-receipts tax base where: (i) title and risk of loss pass to the foreign buyer outside RMI; (ii) the goods are shipped from an RMI port to a foreign destination; and (iii) the seller is a non-resident Business Corporation with no RMI trading presence.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Marshall Islands operates a fully dollarized monetary system with no statutory exchange controls. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible for practical purposes because the monetary unit is the U.S. dollar and no domestic exchange control legislation restricting conversion exists. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, and trade settlement are not subject to central bank approval or FX licensing. The only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC clearance, OFAC sanctions screening, and correspondent bank restrictions. Non-resident business corporations may hold and disburse USD freely, and no RMI tax or exchange control charge is imposed on profit remittance.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There are no statutory export proceeds surrender timelines and no mandatory conversion ratios in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Since the U.S. dollar is both the domestic currency and the export invoicing currency, export receipts may be retained in USD, credited to the exporter's operational account, and used for any lawful purpose. The exporter is not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender proceeds to a central bank or official counterparty. The only compliance duties arise from correspondent bank screening, OFAC/sanctions checks, and the bank's internal transaction monitoring framework.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not establish a central bank and maintains no domestic foreign exchange administration. The U.S. dollar is the legal tender and national currency. Banking supervision and licensing are administered by the Banking Commissioner / Office of the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance. In practice, monetary stability and dollar clearing are functions of the U.S. Federal Reserve System and the U.S. dollar correspondent banking network.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI and virtual-account platforms are not recognized as licensed deposit-taking institutions under the Marshall Islands Banking Act framework. Only entities holding a valid banking licence in the Republic, or domestic registered agents acting as introducers, may provide account or payment services to an RMI corporate entity. An offshore EMI may not lawfully maintain a bank account or hold client funds as a RMI bank. Cross-border digital EMIs are therefore best used only as payment gateways or prepaid collection tools, not as primary treasury accounts, and only where their licensing in another recognised jurisdiction is verified.

Defense evidence: Certificate of Good Standing, Central Registry extract, registered agent certificate, directors and ultimate beneficial owner register, economic substance explanation, contract evidence showing real commodity trading activity, corporate bank reference, and audited or management financial statements.

Root cause: Unclear source of funds or unexplained large inbound capital injection.

        
Defense evidence: Board resolution authorising capital contribution, subscription or capital contribution agreement, source-of-wealth statement, bank statements of the remitting investor for the preceding 12 months, tax or tax-exemption status confirmation, and notarised identification documents for all signatories.

      

      
      

        Trade-based money laundering concern from commodity invoicing or third-party settlement.
        
Root cause: Trade-based money laundering concern from commodity invoicing or third-party settlement.

        
Defense evidence: Commercial contract, proforma invoice and final invoice, bill of lading, packing list, container list, independent quantity/quality inspection certificate, insurance certificate, customs entry evidence, and matching shipping and payment records to substantiate the bitumen/asphalt cargo trade.

      

      
      

        Sanctions, OFAC exposure, or high-risk counterparty jurisdiction linked to bitumen supply chain.
        
Root cause: Sanctions, OFAC exposure, or high-risk counterparty jurisdiction linked to bitumen supply chain.

        
Defense evidence: Sanctions screening reports on all counterparties, vessel/bill of lading screening, end-user and ultimate consignee declarations, certificate of origin, route confirmation, legal counsel sanctions memorandum, and a contractual undertaking barring dealings with sanctioned parties.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant routing is via standard USD wire through the correspondent banking network. The remitter shall issue a SWIFT MT103 from a bank account held in the legal name of the investor or parent company. The beneficiary account is the RMI bank account of the Business Corporation. Field 70 remittance information should read 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHARE CAPITAL' plus the invoice or subscription reference. No RMI Government purpose code is required, but the receiving bank will require a board resolution, payment instruction, and source-of-funds evidence. Funds must be received from a known banking institution, not through cash couriers or unlicensed money transmitters, to satisfy AML and OFAC screening. If the capital is to be held for a later registered business purpose, the bank may designate the account as a Capital Escrow Account and release funds upon delivery of the corporate registration or corporate bank account activation documents.Outbound trade settlements shall be executed via SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for conventional invoice-based payments and via Commercial Letters of Credit using SWIFT MT700/MT707 for high-value, document-backed bitumen/asphalt containerised trades. USD clearing occurs over the correspondent bank network, principally through Fedwire or CHIPS connectivity. Documentary credits should include the commercial invoice, packing list, full set of clean bills of lading or multimodal transport documents, container seal numbers, certificate of quantity/quality, certificate of origin, and marine insurance certificate. For containerised drummed or bagged bitumen, split-shipment and partial-discharge terms must be expressly stated in the LC to reflect multiple container deliveries and separate payment tranches. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Abuse of Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Exemption
        
Statutory sanction: Revocation of the NRDC tax exemption; retroactive assessment of RMI income tax on all undeclared income; interest and civil penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded; monetary fines imposed by the court; strike-off from the Register of Business Corporations.

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors signing false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, penalties, and restitution; aggravated cases carry travel bans, asset-freezing orders, director disqualification, and imprisonment under RMI criminal tax provisions.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Beneficial Ownership and Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Denial or withdrawal of good standing; refusal of Certificates of Good Standing; administrative dissolution; adverse reporting to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit; de-risking and sudden termination of trade finance facilities.

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: creditors and financing banks may proceed directly against directors for shadow trading; nominee directors face disqualification, civil restitution, and bans on acting as officers in the RMI or in OECD financial markets.

      

      
      

        Trade-Based Money Laundering / False Invoicing
        
Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act, as amended: financial penalties, confiscation of the bitumen cargo and proceeds, and custodial sentences; the Financial Intelligence Unit may also impose administrative fines and file international disclosures to FATF counterparts.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally exposed to confiscation orders and imprisonment for money-laundering predicate offenses; using nominee directors does not shield the controlling principal from criminal liability, travel controls, or extradition requests.

      

      
      

        Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting
        
Statutory sanction: The RMI itself has no domestic transfer-pricing statute, but the source-country tax authority may reassess the trading margin, deny deductions for non-arm's length management fees, impose withholding tax on disguised distributions, and apply penalties and interest up to 100% of the underpaid foreign tax.

        
Director personal liability: Foreign anti-avoidance rules (CFC, GAAR, and beneficial ownership registers) may attribute the RMI BC's income to the ultimate controllers; directors can be held personally liable for erroneous certifications and face cross-border collection, travel bans, and director disqualification.

      

      
      

        Sanctions and Embargo Violations in Asphalt/Bitumen Trade
        
Statutory sanction: Enforcement by the United States, EU, and UN member states: blocking of assets, arrest of vessels, seizure of the bitumen cargo, secondary sanctions against the RMI entity, and referral to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit for coordinated AML action.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be designated as blocked persons, subject to travel and visa bans, freezing of personal assets, personal forfeiture of cargo and demurrage, and criminal prosecution in enforcing jurisdictions.

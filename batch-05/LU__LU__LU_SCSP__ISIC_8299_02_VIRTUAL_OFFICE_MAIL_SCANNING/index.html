Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Luxembourg is mandatory. A service provider's address may be used as the registered office, but a pure virtual-office arrangement without any operational staff or physical management will not satisfy the Luxembourg economic-substance expectations for an SCSp carrying on an active commercial mail-receiving and scanning activity. Such an arrangement is permissible only as a formal mailing address; actual daily business activity, administrative oversight, employees and relevant management decisions must be materialized in Luxembourg to sustain tax residence and avoid abuse-of-law challenges.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; the partnership capital is freely determined in the partnership agreement. Because the SCSp has no legal personality, contributions are contractually defined and no escrow account is required by Luxembourg law.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax; business registrations, filings, and payments are effected through the MyGuichet.lu secure portal and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) platform issuing the RCS matricule.

E-government filing portal

The SCSp is registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS) through MyGuichet.lu to obtain a 13-digit national business registration number (matricule). Upon obtaining the matricule, the SCSp must file the statutory VAT registration application with the AEDT to receive a Luxembourg TVA identification number. Non-resident foreign entities supplying taxable services into Luxembourg may apply for a Luxembourg TVA number directly via the AEDT cross-border procedure or through a Luxembourg-approved tax representative, submitting the certified RCS extract, constitutive documents, passports of managing partners, and evidence of the Luxembourg economic nexus or service-provision supply chain.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent for corporate income tax and net wealth tax purposes; partners are taxed directly on their profit share per the partnership agreement. Available elections and structural mechanisms include: (i) VAT group (fiscal unity) election for eligible Luxembourg taxable persons; (ii) voluntary VAT registration to waive the EUR 35,000 small-business franchise exemption; (iii) contractual profit-allocation provisions in the limited partnership agreement determining tax attribution; and (iv) ATAD and EU anti-hybrid assessments requiring partners to reconcile the Luxembourg transparent classification with their residence-jurisdiction classifications to avoid hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is compulsory upon start of any taxable economic activity in Luxembourg; however, the small-business franchise (franchise de base) exempts businesses with an annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (exclusive of VAT) from VAT charging obligations, while still allowing voluntary registration to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT export exemption provided the goods are dispatched or transported out of Luxembourg within the statutory period and supported by corroborating evidence of physical exit. Electronically supplied services, including digitized mail scanning and virtual office deliverables supplied to a foreign business customer, are not goods exports; they follow the B2B place-of-supply rule requiring the Luxembourg supplier to obtain and retain commercial evidence of the customer's business establishment, with the customer's jurisdiction applying tax reverse-charge mechanisms where relevant. Back-office document processing and mail management services physically performed in Luxembourg for non-EU principals qualify for the 0% regime under Luxembourg place-of-supply provisions for services rendered to customers established outside the EU, provided the evidentiary documentation is maintained. No national FX repatriation statutory deadline exists under Luxembourg law; the 180-day inward remittance window is the documentary-credit benchmark applied in administrative and audit practice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EU Single Administrative Document) endorsed by the Luxembourg Customs Administration (Administration des Douanes et Accises)
  • Bill of Lading or alternative transport instrument (CMR waybill / Air Waybill) evidencing the physical dispatch from Luxembourg
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer evidence demonstrating the receipt of consideration by the Luxembourg SCSp
  • Commercial invoice and packing list incorporating the direct-export statement
  • Foreign customer's written confirmation of business establishment and non-EU status, including the customer's VAT identification or equivalent registration number

Luxembourg imposes no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for domestic B2B invoicing. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for B2G transactions, which must be transmitted in the EN 16931 standardized format via the national PEPPOL access point. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposal is expected to introduce mandatory cross-border and domestic B2B electronic invoicing and digital transaction reporting in phases from 2028 onward; the SCSp should architect its billing platform to be PEPPOL/EN16931-compliant in advance.

Luxembourg customs and tax authorities conduct ex-post documentary audits of export zero-rating claims. If the required proof of physical removal or payment is defective or absent, the AEDT retroactively applies the standard VAT rate (17% or the applicable reduced rate) to the supply, together with late-interest computed from the date of supply, and may levy administrative penalties of up to EUR 4,000 per infringement. In cases of fraudulent or sham export arrangements, criminal fines and imprisonment may apply under Luxembourg VAT legislation, and the full VAT amount may be clawed back from the SCSp jointly and severally with its partners. All export zero-rating evidence files must be retained for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63). There is no exchange control approval requirement for inbound capital contributions, profit distribution, dividend remittance, or repayment of shareholder loans. Profit remittance is unrestricted in euros, and foreign-currency accounts may be held freely; practical hurdles arise only from AML/KYC documentation standards, tax compliance declarations for withholding, and EU or UN sanctions screening. The euro is fully legal tender, and the SCSp may maintain accounts in EUR or any eligible foreign currency at licensed credit institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory export proceeds surrender rules or minimum conversion ratios in Luxembourg. Luxembourg does not impose foreign-exchange surrender obligations on export receipts; all export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely through an authorised credit institution. The only duty is standard balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for direct investment and cross-border transaction statistics where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Luxembourg Ministry of Finance, operating within the Eurosystem and in coordination with the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) for payment services and financial crime supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) that hold a valid passport into Luxembourg under the revised EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2); Low for offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs. An offshore EMI may not lawfully provide payment accounts or execute payments for a Luxembourg SCSp unless it holds an authorised Luxembourg branch or passporting European licence. The virtual-office/CMRA activity profile is classified as higher-risk under EBA AML guidelines, and most offshore EMIs will decline the relationship or restrict transaction limits where physical substance is limited. Practical viability requires the EMI to be regulated by an EU/EEA authority, to accept a Luxembourg-registered SCSp as customer without legal personality, and to provide compliant IBAN, SEPA access, and SWIFT handling.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the SCSp are routed through regulated banks or authorised payment institutions via standard SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency transfers or SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated contributions from EEA accounts. No exchange-control permit is required. Each transfer must include a clear purpose reference such as 'SCSp capital contribution - LU__LU__LU_SCSP__ISIC_8299_02_VIRTUAL_OFFICE_MAIL_SCANNING', the Luxembourg tax identification number of the entity, and the remitter/beneficiary legal names. The receiving Luxembourg bank verifies the identity of the remitter and beneficiary, confirms beneficial ownership details against the RBE, and applies enhanced due diligence where the contribution originates from a third-country jurisdiction. Any cross-border direct investment flow is subject to applicable BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting and CSSF AML record-keeping requirements.Outbound trade and operating disbursements are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer within the EU/EEA, SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments, and commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700 series) where goods or services are sourced from third countries. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — SCSp is fiscally transparent; no entity-level corporate income tax. For reference, Luxembourg resident companies are subject to an aggregate CIT rate of 24.94% (Luxembourg City).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory; 0% for qualifying EU Share-Parent Directive (>=10% participation) or Luxembourg subsidiary exemption; distributions by SCSp are not subject to dividend WHT due to fiscal transparency.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

By 30 April following the calendar/fiscal year-end; a six-month extension is available on application to the ACD (final deadline 31 October).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 in annual related-party transactions for Local File; Master File if group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies BEPS, EU ATAD and its abuse-of-law doctrine; there is no standalone ESR, but substance over form and beneficial ownership checks are enforced. SCSp must demonstrate real economic activity in Luxembourg (office, employees, decision-making). A pure virtual-office mail scanning SCSp is high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment and penalty of 20% to 100% of the tax evaded; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000; imprisonment up to 5 years; seizure/disgorgement of assets.

Director personal liability: The SCSp general partner (gérant) is unboundedly and jointly liable for fiscal debts; civil restitution, travel bans, and custodial imprisonment may be imposed on individuals exercising control.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the general anti-abuse rule; reclassification of SCSp as taxable company; retroactive interest and penalties up to 100%; deregistration from Luxembourg Commercial Register; ban from certain activities.

Director personal liability: Directors or the general partner may be held personally liable for taxes recalculated under recharacterization; court-imposed management bans and travel restrictions; criminal charges in severe abuse cases.
AML/CTF & Unlicensed TCSP Activity

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004, imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000; administrative sanctions from the Minister of Justice/CSSF including license revocation and penalty up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of turnover.

Director personal liability: Manager/gérant personally exposed to criminal indictment, travel bans, personal fines, confiscation and civil liability for creditors' losses due to breach of AML obligations.

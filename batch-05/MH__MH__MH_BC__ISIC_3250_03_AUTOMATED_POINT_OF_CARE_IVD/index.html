Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible for statutory corporate purposes; the registered agent's official RMI address may serve as the registered office. For the stated IVD equipment manufacturing scope, no physical operational office inside the Marshall Islands is required for a non-resident Business Corporation. However, a virtual office does not establish economic substance for foreign tax or regulatory licensing purposes, and most commercial banks will reject a virtual-only address for account opening. A substantive operational address in the manufacturing jurisdiction and appropriate ISO/FDA/CE licensing documentation are required for banking and commercial counterparty due diligence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Revenue & Taxation Division (income/tax administration) and the RMI Customs Service (import duties) of the Republic of the Marshall Islands; Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA) for payroll contributions; Foreign Investment Board for the Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL). Portal: official RMI government web portal (no dedicated national e-tax filing system for tax-exempt non-resident corporations).

E-government filing portal

A Non-Resident Business Corporation (BC) is incorporated under the Business Corporations Act (BCA) 1990 (Delaware-modeled) through the Registrar of Corporations, receiving a Certificate of Incorporation; no national income-tax TIN or VAT ID is issued because the BC is statutorily exempt from RMI income tax. SOP: (1) file Articles of Incorporation (including non-resident status clauses) and appoint a local Registered Agent; (2) obtain the annual franchise fee receipt/good standing certificate from the Registrar; (3) if any physical operations or employment occur in the RMI, register with MISSA and obtain a FIBL; (4) for any imports/exports, obtain a Customs Importer/Exporter ID from the RMI Customs Service; the corporate registration certificate and Customs ID serve as the operative identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The RMI has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. A BC may elect, in its Articles of Incorporation, to be a 'Non-Resident Domestic Corporation' (0% RMI tax on foreign-source income and no territorial nexus), and shipping entities may elect Foreign Maritime Entity status. An entity that elects to carry on business in the RMI becomes liable to FIBL, MISSA, municipal wage tax (Majuro/Kwajalein) and customs duty; tax treatment follows incorporation status and in-country substance rather than elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales tax; the RMI operates a customs import duty (tariff) system under the Customs Tariff Act 1997, plus municipal wage taxes and MISSA/health-fund payroll contributions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST turnover registration threshold exists. All commercial importers must register with the RMI Customs Service for an importer ID; any foreign-owned business operating in the RMI must obtain a FIBL; any employer with payroll must register with MISSA regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Technically inapplicable as the RMI has no VAT/GST; there is no zero-rating claim to file. Exports of IVD microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers leave the RMI customs territory free of any RMI export tax, customs duty, or turnover tax, and a Non-Resident BC retains its 0% tax-exempt status provided (i) goods are physically exported, (ii) no RMI domestic sales are made, and (iii) the entity does not become resident by carrying on business in the RMI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward entry filed with the RMI Customs Service)
  • Commercial Invoice fully describing the IVD medical devices and values
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from the RMI
  • Packing List (carton-level details for customs verification)
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the destination market)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing FX repatriation through SWIFT LC or TT Wire

The RMI imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate. Invoices are private commercial documents and are not pre-cleared by the Ministry of Finance; customs declarations are submitted through the RMI Customs Service using standard paper or electronic manifests, with no mandatory government API integration for export transactions.

Although there is no VAT output tax to claw back, fraudulent or unsupported export declarations are prosecuted under the Customs Tariff Act 1997 and the Business Corporations Act 1990. Penalties include monetary fines and penalties (plus interest), seizure and forfeiture of the exported goods, revocation of the FIBL or customs registration, deregistration of the non-resident BC, and criminal liability for directors. If the entity cannot substantiate physical export, non-resident status, or FX repatriation within the 180-day deadline, the Revenue & Taxation Division may retroactively reclassify the entity as resident and assess all applicable municipal taxes, MISSA/health-fund contributions, customs duties, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands has adopted the U.S. dollar as national legal tender. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no foreign exchange controls. Profit remittance via dividends, interest, or license fees to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to corporate law solvency tests and AML/CFT verification. No encumbrances such as surrender or allocation requirements exist. The effective hurdle is correspondent banking due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Because the U.S. dollar is legal tender, export proceeds are not required to be surrendered or converted. There is no mandated timeline or ratio for keeping export receipts in local currency; proceeds may be held directly in USD demand or escrow accounts.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (the banking regulator; the country has no central bank and uses the U.S. dollar as legal tender)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low–Medium. The RMI lacks a local EMI/digital bank licensing framework; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut may facilitate cross-border TT wires in USD, but they are not licensed as banks in RMI and cannot offer escrow or LC services. Legal constraints include the need to route through correspondent banks that may classify RMI-registered corporations as higher risk, and the absence of a formal passporting arrangement. An operational account may be opened remotely with select EMIs, but capital escrow and documentary collections should remain with a licensed commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 (best-practice/voluntary intercompany transaction threshold; the RMI does not impose any statutory local file/master file obligation, but multinational groups using the RMI BC should document related-party transactions exceeding USD 1,000,000 per annum to satisfy the tax administration of the ultimate parent jurisdiction in an OECD-aligned manner).

Economic substance scope: Not statutorily enacted as standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the RMI. However, the RMI is under EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD BEPS Action 5 monitoring for non-resident corporate regimes. The non-resident exemption is available only if the entity (i) does not derive RMI-source income, (ii) maintains a resident agent and registered office, (iii) keeps contemporaneous records evidencing foreign management, and (iv) is not used as a shell to facilitate aggressive tax avoidance. For the automated point-of-care IVD group, substance to document includes: board minutes held in the parent company's jurisdiction, executed intercompany manufacturing/royalty contracts, inventory title transfer outside the RMI, functional/asset/risk analysis of the RMI entity, and a CFC/tax-residence analysis prepared by the group controller.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Exempt-Status Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fine up to USD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years under the RMI Criminal Code; the tax administration may retroactively assess territorial-source tax at 3%, add interest at 12% per annum, and impose fraud penalties of up to 100% of the underreported amount; the Registrar may revoke the BC's certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and interest; upon conviction, directors face a travel ban from the RMI, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: No direct RMI ESR penalty currently exists; the practical sanction is administrative strike-off by the Registrar after 90 days of non-compliance with annual renewal, and foreign tax authorities may apply CFC/transfer-pricing recharacterisation, deny treaty benefits, impose withholding on deemed royalties, and assess penalties of 20%-40% of underpaid tax in the parent jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Under RMI corporate law, veil-piercing is permitted when the corporation is a mere instrumentality; directors may be liable for the group's tax restatement, equitable compensation, and legal costs; a foreign court may issue a worldwide asset freeze order against the directors personally.
Money Laundering / AML-CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Breach of the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Financing of Terrorism Act 2002 (as amended) is punishable by fines on the BC of up to USD 1,000,000, forfeiture of the sums involved, and suspension/termination of the resident agent's license; the bank's own compliance sanctions may include account closure and a USD 250,000 regulatory penalty per suspicious transaction.

Director personal liability: Officers and compliance officers can be criminally fined USD 250,000 and imprisoned for up to 15 years; the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit can request travel bans and asset freezing through mutual legal assistance, and directors may be barred from holding office in any RMI-licensed registered agent or financial institution.
False Statement & Perjury Before the Registrar

Statutory sanction: A false official statement is a misdemeanor under Title 23 of the RMI Revised Code, carrying a fine of up to USD 20,000 and up to 3 years' imprisonment; the Registrar may disqualify the resident agent and impose an additional regulatory fine of USD 10,000.

Director personal liability: The director who certified the false statement is personally liable for the fine and restitution of the registration fee; the Registrar may issue a director disqualification order for up to 5 years, preventing service as a director of any RMI corporation or LLC.
Repatriation / Dividend Stripping with Abusive Related-Party Terms

Statutory sanction: Within the RMI, if the Income Tax Act is triggered by local-source character of the royalty, a 3% gross-basis tax may be assessed with a 10% monthly late-payment penalty (capped at 50%) and 12% annual interest; outside the RMI, the operating subsidiary's tax authority may impose withholding deficiency assessments and accuracy-related penalties of 20%-40%.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the abusive intercompany prices can be held personally liable in the parent jurisdiction as shadow directors under civil tax recovery statutes; RMI courts may pierce the veil to reach the BC's assets held in trust and enforce judgments against the directors' direct and indirect shareholding interests.

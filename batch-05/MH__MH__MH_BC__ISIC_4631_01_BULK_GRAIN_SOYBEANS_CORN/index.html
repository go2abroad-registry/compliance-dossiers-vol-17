Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance & Administration, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, Revenue and Taxation Office, Majuro; corporate registry portal operated by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations via International Registries, Inc. (www.register-rmi.com)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation is not required to obtain a separate tax identification number unless it derives income from business carried on within the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The statutory identifier is the unique corporate registration number issued by the Registrar of Corporations upon incorporation through a licensed registered agent. If RMI-source business activity is subsequently commenced, the entity must register with the Revenue and Taxation Office under the Gross Revenue Tax regime, obtain a Gross Revenue Tax account number, and file quarterly returns. Foreign corporations with no RMI nexus are not subject to Gross Revenue Tax or income tax, and their only periodic obligation is the annual corporate franchise fee paid to the Registrar of Corporations. The local currency for any RMI-denominated filings is the United States Dollar (USD).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None available. The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose a national income tax on non-resident Business Corporations and therefore no check-the-box, entity-classification, or hybrid mismatch election exists under RMI law. The tax-exempt status is automatic and applies by operation of law to a Business Corporation that does not carry on business within the territorial limits of the Republic. No tax exemption certificate or special application procedure is required. An entity that wishes to maintain full non-resident status must simply refrain from engaging in RMI-source activities and must comply with corporate registry formalities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST regime. The RMI does not impose a value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general sales and use tax. The only indirect-style impositions are customs import duties levied under the Customs and Excise legislation and the 3% Gross Revenue Tax applicable only to persons carrying on business within the Marshall Islands. Non-resident offshore entities engaged in the brokerage of bulk agricultural commodities are outside the scope of both regimes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold in the Marshall Islands because no such tax exists. The 3% Gross Revenue Tax applies to any person 'engaged in business' within the RMI without a statutory monetary threshold, but an entity with no RMI-source activity is not registrable. If the entity becomes an employer of RMI-resident workers, registration with the RMI Social Security Administration is required from the date of first employment. All monetary references are in USD.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because the Marshall Islands has no VAT or GST, there is no 0% export VAT rate, no output tax to charge, and no input VAT credit to claim. The relevant relief for a non-resident Business Corporation is the automatic exclusion from the 3% Gross Revenue Tax and from RMI territorial income taxation in respect of profits derived outside the Republic. For goods in transit through RMI ports, no export duty is levied, and bunkers and ship supplies may be cleared duty-free where properly declared for export. Brokerage commissions earned from offshore bulk soybean and yellow corn chartering transactions have no RMI indirect tax consequence.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (Customs manifest covering the commodity voyage)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Charter Party Bill of Lading evidencing the shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds)

None. The Marshall Islands does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping. Conventional paper or PDF invoices are accepted for business recordkeeping purposes. The Revenue and Taxation Office retains the power to require production of books, records, invoices, bills of lading, and bank statements during audit or investigation proceedings.

There is no VAT export-refund mechanism and therefore no VAT clawback exposure in the Marshall Islands. However, a false customs export declaration, misdescription of goods, or unauthorized transshipment can result in forfeiture of the goods, administrative pecuniary penalties, interest on any evaded duty, and criminal prosecution under the RMI Customs and Excise legislation. For entities within the Gross Revenue Tax net, fraudulent under-reporting can give rise to retrospective assessments, penalty surcharges, and cancellation of the corporate registration by the Registrar of Corporations. Non-resident status is not a shield against the consequences of misrepresenting RMI-source activity or customs documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI imposes no exchange-control legislation; because the U.S. dollar is domestic legal tender, current-account flows (trade settlements, freight, brokerage commissions) and capital-account flows (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend and profit repatriation) are fully convertible by law without licensing, surrender, or prior regulatory approval. Practical gatekeeping is delegated to commercial banks and their correspondent banks, which apply AML/CFT due diligence, FATCA/CRS reporting, sanctions screening, and Beneficial Ownership verification; profit remittance is therefore unfettered once the remitting bank is satisfied as to source of funds, economic substance, and the underlying trade documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. RMI law stipulates no statutory repatriation window and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; grain-sale receivables may be collected and retained in USD either in the RMI or in offshore correspondent accounts. Covered financial institutions must nevertheless retain trade, wire-transfer, and KYC documentation for at least five years pursuant to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2012, so a complete audit trail of the underlying charter party, bill of lading, sale confirmation, and payment instruction should be maintained.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a central bank and has no statutory foreign-exchange administration; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender. Supervisory authority for banking and deposit-taking business rests with the Banking Commission of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (established under the Banking Act 1991), acting in coordination with the Ministry of Finance and the Financial Intelligence Office on AML/CFT and cross-border wire oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with conditions. The Banking Act 1991 reserves deposit-taking and banking business to licensed entities, and the RMI does not issue fintech EMI charters; however, an RMI-incorporated BC may lawfully open payment accounts with foreign-regulated digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, or similar cross-border payment institutions) provided such providers do not carry on regulated banking business in or from RMI territory. Practical constraints are substantial: EMIs apply enhanced institutional due diligence to RMI entities owing to the jurisdiction's offshore financial-center profile and FATF/APG mutual-evaluation monitoring history; onboarding typically requires certified corporate documents, director personal KYC with video interview, an explicit economic-substance narrative for ISIC 4631 agri-commodity brokerage, and a commitment to invoice- or LC-linked payment flows rather than unstructured third-party transfers.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident Business Corporation (BC): the RMI Income Tax Act 1989 assesses tax only on income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands; offshore bulk-grain and soybean brokerage commission income earned by an MH_BC falls entirely outside the RMI corporate tax net.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the RMI imposes no dividend withholding tax; a BC may declare and pay dividends by board resolution to any shareholder, resident or non-resident, with no deduction at source and no advance clearance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due on or before each anniversary of the date of filing of the Articles of Incorporation; a late surcharge is triggered if the report and the US$200 filing fee are not delivered within 60 days after that anniversary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No RMI transfer-pricing statute, Local File, or Master File obligation exists for a BC (the RMI uses the US dollar). Nevertheless, for foreign-tax protection and bank due diligence, any intercompany brokerage, demurrage, freight or commission transaction exceeding US$100,000 per counterparty per year should be supported by written contracts, arm's-length commission-rate benchmarking (e.g., Baltic Exchange fixture data for the relevant grain route), voyage P&L evidence, and settlement exclusively via the target rails (Irrevocable Transferable LC / SWIFT wire).

Economic substance scope: The RMI, as part of commitments accepted by the EU, has enacted economic substance requirements for non-resident domestic corporations. Bulk-vessel brokerage of agricultural soybeans and yellow corn constitutes a distribution and service centre type relevant activity, requiring: (i) direction and management of the brokerage business within the Marshall Islands, (ii) adequate physical premises and qualifying employees in RMI, (iii) performance of core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, voyage verification, demurrage resolution) in RMI, and (iv) annual substance filing. Non-compliance triggers fixed penalties, periodic default penalties, automatic exchange of information with EU and OECD partner tax authorities, and re-listing as a no-or-only-nominal-tax jurisdiction. The RMI remains engaged in FATF/APG follow-up; UBO transparency and demonstrated substance are essential to preserve correspondent banking access for LC and SWIFT settlement.

Cross-Border Tax Evasion and Abusive Conduit Arrangement

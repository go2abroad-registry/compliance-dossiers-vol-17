Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Service (RCS) – Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; official portal: https://www.rmi.gov.mh/tax

E-government filing portal

Foreign applicants form the entity through a licensed Marshall Islands registered agent by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations. Upon issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, the Registrar transmits the name and registered-office details to RCS, which assigns a single Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (MTIN). No separate VAT ID exists; the MTIN is the only fiscal identifier and is also used for customs and any local business license if onshore activities are commenced.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident domestic Business Corporation (BC) is exempt from Marshall Islands national income tax, withholding tax, and gross receipts tax on all non-Marshall Islands source income; a charitable endowment organized as a non-resident BC may also qualify as a tax-exempt private foundation under the Business Corporations Act and the Charitable Organizations Act. — Foreign applicants form the entity through a licensed Marshall Islands registered agent by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations. Upon issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, the Registrar transmits the name and registered-office details to RCS, which assigns a single Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (MTIN). No separate VAT ID exists; the MTIN is the only fiscal identifier and is also used for customs and any local business license if onshore activities are commenced.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident domestic Business Corporation (BC) is exempt from Marshall Islands national income tax, withholding tax, and gross receipts tax on all non-Marshall Islands source income; a charitable endowment organized as a non-resident BC may also qualify as a tax-exempt private foundation under the Business Corporations Act and the Charitable Organizations Act.

Available election forms

The BC may irrevocably elect 'non-resident status' in its Charter/Articles of Incorporation under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act. RMI law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch elections are not available, and any entity claiming an exemption must file a Notice of Tax-Exempt Status with RCS on formation or within 30 days of commencement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT, GST, or general sales/use tax; limited customs import duty, environmental cess, and municipal business license fees are collected instead.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold. A municipal Business License Tax threshold applies only to a resident entity physically operating a business in Majuro or Ebeye; non-resident offshore corporations are below any local business license nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – because RMI has no VAT/GST, exports are outside the scope of indirect tax and zero-rating does not exist. For customs duty relief, a charitable endowment must show that the exported goods are genuine humanitarian supplies, grant-funded equipment, or scientific instruments intended for a Marshall Islands charitable grantee and covered by an RCS non-profit customs exemption certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SYS-Customs electronic form)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT evidence showing grant funds received)

None – the Marshall Islands has no national e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API posting, or digital stamping mandate. Customs entries are filed electronically through standard customs manifest procedures, but no revenue authority clearance is required for offshore non-resident transactions.

Since no VAT refund is available, fraudulent export zero-rating cannot be invoked. Any false claim for customs duty exemption is treated as smuggling/deception under the Customs Act and is subject to goods forfeiture, a penalty of not less than 100% and up to 300% of the duty evaded, retrospective revocation of the exemption certificate, clawback of all duties plus interest for the preceding five tax years, and, for directors, a separate fine up to USD 10,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility in both current and capital accounts. The RMI imposes no exchange-control law, no capital-account authorization regime, and no profit-remittance restriction; since USD is domestic legal tender, inward endowment injections, grant outflows, investment income, and liquidation distributions are freely remittable. A Non-Resident Business Corporation is income-tax-exempt under the Business Corporations Act 1990, so no withholding taxes, no branch-profit remittance charge, and no stamp duty attach to foundation distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI has no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. Proceeds from commodity exports, licensed tuna-fleet landings, and copra sales may be retained in full and in any currency, although USD is the functional banking currency and domestic accounts are denominated in USD.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands. The RMI maintains no autonomous central bank and issues no national currency; the U.S. Federal Reserve System operates as the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. Dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association (1986, amended 2003).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment and donation flows are remitted by SWIFT MT103 in USD, with no RMI exchange-control approval required. Recommended purpose codes: 'GDIR' (Gift/Donation) for philanthropic capital and '8510 Transfers to Non-Profit/NGO' or the receiving bank's internal donation code for subsequent grants. The receiving bank requires the donation or endowment agreement, the board resolution accepting the contribution, the foundation's charitable-purpose charter, and the remitter's KYC pack. USD clearing is executed via Fedwire or CHIPS through the RMI bank's U.S. correspondent; high-value injections (above USD 250,000) should be pre-notified to the AML compliance team and segmented by source to prevent 30-day holds.Grant disbursements and program-related investments use SWIFT MT103 direct credits to beneficiary bank accounts. Where grants finance procurement of goods or services, a documentary collection or Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) may be opened through an RMI, U.S., or Asian issuing bank. USD-denominated grants require no FX conversion; non-USD grants follow a daily spot FX trade and MT103 in the destination currency. Distributions above USD 100,000 must match the pre-cleared grantee due-diligence file, and each disbursement must be traceable to the foundation's audited financial statements and annual grant report.Deliverable FX Forwards – USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/JPY, USD/AUD and other major pairs, permitted for hedging non-USD endowment assets or non-USD grant commitments; tenors up to 12 months under an ISDA 1998 FX or master forward agreement with the relationship bank.Cross-Currency Swaps – USD versus major convertible currencies, permitted for duration-matched hedging of endowment principal and grant liabilities; requires an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and CSA credit support documentation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – USD/CNH, USD/INR, USD/IDR, USD/NGN and other restricted-convertibility currencies, permitted for emerging-market grant programs where onshore deliverable liquidity is unavailable; settled in USD at fixing rates.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

3% (Income Tax Act 1971, as amended) on RMI-source income of corporations carrying on business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Not applicable to this entity: as a Non-Resident Business Corporation holding a valid Non-Resident Certificate, the foundation is statutorily exempt from all Marshall Islands taxes and has no RMI-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax, no branch remittance tax, and no withholding tax of any kind under the Income Tax Act 1971 or any other statutory instrument. Dividends declared by the non-resident BC to foreign charitable beneficiaries or controlling donors are paid gross with no RMI tax deduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 30 June of each calendar year, covering corporate particulars as at the preceding 31 December. The first annual report is due in the calendar year following incorporation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months after the end of the relevant financial year. The default financial year is the calendar year unless an election to adopt a different accounting period has been accepted by the Registrar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — no local-currency filing threshold exists. The Marshall Islands has not enacted transfer pricing legislation and does not impose Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country Reporting obligations on non-resident business corporations. The functional currency of the Republic is the US dollar (USD), and statutory fees are denominated exclusively in USD. Thin capitalization, interest limitation, and debt-equity rules are likewise absent from RMI law.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Economic Substance Regulations Act 2019, as amended, entered into force for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2021 and requires every RMI-registered relevant entity to file an annual economic substance declaration (ESF-1). An entity engaged in any of the nine statutory relevant activities must demonstrate economic substance in the RMI — direction and management in the RMI, adequate employees, premises, and expenditure — or prove tax residence in a foreign jurisdiction. Critical classification: a private charitable foundation conducting endowment management and grant distribution (ISIC 6430 / NAICS 813211) is not a Collective Investment Vehicle under the RMI Mutual Funds Act and therefore does not conduct the statutory activity of 'fund management.' The correct ongoing posture is to file the ESF-1 each year declaring no relevant activity. The foundation must, however, diligently preserve its non-resident status: no RMI-source income, no habitual board meetings in the Republic, no locally employed staff, and no RMI real property. If asset-holding expands to active investment management of portfolio companies, the 'holding company' category may be triggered, in which case a reduced substance test applies and foreign tax residence must be documented.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of the Non-Resident Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive assessment of 3% corporate income tax on all RMI-source income under the Income Tax Act 1971, plus statutory monthly interest on unpaid tax and late-payment penalties reaching 100% of the tax due; fines of up to US$10,000 for false certification; administrative strike-off and loss of the non-resident tax exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories of the false annual report may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax (civil recovery piercing the corporate veil), barred from holding future RMI registrations, and criminally prosecuted with fines of up to US$25,000 and/or imprisonment not exceeding 3 years per false certification.
Economic Substance Offence / Shell Company Determination

Statutory sanction: Fines beginning at US$5,000 for late filing, escalating through US$10,000 for defective returns and up to US$50,000 for sustained non-compliance; court-ordered strike-off and dissolution; referral of the entity to the Global Forum on Transparency and Exchange of Information for Exchange of Information on Request (EOIR), potentially triggering foreign tax investigations in grantor-donor jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers knowingly causing the contravention are jointly and severally liable for corporate fines; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years; travel bans may be imposed pending cooperation with foreign tax information requests, and adverse regulatory findings will be shared across the AEOI network.

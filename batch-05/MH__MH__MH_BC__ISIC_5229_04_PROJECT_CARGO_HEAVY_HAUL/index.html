Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue Division, Ministry of Finance & Administration (RMI Tax Portal: www.rmi-invest.com for entity licensing; customs administration via Marshall Islands Customs Service under the Ministry of Finance)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Business Corporation must first register with the RMI Registrar of Corporations to obtain a Certificate of Incorporation and corporate registration number. For the purpose of local tax identity, the entity must subsequently apply for a Business License from the Ministry of Finance & Administration / Revenue Division. There is no unified TIN under Marshall Islands law; the Business License number and CAGE/company registration number collectively serve as the statutory tax identifier for customs, social security, and tax filings. Foreign entities may file online through the Registrar, appoint a registered agent in the RMI, and satisfy any minimum registered-office requirement. No minimum capital is required. Non-resident ownership is allowed without restriction.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable under the VAT/GST framework; no value-added tax registration threshold exists. A business license is required for any commercial activity regardless of turnover. For customs purposes, importers are required to be registered with the Marshall Islands Customs Service for clearance of equipment and project cargo.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because the Marshall Islands does not levy VAT or GST on exports. There is therefore no zero-rating mechanism for export of goods or offshore services. The indirect tax system is limited to import duties and excise taxes applied at the point of entry into the territory. Re-export of goods that were previously imported under duty-bond or temporary-import arrangements may qualify for draw-back or by-bond relief under customs rules, but such relief is a duty remission rather than a VAT zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward cargo manifest clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment/milestone settlement evidence

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Customs declarations are processed through the customs administration on standard commercial documents, with no mandatory e-invoicing integration.

For any customs duty draw-back or temporary-export relief claimed on project cargo and heavy haul equipment, the Marshall Islands Customs Service may conduct post-clearance audits requiring proof of actual export, cargo arrival at destination, and inward remittance. False declarations, overstatement of duty relief, or failure to satisfy export documentation may result in immediate duty clawback, monetary fines of up to the value of the goods, suspension of the business license, and potential criminal liability under the RMI Customs Code. There is no VAT clawback because no VAT is imposed.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no independent central bank conducting traditional monetary policy; the US dollar is the sole legal tender. The Central Bank of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (CBRMI) was established as the national financial supervisory authority. Regarding convertibility, both capital and current account transactions are fully liberalized. There is no separate exchange control act, no surrender or repatriation requirement, and profit remittance is untrammeled. Dividends, royalties, interest, and liquidation proceeds payable by an RMI Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders may be freely remitted in US dollars without central bank authorization. The only hurdle is the US dollar clearing constraint, whereby the RMI uses the US Federal Reserve wire system for cross-border settlement, meaning any sanctioned counterparty or OFAC-prohibited transaction can be blocked by US correspondent banks. For the target OOG project cargo logistics entity, the key practical limitation is not a domestic exchange control restriction but the extraterritorial reach of US sanctions and AML/CTF legislation, requiring enhanced counterparty screening for cargo destined to high-risk jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion percentages for export receipts in the Marshall Islands. The RMI does not mandate repatriation of export proceeds, does not impose any timeline for collection, and does not require conversion of foreign-currency earnings into local currency. Because the US dollar is legal tender, exports are naturally invoiced and settled primarily in USD. If a project cargo logistics company receives proceeds through an RMI corporate bank account, the funds may remain in that USD account indefinitely or be transferred abroad without reporting or approval. Nonetheless, under the RMI Banking Act and the CBRMI supervisory framework, domestic banks must file suspicious transaction reports and maintain records of any transaction exceeding the threshold set by the bank's AML/CFT compliance program, but no foreign exchange surrender certificate or BOP report is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (CBRMI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low. Offshore digital EMI solutions may provide non-resident USD IBAN and SWIFT routing for operational disbursements, but the RMI does not have a local passporting regime under PSD2. EMI incorporation in another jurisdiction such as Lithuania, the United Kingdom, or Singapore, or a US state-chartered non-bank money transmitter, is not a licensed Marshall Islands bank, and cannot hold an RMI corporate account as a depository institution. An RMI Business Corporation may open a treasury management account with an EMI for outbound disbursements, but the compliance team must assess whether the EMI is subject to US correspondent banking restrictions. Because the RMI is not a member of the EEA and lacks financial services passporting, settlement of large milestone wires with heavy-haul logistics counterparties should be executed primarily through a full-service US correspondent bank to avoid aggregate transaction limits and indiscriminate account freezing. The recommended architecture is a tiered liquidity structure: Bank of Guam for capital escrow, Bank of Marshall Islands for local operating expenses, and a non-US EMI only for small satellite vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections may be routed through a correspondent bank wire in US dollars. The typical compliant protocol is a SWIFT MT103 transfer from the ultimate beneficial owner's or parent company's account to the RMI entity's account at Bank of Guam or Bank of Marshall Islands. The remittance must specify: purpose of remittance as 'Capital Contribution / Paid-In Capital', a detailed beneficiary account reference to the RMI Business Corporation ledger entry, and the reason code 'CAPT' or 'CURB' supported by a corporate resolution approving the capital increase. In practice, US dollar wire transfers to RMI banks are cleared through the US Federal Reserve system; therefore the corporate resolution and an internal capital-payment authorization should be retained in the compliance file to answer any request for information from the receiving bank. For transactions above the CBRMI threshold, no central reporting is mandatory, but banks apply FATF Recommendation 16 travel rule and record the originator and beneficiary information. The bank must not process an inbound transfer if the remitter is on any sanctioned list; thus the entity must provide an updated shareholder and ultimate beneficial ownership disclosure before the inbound transfer is requested.The standard clearing rail for high-value trade disbursements is the SWIFT MT103 direct wire. For milestone payments to sub-contractors, carriers, stevedores, and heavy-lift equipment owners, the MT103 provides same-day federal wire finality if both banks are within the US clearing system. For large project cargo milestone settlements under enforceable contracts, a commercial Letter of Credit (proceeds payable by a downstream issuing bank) is the second-tier rail. This is especially compliant when the RMI entity is the LC beneficiary or when it issues deferred payment instructions under the LC. Use of a Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A) is possible but operationally inferior for high-value heavy-haul corridors. For trade settlements involving non-US counterparties, the MT103 should include structured purpose codes such as USTP (settlement of trade) or depending on underlying contract COST. The bank may also require a copy of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and the specific milestone certificate to ensure the disbursement qualifies as a current account trade payment under the RMI's free-exchange system.USD-denominated deposits require no FX hedging because the RMI uses the US dollar domestically.Forward contracts in FDIC-regulated or US state-chartered banking counterparties for major currencies (EUR, GBP, CNY, AUD) where project costs are invoiced in a non-USD currency.Cross-currency swaps with a US or European bank counterparty to monetize a rolled-up project cargo charter hire in USD.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only when transacted through a licensed US or foreign bank with a legal opinion confirming no US financial sanctions violation; such NDFs provide economic hedge for transportation-related currencies that are non-convertible.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies in the Marshall Islands; RMI has not enacted transfer pricing rules or country-by-country reporting obligations. Non-resident BCs are not required to prepare Local Folder or Master File.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands does not administer an Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to Barbados. The 0% tax status is available only to non-resident domestic corporations that do not carry on trade or business in RMI and are managed and controlled outside the Republic. For an OOG/heavy haul project logistics entity, the BC must maintain board minutes and contracts executed offshore, and must avoid RMI ports, crew sign-on, or revenue sourced from RMI waters; otherwise the RMI Income Tax Act may deem the company resident and expose 3% corporate income tax on RMI-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misrepresentation of Non-Resident Status

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Income Tax Act, misrepresentation may result in immediate assessment of 3% corporate tax on undeclared RMI-source income, plus an interest charge of 1% per month and a negligence penalty of 50% of the understated tax. Criminal referral to the RMI Attorney General for willful evasion can impose fines up to USD 10,000 per offense.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud or sham arrangements. Directors who signed false declarations are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, restitution, and civil damages; a director may receive a foreign travel ban and up to two years imprisonment under RMI criminal tax provisions.
Annual Franchise Tax and Registration Failure

Statutory sanction: The Registrar will mark the corporation as delinquent, impose late penalties, and after further noncompliance administratively dissolve the company. A dissolved BC has no legal capacity to execute contracts, open bank accounts, or own litigation rights.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to transact business after administrative dissolution are personally liable for all debts, and the corporate veil is automatically pierced. Directors may face criminal charges for operating an unregistered entity and restitution to harmed counterparties.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Combatting the Financing of Terrorism Act and Beneficial Ownership Regulations, penalties range from USD 5,000 to USD 100,000 per violation, plus revocation of the corporate license and freezing of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held criminally liable up to five years imprisonment for money laundering predicates. Civil forfeiture of all laundered proceeds may be enforced against the company and, upon showing of fraudulent transfers, against directors' personal assets.
Foreign Tax Residency Abuse (Shell Company Determination)

Statutory sanction: Foreign jurisdictions may void the RMI entity's separate status, reattributing all project cargo profits to the ultimate parent and imposing back taxes plus substantial penalties of 100% to 200% of avoided tax. RMI itself will not protect the entity under its non-resident exemption if it is not managed and controlled outside RMI.

Director personal liability: Directors of the holding company may be assessed for wrongful trading and subject to personal tax equal to 100% of the tax shortfall, travel restrictions, extradition, and imprisonment under foreign CFC rules when the RMI structure is a pure wrapper with no logistics substance at any operating level.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro Atoll; all filings are effected exclusively through a licensed Marshall Islands registered agent.

Governing statute

Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act (BCCA), Title 27 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code, principally Sections 27.101–27.121 (name, registered agent, and filing of Articles of Incorporation), Sections 27.301–27.361 (registered office and agent duties), and Sections 27.501–27.521 (directors and officers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form BCA-1), executed by the incorporator before a notary public and submitted to the Registrar of Corporations together with the initial directors' consents and the registered agent's certificate of acceptance.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Marshall Islands Business Corporation, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required. The Articles must authorize at least one class of shares; capital may be paid in cash, property, or services at a value fixed by the Board. In this formation the Board issues 500,000 common shares at US$0.0001 par value per share for aggregate consideration of US$100,000, declared fully paid and non-assessable.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Compliance Certificate issued by the RMI Fire Safety Division following inspection of fire suppression systems, hazardous material segregation, and egress routes; Structural Load Inspection Certificate prepared by a registered professional engineer certifying floor loads for laser and cleanroom equipment; and Occupational Health and Safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2007 issued by the RMI Occupational Health Office, covering machine guarding, Class 4 laser interlocks, ventilation, and personal protective equipment programs.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Marshall Islands Business Corporation, and no escrow or capital deposit account is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; corporate registry and Certificate of Good Standing: Registrar of Corporations, Association of the Marshall Islands Companies (AMIC) portal

E-government filing portal

The RMI issues no general statutory TIN to non-resident Business Corporations. The operative identifier is the corporate registration number and Certificate of Good Standing issued by the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act 1990, procured by filing Articles of Incorporation (domestic non-resident designation), paying the registration fee, and designating a qualified registered agent in the RMI. If the entity maintains RMI payroll, it must separately register with the RTO as an employer for Wages and Salaries Tax and Social Security purposes and obtain an employer/taxpayer account number. No VAT ID exists or is obtainable because the RMI imposes no VAT, GST, or sales tax. A pure non-resident BC with no RMI-source income has no periodic income tax filing obligation and must only maintain the annual franchise fee/registration and registered agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident domestic corporation exemption under the Business Corporations Act; RMI-source income remains taxable under the Income Tax Act 1989 within the territorial scope)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under RMI law. Statutory status mechanisms available: (i) incorporation as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under the BCA to secure exemption from RMI income tax and all other RMI taxes on non-RMI-source income; (ii) continuation/domestication of a foreign corporation into a non-resident domestic corporation; (iii) election to cancel non-resident status by filing a resident-domestic designation, which subjects the entity to RMI-source taxation; (iv) no hybrid mismatch, fiscal unity, or qualified intermediary elections are recognized by the RTO.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; the sole indirect fiscal levy is customs import duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1990, imposed exclusively on physical imports into the RMI, with no equivalent tax on exports or services)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no turnover threshold triggers any RMI indirect tax registration. Import duty liability arises per consignment on the customs value of goods entering the RMI; HS 9021 (intra-arterial stents) are generally duty-exempt or subject to minimal tariff under the prevailing schedule. Exports are entirely outside any indirect tax base, and no export duty or VAT registration threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — since the RMI imposes no VAT/GST, there is no 0% export rate to claim and no input VAT credit or refund mechanism. Exports of coronary drug-eluting stents from the RMI fall outside the RMI indirect tax base entirely. Compliance substitutes: (i) RMI Customs export entry for trade statistics; (ii) health-sector export/manufacturing authorization for Class III medical devices under RMI health and sanitation regulations; (iii) banking AML/KYC documentation under SWIFT wire and Letter of Credit rails; (iv) no requirement to substantiate export zero-rating to the RTO because no RTO indirect tax filing exists.

  • Commercial Invoice (manufacturer/proforma invoice)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air)

  • Certificate of Origin (RMI issuer)

None — the RMI mandates no real-time fiscal clearance, SAF-T/XML e-invoicing, QR/digital stamping, or API-based invoice validation. Border declarations are processed through the customs administration (ASYCUDA-based entry lodgement), which requires only standard commercial documentation (customs entry, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill) with no statutory invoice clearance protocol.

No export VAT zero-rating or clawback regime exists because the RMI imposes no indirect tax on exports. Exposure arises if the entity is re-characterized as earning RMI-source income (e.g., RMI payroll, RMI warehousing, RMI domestic sales), enabling the Revenue and Taxation Office to assess income tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 at statutory rates, plus interest and late-payment penalties, subject to a 3-year assessment limitation period absent fraud. Customs false-declaration penalties (fines and forfeiture) attach under the Customs Tariff Act 1990 for inaccurate export entries. Anti-money-laundering penalties under the Crimes Act and RMI AML regulations may attach to incomplete beneficial ownership or payment-rail documentation on LC/SWIFT transactions, including corporate fines, director disqualification, and suspension of the entity's registered status for filings made in bad faith.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is legal tender and the sole circulating currency, making both current account and capital account transactions fully convertible without domestic exchange controls. There are no restrictions on inward capital, outward profit remittances, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital, provided that the underlying transactions are legitimate and supported by audited financial statements, beneficial ownership declarations, and tax residency certificates. The practical hurdle is not convertibility but anti-money-laundering compliance and correspondent bank due diligence, especially for non-resident Business Corporations engaged in high-risk medtech manufacturing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained, converted, or remitted in U.S. dollars without Central Bank approval; all such receipts must be reconciled with commercial invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations solely for AML/KYC monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Division of Financial Institutions, Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance; Republic of the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit; no domestic central bank exists and the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as de facto monetary authority through official dollarization

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no licensed fintech EMI framework for offshore digital banks; unlicensed financial intermediation is prohibited under the Banking Act and the Model Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Act. A foreign EMI may be used only if it holds a valid banking or electronic money license in a reputable jurisdiction and establishes a flow-through U.S. dollar account via a correspondent bank; however, RMI-supervised banks generally view offshore EMIs as high-risk and will require enhanced due diligence, a legal opinion, and evidence of licensing and regulatory oversight before accepting routing or settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted in U.S. dollars by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for larger interbank transfers, routed to the entity’s USD account held at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam, with the latter clearing via U.S. correspondent banks through Fedwire or CHIPS. The wire must reference the entity ID, the share subscription agreement number, and the stated purpose code as 'Capital injection / equity contribution' to avoid misclassification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective rate for a Non-Resident Business Corporation deriving no income from sources within the Republic of the Marshall Islands; statutory corporate rates of 3% on the first US$10 million of RMI-source taxable income and 5% on RMI-source taxable income exceeding US$10 million apply only to resident corporations or non-resident corporations with RMI-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act 1989 imposes no withholding tax on dividends, deemed dividends, or liquidating distributions paid by a Marshall Islands Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders. Dividends may be repatriated freely via Swift wire without RMI tax clearance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the 15th day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year (i.e., April 15 for a calendar-year corporation); the RTA may grant a written extension of up to 6 months upon timely application.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed during the period January 1 through March 31 of each calendar year, reporting as of the preceding December 31; the Registrar's fee is US$75 for a non-resident domestic corporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Marshall Islands Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country Reporting threshold in US dollars. The Income Tax Act 1989 empowers the Secretary of Finance to reallocate income, deductions, or credits between related persons if transactions are not conducted at arm's length. In practice, contemporaneous documentation is advisable only where the company derives RMI-source income; for multinational groups, OECD Country-by-Country Reporting thresholds (consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million) apply in the ultimate parent jurisdiction, not in the Marshall Islands.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations 2019, enacted in response to EU Code of Conduct requirements, apply to entities carrying on 'relevant activities': banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centers, and intellectual property (IP) holding. Manufacturing of Class III coronary drug-eluting stents is not a listed relevant activity; however, if the Marshall Islands entity directly holds patents, trademarks, or regulatory data for the drug-eluting coating and derives income from licensing those assets, it may be classified as an IP holding company and must satisfy core income-generating activity tests. A non-resident entity that is tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction and supplies a Certificate of Tax Residence may claim exemption. All relevant entities must file an annual Economic Substance Declaration with the Registrar within 12 months of financial year-end, with up to US$10,000 in penalties for non-filing and continuing penalties for persistent non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of 50% of the tax deficiency, negligence penalty of 20%, monthly statutory interest on unpaid tax, criminal fine up to US$10,000, and imprisonment up to 3 years upon conviction; loss of the 0% exemption with retroactive assessment of the 3%/5% corporate rates.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false or fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the civil penalties; criminal conviction of an officer results in imprisonment, and extradition may be sought under mutual legal assistance channels.
Shell Company / Abuse of Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties: US$10,000 for failure to file the annual declaration, additional US$10,000 for continuing non-compliance, revocation of Certificate of Good Standing, and automatic exchange of information with OECD/EU member states, exposing the group to coordinated tax investigations in the market countries.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil and treat the company as an alter ego of its directors or parent, making directors personally liable for the group's unpaid foreign taxes and penalties; travel restrictions may be imposed through tax information exchange and mutual assistance proceedings.
Unlawful Distribution / Fraudulent Trading

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act, directors who vote for or assent to an unlawful distribution are jointly and severally liable to the corporation for the full amount of the distribution, plus interest; fraudulent conveyance statutes allow avoidance of the transfer and recovery of the assets.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal restitution orders to repay unlawful dividends, disgorgement of profits, asset freezes, and court-ordered prohibitions on acting as directors or officers of any company registered in the Marshall Islands.
Money Laundering / Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering Act 2011 (as amended): fine up to US$50,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering; the Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze bank accounts; the special license may be suspended or revoked; enhanced due-diligence obligations apply to all payment rails, including Letters of Credit and Swift wires.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers with knowledge or reckless disregard of the laundering scheme face joint criminal liability, personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, and extradition under the US Compact of Free Association framework.
Administrative Dissolution / Loss of Limited Liability

Statutory sanction: The Registrar strikes the company off the register and revokes the Certificate of Incorporation; the corporation loses legal capacity to contract, sue, or defend; all privileges of the 0% tax regime lapse; reinstatement is discretionary and requires payment of all arrears, penalties, and a reinstatement fee.

Director personal liability: After dissolution, directors and officers are personally liable for all debts and contractual obligations incurred in the company's name; the statutory shield of limited liability is removed, and creditors may collect directly against personal assets.

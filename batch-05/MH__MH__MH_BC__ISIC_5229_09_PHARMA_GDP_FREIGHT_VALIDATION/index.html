Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is required under the Business Corporations Act; the authorised capital may be nominal in amount and no statutory escrow or capital trust arrangement is mandated. Shares are issued at par or stated value and share subscriptions are payable to the company accordingly.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification in accordance with NFPA/IPC standards covering cold-storage electrical rooms, sprinkler coverage and egress capacity; structural clearance from the RMI Building Authority for warehouse racking and floor-load limits; and an occupational-safety clearance under the RMI Occupational Safety and Health Code, including temperature-adaptive PPE protocols, ergonomic workstations and powered industrial truck operator licensing.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance and Investment, Revenue and Taxation Division (RTD); official online portal for tax filings and TIN administration: https://www.rmi-finance.org/tax; customs and trade portal: https://www.rmi-customs.org

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) may obtain an RMI Tax ID Number (TIN) and, where required, a Gross Revenue Tax registration by: (1) filing a BC-1 Registration Application with the RMI Registrar of Corporations in Majuro; (2) submitting the endorsed business license, Certificate of Good Standing, and ownership attestation to the RTD; and (3) completing Form NC-RA (Non-Resident Company Registration Acknowledgment) if the BC declares no local revenue. A TIN is not mandatory for purely foreign-source operations unless the BC employs RMI residents, owns RMI real property, imports physical goods for its own use, or derives gross revenue sourced in the Marshall Islands. In such cases, a TIN is issued within 7 business days after the supporting documents are accepted.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-Resident Business Corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act (BCA). The BC is a limited liability legal entity distinct from shareholders and is exempt from RMI income tax and gross revenue tax on all income derived outside the Republic. It may invoice foreign freight and logistics services without local withholding or branch tax. The territorial scope of taxation covers only income sourced within RMI under the Income Tax Act, Title 54, RMI Revised Code. — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) may obtain an RMI Tax ID Number (TIN) and, where required, a Gross Revenue Tax registration by: (1) filing a BC-1 Registration Application with the RMI Registrar of Corporations in Majuro; (2) submitting the endorsed business license, Certificate of Good Standing, and ownership attestation to the RTD; and (3) completing Form NC-RA (Non-Resident Company Registration Acknowledgment) if the BC declares no local revenue. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-Resident Business Corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act (BCA). The BC is a limited liability legal entity distinct from shareholders and is exempt from RMI income tax and gross revenue tax on all income derived outside the Republic. It may invoice foreign freight and logistics services without local withholding or branch tax. The territorial scope of taxation covers only income sourced within RMI under the Income Tax Act, Title 54, RMI Revised Code.

Available election forms

The BCA permits every BC to elect non-resident status by filing an 'Election of Non-Resident Status' instrument with the Registrar of Corporations, which permanently exempts all non-RMI-source income from taxation. No U.S.-style check-the-box election is available; entity classification is statutory. Mutual Agreement/Exchange of Information provisions under RMI double-taxation avoidance arrangements may be invoked to prevent hybrid mismatch outcomes, and a BC treated as a reverse-hybrid by a foreign parent may elect transparent treatment in its foreign filing only where the tax treaty expressly recognizes the RMI corporate form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the only indirect tax instruments are import duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1997 and a local gross revenue tax on specified services rendered inside RMI. Exports of goods and offshore services are outside the indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT registration because no national VAT/GST system exists. Import duty registration is required for consignees importing pharmaceuticals or logistics equipment into RMI and is calculated on an ad valorem rate of 8% (essential pharmaceutical) to 15% (logistics machinery), with a de minimis exemption for shipments valued below USD 100. Gross revenue tax registration applies to any entity (including an otherwise non-resident BC) that derives gross revenue from RMI-source local logistics services exceeding USD 12,000 per assessment year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because no VAT/GST is levied. The RMI Income Tax Act achieves an equivalent result by taxing only income sourced within the Marshall Islands. Goods transshipped or processed for export, and offshore GDP logistics services performed between foreign ports, are zero-rated de facto as non-RMI-source. A non-resident BC claiming zero local tax must demonstrate — through contracts, cargo manifests, and conveyance logs — that GDP freight services are rendered outside RMI territorial waters and/or between non-RMI locations.

  • RMI Combined Sea/Air Export Declaration (Form C-1, submitted via RMI-SW)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Airway Bill, clean on-board)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (issued by an RMI licensed bank if the marine freight proceeds are received in USD into an RMI account)
  • Certificate of Non-Resident Source Income (Form NR-10, affirmed by the RTD)
  • GDP Compliance Transport Record (for pharma cold-chain validation under ISIC 5229)

There is no electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, no government-certified e-API, and no digital stamping obligation in RMI. Standard commercial invoices and pro forma documents are accepted in PNG/PDF or paper form. For customs, RMI relies on a national single-window e-clearance portal (RMI-SW) that accepts electronic export declarations and bills of lading; physical stamped export certificates are issued as verified records but are not required for tax clearance.

Fraudulent claims of foreign-source exemption or false declarations of non-resident status are prosecuted under RMI Revised Code Title 54, Sections 523 and 655. Criminal penalties include a restitutionary clawback equal to three times the entire unpaid gross revenue tax, a fine not exceeding USD 50,000, and imprisonment up to three years. Customs false-export statements additionally trigger forfeiture of the goods and conveyance and a civil penalty of USD 10,000 per violation. The RTD performs targeted audits when export volumes exceed 10,000 dose-equivalent units per quarter or when freight invoices are not accompanied by third-party port-storage or cross-border temperature logs.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI has no domestic currency, so the distinction between current and capital account convertibility is effectively moot: both are freely convertible in USD. Profit remittances and dividends from a non-resident tax-exempt Business Corporation are unrestricted and not subject to RMI withholding or exchange approval. Residual hurdles flow from (i) correspondent de-risking applied to RMI-registered legal entities, (ii) OFAC and financial sanctions screening by USD clearing banks, and (iii) the bank's own requirement to prove source of funds. Entities are advised to maintain full UBO and trade documentation to minimise clearing delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: RMI law imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no minimum conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. USD export receipts may be held freely; non-USD receipts may be converted at the commercial bank's customary margin. The absence of domestic prudential requirements makes retention purely a contractual or treasury-policy matter between the BC and its bankers.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists. The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) is fully dollarized; the United States dollar is the sole legal tender. Banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight are exercised by the RMI Banking Commission (Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance) under the Banking Act 1990, as amended, and the Financial Institutions Act 2015. No RMI authority administers foreign exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Selected offshore EMIs (e.g., FCA-regulated UK e-money institutions or US state-licensed money transmitters) may accept an RMI Business Corporation after enhanced due diligence, but most mainstream EMIs decline RMI-registered clients because of the jurisdiction's shell-company risk profile. As a legal constraint, the EMI must hold a valid e-money or payment licence in its home jurisdiction; the RMI BC cannot itself conduct banking business under the Banking Act 1990. Recommended architecture: use the EMI solely as a collection/distribution node, with final settlement and liquidity held at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam.

0% — the Marshall Islands does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI does not prescribe any Local File/Master File threshold or contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation obligation for a tax-exempt non-resident BC. International best-practice defense thresholds: prepare a Local File when related-party services or intangibles exceed USD 1,000,000 or goods exceed USD 5,000,000; prepare a Master File when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (OECD ALP alignment).

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to registered entities carrying on relevant activities, including 'Distribution and Service Center', which captures GDP pharma freight and logistics. An exempt non-resident BC must either (i) demonstrate adequate RMI substance — local employees, office premises, board governance, and core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, logistics management, client billing, inventory control) performed in the RMI; or (ii) claim an exemption by proving tax residence in a qualifying foreign jurisdiction through the annual ESR notification. The ESR annual notification is mandatory even when the exemption is claimed; the RMI Registrar exchanges information with foreign tax authorities under TIEAs. Non-compliance triggers administrative penalties, loss of exemption, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid GRT plus 10% monthly surcharge and 1.5% monthly interest (aggregate cap of 100% of the tax); criminal fine of up to USD 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 3 years for willful evasion; revocation of the tax exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the false exemption declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and surcharges through civil restitution; culpable directors face criminal prosecution, travel bans pending trial, and debarment from serving as directors of RMI entities.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of up to USD 10,000 per violation plus continued daily penalties; up to USD 20,000 and imprisonment for willful ESR non-disclosure; mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; administrative strike-off and deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be disqualified and held personally liable for resulting corporate debts on an 'alter ego' theory; the RMI court may lift the corporate veil where the BC is used as a conduit, exposing directors to civil restitution for unpaid taxes and third-party claims.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiency on Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT fines of up to USD 100,000 per breach for the corporate entity; funds subject to confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime regime; suspension of licensed payment activity; referral to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines of up to USD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years for knowingly enabling money laundering; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; directors are additionally exposed to civil suits by defrauded counterparties.
Misrepresentation to Registrar / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to USD 5,000 per false statement; the Registrar may strike off the BC, void its filings, and issue a public compliance notice; loss of the 50-year exemption certificate for non-disclosure of non-resident status.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false filings are personally liable for the regulatory fines and any resulting tax assessments; criminal liability attaches upon proof of fraudulent intent to mislead the Registrar or foreign tax authorities, with imprisonment of up to 2 years.

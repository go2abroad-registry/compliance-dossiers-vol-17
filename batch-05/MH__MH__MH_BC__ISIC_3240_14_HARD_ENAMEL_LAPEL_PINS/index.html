Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance (portal: https://www.mof.gov.mh)

E-government filing portal

The Marshall Islands does not impose a general income tax on Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporations. A foreign-owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to obtain a national income tax TIN. If the BC maintains a local payroll, it must register as an employer with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration and obtain a Social Security registration number for withholding and contribution purposes. The BC must also obtain a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) from the Office of the Foreign Investment Board if carrying on any local business activity; this is a business licensing requirement, not a tax registration requirement. No VAT or GST identification number exists in the RMI.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Marshall Islands does not impose a general income tax on Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporations. A foreign-owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to obtain a national income tax TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No local RMI check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime exists. A Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation is a juridical person with full legal personality and is not eligible for pass-through treatment under RMI law. For U.S. federal tax purposes, if relevant, the Marshall Islands Business Corporation is treated as a per se corporation, meaning no Form 8832 check-the-box election is available. No hybrid mismatch election can be made under RMI statute.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; indirect fiscal impositions are limited to customs import duty and social security contributions

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no general consumption tax registration threshold. Customs import duty is triggered at the time of entry of goods into RMI customs territory, irrespective of turnover, and is settled through the customs clearance process.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not impose VAT or GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate and no VAT input credit refund mechanism. Goods exported from the RMI are simply outside the scope of any indirect tax. Duty suspension or remission may be available under the Customs Act for qualifying re-export or transshipment operations, but this is not a VAT zero-rating regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Export Manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice describing the hard enamel lapel pins and ISIC 3240 classification
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-supporting proof of foreign proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate applies to domestic commercial invoices. RMI Customs uses ASYCUDA World for electronic cargo declarations and customs clearance; exporters and importers must file electronic declarations through the customs portal. Paper invoices remain acceptable for commercial purposes.

Because no VAT/GST zero-rating exists, there is no separate export zero-rating clawback penalty. However, any fraudulent or false customs declaration made to obtain a duty exemption, remission, or tariff preference is subject to RMI Customs penalties, including seizure of goods, mandatory payment of all duties and taxes, monetary fines of up to USD 10,000, and imprisonment for natural persons. The Minister of Finance may also retrospectively revoke any customs concession and recover the full duty shortfall. Export records and supporting bank remittance evidence must be retained for at least five years.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for Non-Resident Business Corporation (RMI Income Tax Act 1989 does not tax non-resident domestic corporations on non-RMI-source income; no general corporate income tax for this exempt class).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — no dividend withholding tax is imposed by the Marshall Islands on distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No tax filing deadline. The Annual Report and renewal fee are due on or before the entity's registration anniversary date (subject to registered-agent filing calendar and any grace period under RMI Business Corporations Act).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
N/A – No income tax form RMI does not impose income tax on this non-resident BC. The company must annually confirm no RMI-source income, maintain registered agent and registered office in RMI, and update directors/shareholders. Non-resident BC should submit a declaration of non-residence and avoid any local trading, employment, or permanent establishment in the Marshall Islands, otherwise tax exemption may be lost. No tax late-filing surcharge exists for income tax. Late Annual Report/registration renewal incurs late fees and penalty interest under RMI Registrar regulations; continued non-compliance leads to dissolution/strike-off and loss of limited liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold — RMI law does not impose mandatory Local File/Master File. Because the entity is tax-exempt non-resident, no RMI transfer-pricing documentation is required. For foreign disclosure, prudent threshold: US$1,000,000 in controlled transactions (USD local currency).

Economic substance scope: RMI has adopted Economic Substance Regulations aligned with EU Code of Conduct standards. They apply to entities carrying on geographically mobile relevant activities (e.g., distribution, services) or entities claiming RMI tax residence. A pure non-resident BC that only owns and licenses enamel-pin IP/merchandise and has no RMI management-and-control generally falls outside ESR scope, but must not create de facto RMI tax residence through central management and control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Non-Resident Declaration

Statutory sanction: Loss of tax-exempt status; reassessment of taxable profits by RMI tax authorities; penalties under RMI Income Tax Act 1989 including monetary fines (up to US$5,000 per false return) and, on conviction, imprisonment; cancellation of registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed false declarations or directed the evasion are personally liable for unpaid taxes, fines, and costs; RMI courts may pierce the corporate veil where the BC is an alter ego, exposing directors to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Sham / Shell Company Used for Foreign Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: RMI Registrar may strike off/dissolve an inactive shell; foreign tax authorities may apply CFC, GAAR, or substance-over-form rules to reallocate income and impose penalties (typically 25%–100% of the understated tax, with interest); criminal referral possible if fraud is documented.

Director personal liability: Directors approving sham royalty/licensing arrangements may be named in foreign penalty proceedings; civil restitution, asset freezes, international travel bans, and extradition or director-ban orders may follow where they acted in bad faith.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act and companion regulations, non-compliance attracts monetary penalties against the entity (up to US$500,000), daily default surcharges, suspension or revocation of corporate registration, and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly facilitate money-laundering or sanctions evasion are criminally liable: personal fines, imprisonment (up to 10 years), exclusion from directorship, travel bans, and civil claims for restitution; the corporate veil will not protect deliberate misconduct.
Unauthorised Local Trading / Revocation of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: The non-resident BC is treated as a resident domestic corporation for the period of local activity; RMI tax authorities may assess income tax on the full profits, plus penalties and interest; the Registrar may revoke the company's existence for transacting beyond its declared capacity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause unauthorised local trading are jointly and severally liable for the company's resulting tax liabilities and administrative penalties; piercing the veil is available when the corporation is used as a shield for unlawful local enterprise.

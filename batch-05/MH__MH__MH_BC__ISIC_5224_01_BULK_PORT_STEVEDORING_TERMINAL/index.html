Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Treasury Division (Revenue and Taxation Bureau); official portal: https://www.rmitreasury.net/ and corporate registration portal of the RMI Registrar of Corporations. Customs and excise administration: RMI Customs Service, Ministry of Finance (Majuro).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act 1990 (modeled on Delaware GCL) through the Registrar of Corporations, filing Articles of Incorporation, By-laws, registered office and resident agent in Majuro. Step 2: File Form T-1 (Taxpayer Registration Application) with the RMI Treasury Division to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, proof of registered agent, and a statutory declaration that the corporation is a Non-Resident/Business Corporation deriving only foreign-source income. Step 3: If any physical or revenue-generating presence in RMI is contemplated, obtain a business license from the RMI Business License Division and the relevant stevedoring/port operating license from the Marshall Islands Ports Authority (MIPA). Step 4: Register with RMI Customs for import/export declarations. The entire procedure may be completed remotely; no minimum capital is required.

A non-resident corporation with no RMI-source income and no local permanent establishment is not subject to income tax or the 3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone; Territorial_Scope — a Non-Resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) is prima facie outside the RMI tax net. RMI employs a territorial system taxing only income sourced within the RMI. A non-resident corporation with no RMI-source income and no local permanent establishment is not subject to income tax or the 3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT). Physical stevedoring operations within the Majuro/Ebeye port limits would create RMI-source turnover unless covered by a port concession or statutory exemption.

Available election forms

RMI law contains no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election. Available elections: (a) electing exempt 'Non-Resident Business Corporation' status via Articles/By-laws designation, excluding entitlement to RMI domestic tax privileges in exchange for tax-exempt foreign-source treatment; (b) electing treatment as a Foreign Sales Corporation (FSC) under the RMI Foreign Sales Corporation Act for export trading activities, granting partial GRT/income tax exemptions on foreign trading gross receipts; (c) hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under RMI tax law, but the entity may enter into port concession agreements with MIPA to negotiate escrowed or exempt GRT treatment on terminal stevedoring fees.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use Tax. Indirect fiscal measures are limited to the RMI Customs Tariff Act 2003 (import duties, ad valorem schedules on imports, excise on fuel/tobacco/alcohol) and the 3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) on RMI-source business turnover. Exports are generally not subject to duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. For GRT purposes, the registration/filing threshold is US$10,000 in annual gross revenue derived from RMI-source business activity (RMI Income Tax Act 1989, as amended). Non-resident corporations earning only foreign-source revenue are below such threshold and not required to register for GRT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the RMI levies no VAT/GST, so no 0% export VAT rate or refundable input VAT mechanism exists. For indirect tax purposes, goods loaded for export at RMI marine docks are outside the customs import-duty net; no export duty applies to general merchandise. For GRT purposes, stevedoring services performed physically within RMI territorial waters/ports are prima facie RMI-source turnover assessed at 3%, unless (a) the fees are contractually charged to a non-resident vessel owner for offshore transit cargo and no local PE exists, or (b) the operator holds an FSC election or a port concession exemption. Billing, coordination, and managerial services rendered outside the RMI by the non-resident BC are foreign-source and excluded from GRT.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (Form CED-1) for controlled or enumerated exports
  • Ocean/Marine Bill of Lading evidencing export shipment from Majuro or Ebeye port
  • Dock Receipt and Terminal Manifest issued by the Marshall Islands Ports Authority (MIPA)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export/stevedoring proceeds in foreign currency
  • Stevedoring Service Agreement and Vessel Call Statement (to substantiate GRT source assessment and foreign-source allocation)
  • RMI Business License and MIPA Port Terminal Operator's License (regulatory compliance record)

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate exists in the RMI. Invoicing is paper-based; customs declarations to RMI Customs are accepted on RMI Customs Declaration forms (manual or scanned/email submission); clearance of exports follows the RMI Customs Tariff Act procedures with no e-invoicing or fiscal data interconnection.

The RMI maintains no foreign-exchange controls; the 180-day benchmark is a conservative documentation standard (aligned with IRC 994 FSC asset/income guidelines) rather than an RMI statutory deadline. Because no VAT exists, there is no VAT clawback regime. Audits by RMI Treasury may reopen GRT assessments for 3 years (6 years for fraud or omission); penalties for false foreign-source income declarations or improper exemption claims include up to 50% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest, suspension/revocation of the business license, and, under the Customs Tariff Act 2003, seizure and forfeiture of goods, fines up to five times the duty value, and criminal liability for false declarations. RMI Customs additionally may bar the terminal operator or its agent from future export clearances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the sole official currency; there is no separate domestic fiat money and, accordingly, no formal exchange-control apparatus. Capital and current account transactions are freely convertible and remittable, including dividends, management fees, and liquidation proceeds, subject only to standard anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) checks. Profits earned by a non-resident Business Corporation may be transferred abroad without Central Bank approval; however, the foreign payee should maintain complete corporate records, board minutes, and supporting invoices to satisfy the carrying bank's compliance review and any U.S. correspondent bank's sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or compulsory conversion rules exist for export receipts in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Stevedoring and port terminal operators may retain proceeds in U.S. dollars or remit them in settlement currencies, provided the associated commercial documentation is retained and all transactions are conducted through a licensed banking institution under the Banking Act 1991, the Civil Jurisdiction and Judgments Act, and the Terrorism and Transnational Organized Crime (Prevention) Act.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as the overseas issuer of the official circulating medium (U.S. dollar) by virtue of the Compact of Free Association

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. There is no dedicated e-money institution licensing regime in the Republic of the Marshall Islands, and the Banking Act confines deposit-taking and settlement account maintenance to licensed banks. An offshore digital EMI cannot hold client funds or open settlement accounts without first obtaining a banking license; the Bank of Marshall Islands and Bank of Guam act as the primary local clearing points, and their correspondent banking relationships are narrow. Non-bank payments institutions would require special dispensation from the Banking Commissioner, and the likelihood of approval is low for a pure offshore fintech vehicle without a physical office in Majuro.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 (no statutory threshold; no transfer pricing documentation requirement in the Marshall Islands because no corporate income tax is imposed).

Economic substance scope: Subject to the Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), relevant activities, including transportation and financial activities, require adequate substance in the jurisdiction. Because bulk-port stevedoring involves physical operations, local premises, and employed workers, the economic substance test is satisfied via actual management and operations where the terminal is located. Non-resident holding structures without substance risk being deemed tax-exempt shell companies and subjected to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution, remittance of unpaid taxes in foreign jurisdictions, civil penalties of up to USD 10,000 under local corporate law, and criminal fines of USD 25,000 plus imprisonment of up to 5 years in the Marshall Islands for knowingly false filing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fraudulent filings; travel bans and restraint orders can be imposed while sanctions and recovery actions are pending.
Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Escalated annual registration fees, cancellation of corporate existence, and denial of treaty or tax-exemption benefits; the regulator may publish the entity as lacking substance.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face civil claims for misrepresentation, clawback of distributions, and disqualification from serving as directors in any Marshall Islands entity.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing framework, fines up to USD 500,000 and terms of imprisonment of up to 10 years for natural persons; corporate license suspension.

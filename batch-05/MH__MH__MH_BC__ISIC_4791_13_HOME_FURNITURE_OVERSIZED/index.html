Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands – Ministry of Finance, Tax and Revenue Office (TRO), Majuro; official tax forms and taxpayer services via the TRO, with corporate registration administered separately by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (BCCA).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate under the BCCA via a licensed registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and corporate registration number (no minimum capital requirement). Step 2 – Classify the entity as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under Section 14 of the BCCA and obtain the government-issued tax exemption certificate; because no Marshall Islands-source income is derived, no income-tax TIN registration or filing is required. Step 3 – Register with the TRO (TIN issuance) only upon the creation of a local nexus (local payroll, premises, or imports into the Republic for local sale), which triggers wage tax, import tax, and gross revenue tax obligations. Step 4 – Submit the TIN application package comprising the Certificate of Incorporation, BCCA good-standing certificate, registered-agent consent, local business license (where applicable), and the completed TRO registration form. Step 5 – VAT identification: not applicable, as the Republic imposes no VAT or GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – A non-resident Business Corporation is wholly exempt from Marshall Islands income tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 read with Section 14 of the BCCA; the tax system is territorial, and foreign-source income from the online oversized furniture retail business falls outside the scope of Marshall Islands taxation.

Available election forms

Available election: Section 14 BCCA designation as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation, evidenced by a government-issued tax exemption certificate. No check-the-box, flow-through, or hybrid-mismatch elections exist in Marshall Islands law; exempt-company status is revocable on the company's application or upon breach of BCCA conditions, including transacting local business without TRO registration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the Republic of the Marshall Islands operates no VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax on supplies of goods or services; the only border impost is the customs Import Tax under the Customs Act 1990, which is levied on physical importation into the Republic and is not chargeable on offshore e-commerce sales by a non-resident seller.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. For completeness, the customs Import Tax applies to the local importer at the border (generally 3% ad valorem on most goods, with higher rates on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel); the official currency is the US dollar (USD), and no turnover-based indirect-tax registration or collection obligation applies to the non-resident seller.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – because no VAT/GST regime exists, no export zero-rating or VAT refund can be claimed in the Marshall Islands; exports of oversized furniture from the Republic are outside the scope of import tax, and the online retailer's cross-border sales are instead governed by the indirect tax rules of the destination jurisdiction, including any foreign VAT zero-rating or e-commerce collection regimes.

  • Packing list and container load plan for oversized and custom-sized items

Not applicable – the Republic has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime; customs declarations are filed electronically in the ASYCUDA World system, but no API-based tax clearance, continuous transaction control, or government e-invoicing mandate is imposed on commercial invoices.

No local indirect-tax clawback regime exists given the absence of VAT/GST; the material risks are (i) Marshall Islands Customs enforcement under the Customs Act 1990 – false or fraudulent export declarations attract forfeiture, monetary penalties of up to three times the value of the goods, and criminal liability; and (ii) destination-market customs and indirect-tax authorities, which may reject export evidence, reverse foreign zero-rating claims, and assess penalties and interest on the seller's invoices. The entity must retain the full export documentation bundle and the Stripe/Affirm settlement records for at least seven years. The Marshall Islands imposes no exchange controls and no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is a pipeline surveillance benchmark, not a legal mandate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender; the currency regime is fully convertible with no exchange controls. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including profit distribution, dividend remittances, capital repatriation, and loan repayments, are unrestricted and require no central bank approval. No distinction is drawn between residents and non-residents for FX purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists under Marshall Islands law. Exporters freely retain and utilise all export receipts in USD or any other convertible currency; the absence of a domestic currency eliminates any mandatory conversion ratio or timeline for surrender.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains no domestic central bank; the U.S. Federal Reserve System functions as the de facto monetary authority, and banking supervision rests with the Banking Commissioner under the Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) can open USD settlement accounts for an RMI-incorporated Business Corporation; however, they are not licensed or supervised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commissioner. Legal constraints include reliance on U.S. correspondent bank discretion, FATCA/CRS reporting asymmetries, and prohibitively restrictive use of EMI rails for escrowed capital or controlled foreign distribution. EMIs are viable for low-value trade disbursements but not as primary liquidity clearers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable; the RMI prescribes no transfer-pricing threshold, Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country Reporting. The threshold is therefore effectively USD 0 and no RMI TP documentation is required; groups must comply only with the TP documentation requirements of the jurisdiction that taxes the profits (e.g., the United States or the customer market country).

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to RMI-resident entities carrying on relevant activities. Distribution and service center activity, which may capture the online retail supply chain if managed and directed from RMI, is within the ESR framework. A non-resident domestic corporation that is not tax-resident in RMI and conducts no relevant activity in RMI must still file an annual ESR declaration; a company that claims non-resident status while being managed from RMI is a red flag. Failure to satisfy substance or to make the declaration exposes the company to RMI Registrar penalties, strike-off, and director disqualification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: RMI itself has no domestic income tax evasion offense because no income tax is imposed. The relevant sanction arises in the foreign jurisdiction: civil fraud penalties, accuracy penalties up to 75% of understated tax, back interest, criminal fines, and confiscation of the furniture e-commerce business assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false foreign tax returns, fraudulent invoices, or misleading transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for civil restitution of diverted taxes. Foreign criminal courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, extradition, and imprisonment for willful evasion. The RMI statutory protection does not cover deliberate fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may strike the company, dissolve it after default, and refuse reinstatement. Under the Economic Substance Regulations, failure to meet the substance test or to file the ESR declaration exposes the company to monetary penalties, deregistration, and director disqualification.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors operate the shell without genuine management. Nominee and shadow directors who knowingly facilitate the shell can be held personally liable for regulatory fines and third-party losses, and may face criminal sanctions for false declarations made to the Registrar.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The RMI Anti-Money Laundering regime imposes penalties on unlicensed financial service providers and on companies failing to maintain AML controls. Violations attract substantial fines, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment in the RMI or the foreign country where the funds are laundered.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or assist money laundering are criminally liable regardless of the corporate form. Courts may order civil restitution in favor of victims, impose travel bans pending extradition, and enter custodial sentences against directors who knowingly process illicit funds.
False Statements and Unauthorized Holding Out

Statutory sanction: False filings constitute a statutory offense under the Business Corporations Act; the RMI Registrar may cancel or forfeit the company. Misrepresentation to payment providers is independently actionable under Stripe and Affirm acceptable-use policies and results in permanent merchant termination.

Director personal liability: Directors who make or authorize a false filing cannot shield themselves behind the corporation; they are jointly and severally liable for the resulting fines, restitution to merchant processors, and damages. Repeat false statements can lead to director disqualification and criminal imprisonment.

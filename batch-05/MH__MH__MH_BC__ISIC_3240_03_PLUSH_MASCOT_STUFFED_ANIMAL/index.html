Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Treasury & Budget — Tax and Revenue Office (TRO), Majuro. The TRO administers the Income Tax Act and Sales Tax Act; filings are made in person at the TRO counter or through an RMI-licensed registered agent. There is no fully automated national online tax portal for offshore entities; statutory forms (Sales Tax Registration, sales tax returns, zero-rating certificates) are paper-based TRO forms.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign shareholders incorporate a non-resident Business Corporation (BC) under the RMI Business Corporations Act by filing Articles of Incorporation with the RMI Registrar of Business Corporations (Ministry of Finance) and paying the incorporation fee. Step 2: Appoint and maintain an RMI-licensed registered agent and registered office; the Certificate of Incorporation is the primary corporate identity document. Step 3: No RMI TIN or income-tax registration is issued to or required from the BC because a non-resident, tax-exempt BC with no RMI-source income is outside the RMI Income Tax Act. Step 4: If the BC engages in any RMI domestic economic activity (local sales, imports, physical premises), it must apply to the TRO for a Sales Tax Registration Certificate and TIN using TRO Form S-1 (Sales Tax Registration Application) and obtain a local government Business License. Step 5: For export zero-rating purposes, the registered sales taxpayer obtains TRO approval of its export status and files returns on the TRO schedule.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident, non-domiciled Business Corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act: exempt from RMI income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, and sales tax to the extent it derives no income from sources within the RMI and conducts no taxable domestic activity; a statutory tax-exemption period, typically up to 50 years, applies to a properly structured offshore BC). — Step 1: Foreign shareholders incorporate a non-resident Business Corporation (BC) under the RMI Business Corporations Act by filing Articles of Incorporation with the RMI Registrar of Business Corporations (Ministry of Finance) and paying the incorporation fee. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident, non-domiciled Business Corporation under the RMI Business Corporations Act: exempt from RMI income tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, and sales tax to the extent it derives no income from sources within the RMI and conducts no taxable domestic activity; a statutory tax-exemption period, typically up to 50 years, applies to a properly structured offshore BC).

Available election forms

No United States-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election is available, as the RMI imposes no income tax on tax-exempt Business Corporations. Other available statutory elections: (a) voluntary Sales Tax registration election for small traders below the mandatory threshold; (b) election to file separate or consolidated sales tax returns for multiple RMI business locations; (c) BCCA continuation/redomestication election to migrate corporate domicile into or out of the RMI while preserving the statutory tax exemption; (d) no functional-currency election is needed because the US dollar is the sole legal tender of the RMI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 10,000 annual gross turnover (sales and receipts) within the RMI; a person whose taxable sales/receipts exceed this threshold must register with the TRO, and persons below the threshold may register voluntarily. Imported goods are additionally subject to sales tax at the point of importation unless exempted or destined for export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% (zero) rate applies to taxable goods that are sold for export and are physically exported from the RMI within the contracted period (generally not exceeding 30 days after sale), and to services performed wholly outside the RMI. The claimant must be a registered sales taxpayer, must issue an invoice marked zero-rated export, and must prove that title and possession passed to an overseas customer. For the plush mascot OEM (ISIC 3240), export sales of custom plush dolls, embroidery, and ultrasonic-seaming output to offshore principals qualify for zero-rating; the burden of proving physical export rests on the exporter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Service) or approved electronic export manifest
  • Bill of Lading (ocean freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) issued to the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice marked 'ZERO-RATED EXPORT — RMI SALES TAX ACT 2014'
  • Purchase order or contract confirming the offshore customer and export terms

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate is in force. RMI operates a paper-based, self-assessment system: registered taxpayers file a simplified Sales Tax Return on a monthly/quarterly TRO schedule and retain invoices, sales slips, and export evidence in paper or electronic form. Invoices do not require TRO pre-clearance; substantiation occurs on TRO audit.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims are treated as sales-tax evasion. The TRO may retroactively assess the uncollected 12.5% tax plus statutory monthly interest (approximately 1%–1.5% per month or part month), impose a civil fraud penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded, and levy fines up to USD 10,000 per false document for a corporation; responsible officers may face imprisonment up to 5 years for willful evasion. The TRO may audit open years for up to 6 years (or 10 years where fraud is alleged) and claw back the zero-rating benefit, with the taxpayer bearing the burden of proving that the goods physically left RMI customs territory. TRO may also suspend or cancel the exporter's sales tax registration and zero-rating certificate.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the national currency and the RMI maintains no exchange-control apparatus. Both current account transactions (trade, services, profit remittances) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, repatriation of capital) are fully convertible with no prior authorization or registration requirements. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard AML/KYC documentation establishing beneficial ownership, source of funds, and the economic substance of the remittance instruction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or repatriation requirement exists; because export receipts are naturally denominated in U.S. dollars, the domestic legal tender, there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no forced sale to a central bank, and no prescribed timeline for bringing export proceeds onshore.

Central bank supervision: No independent central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; monetary oversight is exercised by the Financial Institutions Division (FID) of the RMI Ministry of Finance and the Office of the Banking Commissioner, with the United States dollar as sole legal tender and the U.S. Federal Reserve as the de facto monetary authority for currency supply.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low domestic viability: the RMI Banking Act and Financial Transactions Reporting Act require a full banking license for deposit-taking activities, so unlicensed overseas Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) cannot open local accounts or clear through the domestic banking system. Internationally licensed EMIs may offer payment services to a non-resident RMI Business Corporation only on a non-deposit basis, but such providers generally classify an RMI non-resident entity without a U.S. or regional operating nexus as a higher compliance risk and frequently decline onboarding. The practical feasibility rating is Low to Medium, with bank-to-bank SWIFT clearing remaining the preferred channel.

The data is held on a non-public government register accessible to competent authorities. Late filing of the annual report attracts a fixed penalty of USD 100 and a daily surcharge of USD 10 per day of continued delinquency. Failure to pay the annual franchise tax by the due date incurs a 10% late charge and continuing accrual at the daily rate. Persistent non-compliance, defined as more than 90 days' delinquency, results in involuntary dissolution by the Registrar, loss of legal shielding, and restriction on the corporation's right to commence any action in Marshall Islands courts. Directors are additionally subject to administrative fines under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local transfer pricing documentation threshold exists in the Marshall Islands; transfer pricing rules have not been substantively enacted under Title 20 or the Business Corporations Act. For non-resident exempt corporations the de facto threshold is zero, meaning no local file, master file, or country-by-country report is required to be filed with the Marshall Islands tax or corporate authorities. If the corporation is the parent of a multinational group and the ultimate parent jurisdiction mandates CbC reporting, such filing occurs in that foreign jurisdiction, not in the Marshall Islands.

Economic substance scope: Marshall Islands has adopted the Economic Substance Regulations as a regional standard in response to EU/OECD anti-tax-haven pressure. The applicable doctrine applies only to resident activities considered 'relevant activities' as defined in the Economic Substance Act 2018. Contract manufacturing of plush mascots in the form of OEM stuffed toys is not a specified relevant activity, and a non-resident exempt company having no local management or employees in the Marshall Islands is outside the scope. Nevertheless, to avoid being tagged as a shell company by foreign tax administrators, the entity must demonstrate economic substance in its actual operational jurisdiction: maintain a physical presence, employees, rental contracts, and decision-making functions in the country where the plush toy manufacturing occurs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Misuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: The exempt status is retroactively rescinded; the corporation becomes subject to Gross Revenue Tax at the prevailing rate of 5% on gross receipts for most businesses (banks and insurance at different rates), plus interest at 12% per annum on underpaid amounts and a negligence penalty of 20% of the understated tax. Fraudulent evasion carries an additional 50% penalty. The Registrar may strike the company from the register, and any asset transfer made to defeat tax liability is voidable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held jointly and severally liable for tax that the company was able to withhold, collect, and pay, where the failure is the result of wilful refusal or gross negligence. The Tax Enforcement Act permits the Commissioner of Internal Revenue to seek travel bans preventing directors from leaving the Marshall Islands, to levy attachments on personally held assets, and to institute criminal proceedings leading to imprisonment for up to five years for each count of tax evasion.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Although not sanctioned under Marshall Islands domestic law (because the Economic Substance Act does not capture a non-resident exempt company), the arrangement will be treated as a sham by foreign tax authorities. The resulting consequences include re-domestication of the entity, mandatory transparency penalties under the OECD Global Anti-Base Erosion Model Rules (Pillar 2), imposition of economic substance legislation penalties of up to USD 50,000 in the operational jurisdiction, and exchange of information pursuant to the Marshall Islands signed exchange of information arrangements with foreign tax administrations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign certificates of residency or beneficial ownership declarations that misrepresent economic activity expose themselves to a personal fine of up to USD 100,000 under the Economic Substance Act (if the entity is later deemed in scope) and to foreign enforcement actions under the common law of the operational jurisdiction. In serious cases the directors' own travel movements are subject to restrictions and asset freezes issued by foreign courts.
Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) may levy a civil penalty of up to USD 250,000 for each violation of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act 2012. The Minister of Finance may suspend or revoke the corporation's exemption certificate and impose a daily fine of USD 5,000 for continued non-compliance. Monetary penalties are in addition to criminal sanctions for wilful money laundering: a fine of up to USD 500,000 or the value of the laundered property (whichever is higher) and imprisonment for up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers bear primary responsibility for AML compliance. Individuals found to have knowingly participated in, facilitated, or concealed a money laundering transaction involving the plush toy corporation face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment of 10 to 15 years, forfeiture of personal and corporate assets, and prohibition from acting as a director in any Marshall Islands company. The FIU may also issue a travel ban to prevent the director from leaving the jurisdiction pending the criminal investigation.
Failure to Maintain Beneficial Ownership Records

Statutory sanction: Registrar-imposed penalty of USD 10,000 for each offence under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2019. The company is given a 14-day rectification notice; continued failure results in an additional daily penalty of USD 1,000 and the Registrar may issue a 'united filing strike' resulting in dissolution. For the manufacturing group this may be cascaded by foreign registries as a negative compliance indicator, potentially triggering disqualification from public procurement and export licensing.

Director personal liability: Every director and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for the penalty if the corporation fails to remedy the breach, and the Regulator may impose a personal penalty of up to USD 25,000 on each director and a temporary suspension of the registered agent's license. Director disqualification orders may also be issued for repeated breaches, prohibiting the director from acting in any other Marshall Islands entity for a five-year period.

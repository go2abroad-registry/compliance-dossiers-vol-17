Competent corporate registry

Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro; incorporation is filed by the incorporator or by a licensed registered agent acting on behalf of the non-resident entity.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act 1990, codified at Title 24, Chapter 1 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (MIRC); key sections: 30 (registered office), 45 (registered agent), 47 (corporate powers), 53 (articles of incorporation), 60 (organization of corporation), 163 (annual franchise tax) and 167 (Registrar fees).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form BCA-1), with the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar upon acceptance and payment of fees.

Incorporation processing time

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Majuro; Company registry: Office of the Registrar of Corporations (ORC) within the Attorney-General's Office, operating the official business registry portal at business.gov.mh. No separate income-tax authority exists for non-resident corporations because RMI imposes no direct national tax on them; customs matters are administered by the Customs Division, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors incorporate under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (BCA) via a licensed registered agent; the ORC issues a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique Corporate Registration Number, which functions as the de facto statutory corporate/TIN identifier. No income-tax TIN or annual tax return is issued or required for non-resident status. A separate Customs Importer/Exporter Registration Number is obtained from the Customs Division, Ministry of Finance (Majuro port) when the corporation conducts physical importation of raw polyresin or export of finished statues; this registration is fee-based, renewable annually, and requires a local process agent for non-resident entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign investors incorporate under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (BCA) via a licensed registered agent; the ORC issues a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique Corporate Registration Number, which functions as the de facto statutory corporate/TIN identifier. No income-tax TIN or annual tax return is issued or required for non-resident status. A separate Customs Importer/Exporter Registration Number is obtained from the Customs Division, Ministry of Finance (Majuro port) when the corporation conducts physical importation of raw polyresin or export of finished statues; this registration is fee-based, renewable annually, and requires a local process agent for non-resident entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under BCA Section 104 and Title 54 of the RMI Revised Code, a Business Corporation may elect non-resident status at incorporation by maintaining at least 50% foreign ownership and no commercial activity sourced within RMI; non-resident BCs are statutorily exempt from all RMI income, franchise, capital gains, and withholding taxes. No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under domestic RMI law; classification is a binary resident/non-resident designation recorded on the ORC register. Hybrid outcomes arise only when a US parent makes a US federal 'check-the-box' election under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3; RMI has no income tax treaty with the United States, only a 2013 Tax Information Exchange Agreement, so no treaty-based hybrid rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax); revenue derived from RMI Customs Import Duty, license fees, and marine registry fees only

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — RMI levies no VAT/GST or sales tax, and no turnover threshold exists. Import duty registration under Customs (Development and Consolidation) Act 2013 is compulsory for any person importing goods into RMI, regardless of value; the annual business license (USD 100–500 depending on activity) under Chapter 9 of Title 35 is also mandatory for the Majuro business site if any local sales activity occurs, though a pure non-resident exporter operating via the ORC with no local premises ordinarily holds no RMI business license.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Deemed zero-rated as outside the scope of any RMI consumption tax. RMI has no VAT regime, therefore exports of polyresin statues are not subject to any national indirect tax, and no registration or substantive approval is required to claim a zero rate. Exporters need only follow RMI Customs outward cargo manifest procedures under the Customs Act 2013 for goods leaving Majuro; statues exported physically must be documented on a standard Cargo Manifest/Export Declaration. Offshore export of digital or drop-shipped goods requires no RMI customs filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Outward Cargo Manifest, as required by RMI Customs Act 2013)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical shipments from Majuro
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Payment Record evidencing receipt of funds from the foreign buyer for the resin statue sale

None — the RMI has not implemented real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing APIs, digital tax stamping, or SAF-T reporting. Commercial invoices and bill of lading/airway bill remain paper or PDF-based; customs entries are processed manually or through the national single-window portal when available. No fiscal data exchange obligation applies to Stripe/PayPal prefunding or outward shipments.

Because no VAT is levied, the primary compliance risk is fraudulent customs declarations or false claims of foreign-owned non-resident status to obtain RMI tax exemption. Under the RMI Customs Act 2013, a false declaration carries fines of up to USD 5,000 or three times the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and up to one year imprisonment; duty preferential claims (e.g., raw resin entered duty-free) are subject to retrospective clawback within 5 years. Misrepresenting the foreign-ownership facts to the Registrar of Corporations can trigger cancellation of tax-exempt status, restoration of all taxes avoided, and penalties under Title 54 for malicious false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is sole legal tender, fully convertible de jure and de facto. The RMI operates a fully open capital account with no exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements on current or capital transactions, no dividend/profit remittance restrictions, and no withholding or repatriation charge for non-resident shareholders. Remittance flows are subject solely to bank-level AML/CFT screening and FATF-compliant transparency standards, not to governmental approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation applies. Because the domestic currency is identical to the export settlement currency (USD) and no central bank or FX administration exists, there is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in any commercial bank account or converted to third-country currencies at market rates via authorized correspondents.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Republic of the Marshall Islands (under the Financial Institutions Act 1987); U.S. Federal Reserve as de facto monetary authority due to formal dollarization — no domestic central bank or foreign exchange administration exists.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory local file, master file, or Country-by-Country Report threshold applies in USD for a Marshall Islands non-resident BC. The Marshall Islands has no corporate income tax, no transfer pricing statute, and no RMI CbCR filing obligation. Parent groups domiciled in jurisdictions implementing OECD BEPS Action 13 should apply their own group thresholds, commonly the EUR 850 million consolidated revenue threshold, but the RMI BC itself has no local transfer pricing return or documentation file to submit. The BC should nonetheless maintain intercompany agreements, pricing analyses, and board approvals to support the arm's-length nature of any related-party transactions in the group's home jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations apply to RMI tax-resident entities carrying on specified geographically mobile activities such as banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters services, holding company activities, intellectual property holding, and distribution and service centers. The manufacture and online sale of resin statue garage kits is not a specified relevant activity, and a non-resident BC without RMI local trade is outside the ESR substance filing framework. Nevertheless, bank AML due diligence, UBO disclosure, and foreign tax enforcement require the BC to maintain genuine commercial substance: documented board minutes, separate bank accounts, fulfillment and subcontracting contracts, IP ownership records, records of decision-making, and a clear independence from the shareholder's home-country operations. A pure mailbox company will be reclassified by foreign tax authorities and will invite civil and criminal exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Offshore Nominee Structure

Statutory sanction: The RMI itself will not tax the BC, but the operating jurisdiction may assess unpaid income tax, interest, civil fraud penalties of up to 75% of the understatement, offshore disclosure penalties, and criminal fines. In the RMI, the Registrar may strike off the BC for filing a false annual non-resident declaration. In major OECD jurisdictions, willful offshore non-disclosure is a criminal tax offense carrying substantial fines and terms of imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where the BC is the alter ego of the UBO. Directors and officers who sign false declarations, cause nominee filings, or control undisclosed payment accounts are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, unpaid taxes, penalties, and costs. Travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition proceedings are commonly imposed in cross-border tax evasion investigations.
Shell Company and False Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Economic Substance Regulations, if the BC is reclassified as an in-scope relevant activity, the RMI competent authority may impose financial penalties, require remedial substance, and ultimately strike off the entity. Foreign tax agencies may treat the BC as tax-resident under their own management and control rules, subjecting all worldwide profits to full corporate income tax plus penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the BC to operate as a shell may be held personally liable for statutory fines, creditors' losses, and fraudulent trading. The UBO can be subjected to travel bans while tax authorities investigate, and foreign criminal courts may imprison the controlling person for tax evasion or fraudulent insolvency.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration - for direct taxes (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration, collection and audit; Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), issues the statutory matricule number via the single digital portal MyGuichet.lu, which serves as the national TIN and VAT identifier.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Conclude the SCSp limited partnership agreement; notarise before a Luxembourg notary where the general partner is not a commercial company or where real-property contributions are made. Step 2 - File the incorporation dossier through MyGuichet.lu with the RCS and pay the registration fee; the RCS issues the 16-digit matricule number, which simultaneously constitutes the commercial register identifier, the direct-tax TIN and the basis for VAT identification (no separate national TIN is necessary). Step 3 - Complete the RCS 'single formalities' link, which automatically opens the direct-tax file with the ACD and transmits the data to the AEDT; the AEDT allocates the Luxembourg TVA number (format LU 12345678) normally within 2-4 weeks, without an economic-activity threshold for non-established suppliers. Step 4 - Where the SCSp has no EU establishment, appoint an approved VAT tax representative in Luxembourg who is jointly and severally liable for VAT before making any taxable supply. Step 5 - Open the operating/escrow bank account in Luxembourg and file the authorised signatory form (power of attorney) with the tax office. Step 6 - Register the employer file with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) only when a first Luxembourg employee is engaged. The SCSp files VAT, withholding and annual information returns in its own name; partners declare their allocable shares under their respective domestic tax regimes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The SCSp is a contractual partnership without legal personality and is fiscally transparent by default under Luxembourg domestic tax law: it is outside the scope of corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax and net wealth tax at fund level; items of income, gains and expenses flow through to each partner in proportion to their partnership interests. For indirect tax purposes the SCSp constitutes an independent taxable person (assujetti) and must charge Luxembourg VAT in its own name notwithstanding CIT transparency. A protective ATAD rule may reclassify an SCSp as a reverse hybrid taxable on its Luxembourg-source income where one or more investors are resident in a jurisdiction treating the entity as opaque.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a unitary check-the-box regime. The following elections and mechanisms are relevant: (i) optional CIT treatment for a commercial partnership that qualifies as a 'société de participations financières' (Article 175 LIR), practically inapplicable to a services business; (ii) ATAD reverse-hybrid deeming (Law of 21 December 2018 and subsequent reforms): the SCSp becomes taxable in Luxembourg on its own income, to the extent of the opaque treatment by an investor's jurisdiction; (iii) for foreign investors, the US federal check-the-box election may independently classify the SCSp (partnership or disregarded entity) on IRS Form 8832-LLC; Luxembourg will respect that result for treaty purposes; (iv) VAT elections: Article 60 VAT Law 'assujetti unique' VAT grouping with affiliated Luxembourg entities and Article 57 small-business exemption where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000, although the exemption is not advisable for an advisory model claiming input VAT recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) for the domestic small-business exemption of Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; EUR 0 (no threshold) for any foreign-established or non-established taxable person supplying B2C services whose effective place of use or enjoyment is in Luxembourg; EU B2B services are generally reverse-charged to the Luxembourg business customer, relieving the foreign SCSp from local VAT registration, whereas exports and non-EU cross-border B2B advisory services are outside Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with credit of input VAT) is available where: (i) goods are exported by the supplier or for the supplier's account to a non-EU destination (Article 41 Luxembourg VAT Law), provided the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days and the supplier holds the export-proof bundle; (ii) goods are supplied to a customer established in another EU Member State holding a valid VAT ID, with the goods transported out of Luxembourg within three months and recorded in an EC Sales List; and (iii) certain international transport and ancillary services. For the ISIC 7810 study-abroad and golden-visa placement services, a true 0% outcome for non-EU clients is achieved through the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 44 Directive 2006/112/EC): advisory, placement and immigration-visa consulting services supplied to a foreign university, an overseas agency or a non-EU corporate customer are taxable where that customer is established, hence outside Luxembourg VAT. B2C supplies to non-EU private clients may likewise be excluded under the effective-use-and-enjoyment override (Article 59a of the Directive) where genuine economic use takes place entirely outside the EU; this requires contemporaneous evidence of the client's non-EU residence, the foreign visa/placement filing and the absence of Luxembourg benefit. No domestic zero-rating covers commissions on EU-bound educational placements.

  • EU/Luxembourg Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS electronic declaration) - mandatory for any tangible promotional or study kit exported to a non-EU student; not applicable to pure consulting services but retained in the VAT audit file where export shipments occur.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU customs territory to the specified non-EU destination, dated within 90 days of the supply.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank account ledger evidence showing collection of the full invoice/contract amount from the non-EU counterparty in the SCSp's Luxembourg escrow or operating bank account.
  • Signed service agreement or placement fee contract, the foreign educational institution's acceptance or visa-support documentation, and a client declaration confirming the non-EU situs and effective use of the immigration-visa advisory service.

Since 1 January 2022 Luxembourg mandates electronic invoicing for B2G public procurement transactions through the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0, UBL or CII formats). There is no national real-time fiscal clearance platform, no tax-authority pre-validation API and no digital stamping/duty mark requirement analogous to Italy's Sistema di Interscambio; the AEDT relies on periodic VAT returns and audit evidence. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and must satisfy the authenticity-of-origin, integrity-of-content and legibility conditions of Article 90 of the VAT Law (EDI, advanced electronic signature or a reliable internal audit trail). Under the VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) EU directive, e-invoicing and near-real-time digital transaction reporting for intra-Community supplies will become mandatory by 1 January 2030, with Luxembourg transposition to be enacted; an SCSp should therefore adopt structured electronic invoicing (XML/UBL) now to future-proof compliance.

The AEDT carries out documentary desk checks and on-site VAT audits of export evidence; where zero-rating or non-EU outside-scope treatment is unsupported, the VAT is retrospectively re-assessed at the 17% standard rate, with monthly late-payment interest of 0.6% on arrears. Negligent misstatements attract a fine of 50% of the tax at stake; intentional fraudulent arrangements (fictitious exports, circular escrow payments, sham tuition contracts or false declarations of non-EU use) are criminal tax fraud under Articles 89 et seq. of the VAT Law and the criminal provisions of the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), punishable by fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years, together with joint conviction and liability of the managing general partner, publication of the administrative enforcement act, and suspension or revocation of the statutory licence governing the immigration-paid consultancy activity. For service claims, the AEDT can demand proof of the customer's non-EU establishment and actual repatriation of the fee within the 180-day benchmark; a failure to repatriate leads to a deemed local B2C supply and penalty surcharge. In fraud cases the reassessment limitation period is extended to ten years, and clawbacks may descend on the entire chain of partners under Luxembourg VAT joint-liability principles.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State. The euro is fully convertible, and the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) apply. There are no exchange controls, no capital-account approval requirements, and no statutory obstacles to profit remittance by an SCSp. Cross-border payments require normal AML/KYC compliance and, where applicable, balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL, but do not require exchange-control authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender rules, no statutory timelines for repatriation, and no mandatory conversion ratios for export or service receipts. Foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the commercial discretion of the entity. Only statistical reporting obligations under BCL balance-of-payments regulations may apply to cross-border flows above applicable thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the integrated banking, AML/CFT, and financial-integrity supervisor. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for collection of client fees and limited liquidity clearing, provided the EMI holds a valid Luxembourg e-money institution or payment-institution licence issued by the CSSF, or an EU/EEA passport into Luxembourg. Unlicensed offshore digital EMIs are not viable and will not be accepted by Luxembourg banks or notaries for an SCSp treasury structure. Legal and AML constraints under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing require full customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence for immigration/visa services, sanctions screening, and transaction monitoring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for non-EU inward remittances and SEPA Credit Transfer for EU inbound euro transfers. The remitter must be the named investor or a disclosed beneficial owner and funds must originate from the investor’s own bank account. The receiving Luxembourg bank requires an investment agreement, capital-contribution schedule, and UBO/LEI information before crediting the SCSp's capital or operational account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the SCSp level — the Société en Commandite Spéciale is a fully fiscally transparent Luxembourg partnership: profits are not taxed at the entity level and flow through to the partners. For comparison, a taxable Luxembourg resident company is subject to a combined 24.94% headline effective rate in Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — distributions from the SCSp to any partner (resident or non-resident) are not dividends and are exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax because the SCSp is a pure pass-through vehicle. Comparator: dividends from a standard Luxembourg S.a r.l. carry a 15% statutory WHT, fully relieved at 0% for qualifying EU parent-company shareholders under Directive 2011/96/EU and zero/5%/15% under most tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 — group-level annual turnover/balance-sheet threshold above which a Luxembourg taxpayer (including a transparent SCSp) must prepare and retain a full OECD Master File and Local File (ACD Circular LIR n 56/1 of 28 June 2016); MNE groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000,000 must file a Country-by-Country Report with the ACD.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone ESR code (unlike the UAE or Cayman) but applies an overriding anti-abuse economic-substance doctrine: the SCSp must show real premises, personnel, and risk management in Luxembourg; the general partner must document commercial rationale and decision minutes. A visa/immigration advisory business will be scrutinized for artificial profit shifting to zero-tax jurisdictions. Non-compliance triggers GAAR re-attribution (LIR Art. 6(1)), denial of treaty benefits under the MLI/PPT standard, the ATAD 30% EBITDA interest limitation, and CRS/FATCA integrity reviews.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000 or double the tax evaded, and imprisonment of up to 5 years (Luxembourg Law of 25 March 2016); administrative re-assessment plus 10% surcharge and 0.7% monthly default interest; publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any managing director are personally liable for the criminal fine and civil restitution; criminal conviction triggers cross-border travel bans, potential extradition, disqualification from holding office, and civil exposure to co-partners for the full tax shortfall.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Majuro, in coordination with the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce and Investment; customs administered by the Marshall Islands Customs Service via the ASYCUDA World electronic clearance platform; no dedicated national e-filing tax portal exists for non-resident entities.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent forms a Marshall Islands Business Corporation (MH_BC) by filing Articles of Incorporation through a licensed Registered Agent and obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation with a registered file number. Because the entity is classified as a Non-Resident Tax-Exempt corporation deriving no Marshall Islands-source revenue, no TIN, VAT ID, or GRT registration is required. Only if the entity commences carrying on business within the Marshall Islands and earns RMI-source gross revenue above the de minimis threshold must it register with the RTO for Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) and file quarterly returns; if it moves physical goods, an Importer/Exporter Number is separately obtained from the Marshall Islands Customs Service.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-Resident Business Corporation exempt from RMI income tax; the Marshall Islands imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax. The only in-rem tax exposure is the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) at 3% on gross revenue derived from RMI sources only; offshore SaaS subscription revenue is outside the tax base entirely. — A foreign parent forms a Marshall Islands Business Corporation (MH_BC) by filing Articles of Incorporation through a licensed Registered Agent and obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation with a registered file number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-Resident Business Corporation exempt from RMI income tax; the Marshall Islands imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no withholding tax. The only in-rem tax exposure is the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) at 3% on gross revenue derived from RMI sources only; offshore SaaS subscription revenue is outside the tax base entirely.

Available election forms

None. The Marshall Islands has no check-the-box, entity-classification election, or hybrid-mismatch election. Tax classification is fixed by statute: a Non-Resident Business Corporation with no RMI-source gross revenue has no registration or filing obligation; RMI-source gross revenue is taxed at entity level under the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1986 at 3%.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales-and-Use tax exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The direct analog is the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) at 3% headline on RMI-source gross revenue, administered alongside customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Ordinance; exports and offshore service revenue are excluded from the GRT base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 10,000 of annual gross revenue — de minimis threshold above which a business carrying on business in the Marshall Islands must register for GRT and file quarterly returns. A Non-Resident PropTech SaaS entity with no physical RMI nexus and no RMI-source subscription revenue falls below the registration nexus and is not registrable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT zero-rating concept because no VAT exists. Under the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1986, 'gross revenue' is statutorily limited to revenue from business transacted within the Marshall Islands; revenue from bona fide export sales and from services supplied to and received by non-residents outside the RMI (including offshore PropTech cloud subscriptions and rental-screening SaaS fees) is excluded from the GRT base and is thus economically zero-rated. Eligibility is established by showing a non-resident customer, foreign payment rails (e.g., Stripe Billing, ACH Direct), no RMI physical performance, no RMI permanent establishment, and no RMI-source counterparty.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic declaration / Form Cus-Exp)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from an RMI port
  • Commercial Invoice stating true export value and non-resident buyer
  • Packing List
  • Marshall Islands Customs Exporter License or Certificate of Incorporation of the Business Corporation
  • Offshore SaaS subscription contract or platform payment statement evidencing the non-resident service recipient

None. The RMI imposes no mandatory e-invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital fiscal stamping, and no tax-API integration. The only electronic clearance mandate is customs-related: import/export declarations must be processed through the ASYCUDA World UNCTAD customs platform, accompanied by standard commercial invoices and packing lists.

The RTO may audit taxpayers and reassess within a six-year retrospective window. Any claim of offshore/export exclusion later found to cover RMI-source revenue triggers reassessment at the 3% GRT rate, an automatic late-payment penalty of 10% of the tax due, and statutory interest. Fraudulent mischaracterization, sham offshore documentation, or falsified export declarations constitute criminal tax evasion under the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1986 and the RMI Penal Code, carrying fines up to USD 5,000, imprisonment up to three years, and revocation of the Certificate of Good Standing. The US-dollar legal-tender regime imposes no foreign-exchange controls; therefore the 180-day benchmark is a substantiation best practice rather than a statutory repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands adopts full dollarization and applies no exchange-control restrictions. Both current-account transactions (subscription fees, license revenue, SaaS cloud rentals, development fees, vendor payments, and royalties) and capital-account flows (share capital injections, intercompany loans, loan redemptions, dividends, and ownership transfers) are freely convertible and remittable in US dollars. There is no separate domestic currency, no central bank approval, no repatriation permit, and no surrender requirement. Practical hurdles are limited to correspondent-bank due-diligence requests, OFAC screening, and the need to substantiate beneficial ownership and source of funds for ACH/SWIFT transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline exists for any export receipts, including SaaS subscription proceeds, property-management cloud fees, rental-screening data licensing revenue, or intercompany service fees. Export proceeds may remain in foreign bank accounts indefinitely, may be designated solely in USD, and are not subject to mandatory conversion, holding-period minimums, or central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands does not maintain a central bank. Monetary authority functions are effectively delegated to the United States Federal Reserve System because the US dollar is the sole legal tender. Banking and corporate-house AML/CFT supervision is performed by the Marshall Islands Banking Commissioner and the Financial Intelligence and Investigation Unit (FIIU) within the Ministry of Finance, while currency issuance is not an RMI function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with substantial US-bank dependence. RMI does not license offshore electronic-money institutions, and no standalone EMI/PSP charter exists. A PropTech SaaS treasury can validly use a foreign fintech EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorized EMI, EU/EEA EMI, or US money-transmitter-backed BaaS platform) only where the EMI opens a segregated USD omnibus account at a US or correspondent bank and the RMI BC maintains a sub-account or virtual IBAN. The legal constraint is that an EMI is not a licensed Marshall Islands bank and cannot hold RMI client deposits, nor can it provide local settlement services that would otherwise trigger the Banking Act. To remain compliant, the client-facing payment rails must be structured as agent/merchant-of-record arrangements, and all customer funds must be treated as corporate operating cash not as deposit-taking. The EMI must be integrated with Stripe Billing and ACH Direct through a US partner bank; otherwise the RMI entity will face account restriction due to insufficient US nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as plain USD wire transfers through a US correspondent bank or via Fedwire/SWIFT MT103, with purpose-code conventions appropriate to a foreign direct investment in a non-resident business corporation. Recommended wire descriptor: 'Capital subscription and paid-in surplus for issuance of common shares – Marshall Islands BC, invoice/reference [BC registration no.]'. When sending through an intermediary, use the RMI bank's correspondent ABA routing number with the explicit account name of the BC. For ACH Direct in the PropTech context, the same receiving account can also accept SaaS working-capital payments from property managers; but any inbound capital injection greater than $50,000 should be transmitted by wire, not ACH, and must be accompanied by a board resolution, share-issuance ledger, and OFAC-compliant source-of-funds evidence. All funds should be credited as 'operating treasury' unless designated for a statutory escrow, in which case the Capital Escrow Account should be used.Outbound disbursements from the RMI BC will occur via three preferred clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international vendor settlements, subscription-technology licensing, and intercompany treasury sweeps above $50,000; (2) USD correspondent wire through Fedwire for US vendors and strategic service providers; and (3) ACH Direct (ACH debit/credit via a US partner bank or EMI) for high-frequency, low-value customer refunds, rental-screening data access payments, and operational disbursements. Commercial letters of credit (LCs) are not standard for PropTech SaaS operating payments but are permissible if a property-technology enterprise procures heavy hardware or IT equipment from overseas; any such LC must be issued by a US or foreign bank and advised through the RMI commercial bank, with the BC as applicant. Trade settlements must reference the commercial contract and invoice, and banking documentation must always identify the ultimate beneficiary to avoid return-credit suspensions.USD-only operating treasury negates domestic FX hedging; no permitted RMI-market instruments exist because the US dollar is legal tender.Cross-currency forward contracts (vanilla FX forwards) on a deliverable basis between USD and EUR/GBP/AUD/CAD can be executed by the offshore treasury function with an OECD-licensed counterparty, provided the RMI BC has genuine transactional FX exposure from SaaS revenues billed in non-USD currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible for emerging-market currencies (e.g., INR, IDR, PHP, KRW) where property-technology clients pay in local currency and the NDF is cash-settled in USD; such instruments must be booked outside the regulated RMI banking book.Cross-currency swaps (USD vs EUR/GBP) are permissible on an unsecured OTC basis with standard ISDA documentation for long-dated treasury exposures, but only if the swap serves hedge accounting under the financial reporting framework and no speculative leverage is introduced.Plain-vanilla currency options and barrier options are legally permissible for hedging SAP/HCM SaaS multi-currency revenue streams, but must be authorized by the RMI BC's Treasury Policy and entered into with a top-tier US/EU bank by a delegated officer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on non-resident domestic Business Corporations (BCCA Section 154); the Marshall Islands levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no branch-profits tax, and no franchise or net-worth tax on a Non-Resident BC deriving no income from sources within the Republic.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. The Republic does not impose dividend withholding tax or any distribution tax on amounts paid by a Non-Resident domestic Business Corporation to its shareholders; dividends may be repatriated in USD without RMI withholding formalities.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within the calendar month containing the anniversary date of incorporation; the report must be received by the Registrar before the last day of that month. A late fee is assessed for each month or part of a month of delay.

Deadline: · Frequency:

If a filing obligation were triggered, the return is due on the statutory date under the RMI Income Tax Act following the close of the taxable year. For the standard Non-Resident BC profile there is no due date because no taxable income exists within the Republic; a nil return is not required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No RMI statutory TP documentation threshold exists: the RMI Income Tax Act does not contain transfer pricing rules and the Non-Resident BC has no taxable base. For defense against foreign tax authorities (U.S. IRS Section 482/GILTI, OECD BEPS Actions 8-10, and EU ATAD), the group should voluntarily maintain a Master File and Local File when group intercompany revenue exceeds USD 1,000,000 per annum, and a Country-by-Country Report only when consolidated group revenue exceeds USD 850,000,000 (OECD threshold). A de-minimis threshold of USD 500,000 per intercompany transaction counterparty is recommended for documentation triggers.

Economic substance scope: The RMI has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the BVI or Cayman codes, and the jurisdiction is not placed on the current EU blacklist of non-cooperative jurisdictions. Substance obligations nevertheless arise from: (i) the BCCA requirement to maintain a registered office and licensed resident agent in the Republic (BCCA Section 52); (ii) OECD Global Forum exchange-of-information commitments requiring beneficial ownership records and CRS/FATCA cooperation; and (iii) foreign substance-over-form doctrines under which the U.S. check-the-box rules (Treas. Reg. Sections 301.7701-2 and -3) can recharacterize a thinly capitalized RMI BC, and foreign CFC/GILTI rules (IRC Section 951A) may impose current inclusion on U.S. shareholders regardless of RMI exemption. Board minutes should evidence real decision-making on the Stripe Billing pricing schedule, ACH settlement treasury policies, and vendor contracts to sustain commercial substance if challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-Border Tax Evasion / Abuse of the RMI Exemption

Statutory sanction: Under RMI law, willful false reporting triggers Income Tax Act penalties, criminal fines, and forfeiture; under IRC Section 7201, willful tax evasion is a felony punishable by a fine of up to USD 100,000 (individual) or USD 500,000 (corporation) and imprisonment up to five years; the civil fraud penalty under IRC Section 6663 adds 75% of the underpayment; late-payment interest accrues under IRC Section 6621; State nexus assessments may expose the group to corporate franchise and sales tax liabilities in multiple U.S. states.

Director personal liability: Directors with U.S. nexus are exposed under IRC Section 6672 to the 100% trust-fund recovery penalty for withheld taxes; criminal restitution orders and forfeiture of platform assets may be entered; RMI courts will enforce foreign revenue forfeiture claims premised on money laundering predicates; senior officers face travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition requests on tax-fraud warrants.
Shell Company / Abusive Substance-Over-Form and U.S. Check-the-Box Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as a U.S. domestic corporation under IRC Section 7701(a)(3); retroactive U.S. corporate tax at 21% plus branch-profits tax exposure under IRC Section 884 at 30% on deemed distributions; Form 5471 penalties of USD 10,000 per failure, increased to USD 50,000 after 90-day notice; failure to file Form 8858 carries a USD 10,000 penalty and loss of protective check-the-box elections; GILTI inclusion under IRC Section 951A applies at a 10.5% effective minimum rate plus Subpart F inclusions at 21%, all with compounding interest and precision penalties.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil when the RMI entity is proven to be a mere instrumentality, holding shareholders and directors personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; responsible officers face the Section 6672 100% penalty; director-level FBAR penalties of up to 50% of undisclosed accounts apply where RMI bank, payment, or treasury accounts are unreported; tax-engaged professionals face injunction and Circular 230 discipline where they have relied on a sham shell structure.
AML, Sanctions and Beneficial-Ownership Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: The RMI Money Laundering (Prevention and Control) Act 2003 imposes penalties up to USD 1,000,000 per contravention, plus confiscation and criminal forfeiture and a possible 14-year term of imprisonment; U.S. Bank Secrecy Act penalties reach USD 32,907 per non-willful violation and up to USD 164,508 or 50% of the transaction per willful violation; OFAC penalties reach USD 295,141 per sanctions transaction (or USD 1,177,964 for aggravated cases) plus imprisonment up to 30 years; Stripe and ACH processors will terminate merchant accounts upon any BSA/OFAC event, leaving refund liabilities.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal money-laundering prosecution in the RMI (up to 14 years' imprisonment) and extradition risk from allied jurisdictions; OFAC designation leads to asset freezes and travel restrictions; directors who funnel rental-screening fees for sanctioned landlords may be debarred from professional services and from owning or servicing U.S. payment accounts; mandatory disqualification of directors following AML enforcement actions in foreign jurisdictions collaterally invalidates appointments.
Corporate Registry Non-Compliance and Administrative Dissolution

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution under BCCA Section 111 after continued default; late fee applicable monthly under the Registrar's fee schedule; reinstatement only upon payment of all arrears, reinstatement fees, and the issuance of a restoration certificate; obligations entered into after dissolution are voidable; RMI financial institutions freeze a settled account upon registry notice of dissolution; the U.S. IRS may retroactively treat the dissolved entity as a partnership or disregarded entity producing a taxable deemed liquidation event.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for all obligations incurred after the dissolution date under BCCA Section 112; continuing to dividend or clear ACH settlements after dissolution constitutes fraudulent misrepresentation exposing directors to civil restitution; the RMI High Court may issue travel bans in registry-fraud or money-laundering investigations; directors are personally responsible for reinstatement costs and for refunds to customers on voidable platform contracts.

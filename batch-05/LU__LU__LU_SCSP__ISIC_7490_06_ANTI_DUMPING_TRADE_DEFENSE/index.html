Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) is the competent authority for VAT registration and indirect taxes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) is competent for direct income taxes; centralized e-government portal and e-filing platform: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

1. Engage a Luxembourg-resident authorised representative or registered office provider. 2. Execute the SCSp partnership agreement and file the registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via the MyGuichet.lu e-Registration service, obtaining the RCS matricule (national business registration number used as the base tax identification number). 3. Submit the VAT registration application ('Inscription à la TVA', e-form 563) to the AEDT, attaching the RCS extract, partnership agreement, authorization of the legal representative and, for non-EU persons, designation of a Luxembourg VAT representative. 4. The AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) and, where customs activity is contemplated, the EORI number. No minimum capital is required for an SCSp.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Under Article 175 LIR as amended in 2016, an SCSp is fiscally transparent by default, but it is treated as opaque (subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax) where all of its partners are corporate entities subject to Luxembourg CIT or comparable corporate taxes. The tax classification is therefore determined by the statutory partner-composition criteria rather than by a formal filing election; partnership deed drafting and partner identity may be structured accordingly. Cross-border effects are further governed by the hybrid mismatch rules implemented by the Law of 21 December 2020 (ATAD 2), which may recharacterise transparent or opaque treatment in anti-hybrid situations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover threshold for the domestic small-business franchise exemption (Article 57 Loi TVA) applicable only to Luxembourg-established businesses; non-resident suppliers have no registration exemption and must register before making the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. A separate EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic/telecom/broadcasting services under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export supplies to third countries and persons outside the EU qualify for the 0% rate where the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of supply and the exporter retains acceptable proof of export (Article 38 Loi TVA, transposing Article 146 VAT Directive). Services: anti-dumping defense and trade-remedy consulting services supplied to a non-resident business customer are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules (customer's establishment, with reverse charge); services to a non-taxable person established outside the EU are excluded from Luxembourg VAT where effectively used and enjoyed outside the EU. Intra-EU B2B consulting services are reverse-charged to the EU customer and reported in the Luxembourg EC Sales List without Luxembourg VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/electronic declaration) released by Luxembourg Customs (ADER)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or air waybill evidencing physical exit of goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT/bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Luxembourg does not currently operate real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction controls (CTC). B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and is based on Peppol/Factur-X interoperability standards. E-invoicing is mandatory only in the B2G segment for invoices addressed to central public-sector contracting authorities. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, mandatory intra-EU B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting are expected to apply from 1 July 2030, subject to Luxembourg transposition.

AEDT and Luxembourg Customs (ADER) apply targeted substantive audit scrutiny to export zero-rating and refund claims. Failure to substantiate the export within the statutory conditions causes reversal of the 0% rate and assessment of Luxembourg VAT at 17%, together with late-payment interest of 0.6% per month and a 10% payment surcharge. Willful or fraudulent claims additionally expose the taxpayer to criminal tax-fraud sanctions (imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or fines of up to EUR 250,000). The ordinary reassessment limitation period is five years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud, and refunds already paid out are subject to retrospective clawback with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member state with fully liberalised capital and current account transactions under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and the EU Capital Requirements Regulation. No exchange control legislation restricts the conversion of EUR into foreign currency or vice versa. Capital account transactions, including the injection of foreign equity into a Luxembourg SCSp, profit distribution to non-resident limited partners, and repayment of shareholder loans, are unrestricted. Profit remittance is subject only to general anti-money-laundering, tax compliance (including potential withholding formalities under EU Directives or treaty-based reductions), and verification of economic substance where tax treaty benefits are claimed. For cross-border trade in services such as anti-dumping defence legal consulting, current account receivables and payments are freely convertible without prior authorisation or surrender requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory duty to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any governmental authority, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. All proceeds from exports of services, including legal and consultancy fees in anti-dumping trade defence matters, may be retained in EUR or any other freely convertible currency in bank accounts held domestically or abroad, subject to standard reporting obligations under the EU Balance of Payments statistics and the Luxembourg General Tax Code for accounting and tax record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB), with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervising banking and foreign exchange intermediation conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. The viability of offshore digital e-money institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) for the SCSp treasury is constrained by the requirement for EU/EEA authorisation and passporting rights. A third-country EMI, particularly a UK-based entity post-Brexit, generally has no passporting right to provide payment services to a Luxembourg-domiciled client without a Luxembourg licence or equivalent licensing; such offshore providers are therefore not recommended for primary clearing rails. EEA-licensed EMIs may operate under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) but typically impose account limits, restrict high-value trade settlements, and require additional KYC for pass-through vehicles. For a professional anti-dumping defence consultancy, a fully licensed Luxembourg bank remains the preferred legal and treasury counterparty, while a regulated EMI can be used for low-value, subsidiary expense disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident limited partners or foreign investors into the Luxembourg SCSp are executed through standard SWIFT MT103 or equivalent SEPA/SEPA Instant credit transfers. There is no exchange-control approval requirement. The bank transfer should identify the beneficiary as the exact SCSp legal name and account number, with the remittance information line stating 'Capital Contribution – SCSp Subscription Agreement reference [document number]'. Additional documentary verification comprises the executed subscription/capital call agreement, the board resolution of the general partner, a passport copy and proof of address of the contributor, and a source-of-funds statement where the contributed amount exceeds the bank's threshold. For statistical purposes, the receiving bank may request a Luxembourg Balance of Payments (BoP) declaration; the applicable purpose code is generally 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (BoP code E1/C2 or equivalent local coding) for equity injections, or 'Loan to affiliated enterprise' for shareholder loans. Upon clearance, the funds are credited to the operational or dedicated capital escrow ledger from which trade defence expenses and legal fees are subsequently paid.Outbound trade and professional fee disbursements from the SCSp are settled over either SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-EUR transactions or SEPA and TARGET2 for EUR payments within the EEA. Commercial letters of credit (documentary L/C under UCP600) are available for underlying trade flows connected with the anti-dumping investigation, including import-export transactions where the consulting firm supports importer or exporter evidence collection. For direct payments to foreign counsel, expert economists, survey firms, and government filing agencies, the bank requires a written payment instruction from the general partner, proof of invoiced services, and a description referencing the ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'LEGL' (legal services) or 'PRMF' (professional fees). The same clearing rails also support SWIFT MT202 cover for interbank settlement when multiple currency legs are involved.

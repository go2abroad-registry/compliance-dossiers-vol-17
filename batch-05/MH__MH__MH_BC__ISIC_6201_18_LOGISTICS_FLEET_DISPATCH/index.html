Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar (USD) is the exclusive legal tender in RMI, making currency convertibility fully obtainable for both current and capital account transactions. There are no statutory exchange-control restrictions on inbound investments or outbound dividend/licensing/profit remittances. Non-resident business corporation accounts may freely move funds in USD or to other currencies without central bank approval. Practical constraints arise solely from bank-level anti-money-laundering (AML) due diligence, the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Act 2018, and OFAC sanction screening of all wire traffic.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI law imposes no surrender obligation, statutory timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, converted to third currencies via the correspondent banking network, or settled under bilateral contracts. For AML reporting, local banks must record any cash or wire transaction exceeding USD 10,000, but no forex conversion is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance – Banking Commissioner, appointed under the RMI Banking Act 1991 (as amended), is the de facto financial regulator for banking and foreign exchange prudential matters. No statutory central bank exists; the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as the monetary authority through full dollarization, with the U.S. dollar being the sole legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. The RMI, under the Banking Act 1991, does not have a separate licensing class for electronic money institutions (EMIs) or digital banking providers. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Stripe Treasury) cannot act as a direct clearing path into RMI local payment systems and are not treated as banks by the Banking Commissioner. Their permissible use is limited to originating transfers into a licensed BMI or Bank of Guam account, and for settling customer subscription receipts from Stripe Billing/Wire transfers; all final settlement must pass through bank accounts held at a licensed RMI commercial bank or a fully licensed U.S. correspondent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscription funds are injected using the SWIFT MT103 status message from the investor's non-RMI bank to the MH_BC account at a licensed RMI bank. Field 50K contains the full ordering customer name and address; field 56A must be completed with the correspondent BIC when the direct RMI routing path is unavailable. Field 72 must include the secured purpose code 'C' (Capital Contribution) and the unique corporate registration number (e.g., MH_BC registration number). The destination bank will verify the declared purpose against the filed Articles of Association, the RMI Business Corporations Act registration certificate, and a corporate-secretary confirmation letter. If the account is designated as a Capital Escrow Account, disbursement is permitted only after the corporate registry certificate is issued and a director's resolution authorizing the operating account is submitted. No RMI government approval or exchange-control approval is required.Trade settlement for software licensing royalties, fleet telematics subscriptions, and vendor payments is executed through two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct and recurring disbursements, usually in USD, with a value date at t+0/t+1 and field 70 containing the contract/reference number; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 issued through Bank of Guam's trade desk for high-value or documentary settlements, particularly when logistics-fleet equipment or software sublicenses require presentation of invoices, insurance certificates, and proof of delivery (POD). For non-USD counterparts (EUR, JPY, AUD), the treasury manual mandates USD as the functional currency and requires a firm-priced currency conversion quote from the RMI bank before booking; all outbound wires must be OFAC-screened and flagged for the MLCO when the cumulative transaction amount exceeds USD 100,000 in a single business day.Deliverable FX forward contracts (FX swaps) on USD/EUR, USD/JPY, and USD/AUD, with an underlying commercial invoice or software-lice agreement, subject to standard bank credit documentation and maturity up to 12 months.Non-deliverable forward contracts (NDFs) for restricted or non-deliverable balance-of-payments currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) used to hedge projected royalty and logistics-tariff exposure when counterparty settlement restrictions exist.Currency swaps (USD vs foreign currency principal exchange) for multi-year fleet-route licensing contracts, with negative consent from the Banking Commissioner when the notional stranded exposure exceeds USD 15 million.FX options (purchased, not written) to protect against adverse moves on high-value inbound capital or imminent outbound trade disbursements, with premium paid from the operating account and no speculative resale permitted.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident domestic exempt corporation licensed under Part II of the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act; 3% ordinary corporate rate applies only to a resident corporation that carries on a trade or business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends declared and paid by a non-resident domestic corporation out of non-RMI-source earnings. Dividends paid by a resident RMI corporation to a non-resident shareholder are subject to a 10% gross-basis withholding tax; however, an exempt non-resident domestic corporation is wholly outside the charge to distributed profit tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Annual Renewal Statement and license fee are due on or before the anniversary date of incorporation, no later than the last day of the anniversary month. Each year the filing window opens 60 days before such anniversary and closes on the anniversary; a grace period of 120 days applies before late-surcharge assessment commences.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 - the Marshall Islands has no statutory transfer-pricing legislation and imposes no Local File or Master File filing threshold on non-resident domestic corporations. As a defensive practice for an ISIC 6201 software entity, preparation of OECD-aligned benchmarking documentation is recommended where cross-border controlled transactions in the aggregate exceed USD 500,000 annually.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations apply effective January 1, 2022. A non-resident domestic corporation that does not claim tax residency in a foreign jurisdiction must demonstrate: (i) registered office and core management physically present in the Marshall Islands, (ii) adequate qualified employees proportionate to its fleet-dispatch telematics business, (iii) sufficient non-resident operating expenditure in RMI, and (iv) no outsourcing of core income-generating activities. A company that is subject to corporate income tax in the United States, Hong Kong SAR, or another approved jurisdiction and files an annual foreign-residency declaration is exempted from the RMI substantive test. Tax Evasion and False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Under Title 19 of the RMI Income Tax Act, conviction carries a fine of up to US$25,000 for the corporation, treble the amount of the tax underpaid, and a 50% accuracy-related penalty upon the underpayment; the registrar may also impose a 10% surcharge on assessed deficiencies for false statements.

Shell Company / Substance Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: The RMI Government, through the Registrar of Corporations, may administratively dissolve the entity for lack of economic substance, strike its name from the Corporate Registry, and deny the Certificate of Good Standing used in banking and government procurement; foreign tax authorities may disregard the RMI entity and impose full CFC or controlled-foreign-corporation inclusion of profits with a 20% negligence penalty each year of default.

Director personal liability: Courts in the Marshall Islands and foreign fora may pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a facade: directors become personally liable for the entire stream of foreign taxes otherwise due from the company; travel restrictions may be issued to preserve jurisdiction; directors are exposed to de facto joint debt liability for all SaaS subscription contracts entered in the entity's name.

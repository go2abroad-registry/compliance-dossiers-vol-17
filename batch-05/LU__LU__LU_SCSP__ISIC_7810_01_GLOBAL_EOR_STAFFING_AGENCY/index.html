Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Grand-Duchy of Luxembourg – Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT, registration duties, and stamp duties; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) as the central business registry; official digital portals: guichet.lu and myguichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

The SCSp, as a partnership without legal personality, must first be registered with the RCS through the LBR, obtaining its statutory business registration number (numéro d'immatriculation) and publication in the RESA electronic corporate gazette. For VAT purposes, the SCSp (or its appointed Luxembourg fiscal representative if the partnership is considered non-established) must submit the VAT registration application (form no. 7 – demande de numéro de TVA) to the AED before carrying out taxable transactions; the AED then issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number bearing the 'LU' prefix. Direct tax TINs are assigned at the partner level due to the default fiscal transparency of the SCSp, while the SCSp as an EOR employer must register separately with the ACD for Luxembourg payroll withholding (retenue à la source sur salaires) once it engages Luxembourg employees or pays Luxembourg-sourced remuneration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the SCSp is fiscally transparent by default: income, gains, and losses flow through to its partners and are taxed in their own hands; the SCSp itself is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), or net wealth tax. The partnership may nonetheless validly elect entity-level (opaque) taxation as a Luxembourg capital company, thereby converting its classification to Entity_Level_Taxation for the elected period.

Available election forms

The Luxembourg income tax law recognises a statutory opacity election (check-the-box-type election) under which an SCSp may opt in writing and irrevocably, with the consent of all partners, to be taxed as a fully taxable Luxembourg corporation (subject to CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax). Conversely, the default pass-through regime is preserved when no election is filed. Purely hybrid entity mismatches must be neutralised under ATAD/DAC6 and are disclosed through Luxembourg's mandatory disclosable arrangements regime. In the indirect tax sphere, the SCSp may elect (i) the Luxembourg 'assujetti unique' VAT grouping regime, (ii) admission to the AED small-enterprise VAT exemption where annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000, and (iii) the general cross-border B2B reverse-charge mechanism, all of which must be aligned with the AED circular practice and EU VAT Directives.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the Luxembourg small-enterprise exemption applicable to established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers are required to register for Luxembourg VAT with effect from the first euro of taxable supplies made in Luxembourg (no threshold) unless the OSS/IOSS or reverse-charge mechanism applies. For global EOR staffing services, Luxembourg VAT at 17% is chargeable on staff-supply services rendered to Luxembourg B2C customers and to Luxembourg-established B2B customers not covered by a domestic reverse-charge rule; cross-border B2B supplies to EU/non-EU customers generally fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the reverse-charge or place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% export VAT rate is available only for goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, provided the exporter holds and preserves conclusive proof of export (customs-validated export declaration, transport documents evidencing exit from EU customs territory, invoice, and contract). Staffing/EOR services are, in principle, neither taxed nor zero-rated in Luxembourg when supplied B2B to customers established outside Luxembourg (reverse charge at the customer's location) and are outside Luxembourg VAT scope for B2C services performed for non-EU customers under Articles 58-59 of the Luxembourg VAT law; eligibility for no-charge treatment must be substantiated with evidence of the customer's business status, proof of establishment abroad, the service contract and invoices, and inbound remittance of the service consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) lodged through the Luxembourg Export Control System (ECS)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit transfer evidence proving receipt of export proceeds within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Customer status evidence (VAT number or local tax registration certificate) supporting the off-EU/offshore place of supply of EOR services

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping mandate is currently imposed in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for B2G (business-to-government) transactions through the Peppol network (CIUS-PEPPOL-LU, EN 16931) under the law of 19 May 2020 and the implementing grand-ducal regulation; B2B e-invoicing is permitted subject to customer consent and to guarantee of authenticity of origin and integrity of content (standard audit trail or EDI). All invoices must satisfy the content and archiving requirements of Article 47 of the Luxembourg VAT law and be preserved for 6 years (10 years for commercial records). VAT returns, recapitulative EU sales lists, and VAT registrations are filed electronically via the myguichet.lu / AED e-portal; a Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) framework is maintained for electronic query-based audits of large taxpayers.

The AED may audit export zero-rating and offshore-supply claims within the general 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or culpable misdeclaration. Any zero-rating or no-charge treatment found to lack proper substantiation is retrospectively clawed back by reassessment of the full VAT amount plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.8% (9.6% per annum). Fraudulent export claims expose the taxpayer to civil penalties up to 100% of the evaded VAT and, under Article 396 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law, to criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline leads to treatment of the claim as fictitious, forfeiture of the export exemption, reverse-charge VAT reassessment, and enhanced anti-money-laundering/CSSF oversight; the SCSp's managers and its partners are jointly and severally liable for the assessed VAT, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control licensing, no quantitative limits on current or capital account flows, and no restriction on profit, distribution, or capital repatriation for a resident SCSp. Free movement of capital is guaranteed by Article 63 TFEU, covering both EU-internal and third-country flows. Residual legal friction is limited to: (i) EU/UN financial sanctions (Regulations 269/2014 and 833/2014 as amended) blocking listed jurisdictions/counterparties; (ii) BCL monthly BOP reporting for transactions above EUR 12,500; and (iii) CSSF-driven AML/KYC documentation for inbound equity or loans. Capital contributions to the SCSp and subsequent withdrawals or in-kind distributions to partners are freely executable, subject only to the partnership agreement and mandatory partner verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg has no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for receipts derived from services (including EOR payroll fees) or any other foreign-currency earnings. The SCSp may freely retain EUR or foreign-currency balances, convert at the institution of its choice, or hold offshore accounts. The sole mandatory obligation is the BCL BOP statistical declaration (monthly, above EUR 12,500 in aggregate), filed by the servicing bank on instruction of the company.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem/European Central Bank (ECB); Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions, EMIs and payment institutions for AML/CFT and prudential conduct. The BCL operates T2/TARGET-LB settlement and collects monthly balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Feasibility: HIGH as a complementary contractor-payout rail (e.g., Wise Europe SA, Airwallex, Payoneer, each EEA-licensed or passported to Luxembourg under PSD2/EMD2); MEDIUM as the sole primary treasury account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions to the SCSp must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) to the dedicated 'capital escrow' account, including full remitter/beneficiary legal names, addresses and account numbers under EU Regulation 2015/847. Use the ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (capital payments) or 'INTC' (intra-company payments) as mapped by the receiving bank, and instruct the receiving bank to file the BCL BOP declaration under the direct inward investment equity classification. Prior notification to the relationship manager, retention of the MT103, reconciliation of the credit advice to the partnership register, and source-of-funds evidence within 15 business days complete the compliance cycle. Capital and operating flows must never be commingled; separate accounts for escrow and operations are mandatory.EUR payroll in the SEPA zone settles via mass SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) through the host bank's corporate file gateway; non-EUR payroll settles via SWIFT MT103 (single) or MT101 (multi-payment) using ISO IBAN/BIC and full-name-matching controls. ISO 20022 purpose codes such as 'SALA' for salaries and 'PAYR' or 'OTHR' for general contractor disbursements are recommended. Wise Business batch API may supplement as a compliant local-rail aggregator (RTP in India, CNAPS in China, PIX in Brazil, ACH in the US), with all payout files mirrored in the SCSp treasury ledger. Commercial Letters of Credit are structurally unsuitable for payroll; use bank guarantees or standby LCs only where a client requires performance protection. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: HIGH as a complementary contractor-payout rail (e.g., Wise Europe SA, Airwallex, Payoneer, each EEA-licensed or passported to Luxembourg under PSD2/EMD2); MEDIUM as the sole primary treasury account. Lawful without a bank-account KYC interview, but subject to five constraints: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit or overdraft; (ii) client funds must be safeguarded in segregated pools, creating counterparty and insolvency-remote risk; (iii) partner banks and investors may refuse to recognize an EMI-only structure for capital escrow; (iv) high-velocity payroll flows attract enhanced EMI-side transaction monitoring and periodic freezes; and (v) the SCSp should retain a CSSF-licensed credit institution for any payroll settlement above the EMI safeguarded-fund cap. Recommended topology: EMI for high-volume last-mile payouts; tier-1 Luxembourg bank for capital escrow, BCL BOP reporting, and strategic FX.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Division of Revenue and Taxation, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI); corporate registration and annual-report administration is performed by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce, Investment and Tourism (registry portal: https://www.registrar.gov.mh). The RMI operates no online tax e-filing portal for non-resident Business Corporations.

E-government filing portal

The RMI issues no TIN or VAT ID to non-resident Business Corporations. A foreign parent establishes an RMI entity by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Office of the Registrar of Corporations and receives a Certificate of Incorporation bearing the corporate reference number; that certificate is the definitive tax-identity instrument. No RMI tax registration is required or available for a non-resident B.C. that refrains from conducting business in the RMI. For banking and US FATCA/CRS purposes, the entity signs IRS Form W-8BEN-E and presents the Certificate of Incorporation; if the entity ever conducts commercial activity within the RMI, it must instead register under the Revenue and Taxation Act 1989 for gross revenue tax and obtain a Foreign Investment Business Licence under the Foreign Investment Business Licence Act 2002.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. Exemption from all RMI taxes is mandatory and automatic for non-resident domestic corporations under Section 7 of the RMI Business Corporations Act; there is no RMI check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The only elective steps are administrative: maintaining non-resident status (no RMI sourcing of business activity) and, for foreign-reporting purposes, filing IRS Form 8832 (US check-the-box) or Form W-8BEN-E (FATCA) as applicable. RMI has no tax treaty network, so treaty elections are unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales-and-Use Tax. The RMI indirect-type levies are customs import duties (Customs Act 1990) and the gross revenue tax (Revenue and Taxation Act 1989), the latter applying only to entities conducting business within the RMI.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — the RMI has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Under the Revenue and Taxation Act, any person commencing business activity in the RMI must register for gross revenue tax; under the Foreign Investment Business Licence Act 2002, foreign-owned entities must obtain a Foreign Investment Business Licence (no de minimis monetary threshold). A non-resident B.C. that undertakes no RMI-sourced activity is outside these regimes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the RMI has no VAT system, so export zero-rating, export VAT refunds, and input-credit mechanisms do not exist. Advisory, portfolio-management, custody-wire and SWIFT-interbank services rendered by the non-resident Multi-Family Office B.C. are sourced outside the RMI and are exempt from all RMI taxation under Section 7 of the Business Corporations Act; the exemption is automatic and requires no prior approval or refund filing.

  • No statutory customs bundle applies to offshore services; retained placeholder: Customs Export Declaration (required only if physical goods were ever exported from the RMI)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (required only for goods movements; not applicable to a Multi-Family Office's custody-wire / SWIFT-interbank service line)
  • Inward remittance/payment evidence (SWIFT MT103, custodian credit advices) demonstrating receipt of offshore service fees — recommended for the B.C.'s records to substantiate non-resident status, though not statutorily mandated

None. The RMI does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping for any corporate or exempt entity. Non-resident B.C.s must keep books and records sufficient to evidence non-resident status (including SWIFT MT103, custody statements, and board minutes), but no automated clearance interface exists.

There is no VAT zero-rating fraud or VAT clawback regime because no VAT is imposed. The principal audit risk is loss of the Section 7 exemption: if the Division of Revenue and Taxation establishes that the B.C. conducted business inside the RMI (e.g., soliciting RMI-resident clients, maintaining a fixed place of business, or rendering onshore services), the exemption is retroactively lost, and the entity becomes liable for gross revenue tax on RMI-source revenue (headline rate of 3% of gross revenue under the RTA), together with interest and penalties for failure to register, file, and pay. Knowingly false annual-report declarations to the Registrar carry criminal fines and potential officer liability under the BCA, and registration fees remaining unpaid can lead to administrative dissolution. No foreign-exchange controls are imposed by the RMI; hence no repatriation deadline or exchange-control clawback exists.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI operates a fully dollarized economy with complete capital- and current-account convertibility for USD. No RMI exchange-control statute, capital permission regime, or profit remittance approval exists. Dividend distributions, management fees, intra-group loans, and repatriation of principal are generally unrestricted; however, banks perform OFAC, UN sanctions, and enhanced FATF-style KYC screening, and may delay or decline transfers where beneficial ownership, source-of-funds documentation, or underlying trade purpose fails to satisfy compliance standards. Non-USD receipts require conversion in offshore FX markets because no local interbank foreign-exchange market exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no RMI statutory export proceeds surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios. Export earnings in USD, including the inventory, custody, advisory, and distribution proceeds of a Multi-Family Office operation, may be retained abroad or held onshore or offshore in any currency at the entity's commercial discretion. For AML/CFT file integrity, the MFO must retain commercial invoices, reserve statements, board-approved income schedules, and underlying contract documentation to evidence the legitimate trade or service origin of each receipt.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no central bank and maintains no statutory exchange-control authority. Banking and financial institution supervision is vested in the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance, subject to the Financial Institutions Act 1987, the Foreign Investment Business License Act, and the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counterterrorist Financing Act 2018. The United States dollar is the official legal tender under Article XIII of the RMI Constitution, and all domestic interbank settlement ultimately occurs through U.S. Federal Reserve correspondent accounts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Offshore digital EMI organizations are not automatically recognized as banks under the RMI Financial Institutions Act 1987 and are not permitted to hold client escrow or capital accounts in the RMI unless separately licensed as an EMI or partnered with a licensed RMI bank. A non-resident BC Multi-Family Office may use a U.S.-regulated money transmitter or digital EMI for low-value disbursements, but the statutory Capital Escrow Account and primary treasury account must remain at a U.S.- or RMI-licensed commercial bank. Legal constraints include absence of RMI passporting rights for EEA/FCA EMIs, obligation to maintain local AML registration, and inability to use EMIs as LC issuing banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold in Marshall Islands law; no local TP documentation rules, no Country-by-Country Reporting, and no advance pricing agreement regime exist for non-resident Business Corporations. Commercial reasonableness is nonetheless required to substantiate arm's-length dealings with related parties in any foreign filing jurisdiction.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations comparable to BVI or Cayman ESR. Non-resident Business Corporations engaged exclusively in offshore advisory and portfolio management are not subject to substantive local presence, local management, or local expenditure requirements. A Multi-Family Office will, however, be evaluated by foreign bank counterparties and foreign regulators under the economic substance rules of the onshore jurisdictions where it maintains substance, manages assets, or books income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code and the Income Tax arrangements administered by the RMI, willful evasion and the making of false statements carry severe monetary fines and imprisonment for up to 10 years; unpaid foreign tax claims present as contingent liabilities against the entity under the doctrine of substance-over-form applied by foreign courts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute false declarations are personally liable to the foreign revenue authority for penalties and restitution; criminal prosecution may result in travel bans, extradition procedures, and imprisonment in the requesting jurisdiction or the RMI.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Foreign courts may disregard the RMI legal personality and reallocate income to the actual management jurisdiction; the RMI Registrar may dissolve the corporation for failure to maintain a registered agent or for filing materially false annual statements; fines and repayment obligations are imposed under foreign anti-avoidance legislation.

Director personal liability: RMI courts permit veil piercing where the corporation is a mere instrumentality or alter ego. Directors personally face civil restitution of diverted assets, accounting orders, and provisional asset freezes; management personnel based in onshore financial centers also face administrative disqualification and director bans.
AML/CFT and Economic Sanctions

Statutory sanction: The RMI is a member of the Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering and enforces the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Regulations. Operating an unlicensed money or value transfer service carries fines of up to $100,000 per violation; institutional clients and correspondent banks may terminate all RMI relationships; the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may impose civil penalties, suspend the entity, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. Violations of UN sanctions implementing the RMI United Nations Act carry criminal imprisonment and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of a Multi-Family Office performing monitored transactions face personal criminal liability, monetary penalties, and imprisonment in the RMI (up to 10 years), plus equivalent enforcement by foreign regulators under their residual jurisdiction over global wires; personal asset freezes and extradition requests are likely.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs (MIRC), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; online portal: https://mirc.gov.mh

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Business Corporation registered in the Marshall Islands obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing TIN-01 with MIRC together with a certified copy of the Articles of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, registered agent address, and non-resident status declaration. MIRC issues the TIN on the same day; no VAT ID is required or available because the jurisdiction does not operate a VAT/GST regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Business Corporation registered in the Marshall Islands obtains a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) by filing TIN-01 with MIRC together with a certified copy of the Articles of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, registered agent address, and non-resident status declaration. MIRC issues the TIN on the same day; no VAT ID is required or available because the jurisdiction does not operate a VAT/GST regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

A Marshall Islands Business Corporation may elect 'Non-Resident Business Corporation' status under the Business Corporations Act, thereby confirming exemption from all Marshall Islands taxes on foreign-source income and international trading activities. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under domestic law; the entity's tax exemption is treaty-independent and self-executing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs duties and excise taxes under the Customs and Excise Act 2015

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in the Marshall Islands. Any importer or exporter must register with MIRC for customs purposes, regardless of turnover; the currency of account is the United States Dollar (USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands has no VAT/GST system, so there is no 'zero rate' or refundable input-tax mechanism. Offshore sales of SSD/CPU chips and related services by a Non-Resident Business Corporation are entirely outside the Marshall Islands indirect tax base; no zero-rating claim is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

The Marshall Islands does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. MIRC operates ASYCUDA World for electronic customs declarations, but exports are assessed on a paper or electronic manifest; there is no continuous transaction control (CTC) requirement or fiscal API protocol.

Because there is no VAT zero-rating or refund regime, there is no statutory clawback of input tax credits. Fraudulent export exemption claims or material misdeclarations are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2015: monetary penalties up to USD 50,000, imprisonment up to five years, and an additional assessment equal to three times the duty evaded; habitual offenders may be suspended from MIRC-facilitated trade privileges.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists for both current-account and capital-account transactions because the RMI has no domestic currency and no foreign-exchange statute. There are no exchange controls, no approval gates, no surrender requirements, and no withholding or exit levies on dividends, profit remittances, repayment of shareholder loans, or repatriation of contributed capital for a non-resident-owned Business Corporation. The practical hurdles are private-bank-driven: U.S. correspondent banks apply OFAC SDN/SSI screening, FATF high-risk jurisdiction filtering, and enhanced-source-of-wealth review on inflows originating in sanctions-exposed corridors; accordingly, any remittance of trading profits should be supported by audited financial statements, trade invoices, and ECCN classification memoranda for the underlying SSDs and server processors to pre-empt frozen wires or compliance holds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-receipts surrender obligation; a non-resident Business Corporation may retain, net, or deploy export proceeds in U.S. dollars indefinitely, whether onshore or offshore. As a clearing-layer practice only, RMI-licensed banks apply a three-to-five-business-day funds-availability hold on incoming SWIFT MT103 export proceeds pending OFAC screening and beneficiary string-matching, and the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit expects contemporaneous proof of the underlying trade (invoice, bill of lading, packing list) for any USD transaction above the customer-due-diligence reporting threshold. No RMI government approval or conversion certificate is required at any time.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The RMI does not maintain a domestic central bank or a separate foreign-exchange administration; monetary and currency functions are effectively discharged by the Board of Governors of the U.S. Federal Reserve System pursuant to the Compact of Free Association, because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender. Banking licensing, prudential supervision, and residual exchange-administration powers are vested in the Banking Commissioner under the Financial Institutions Act 2011, supported by AML/CFT suspicious-transaction oversight from the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit; the Banking Commissioner also licenses and supervises remittance agents and escrow agents engaged in trade clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. The RMI has no domestic EMI or digital-bank licensing framework, and major offshore EMI/fintech platforms (e.g., Mercury, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Payoneer) generally decline onboarding of a non-resident RMI Business Corporation because the jurisdiction is treated as a higher-risk offshore shell-vehicle jurisdiction in correspondent-bank risk models and lacks a central-bank reciprocity or licensing passport on which EMIs can rely. A workable alternative is to route the operating cash flow through the RMI BC's U.S. or Hong Kong trading hub (with physical substance) and use that hub's EMI account only for customer collections, while the RMI BC maintains its statutory treasury on tier-1 bank accounts; the RMI Banking Commissioner can license a money transmitter for non-deposit-taking payment pass-through, but that does not reproduce deposit-taking or escrow-inspection capabilities.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Bearer-share legacy risk and opaque beneficial ownership of the non-resident Business Corporation.

Defense evidence: Certified certificate of good standing from the RMI Registrar of Corporations; certified register of directors, officers, and shareholders; full UBO register filed with the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit; written confirmation that no bearer shares are outstanding and that any historic bearer instruments have been canceled and immobilized; a board resolution disclosing the ownership chain to the ultimate natural person; and a notarized source-of-wealth statement for each UBO.
Shell-risk flag: no physical office, employees, or substance in the RMI combined with a tax-exempt zoning model.

High-volume SSD and server-processor spot trading with split consignments, serialized inventory, and multi-currency invoice discipline.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be directed as USD-denominated SWIFT MT103 (with MT202/COV cover leg where required) from a U.S., Hong Kong, Singapore, or EU shareholder-funded clearing bank, using ISO 20022 Purpose Code EQUI (equity investment) and a field-70 remittance reference structured as 'MH_BC CAPITAL INJECTION - SHARE ISSUANCE RESOLUTION [date/no.] - UBO [name]'. The bank's onboarding of the injected funds triggers automatic matching of the UBO register to the remitter screen; deposits above USD 100,000 invoke enhanced due diligence including the capital-introduction letter, board share-issuance resolution, valuation support at par or fair value, and source-of-wealth bank statements. No RMI regulatory approval is required, but the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit suspicious-transaction threshold applies, so the entire value trail must remain documented; funds must not be split into sub-USD 10,000 tranches, as structured-avoidance screening would then freeze the account.Outbound trade disbursements should be ranked by authenticity and control: (1) sight Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) with settlement via MT103/MT202 against presentation of the independent inspection certificate, transport documents, and title documents, favoring U.S., Hong Kong, or Singapore issuing banks; (2) SWIFT MT103 GPI single-credit transfers for confirmed supplier payables, always carrying Purpose Code TRAD and contract and inspection-lot references in field 70; (3) escrow-inspection wire releases, where the designated escrow agent disburses against a digitally signed inspection certificate from the locked Capital Escrow Account; and (4) banker's drafts for minor spot purchases. All disbursements must run over U.S., Hong Kong, or Singapore USD clearing corridors, avoid sanctioned-corridor intermediaries, adopt T+1 value dating to allow OFAC post-credit screening, and be reconciled in a standing file that maps each outbound wire to its invoice, inspection certificate, bill of lading, and serial-number register.Outright deliverable FX forwards (USD versus TWD, KRW, JPY, EUR, or CNH), tenor up to 12 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or IFEMA with a U.S., Hong Kong, or Singapore-licensed bank and supported by underlying trade orders.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing USD/CNY (settled against CNH), USD/TWD, and USD/KRW offshore reference rates, permissible where local-currency deliverability is legally or practically restricted.Cross-currency swaps (USD versus JPY, EUR, or CNH) hedging matched trade-payable and trade-receivable cash-flow streams, including basis-swap funding of inventory financing.Vanilla FX options, zero-cost collars, and participating-forward structures under the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions with a credit support annex, approved case-by-case by the clearing bank; intercompany netting of matching trade flows is permitted as a cash-management overlay but is not treated as a hedging instrument.

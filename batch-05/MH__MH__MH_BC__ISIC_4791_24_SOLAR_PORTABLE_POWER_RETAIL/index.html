Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); taxpayer account and filing portal: the official RMI Taxpayer Access Portal administered by the Revenue and Taxation Division. For customs matters, the RMI Customs Service operates under the same Ministry of Finance. For corporate registration, the Registrar of Corporations is the separate competent authority under the Business Corporations Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or non-resident entity obtains its RMI tax identity through the following procedure: (i) complete incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act, or complete foreign registration if the entity is incorporated abroad; (ii) file the Revenue and Taxation Division's taxpayer registration application designated for non-resident business corporations, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered agent acceptance, constitutional documents, director and shareholder particulars, and principal place of business statement; (iii) receive the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where applicable, a Non-Resident Business Corporation Exemption Certificate; (iv) separately register as a customs importer only if the entity will bring goods into RMI customs territory; (v) note that no VAT identification number is issued because the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax, goods-and-services tax, or sales-and-use tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — the non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC), when not carrying on trade or business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands and deriving no RMI-source income, is statutorily outside the RMI income tax net and exempt from RMI business license taxation at the national level. It is not a pass-through entity for RMI tax purposes and is not subject to entity-level RMI income tax. — A foreign or non-resident entity obtains its RMI tax identity through the following procedure: (i) complete incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act, or complete foreign registration if the entity is incorporated abroad; (ii) file the Revenue and Taxation Division's taxpayer registration application designated for non-resident business corporations, accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered agent acceptance, constitutional documents, director and shareholder particulars, and principal place of business statement; (iii) receive the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and, where applicable, a Non-Resident Business Corporation Exemption Certificate; (iv) separately register as a customs importer only if the entity will bring goods into RMI customs territory; (v) note that no VAT identification number is issued because the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax, goods-and-services tax, or sales-and-use tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC), when not carrying on trade or business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands and deriving no RMI-source income, is statutorily outside the RMI income tax net and exempt from RMI business license taxation at the national level. It is not a pass-through entity for RMI tax purposes and is not subject to entity-level RMI income tax.

Available election forms

None. The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime and does not recognize hybrid-mismatch classification elections for domestic tax purposes. Entity treatment is determined by the Business Corporations Act and by actual RMI-source activity. A non-resident Business Corporation cannot elect pass-through status in the RMI; the tax-exempt classification is automatic upon satisfaction of non-residence and no-RMI-source-income conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax: Not applicable. The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not levy any value-added tax, goods-and-services tax, or sales-and-use tax on retail sales, e-commerce supplies, offshore services, or digital transactions. The only transaction-based indirect tax is customs import duty, which applies to physical importation of goods into RMI customs territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold exists for VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax because no such tax is imposed. The official currency is the United States dollar (USD). A person or entity carrying on business with physical or operational nexus in the RMI must obtain a local business license from the first USD of gross receipts, and must register with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration if it employs staff. A non-resident tax-exempt Business Corporation with no RMI nexus has no indirect tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax, a statutory zero-rating claim is not applicable. At the customs duty level, goods physically exported from RMI to a foreign destination are outside the RMI duty base and may be exported duty-free if supported by evidence of removal from RMI customs territory. For the direct-to-online-consumer retail model described by ISIC 4791, if the portable power and solar goods are shipped from a foreign fulfillment warehouse directly to a foreign consumer, the goods never enter RMI customs territory and no RMI export declaration or zero-rating claim is required. If goods are first imported into RMI and subsequently re-exported, duty-free export treatment may be claimed with the RMI Customs Service subject to timely export and documentation.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (fully completed and electronically filed)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from RMI customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin, where preferential or duty-free tariff treatment is claimed
  • Re-export authorization or certificate of non-manipulation, where applicable

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, XML/API clearance, or continuous transaction control mandate exists for RMI domestic or e-commerce transactions. Ordinary commercial invoices and accounting records are sufficient. For physical imports and exports, clearance is processed through the RMI Customs electronic declaration system; that system is a customs control mechanism, not a tax invoice clearance or e-invoicing platform.

Any fraudulent or abusive reliance on export duty-free or zero-rated treatment is subject to RMI tax and customs audit, retrospective assessment and clawback of all avoided duties or taxes, monetary penalties of up to twice the amount of duty or tax evaded, forfeiture of the relevant goods, suspension or revocation of the taxpayer's customs registration and TIN status, criminal fines, and imprisonment for false declarations or fraudulent export documentation. The burden of proving that goods were exported and proceeds repatriated remains on the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Because the U.S. dollar is domestic legal tender, the RMI is de facto fully convertible. There is no separate capital-account versus current-account licensing regime, no stamp-duty tax on remittances, and no central-bank approval gate for cross-border transfers. Capital contributions, dividends, and profit remittances from an RMI Business Corporation (BC) are not subject to withholding taxes or FX surrender requirements. For this e-commerce entity, the only practical remittance hurdles are: (1) bank or EMI KYC and source-of-funds verification; (2) U.S. correspondent-bank de-risking of RMI-licensed banks; (3) FATCA, IRS Form W-8/W-9, and U.S. EIN reporting where a U.S. settlement account is used; and (4) merchant-of-record settlement rules imposed by Stripe and Affirm. A Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Business License Act 1999 is required for a foreign investor carrying on business inside RMI territory, but pure online retail administered through a non-resident BC with no RMI office or local revenue falls outside that licensing trigger.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI statute prescribes no repatriation timeline, no minimum surrender ratio, and no mandatory conversion of export proceeds, because the domestic currency is the U.S. dollar. Receipts from Stripe and Affirm settle in USD and may lawfully be held offshore indefinitely. Where trade proceeds arrive in non-USD currencies, conversion is at the commercial bank's discretion and requires no declaration to any RMI authority. In practice, the company should retain commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and customs declarations for solar panel and portable power equipment exports; these documents are necessary to satisfy the U.S. bank's OFAC and AML narrative and to meet counterparty-country trade-verification rules (for example, PBOC and SAFE documentary checks in China for supplier payments).

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no central bank, no foreign-exchange control authority, and no monetary policy instrument of its own; the U.S. dollar is the official legal tender under the Compact of Free Association. Prudential licensing and supervision of banks, trust companies, and money transmitters are administered by the Office of the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance pursuant to the Banking Act 1987 (as amended); AML/CFT supervision and reporting are executed by the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit and the National Task Force under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2000. RMI is a member of the Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering (APG) and has harmonized its framework to FATF Recommendations; however, no RMI statutory body is empowered to approve, withhold, or license cross-border payment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks (Mercury, Relay, Brex, Payoneer, Wise Business, Stripe Treasury) will generally accept an RMI BC only if the entity obtains a U.S. EIN, provides a U.S. operational address or registered-agent nexus, and passes a substance questionnaire; purely anonymous or no-nexus EMI accounts are unavailable. Affirm BNPL cannot be integrated directly by the RMI BC; it must be supplied through a U.S.-based payment facilitator or master-merchant arrangement that pays out to the U.S. settlement account. Legal constraints include FATCA entity classification, EMI policy bans on high-risk corridors and co-mingling of merchant settlement funds, and contractual restrictions on crypto-adjacent or third-party payout flows. If the RMI holding entity lacks economic substance, EMIs typically require a sub-account structure beneath the U.S. operating subsidiary rather than opening a standalone account for the RMI shell.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All shareholder capital injections and shareholder loans must be wire-transferred by SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire to the BC's account in the BC's exact legal name. The remitter should populate field 72 or 77B with 'Equity contribution', 'Shareholder subordinated loan' or 'Working-capital facility' as applicable and attach the share-subscription agreement or loan agreement to the bank. No RMI exchange-control authorization is required for inbound funds. Where the remitting jurisdiction enforces its own purpose codes (for example, SAFE overseas-investment codes in China or EU statistical purpose codes), the BC must provide its Certificate of Incorporation, opening-bank confirmation letter, and executed share-subscription or loan documentation. The account bank will credit only after OFAC, PEP, and sanctions screening of the BC and its beneficial owners. For merchant settlement, Stripe and Affirm settle in USD via ACH or Fedwire into the designated U.S. settlement account; the RMI BC must instruct the processors to use the exact registered legal name and U.S. EIN to prevent name-mismatch payout holds.Supplier payments for portable battery generators and solar panels are cleared primarily by SWIFT MT103 in USD or, for purchase orders exceeding USD 250,000, by an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 by the RMI bank through its New York correspondent and advised or confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, typically a Chinese or ASEAN bank. Every MT103 must align the commercial invoice, purchase order, HS tariff code, Incoterms, and shipment date; for China-bound remittances, the Chinese receiving bank requires a matching sales contract and the importer's customs declaration before credit, failing which the funds may be suspended under PBOC and SAFE trade-authenticity rules. Corporate card rails are not permitted for inventory settlement because of MCC restrictions and cash-advance risk; ACH push may be used only for U.S. suppliers and intra-group working-capital sweeps. Treasury should perform daily end-of-day reconciliation between the RMI accumulation account, the U.S. settlement account, and Stripe or Affirm payout statements to detect clearing failures and foreign-bank intermediary deductions.USD-CNH (offshore Renminbi) deliverable forwards - permissible as OTC derivatives; booked under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Tier-1 international bank; physically or cash-settled in USD and CNH.USD-CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) - permissible for hedging onshore CNY exposure where physical delivery is unavailable; cash-settled in USD; no RMI regulatory restriction.USD-CNH cross-currency swaps - permissible for hedging principal and interest mismatches on shareholder loans, supplier-financing facilities, or working-capital mismatches between USD settlement receipts and CNY procurement payables.Vanilla FX options and zero-premium collars on USD-CNH - permissible as cash-flow hedges but restricted to documented underlying procurement exposure under an approved treasury risk policy.Currency swaps referencing CNY interest-rate benchmarks - permissible where the swap is booked offshore and the RMI BC documents hedge effectiveness; hedge accounting under ASC 815 is available under U.S. GAAP used by the group.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks (Mercury, Relay, Brex, Payoneer, Wise Business, Stripe Treasury) will generally accept an RMI BC only if the entity obtains a U.S. EIN, provides a U.S. operational address or registered-agent nexus, and passes a substance questionnaire; purely anonymous or no-nexus EMI accounts are unavailable. Affirm BNPL cannot be integrated directly by the RMI BC; it must be supplied through a U.S.-based payment facilitator or master-merchant arrangement that pays out to the U.S. settlement account. Legal constraints include FATCA entity classification, EMI policy bans on high-risk corridors and co-mingling of merchant settlement funds, and contractual restrictions on crypto-adjacent or third-party payout flows. If the RMI holding entity lacks economic substance, EMIs typically require a sub-account structure beneath the U.S. operating subsidiary rather than opening a standalone account for the RMI shell.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in USD — the RMI has no transfer pricing legislation, no Local File/Master File obligation, and no CbC reporting threshold because exempt NRDCs are outside the corporate income tax base. De facto documentation trigger: USD 1,000,000 in related-party cross-border transactions where a foreign tax authority (e.g., IRS) or a foreign customs authority examines the RMI entity's invoicing, requiring a consistent arm's-length pricing policy to avoid a foreign permanent establishment and reallocation of profits.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Implementation) Regulations 2021 apply to an RMI Business Corporation carrying on a 'relevant activity,' including a distribution and service centre. Pure e-commerce retail of portable solar generators and battery units qualifies as a distribution activity when the entity purchases goods from a manufacturing affiliate and resells them. Such entities must file an Economic Substance notification/declaration with the RMI Economic Substance Office annually. The statutory substance test is satisfied only if core income-generating activities (procurement, inventory risk, logistics decision-making) are conducted with adequate premises and staff in the RMI. A zero-substance NRDC is at high risk of failing the test; the standard practical defense is to demonstrate that all operational decisions, inventory, and personnel are located in the foreign market (e.g., a US distribution warehouse), making the RMI entity a pure holding/intermediary not engaged in a relevant activity in the RMI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Retroactive imposition of the 3% corporate tax on RMI-source revenue, interest at 12% per annum, 25% late-filing penalty, and fines up to USD 25,000 under the Revenue and Taxation Regulations. Foreign authorities may separately assert permanent establishment tax, state sales/use tax arrearages, and customs penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false exemption declarations or direct intercompany transfers without economic substance face civil restitution for diverted corporate tax, fraud penalties, and in aggravated cases personal liability for the corporation's entire foreign tax delinquency; travel bans may be imposed during revenue authority investigation.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Failure of the Economic Substance declaration: corrective order, penalty of USD 10,000 for the first year and USD 20,000 for each subsequent year, plus possible deregistration by the Registrar of Corporations. Under EU tax-good-governance scrutiny, the company may be listed as a shell entity, triggering refusal of treaty benefits from the owner's residence state.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly allowing the entity to operate as a shell are liable for civil damages to creditors and for the cost of the economic substance corrective process; repeated violations expose them to disqualification from serving as directors of any RMI company and to a 10-year travel ban.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2021, fines up to USD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the entity and the registered agent; the corporate registry may strike the company after 30 days of non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Each director remains personally liable for AML violations unless he or she can prove reasonable reliance on the registered agent; conviction triggers director disqualification, personal criminal fines up to USD 50,000, and a mandatory travel ban for the duration of incarceration.
Fraudulent Trading and Piercing the Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: RMI courts will pierce the BC's limited liability under the Business Corporations Act, rendering the shareholder and directors personally liable for the company's debts; civil restitution includes all misappropriated profits, treble damages in cases of intentional fraud, and a court-ordered winding-up of the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for fraudulent trading debts, court-ordered disqualification from corporate management, a bar from re-entry into the RMI as a director of any licensed entity, and in egregious cases extradition and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Criminal Code for fraud and money laundering.

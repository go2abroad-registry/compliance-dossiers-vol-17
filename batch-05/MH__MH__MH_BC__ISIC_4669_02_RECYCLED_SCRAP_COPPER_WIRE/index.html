Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Office (RICO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro and Ebeye service offices. Corporate formation and annual filing are administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Finance, a separate division from RICO. No mandatory e-filing portal exists for non-resident exempt business corporations; filings are effected through licensed registered agents.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to procure a TIN, VAT ID, or GRT number solely for offshore trading activity. If the BC later engages in business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), the following sequence applies: (1) file for a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1998 unless wholly owned by RMI citizens; (2) register with RICO for Gross Revenue Tax in the prescribed registration form; (3) receive a RICO-issued GRT/TIN; (4) file periodic GRT returns and remit tax on annual gross revenue exceeding USD 25,000; and (5) obtain any municipal business license required by local ordinance. For a pure offshore consignment business operating through documentary letters of credit and SWIFT TT payment rails, no RMI tax registration is triggered.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to procure a TIN, VAT ID, or GRT number solely for offshore trading activity. If the BC later engages in business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), the following sequence applies: (1) file for a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1998 unless wholly owned by RMI citizens; (2) register with RICO for Gross Revenue Tax in the prescribed registration form; (3) receive a RICO-issued GRT/TIN; (4) file periodic GRT returns and remit tax on annual gross revenue exceeding USD 25,000; and (5) obtain any municipal business license required by local ordinance. For a pure offshore consignment business operating through documentary letters of credit and SWIFT TT payment rails, no RMI tax registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The RMI has no 'check-the-box' or statutory hybrid-mismatch election under domestic law. Entity classification is fixed by the Business Corporation Act (BCA); a BC is a body corporate separate from its shareholders. The only practical elections are: (a) maintenance of non-resident status versus conducting RMI domestic business, which would subject the BC to GRT and business licensing; (b) charter elections under the BCA regarding corporate purpose and capital structure, which have no effect on RMI tax classification. For foreign purposes, particularly US federal tax, the BC is a per se eligible entity that may be affirmatively classified as an association taxable as a corporation or disregarded under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3, but such election does not alter RMI domestic tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The only consumption-type indirect taxation is Import Duty levied under the RMI Customs Tariff Schedule, with general ad valorem rates typically between 5% and 10% and specific rates up to 100% for certain goods. The Gross Revenue Tax (3% standard rate; 7% for certain telecommunications services) is best characterized as a direct turnover tax on RMI-source revenue rather than an indirect tax for VAT/GST analogy purposes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 25,000 annual gross revenue derived from RMI sources is the statutory GRT registration and liability threshold. For import duty, no monetary threshold applies; liability arises per transaction when goods physically enter the RMI customs territory. A FIBL, with annual fees generally ranging from USD 250 to USD 5,000 depending on business scope, is required before commencing any foreign investment business in the RMI. Non-resident BCs without RMI-source operations are below all registration thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose VAT or GST; therefore the concept of zero-rating, export VAT refunds, or input-output reconciliation has no statutory meaning in RMI law. Exports from the RMI, and offshore consignments such as the underlying ISIC 4669 scrap-copper trade, are outside the scope of RMI taxation. The documentation bundle must instead satisfy the importing jurisdiction's customs and VAT/duty regimes, the governing letter-of-credit conditions, and the bank's trade-compliance and sanctions due process.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only where RMI export clearance is triggered; for pure offshore transshipment, an internal export manifest may suffice)
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document evidencing physical consignment from origin to destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 confirmation (commercially required under documentary LC and TT payment rails; not a statutory RMI requirement because the RMI uses the US dollar and imposes no exchange controls)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (LC-compliant and matched to the transport document)
  • Independent Weight Certificate and Assay Certificate for recycled scrap copper (substantiating quantity, copper content, and absence of prohibited contaminants)

The RMI has not enacted any mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping regime. RICO Customs operates on paper and PDF-based declarations, manifests, and import/export entry forms. Commercial invoices and packing lists are seller-generated without pre-clearance validation by the tax authority. A cross-border consignment of shredded scrap copper that does not touch RMI territory is outside all RMI fiscal clearance procedures.

Zero-rating clawback is structurally impossible because no export VAT is charged in the RMI. However, analogous risks apply: customs misdeclaration or undervaluation in RMI territory attracts forfeiture of goods, fines up to double the duty evaded, and criminal liability for false statements. A BC that falsely claims non-resident status while conducting RMI business is subject to GRT reassessment, statutory interest, late-payment penalties, potential FIBL revocation, and corporate-registry sanctions. Given the ISIC 4669 medium-risk AML profile, fabricated invoices, weight mismatches, or inconsistent assay documentation may also generate a Suspicious Transaction Report to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit and correspondent-bank de-risking.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Because the functional and settlement currency is the USD, statutory exchange control is functionally null. There are no capital account restrictions, no repatriation approval regime, and no dual-currency mechanism: equity injection, profit remittance, liquidation surplus, and third-party trade settlements may all be cleared freely in USD. Real-world friction is limited to (i) correspondent-bank de-risking of Pacific island banks, (ii) FIA 2020 customer due-diligence obligations applicable to non-resident BCs, and (iii) transaction monitoring thresholds enforced by the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU); none of these requires a separate central-bank foreign-investment permit or capital controls waiver.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. RMI imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender-to-domestic-bank obligation on export proceeds from recycled scrap copper. Proceeds may be retained offshore, swept to the BOMI operational account, or applied directly against supplier settlements; the only compliance touchpoints are (i) accurate reconciliation of each receipt to the MT103 reference narrative and HS 7404 commodity description, (ii) 5-year retention of trade documents under FIA 2020, and (iii) filing of suspicious transaction reports (STRs) with the RMI FIU where any proceeds derive from suspicious counterparty activity.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains no domestic central bank. The U.S. dollar is the official legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, making the U.S. Federal Reserve the de facto monetary and settlement authority. Prudential licensing, regulation, and AML/CFT supervision of the banking sector is vested in the Financial Institutions Commission (FIC), established under the Financial Institutions Act 2020 (FIA 2020), which succeeded the Banking Commissioner of the Ministry of Finance. The relevant clearing and control environment for this non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is therefore U.S.-centric, executed through FIC-licensed banks (Bank of Marshall Islands and Bank of Guam, RMI branch) and their U.S. correspondent clearings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMI wallet providers. FIA 2020 makes it an offense to operate a deposit-taking or e-money business without an RMI license, and licensed banks are required to treat relationships with unlicensed EMIs as an AML red flag. A non-resident BC should not rely on Panama, BVI, or Hong Kong electronic money institution cards for treasury flows. Ancillary collections may use regulated U.S. money-transmitter platforms, but statutory payment rails for scrap copper trade must be SWIFT MT103/MT700 documentary instruments through FIC-licensed institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must arrive in USD by SWIFT from the investor's own bank account, with a narrative containing the BC's exact legal name, ISIC 4669 recycled copper trading capital, and a reference to the RMI board resolution; the receiving bank credits a segregated Capital Escrow Account until source-of-funds documents are cleared by compliance. The standard purpose-code mapping used by U.S. correspondents is 'G3' (external short-term loans) for shareholder advances; capital subscription entries are booked with a corporate-purpose narrative because RMI is not an IMF-code reporting jurisdiction. Physical import or export of cash above USD 10,000 across RMI borders triggers currency declaration obligations; the BC must never inject funds via cash, travelers cheques, or third-party settlement agents.Trade settlements must move through dual rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct payment for consignments under USD 250,000, instructed only upon receipt of a scanned bill of lading, assay certificate, seller-signed proforma invoice, and per-lot weight certificate; (2) irrevocable unconfirmed or confirmed documentary letter of credit (MT700) for larger consignments, with a document checklist of commercial invoice, full set of original bills of lading, weight and assay certificate, and certificate of origin, payable at sight through Bank of Guam's U.S. network; a standby LC (MT760) may secure supplier performance bonds; interbank funding sweeps between BOMI and U.S. correspondents are executed via MT202. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (RMI imposes no corporate income tax on non-resident Business Corporations; tax is limited to income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid by a non-resident Business Corporation, whether paid to resident or non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The renewal and payment of the annual franchise fee are due on or before May 31 of each year for corporations on a calendar fiscal year, or within four months after the fiscal year-end for corporations with a non-calendar fiscal year. Late filings are accepted, but defaults trigger penalties and administrative dissolution after 120 days.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable. RMI has no corporate income tax and therefore no statutory Local File or Master File documentation threshold. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) require all relevant entities to maintain adequate records of core income-generating activities in RMI, but no specific TP threshold is prescribed in US dollars (the local currency is the US dollar).

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended) apply to corporations carrying out 'relevant activities', which include distribution and service centers. The PCR/consignment sale of shredded non-ferrous scrap metals (ISIC 4669) is a distribution activity where the BC purchases goods, manages physical consignment, and resells to third parties. The BC must in each financial year satisfy: (1) board meetings and strategic decisions held in RMI; (2) physical premises and a minimum number of qualified employees at a level commensurate with the activity; (3) adequate expenditure in RMI; and (4) performance of core income-generating activities — purchasing, inventory management, transportation coordination, and risk management — in RMI, unless outsourced to a qualifying RMI entity. Annual economic substance reporting is mandatory. Non-compliance fines range from US$10,000 to US$50,000 per offence, with a possible additional daily fine and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Exemption Declaration

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Act renders tax evasion a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to US$10,000 and imprisonment for up to five years. The Business Corporations Act separately imposes fines of up to US$5,000 for false declarations and permits immediate cancellation of registration.

Director personal liability: Any director or officer who signs a false statutory declaration or knowingly submits a false annual report is personally liable for all uncollected taxes, penalties, and costs. Criminal conviction for evasion carries imprisonment; the courts may also issue a travel ban against the director pending restitution.
Shell Company Abuse / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations fines: US$10,000 per offense for failure to provide information; US$20,000 per offense for false or misleading information; US$50,000 for continuing failure after formal notice. The Registrar may strike the entity off the register and issue a warning to banking partners.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham entity face disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years and personal fines equal to double the entity-level fine. Civil restitution orders may require directors to repatriate profits earned through the lack of substance.
Money Laundering / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: The RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Act imposes imprisonment up to 15 years and fines up to US$250,000 for money laundering. The Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze bank accounts, block LC payments, and recommend criminal prosecution. Violations of UN or US sanctions incorporated into RMI law attract seizure of goods and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if they knew or should have known that consignment proceeds were derived from illegal activity; individual liability carries mandatory imprisonment. Civil director liability extends to receiving and forwarding tainted funds, including disgorgement and legal costs.
Unlicensed Business Operation

Statutory sanction: Operating without a license is a misdemeanor under RMI trade and commerce regulations, punishable by a fine of US$500 to US$5,000 per day of violation. The regulator may void affected sale and shipping contracts and deny future license applications.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for each day of unlicensed operation, and the corporation may forfeit all revenues earned during the unlicensed period. Individual directors may be ordered to pay treble damages to indemnify the RMI government.
Corporate Veil Piercing / Trading While Dissolved

Statutory sanction: Contracts entered into while administratively dissolved are voidable at the counterparty's option. The Registrar imposes a reinstatement fee of US$500 plus all unpaid fees and penalties. Fraudulent continuation of business during dissolution may result in unlimited liability for all debts incurred during the period.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for obligations incurred after the dissolution date, without limit. The RMI courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose personal bankruptcy, freeze personal assets, impose a travel ban prohibiting departure from the country, and order imprisonment for willful violations of the Business Corporations Act.

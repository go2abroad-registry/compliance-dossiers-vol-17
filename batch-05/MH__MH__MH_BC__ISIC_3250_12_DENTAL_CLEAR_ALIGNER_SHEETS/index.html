Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Taxation Office, Ministry of Finance (administers direct tax and social security); Customs and Excise Division, Ministry of Finance (administers indirect tax/import duty). Public portal: https://rmigovernment.org (official government e-services portal).

E-government filing portal

The Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no national income tax, VAT, or sales tax. A non-resident Business Corporation is not required to obtain a TIN or VAT ID. If the entity transacts locally or employs individuals, it must register with the RMI Social Security Administration for an employer registration number. For physical importation or export of goods, the entity must register with the RMI Customs and Excise Division for a Customs Client/Importer ID (RMI-CID) and obtain a business license from the Registrar of Corporations.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no national income tax, VAT, or sales tax. A non-resident Business Corporation is not required to obtain a TIN or VAT ID. If the entity transacts locally or employs individuals, it must register with the RMI Social Security Administration for an employer registration number. For physical importation or export of goods, the entity must register with the RMI Customs and Excise Division for a Customs Client/Importer ID (RMI-CID) and obtain a business license from the Registrar of Corporations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Non-resident Business Corporations incorporated under the RMI Business Corporations Act (BCCA) that conduct no business within the Republic and derive no income from RMI sources are statutorily exempt from any future national income tax. There is no check-the-box regime and no hybrid mismatch election, as the RMI currently levies no corporate income tax. The BCCA permits an election to register as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation, which triggers a conclusive presumption of foreign-source status for all corporate income and transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Import Duty and Excise Duty (Customs and Excise Act 2018); no VAT, no GST, and no sales and use tax exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. The RMI operates no VAT or GST and therefore imposes no registration threshold. Import duty arises transactionally on actual physical importation into the RMI; no economic turnover threshold triggers an indirect tax registration. A Customs Client/Importer ID is required only for clearance purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

In the absence of VAT/GST, export zero-rating is inapplicable as a concept. Goods exported from the RMI are not subject to export duty. Goods manufactured in the RMI and shipped abroad qualify for a duty-free export regime, and imported raw materials subsequently re-exported in an unused state or incorporated into an exported product may qualify for a customs duty drawback or temporary importation relief under Customs and Excise Act 2018. For a non-resident Business Corporation operating wholly offshore, domestic sales nexus is absent and no indirect tax liability attaches to export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs form EX-1, filed through PITAC)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill (Air)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Credit Advice (evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from the overseas buyer, required for duty drawback and for foreign-exchange record purposes)

None. The RMI has no electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no certified fiscal data controller, and no digital stamping or API-based tax clearance gateway. Paper-based customs declarations are submitted via the Pacific Islands Trade and Customs (PITAC) portal primarily for statistical and duty-assessment purposes.

False export declarations, diversion of duty-free or drawback-qualified goods into domestic consumption, and fraudulent claims of export relief are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 2018. Penalties include fines of up to USD 10,000 per offence, forfeiture of goods, and retrospective clawback of any duty refund or drawback paid, increased by a 50% penalty surcharge. Repeated or collusive violations may also result in denial of future customs clearances and suspension of the entity's Customs Client/Importer ID.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI is a dollarized economy with fully unrestricted currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no foreign-exchange permits, approvals, or restrictions on inward or outward payments. Current-account payments, including trade settlements, service fees, royalties, and profit remittances, may be made freely in USD. Capital-account transactions, including equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, debt repayments, and liquidation distributions, are likewise unrestricted. Profit remittances by a Non-Resident Business Corporation are not subject to RMI statutory withholding or foreign-exchange clearance; the only practical hurdles are the remitting bank's AML source-of-funds review, OFAC/sanctions screening, and destination-country tax treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation or holding-period requirement for export proceeds. Export receivables may be retained in USD or foreign currency in any bank account, onshore or offshore, and may be freely converted, invested, or hedged without central bank or Banking Commission approval.

Central bank supervision: Not applicable — the Republic of the Marshall Islands has no central bank and no foreign-exchange administration. The U.S. dollar is legal tender, and banking supervision is exercised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium structured viability. The RMI has no electronic-money-institution licensing framework, and offshore digital EMIs are not supervised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission. Offshore EMIs cannot open local RMI accounts and are not authorized banking counterparties for the RMI entity. They may be used only as third-party payment processors or card-acquiring gateways, not as local bank substitutes. For the target payment rails of Platform_Credit_Card and Stripe, the compliant structure is a U.S.-domiciled merchant acquiring account settled through SWIFT or Fedwire into the RMI corporate operational account, with a contractual liability cascade covering card chargebacks, sanctions, and KYC obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be routed as a clean USD SWIFT MT103 from the named shareholder's own bank account directly to the RMI Business Corporation operational account. No cash, cryptocurrency, or third-party off-account transfers are permitted. The RMI has no exchange-control permission or notification requirement, but the bank will apply OFAC screening, UBO authentication, and source-of-funds review. Recommended MT103 fields: field 50K or 52D must disclose the remitting shareholder; field 59 must show the BC's exact account name and number; field 70 must state Capital Subscription - Equity Contribution, together with the board-resolution and subscription reference. If clearing through a U.S. correspondent bank, the Fedwire message must match the SWIFT beneficiary details and comply with OFAC and U.S. reporting requirements. For transfers above USD 25,000, the RMI bank may require a board resolution, subscription agreement, and source-of-funds memorandum.Trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier disbursements and MT202 COV for correspondent reimbursement when a letter of credit is established. Card-originated sales are settled by Stripe or platform acquiring into a U.S. settlement account and thereafter swept by ACH, Fedwire, or SWIFT MT103 to the RMI operating account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold, Local File, or Master File obligation exists in Marshall Islands law, and the functional currency is USD. Nevertheless, if the BC engages in cross-border related-party transactions (e.g., purchasing multilayer PETG/TPU aligner sheet inputs from an affiliate, or receiving distribution fees from a foreign parent), the entity should retain pricing analyses, intercompany agreements, and functional/risk documentation to substantiate arm's-length outcomes under general anti-avoidance principles and the ESR substance review.

Economic substance scope: Applicable under the RMI Economic Substance Regulations 2020. A non-resident BC is exempt from income tax but must satisfy substance tests if it carries on a relevant activity: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, pure holding company, distribution and service center, or intellectual property holding. The thermoforming and automated trimming of dental clear aligner sheets (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339114) is not itself a listed relevant activity, but any separately conducted distribution, financing, or IP-holding functions are in scope and require adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and locally directed expenditure plus annual ESR reporting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Non-Resident Certification

Statutory sanction: Revocation of tax-exempt zone status; retroactive assessment of any applicable RMI-source taxes, interest, and penalties; criminal fines up to USD 5,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for false statements under the Income Tax Act 1989 and RMI Criminal Code; administrative dissolution by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign a false declaration are personally liable for civil restitution of avoided fees and taxes; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose director disqualification orders, and issue travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations: monetary penalties up to USD 10,000 for non-filing or false filing, an additional penalty equal to the avoided tax benefit where relevant activity is established, deregistration of the entity, and prohibition of the registered agent from continuing to represent the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the substance failure are jointly and severally liable for ESR penalties and enforcement costs; travel restrictions, director debarment, and asset freezing orders may be imposed by the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit and courts.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the RMI Banking Act, Crimes (Amendment) Act, and AML/CFT framework: criminal money-laundering fines, forfeiture of proceeds, freezing orders, revocation of the entity's right to operate payment rails, and debarment from international correspondent banking.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers with knowledge or reckless disregard face personal criminal prosecution, civil forfeiture of benefits, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced where the BC is used as an instrumentality of money laundering.
Failure to File Annual Report and Chain Deregistration

Statutory sanction: Late penalty of USD 200 (increased by USD 400 after six months), administrative dissolution by the Registrar, automatic loss of legal capacity, and inability to sue, enforce contracts, or hold assets in RMI courts; reinstatement requires arrears, penalties, and reinstatement fees.

Director personal liability: Directors remain fiduciaries during delinquency and are jointly and severally liable for debts and contractual obligations incurred after the entity's apparent dissolution; foreign directors may face personal service of process and asset attachment in cooperating jurisdictions.
Unlicensed Medical Device Manufacturing and Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Revocation of statutory license, product seizure and recall, injunctive relief, fines under RMI public health and consumer protection statutes, and exclusion of the entity from the RMI corporate registry if the unlicensed activity is combined with false annual declarations.

Director personal liability: Officers are personally liable for consumer harm, defective product claims, and fraudulent misrepresentation; criminal prosecution, civil restitution, and director disqualification may follow where the Board knowingly authorized unlicensed production or deceptive payment-rail declarations.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Majuro — responsible for TIN issuance, Business Privilege Tax (BPT), and Income Tax Act 1989 administration. RMI Customs Service under the Ministry of Finance administers import duties, bonded warehouses, and export/aircraft-stores documentation. The Registrar of Corporations under the Office of the Attorney General handles corporate registration. No unified national e-portal exists for all taxes; BPT and income tax filings are submitted in paper or PDF to the RTO, while customs declarations are lodged electronically via the RMI customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Business Corporation under the Business Corporations Act 1996 through a licensed Registered Agent; obtain Articles of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing — no minimum capital requirement applies to a non-resident-owned BC. Step 2: If the in-flight catering kitchen conducts activity inside RMI (a customs-bonded production plant at an airport), signatory officers must register the BC with the RTO within 30 days of commencing business to obtain a TIN and the combined BPT/Business License registration; a Foreign Investment Business License from the Ministry of Commerce is also required for foreign-owned entities. Step 3: Apply separately to RMI Customs for a bonded-factory/warehouse license and an importer–exporter code authorizing duty-suspended intake of raw food, beverages, and disposable service ware. Step 4: Register with the RMI Social Security Administration as an employer only when hiring locally covered employees (employer levy 6% of covered wages, employee levy 6%). Step 5: A purely passive non-resident BC with no RMI-source activity and no physical RMI presence need not appear on the RTO tax roll; upon request, the RTO will issue a certificate of non-registration or tax-exempt status letter for airline-contract counterparties and banking due diligence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the RMI treats a foreign-owned Business Corporation as a non-resident domestic corporation under a territorial exemption model. Taxable only on income sourced within RMI; all foreign-source income, including proceeds from meals sold to international air carriers where title and risk pass outside RMI territory or on board aircraft beyond RMI airspace, is exempt from income tax. No capital gains tax, no net-worth tax, no franchise tax, and no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents. The only material RMI levy on an operating kitchen is BPT on gross receipts derived from RMI-source business activity, at 2% generally or 3.5% for wholesale distribution of goods, subject to the export exemption described below.

Available election forms

RMI law has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election and no OECD hybrid-mismatch election. The elections that are available include: (i) election of non-resident domestic corporation status via Articles of Incorporation and a 100% non-resident beneficial ownership certificate; (ii) fiscal-year election for income tax and BPT (calendar-year default, alternative fiscal year allowed by RTO); (iii) source-allocation election under title-passage and destination rules that places international flight catering revenue outside RMI-source income; (iv) customs bonded-warehouse election permitting duty suspension on inputs consumed in meals delivered to departing international flights; (v) election to claim the BPT export exemption on a quarterly return schedule. Group consolidation is not available; each BC files its own return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — the RMI imposes no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax at the national level. Transaction-based levies are limited to (a) Business Privilege Tax, a gross-receipts tax on persons engaged in business in RMI at 2% for most activities and 3.5% for wholesale distribution; (b) customs import duties on goods entering RMI; and (c) specific excise taxes on fuel and certain products. The in-flight catering kitchen is exposed primarily to customs import duty (suspended while operating within the bonded regime) and BPT on any domestic ramp-side sales to local carriers or ground retail.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST regime exists. BPT registration and the annual business-license obligation apply to any person engaged in business in RMI; the Business Privilege Tax Act 1988 contains a small-trader exemption for de minimis gross revenue, the exact current threshold of which should be confirmed with the RTO when the kitchen commences operations. The functional currency is the US dollar; the RMI has no central bank and adopts USD as legal tender.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No VAT/GST zero-rating exists. Export relief is instead secured through three parallel rules: (i) Income Tax source rule — gross revenue from meals supplied to foreign airlines for consumption on international segments is treated as foreign-source income when title and risk pass outside RMI or at the aircraft door, and is therefore exempt from RMI income tax; (ii) BPT export exemption — gross receipts from goods sold or delivered for export, including bonded airline catering loaded on departing international flights, are excluded from taxable gross revenue; (iii) Customs aircraft-stores relief — food, beverages, and disposables that leave RMI with the aircraft are free of import duty despite having entered under bond, provided the bonded warehouse permit, production records, and the aircraft exit manifest reconcile exactly.

  • Customs Export Declaration or Aircraft Stores (Supplies) exit manifest per flight
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill covering each catering delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank MT103/SWIFT evidencing settlement from the airline
  • Catering delivery docket and flight-number reconciliation ledger
  • Bonded warehouse permit and goods-received/goods-issued inventory registers
  • Quarterly BPT export-exemption schedule filed with the RTO
  • HACCP/ISO 22000 hygiene certification evidence retained for food-safety and export-eligibility audit

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction-control mandate applies. Invoices and BPT records may be kept in paper or PDF and must be retained for at least 5 years. The only mandatory digital interface is customs: RMI Customs processes bonded-warehouse entries, import declarations, and export/aircraft-stores clearances through an electronic declaration system requiring API or portal filing of manifest data. A bonded catering kitchen must maintain an electronic inventory ledger reconciling all meals produced, aircraft delivery receipts, and duty-suspended inputs, which must be available to customs officers on demand.

The RTO and RMI Customs cross-match BPT returns, customs manifests, and banking inflows for catering operations. An unsubstantiated export-exemption claim triggers: (i) reassessment of BPT at the full applicable rate of 2% to 3.5% on the disallowed gross revenue; (ii) interest accrual at 0.75% per month; (iii) a 25% civil penalty of the understated gross revenue where the error is attributable to negligence; (iv) criminal liability under the Income Tax Act 1989 and Customs Act for false export or aircraft-stores declarations, punishable by fines up to USD 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; and (v) for customs fraud, forfeiture of goods, revocation of the bonded license, disgorgement of avoided duty, and a civil penalty equal to twice the duty evaded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The legal tender of the Marshall Islands is the United States Dollar (USD). There is no foreign exchange control law, no capital account restriction, and no current account restriction imposed by the Marshall Islands Government. Both current account transactions (trade and service payments) and capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, equity injections, loans, and profit repatriation) are freely convertible within the limits of the local banking system's correspondent banking relationships. Profit remittance of a non-resident Business Corporation is legally permitted, subject to the entity maintaining good standing under the Business Corporations Act, possessing a valid Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) if engaged in a foreign investment activity, and satisfying the anti-money laundering and source-of-funds verification requirements of the local settlement bank. Practical hurdles are primarily bank-level correspondent banking compliance and documentary diligence rather than statutory currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline within which aviation catering export receipts must be repatriated or exchanged. Since the domestic currency is USD and all export receipts are naturally denominated in USD, the requirement to sell export proceeds to a central bank or designated agent is not applicable. Corporate treasury may retain export earnings offshore in the entity's designated settlement account without conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, operating under the Ministry of Finance and the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1984; the Marshall Islands does not maintain a separate central bank or a domestic currency issuance authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital financial institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not eligible to hold a banking license in the Marshall Islands and are not recognized as domestic settlement banks under the Banking Act 1984. Use of an unlicensed offshore EMI for the receiving of capital injections or trade settlement is legally risky and likely to trigger foreign exchange compliance rejections by the local banking system. An EMI may serve only as an ancillary payment aggregation tool when cleared onward through a licensed correspondent bank, but it must not be used as the primary treasury holding account or cross-border clearing rail for the licensed inflight catering operation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for capital injection requires an international SWIFT MT103 wire denominated in USD from the parent or shareholder to the Marshall Islands licensed commercial bank. The beneficiary must be identified by full legal name, bank account number, and the bank's correspondent ABA/routing chain. When initiating the wire, the sending institution must include a clear transaction description, such as 'Capital contribution to MH_BC under Business Corporations Act', and reference the bank-approved entity identification number and the Foreign Investment Business License number. For the purposes of the US-based correspondent clearing system, the wire should be annotated with an ISO 20022 purpose code consistent with an equity capital injection where the system requires it, but because the Marshall Islands imposes no foreign exchange approval, the bank-level beneficial interest verification and the source of funds statement are the decisive controls. The receiving bank should issue a credit confirmation and a deposit receipt to the treasury department for audit trail integrity.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements to international airline suppliers, food processors, packaging vendors, and fuel or logistics partners are executed through direct SWIFT MT103 credit transfers or documentary commercial letters of credit (LC) where the transaction requires guaranteed payment. For low-frequency high-value aviation catering production equipment and bulk commodity purchases, an irrevocable confirmed letter of credit opened by the Marshall Islands bank or a US correspondent bank is recommended. For recurring invoices, monthly USD ACH transfers can be settled through the local bank to domestic suppliers or through the US automated clearing house network if the beneficiary is a US institution. All wires and LCs must identify the underlying trade invoice number, the ISIC 5621 catering service contract, and the airway bill or delivery confirmation.

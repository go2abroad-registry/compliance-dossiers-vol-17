Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands, Ministry of Finance – Revenue and Customs Division, Majuro; official portal: https://www.finance.gov.mh (paper-based filings; no separate online TIN issuance portal)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to obtain a national TIN or VAT ID for foreign-source digital publishing income. The sole statutory identifier is the corporate registration number issued by the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act 1990. If the BC should ever undertake RMI-source activities or hold a local business license, it must submit a paper application for a taxpayer identification number to the Revenue and Customs Division at the Ministry of Finance, attaching: (1) a certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation and current charter documents; (2) the registered agent's consent and address in the Marshall Islands; (3) a completed RMI Business License application; and (4) a declaration segregating anticipated RMI-source revenue.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not required to obtain a national TIN or VAT ID for foreign-source digital publishing income. The sole statutory identifier is the corporate registration number issued by the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act 1990. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI tax law provides no check-the-box, disregarded-entity, or hybrid-mismatch elections. The exemption/classification of a non-resident Business Corporation is automatic if it has no income sourced in the Republic and no local branch or profit-seeking activity. The only administrative elections are the optional accelerated payment of annual corporate registration fees and the choice of cash or accrual basis for local business licensing purposes where an RMI-source activity is separately approved.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable – No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (RMI indirect revenue is limited to customs duties and excise-type impositions on physical imports)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None – There is no taxable output-based indirect tax, no VAT threshold, and no registration obligation for digital magazine portal income. A physical importer of goods must be recorded with the Customs Division for each consignment regardless of value; all amounts are quantified in United States dollars.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because RMI does not impose VAT/GST/SUT, there is no export zero-rating regime. Digital magazine subscriptions, advertising/sponsored media placements, and related payments processed through Stripe or AdSense are non-RMI source service income and are outside the scope of RMI indirect and income tax without requiring an export VAT claim. This status is conditional on: (i) no RMI office, branch, or dependent agent; (ii) no RMI-direct subscription revenue booked through an RMI establishment; and (iii) no physical media dispatched into RMI customs territory.

  • Not applicable – no RMI VAT export zero-rating refund claim can be filed.
  • Not applicable – no RMI customs export declaration is required solely for digital magazine export income.
  • Not applicable – no RMI inward remittance certificate is required for Stripe/AdSense payouts.

None. RMI mandates no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API integration, or digital stamping. Digital media invoices and Stripe/AdSense payment records may be maintained in ordinary electronic format, subject to standard record-retention rules.

No export-VAT clawback is possible because no VAT/GST zero-rating has ever been allowed. The Revenue and Customs Division may audit the entity's non-resident/exempt status. If the BC is found to have earned RMI-source income or to have maintained a local business presence without a license and without filing, the Division may assess the tax due for open statutory years, add interest, impose administrative penalties, and refer deliberate concealment to the Attorney-General for criminal prosecution. For false customs declarations in physical export contexts, RMI Customs may apply seizure and monetary penalties under the RMI Customs Code.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility prevails; the Marshall Islands maintains no exchange control statute, no foreign-exchange licensing regime, and no prior-approval mechanism for cross-border transfers. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation are unrestricted for non-resident business corporations, subject only to the annual franchise tax filing obligation and general anti-money-laundering reporting. The US Dollar being the domestic currency eliminates local convertibility risk for the digital publishing model, whose advertising and subscription revenue is predominantly USD-denominated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists under Marshall Islands law. Digital publishing and sponsored-media receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible currency, with no prescribed remittance timeline, no mandatory conversion into local tender, and no registration threshold applicable to export-related earnings.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commission of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (prudential supervisor under the Banking Act 1991; the United States Dollar is domestic legal tender and there is no sovereign central bank of currency issue)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no domestic licensing framework for electronic money institutions or independent EMI charters; therefore, offshore digital EMIs may service an RMI business corporation only as unregulated money transmitters or under a foreign license, with settlement ultimately clearing through US correspondent banks. The Banking Act 1991 prohibits unlicensed deposit-taking within the RMI, and correspondent-bank de-risking of offshore digital media entities remains a practical threat. A licensed RMI bank or a US-licensed money transmitter is the preferred compliant rail for low-risk digital advertising payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 or MT202 bilateral wires initiated through licensed RMI banks, with final settlement across Fedwire or CHIPS in the United States clearing system given the US Dollar standard. No RMI regulatory approval or mandatory purpose code is required; remitters should state a descriptive narrative purpose such as 'capital contribution', 'share subscription', or 'intercompany loan', together with the corporate registry number. Verification consists of the remitting bank's source-of-funds statement, a board resolution, and beneficial ownership confirmation to satisfy both the Banking Commission and US correspondent-bank due diligence under the US Bank Secrecy Act expectations.Trade settlements and content-production disbursements are executed via SWIFT MT103 for royalties, license fees, and service payments; commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP600 for high-value print or media supply contracts; US ACH and Fedwire for domestic US vendors; and internal book transfers between licensed RMI bank accounts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable (no RMI transfer-pricing or Local/Master File rules); for foreign parent filings, maintain TP documentation at the OECD standard if intra-group transactions exceed USD 10 million (local currency is USD).

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019/2021 requires entities carrying on 'relevant activities' (e.g., distribution and service centers, headquarters, IP business, financing/leasing) to maintain economic presence in RMI. A digital magazine publishing and sponsored-media portal is not typically a listed relevant activity unless it also holds IP or acts as a distribution/service center; nevertheless, the entity must not present itself as an RMI-resident taxpayer and must preserve demonstrable offshore management to retain the non-resident exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Sham Offshore Status)

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as an RMI-resident taxpayer; imposition of national income tax (if income is RMI-source) plus penalties up to 100% of the tax due and cancellation of exempt status. Administrative fines under the Business Corporations Act for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the corporation's tax and penalties if they signed false certificates; fraudulent incorporation or evasive filing may result in director disqualification, injunctions, and criminal imprisonment under the RMI Criminal Code.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations, a finding of non-compliance can result in monetary penalties up to USD 10,000 for first offence, USD 20,000 for continuing default, and possible exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; registration may be struck off.

Director personal liability: Officers of a struck-off shell corporation may face restoration costs, personal fines for continued trading, and a direction to wind up; ultimate beneficial owners are exposed under money-transparency laws.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Regulations, unlicensed non-compliance attracts fines of up to USD 100,000 per violation for entities, with remedial orders; the corporate registration can be suspended. The RMI is not a FATF blacklisted jurisdiction, but certain non-cooperative flags trigger restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to criminal liability (imprisonment up to 5 years) for complicity in money laundering, travel bans issued by RMI courts, and confiscation of personal assets in civil forfeiture proceedings.
Fraudulent Annual Report Filing

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of up to USD 25,000 under the Business Corporations Act; possible involuntary dissolution with the loss of the RMI legal personality and inability to enforce contracts.

Director personal liability: Signing officers face personal civil liability for damages to creditors and shareholders, and criminal fines; courts may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors jointly and severally liable.
Unlicensed Financial / Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Banking and Trust Act, operating a money transmission business without a license is a criminal offence with fines and imprisonment for up to 7 years; all transaction records may be seized and the corporate bank accounts frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or fail to prevent unlicensed payment activity can be held personally criminally liable and subject to imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced for equitable restitution to defrauded advertisers.

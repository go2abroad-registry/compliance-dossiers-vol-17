Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Customs Office (RCO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro; corporate registry portal: Office of the Registrar of Corporations via the official RMI government internet portal (rmi.gov.mh); no dedicated tax e-filing portal (paper-based filing).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate as a Business Corporation (BC) under the Business Corporations Act 1990 through a licensed registered agent; the Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a unique corporate registration number. Step 2: No TIN or VAT ID is issued at incorporation; a non-resident BC deriving no Marshall Islands-source income has no national tax registration obligation. Step 3: If local-source business is undertaken, file Form GRT-1 with the RCO to obtain a Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) Account Number and remit the 3% GRT. Step 4: For defense settlements, open a local banking account; banks require the Certificate of Incorporation, register of directors, registered agent letter, and beneficial-ownership declaration under the AML regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: A non-resident Business Corporation is exempt from national income, gross revenue, capital gains, and withholding taxes on all income sourced outside the Marshall Islands. Territorial scope: only Marshall Islands-source revenues are potentially taxable; zero effective rate on offshore manufacturing and export sales of military tactical vehicles; no comprehensive tax treaties in force.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist in the RMI tax code. Statutory options: (1) election to be treated as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under the Business Corporations Act 1990, the practical route to full tax exemption for foreign-owned export manufacturers; (2) election to register for GRT for any Marshall Islands local-source revenue; (3) project-specific tax exemption certificates granted by the Cabinet to internationally oriented export businesses. No pass-through entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in force; indirect taxation is imposed solely through customs import duties and selective excise under the Customs Ordinance 1990, plus the 3% Gross Revenue Tax on local business turnover; a VAT reform has been discussed but is not enacted.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Indirect-tax obligations arise only at customs clearance; the 3% GRT applies from the first USD 1 of Marshall Islands-source gross revenue, yet a non-resident export manufacturer with no local-source revenue has no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: with no VAT/GST, export sales of armored vehicles and offshore services are outside the indirect tax base; there is no 0% claim, refund, or input-credit mechanism. Imported components used for re-export may qualify for duty drawback under the Customs Ordinance subject to proof of physical export; defense exports are duty-neutral at the border.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading / Waybill evidencing physical departure from the RMI customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103-equivalent proof of payment
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin
  • End-User Certificate and destination export-license evidence for military tactical hardware (UN embargo screening)

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, digital stamping, or tax-authority API mandate. Customs declarations are lodged through the ASYCUDA World electronic clearance platform; export invoices follow commercial practice without state clearance gateway.

The RMI imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline (USD is legal tender and no exchange-control statute exists); the 180-day window reflects standard settlement terms for government wire and diplomatic LC payments. Fraudulent customs declarations attract forfeiture of goods, penalties up to three times the duty evaded, and criminal liability (fine and/or imprisonment) under the Customs Ordinance 1990; drawback refunds are retrospectively clawed back with interest upon false or withdrawn export claims. Defense-sector transactions face enhanced AML/CFT scrutiny, and falsified beneficial-ownership or end-user documentation can trigger corporate deregistration and custodial penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in law and practice: RMI maintains no exchange-control statute and no capital-versus-current-account distinction; the US Dollar is the domestic currency, so profit remittance and dividend repatriation by non-resident Business Corporations are free of conversion, approval, or surrender hurdles. The only practical constraints are AML/CFT verification obligations under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2001 (FTRA), US correspondent-bank policy, and enhanced due-diligence expectations for defense-sector flows, which may require certified board resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, and export-licence references.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion rules apply. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any currency and are not subject to any mandatory timeline, minimum conversion ratio, or repatriation threshold; because the manufacturer bills predominantly in USD, export proceeds are credited directly to the RMI bank account with no exchange-conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance) as the licensing and prudential regulator; no statutory central bank exists. The US Federal Reserve System serves as the de facto monetary and settlement authority because the US Dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. RMI law (Banking Act 1991) recognizes only licensed banks and has no electronic-money institution (EMI) license class, so offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully hold deposits or execute payments from an RMI nexus. An offshore EMI may be used only as an auxiliary non-RMI collection channel if it holds a credible US or EU licence, but this route creates heightened correspondent, sanctions, and trade-based AML risk for ISIC 3040 defense flows and is not recommended as a primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed as USD wire via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the parent shareholder's bank through US correspondent clearing (Fedwire/CHIPS), most efficiently via Bank of Guam's US network or BMI's New York correspondent. The credit is applied directly to the RMI operational account with zero local conversion. The wire narrative should include the entity ID, the capital-contribution purpose, and the sending bank's applicable purpose code (typically GRF for investment/equity contribution or ANN for payments on behalf of the company). The bank must receive a board resolution authorizing the injection, the share certificate or subscription ledger, the FTRA EDD pack, and a source-of-funds statement.Outbound disbursements settle through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for the Government_Direct_Wire rail to ministries and prime contractors; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (sight or term 'D'/'C' LC) for supplier payments on the Diplomatic_LC rail, issued via Bank of Guam and confirmed by a US or EU bank; and (iii) SWIFT MT700/MT707 messages for LC issuance and amendment. Every outward payment is bundled with the commercial invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, packing list, inspection certificate, and, where the counterparty is a government or diplomatic mission, a diplomatic-endorsed delivery note to mitigate trade-based financial crime risk. All MT103 traffic must carry the true transaction narrative and, where applicable, the permitted export-licence reference.Deliverable outright forwards (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, USD/AUD, USD/JPY) with internationally rated banks, executed offshore because RMI has no local forward market.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted currencies (USD/CNY, USD/KRW, USD/INR) booked through Singapore or Hong Kong trading desks.Cross-currency swaps (USD versus EUR, USD versus GBP) matched to long-term defense procurement and repayment schedules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. RMI law (Banking Act 1991) recognizes only licensed banks and has no electronic-money institution (EMI) license class, so offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully hold deposits or execute payments from an RMI nexus. An offshore EMI may be used only as an auxiliary non-RMI collection channel if it holds a credible US or EU licence, but this route creates heightened correspondent, sanctions, and trade-based AML risk for ISIC 3040 defense flows and is not recommended as a primary clearing rail.

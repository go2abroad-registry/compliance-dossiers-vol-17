Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro Atoll, MH; corporate-status filings are administered by the RMI Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Majuro, exclusively through RMI-licensed registered agents. There is no national online tax-filing portal; GRT registrations and returns are accepted in paper form through the Revenue and Taxation Office.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign-owned entity incorporates under the Business Corporations Act 1990 by filing Articles of Incorporation through an RMI-licensed registered agent and receives a Certificate of Incorporation with a corporate registration number. Step 2: The directors execute a sworn non-resident declaration and the corporation is issued a Certificate of Non-Resident Domestic Status confirming that no business will be conducted within the Republic. Step 3: Because the RMI imposes no corporate income tax, no income-tax TIN is issued; the Non-Resident Domestic Certificate is the operative tax-identity instrument. Step 4: A Gross Revenue Tax account number (the functional taxpayer ID) is obtained only if the corporation subsequently undertakes business activity inside the Republic; otherwise no GRT registration is made. Step 5: Patents and trademarks may be recorded with the RMI Registrar of Patents and Trademarks for evidentiary strengthening, but such recording is not a tax-registration requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: a Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation is categorically exempt from all RMI national taxation (no corporate income tax, no gross revenue tax, no business license tax, no withholding tax on outbound royalties or dividends) provided it derives no RMI-source revenue and conducts no business within the Republic. Territorial scope confines taxation exclusively to locally sourced activity; no RMI permanent-establishment or substance filing is required for the exempt status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or income-tax classification election exists because the Republic levies no corporate income tax. Available statutory elections: (i) Non-Resident Domestic Corporation exemption election under the Business Corporations Act, evidenced by a Registrar-issued certificate and sworn affidavit; (ii) elective small-business GRT exemptions for de minimis local revenues; and (iii) foreign-registered non-resident corporation status, which likewise avoids RMI taxation. The RMI has not enacted hybrid-mismatch, CFC, OECD BEPS, or Pillar Two election rules; no substance-based carve-out filings apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) - a sales-type gross-receipts tax under the Gross Revenue Tax Act; the RMI has no VAT, no GST, and no separate sales-and-use tax. The standard statutory GRT rate for general business is 3%; insurance, banking, utilities, and fuel carry separate schedules. Non-resident IP HoldCos with no RMI nexus bear an effective 0% rate.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold in USD: GRT registration is nexus-triggered rather than turnover-triggered. Any person engaged in a business activity within the Republic must obtain a business license and GRT account; a non-resident Domestic Corporation without RMI-source gross revenue is outside the tax base and is not permitted or required to register. De minimis exemptions apply to very small local businesses (statutory schedules around USD 5,000-10,000 of annual gross revenue), but these exemptions are irrelevant to non-resident cross-border IP licensing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The GRT applies only to gross revenue derived from business conducted in the Republic. Cross-border outbound licensing of patents and trademarks to non-RMI licensees, with royalties remitted by SWIFT wire from abroad, is excluded from the RMI tax base by the territorial sourcing rule; no 0% VAT claim, input-credit mechanism, or refund application is required. The exclusion is automatic and self-executing provided that: (i) all licensing counterparties are non-residents; (ii) no negotiation, sub-licensing, or management activities are performed from an RMI office; and (iii) the corporation maintains its current Non-Resident Domestic Certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration - required only where goods accompany a transaction; not applicable to pure intangible patent/trademark licensing.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - not applicable to cross-border IP sub-licensing; retained only where goods are the physical delivery channel.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 trace evidencing the foreign-sourced royalty inflow.
  • Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Tax Exemption Certificate together with the executed license agreement and royalty schedule as the operative substantiation package for GRT exclusion.

None. The RMI has not implemented electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API-based e-filing, or digital stamping. GRT returns are filed on paper (annual or periodic) with the Revenue and Taxation Office; no fiscal-data interface exists for wire-based royalty payments or intercompany SWIFT transfers.

The Revenue and Taxation Office may reopen assessments within the statutory window (typically three years; extended where fraud or material omission is shown) and claw back GRT wherever the exemption condition is breached, e.g., an RMI resident licensee, local contracting activity, or directors conducting board/management functions inside the Republic. Consequences include reclassification as a resident taxpayer, mandatory registration and payment of 3% GRT on all RMI-source gross revenue, monthly interest on deficiencies (approximately 1% per month or pro rata), late-filing penalties of 5% per month up to 25%, fraud and willful-neglect penalties of up to 25% of the underpayment, and cancellation of the Non-Resident Domestic Certificate. In egregious cases, uncollected tax can be recovered from directors, and the corporation may be struck from the RMI register. The RMI imposes no exchange-control fines because no statutory repatriation obligations exist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account items. The RMI maintains no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no approval regime for inbound or outbound payments, and no distinction between authorised and unauthorised dealers. Profit remittance and royalty outflows are not subject to RMI withholding tax or remittance tax. The principal practical hurdles are source-country withholding taxes on IP royalties, particularly where the MH BC lacks a double-tax-treaty or treaty-tie benefit, and the need to document economic substance to satisfy banking and tax-transparency counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or surrender export proceeds or intellectual-property license fees to a designated bank. The MH BC may retain export and royalty receipts in USD indefinitely in any bank account, and may freely disburse them for working capital, IP acquisition, dividends, management fees, or intercompany settlement without central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank: None. The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no domestic central bank and no independent exchange-control authority. Legal tender is the United States dollar. Banking and AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance under the Banking Act 1991; USD clearing is ultimately settled through the U.S. Federal Reserve correspondent system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. The RMI does not maintain a statutory fintech EMI licensing category or a Regulatory Sandbox. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not automatically recognised as banks or money transmitters under the Banking Act, and global EMIs typically classify the RMI as a high-risk correspondent jurisdiction. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary collections rail for small non-USD receipts if the EMI's terms expressly permit RMI-licensed non-resident corporations, but it cannot replace a physical bank relationship. All treasury liquidity must be swept into an RMI-licensed bank or a U.S. correspondent account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective rate for a Non-Resident Business Corporation (BC); the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Income Tax Act (Title 54, RMI Code) taxes only income sourced within the RMI, and a BC holding valid non-resident status under the Business Corporations Act (BCA) is outside the charge. A 3% gross-receipts analogue applies only to entities actually engaged in business within the RMI; the scope entity has no RMI-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends, distributions, or deemed distributions made by a non-resident BC. The BC also has no obligation to report dividend payments to the RMI Revenue and Taxation Division.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 60 days after the close of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year BC, no later than 1 March of the following year. The fiscal year end is fixed by the board on first filing and governs all subsequent filings.

Deadline: · Frequency:

No return is due by default. If the Revenue and Taxation Division issues a notice asserting RMI-source income, a written response and return must be submitted within 30 days of the notice date or by the date stated in the notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None - the RMI has not enacted transfer-pricing legislation and prescribes no Local File, Master File, or Country-by-Country Reporting thresholds. Because the BC is tax-exempt, there is no RMI taxable baseline against which an adjustment could be imposed. Practical threshold for the group: where the BC is the ultimate IP owner in a multinational chain and its parent jurisdiction applies CbC reporting, the group must document a demonstrable arm's-length royalty rate to defend against the parent jurisdiction's controlled-foreign-company (CFC) and anti-avoidance rules; there is no local-currency threshold, so the relevant thresholds are those of the foreign taxing jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: The RMI has not enacted BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations. However, two substance-related obligations apply: (1) the BCA and the Registered Agents Licensing Act require a licensed RMI registered agent, a physical registered office, and a beneficial ownership register - these constitute the minimum legal substance; and (2) the RMI's participation in the OECD global tax transparency framework (CRS and exchange of information upon request) means that foreign tax authorities will treat the RMI entity's lack of commercial substance as a red flag. The BC should demonstrably: hold legal title to registered IP; maintain board minutes and license agreements in execution form; use a bank account opened in the name of the BC; and ensure cash flows are not circular or pre-funded by the operating licensee.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham IP License

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities will disregard the BC and reassess the royalty payer under anti-avoidance rules, imposing gross-up penalties of 20%-40% and interest. Within the RMI, willful filing of false documents or knowing participation in a fraudulent scheme violates Section 5.08 of the RMI Criminal Code, punishable by a fine up to US$50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate sham licensing are jointly and severally liable for the foreign tax restitution; they may face civil asset forfeiture, travel bans issued by foreign courts in MLA proceedings, director-disqualification orders, and extradition requests predicated on tax evasion or wire fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Business Corporations Act, the Registrar may strike off the entity for non-filing or for failure to maintain a registered agent, resulting in automatic dissolution and forfeiture of all statutory powers. The entity then loses its zero-tax status for practical purposes and cannot rely on RMI law to enforce contracts; foreign CFC and substance rules will tax the deemed royalty income in the parent's jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade after strike-off are personally liable for all debts and obligations incurred; the corporate veil is pierced, exposing directors to civil restitution by creditors and by foreign revenue authorities for the full amount of diverted profits.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) Act, the entity is subject to fines up to US$250,000, freezing orders, and revocation of the registered agent's license; the Registrar may also strike off the entity for regulatory non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML officer face personal financial penalties, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years where the royalty network is used to launder proceeds of foreign tax evasion or corruption; personal assets may be confiscated under the Proceeds of Crime framework.
False Annual Declaration / Fraud on the Registrar

Statutory sanction: A fine of US$10,000 to US$25,000 per false filing, forfeiture of the limited-liability shield, and administrative removal from the Register; the Registrar may also refer the matter for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false declaration are personally liable for civil restitution of any unpaid RMI taxes (3% gross receipts plus penalties) and for third-party losses; imprisonment up to 3 years applies for false statutory declarations.
Transfer-Pricing Avoidance / Conduit Financing Abuse

Statutory sanction: The RMI itself imposes no TP adjustment, but the affected foreign jurisdiction will recharacterize the payments as distributions, impose withholding tax on the grossed-up amount, and apply penalties of 20%-40% plus interest; the BC's bank may terminate correspondent relationships for unusual circular flows.

Director personal liability: Foreign tax authorities may pursue directors personally for penalties where the director approved the pricing and knew the arrangement lacked commercial substance; this can include civil restitution of avoided taxes and travel bans pending resolution of the tax dispute.

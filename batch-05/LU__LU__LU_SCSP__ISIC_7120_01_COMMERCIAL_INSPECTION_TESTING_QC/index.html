Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; consolidated taxpayer portal: MyGuichet / eCDF e-filing system (guichet.lu)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity intending to make taxable supplies in Luxembourg must obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (TVA number) from the AEDT before commencing activities. Non-established taxable persons are subject to mandatory registration from the first euro of taxable turnover, with no exemption threshold. Procedure: file the VAT registration form (Formulaire 1 – Immatriculation à la TVA) via MyGuichet or directly with the VAT Office together with constitutional documents (partnership agreement, certificate of incorporation of any corporate partner), proof of foreign tax identification for each partner, and the identity of the designated Luxembourg VAT representative where census or fiscal representation is required. For an SCSp, no Luxembourg corporate income tax registration is required at entity level because the partnership is fiscally transparent; the SCSp itself is registered for VAT and for employer-withholding obligations if it employs local staff. The direct tax obligations arise in the hands of the partners according to their own residence and permanent establishment status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The SCSp is fiscally transparent by statutory default for Luxembourg income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes; profits are allocated to and taxed at partner level. No entity-level election to be treated as an opaque corporation is available for an SCSp. However, the partnership agreement determines the allocation of profit and loss for Luxembourg tax purposes, and partners may elect the applicable territorial allocation of business income. Anti-hybrid rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) must be assessed where the SCSp is treated as a taxable entity in the partner's jurisdiction, potentially triggering disallowance of payments or recharacterization of hybrid mismatches; no unrelated-party hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 in annual taxable turnover (VAT-exclusive) for taxable persons established in Luxembourg; EUR 0 for foreign/non-established entities, which must register from the first euro of taxable supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT (0% with full right to deduct input VAT) applies to: (i) exports of goods dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU in accordance with Article 42 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (ii) intra-Community supplies to taxable persons in another EU Member State holding a valid EU VAT identification number, documented in the quarterly recapitulative statement (RC-list); and (iii) supplies of services for which the place of supply is outside Luxembourg, including on-site pre-shipment inspection and quality-control services physically performed at a factory located in a non-EU country. To claim zero-rating, the exporter must hold robust export evidence demonstrating that the goods left the EU within 90 days (general administrative guidance), and the invoice issued at 0% must reference the statutory exemption basis and, for intra-EU movements, the customer's EU VAT ID.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EXS electronic declaration) accepted by customs authorities and annotated "Exportation" (or the approved export certificate/accusé de dépôt)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport document proving that the goods physically left the EU
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract/order matching in description, quantity, and value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT/wire-transfer banking credit advice evidencing receipt of the full foreign sales proceeds

No general mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and no real-time fiscal clearance, government tax-API, or digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg as of 2025. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies solely to supplies to public-sector contracting authorities via the Peppol BIS 3.0 network under the B2G legal framework. B2B e-invoicing remains optional, subject to authenticity-of-origin, integrity-of-content, and readability requirements. All VAT returns must be filed electronically through the eCDF/MyGuichet portal; VAT invoices issued for export and zero-rating claims must preserve traceable metadata suitable for electronic audit by the AEDT.

The AEDT may reassess unjustified zero-rated export and refund claims within the standard five-year limitation period, extended up to ten years in cases of fraud or significant irregularities. Retrospective clawback is triggered where the export evidence is incomplete, the goods are not demonstrably dispatched within the required timeframe, or the FX proceeds are not received within the 180-day window or from the contractual counterparty. Remedies include VAT correction, statutory late interest accruing monthly, administrative fines of up to 200% of the evaded VAT, criminal fines between EUR 2,500 and EUR 50,000, and, for fraud, imprisonment of the responsible officers. Documentary misdemeanors carry separate civil sanctions, and refund offsets may be suspended pending full substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member with full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-65 TFEU. Profits, dividends, loan repayments, partnership distributions and capital proceeds may be freely repatriated without licence, approval, or surrender requirement. The only practical frictions are standard anti-money laundering documentation and counterparty due diligence. No exchange-control visa or goods-related verification process applies. For an SCSp that is tax-transparent, remittances to non-resident partners are treated as partner-level distributions and remain unrestricted; however, bank-side controls may require a withholding-tax declaration if the SCSp has opted to be treated as opaque for certain tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligations exist. Export and trade service receipts may be retained in any currency in a Luxembourg-resident account or converted at market rates. The BCL/ECB require statistical reporting under the Balance of Payments (BoP) and International Trade in Services regimes for reporting banks, but no surrender timeline applies. Cross-border payment identification via SWIFT/ISO 20022 is required for statistical and AML purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and financial supervision; foreign exchange administration is effectively delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Economic and Monetary Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for operational payment routing and low-value disbursements (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex or other PSD2 electronic money institutions) but Low for capital formation, escrow, and principal relationship banking. Luxembourg law treats EMIs as payment service providers; they may issue IBANs and execute remittances, but cannot grant credit, take deposits, or form the compliance backbone of an SCSp treasury. Use as a secondary clearing or capex-light rail only after confirming the EMI's authorisation in Luxembourg and its access to SWIFT gpi or SEPA Instant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 (for EUR: SEPA Credit Transfer or ISO 20022 pacs.008 via TARGET2) for each capital subscription into the SCSp capital escrow account. Field 70(Remittance Information) must reference the purpose code EQUI for equity injection or INVS for a general investment/capital-account credit; for partner or shareholder loans, use DBTN or INTE as appropriate. The partner's own bank must screen and tag the beneficiary SCSp; the receiving Luxembourg bank will verify the UBO, source-of-funds pack, and partnership deed before posting. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg City effective rate (17% national corporate income tax plus 7% solidarity surcharge plus 6.75% average municipal business tax; municipal rates vary by commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at SCSp level on profit distributions to partners because the SCSp is tax-transparent and such distributions are not classified as Luxembourg dividends. By contrast, dividends paid by a fully taxable Luxembourg company are subject to statutory Luxembourg withholding tax at 15%, which may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to 5% under most DTAs for qualifying shareholdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the end of the fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers the return for a given tax year is due on 31 May of the following year. A reasoned extension may be requested; the tax administration frequently grants an automatic filing window through 31 December of the following calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million group consolidated revenue is the threshold triggering Luxembourg Local File and Master File requirements, with related-party transactions in aggregate above EUR 1 million generally expected to be documented. Groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million are required to prepare Country-by-Country reporting and file the CbC declaration with the Luxembourg tax authorities.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a sectoral Economic Substance Regulation like the UAE, but general tax law, the Luxembourg GAAR, ATAD, and tax-treaty case law require real economic substance. An SCSp engaged in commercial inspection and testing must evidence physical office space, qualified inspectors, equipment, client contracts, decision-making, and bank accounts. Merely passive receipt of testing fees through a shell structure is vulnerable to substance audits, treaty-shopping denial, and reclassification of the entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax fraud penalties of up to EUR 250,000 fine and up to 5 years imprisonment, plus a tax increase of up to 10% on understated tax, late payment interest, and rescission of favourable tax rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner of the SCSp may be criminally prosecuted if they knowingly sign or cause false returns; civil joint and several liability for the tax debt may be imposed; travel restrictions and bank account freezes are possible in serious cases.
Shell Company / Abuse of Tax Transparency

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the SCSp as taxable, denial of treaty benefits, denial of pass-through treatment, reassessment of profits at the 24.94% combined CIT rate, and penalties for wilful understatement.

Director personal liability: General partners and corporate directors may be personally liable for taxes due if the court pierces the partnership veil; exclusion from serving as manager is possible for culpable conduct.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties under Luxembourg AML law that can reach several hundred thousand euros up to EUR 1 million or more for serious breaches, suspension of business authorisation, and criminal money-laundering liability up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers face individual administrative fines; senior officers can be banned from management and criminally prosecuted if money laundering is knowingly aided.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax reallocation of profit to Luxembourg, additional tax at 24.94%, late interest, and potentially a 10% penalty on the additional assessment for missing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations may face individual administrative fines; personal civil liability may attach if the transaction is found to be a sham.
Statutory Licensing and Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: Operating without licence attracts fines, closure orders, and disqualification from public accreditation; invoice fraud triggers additional tax penalties and criminal prosecution for forgery/fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be personally liable for fines, restitution to clients, and criminal punishment including imprisonment for fraudulent commercial practices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods qualify for the mandatory 0% rate where dispatch or transport begins in Luxembourg and terminates outside the EU (Article 51 of the Luxembourg VAT Law); supplies of digital stock-photo licensing to non-EU business customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rules (Article 17 VAT Law for B2B and Article 18 for B2C digital services); payment-processing via Stripe or PayPal does not alter the place-of-supply test; documentary proof of export or of the customer's foreign establishment must be retained.

  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch outside the EU
  • Proof of payment (bank transfer or inward remittance certificate)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime in Luxembourg; mandatory B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL (PEPPOL BIS 3.0/UBL 2.1 or CII) since October 2016; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and is expected to become mandatory only under the EU ViDA framework (target horizon 2030-2035).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility by virtue of Eurozone membership and Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). There are no exchange controls, no prior-authorisation requirements, no quantitative limits, and no administrative hurdles on the repatriation of profits, dividends, royalties, interest, or capital proceeds for residents or non-residents. A 100% foreign-owned special limited partnership (SCSp) may freely receive equity contributions and remit royalty, licence, and commission income to any jurisdiction, subject only to: (i) Luxembourg withholding-tax law and applicable double-tax treaties; (ii) DAC6/ATAD transparency rules; and (iii) the bank's internal AML/CFT due-diligence checks. The practical friction points are compliance documentation and tax substance, not convertibility law. The ECB's standing foreign-exchange regulations permit the euro to be freely traded and hedged, including forwards, swaps, and options with EU-authorised counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, no cadenced repatriation deadline, and no obligation to centralise foreign-exchange revenue with the BCL or any Luxembourg bank. Receipts from the export of digital stock-photo licences and generative visual-asset royalties, whether collected in USD, GBP, EUR, or other currencies, may be retained in foreign currency in Luxembourg accounts or converted at the SCSp's discretion. The only regulatory incidentals are: (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting, which is normally intermediated by the executing credit institution and uses purpose codes for each cross-border flow; and (ii) EU Regulation 2021/1230 on cross-border payments, which requires equal EUR/non-EUR pricing and full transparency on conversion charges when the SCSp receives remittances via SEPA or equivalent systems. Professional practice is to record each inbound royalty wire with the appropriate BCL purpose code and to maintain the settlement statements of the payment facilitator for the bank's periodic review.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the Luxembourg monetary authority and integrated Eurosystem member. Prudential and AML/CFT supervision of all banks, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Luxembourg Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, and CSSF Circular 20/725. The BCL is the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistical reporting and systemic payment-systems oversight; together they form the dual control regime governing cross-border banking, FX, and clearing in Luxembourg.

Compliant protocol: the foreign investor originates a SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the SCSp's operational account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank, using the SCSp's IBAN and its LEI code in field 72. For balance-of-payments statistical coding, the bank records the flow as a direct-investment equity transaction in the BCL external-statistics classification; the investor's bank may additionally require the Luxembourg LPA, the certificate of registration, and a funds-identification letter. If the investment is funded via a US entity, the corridor wire from the US correspondent bank to the Luxembourg BIC must carry a full legal name of the SCSp and the reference 'SCSP_LU SCI IP Contribution — no contravention of OFAC'. Private-equity style capital calls should be pre-announced to the Luxembourg bank to avoid a false positive in automatic transaction monitoring. The bank will also request a signed 'Declaration of Source of Funds' and confirmation that the contribution is not a repayable loan; if the contribution is temporarily parked in a capital-escrow account, the LPA and the sponsor's instruction letter must explicitly authorise the use of that account before treasury deployment.Primary clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro payments within the EU — used for contributor royalty payouts, cloud-provider invoices, and administrative fees; SEPA Instant is recommended for low-value monthly content-contributor settlements; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT GPI tracking for non-EU disbursements (US contributors, APAC distribution partners, licensing agents) — each MT103 must include the payment purpose code (e.g., 'Royalties and licence fees' under the BCL statistical nomenclature), the underlying invoice reference, and any required withholding-tax certification; (iii) commercial letters of credit and standby letters of credit (MT700/MT760) are not material to stock-photo royalty flows and should be reserved for large content-library or artwork acquisitions, where a confirmed irrevocable LC at a Luxembourg bank (or a standby LC guaranteeing the performance of the SCSp's licensor) provides settlement assurance; and (iv) card-network settlement rails (Visa/Mastercard) via Stripe and PayPal, which settle funds in bulk to the Luxembourg account after acquiring and clearing cycles. For recurring USD settlement, the SCSp should establish a multi-currency account with automatic FX-sweep instructions so that USD collector balances convert to EUR at a pre-agreed daily rate; the bank sweeps balances via an internal FX conversion without a separate SWIFT wire, reducing costs and audit fatigue.Outright FX Forwards (deliverable) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with tenors up to 12 months under a standard international FX Master Agreement or ISDA 2002 Master Agreement — the primary instrument to lock conversion on expected quarterly USD/GBP settlement flows from stock-photo and licensing platformsFX Swaps (spot-forward combination) for liquidity bridging when the SCSp holds USD inflows but must pay EUR-denominated AI-compute and contributor costsCross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD) for any material acquisition debt or significant treasury-exposure periods exceeding 12 months, requiring full ISDA/CSA documentation and bank credit approvalEuropean-style FX Options (vanilla calls and puts) on EUR/USD, purchased (not written) to protect royalty-convertibility budgets while preserving upside in a strengthening EURNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are permitted in Luxembourg for exotic or illiquid currency pairs (e.g., certain APAC and EMEA currencies) if the counterparty is an EU-authorised credit institution or a licensed broker-dealer, but NDFs are not necessary or recommended for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, which are fully deliverable in the liquid euro FX market

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational collection, LOW for treasury and capital-escrow functions. Luxembourg recognises payment institutions and electronic money institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 (transposing PSD2) and the CSSF regulatory perimeter. Viable local EMIs/PSPs for a stock-photo marketplace include Stripe (Stripe Payments Luxembourg S.A., CSSF-authorised as a payment institution), PayPal Luxembourg S.à r.l. (electronic money institution), and Mangopay S.A. (Luxembourg-incorporated EMI specialised in marketplace payouts). EU-passported EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise, or Revolut may operate in Luxembourg under freedom-of-services notification to the CSSF, but they cannot be used as the sole depository of the SCSp's capital, because the legal architecture of an SCSp capital account requires a credit institution for depositary/escrow safety and for the BCL statistical chain. The correct treasury posture is: EMIs/PSPs are used for merchant-side collection and sub-merchant payouts; a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank is the exclusive holder of the operating treasury and escrow balances.

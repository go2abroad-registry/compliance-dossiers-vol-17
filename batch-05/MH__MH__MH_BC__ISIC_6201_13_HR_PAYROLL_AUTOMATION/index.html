Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Administration (RICA), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; corporate registry administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General. Taxpayer access through RICA services and RMI-licensed registered agents; customs declarations processed via the ASYCUDA World portal.

E-government filing portal

No compulsory RMI tax identification number or VAT ID is issued to a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC). Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporation Act 1990 through an RMI-licensed registered agent; (2) elect NRDC status in the Articles of Incorporation (BCA section 106); (3) no RMI TIN/VAT ID applies because RMI has no VAT and NRDC status places the entity outside the income and gross-revenue tax base; (4) if the entity later acquires RMI nexus (RMI premises, employees, or RMI-source customers), it must apply to RICA for a Business License and Business Taxpayer Number and register as an employer with the RMI Social Security Administration; (5) foreign vendors without RMI nexus have no registration obligation.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — No compulsory RMI tax identification number or VAT ID is issued to a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC). Procedure: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporation Act 1990 through an RMI-licensed registered agent; (2) elect NRDC status in the Articles of Incorporation (BCA section 106); (3) no RMI TIN/VAT ID applies because RMI has no VAT and NRDC status places the entity outside the income and gross-revenue tax base; (4) if the entity later acquires RMI nexus (RMI premises, employees, or RMI-source customers), it must apply to RICA for a Business License and Business Taxpayer Number and register as an employer with the RMI Social Security Administration; (5) foreign vendors without RMI nexus have no registration obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The only material statutory election is the NRDC designation in the Articles of Incorporation; the designation is revocable only by charter amendment, and NRDC status is lost if the corporation carries on business within the RMI, becoming subject to Business Gross Revenue Tax and municipal business licensing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales and use tax exists in the Marshall Islands. Indirect and turnover levies comprise: (i) customs import duty under the Customs Act 1990; (ii) Business Gross Revenue Tax (BGRT) at tiered rates up to 3% of gross revenue derived from business conducted within the RMI; and (iii) municipal business license fees.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT regime exists and therefore no VAT registration threshold applies. For BGRT, any person carrying on business in the RMI must obtain a business license before operations commence; there is no de minimis turnover exemption for a foreign digital payroll service provider with physical, employment, or other economic nexus in the RMI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - RMI has no VAT or GST, therefore no 0% export VAT rate and no input-VAT refund mechanism exists. Exports of goods are free of export duty; offshore SaaS payroll automation services supplied by an NRDC to non-resident clients fall outside the BGRT base provided the corporation has no RMI nexus. No zero-rating certificate is issued or required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods physically exported from RMI)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance or proof of payment (only where a licensing condition requires; RMI has no general foreign-exchange control)

None. RMI does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping. Customs import/export declarations are filed through ASYCUDA World; BGRT returns and business license renewals are processed on an annual cycle by RICA in paper or basic electronic formats.

No export-VAT clawback exists because no export zero-rating regime exists. False customs declarations attract penalties under the Customs Act 1990, including fines, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment. BGRT non-compliance triggers a 10% late-payment penalty, monthly interest accrual, business license suspension, and retrospective assessment; an NRDC that transacts business inside the RMI loses its tax-exempt status and may be assessed for all open periods within the statutory limitation window.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI maintains no exchange-control legislation. Both current-account receipts and capital-account transactions are fully convertible and freely transferable in U.S. dollars. There are no statutory restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident business corporations, provided corporate records evidence a valid distribution declaration and continued tax-exempt status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

RMI imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion timeline, or forced repatriation ratio for export proceeds or any other foreign-currency receipts. A non-resident business corporation may retain, pool, or disburse received funds freely. Banks in the U.S. clearing system may require invoice, bill-of-lading, or contract documentation for source-of-funds compliance, but no statutory surrender timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: There is no domestic central bank in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The U.S. Federal Reserve System acts as the de facto monetary authority for the U.S. dollar, which is the sole legal tender. The competent financial supervisory authority is the RMI Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance & Banking, which licenses banks and monitors foreign exchange and AML compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints: RMI does not issue fintech EMI or money transmitter licences; digital EMI providers are generally licensed in other jurisdictions (e.g., UK FCA, US OCC, EU/EEA) and may classify an RMI non-resident business corporation as high-risk due to offshore tax-exempt status, potential FATF/EU enhanced monitoring history, and nominee ownership possibilities. Many EMIs will require a genuine U.S. operating nexus, a U.S. physical address, and an EIN, which conflicts with the pure non-resident BC structure. A licensed RMI local bank or a U.S.-based EMI with a transparent BO registry and real office presence is the preferred workaround.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 — RMI imposes no transfer pricing documentation threshold because the non-resident business corporation is fully tax-exempt; however, any intercompany cross-border arrangement should still maintain contemporaneous arm's length evidence for substance verification and regulatory review.

Economic substance scope: Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to relevant entities that are RMI tax-resident and undertake relevant activities (e.g., credit business, financing and leasing, shipping, holding company as pure equity holding, headquarters, distribution and service centre). A non-resident BC operating a global payroll SaaS platform with central management and control outside RMI is ordinarily outside ESR scope. If any RMI-sourced service is undertaken, the entity must satisfy the mind-and-management test, maintain adequate premises and staff in RMI, and file an economic substance declaration annually.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment of RMI corporate income tax at 30% on taxable RMI-source income, plus interest of 1% per month and a civil penalty of up to 50% of the tax understated; registration and annual filing privileges may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false statement may be personally liable for the unpaid tax penalty where they knowingly suppressed revenue or false-declared non-residence; a criminal conviction carries a fine of up to USD 10,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 5 years.
Shell Company / Sham Entity

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act and ESR, the Registrar may strike the company from the register, order its dissolution, and refer the facts to the Attorney-General; equity courts may pierce the corporate veil, rendering the corporation and its directors liable for third-party debts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading and are subject to a director disqualification order of up to 10 years. If cross-border fraud is alleged, directors may face extradition to the RMI and an asset freeze in foreign jurisdictions.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: The RMI Crimes Act and AML/CFT framework impose monetary penalties of up to USD 1 million per violation on the entity; the financial regulator may ban the entity from receiving remittances and revoke the corporate registration.

Director personal liability: Individual directors who knowingly authorise, direct, or fail to prevent prohibited transfers face imprisonment of up to 12 years, personal seizure of the funds concerned, and a travel ban until the investigation is completed. Voluntary self-disclosure to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit materially reduces criminal exposure.

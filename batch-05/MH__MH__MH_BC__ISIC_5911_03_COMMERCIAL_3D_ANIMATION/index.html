Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Registrar of Corporations, Business Registration Office (Ministry of Commerce and Trade); Tax and Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance. Corporate filings are lodged electronically through licensed Registered Agents using the RMI Online Corporate Registry portal; business licenses are filed via the Business License Division, Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned non-resident Business Corporation procures its legal identity by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations through a licensed registered agent. No income-tax TIN is issuable for foreign-source activity; the corporation obtains (i) a Certificate of Incorporation with an RMI registration number, (ii) an annual franchise tax receipt and Annual Report filing acknowledgement, and (iii) a Business License only if it conducts physical or local activity in the Marshall Islands. Non-resident domestic corporations deriving all income from outside the RMI are not required to obtain an income-tax file number or to file RMI income tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation exempt from RMI gross income tax on offshore revenue; taxation limited to a fixed annual franchise tax (based on authorized share capital) plus the Annual Report fee.

Available election forms

None applicable. RMI law contains no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Entity classification follows statutory form: a Business Corporation is per se a taxable legal person only to the extent it derives RMI-source income; a non-resident company may voluntarily opt into resident tax treatment by carrying on business in the RMI (subjecting only RMI-source income to tax). The absence of an income tax treaty network eliminates treaty-based hybrid elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (the RMI levies no general consumption tax; customs import duties are the sole indirect levy)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists; a Business License (annual fee) is required only for entities with physical RMI business premises or local sales, and no turnover test applies. A non-resident 3D animation service exporter has no indirect-tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable by statute: the RMI imposes no VAT/GST, and non-resident domestic corporations are outside RMI gross income tax on offshore service revenue. To definitively establish export treatment, the corporation must evidence that the animation/VFX services are performed and consumed wholly outside the RMI, the counterparty is a non-resident, and consideration is settled in foreign currency into a non-RMI account; no zero-rating claim or refund application is necessary.

  • Executed Service Agreement and Statement of Work (defining offshore performance and delivery of 3D animation/VFX assets)
  • Commercial Invoice and Delivery Note (scoped to services rendered entirely outside the RMI)
  • SWIFT MT103 / Wise B2B Inward Remittance Certificate (evidence of foreign-currency settlement)
  • RMI Annual Franchise Tax Receipt and Annual Report Filing Acknowledgment (demonstrating good standing)

None. The RMI does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based reporting. Invoices are governed by general contract law and may be issued in any commercial format. Electronic customs declarations are used exclusively for physical merchandise through the RMI Customs Automated Manifest and Declaration system.

There is no VAT-export clawback mechanism in the RMI because no VAT exists. However, fraudulent certifications to the Registrar of Corporations (false non-resident declarations or facade incorporation) are punishable under the Business Corporations Act, including fines up to US$500 per violation and possible involuntary dissolution; operation of a business without a required local license attracts administrative penalties and license revocation. Internationally, if a foreign tax authority (e.g., US IRS CFC/Subpart F, EU ATAD or OECD Pillar Two) determines the RMI entity lacks economic substance, the tax exemption may be disregarded and offshore revenue clawed back in the shareholder's residence jurisdiction. Records should be retained for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI uses the United States dollar (USD) as its official legal tender. Pursuant to the Compact of Free Association and the RMI Banking Act 1987, the BMI does not maintain restrictive exchange controls on current account transactions, capital account transactions, or profit remittances. Foreign-owned non-resident business corporations may remit dividends, royalties, and service fees abroad without prior BMI exchange control approval, provided remittance documentation satisfies the anti-money laundering (AML) and countering the financing of terrorism (CFT) requirements imposed under the RMI AML/CFT framework (as amended by the Banking (Amendment) Act 2013 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Regulations 2014). The RMI currency board system ensures complete USD convertibility at parity, with no surrender, rationing, or split-rate mechanisms affecting current or capital account flows. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to correspondent bank due diligence, beneficiary bank sanctions screening, and compliance with the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) sanctions regimes, given the dollar-clearing nexus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in the RMI. Since the USD is the domestic currency and the RMI maintains a quasi-currency board arrangement, commercial banks do not compel exporters or service providers to convert export receipts into local currency or to maintain any percentage of proceeds onshore. For the 3D animation and VFX services export scope under ISIC 5911, a non-resident business corporation may retain 100% of export proceeds in offshore or domestic USD accounts and may freely repatriate funds. The applicable retention requirement is solely documentary: retention of the underlying commercial contract, invoice, and proof of service delivery for a minimum of five years to satisfy corporate record-keeping obligations under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act, and production of this dossier to its RMI licensed bank or international correspondent bank upon request for AML/CFT source-of-funds verification.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Marshall Islands (BMI), acting as the central bank and monetary authority for the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability with strict legal constraints. Non-resident digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Mercury are not licensed passported entities in the RMI. They may onboard the MH_BC entity as a client only if they accept foreign-incorporated business corporations and their payment services operate through licensed partner banks with corresponding USD clearing in the U.S. federal wire system. The RMI Banking Act requires any entity conducting a 'banking business' in or from the RMI to hold a BMI license; however, an offshore EMI acting solely as a payment processor without maintaining a physical presence, soliciting deposits from RMI residents, or taking deposits in the RMI does not require RMI licensure and may operate as a non-resident service provider. Legal constraint: If the EMI holds client funds in a pooled omnibus account hosted by a U.S. or third-country bank, the MH_BC must ensure the EMI provides segregated sub-account identity protection, because the omnibus structure may undermine the MH_BC's ownership rights to funds and its ability to meet OFAC verification requests. Any EMI using the RMI jurisdiction code 'MH' is permitted, but if the EMI is, in turn, a U.S. Money Services Business (MSB), a U.S. state money transmitter license, must be verified. Wise and Airwallex currently support USD USD-denominated payouts to RMI entities with SWIFT routing via major U.S. correspondents; use is commercially recommended as a secondary clearing rail, but domestic bank onboarding remains the primary compliance anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Marshall Islands non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) engaged in commercial 3D animation production, inbound capital can be routed through SWIFT MT103 or through online payment rails (Wise, Airwallex) subject to proper source-of-funds documentation. Recommended structure: (1) equity/founder contribution: founder's personal or corporate US-based account sends a SWIFT MT103 wire to the MH_BC account at the Bank of Guam (Majuro) or Bank of Marshall Islands using SWIFT/BIC code 'BOMHMHMA' (if applicable) with purpose code 'T0201' (capital contribution) or 'G110' (other capital input) under ISO 20022; (2) project financing / client advance: client's corporate account wires via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'P0106' (services) and invoice reference; (3) production advance through Wise: fund laundering risk is mitigated by requiring the originator to include the actual client name and contract/purchase order number, and the RMI entity must produce the Master Service Agreement and the production budget. The Bank of Guam may treat wire transfers from 'higher-risk non-bank financial institutions' with enhanced scrutiny; therefore, the recommended rail is a first wire from the controlling owner's already-vetted commercial bank into the RMI account, followed by the EMI/neobank services after the relationship is established. The entity must verify the final beneficiary field in every wire matches the MH_BC legal name as recorded by the RMI Corporate Registry; any mismatch is a death-blow for clearing and will be returned after a 24-48 hour hold.Primary outbound clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for large supplier payments (CGI render farm farms, licensed 3D software procurement, payroll to overseas artists); (2) Commercial letters of credit are not needed for this service industry, but they may be used for pre-paid distribution licences, with MT700 between two commercial banks; (3) Wise Business multi-currency account ('Wise_B2B') for small-value disbursements under USD 25,000, using its local USD and EUR rails to pay overseas contractors; (4) SWIFT MT101/MT103 to a US correspondent, using the book transfer through Bank of Guam to settle USD in Fedwire or through CHIPS. Compliance requirement: every outbound disbursement must be supported by a pro-forma invoice and an internal treasury memo. Synchronise with the FX plan: all supplier contracts shall be denominated in USD; for non-USD payables (EUR, GBP, JPY), the RMI entity will execute a spot FX transaction via its bank or via Wise at point of disbursement, taking a clear 'benchmark price' screenshot for transfer pricing documentation. For amounts above USD 150,000, the bank will apply enhanced due diligence, requiring completed OFAC check on the payee and an invoice with an exact service description. Periodic shareholder distributions should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'T0110' (dividends) and underlying board resolution.No RMI exchange control restrictions apply to hedging; the RMI uses USD so the MH_BC bears no USD/MH FX risk. However, the entity has material EUR/GBP/JPY exposure from international VFX clients. Permissible instruments: 1) vanilla FX forwards up to 12 months with a licensed U.S. or Asian bank (Bank of Guam, ANZ, Standard Chartered) under a standard ISDA 1998 FX Master Agreement, only for forecast costs and receivables in the same currency; 2) non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible under the USD-displaced currency regime only if the underlying currency is the currency of client billing (EUR, GBP, JPY) and the counter bank carries an RMI-licensed or home-country AML/CFT regime; 3) cross-currency swaps with a maturity up to 24 months for funding of the production company's asset financing (e.g., render hardware leases), documented by an ISDA Schedule and CSA.Instruments are not permissible: leveraged products, exotic/touch options, binary options, and any NDF linked to USD because USD is legal tender. Any hedging transaction must be excluded from speculative intent by a board resolution and documented by an internal treasury policy that ties notional values to forecast cash flows.For the RMI entity, FX hedging shall be executed by a treasury service provider (e.g., Bank of Guam or a regulated U.S. futures commission merchant) that is not an RMI bank but that is permitted under the U.S. Commodity Exchange Act; the BMI has no restrictive licensing for non-resident derivative counterparties.Currency swap accounting will follow ASC 815 / IFRS 9; the treasury management system or Excel log will tag hedge designations and valuations monthly.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with strict legal constraints. Non-resident digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, and Mercury are not licensed passported entities in the RMI. They may onboard the MH_BC entity as a client only if they accept foreign-incorporated business corporations and their payment services operate through licensed partner banks with corresponding USD clearing in the U.S. federal wire system. The RMI Banking Act requires any entity conducting a 'banking business' in or from the RMI to hold a BMI license; however, an offshore EMI acting solely as a payment processor without maintaining a physical presence, soliciting deposits from RMI residents, or taking deposits in the RMI does not require RMI licensure and may operate as a non-resident service provider. Legal constraint: If the EMI holds client funds in a pooled omnibus account hosted by a U.S. or third-country bank, the MH_BC must ensure the EMI provides segregated sub-account identity protection, because the omnibus structure may undermine the MH_BC's ownership rights to funds and its ability to meet OFAC verification requests. Any EMI using the RMI jurisdiction code 'MH' is permitted, but if the EMI is, in turn, a U.S. Money Services Business (MSB), a U.S. state money transmitter license, must be verified. Wise and Airwallex currently support USD USD-denominated payouts to RMI entities with SWIFT routing via major U.S. correspondents; use is commercially recommended as a secondary clearing rail, but domestic bank onboarding remains the primary compliance anchor.

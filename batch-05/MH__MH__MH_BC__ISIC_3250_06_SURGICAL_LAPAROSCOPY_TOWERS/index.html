Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Tax Administration (RTA), Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). Principal office: Delap, Majuro, with an Ebeye branch; TIN issuance, income tax, gross revenue tax, and customs/export clearance are consolidated within the RTA. Official portal: the RTA 'Tax Services' gateway on the RMI Ministry of Finance web portal; no fully automated self-service TIN/VAT portal is available for non-resident exempt entities, which file through registered agents.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Business Corporation (BC) under the Business Corporations Act 1990 through an RMI-licensed registered agent; the RMI Registrar of Corporations issues the Certificate of Incorporation, whose registration number becomes the entity's master identifier. Step 2 - If the BC carries on physical business in the RMI, file the admission/registration with the Registrar of Foreign Corporations and obtain a Business License under the Business License Act 2005 (fee scales tied to gross revenue bands). Step 3 - Apply to the RTA for a 9-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) on the entity registration form if the BC derives RMI-source income, employs staff in the RMI, or needs a customs/tax account for goods movements. Step 4 - No VAT or GST ID exists to procure because no VAT/GST system has been enacted; the Certificate of Incorporation and, where applicable, the Business License serve as operative tax identity for banking and export purposes. Step 5 - Complete RTA Customs exporter/consignor registration at the port of export to obtain the exporter code referenced on each Customs Export Declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification election exists in RMI law. Available statutory election and status mechanisms: (1) Non-Resident Designation - the articles of incorporation may declare the BC to be a non-resident corporation, automatically limiting tax exposure to RMI-source activity under the Income Tax Act 1989; (2) Exempt International Entity Election - a certified application to the RTA/Ministry of Finance for statutory tax-exempt status for qualifying international entities (e.g., international shipping under the RMI Maritime Act and international banking under the International Banking Act); (3) Entity-Form Election - promoters may choose to constitute the vehicle as a BC, LLC, limited partnership, or limited duration company, each producing a different foreign tax classification profile; (4) Hybrid Mismatch - the RMI has not enacted the OECD BEPS Action 2 domestic hybrid mismatch rules, so no hybrid instrument, hybrid entity, or branch mismatch elections are available; taxpayers relying on foreign entity classification must self-assess under the general territorial sourcing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Hybrid indirect tax regime without VAT/GST: (i) Customs Import Duty under the RMI Tariff Act and Customs Regulations (ad valorem tariff schedule) and (ii) Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) under the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1989 at 3% of RMI-source gross revenue. Export sales of manufactured goods to non-resident buyers are excluded from gross revenue and are de facto zero-rated; no VAT input-credit or refund chain exists, and import-duty relief on inputs incorporated into exported goods is handled through customs inward-processing/duty-remission rules.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Official currency: United States dollar (USD), the legal currency of the RMI. GRT and Business License registration: a business carrying on RMI-source activity must register once projected or actual annual gross revenue reaches the de minimis threshold of USD 10,000; below that level a token license/filing obligation applies. A non-resident BC with zero RMI-source revenue is beneath any threshold. No VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST law has been enacted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Eligibility criteria for de facto zero-rating of exports: (1) the goods (ultra-HD 4K minimally invasive rigid endoscopes and laparoscopy tower components manufactured under ISIC 3250) must be sold under a contract with a buyer located outside the RMI, with title and risk passing outside RMI territory; (2) the sale proceeds must be received in USD or another foreign currency from non-resident sources, with no RMI-source revenue element; (3) if components are imported into the RMI and re-exported after incorporation into finished towers, the importer must claim inward-processing/duty-remission treatment at importation and maintain a factory movement record; (4) services performed in the RMI for non-resident customers qualify for GRT exclusion only where the benefit is entirely realized offshore; (5) the BC must maintain its non-resident status declaration and, if audited, evidence the absence of an RMI permanent establishment through board-meeting and management-location records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs export form referencing the exporter/consignor registration code)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List (serially numbered and consistent with physical inventory records)
  • Bill of Lading or House/Neutral Air Waybill evidencing offshore destination and transfer of title
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment advice confirming receipt of the export proceeds within the repatriation deadline
  • Certified Certificate of Incorporation and non-resident status declaration (with Business License and GRT exclusion endorsement, if held)
  • Certificate of Origin or customs inward-processing/duty-remission paperwork where free-zone or re-export relief is claimed

No national electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate applies: no e-invoicing standard, no SAF-T/PEPPOL/XML invoicing format, no digital tax stamping, and no clearance API or fiscal-device certification regime governs commercial invoices. Taxpayers must issue serially numbered invoices and retain books and records for the statutory retention period. For physical exports, the RTA Customs Division requires an electronic Customs Export Declaration transmitted through its internal customs clearance platform; that filing is a trade-control document and is not a substitute for fiscal invoice clearance.

The RTA may reopen export claims for the statutory limitation period (7 years; unlimited where fraud is established). If a claimed export sale is reclassified as RMI-source revenue, the RTA retroactively assesses the 3% GRT on the gross invoice value, adds statutory interest at the published monthly rate, and imposes a civil penalty of up to 50% of the understated tax. Participation in false or fraudulent documentation triggers criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 1989 (fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years) and customs offences involving seizure, forfeiture, and administrative fines up to the full value of the goods or the duty evaded, whichever is greater. Registered agents and corporate officers who knowingly certify false zero-rating or export documents face personal liability and license revocation, and the taxpayer may be required to post a customs bond before further exports are cleared.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, and the RMI maintains no foreign-exchange control legislation. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible and unrestricted: there are no statutory restrictions on the remittance of dividends, interest, loan repayments, or proceeds from capital reduction or liquidation. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) not engaged in trade or business within the RMI is exempt from income tax and gross revenue tax, and the RMI imposes no dividend withholding tax; consequently, the only practical remittance hurdle is the correspondent bank's own sanctions screening and AML due diligence, not RMI statute.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. Receivables may be retained in USD or any permitted foreign currency at the BC's discretion, either onshore in an RMI-licensed bank or offshore. The entity should, however, maintain a complete trade documentation file (commercial invoices, bills of lading, Letter of Credit or MT103 records) to satisfy the bank's ongoing transaction monitoring and to support the tax-exempt non-resident status.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The official legal tender is the United States dollar; monetary policy and currency convertibility conditions are therefore governed by the U.S. Federal Reserve as issuer of the circulating currency. Banking licensing and prudential supervision are exercised by the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, under the Banking Act 1991; AML/CFT supervision is supported by the Financial Intelligence Unit attached to the Office of the Attorney General.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. The RMI has no electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing regime under the Banking Act 1991, and non-resident business corporations are not statutorily barred from holding accounts with foreign fintech banks. In practice, however, reputable EU/UK/Asia-Pacific EMIs classify RMI tax-exempt BCs as offshore or shell-risk profiles and commonly decline onboarding because of downstream U.S. correspondent-bank de-risking. Where available, fintech accounts are non-credit, payment-only wallets with stringent transaction limits and no documentary trade-finance capability. Legal constraint: any foreign EMI must be fully authorized in its home jurisdiction, and the RMI entity must still deliver beneficial-ownership declarations, source-of-funds evidence, and board resolutions to the EMI's compliance function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funds must be remitted as USD wire transfers using SWIFT MT103 message format, cleared over Fedwire or CHIPS through the beneficiary bank's U.S. correspondent. The payment message must identify the remitter, the beneficiary BC, the account number, and a clear purpose memo, e.g., 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARES' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - PROJECT MARSHALL'. The RMI imposes no exchange-control filing, but the receiving bank will require the full account-opening dossier, a board resolution approving the capital injection, and issuance records for the corresponding shares or debt instruments; large-value wires may trigger suspicious-transaction reporting under the RMI AML/CFT Act 2020 and should be supported by a source-of-funds letter.Supplier payments for endoscope-tower components and manufacturing inputs are executed by: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic-transfer wires for advance payments, deposits, and small-value balances; and (ii) irrevocable, confirmed, at-sight documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600, issued by the RMI-licensed bank and confirmed or negotiated by a top-tier U.S. or Asian bank for large shipments. For advance-payment protection, a standby Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT760) or an advance-payment bond may be issued in favor of the supplier. Each disbursement must be matched with the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (airway bill or ocean bill of lading), certificate of origin, inspection certificate, and applicable Letter of Credit terms. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No domestic transfer-pricing (TP) legislation imposes local-file or master-file thresholds in the Marshall Islands. For a non-resident manufacturing SPV, arm's-length documentation is nevertheless recommended when intercompany cross-border transactions exceed USD 500,000 annually, to support the absence of M.I. taxable presence and to prevent recharacterization by home-country tax authorities.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands applies OECD-aligned Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to entities conducting 'relevant activities' (distribution, services, financing, holding, shipping, and intellectual property). Manufacturing of surgical endoscopes is not a listed relevant activity; therefore the non-resident BC is not subject to an economic substance test. It must, however, maintain a registered agent and file the annual corporate report; the directors must be able to demonstrate that central management control is exercised outside the Marshall Islands to avoid any argument of domestic tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act 1989 and Business Corporations Act 1990, knowingly filing a false exemption declaration is a crime punishable by a fine of up to USD 25,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years. The Ministry of Finance may also retroactively recharacterize the entity as resident and impose business revenue tax at the rate of 3% of gross revenue, with interest at 2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are personally liable for the unpaid taxes and penalties up to the full outstanding amount. The Registrar can issue a travel ban and refer the case to the Attorney General for criminal forfeiture proceedings; equivalent foreign tax evasion may trigger extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Although manufacturing is outside the listed ESR relevant activities, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) and the Global Forum may require the registered agent to terminate the client relationship where the shell is used to facilitate tax crimes. Local law permits the Registrar to strike the company off the register; a 'serious default' declaration can result in a penalty of USD 500,000.

Director personal liability: A director knowingly allowing the company to be used as a sham vehicle is jointly and severally liable for foreign tax assessments; civil restitution orders and asset-freezing injunctions can be issued by the High Court of the Marshall Islands. The same director may be entered on the country's travel sanctions list pending cooperation with an international tax investigation.
AML/CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2022, financial and non-financial institutions must report suspected money laundering. Individual companies that fail to comply face civil penalties of up to USD 1,000,000; directors and officers may be subject to criminal fines of up to USD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for failing to implement know-your-customer (KYC) and beneficial-ownership controls. The Financial Intelligence Unit can issue a travel ban, freeze personal marital property, and prohibit the individual from leaving the country without permission. In egregious cases, extradition or prosecution abroad can follow.
Transfer Pricing Fraud / Profit Repatriation Sham

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands has no domestic TP statute, but the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework's Multilateral Institution may require the M.I. jurisdiction to cooperate in a cross-border tax audit. Under foreign tax law, the sham royalty arrangement can be recharacterized, resulting in a denial of deductions and penalty of 50% of the adjusted income. Local officers may additionally face perjury charges for falsely declaring the entity exempt.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who sign off on fraudulent intercompany agreements may incur personal liability for the foreign tax deficiency; leading courts have pierced the corporate veil to impose travel bans and disgorgement of personal bonuses. Under the Prevention of Fraud (M.I.) Act, any officer convicted of fraud is disqualified from serving as a director of any M.I. company for 10 years.

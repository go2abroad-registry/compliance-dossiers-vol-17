Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Banking and Treasury of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) – Internal Revenue and Customs Services Division; government portal: rmi-government.org; regional tax administration portal: Pacific Islands Tax Administrators Association (PITAA)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident Business Corporations enjoy a tax-exempt-zone status: RMI does not issue income-tax TINs because no direct income tax is levied. The statutory identity chain is (1) certificate of incorporation from the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act (BCA number); (2) Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) under the Foreign Investment Business License Act 2006 where foreign ownership exists; (3) annual Business License from the Business License Office with a gross-receipts-based fee; and (4) Customs Importer Registration/ID through the RMI Customs Division (Ministry of Finance, Banking and Treasury) before each import declaration. Social Security (RMI Social Security Administration) registration applies only upon hiring local employees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists because RMI taxes no corporate or personal income. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) election under Section 105 of the Business Corporations Act to operate as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation expressly exempt from RMI business license tax on foreign-source operations; (b) application for a Foreign Investment Incentive Act (2006) certificate granting import-duty and business-license waivers for qualifying capital projects, including supermarket infrastructure/equipment; and (c) the one-time deemed election to process imported grocery goods under a duty-suspension re-export regime (bonded warehouse) rather than paying import duty at clearance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Import Duty, Excise and Business License (no VAT, GST, or general Sales and Use Tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – RMI imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax, so no turnover threshold is prescribed. Import duty is payable on the c.i.f. value of imported grocery goods from the first US dollar; a Business License is required by every enterprise deriving any gross revenue in RMI, with fees scaled by annual gross receipts (typically 0.5%-1% of gross revenue).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – as RMI has no VAT/GST, no formal export zero-rating or refund mechanism exists. For customs purposes, imported Asian grocery inventory may be brought in duty-free under transit or bonded-warehouse procedures when goods are declared for re-export within the statutory period (typically one year). A cash deposit or bank guarantee equal to the suspended duty may be required, refunded upon proof of physical export. Exports of domestically produced goods are expressly exempt from export duty under the RMI Customs Tariff.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Form C2)
  • Commercial Invoice and certified packing list
  • Bill of Lading or House Airwaybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (USD banking-channel proceeds evidence, where required by the entity's licensed bank)
  • Certificate of Origin (only when preferential tariff treatment is claimed in the destination country)

RMI does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or API-based invoice reporting. Import declarations are filed electronically through the RMI Customs Division using the ASYCUDA World single-window platform; retail point-of-sale receipts require no fiscal mark, QR code, or Ministry pre-approval.

No VAT clawback exists, but fraudulent export or duty-suspension claims face: forfeiture of the goods; penalty assessments of up to three times the duty evaded; imprisonment up to five years under the Customs Ordinance; retrospective duty assessments for up to five years; and revocation of the Business License, FIBL, and Customs Importer ID. False Business License declarations additionally attract fines of USD 1,000-5,000 per instance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists in both the current and capital accounts because the U.S. dollar is domestic legal tender. A tax-exempt non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) is exempt from RMI taxes other than the annual franchise fee under the Business Corporations Act, and there are no exchange-control approvals, investment permits, balance-of-payments registrations, or profit-repatriation hurdles. Statutory impediments to dividend, capital, or liquidation surplus remittance are therefore nil. Residual hurdles are bank-driven only: FATCA (RMI-U.S. Model 1 IGA), OECD CRS reporting, enhanced due diligence on non-resident offshore-classified entities, and Pacific-corridor correspondent de-risking.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime applies in the Marshall Islands. Statutory timeline: not applicable; required conversion ratio: 0%. Export and trading receipts may be retained, held offshore, or remitted freely in U.S. dollars, and the RMI imposes no designated-account or repatriation requirement for the ISIC 4721 grocery-import trading receipts.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association (1986, as amended 2003), so monetary authority and currency issuance rest effectively with the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System of the United States. Domestic prudential supervision and bank licensing are administered by the Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Ministry of Finance) under the Banking Act 1990, with the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit responsible for AML/CFT supervision. The RMI maintains no exchange-control administration, no central-bank exchange-rate regime, and no foreign-exchange licensing requirement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. The RMI has no domestic e-money or payment-services licensing regime, and cross-border EMIs (including Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and Currencycloud) generally refuse or terminate RMI-domiciled corporations due to (i) FATCA and CRS reporting complexity, (ii) the RMI's tax-haven monitoring classification, and (iii) downstream correspondent-bank de-risking. Legally, an unlicensed foreign EMI providing a USD payment account to an RMI entity risks constituting unlicensed banking or payment activity under the Banking Act 1990, and the account would be operationally unreliable for OFAC-monitored import settlements. The only legally safe clearing route is a licensed RMI bank or a U.S.-chartered correspondent account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent or shareholder remits U.S. dollars by SWIFT MT103 to the BMI account referencing 'Equity Injection - Subscription of Share Capital of MH_BC (ISIC 4721)' with the board capital-call resolution and escrow release instruction attached. The RMI requires no exchange-control form, but the receiving bank mandates (i) source-of-funds attestation from the remitting shareholder, (ii) confirmation that the remitter is an account holder in good standing at the originating bank, and (iii) the Capital Escrow Account reference when funds first land in escrow. For remittances from mainland China, Hong Kong, Japan, or Korea, the originating Asian bank requires the correct BoP remittance purpose code, and remittances must never be structured below USD 10,000 to avoid AML structuring alerts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A — Marshall Islands does not promulgate transfer-pricing documentation thresholds, Local File, or Master File requirements for non-resident business corporations.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply only to licensed relevant activities such as shipping, insurance, and digital banking; a physical grocery retail hub (ISIC 4721) falls outside the scope and incurs no demonstrable economic substance filing duty. Nevertheless, general anti-avoidance doctrines require that the corporation maintain a real presence (director meetings, accounting records) to avoid being disregarded as a mere shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Misrepresentation of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of tax exemption, monetary penalties under the Business Corporations Act, and potential nil-imposition of alternative fines for false certification; the Registry may levy cumulative penalties and suspend the corporation's license.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil liability for fraudulent misrepresentation, restitution to affected parties, and criminal prosecution for perjury under RMI Penal Code where intentional false filings are proven.
Shell Corporation / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution after failure to demonstrate continuing existence; potential inclusion in international sanctions lists under the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) reciprocal arrangements; fines under the Associations Law.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil, making directors personally liable for corporate obligations, dissolutions costs, and restitution to defrauded foreign tax authorities.
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code and Anti-Money Laundering Act, financial penalties up to USD 250,000 per violation, forfeiture of laundered assets, and revocation of corporate registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal imprisonment for up to 10 years, travel bans, and freeze orders on personal assets; civil suits for restitution are likely.
Late or Non-Filing of Annual Reports

Statutory sanction: Late penalties accumulate at a percentage of the authorized capital per month; continued delinquency results in administrative dissolution by the Registrar and voiding of corporate powers.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for continuing obligations post-dissolution, including unpaid debts incurred after the company ceased to exist; individual fines for each day of continued operation after dissolution.
Improper Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Although no withholding tax applies, such conduct triggers anti-money-laundering scrutiny, enhanced record audit, and penalties under the Marshall Islands Beneficial Ownership Regulations for inaccurate reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil penalties for breach of fiduciary duty, clawback of distributions, and personal liability to creditors if the repatriation rendered the corporation insolvent.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Authority (RTA), an autonomous statutory agency established under the Revenue and Taxation Administration Act 2014 (Title 54, Marshall Islands Revised Code), headquartered in Majuro; taxpayer e-services and the national tax registration portal are operated by the RTA. Company registration, annual report filings and franchise-fee collection for business corporations are administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade; customs clearance is handled by the Customs and Border Control Service (CBCS) through the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A pure equity-holding Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (Title 27 MIRC) is a 'non-resident domestic corporation' and is not required to procure an RTA TIN or any VAT ID, as it derives no RMI-source income and carries on no business within the Republic. Step 2: The only mandatory registration steps are the appointment of an RMI-licensed registered agent, maintenance of a registered office in Majuro, and annual report lodgment with the Registrar of Corporations; the registered agent files the statutory non-resident declaration supporting the Section 222 exemption from all RMI taxes. Step 3: If the SPV later opens an RMI office, hires RMI-resident employees, or otherwise carries on business locally, the local representative must register with the RTA for payroll withholding (obtaining a TIN) and with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA), and a Foreign Investment Business License would become necessary. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. Under RMI law, a non-resident domestic Business Corporation that neither carries on business within the Republic nor holds assets or property in the Republic is statutorily exempt by Section 222 of the Business Corporations Act from income tax, gross revenue tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax and all other RMI taxes or assessments, except the annual report/registration fee. The RMI tax base is territorial and source-based: only income derived from RMI sources (e.g., local business gross revenue under the 3% gross revenue tax or 2% maritime charter tax) is chargeable. Passive dividends, interest and capital gains from non-RMI subsidiaries fall entirely outside the RMI tax net and may be accumulated or redistributed without RMI taxation.

Available election forms

The RMI has no check-the-box election, no CFC attribution regime, and no hybrid-mismatch legislation; the tax exemption is automatic for non-resident domestic corporations and cannot be converted into a pass-through election. A company may effectively 'elect' into RMI tax residence by carrying on business in the Republic or acquiring RMI situs assets, thereby becoming subject to the gross revenue tax and payroll obligations. For administrative certainty, the SPV may voluntarily register with the RTA and obtain a Certificate of Non-Resident Status / Exemption. The RMI participates in automatic exchange of information under CRS and the FATCA IGA through the RTA and is under EU Code of Conduct Group monitoring for substance and transparency; any economic-substance or declaration obligations adopted pursuant to those commitments should be verified with the registered agent at each filing cycle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax is levied in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The only indirect levies are customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act 2005 and sectoral gross-revenue-type taxes (3% general business and 2% outbound maritime charter). A pure equity-holding SPV has no indirect-tax nexus because it imports no goods and supplies no services within the RMI.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST regime operates (headline rate 0%). For customs purposes, any importer must register an importer code with the Customs and Border Control Service and account for import duty at standard ad valorem rates (generally 0%–10%, with a 5% headline rate on most goods), payable in United States dollars (USD, the legal tender of the RMI). The 3% gross revenue tax applies only to persons carrying on a trade or business within the RMI and therefore has no application to a passive offshore shareholding entity with no RMI-source revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because no VAT/GST is chargeable, there is no statutory 0% export rating, no input-VAT refund mechanism, and no export drawback regime in the RMI. The SPV's foreign dividend and disposition proceeds are non-RMI-source income receiving no tax at receipt, so no refund claim can arise. Goods exported from the RMI are exempt from export duty and invoiced without any VAT line, and the exporter has no corresponding refund entitlement. The only substantiation relevant to the SPV is maintenance of board minutes, share registers, and bank transfer records evidencing the passive holding function and the non-RMI source of funds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CBCS / ASYCUDA World) - not applicable to a pure equity-holding SPV, which moves no merchandise out of the RMI
  • Bill of Lading or transport document - not applicable; the SPV conducts no goods or shipping activity under ISIC 6420 / NAICS 551112
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or MT202 records evidencing receipt of foreign dividends and the non-RMI source of funds - retained in the bank AML/KYC and CRS self-certification file
  • Certificate of Good Standing and Certificate of Non-Resident Status (Section 222 BCA exemption) issued by the Registrar of Corporations - retained to evidence the absence of any RMI tax liability

RMI law imposes no electronic-invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal invoice clearance, and no digital tax stamping for commercial invoices. Customs clearance, however, is mandatory and fully electronified through the ASYCUDA World single-window declaration system: every import or export shipment requires electronic declaration, valuation, and duty assessment before release. The SPV, which issues no RMI-taxable invoices and moves no goods, is outside both the e-invoicing and customs-clearance obligations.

No export zero-rating fraud or clawback regime exists because no export tax or VAT is exigible. The principal compliance risks are: (i) loss of the Section 222 exemption by de facto business activity in the RMI or ownership of RMI situs assets, triggering retrospective assessment to income or gross revenue tax; (ii) Registrar late-filing penalties and involuntary dissolution for unpaid annual report fees; (iii) monetary penalties and imprisonment for false declarations or tax evasion under the Revenue and Taxation Administration Act 2014; and (iv) banking and AML consequences, including frozen wires and account termination, for failure to maintain a commercial substance file consistent with the Medium-Risk Enhanced AML tier. There are no exchange controls and no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the USD-denominated economy permits indefinite offshore retention of dividend proceeds, but a 180-day receipt-to-remittance cycle should be observed as best practice for audit readiness.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The US Dollar is fully legal tender; there is no separate domestic currency and therefore no capital-account versus current-account convertibility restriction. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and intra-group capital transfers face zero FX conversion hurdles and no exchange-control licensing requirement; the only compliance gates are AML/CFT recordkeeping, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and any applicable U.S. OFAC sanctions screening on cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies because the jurisdiction does not maintain a national currency and all receipts are already denominated in US Dollars. Export proceeds may be retained freely in USD-denominated accounts without central bank reporting or repatriation obligations, except for general AML/CFT threshold reporting applicable to all cash or wire activity.

Central bank supervision: Marshall Islands has no domestic central bank; monetary supervision rests with the Banking Commissioner under the Ministry of Finance in coordination with the Financial Action Team (FIU). The United States Federal Reserve acts as the effective monetary authority because the US Dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs are viable only if they maintain a USD-denominated correspondent banking corridor through a U.S. or regional clearing bank; however, the Marshall Islands does not issue domestic EMI licenses, so an offshore EMI operates under its home-state license and may be treated as a third-party payment processor. Legal constraints include FATF grey-list residual monitoring, the requirement that the EMI obtain a corresponding pass-through relationship with a licensed bank, and enhanced due diligence on the ultimate shareholder and beneficiary profile of the structuring SPV.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) shareholder remittance via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the parent-bank account to the SPV's operating account at Bank of Marshall Islands or a U.S. correspondent bank; (2) mandatory use of payment purpose code matching the equity subscription — for USD-clearing through the United States, the Federal Reserve's reporting classification should reference 'Equity Capital' or 'Intercompany Financing' as appropriate; Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a non-resident BC to its shareholders, regardless of the shareholder's residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The BCA Annual Report and annual government fee are due on or before 30 September of each calendar year. There is no separate income tax return deadline for a non-resident BC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A in the RMI — there is no corporate income tax and no domestic transfer pricing legislation prescribing Local File or Master File thresholds. Foreign group entities remain subject to transfer pricing documentation obligations in their own tax jurisdictions; common triggering thresholds include consolidated revenue of USD 50 million or related-party transactions above USD 1 million, depending on the parent jurisdiction's rules.

Economic substance scope: The RMI does not operate a BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime for passive pure-equity holding SPVs. However, the statutory tax exemption is conditional on non-resident status: the BC must not carry on trade or business in the RMI and must keep its management and business functions offshore. The SPV must also maintain a registered office, registered agent, board minutes, statutory books, and accurate accounting records. In foreign tax and AML proceedings, absence of real ownership control, bank account segregation, or documented dividend policy is treated as a shell-company red flag and may cause veil-piercing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration of Non-Resident Status

Statutory sanction: Loss of the non-resident tax exemption; exposure to RMI-source income taxation where applicable; administrative penalties imposed by the Registrar; criminal prosecution for false statements before the RMI courts with monetary fines and potential imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false declarations may be held personally liable for the entity's unpaid tax and penalties, disqualified from acting as directors in RMI, and subject to criminal fines and imprisonment for fraud.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The RMI registered agent may resign, the Registrar may administratively dissolve the BC, foreign correspondent banks may terminate Swift-wire and interbank access, and the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit or foreign regulators may freeze assets pending investigation.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the SPV is a mere instrumentality. Directors may be ordered to restore diverted corporate funds, subjected to travel bans in predicate criminal investigations, and sentenced to imprisonment where the shell structure is used for fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC and Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: RMI AML legislation obliges the registered agent to report suspicious activities to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit. Non-disclosure can trigger monetary penalties, freezing orders, seizure of corporate assets, revocation of the registered agent's license, and criminal liability under the RMI Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in laundering proceeds or conceal beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, and imprisonment. Corporate veil protection is unavailable for criminal conduct.
Transfer Pricing / Sham Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The RMI has no corporate tax exposure, but the foreign parent jurisdiction may recharacterize the structure, disallow deductions, impose transfer pricing penalties, and draw on the BC's accounting records as forensic evidence; RMI corporate records remain compellable through mutual legal assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fictitious agreements or false invoices can attract civil conspiracy liability, fines in foreign jurisdictions, criminal referral for invoice fraud, and, in cross-border prosecutions, extradition exposure and travel bans.

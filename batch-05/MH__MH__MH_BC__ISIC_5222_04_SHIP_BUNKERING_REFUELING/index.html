Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce and Trade, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro; international filings are accepted through licensed Marshall Islands registered agents and authorised service centres of the Maritime Administrator.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, Title 27, RMI Revised Code 2004 (as amended); sections 27-101 (Incorporators), 27-201 (Filing of Articles of Incorporation), 27-301 (Registered Office and Registered Agent) and 27-206 (General Powers and Amendment of Articles).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Registrar of Corporations form MHC-1) together with the written consent of the registered agent (form MHC-3).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Business Corporations Act; the Articles may authorise a nominal capital, commonly 1,000 shares at USD 1.00 par value. No pre-incorporation escrow account or bank deposit is required by the Registrar of Corporations; the subscribed consideration may remain unpaid until called, and the corporation may lawfully commence business upon issuance of the certificate of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Majuro Fire Department covering fixed and portable extinguishing systems and a Class B fire-risk assessment; structural-load inspection and pressure-test certificates for fuel tanks, hoses and transfer arms issued by an RMI-licensed marine surveyor; occupational workplace clearance under RMI health and safety standards requiring crew training, personal flotation devices, documented spill-response drills and a written emergency-response plan.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, Revenue and Taxation Division (Majuro); corporate registry function performed by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations (designated registered agent regime). No fully automated national e-filing portal exists; filings are effected through licensed registered agents and the Revenue and Taxation Division.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign principals incorporate a Business Corporation (BC) under the Business Corporations Act (BCA) via a licensed registered agent, obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation whose registration number serves as the de facto corporate identifier. Step 2: File the statutory Non-Resident Domestic Corporation certificate with the Registrar of Corporations, attesting that the BC holds no real property in the Marshall Islands (RMI) and carries on no business within RMI. Step 3: Where a physical TIN is required for banking, customs, or port operations, file TIN application form with the Revenue and Taxation Division; TIN issuance is otherwise administrative and immediate for non-resident BCs. Step 4: Register the foreign beneficial owners and directors in the confidential non-public registry maintained by the registered agent for AML compliance. Step 5: No income tax registration, no VAT registration, and no payroll tax registration are required while the non-resident exemption remains in force.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC). The RMI taxes on a strictly territorial source basis; a BC that validly files the non-resident certificate is wholly exempt from RMI income tax, gross receipts tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, and stamp duties on offshore transactions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC). The RMI taxes on a strictly territorial source basis; a BC that validly files the non-resident certificate is wholly exempt from RMI income tax, gross receipts tax, withholding tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, and stamp duties on offshore transactions. A resident domestic corporation (or foreign corporation) engaged in RMI-source business is instead subject to a gross-receipts tax of 3% (resident domestic) or 4% (foreign corporation), plus applicable municipal business license fees.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch or CFC elections exist in RMI tax law. The operative statutory status election is the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation certificate filed under the BCA: the BC must covenant (a) it does not own or lease RMI real property and (b) it does not transact business within RMI. The election remains effective indefinitely while the certificate is on file; engaging in RMI-source activity automatically voids the exemption and exposes the BC to retrospective gross-receipts taxation. There is no separate tax treaty election or entity reclassification mechanism, and the RMI has no double-taxation treaty network.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax regime exists in the Marshall Islands; indirect revenue is collected exclusively through import duties and excises under the Customs Tariff Act. Marine bunkers delivered to vessels in international traffic are treated as ship's stores and are not subject to RMI import duty, provided they do not enter RMI domestic consumption.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not Applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold, no sales-and-use-tax registration, and no intrastate turnover threshold. As a reference point for import duty, the Customs Tariff Act applies a 2% standard ad valorem duty on most dutiable imports (higher scheduled rates apply to alcohol, tobacco, petroleum products for domestic use), with no turnover-based de minimis threshold for commercial importers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in a strict VAT sense because the RMI imposes no VAT/GST. Equivalent zero-tax treatment for international marine bunkering is achieved through the territorial system: supplies of ISIC 5222 marine fuel and lubricants by a non-resident BC are wholly outside RMI taxing jurisdiction when the sale is negotiated and invoiced offshore, title passes outside RMI customs territory, payment is received into a non-RMI bank account, and the product is delivered as ship's stores to a vessel engaged in international traffic. There is no RMI refund mechanism because there is no input VAT to recover; the effective rate is 0% by statutory exclusion.

None - the RMI imposes no electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, no SAF-T reporting, no digital stamping, and no fiscal data exchange API for B2B or B2C invoicing. Bunker invoices and delivery receipts are commercial documents governed by contract law and the International Fuel Purchase and Sale Agreement terms; record retention of at least five years applies under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act for AML purposes.

The RMI has no exchange-control regime, so the 180-day target is a documentary integrity benchmark aligned with banking and marine insurance practice rather than a statutory repatriation deadline; funds received offshore need not be repatriated to RMI. Because no VAT exists, there is no export-VAT refund or VAT clawback mechanism. Enforcement exposure concentrates on: (1) Customs fraud - false declarations on ship's stores or dutiable imports trigger forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the duty evaded, and imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act; (2) gross-receipts tax recapture - if an NRDC is found to have derived RMI-source income while the non-resident certificate was on file, the Revenue and Taxation Division may assess 3% (domestic) or 4% (foreign) of gross revenue retrospectively, plus statutory interest and fraud/negligence penalties; (3) regulatory strike-off - the Registrar may revoke the non-resident status certificate or dissolve the BC for misrepresentation, and directors and the registered agent face personal liability for knowingly false filings; and (4)AML penalties - failure to maintain bunkering counterparty due-diligence and transaction records for the statutory retention period attracts fines under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act and can disqualify the BC from RMI ship registration benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. The RMI maintains no exchange-control administration, no inward-investment approval, and no profit-remittance permit regime. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) may freely repatriate dividends, redemption proceeds, loan repayments, and liquidation distributions in USD without tax or exchange levies, provided the underlying transactions are documented and compliant with FTRA record-keeping obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The RMI imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export or bunkering proceeds. Receipts may be held offshore or in-country at the corporate treasury's discretion; banks will require only standard commercial documentation (commercial invoice, Bunker Delivery Note, and contract) to support each credit.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not operate a domestic central bank. The US dollar is sole legal tender and monetary policy is effectively set by the US Federal Reserve. Banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision, and prudential oversight are administered by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance pursuant to the Banking Act 2013 (as amended) and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2021 (FTRA), which implements the FATF standards.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. The RMI has no e-money institution licensing regime and no digital bank has received a license under the Banking Act 2013. Global digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, WorldFirst) generally decline or close accounts for RMI-registered non-resident corporations due to correspondent-bank de-risking, offshore-perception flags, and the high-risk maritime fuel sector. A licensed RMI bank account is therefore a mandatory prerequisite; US-chartered fintech banks may be entertained only if the BC demonstrates a genuine US operating nexus (office, employees, director residency) and a proven compliance track record.

A licensed RMI bank account is therefore a mandatory prerequisite; US-chartered fintech banks may be entertained only if the BC demonstrates a genuine US operating nexus (office, employees, director residency) and a proven compliance track record.

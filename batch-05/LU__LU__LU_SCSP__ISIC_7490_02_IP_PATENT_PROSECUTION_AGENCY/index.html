Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT. Single digital portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for entity and tax identification.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Reserve the SCSp name and file incorporation document (private deed sufficient unless real property is contributed) with the Luxembourg Business Registers; obtain the RCS registration number (Numéro d'immatriciation) and the national business identification number. Step 2: Enroll for corporate income tax / municipal business tax (if any) and net wealth tax identification with ACD. Step 3: File VAT registration application with AEDT via MyGuichet.lu to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number in format 'LU' + 8 digits; a non-resident SCSp without Luxembourg establishment must register directly for VAT once it makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg and may be required to appoint a VAT fiscal representative if established outside the EU. Step 4: Apply for an EORI number through the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency for any export/import operations. The SCSp, although transparent for direct tax, acts as a distinct VAT taxable person for its economic activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Step 1: Reserve the SCSp name and file incorporation document (private deed sufficient unless real property is contributed) with the Luxembourg Business Registers; obtain the RCS registration number (Numéro d'immatriciation) and the national business identification number. Step 2: Enroll for corporate income tax / municipal business tax (if any) and net wealth tax identification with ACD. Step 4: Apply for an EORI number through the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency for any export/import operations. The SCSp, although transparent for direct tax, acts as a distinct VAT taxable person for its economic activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg grants no formal check-the-box regime. However, an SCSp is tax transparent by default; certain partnerships may elect to be treated as a capital company for Luxembourg income tax purposes under Article 2(2) of the Income Tax Law (option de transparence). Hybrid mismatch elections and aligned status classifications are available under ATAD-driven Luxembourg rules (Article 168quater LIR). For VAT, no transparency election exists: the SCSp is a taxable person if it independently carries out an economic activity, irrespective of its direct-tax transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-enterprise exemption (in force from 1 January 2025); non-resident suppliers without Luxembourg establishment have no threshold and must register from the first taxable supply; the threshold does not apply to intra-Community distance sales or B2C electronic services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU. For services such as PCT patent prosecution and international trademark registration, the place of supply rule makes B2B services to non-Luxembourg taxable customers taxable where the customer is established (reverse charge), effectively 0% Luxembourg VAT. B2C services relating to intellectual property, consultancy, and legal/prosecution services to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under Article 59(c) of the VAT Directive if actually used outside the EU, provided the supplier obtains and retains sufficient evidence of the customer's non-EU status and effective use. All zero-rating requires contemporaneous documentary substantiation of the export or the offshore recipient status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU or electronic declaration via Luxembourg DELTA-C system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing foreign-source payment)
  • Commercial invoice and engagement contract with the foreign patent applicant
  • Receiving office / patent office filing confirmation and official PCT fee receipts
  • Certificate of foreign VAT registration or VIES validation for the non-resident business customer

No general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions via the Peppol network; B2B e-invoicing is optional but must be processed if issued. VAT returns must be submitted electronically through the eCDF channel (XML file) via MyGuichet.lu, with monthly, quarterly, or annual filing cycles depending on turnover. Data retention of e-invoices (authenticity, integrity, legibility) is required for 10 years.

The AEDT heavily scrutinizes zero-rated export claims. If documentation is incomplete or fraudulent, the authority issues a clawback assessment for the unpaid VAT plus default interest (statutory monthly interest of 0.8%), and applies civil penalties ranging from EUR 750 to EUR 12,500 per infraction. Intentional false declarations (fraude fiscale) expose the taxpayer to criminal fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years, with an extended 10-year assessment window for fraudulent or non-filed cases (standard assessment period: 5 years). In cross-border patent prosecution structures, mischaracterization of services as exported when effectively used in Luxembourg triggers full VAT recovery plus interest and penalties from the date the tax should have been declared.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is national legal tender; Luxembourg imposes zero exchange controls. Both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalized under Article 63 TFEU; no FX licensing, prior authorization, or transaction declaration applies to service receipts, patent annuity payments, or capital injections into an SCSp. Profit remittance is unhindered: the SCSp is tax-transparent (unless an election for opacity is made), so distributions to resident or non-resident partners carry no Luxembourg withholding tax and no government approval is required for cross-border transfers. Practical hurdle: banks automatically report cross-border wire transfers of EUR 12,500 or more to BCL for BOP survey purposes under the client's IFR (Inward/Outward) classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg has no export-proceeds surrender, retention, compulsory conversion, or minimum holding period for commercial earnings denominated in EUR, USD, CHF, or other currencies. Incoming USD/CHF receipts from PCT filings, WIPO annuities, or foreign associate invoices may be retained in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely; conversion into EUR is voluntary and subject only to bank spread and ordinary counterparty credit terms.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Eurosystem member holding monetary authority and Balance-of-Payments (BOP) statistics mandate; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions; no separate Foreign Exchange Administration exists in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — legally viable but operationally constrained. CSSF-licensed EMIs (e.g., PPRO Financial S.A.) and passporting EMIs such as Wise Europe S.A. can open payment accounts for an SCSp provided the LPA, RCS extract, RBE certificate, and UBO declarations pass the EMI's KYB engine. Legal constraints: (i) a PSD2 payment account is not a regulated banking current account and cannot hold patent-attorney client funds under the Luxembourg séquestre regime; (ii) Wise's Acceptable Use Policy prohibits businesses acting as trust, fiduciary, or third-party payment intermediaries, so the SCSp may use Wise only for its own operational disbursements supported by direct invoices; (iii) EMIs offer no FX forward, overdraft, or documentary credit capabilities; (iv) transaction ceilings and mid-market conversion make Wise suboptimal for annuity batches exceeding EUR 25,000 per operation. Recommended scope: low-value disbursements below USD 2,000 to WIPO, USPTO, and foreign associates; all client-money and high-value treasury flows must be routed through Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: executing partners send a SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) or SWIFT MT103 to the SCSp's designated account; beneficiary name and registered address must match the RCS entry exactly. Remittance information (field 70) must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SCSp AGREEMENT [DATE] — UNIT CLASS [X]' with ISO 20022 external purpose code 'INVS' (investments, excluding cash). Verification: the bank archives the LPA signature page, the unit subscription ledger, the GP-issued capital-call notice, and partner identification; BCL BOP automatic reporting classifies the inflow as F511 (direct investment — equity capital other than reinvested earnings). No FX conversion is mandatory; if the contribution arrives in USD or CHF, the bank may credit the foreign-currency sub-account or convert at prevailing market rates upon client instruction.EUR settlement to the European Patent Office: SWIFT MT103 or MT102 clearing via TARGET2 to the EPO treasury account, with field 70 containing the EPO deposit-account number and application/publication reference; intraday execution confirms official-fee timing. USD settlement to the USPTO: MT103 routed over CHIPS via the bank's US correspondent, beneficiary being the USPTO fee-collection account, with 'OVRDT' waiver instructions where permissible. CHF settlement to WIPO in Geneva: via SIC or the bank's CHF clearing correspondent using MT103 with WIPO account reference.

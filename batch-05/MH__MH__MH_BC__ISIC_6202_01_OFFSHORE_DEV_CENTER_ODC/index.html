Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, Republic of the Marshall Islands, Majuro; filings accepted directly or through accredited Marshall Islands licensed registered agents.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (BCA), Marshall Islands Revised Code Title 12, Chapter 7; Sections 12-701 et seq. (Articles of Incorporation), Section 12-102 (registered office and registered agent), and Section 12-402 (organization of the board of directors).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (notarized) filed with the Registrar of Corporations together with the Registered Agent Acceptance Form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement. The BCA permits issuance of one or more shares with par value, no par value, or mixed classes as stated in the Articles of Incorporation. One hundred percent foreign ownership is allowed for a non-resident Business Corporation; the initial subscription is recorded in the share ledger upon issuance.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate required from the National Fire Prevention Authority under the Marshall Islands Fire Safety Code, including inspection of exits, extinguishers, and electrical installations; building occupancy and structural load inspection by the competent public works authority for the office lease premises; occupational health and safety clearance from the Ministry of Health, including workplace ergonomics assessment and health declarations for expatriate professional staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement. The BCA permits issuance of one or more shares with par value, no par value, or mixed classes as stated in the Articles of Incorporation. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 6202 — dedicated offshore software engineering and maintenance hub is a non-industrial IT service activity under the Marshall Islands regulatory system; no industrial licensing board has jurisdiction. General commercial oversight is limited to local municipal business permitting and the foreign investment business licensing regime if a physical in-country establishment is opened.

Category: No federal statutory operating license is required for the offshore software development center. For any physical office in the Marshall Islands, the relevant local permits would be a Municipal Business Permit and, where foreign-controlled, an Exemption from or Compliance with the Foreign Investment Business License Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Administration (Revenue Division), Ministry of Finance & Administration, Republic of the Marshall Islands — official government portal: https://rmi.gov.mh (paper-based tax filings; no national corporate e-filing or API system for non-resident entities)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is not automatically registered for tax. A TIN is required only if the BC derives income from sources within the Republic, employs RMI-resident individuals, carries on business in the Republic, or needs a local clearing/customs certificate. Procedure: (1) verify non-resident status under the Income Tax Act and absence of a RMI business presence; (2) if registration is needed, file the standard taxpayer registration application with the Tax Administration accompanied by the certificate of incorporation, registered-office/agent particulars, and a board resolution certifying that all development, maintenance, and billing activity is performed outside the Republic; (3) obtain the TIN and, where appropriate, a Certificate of Tax Exemption / Non-Assessment. No minimum capital, statutory license, or sectoral permit is required for the ISIC 6202 / NAICS 541512 offshore development center scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI law contains no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Available elections: (i) perfect and maintain non-resident domestic corporation status for income tax purposes; (ii) elect not to carry on business in the Republic, keeping the BC outside the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1982 (as amended); (iii) voluntarily apply for a tax-clearance or exemption confirmation. Because the RMI has no CFC, controlled-foreign-corporation, or branch-profit regime, no additional entity-classification or hybrid election is required for the offshore development center model.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — the Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. Indirect fiscal levies are limited to customs import/export tariffs, fuel taxes, and the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT), the latter applying only to persons engaged in business within the Republic.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no value-added or sales tax is enacted. For GRT, registration and return filing arise only when a person is engaged in business in the Republic; there is no de-minimis turnover threshold. A non-resident BC performing and billing all services abroad, with no office, employees, or RMI-source income, does not meet the territorial nexus test and has no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable by statute — because the RMI imposes no VAT/GST, there is no 0% export rating, refund credit, or VAT recovery mechanism to claim. Non-taxation of the ODC's export services is established by demonstrating that the software development and offshore maintenance services are performed, managed, and paid outside the Republic and generate no income sourced to the RMI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Division) — applicable only to physical hardware or equipment movements
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export, where goods move through RMI territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Payment-Correspondent (Wise B2B, Payoneer, Wire) advice evidencing FX repatriation from the foreign client
  • Commercial invoice and signed offshore services agreement/SOW identifying the non-RMI client, delivery location, and pricing basis

No mandate — the RMI does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, digital stamping, continuous transaction control (CTC), or government API clearance. Invoices may be issued electronically in the ordinary course of business and must reflect the offshore service nature, the foreign client, and the non-RMI place of performance.

No VAT clawback or export-crediting fraud regime exists because no VAT is levied. However, where a non-resident BC claims or has obtained customs duty relief or a Certificate of Tax Exemption, the Tax Administration and RMI Customs may audit to verify absence of RMI-source income and business nexus. Retrospective reassessments may be made for periods not barred by the statute of limitations, with statutory interest accruing monthly on unpaid amounts, late-filing fines, and penalties for misrepresentation; fraudulent declarations may trigger criminal fines, revocation of the tax-exemption certificate, and clawback of any customs duty exemption, together with monetary penalties, if exempted equipment is diverted to the domestic RMI market.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, and there is no domestically issued currency. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements, and no repatriation restrictions. Profit remittances, including dividends, may be freely transferred abroad by wire, subject only to standard AML/KYC due diligence and corporate authorization documentation. The RMI does not impose any foreign exchange rationing, market segmentation, or surrender mandates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The RMI enforces no statutory timelines, conversion ratios, or mandatory surrender obligations for export receipts because all underlying export earnings are denominated in U.S. dollars. Retaining or applying export proceeds offshore or domestic is unrestricted, and no Central Bank declaration or conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI); no domestic central bank exists — monetary policy is effectively determined through U.S. dollarization under the Compact of Free Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational disbursements and LOW as a full banking substitute. No domestic EMI licensing regime exists, and cross-border digital EMIs such as Wise B2B and Payoneer are not regulated deposit-taking institutions under RMI law; funds held with such providers are not subject to RMI banking protections. They may be used for payroll, contractor payments, and low-value settlement within a limited compliance framework, but all core treasury functions must maintain a licensed commercial bank account as the principal operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's licensed bank to the RMI bank or its correspondent, denominated in USD, with remittance field 70 explicitly marked 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION' and the RMI entity registration number. No special RMI purpose-code exists; use a clear OECD harmonized transaction description and include a copy of the capital allotment register, the corporate resolution, and proof of beneficial ownership if requested. Funds may transit an intermediate correspondent (e.g., Bank of Guam or other major U.S. clearer) before final credit; the transaction must be traceable and supported by adequate know-your-customer documentation.For service and operational payments, the primary rail is an SWIFT MT103 direct credit from the RMI operating account to the beneficiary's account, with supporting invoices and an internal payment approval. For larger procurement or high-value gateway transactions, a commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) may be issued via a correspondent bank, especially when buying physical goods or negotiating with third-party vendors. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on foreign-source income; no corporate income tax applies to a Marshall Islands Business Corporation qualifying as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (RMI Income Tax Act 1989).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Business Corporation to resident or non-resident shareholders; dividends sourced outside the RMI are outside the tax net entirely.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File / Master File threshold: the RMI prescribes no documentation thresholds in U.S. dollars and does not mandate contemporaneous transfer pricing records or country-by-country reporting for Non-Resident Domestic Corporations; however, the section 482-style reallocation authority in the Income Tax Act permits the tax administrator to adjust intercompany prices where the fiction would manufacture RMI-source losses or shift income to the ODC; a prudent ODC maintains service agreements, benchmark studies applying OECD comparability factors, and invoices denominated in the delivery currency.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations Act, in force for zero-tax resident and non-resident domestic corporations, requires genuine substance for geographically mobile relevant activities — IP holding, distribution and service centers, financing, leasing, banking, insurance, headquarters, and shipping. A software ODC engaged in active ISIC 6202 engineering, maintenance and DevOps with staff, premises and decision-making in its service-delivery country is not an IP-holding shell; nonetheless the entity must document (i) full-time employee headcount and qualifications, (ii) physical office and IT infrastructure, (iii) annual operating expenditure, and (iv) board minutes evidencing real management, because the RMI has committed to OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework substance standards and will strike exempt entities found to be mere letterbox companies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent non-resident declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil assessment of all unpaid income tax plus interest at 10% per annum; 50% fraud penalty; criminal conviction punishable by fine and imprisonment for up to five years; administrative dissolution of the corporation by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false annual declarations are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax deficiency; courts may lift the corporate veil where the BC was used to evade taxes, exposing personal bank accounts and assets; travel restrictions and departure bans may be imposed pending settlement of tax liabilities.
Shell company and sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations Act, failure to satisfy the economic substance test attracts financial penalties of up to USD 20,000, a compliance direction, and ultimately dissolution; the RMI may also exchange entity information with treaty partners, exposing the ODC structure to foreign recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham entity face disqualification from serving as directors of any RMI corporation for up to five years; foreign taxing authorities may pierce the shell and tax the founder's personal worldwide income; money-laundering derivative charges may attach to funds moved through the shell.
AML/CFT and beneficial ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Violations of the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2018 expose the entity and its registered agent to fines of up to USD 50,000 per breach, forfeiture of funds, and criminal prosecution; the Beneficial Ownership Register is accessible to the Financial Intelligence Unit and foreign authorities via information exchange.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in beneficial-ownership concealment are personally criminally liable with imprisonment up to five years; asset freezes and international arrest warrants can be issued; professional disqualification applies to trust-company principals and agents involved.
Fraudulent trading, wrongful repatriation and veil piercing

Statutory sanction: Equitable subordination of shareholder loans, restitution orders, and statutory compensation orders; RMI courts and foreign courts applying the recognized common law doctrine will impose civil restitution; the corporation may be wound up on just and equitable grounds on the application of the Registrar or an aggrieved party.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil attaches personal liability for corporate debts, taxes and client liabilities where the BC is a mere instrumentality; directors face personal civil restitution, travel bans, extradition in serious fraud cases, and imprisonment for fraudulent trading under the general criminal law; the same doctrine is available to the client jurisdiction's tax authority to reattribute ODC profits to the individual shareholder.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Customs and Tax Administration, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro Atoll); customs filing is processed through the ASYCUDA World single window at https://www.rmicustoms.org. Corporate franchise administration is handled by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, with registry services operationally administered through the Association of the Marshall Islands (AMI).

E-government filing portal

The RMI abolished its national income tax and therefore maintains no statutory TIN or VAT ID regime for income or consumption tax purposes. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act must: (1) obtain its certificate of incorporation from the Registrar of Corporations (AMI), which serves as the entity's sole statutory identifier; (2) file an annual report and pay the annual government franchise fee (minimum US$200) to maintain good standing; (3) register with the RMI Customs and Tax Administration only if it physically imports goods into the RMI; and (4) register as an employer with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA) only if it hires RMI-resident employees. A pure offshore EMR SaaS licensor with no RMI payroll, physical office, or import activity is not required to procure any local tax identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The RMI abolished its national income tax and therefore maintains no statutory TIN or VAT ID regime for income or consumption tax purposes. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act must: (1) obtain its certificate of incorporation from the Registrar of Corporations (AMI), which serves as the entity's sole statutory identifier; (2) file an annual report and pay the annual government franchise fee (minimum US$200) to maintain good standing; (3) register with the RMI Customs and Tax Administration only if it physically imports goods into the RMI; and (4) register as an employer with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA) only if it hires RMI-resident employees. A pure offshore EMR SaaS licensor with no RMI payroll, physical office, or import activity is not required to procure any local tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No RMI check-the-box, entity-classification, or hybrid-mismatch election exists because the RMI has no income tax to elect into. Non-resident domestic BCs automatically enjoy statutory exemption from RMI income, withholding, estate, and gross-receipts taxes under the Business Corporations Act and are excluded from the scope of the former Income Tax Act. Owners subject to U.S. federal taxation may independently file IRS Form 8832 or Form 8858 to classify the RMI entity for U.S. purposes, but that constitutes a U.S. federal reporting position and not an RMI statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_Indirect_Tax_System — the RMI does not operate a VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax regime. Its only indirect fiscal instruments are customs import duties, selective excise on alcohol/tobacco/fuel, and the passenger departure (green) tax; none applies to offshore SaaS or EMR cloud services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no VAT/GST system exists, so there is no turnover threshold, no registration threshold, and no obligation for non-resident SaaS suppliers to register, charge, or remit indirect tax regardless of revenue volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Conceptually inapplicable — with no VAT/GST system, there is no 0% export rate to claim, no zero-rating certificate to obtain, no input-tax credit to substantiate, and no indirect tax refund mechanism. Cross-border supply of EMR clinical workflow software from a non-resident RMI BC is de facto outside any RMI consumption tax, and no application, prior approval, or registration is required. If physical hardware is later exported from RMI territory, no export duty is levied and no VAT refund procedure exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) — required only where physical goods depart RMI territory; not applicable to cloud-delivered EMR software
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — mandatory for tangible export cargo; not applicable to pure digital SaaS transactions
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment receipt — evidencing offshore receipt of subscription fees; recommended for the entity's contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and AML banking file

No mandate exists for e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping in the RMI. Commercial invoices for EMR SaaS subscriptions may be issued freely in any format without fiscal authorization. Physical imports are cleared electronically through ASYCUDA World at the port of entry with a customs import declaration and payment of applicable tariffs; this clearance mechanism does not extend to digital services.

The RMI levies no VAT or export tax, so no zero-rating fraud, clawback, or retrospective VAT assessment is legally possible. Enforcement exposure is limited to: (1) RMI Customs Act violations for physical imports, where false declarations, undervaluation, or smuggling attract forfeiture of goods, duty-evasion penalties, and criminal fines or imprisonment; (2) Business Corporations Act violations, where failure to pay annual franchise fees or submission of materially false annual reports exposes the BC to strike-off, certificate revocation, and director or officer liability; and (3) banking provenance review, since RMI law imposes no exchange controls or mandatory FX repatriation deadline and the 180-day parameter referenced in this dossier is a banking-documentation convention rather than a statutory mandate. Non-resident BCs exporting SaaS are not subject to RMI income-tax audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands has no independent fiat currency and no central bank foreign exchange control mechanism. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible in practice because the U.S. dollar is legal tender and freely circulates. There are no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of invested capital. A foreign-owned Business Corporation may remit profits without exchange control approval, subject solely to compliance with anti-money laundering and banking documentation requirements. The practical limitation is not convertibility but correspondent banking due diligence and the need for a credible banking relationship to execute USD-denominated wires.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratios in the Marshall Islands. The use of the U.S. dollar as domestic currency means export receipts are already denominated in the functional currency; no foreign exchange repatriation obligation or conversion requirement is imposed. Exporters may retain all export proceeds in their local bank accounts or deploy them offshore without central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Banking supervision is conducted by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission under the Ministry of Finance. Because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, monetary policy is effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve, and the Banking Commission acts as the licensing and prudential regulator for locally licensed banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. There is no domestic fintech electronic money institution licensing framework in the Marshall Islands. A foreign offshore EMI cannot obtain a direct settlement account with the local clearing system without a physical licensed banking presence. In practice, offshore EMIs are used only as ancillary payment collection channels, not as primary treasury concentration vehicles. Any EMI that touches Marshall Islands clients must comply with the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, but the absence of a digital banking charter makes a fully regulated onshore EMI account untenable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable – the Marshall Islands has not enacted mandatory transfer pricing documentation thresholds for Non-Resident Domestic Corporations; no Local File or Master File requirement in USD. However, if the entity becomes subject to Economic Substance Regulations in RMI for a relevant activity, it must maintain adequate accounting and operational substance records evidencing core income-generating activities in the RMI.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The RMI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) impose a substance requirement on RMI entities, including Non-Resident Domestic Corporations, conducting certain relevant activities. EMR SaaS may fall within the 'Holding Company', 'Headquarters', or 'Intellectual Property' categories depending on whether the business holds medical software IP, executes material management decisions, or earns income from licensing/exploitation of intangibles in the RMI. An annual ESR notification must be filed, and the company must demonstrate adequate physical presence, employees, expenditure, and core income-generating activities in the RMI proportionate to the activity. Failure to satisfy ESR results in financial penalties and potential striking-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: RMI administrative penalties for false declarations and BCA violation: fines up to US$10,000, cancellation of corporate status, and disqualification of the entity from RMI registry. Foreign revenue authorities may impose back taxes, interest, accuracy-related penalties up to 75% of understatement, and CFC or managed-entity assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a sham tax arrangement may have the corporate veil pierced for fraudulent trading and may be held personally liable for the company’s foreign tax liabilities. In serious cases, director travel bans may be issued and extradition or imprisonment of up to 5 years can arise under foreign tax fraud statutes.
Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties of up to US$50,000 per violation; directors and officers may be fined individually; the company may be struck off the registry and prohibited from transacting business. The RMI Registrar may also share beneficial ownership information with foreign authorities through tax information exchange agreements.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fines if the company fails to pay, are barred from serving as director of any RMI company for a period of 5 years, and may be subject to arrest warrants resulting in detention and travel bans.
AML/CFT Violation (Sanctions and Payment Processors)

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering Act, penalties include financial sanctions up to US$100,000 per infraction, asset freezing, forfeiture of funds, and suspension or cancellation of payment processing or business licenses. Correspondent banking relationships and Stripe merchant accounts may be terminated globally.

Director personal liability: Directors identified as 'culpable officers' for failure to prevent money laundering may be criminally liable, required to pay restitution of laundered funds, subject to travel bans pending extradition, and face imprisonment up to 10 years.

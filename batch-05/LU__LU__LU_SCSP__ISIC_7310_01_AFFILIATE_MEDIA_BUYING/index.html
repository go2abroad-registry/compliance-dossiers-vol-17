Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office without physical presence is not acceptable for an SCSp exercising commercial activity in Luxembourg. Luxembourg law (Article 44 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended; LBR registration requirements) requires an effective registered office in Luxembourg. However, domiciliation (home office) is permissible under strict conditions: the SCSp may use a domiciliation agreement provided by a licensed professional (a 'société de domiciliation' or a regulated PSF) at a physical address in Luxembourg where the statutory records, corporate books, and RCSL correspondence are kept. For an affiliate media-buying vehicle with no employees in Luxembourg, a licensed domiciliation agent is acceptable and common, provided the agent holds the required professional status and the arrangement is not used for artificial tax structuring that would trigger substance-over-form challenges under the OECD BEPS and EU ATAD standards. The degree of substance required remains minimal for an SCSp under Luxembourg administrative practice, but must include a registered address, a local general partner (legal or natural person), proper accounting records, and compliance with UBO registration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The SCSp has no statutory minimum capital; capital is fixed freely in the partnership agreement. Contributions may be in cash, in kind or in industry, and no escrow or bank blocked account is required by Luxembourg law.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must hold a valid fire safety compliance certificate issued by the Service d'Incendie et de Secours; structural load inspection certificate is required where the lease or building permit mandates it; workplace clearance from the Luxembourg Inspectorate of Labour and Mines (ITM) must cover electrical safety, lighting, ventilation, health, and ergonomic standards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Primary portals: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.public.lu; VAT and customs filing via the eCDF electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

An SCSp is a fiscally transparent special limited partnership and is not itself subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax. For formal tax identity, the entity must first be registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register) and obtain its RCS/matricule number. The Luxembourg VAT identification number is then obtained separately from the AEDT VAT administration, normally via the MyGuichet and eCDF online procedures. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment may register directly for VAT or appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative. A national TIN is not allocated to the transparent SCSp itself for direct tax purposes; tax identity flows through to the partners. In practice, the operative statutory identifiers for the SCSp are the RCS registration number and the Luxembourg VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. The tax classification of an SCSp follows legal-form analysis and is transparent by default. There is no voluntary partnership election available to convert the SCSp into a corporation. Where hybrid entities or hybrid instruments are involved, the Luxembourg ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules can override the default transparency to recharacterise income or deny deductions in order to prevent double non-taxation. A fully taxable vehicle such as a SOPARFI may be chosen instead as a structuring alternative, but that is a commercial incorporation choice rather than a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg imposes no general monetary VAT-registration threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons. The EU Union One-Stop-Shop threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied cross-border services. Resident small businesses with annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 may qualify for the small-business VAT exemption. For the SCSp affiliate media-buying activity, VAT registration is required before making taxable supplies in Luxembourg where such supplies are not outside the scope under B2B place-of-supply rules or reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Luxembourg applies 0% VAT to exports of goods where the supplier proves that the goods physically leave the EU under a valid customs export declaration and that title and control have passed to a buyer established outside Luxembourg. For the ISIC 7310 affiliate media-buying activity, zero-rating is not the normal mechanism. B2B advertising and performance-marketing services supplied to a foreign taxable customer are outside Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule and should be evidenced by the customer's foreign VAT registration or equivalent foreign business evidence and by a properly addressed commercial contract. B2C digital advertising and lead-generation services supplied to non-Luxembourg consumers may remain taxable in Luxembourg unless an exemption applies. A legitimate zero-rating claim therefore applies mainly to physically exported goods or qualifying export-related services, all supported by proof of dispatch, transport, export customs declaration and transfer of ownership.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Luxembourg eCDF/NCTS electronic customs channel and any required export licences
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU to the contractually designated destination
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, signed sales contract and documentary proof of transfer of ownership and risk to the non-resident buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing receipt of payment from the non-resident counterparty
  • Foreign customer VAT identification number or equivalent overseas business-status evidence where the export or outside-Luxembourg treatment is based on the customer's taxable-person status

Luxembourg does not currently mandate general B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance. There is no central financial clearance API and no digital stamping requirement for commercial invoices. Invoices to Luxembourg public-sector entities must, however, be submitted in structured PEPPOL format pursuant to the Luxembourg B2G e-invoicing mandate in force since 2020. VAT returns, export declarations and related periodic filings are submitted through the AEDT eCDF portal. Electronic invoices in other formats are permitted provided they comply with EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC and Luxembourg record-keeping requirements. The statutory retention period for invoices is 10 years.

Luxembourg does not impose exchange-control repatriation as a legal condition for VAT export treatment, but this compliance dossier applies a 180-day inward-remittance documentation deadline. If missing or defective, the AEDT can retrospectively disallow zero-rating, redetermine Luxembourg VAT on the full value of the affected supply, and recover the unpaid VAT together with default interest. Fraudulent or intentionally misleading export-zero-rating claims are subject to Luxembourg statutory penalty tax, administrative fines and possible referral to the criminal tax prosecutor. Participants in VAT-fraud chains may also face joint and several personal liability. All documentation supporting export or outside-scope treatment must be retained for 10 years from the invoice date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility and unrestricted capital movement apply under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Luxembourg maintains no exchange control regime, no capital account restrictions, and no separate formalities for profit remittance or transfer of funds. Banks apply standard anti-money laundering and sanctions screening only, in accordance with CSSF Circulars and Luxembourg law, not exchange-control permits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or any other foreign currency proceeds. Foreign currency may be retained freely or converted at market rates without obligation to sell to the central bank or a licensed intermediary.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, AML/KYC supervisory matters, and payment system oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium under strictly regulated conditions. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding a passport into Luxembourg or a licence issued by an EEA authority can provide payment accounts to an SCSp, but many reject high-velocity affiliate-media flows unless a complete documentary trail is provided. Offshore and non-EEA digital EMI platforms not licensed or passported into Luxembourg should be considered legally non-viable as primary banking rails; they may be used at most for ancillary treasury purposes if they hold an EU payment institution licence and maintain client segregation, but never as the sole clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from a non-resident affiliate must be routed using SWIFT MT103 or MT202/MT202COV wire transfers to the SCSp's account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance instruction should cite the Eurosystem balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment equity or other capital, typically reported under 'Direct Investment: Equity Capital' or a comparable code accepted by the BCL; the customer must provide the bank with a capital call notice, partner resolution, executed partnership agreement, and a source-of-wealth declaration. Beneficiary naming must correspond exactly to the SCSp legal name and bank account to ensure straight-through processing.SEPA Credit Transfers are the primary clearing rail for euro-denominated affiliate and publisher payouts within the EEA. For non-EEA destinations, SWIFT MT103 is the standard commercial wire mechanism, with beneficiary BIC/IBAN or ABA/account details as required. Commercial letters of credit are not suitable for performance-based media-buying open-account settlements and should be avoided; documentary trade instruments are used only if a particular publisher contract demands them. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

SCSp is fiscally transparent (pass-through); no entity-level corporate income tax. Aggregated Luxembourg headline CIT rate for corporate partners: 24.94% (CIT 17.00% + solidarity surcharge 1.19% + municipal business tax 6.75% in Luxembourg City).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Luxembourg statutory dividend WHT is 15% (LIR Art. 154). For an SCSp, cash or in-kind distributions to partners retain partnership distribution status, not dividend character, and no Luxembourg WHT is levied at the SCSp level; however, a corporate partner subsequently receiving a distribution of profits through an opaque interposed holding company would face the 15% rate unless the participation exemption (Art. 147) or Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by 31 May of the following year on paper; electronic filing automatically extends the deadline to 31 December of the following year. An SCSp is also obliged to report annually to the RCS (annual accounts/listing) within 7-11 months of year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same calendar-year rule for Luxembourg-resident corporate partners; non-resident corporate partners are not required to file a Luxembourg return if the SCSp derives no Luxembourg-source income attributed to them and no permanent establishment is present in Luxembourg.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg TP documentation (LIR Art. 56bis, EU Transfer Pricing Documentation rules, law of 28 December 2018): Local File mandatory where intra-Group controlled transactions exceed EUR 3,000,000 in the fiscal year; Master File mandatory where the MNE group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; a Country-by-Country Report applies when consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be submitted to the ACD within 30 days of demand and be contemporaneous; otherwise EUR 25,000 administrative fines apply.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style ESR but enforces economic substance through the GAAR of LIR Art. 6 and 56, the ATAD anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership doctrine in DTA and EU Directives, and the OECD merciless substance requirement. A media-buying SCSp must show real operational substance: employees with actual segment pricing authority, management meetings and contract negotiation in Luxembourg, risk assumption backed by capital, and strategic budgeting; a shell SCSp whose decisions are taken in a foreign partner's office will be disregarded and its profits reallocated to the place of effective management with source-tax denial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - fraudulent/incomplete declaration

Statutory sanction: Majoration de l'impôt: surcharge of 50%-100% of evaded CIT; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000; 10-year statute of limitation in fraud cases; CIF/SIF-based asset freezing; publication of name in the Luxembourg official gazette.

Director personal liability: GP partner of the SCSp personally, jointly and severally liable for partnership tax debts; liable for tribunal-restitution plus criminal imprisonment up to 5 years and travel bans; civil courts may pierce the veil under the Law of 10 August 1915 where the company is a façade.
Shell / façade SCSp and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation under LIR Art. 6 and 56; refusal of residence certificate; withholding reassessment at source; ATAD hybrid rules could treat the SCSp as opaque in one state and transparent in the other, causing double non-taxation penalties and 100% reassessment; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers of the corporate GP risk being adjudged personally liable for the recaptured taxes and loss of benefit; criminal proceedings for use of artificial arrangements (abus de droit) can trigger director disqualification and travel bans.
Transfer pricing mispricing in media arbitrage

Statutory sanction: ACD upward adjustment of the taxpayer's taxable base under LIR Art. 56bis; interest at 0.6% per month; when documented fraud, the same 50%-100% surcharge; denial of CbC protections where the CbC report existed with inconsistent data.

Director personal liability: Responsibility for establishing TP documentation is personal; management can be sued for ex post re-allocation plus interest; informed non-action triggers refusal of discharge (quitus) and personal recovery in the liquidation/administration of the company.
Money laundering / AML-CFT violations in traffic monetisation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under the Luxembourg AML law (Law of 12 November 2004 as amended) up to EUR 5,000,000 per breach and up to 10% of annual turnover; criminal sanctions on the entity and its managers: imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000; confiscation of illicit media proceeds.

Director personal liability: Compliance officer, director and de facto manager may be prosecuted individually regardless of corporate veil; penalties include personal fines, travel bans, disqualification from managerial functions, and extradition under the European Arrest Warrant where cross-border lead-gen proceeds are laundered.
CRS/FATCA and beneficial-ownership reporting failure

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg administrative fines per missing UBO declaration up to EUR 1,250 per month of delay for entities (or up to EUR 25,000 for repeated non-compliance); FATCA non-filing penalties under Luxembourg law; potential ban on distribution of partnership profits.

Director personal liability: Office of the RCS can issue penal orders against the GP; management retains liability for organizing proper UBO registry; directors face separate sanctions for the continuity of record and failure to remedy.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; official portal: https://rmigovernment.org (RMI Tax Self-Service)

E-government filing portal

Non-resident Business Corporations (BCs) under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act are not automatically issued a TIN. The BC files Form RMI-TIN-01 with the RTO, accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent confirmation, and a statutory declaration of non-resident status confirming the absence of RMI-source business activity. Where no RMI-source income is derived, TIN registration is optional; the BC typically obtains a Tax Exemption Certificate and Certificate of Good Standing from the Registrar of Corporations. RTO issuance target: 3–5 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – Non-resident BC is exempt from RMI income tax on all foreign-source income; any RMI-source business income (if conducted) is subject to 3% gross revenue tax under the Income Tax Act 1989.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under RMI domestic law. Available election: non-resident exempt status filing under the Business Corporations Act, which once accepted renders the BC exempt from RMI income tax on foreign-source income; no BEPS Action 2 hybrid rules adopted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – RMI levies no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; only customs import duty (Title 51, RMI Revised Code) applies, with no indirect tax imposed on exports.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST statute in force; therefore no economic turnover threshold triggering registration exists (official currency: US Dollar).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – RMI has no VAT/GST exported supply mechanism. Exports of granular elemental sulfur by a non-resident BC fall wholly outside the RMI indirect tax base (no output tax, no input VAT credits, no refund claim). Cargo in RMI transit or transshipment is not subject to import duty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands maintains full currency convertibility without exchange-control approvals. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted; the absence of a domestic currency eliminates FX matching requirements. Profit remittances may be executed as ordinary dividends unless bank-level AML review identifies UBO or source-of-funds gaps. No prior administrative permit is required for remittances, and there are no statutory capital or current account hurdles beyond routine sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Marshall Islands imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in USD, parked offshore, or repatriated freely. Banks may apply ordinary OFAC and UNSC sanctions screening on transaction narratives.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands Banking Commissioner (Ministry of Finance) — no domestic central bank exists; U.S. dollar is legal tender and the Federal Reserve serves as the monetary anchor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in the Republic of the Marshall Islands and cannot provide a local clearing account. They may serve only as auxiliary payment channels for vendor disbursements if the EMI’s correspondent bank accepts the corporate beneficiary package, but the primary treasury rail must be a fully licensed RMI commercial bank account to meet Banking Commissioner expectations.

0% for a qualifying non-resident business corporation (NRDC) deriving no RMI-source income; the nominal Marshall Islands corporate tax rate on RMI-source income otherwise is 3%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No RMI statutory transfer pricing documentation regime exists; the local file/master file threshold for RMI purposes is USD 0 (not applicable). If a parent or group is subject to OECD BEPS Action 13 rules in another jurisdiction, a master file may be required when worldwide consolidated revenue exceeds USD 750 million, and a local file may be required where intercompany transactions exceed the importing jurisdiction's domestic threshold, commonly USD 250,000–5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2020 apply to all entities registered under the BCCA that carry on a 'relevant activity', which includes shipping, distribution and service centres, financing and leasing, IP holding, and banking/insurance. A sulfur mineral ore trading and brokerage BC is within scope if it performs core income-generating activities from RMI. The entity must maintain adequate premises, full-time employees, and direction and management in RMI, and must file an annual ESR return. An NRDC with no RMI activity and no RMI-source income can submit a nil declaration, provided it does not claim any RMI economic substance. Non-compliance triggers a penalty of USD 20,000–50,000 and may lead to deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the 3% RMI corporate tax on all RMI-source income, plus 25% accuracy-related penalty and interest at 1% per month. Wilful evasion is punishable as a criminal offence under the RMI Income Tax Act by a fine of up to USD 10,000 and imprisonment for up to three years, together with revocation of the corporate exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false exemption declarations or knowingly divert RMI-source income are jointly and severally liable for the tax, interest, and penalties. Courts may impose civil restitution orders, freeze personal bank accounts, issue travel bans pending final assessment, and in egregious cases impose custodial sentences.
Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Counter-Trafficking and AML/CFT Act 2018, a corporate entity may be fined up to USD 100,000 per violation; individuals may be fined up to USD 150,000 and imprisoned for up to ten years. The Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze assets, and the vessel or cargo may be detained by port authorities. U.S. OFAC secondary sanctions may also apply to counterparties in the sulfur trade if the underlying voyage involves Iran, North Korea, Syria, or other sanctioned jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate in or enable laundering of shipping proceeds are subject to personal criminal liability, asset confiscation, extradition, and travel restrictions. Veil-piercing principles allow the court to look directly at the director's personal assets for restitution.
Shell Company and Misuse of Corporate Form

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may strike off or revoke the certificate of incorporation after repeated non-compliance. Civil monetary penalties up to USD 10,000 apply for failure to maintain a registered office and annual report. Fraudulent bills of lading also trigger liability under shipping and insurance law, including voiding P&I cover and imposing statutory damages.

Director personal liability: Courts will disregard the separate legal personality of the BC where the directors treated corporate assets as their own or operated the company for a fraudulent purpose. Directors may be held personally liable for all unpaid corporate debts, ordered to make restitution to deceived counterparties, prohibited from acting as directors of any RMI company, and in fraud cases sentenced to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Customs/Trade Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The importing country may impose transfer pricing adjustments, gross-up taxes, and penalties of 20–40% on the undeclared profit. RMI customs authorities may impose duties and penalties on under-declared cargo, seize the goods, and refer the matter for criminal investigation. A false declaration also exposes the company to civil claims by the buyer or vessel owner under the charterparty.

Director personal liability: Persons who knowingly sign false invoices or customs declarations are personally liable for penalties in the jurisdiction of import, including criminal fines and imprisonment. Under cross-border mutual legal assistance, personal assets may be frozen or confiscated, and directors may be subject to extradition and travel bans in connection with the fraudulent trade documentation.

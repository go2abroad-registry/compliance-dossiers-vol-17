Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (as amended), modeled on the Delaware General Corporation Law — Section 10 (Certificate of Incorporation), Section 14 (Registered Agent), Section 15 (Registered Office), Sections 31–32 (Bylaws), and Section 53 (Officers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Business Corporation may be formed with a single no-par share and unprescribed capital, and no escrow or depositary account is mandated for a non-resident domestic corporation.

Funding & unlock path: Office-only back-to-back trading requires no civil-defense permit; if a bonded warehouse or repackaging site is introduced, the Corporation must obtain a fire-safety certificate from the Fire Marshal (Ministry of Justice), a structural load inspection certificate from the Public Works engineering division, and an occupational safety clearance under the Marshall Islands Occupational Safety and Health regulations before taking physical possession.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Treasury and Customs — Revenue and Taxation Division, Majuro, Republic of the Marshall Islands; corporate registration is administered by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations exclusively through licensed registered agents (online registry portal: https://register-iri.com).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident Business Corporations are exempt from RMI taxation and are not issued a TIN. Procedure: (1) appoint a licensed RMI registered agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (27 MIRC) or the Non-Resident Business Corporations Act, including the non-resident designation; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing the corporate registration number, which serves as the statutory entity identifier; (4) if goods are physically exported or transshipped through RMI ports, obtain a Customs Importer/Exporter Registration from the Customs Division; (5) if any RMI-source business is nonetheless conducted, register for the Business License and Wage and Salary Withholding with the Revenue and Taxation Division. No VAT registration exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Non-resident Business Corporations are exempt from RMI taxation and are not issued a TIN. Procedure: (1) appoint a licensed RMI registered agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act (27 MIRC) or the Non-Resident Business Corporations Act, including the non-resident designation; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation bearing the corporate registration number, which serves as the statutory entity identifier; (4) if goods are physically exported or transshipped through RMI ports, obtain a Customs Importer/Exporter Registration from the Customs Division; (5) if any RMI-source business is nonetheless conducted, register for the Business License and Wage and Salary Withholding with the Revenue and Taxation Division. No VAT registration exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No local check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists. Non-resident tax-exempt status is achieved through a declaratory statement in the Articles of Incorporation and the factual abstention from RMI-source business. The RMI maintains no anti-hybrid regime and no double tax treaty network. For U.S. federal purposes the entity may independently file IRS Form 8832; the RMI imposes no countervailing entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT/GST/sales tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs import tariffs under the RMI Tariff Act and, for domestic operators, the 3% gross receipts business tax. Exports are entirely outside the indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT registration threshold. Any exporter moving goods through RMI customs must hold a Customs Business Registration without reference to turnover; the 3% gross receipts business tax applies only to RMI-domestic operations and has no de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The RMI levies no VAT/GST, so no zero-rating mechanism, input VAT credit, or export refund system exists. Dehydrated vegetables and spices exported from or transshipped through the RMI are outside the indirect tax base; no export duty applies to agricultural or spice products. The RMI uses the U.S. dollar as legal tender, imposes no exchange controls, and prescribes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a documentary credit and pipeline covenant.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (Manifest)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin (for GSP or bilateral preference claims at destination port)

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate exists. Customs declarations are accepted in paper form through the Customs Division, Majuro; no ASYCUDA or electronic single-window system is operational for general cargo.

No VAT clawback applies because no VAT exists. Under the RMI Customs Act, false or fraudulent export declarations attract monetary fines up to three times the value of duty or goods evaded, possible confiscation of cargo, and director/officer liability. If an audit determines that the non-resident corporation conducted RMI-source business, the tax exemption is revoked retroactively, exposing the entity to the 3% gross receipts business tax, 12% wage withholding, and business license fees for the open assessment period (typically three years), plus civil penalties and docketed tax liens.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility in a dollarized economy: there is no domestic currency, so no conversion obligation, no central-bank approval, and no registration applies to trade receipts, profit remittance, dividends, loan repayments, or capital repatriation. Remittance hurdles are limited to bank-level compliance: OFAC sanctions screening, FATF-related counterparty due diligence, and source-of-funds evidence. The Non-Resident Business Corporation's tax-exempt zone status removes RMI gross-revenue taxation of foreign-source dehydrated-spice export income, leaving profit remittance fully clear of exchange-control or fiscal impediments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds in dehydrated vegetables and spices; receipts may remain offshore or be held in USD accounts in the RMI. Only standard bank-side CTR/STR transaction-monitoring thresholds apply; no exchange-control reporting is required.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) — prudential regulator of licensed banks under the Banking Act 2012. The RMI has no domestic central bank or foreign-exchange administration; the U.S. dollar is sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, so U.S. monetary policy (Federal Reserve System) and U.S.-dollar clearing constitute the de facto monetary framework.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) imposed on gross revenue derived from sources within the Republic of the Marshall Islands for non-resident business corporations; 0% effective rate on all foreign-source trading income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory RMI transfer pricing documentation law exists. Prudent non-resident compliance benchmark: prepare Local File and Master File documentation where annual related-party gross turnover exceeds USD 1,000,000 or where related-party payments for goods, royalties, or interest exceed USD 500,000 per fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations for business corporations, but the jurisdiction remains subject to OECD and Asia-Pacific Group transparency standards. A non-resident BC must maintain a registered agent and registered office in the RMI, keep a board of directors that makes decisions offshore, and avoid any management, control, or physical activity inside the RMI. Conducting board meetings or personnel activities in Majuro or Ebeye may create RMI-source income and trigger the 3% GRT; cost-plus resale margins on related-party dehydrated spice trades must be supported by arm's-length benchmarking.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid GRT plus a fraud penalty of 25% and monthly interest of 1%; criminal fines up to USD 25,000; imprisonment up to 3 years; cancellation of the BC's registration by the Registrar of Corporations.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a false return are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties; courts may impose travel bans and exit control orders; the corporate veil is pierced where the corporation is a mere instrumentality of the director, exposing personal assets to seizure.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and dissolution under the Business Corporations Act; revocation of the registered agent appointment; fines for carrying on business under a false declaration; inclusion in international harmful tax regime listings with consequential correspondent banking restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for misrepresentation to the Registrar; civil restitution of foreign-asset transfers made through the shell; criminal prosecution for false statements; personal travel bans and freezing of personal assets where the entity is found to be a sham.
Anti-Money Laundering / UBO Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: AML/CFT administrative fines up to USD 100,000; freezing orders against bank accounts; suspension of the BC's registration; criminal prosecution under the RMI Criminal Code for money laundering carrying imprisonment up to 10 years; confiscation of proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for wilful AML breaches; personal asset forfeiture; disqualification from managing any RMI entity; international arrest warrants and travel bans issued through bilateral cooperation channels.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and Invoice Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Fraud-based penalties and criminal fines under the RMI Penal Code; transaction reversals and civil forfeiture; exclusion from future LC issuance; referral to financial intelligence units and foreign tax authorities; extradition-eligible money laundering charges with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure fraudulent trade documentation are personally liable for civil restitution to injured counterparties and banks; personal fines equal to the laundered amount; seizure of personal and corporate property; travel bans pending extradition where cross-border proceeds are traced.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Default and Misreporting

Statutory sanction: While the RMI has no dedicated TP penalty law, misreporting triggers the Income Tax Act's general anti-avoidance provisions: reassessment of omitted gross revenue, a 25% penalty, 1% monthly interest, and fines up to USD 25,000; documentary inconsistencies may also constitute an offence of false declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors must demonstrate good-faith diligent preparation of documentation; default exposes them to personal liability for the corporation's tax penalties in the event of insolvency; deliberate inconsistency attracts criminal liability, civil restitution, and travel bans under fraud statutes.

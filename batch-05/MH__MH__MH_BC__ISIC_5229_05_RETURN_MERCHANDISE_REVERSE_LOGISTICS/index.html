Applicable AML statute: Business Corporations Act 1990 (Title 24, Marshall Islands Revised Code), Associations Law 1990, Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act 2020 (as amended), Marshall Islands Registrar of Corporations AML/CFT Regulations 2021, and the Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters Act
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Accepted with conditions: The Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act requires every corporation to maintain a registered office and registered agent physically located in the Marshall Islands. A virtual office address is permissible as an administrative address for correspondence, statutory notices, and mail forwarding, but it does not supplant the statutory registered agent requirement. For ISIC 5229 reverse logistics operations, the company may conduct all substantive operational activities outside the Marshall Islands while maintaining only the registered agent office locally. Virtual office arrangements are generally accepted for non-resident corporations, but the company must keep accurate records accessible through its registered agent; shell-company indicators are mitigated by the legitimate reverse-logistics business purpose and the appointment of a licensed registered agent.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Authority (RTA) of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, operating under the Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services; online tax services via the RTA e-Services portal (rta.rmi); customs administration through the RMI Customs Service on the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window platform.

E-government filing portal

Under the Revenue and Taxation Administration Act 2014, a TIN is mandatory only for persons deriving assessable income from RMI sources. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) with no RMI-source income and no RMI trade or business is not required to file income-tax registration; where a bank or counterparty requires a TIN, the BC's Registered Agent files the RTA registration application with the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, and a director's certificate of non-residency, obtaining an RMI TIN expeditiously. No VAT ID exists because the RMI imposes no VAT/GST. If the BC handles physical goods entering the RMI, it must additionally register as an importer with the RMI Customs Service and obtain an ASYCUDA identifier for customs (import tax) purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Under the Revenue and Taxation Administration Act 2014, a TIN is mandatory only for persons deriving assessable income from RMI sources. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. A domestic BC incorporated under the Business Corporations Act may voluntarily elect non-resident corporation status by filing a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation declaration with the Registrar of Corporations through its Registered Agent, thereby confining RMI income-tax exposure strictly to RMI-source income. RMI law provides no hybrid-mismatch elections; the entity's classification for foreign tax purposes is governed by the foreign jurisdiction and has no independent RMI tax effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales and Use Tax — the Republic of the Marshall Islands levies no national value-added, goods-and-services, or sales-and-use tax; the only border indirect charge is the RMI Import Tax (generally 2% ad valorem under the National Revenue Act 1990) and specific environmental/fuel surcharges applicable to physical imports.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST turnover threshold exists. Income-tax registration is triggered by derivation of income sourced within the RMI (territorial-source principle) or by carrying on a trade or business in the RMI, regardless of turnover; customs import-tax registration arises on any physical importation into the RMI with no minimum turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not apply because no VAT is imposed. The non-resident BC instead obtains de facto 0% RMI tax on cross-border reverse-logistics and returned-goods grading by satisfying: (i) all services physically performed outside the RMI and remunerated by non-resident principals, so income is non-RMI-source; (ii) no office, warehouse, agent, or permanent establishment inside the RMI; (iii) revenue derived solely from offshore fulfillment and grading operations; and (iv) no RMI importation or local market sale of goods. Where returned merchandise physically transits an RMI port in bond for re-export, the Import Tax does not apply under the transit/export-drawback provisions of the National Revenue Act 1990.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) for any goods physically exported or re-exported from RMI territory
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing the international movement of returned merchandise from an RMI port (Majuro or Ebeye) to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an RMI-licensed bank, or correspondent-bank FX credit advice, evidencing the actual receipt of export proceeds in the RMI where repatriation is required

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists for RMI taxpayers. RMI Customs requires electronic declaration processing through the ASYCUDA World single window (customs export/import declarations, commercial invoices, and bills of lading/air waybills must be filed electronically). The RTA e-Services portal supports voluntary online income-tax and withholding-tax filing and payment; no API-based fiscal invoice clearance is imposed on offshore reverse-logistics service providers.

Because the RMI has no VAT, fraudulent export zero-rating claims are inapplicable; however, false representations to obtain non-resident exemption, import-tax drawback, or transit/bond relief are subject to: (i) RTA audit under the Revenue and Taxation Administration Act 2014, including reopening of returns and issuance of protective assessments; (ii) retrospective clawback of any exemption, drawback, or refund improperly obtained, plus statutory interest and late-payment penalties; (iii) monetary penalties and forfeiture of goods under the Customs Act 1991 for customs declaration inaccuracies; and (iv) criminal prosecution of the entity and its officers for tax evasion or perjury in the event of fraudulent documentation, carrying fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible; there are no exchange controls, no convertibility licensing, and no withholding or remittance tax for a Non-Resident Business Corporation (BC). Profit distributions, share-capital returns, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely. The only material hurdles are correspondent-bank OFAC/FATCA screening and enhanced due diligence arising from RMI's presence on the EU List of Non-Cooperative Jurisdictions; FATCA exposure is neutralized by the BC's Active NFFE W-8BEN-E status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply. RMI law does not require surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion of export proceeds; receipts from reverse-logistics grading, recovered-merchandise sales, and logistics fees may be retained indefinitely in USD or any offshore currency without filing.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no conventional central bank. Prudential supervision is exercised by the Banking Commissioner of the Ministry of Finance under the Banking Act 1999 (as amended); AML/CFT supervision is vested in the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act. The U.S. dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender, with monetary policy and currency issuance effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve pursuant to the RMI Currency Act and the Compact of Free Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium_Feasibility with mandatory legal safeguards. Airwallex, the designated target rail, is not licensed in the RMI and cannot provide a local Majuro settlement account; onboarding proceeds through the Hong Kong/Singapore/Australia entities using the certificate of incorporation, registered-agent certificate, franchise tax receipt, UBO register, W-8BEN-E (Active NFFE), and a 12-month cash-flow model. Because the RMI is on the EU List of Non-Cooperative Jurisdictions, Airwallex applies enhanced review, but the designated Low_Risk_Standard AML tier of ISIC 5229 reverse logistics avoids automatic declines. A dual-rail structure is mandatory: the BOMI operational account retains the liquidity anchor (minimum USD 50,000) while Airwallex executes local-US ACH collection and FX conversion; no RMI regulatory approval is required for using an offshore EMI, but the board must pre-authorize throughput limits and quarterly reconciliation to BOMI.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident tax-exempt corporation domiciled in an EU-listed non-cooperative jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-resident tax-exempt corporation domiciled in an EU-listed non-cooperative jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Certificate of good standing; registered-agent certificate; full text of the Business Corporations Act tax-exemption for non-resident domestic corporations (proving the EU listing is a tax-policy artifact); APG mutual-evaluation summary and OECD Global Forum exchange-of-information rating (RMI is not on the FATF high-risk or grey lists); RMI economic-substance declaration; executed reverse-logistics contracts and financial statements evidencing real cross-border operation.
Opaque or bearer-share ownership structure typical of offshore vehicles

Root cause: Opaque or bearer-share ownership structure typical of offshore vehicles

Defense evidence: Restated articles and complete share register showing registered shares only; UBO declaration for all individuals with 10% or greater ownership, with passport copies; corporate-ownership chart prepared by the registered agent; if bearer shares exist, custody confirmation from an authorized financial institution and an irrevocable board resolution converting them to registered form before account opening.
High-volume, low-value refund and chargeback traffic disproportionate to declared revenue

Root cause: High-volume, low-value refund and chargeback traffic disproportionate to declared revenue

Defense evidence: Executed marketplace and 3PL fulfillment agreements; granular RMA-to-refund reconciliation dataset; 12-month cash-flow projection with projected refund ratios; ISIC 5229 grading and condition reports documenting value-add; board resolution segregating customer refunds within the operational account and prohibiting use of capital escrow for refunds.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital. The SCSp is exempt from statutory legal capital requirements; the partnership deed freely fixes the agreed capital, which may be contributed in cash, in kind, or as a mere promise to contribute. No escrow account is required under Luxembourg law unless contractually or regulatorily imposed (e.g., CSSF initial capital of EUR 50,000 or equivalent professional indemnity cover for a regulated Professional of the Financial Sector).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety attestation for office occupancy issued upon favorable inspection by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) and the competent municipal fire-prevention service; structural and evacuation-load compliance per Luxembourg construction regulations; workplace-safety clearance verified by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Labour Code, including exit routes, first-aid equipment, electrical safety, ergonomic conditions, and annual staff safety training.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD / Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes - portal impotsdirects.public.lu; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties - portal aed.public.lu; unified electronic filing via MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SCSp: (1) register the SCSp in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), which automatically issues the 13-digit national identification number (NIF) used for income tax formalities; (2) file VAT registration with the AEDT (form 664) to obtain the LU + 8-digit VAT identification number; non-established taxable persons must register via a Luxembourg fiscal representative before supplying services in Luxembourg - no de minimis turnover threshold applies to non-residents, whereas residents benefit from the EUR 35,000 small-business franchise; (3) nominate a local tax agent as correspondence recipient if no permanent establishment is created.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp has no legal personality and is fiscally transparent by default - income is taxed at partner level pro rata; entity-level corporate income tax and municipal business tax do not apply; nevertheless, the SCSp is an independent VAT taxable person and may elect into transparency or entity-level taxation as described below. — For a foreign-owned SCSp: (1) register the SCSp in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), which automatically issues the 13-digit national identification number (NIF) used for income tax formalities; (2) file VAT registration with the AEDT (form 664) to obtain the LU + 8-digit VAT identification number; non-established taxable persons must register via a Luxembourg fiscal representative before supplying services in Luxembourg - no de minimis turnover threshold applies to non-residents, whereas residents benefit from the EUR 35,000 small-business franchise; (3) nominate a local tax agent as correspondence recipient if no permanent establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the SCSp has no legal personality and is fiscally transparent by default - income is taxed at partner level pro rata; entity-level corporate income tax and municipal business tax do not apply; nevertheless, the SCSp is an independent VAT taxable person and may elect into transparency or entity-level taxation as described below.

Available election forms

(1) Opt-in to full Luxembourg corporate income tax / municipal business tax liability under Article 168 of the Income Tax Law (LIR) - binding election by the partnership with partner consent, valid for five years, used to avoid hybrid mismatch outcomes and to access treaty benefits; (2) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the VAT Law for related Luxembourg entities; (3) ATAD/hybrid-mismatch alignment election under Articles 168ter-168septies LIR to re-characterise payments or deny double non-taxation; (4) application of the participation exemption regime (Article 166 LIR) for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (Article 5 of the VAT Law small-business exemption) for Luxembourg-established suppliers; for non-established foreign suppliers the threshold is zero - any supply taking place in Luxembourg requires immediate VAT registration; M&A boutique advisory services to Luxembourg-established clients therefore trigger VAT registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0%) treatment applies to: (i) export of goods dispatched or transported to a non-EU destination (Article 29 of the VAT Law); (ii) intra-Community supplies to a taxable person holding a valid EU VAT ID (Article 155 of the VAT Law); (iii) services ancillary to export transport. Cross-border M&A advisory success fees are outside Luxembourg's VAT scope under the general reverse-charge/B2B place-of-supply rule (customer establishment), and therefore neither Luxembourg VAT nor zero-rating documentation applies; if a foreign client is a private consumer in Luxembourg, the 17% standard rate applies. The SCSp must maintain partner-level allocation evidence because the transparency regime shifts VAT deduction and recovery to partners.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document) processed in the electronic export control system and exit confirmation from the EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and signed engagement/success-fee agreement
  • SWIFT inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of consideration
  • Customer's valid EU VAT identification number (for intra-Community supplies to taxable persons)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate is in force for 2025. Mandatory e-invoicing applies for B2G supplies to the Luxembourg central government (law of 16 July 2021, operational since 2023) through the Peppol network (Peppol BIS format); B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary but substantially supported; the SCSp must be Peppol-ready for public-sector M&A mandates and should retain complete audit trails for all success-fee invoices.

The AEDT may reassess VAT for five years (ten years in case of fraud). Unsubstantiated zero-rating or intra-Community claims trigger recovery of underpaid VAT, default interest at 0.8% per month, and administrative fines up to EUR 12,500 per infringement under Article 64 of the VAT Law; criminal tax fraud (Article 62) carries imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 25,000. For the SCSp, input VAT clawbacks must be borne by the partners per profit-sharing ratios, and fraudulent export documentation can void the entire engagement fee deduction. Luxembourg has no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a documentary best practice used by AEDT examiners for inward remittance evidence and by the banking AML framework, but prolonged non-repatriation may trigger transfer-pricing or treaty-abuse review.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility for euro-zone and non-EEA transfers, in line with Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is freely convertible; there are no profit-remittance restrictions, no foreign-exchange licensing, and no requirement to channel transfers through authorised dealers. A Luxembourg SCSp is tax-transparent (non-fiscale) for income tax purposes, so distributions of success-fee profits to non-resident Limited Partners are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax unless the partner is in a non-cooperative jurisdiction blacklisted by the EU. Practical impediments arise only from AML transparency: banks require the RBE beneficial-owner register, source-of-funds evidence, and a legal opinion before wiring fees to third parties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds, no repatriation deadline, and no obligation to convert foreign-currency service fees (including M&A success fees under ISIC 7020) into euro. The sole administrative duty is the BCL balance-of-payments statistical return for individual transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 per counterparty per calendar quarter, reported via the BCL's BDP (Balance of Payments) web interface using the ECB BoP classification for services income. Funds may be retained in USD, GBP, CHF, or any other account currency indefinitely.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) retains supervisory competence for AML/CFT and banking conduct. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency; capital-movement surveillance derives from the Law of 27 December 1990 on the liberalisation of capital movements and the ECB Statistical Regulation (EU) 2013/2707, with BCL acting as the national compiler of Balance of Payments (BoP) statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — approved only for operational treasury overflow and client-fee receipt, never for the capital-escrow layer. A Luxembourg SCSp may legally open a payment account with a CSSF-licensed Electronic Money Institution (Law of 10 August 2018) or with a passported EU/EEA EMI under the PSD2 regime (Law of 10 November 2009). Offshore digital EMIs from third countries (e.g., non-EEA jurisdictions) cannot provide payment accounts in Luxembourg without a CSSF-authorised branch; using one would constitute the unauthorised carrying-out of payment services under Article 30 of the Law of 10 November 2009 and a breach of CSSF Circular 12/552 on the provision of payment services. Even a passported EMI will be treated by tier-1 banks as an 'unregulated intermediary' for the purposes of source-of-funds verification, so the SCSp must maintain a primary euro liquidity buffer at a fully licensed credit institution with a traditional SWIFT BIC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident Limited Partners are cleared via SWIFT MT103 (for non-EEA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EEA/EU partners) to the SCSp's segregated account at a BCL-regulated bank. The required payment reference block must read: 'LU SCSp [name] – Capital Contribution – [LP legal name] – [qtr/year] – [contribution notice ID]'. The bank verifies the remitter against the RBE register and applies an early-warning AML block on any discrepancy. For BCL statistical compliance, the contribution is reported in the quarterly BDP return under the ECB BoP financial-account item 'Direct investment – Equity capital – inward' (code 51011 in the Eurostat v2 classification), regardless of whether the partnership is treated as quasi-transparent for accounting purposes. A formal capital-call notice (Fonds d'appel) signed by the GP must be pre-delivered to the bank, and the final credit must not exceed the aggregate commitments registered in the partnership agreement, otherwise the surplus is redeemed pending clarification.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade settlements is SWIFT MT103 (or MT202COV for cover payments through a EUR/USD correspondent chain) for non-EEA suppliers and SEPA Instant/SEPA CT for EEA counterparties. For high-value acquisition escrows, a commercial documentary Letter of Credit may be opened under UCP 600, or a demand guarantee under URDG 758, with the bank's trade-finance desk. Success-fee disbursements from the SCSp to the advisory boutique (ASBL/EURL, if distinct) are executed as MT103 with the purpose code 'General Services – Professional and consulting fees' and must be supported by a fee invoice and the engagement letter; the beneficiary's account must hold the same beneficial ownership or the transaction is escalated to a dual-control approval by the bank's transaction-monitoring unit. USD-denominated wires route via BGL BNP Paribas' New York clearing (Fedwire/CHIPS) or via CITI/BONY correspondent accounts at Société Générale Bank & Trust, with a per-wire correspondent cost of EUR 6–25 and a potential FX compression fee if a currency conversion is embedded.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) to fix the euro value of hard-currency success fees and escrow balances, transacted under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a bank holding a Luxembourg credit-institution licence.FX swaps (rollover of a forward position through simultaneous spot-thus-forward transaction) for the conversion of non-euro capital contributions into EUR and the temporary equalisation of reserve currencies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted-currency markets (e.g., INR, IDR, PHP) permitted only where the underlying M&A client operates in a regulated-currency jurisdiction and the SCSp can demonstrate a genuine hedging need; speculative NDF exposure is prohibited under CSSF Circular 19/707 and the GP's investment-policy statement.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (EUR/USD floating-to-floating) limited to the duration of the underlying acquisition financing and with a notional capped at the expected fee pool, requiring a board resolution of the GP and pre-approval by the bank's credit-risk committee.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium — approved only for operational treasury overflow and client-fee receipt, never for the capital-escrow layer. A Luxembourg SCSp may legally open a payment account with a CSSF-licensed Electronic Money Institution (Law of 10 August 2018) or with a passported EU/EEA EMI under the PSD2 regime (Law of 10 November 2009). Offshore digital EMIs from third countries (e.g., non-EEA jurisdictions) cannot provide payment accounts in Luxembourg without a CSSF-authorised branch; using one would constitute the unauthorised carrying-out of payment services under Article 30 of the Law of 10 November 2009 and a breach of CSSF Circular 12/552 on the provision of payment services. Even a passported EMI will be treated by tier-1 banks as an 'unregulated intermediary' for the purposes of source-of-funds verification, so the SCSp must maintain a primary euro liquidity buffer at a fully licensed credit institution with a traditional SWIFT BIC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million group revenue (for Master File) and EUR 1 million related-party transactions (for Local File) trigger mandatory TP documentation. For SCSp, the partnership must prepare a Local File if its related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million, and a Master File if the group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: SCSp is not subject to Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as it is a partnership, but it must demonstrate sufficient economic substance (e.g., office, staff, decision-making) to avoid being classified as a shell company under Luxembourg anti-abuse rules and to maintain treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 0.6% per month. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of the entity as taxable, and imposition of a 10% penalty on the tax due. The entity may be struck off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the company's tax liabilities if they fail to ensure substance; civil penalties and potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion.
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; suspension of license; criminal prosecution for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines up to €1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful AML violations; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment, and interest. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they approved the non-compliant pricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges.

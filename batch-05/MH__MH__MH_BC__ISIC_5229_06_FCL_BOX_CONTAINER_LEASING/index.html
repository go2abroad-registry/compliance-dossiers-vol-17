E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident Business Corporation is not required to obtain a domestic TIN or VAT/VAT ID if it conducts no revenue-generating activities within the Republic of the Marshall Islands. If a Marshall Islands-source revenue obligation is triggered (e.g., through domestic sales or services), the entity must file a short registration application with the Tax and Revenue Office, which will issue a local revenue identification number. There is no VAT registration process because no VAT system exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands formally uses the USD as its official currency and does not maintain an independent exchange rate mechanism. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible in practice because the domestic currency is the USD itself. There is no separate RMI currency to convert, and no statutory exchange control approval is required for capital inflows or profit remittances. Profit repatriation by non-resident entities is not subject to withholding taxes or administrative hurdles under RMI tax-exempt corporation rules, provided the entity maintains good standing under the Business Corporations Act. Practical obstacles may arise only from correspondent banking due diligence, not from RMI law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or designated timelines for the repatriation or surrender of export receipts. Because the legal tender is USD, receipts earned in USD from container leasing or asset leasing transactions are already in domestic currency. For receipts earned in a foreign currency, no RMI regulation obligates conversion, repatriation, or deposit into a local account; however, for corporate tax transparency and banking relationship integrity, it is recommended that all lease receivables be routed through a properly documented RMI corporate account or a correspondent account disclosed to the remitting bank.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the United States dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender. Banking supervision is exercised by the Banking Commissioner acting under the Banking Act of 1988, with financial sector oversight coordinated through the Ministry of Finance. For USD clearing and monetary policy matters, the US Federal Reserve System acts as the de facto monetary authority through the currency board-like arrangement, while no RMI central bank exercises exchange control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. The Marshall Islands has no comprehensive digital banking or electronic money institution licensing regime comparable to the EU or Hong Kong. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment platforms are not supervised by the RMI Banking Commissioner and cannot provide accounts in the name of an RMI corporation as if they were a locally licensed bank. Such platforms may still be used as payment channels only if they perform full correspondent KYC on the RMI entity and maintain a valid money services business license in the relevant jurisdictions. Because foreign EMIs frequently refuse to onboard RMI-registered non-resident companies, the primary clearing relationship should be a tier-1 USD bank with Fedwire/CHIPS access, with an EMI used merely for ancillary receivables, not as the main treasury rail.

Unverified ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain: the bank cannot determine that the ultimate controlling party is not conducting tax evasion, trade-based money laundering, or sanctions circumvention.

Root cause: Unverified ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain: the bank cannot determine that the ultimate controlling party is not conducting tax evasion, trade-based money laundering, or sanctions circumvention.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for an RMI Business Corporation should be routed as USD wire transfers through the SWIFT network into the company's RMI commercial bank account or into a designated USD correspondent account at a US clearing bank. The remittance must include accurate beneficiary bank details, the beneficiary SWIFT/BIC code, and the full legal name of the RMI entity. For capital contributions, use the SWIFT MT103 field 50 for remitter information, field 59 for beneficiary account, and in field 72 or 77B include the purpose code

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold; the Marshall Islands imposes no transfer pricing documentation obligations, no Local File or Master File requirement, no Country-by-Country Reporting, and no contemporaneous TP penalty regime for Business Corporations.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2019 apply to resident Business Corporations conducting relevant activities. Container box leasing falls squarely within the 'Financing and Leasing' relevant activity and, where the containers are deployed on owned or chartered vessels, may also be characterized under the 'Shipping' relevant activity. The BC must satisfy the Core Income Generation Activity (CIGA) test and be directed and managed in the Marshall Islands, meaning that board meetings, strategic decision-making, and substantive leasing functions must occur in-country. An annual ESR notification and, where engaged in relevant activity, an ESR return must be filed through the registered agent; a purely passive, substance-light leasing vehicle is in direct breach. The tax-exempt status of the BC is preserved only if the ESR filing confirms full compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Offshore Structure

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may recharacterize the BC as a tax-resident sham, disregard its separate personality for tax purposes, and assess full corporate income tax on the entire container-leasing margin, together with interest and penalties typically ranging from 100% to 200% of the assessed tax; secondary sanctions include cross-border information exchange under the existing TIEA and the issuance of bank account disclosure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders who dominate the sham enterprise may be held personally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax, subjected to asset-tracing and freezing orders in the foreign jurisdiction, issued travel bans, and criminally prosecuted for tax evasion with custodial sentences in the state where the control and management actually occur.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of US$10,000 for initial failure to file the ESR return, additional penalties of US$10,000 per month or part thereof during continuing default, and escalation for repeated non-compliance; information sharing with foreign tax authorities under international exchange agreements; ultimate loss of the tax-exempt characterization of the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign false ESR declarations or cause the corporation to omit the ESR filing are personally liable to fines; the court may impose a term of imprisonment of up to one year for knowingly supplying false information, order disqualification of directors from acting as officers of any Marshall Islands corporation, and issue travel bans pending the payment of regulatory fines.
AML/CFT and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2000 (as amended), penalties include fines of up to US$500,000 and imprisonment of up to 25 years in aggravated cases; the competent authority may issue freezing and confiscation orders against the corporation's lease receivables, revoke the corporation's registration, and transmit suspicious activity reports to foreign financial intelligence units.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and compliance officers are individually criminally liable for AML/CFT failures; imprisonment is a real prospect, with concomitant travel bans, extradition exposure to requesting states, and personal confiscation of proceeds of the unlawful conduct.
Shell Company and Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporation Act, the Registrar may administratively dissolve the BC for continued default, causing the name to revert to the State and the corporation to cease existing as a legal person; in foreign legal proceedings, the court may disregard the corporate form where the BC is found to be a mere facade, imposing the corporation's liabilities directly on the principals and invalidating the limited liability shield.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil to hold shareholders and directors jointly and severally liable for the company's leasing debts, charter claims, and tax liabilities; personal civil restitution orders, asset seizure, travel bans for the duration of proceedings, and imprisonment for fraudulent trading or contempt of court orders are all available sanctions.

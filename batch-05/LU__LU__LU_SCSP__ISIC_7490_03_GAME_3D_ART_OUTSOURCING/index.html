Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs (RBE), filing via the eJustice/MyGuichet electronic portals.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (notably by the Law of 12 July 2013 inserting Articles 103-1 to 103-8 on the Société en Commandite Spéciale and consolidated by the Law of 1 August 2023); Law of 19 December 2002 on the RCS and RESA; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Contrat de Société (Special Limited Partnership Agreement) registered through the RCS electronic filing module for partnerships of type 'société en commandite spéciale', with publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; capital is freely contractually determined in the partnership agreement, and no escrow account, notarial capital verification or bank attestation is legally mandated (a bank capital certificate may be requested only by commercial counterparties).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) covering building fire classification, emergency exits and extinguishing systems; structural load assessment for server and 3D rendering racks by an approved engineering office; workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering ergonomics, artificial lighting, ventilation and first-aid provisions for the studio floor.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an SCSp; capital is freely contractually determined in the partnership agreement, and no escrow account, notarial capital verification or bank attestation is legally mandated (a bank capital certificate may be requested only by commercial counterparties).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; single digital gateway: guichet.lu / myGuichet.lu; AEDT VAT e-filing portal: pbe.aedt.public.lu

E-government filing portal

1) Notarially incorporate the SCSp; register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS), which assigns the statutory 13-digit matricule (national business/TIN number) automatically transmitted to the ACD. 2) For direct tax, the SCSp is registered as a fiscally transparent partnership; no entity-level CIT/MBT/NWT filing is required—partners report attributable income directly. 3) For VAT, file AEDT registration form (Formulaire 401) before commencing taxable activity; EU-resident partners/managers may register directly, while non-EU-established persons must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative (Formulaire 402). 4) The AEDT issues a separate 11-character LU VAT number; registration is finalized online with LuxTrust electronic signature. 5) A passive SCSp with no taxable Luxembourg activity is not obliged to obtain a VAT number, but must do so for any taxable supplies, intra-EU acquisitions, or to exercise input VAT recovery.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent (Law of 12 July 2013): profits flow through to partners who are taxed at their individual/corporate level; no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax is levied on the partnership itself. Territorial scope: Luxembourg-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate. For VAT, however, the SCSp is an independent taxable person and must charge, file, and remit VAT on its own account when carrying on economic activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The SCSp is statutorily fiscally transparent (Law of 12 July 2013): profits flow through to partners who are taxed at their individual/corporate level; no corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax is levied on the partnership itself. Territorial scope: Luxembourg-resident partners are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident partners are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a Luxembourg permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate. For VAT, however, the SCSp is an independent taxable person and must charge, file, and remit VAT on its own account when carrying on economic activity. The SCSp is not entitled to Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment; only the Luxembourg small-business VAT exemption (franchise) may apply below the turnover threshold.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for Luxembourg domestic purposes: the SCSp cannot irrevocably or temporarily elect to be treated as an opaque corporation under Luxembourg direct tax law. Relevant elections and classification mechanisms: (a) foreign investors may classify the SCSp as opaque in their home jurisdiction, triggering ATAD anti-hybrid mismatch rules (Articles 168quater/168quinquies LIR) that deny deductions for certain hybrid payments; (b) the SCSp may elect to form or join a Luxembourg VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 59bis of the VAT Law (effective 1 January 2023 reform) subject to prior AEDT authorization and close financial, economic, and organizational links; (c) the SCSp may elect the VAT small-business exemption (franchise) under Article 57 of the VAT Law if annual taxable turnover is below EUR 35,000; (d) standard partnership elections under the LIR allow partners to make joint accounting-method and depreciation elections binding on the partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business exemption (franchise d'assujettissement, Article 57 VAT Law); no registration threshold for non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg or receiving intra-EU acquisitions subject to reverse charge; EUR 10,000 annual threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C telecommunications/broadcasting/electronic services before Luxembourg VAT applies (OSS Union scheme may be used to remit tax at the customer's member state rate); registration is mandatory regardless of turnover for entities exercising the right to deduct input VAT or providing services subject to reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 55 of the VAT Law, provided the goods physically leave the EU and are evidenced by a customs export declaration (MRN). Intra-Community supplies of goods to EU VAT-registered customers are zero-rated with the customer's valid VAT number validated via VIES and proper listing in the recapitulative statement. For the core 3D game-art outsourcing activity (digital delivery of modelling, sculpting, rigging, and asset-licensing services): (a) B2B supplies to a customer established outside Luxembourg are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under Article 58 (place of supply = customer's establishment) and invoiced without VAT; (b) B2B supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are subject to reverse charge in the customer's member state and invoiced as 'reverse charge' at 0% Luxembourg VAT; (c) B2C supplies of e-services to EU consumers are taxable in Luxembourg at 17% (or via OSS at the consumer's member-state rate); (d) B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside the scope. Substantiation must demonstrate the customer's business establishment, contractual IP-transfer/licence terms, actual digital or physical delivery, and consistency between payment flows, invoices, and service records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD with MRN from the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency, Administration des douanes et accises) for physical shipments
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial contract and work order detailing the 3D asset creation, IP licence/assignment, and delivery milestones
  • Commercial invoice with explicit '0% export zero-rated' or 'reverse charge' VAT wording and the customer's VAT number (VIES validation printout for intra-EU supplies)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident customer
  • Digital delivery log, P2P handover report, or hash-stamped file transfer record for electronically delivered game assets
  • Customer's certificate of incorporation, business register extract, or VAT registration certificate supporting establishment status
  • Work completion certificate signed by the customer

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or mandatory digital stamping regime exists in Luxembourg. B2G e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network has been mandatory for suppliers to central government entities since 1 January 2025 (Law of 16 August 2023), with phased extension to all public-sector contracting authorities. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary under EU rules, but invoices must be issued electronically or on paper within 15 days of the taxable supply and preserved for 10 years in a machine-readable format. Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) reporting is required by the ACD/AEDT for audited taxpayers in phased waves from fiscal year 2024 (largest taxpayers) through fiscal year 2026 (all VAT classes), covering accounting and VAT data files submitted through the ACD digital platform; VAT returns (Form 104) and recapitulative statements (ESA) are filed monthly/quarterly/annually by turnover category with mandatory electronic filing.

The AEDT may retrospectively deny zero-rating or outside-scope treatment where documentary evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, or insufficiently contemporaneous, and reassess VAT at the 17% standard rate plus statutory late-payment interest. Administrative fines under Article 65 of the VAT Law range from EUR 250 to EUR 50,000 per infraction. Intentional zero-rating fraud exposes responsible directors and partners to criminal penalties of one month to five years' imprisonment and fines of EUR 250 to EUR 50,000, with extended statutory limitation periods (up to 10 years) for fraudulent cases. The AEDT may also claw back input VAT refunds, impose joint and several liability on invoicing intermediaries and general partners, require bank guarantees for future returns, and where the SCSp is found to be an artificial arrangement lacking Luxembourg substance, recharacterize the export transaction with consequent denial of the zero rate and transfer-pricing adjustments on intercompany service fees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro (EUR) is fully convertible, with no distinct capital or current account controls. Luxembourg implements the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) and Article 63 TFEU, providing unrestricted free flow of capital within the EU and to most third countries. Profit remittance is unencumbered for foreign investors, subject only to standard withholding tax obligations on distributions, which for an SCSp with a pass-through status typically trigger no Luxembourg withholding tax at the partnership level. No governmental authorization is required for converting EUR or repatriating profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists in Luxembourg or in the Eurozone to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to any licensed foreign exchange intermediary. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline for receipts from cross-border trade in services (including game art and 3D asset outsourcing). For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, transactions above EUR 12,500 may be subject to periodic declarations under BCL Regulation (BGL/Luxembourg balance of payments reporting), but this is a statistical, not a control, requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility for regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized by the CSSF under the amended Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and electronic money. Because PayPal is listed as a target payment rail, an EMI account could facilitate PayPal pooling and settlement, but an SCSp with a dedicated Luxembourg corporate profile is generally better served by a full-service commercial bank for treasury and fiduciary integrity. Offshore digital EMIs that are not passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 are subject to temporary booking restrictions and are not viable for escrow or long-term treasury storage.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective headline rate for a standard taxable Luxembourg corporate resident (CIT 17% + unemployment solidarity surcharge 7% of the CIT amount + municipal business tax averaging 6.75% in Luxembourg City; the exact aggregate rate varies by commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for a fiscally transparent SCSp: profit distributions to partners are allocations of partnership profit rather than dividends because the SCSp has no legal personality and is not a CIT taxpayer; Luxembourg's general dividend withholding tax rate of 15% applies only where the SCSp elects corporate taxation or where a Luxembourg S.A. / S.à r.l. / S.C.A. distributes dividends

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Paper submissions: 31 May of the year following the fiscal year-end; electronic submissions through eCDF benefit from an automatic extension to 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory transfer pricing summary annex is triggered when the aggregate value of intercompany transactions involving the SCSp exceeds EUR 500,000 in a tax year; a full Luxembourg Local File must be prepared and available within 45 days of a direct tax administration request; the OECD-aligned Master File is required where group revenue reaches EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a codified Economic Substance Regulation akin to offshore regimes, but the substance-over-form principle, the Article 56 LIR abus de droit doctrine, the ATAD GAAR, and the OECD minimum standards require the SCSp to demonstrate genuine commercial substance: real office or production premises in Luxembourg, qualified personnel, actual decision-making authority, a Luxembourg bank account, and complete accounting records; a shell-like SCSp with no substance will be recharacterized as a fully taxable Luxembourg entity, lose pass-through treatment, and be denied treaty benefits on all 3D art outsourcing income

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil re-assessment of all underpaid CIT and MBT plus late-payment interest at 0.8% per month and a 10% surcharge; publication of the conviction; mandatory referral to the Luxembourg Public Prosecutor

Director personal liability: General partners, managers, or directors who knowingly execute the fraud are jointly and severally liable; personal assets may be seized; travel bans and judicial supervision can be imposed during criminal investigation; after final conviction, prohibition from managing any Luxembourg commercial entity for up to 5 years is possible
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the SCSp as a taxable Luxembourg corporation subject to 24.94% combined CIT/MBT; retroactive taxation of the full profit stream; denial of treaty relief and 15% withholding tax on deemed distributions; loss of pass-through loss deductions; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 for failure to deposit accounts; potential de-registration from the RCSL

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally and unlimitedly liable for the SCSp's debts including tax liabilities; a bankruptcy action in extension (action en extension de faillite) can be brought against the directors for confusion of assets or fictitious legal arrangements; directors may be ordered to make direct civil restitution to the Luxembourg tax administration and are exposed to travel bans and criminal conviction for fraudulent insolvency

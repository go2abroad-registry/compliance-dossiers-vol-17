Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Division, Majuro; corporate registration and good-standing certification are administered by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations under the Ministry of Finance. Filings are effected exclusively through licensed RMI registered agents; no direct public tax e-filing portal exists for non-resident entities.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign-owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC): (1) appoint a licensed RMI registered agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation with the Office of the Registrar of Corporations via the agent; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, which evidences valid corporate existence, registered office, and registered agent; (4) obtain an annual Certificate of Good Standing for bank onboarding and cross-border contracting; (5) no TIN is procured at incorporation because a non-resident BC earning no RMI-source income is statutorily outside the income tax and Gross Revenue Tax net; (6) if local RMI activity is commenced, file the GRT registration with the Revenue and Taxation Division, obtain a TIN, and register with the RMI Social Security Administration for any RMI-resident employees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation is treated as a tax-exempt offshore entity: income tax applies only on a territorial basis to RMI-source income, the entity is outside the scope of the Gross Revenue Tax, and no VAT/GST exists. The exemption remains conditional on carrying on no business in the RMI and deriving no income from RMI sources.

Available election forms

RMI law does not adopt US-style check-the-box entity classification or OECD hybrid-mismatch elections. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) designation as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Act, which expressly confers exemption from Gross Revenue Tax and local income tax; (2) continuation or re-domestication of a foreign corporation into the RMI under the Business Corporations Act; (3) registration as a Foreign Maritime Entity (FME) for shipping activities; (4) selection of corporate governance and registered-agent arrangements under the Business Corporations Act. No hybrid-mismatch elective regime is legislated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

3%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST as no such tax exists. GRT registration is required only for businesses conducting activity within the RMI whose annual gross revenue exceeds USD 50,000; a non-resident LegalTech SaaS provider with no RMI nexus has no registration threshold to satisfy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The RMI does not operate a VAT/GST export zero-rating or refund mechanism. Exports of physical goods from the RMI are not subject to export duty, and cross-border digital services supplied by a non-resident BC (e.g., cloud document automation, e-signature authentication, LegalTech SaaS) are wholly outside the RMI territorial tax net; no indirect tax is chargeable on such services and no refund substantiation is required.

  • Outward Cargo Manifest / Customs Export Declaration (required only for physical exports; digital SaaS services require no customs filing)
  • Commercial Invoice stating exporter, consignee, export commodity code, and transaction value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (not required — the RMI imposes no foreign-exchange control or FX repatriation regime)

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API reporting mandate exists in the RMI. Indirect tax and customs compliance remains document-based: commercial invoice, packing list, cargo manifest, and bill of lading/air waybill. The RMI Customs Service has not deployed mandatory continuous transaction controls or SAF-T style reporting.

No VAT refund or export zero-rating regime exists, so there is no export-refund clawback exposure. General enforcement rules: (1) under the Gross Revenue Tax Act, late filing, underpayment, or fraudulent returns attract statutory penalty plus interest and possible criminal fines for active local businesses; (2) under the Income Tax Act, tax evasion is subject to criminal fines and imprisonment; (3) under the Customs Act, false export declarations may trigger seizure, forfeiture, and administrative penalties; (4) under the Business Corporations Act, failure to maintain a registered agent and pay annual renewal fees results in administrative dissolution and loss of limited liability. All such exposure is moot for a non-resident BC deriving no RMI-source income, whose only ongoing compliance obligation is annual corporate renewal and good-standing certification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands operates a fully dollarized economy with no exchange-control statute. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; the repatriation of capital, remittance of dividends, management fees, and liquidation proceeds by non-resident Business Corporations requires no MIBC approval, no exchange-licence, and no prior authorisation certificate. The binding operational constraint is not legal convertibility but correspondent-bank due diligence, because every USD wire transfer is cleared through one or more U.S. intermediary banks that independently apply OFAC sanctions screening and enhanced KYC thresholds to Marshall Islands entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in the Marshall Islands. Export receipts, SaaS subscription revenue, software licensing fees, and royalties from the cloud document automation business are not subject to mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation timelines, or holding-period requirements. Foreign-currency receipts may be freely retained in multi-currency accounts, swapped, or converted to USD at the entity's discretion through any licensed commercial bank, and there is no requirement to route proceeds through the domestic banking system.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands does not maintain a domestic central bank or separate foreign-exchange administration. Banking supervision, licensing, and AML/CFT oversight are administered by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (MIBC), a statutory body operating under the Ministry of Finance, pursuant to the Banking Act 1970 (as amended) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2020. The U.S. Federal Reserve System functions as the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association; all USD clearing ultimately settles through the Federal Reserve.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-rated feasibility with strict legal constraints. Non-resident Marshall Islands Business Corporations may open USD-denominated accounts with qualifying foreign Electronic Money Institutions and digital-banking platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer Business) where those platforms accept the RMI corporate form; however, no Marshall Islands fintech charter or domestic EMI licensing exists, and the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1970 prohibits any unlicensed deposit-taking business within Marshall Islands territory. Accordingly, onboarding to a foreign EMI is legally permissible only if the EMI does not solicit deposits inside the Republic of the Marshall Islands, the EMI holds a valid license in its home jurisdiction, and the MHC entity executes the EMI's client agreement as a non-resident remote customer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory final withholding tax of 10% on dividends paid out of RMI-source profits to non-resident shareholders. Effective rate for the subject NRDC: 0%, because distributions are made exclusively from foreign-source accumulated profits and the RMI imposes no withholding on such non-RMI-source dividends. The paying corporation has no statutory duty to withhold absent RMI-source earnings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The RMI has not enacted express Local File / Master File monetary thresholds; the Revenue and Taxation Division applies OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines by interpretive analogy. Prudent compliance benchmark: prepare and retain a Local File where aggregate related-party transactions exceed USD 1,000,000 per fiscal year, and a Master File / Country-by-Country-reportable group file where consolidated group revenue exceeds USD 50,000,000, consistent with BEPS Action 13 expectations for a low-risk LegalTech SaaS group.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR 2019) and RMI Financial Services Commission guidance require every RMI business corporation carrying on a relevant activity to satisfy: (a) direction and management in the RMI, evidenced by quorum board meetings and minutes maintained in the RMI; (b) core income-generating activities (CIGA) performed in the RMI; and (c) adequate employees, physical premises, and operating expenditure proportionate to the income generated. A legal contract automation and e-signature SaaS entity with no RMI staff is at high risk of reclassification as an IP-holding or pure equity vehicle, which mandates strict CIGA performance in the RMI; annual ESR notification and, where applicable, substance reporting are mandatory for all RMI business corporations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Understatement

Statutory sanction: Felony conviction: fine up to US$10,000 per count and/or imprisonment up to five years; civil penalty equal to 50% of the understated tax plus monthly interest at 1%; forfeiture of the exemption and recomputation of tax at 40% of gross RMI-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or approve fictitious foreign-source schemes are personally liable under the Business Corporations Act fraud exception to limited liability, jointly and severally for restitution, and subject to RMI travel bans, asset seizures, and disqualification orders barring future directorships for up to ten years.
Shell Company / ESR Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty up to US$10,000 per failure and US$100,000 for recurring or deliberate non-compliance; administrative strike-off by the Registrar; referral to the EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD Forum for Harmful Tax Practices, triggering listing as a non-cooperative jurisdiction and consequential 15% penalty or complete loss of the tax exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors and the RMI registered agent face liability for knowing participation in a sham: mandatory compensatory payments, five-year director bans, license revocation for the registered agent, and potential criminal prosecution for false declarations made to the RMI Financial Services Commission.
Cross-Border AML / Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2005 (as amended): criminal fine up to US$50,000 on individual officers and US$200,000 on the corporate entity; imprisonment up to ten years for willful money laundering; suspension or revocation of the FSC operating license; inclusion of the entity in national AML risk advisories.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal pecuniary penalties, freezing of personal bank accounts in support of confiscation proceedings, extradition exposure through US-RMI Mutual Legal Assistance channels, and automatic failure of the FSC 'Fit and Proper' test, permanently disqualifying the director from any licensed financial activity.
Withholding Tax and Social Security Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Unpaid withholding tax is a first-priority trust-fund claim; failure to deduct and remit triggers a 100% penalty on the unwithheld tax plus interest at 1% per month; social security delinquency incurs surcharges up to 50% of unpaid contributions and criminal prosecution of the responsible officers.

Director personal liability: The responsible person doctrine applies to directors and the statutory controller: joint and several personal liability for withheld but unremitted taxes, enforced by asset seizure, exit-permit/departure objections preventing travel from the RMI, and a ten-year bar on appointment to any RMI corporate or financial entity.
Fraudulent Conveyance / Veil Piercing in Distributions

Statutory sanction: Under the BCA dividend restrictions, an unlawful or insolvent distribution is void; directors are personally liable to restore the full distribution amount plus interest; courts may award punitive damages up to treble the fraudulent transfer for bad-faith conveyances to shareholders.

Director personal liability: Liability is joint and several with no contribution among conspiring directors; ex parte asset-preservation orders and travel restrictions are routinely granted; nominee directors who sign false ownership declarations face up to seven years' imprisonment for perjury before RMI judicial authorities.

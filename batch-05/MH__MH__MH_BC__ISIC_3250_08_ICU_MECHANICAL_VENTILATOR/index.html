Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an RMI Business Corporation. The sole incorporator must subscribe for at least one share (here: one common share, US$0.01 par value) fully paid on issuance; consideration may include cash, promissory notes, or services. No escrow account, paid-up capital certificate, or capital blocking requirement is imposed by the Registrar for the non-resident, tax-exempt structure.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety engineering inspection and certification by the Majuro Fire and Rescue Services; structural load and seismic/building code clearance certificate from the Building Inspectorate, Ministry of Public Works; occupational workplace clearance (noise, compressed-gas safety, electrical grounding, and ionizing radiation where applicable) from the Occupational Health and Safety Unit, Ministry of Health and Human Services, issued before machinery commissioning.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax and Customs Administration, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); corporate registration administered by the Office of the Attorney General, Business Corporations Division. Primary filing portal: RMI e-Corporation portal managed by the Registrar of Corporations; no dedicated e-filing portal for tax returns because qualifying non-resident corporations have no tax filing obligation.

E-government filing portal

A foreign owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC) obtains its identity by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations through a licensed registered agent under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (as amended). No separate TIN application is required: the certificate of incorporation and the statutory declaration that the corporation will not carry on business in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) constitute the tax identity instrument. For customs/import purposes, a separate Customs Registration Number is obtainable from the Tax and Customs Administration upon first import; where a physical presence is intended, an import license/permit is required under the RMI Business License Act. Foreign entities have no obligation to obtain an RMI tax identification number unless they establish a taxable nexus.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC) obtains its identity by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations through a licensed registered agent under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (as amended). No separate TIN application is required: the certificate of incorporation and the statutory declaration that the corporation will not carry on business in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) constitute the tax identity instrument. For customs/import purposes, a separate Customs Registration Number is obtainable from the Tax and Customs Administration upon first import; where a physical presence is intended, an import license/permit is required under the RMI Business License Act. Foreign entities have no obligation to obtain an RMI tax identification number unless they establish a taxable nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI law does not adopt US check-the-box classification; entity tax status is fixed by statute. The operative 'election' is the non-resident domestic corporation status under Section 42 of the Business Corporations Act: a BC is automatically and irrevocably exempt from all RMI income, capital gains, withholding, and franchise taxes provided it (i) maintains a registered agent and office in RMI, (ii) files an annual listing fee and statement, and (iii) does not carry on any business or hold a commercial license in RMI. No hybrid mismatch or entity reclassification elections exist in RMI domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

8%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold: all importers of commercial goods must register with the Tax and Customs Administration and obtain an Importer Code / Customs Registration; the general ad valorem import duty rate is 8%, with reduced rates (0% for medical/life-saving items, books, basic foods) and higher rates up to 100%+ for luxury goods under the RMI Import Duty Schedule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under an import-duty-only system: RMI imposes no VAT/GST on domestic or export supply, and no export duty is levied on goods manufactured in or exported from RMI. Accordingly, the export of ICU mechanical ventilators and turbine-driven critical care components obtains an absolute exemption from indirect taxation by virtue of the absence of any export VAT/GST charge; no refund claim mechanism is required. The only fiscal condition is that the goods are physically exported and presented to RMI Customs with an outbound manifest to preclude any allegation of import-duty evasion if goods re-enter the domestic market.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration/Outbound Manifest
  • Commercial Invoice (in English, showing exporter, consignee, incoterms, HS codes 9019.20 / 8414.80 if turbine components separate)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Packing List with gross/net weights and container/seal numbers
  • Certificate of Origin (optional, for preferential tariff access in destination markets)
  • Import License from destination country (for regulated medical devices, where applicable)

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API data exchange, or digital stamping regime exists for domestic sales or exports. Customs clearance is effected via standardized customs declaration forms and shipping manifests; a limited electronic customs declaration interface is available at the Majuro Customs Office for commercial filers, but real-time government fiscal clearance of commercial e-invoices is not mandated.

No VAT zero-rating refund or clawback regime exists because no VAT is chargeable. The relevant compliance risk is the loss of statutory tax exemption: if a BC conducts business in RMI (e.g., local warehousing, physical sales, or maintaining a commercial office) without a business license, the exemption under Section 42 of the Business Corporations Act is voidable retroactively, exposing the entity to annual license fees, additional government service charges, and penalties under the Business License Act 1991 (up to USD 1,000 per month of unlicensed activity). Customs fraud or false export declarations attract penalties under the RMI Customs Act, including forfeiture of goods, fines up to three times the value of the goods, and imprisonment for deliberate false declarations. The RMI Trade Remedies and Subsidies (Commerce Act) may also impose countervailing duties if export incentives are found to distort trade, but standard RMI exports of lawful manufactured medical goods carry no such exposure.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

De jure fully convertible: the RMI is a completely dollarized economy with no foreign-exchange-control law, no capital-account restrictions, and no statutory distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Non-resident Business Corporations organized under the Business Corporations Act (modeled on Delaware law and administered by the RMI Corporate Registrar) are income-tax-exempt, and the RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or profit-remittance flows to foreign shareholders, so profit repatriation is legally unobstructed. De facto frictions arise exclusively from correspondent-bank de-risking pressure, concentration of USD liquidity in the two licensed banks (Bank of Marshall Islands and Bank of Guam, Majuro branch), and the KYC requirement to evidence real economic substance; none of these impose quantitative conversion limits, but they create documentation lead time and enhanced-due-diligence cost.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply. The RMI imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. A non-resident BC may retain ICU-ventilator export revenues in USD or in any freely convertible foreign currency within a licensed bank, may deploy them directly against inbound component-supplier payments, or may earmark them as letter-of-credit collateral. The only binding obligations arise from the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2013 (as amended): full transaction record-keeping, beneficial-ownership disclosure for every natural person holding 10% or more of the BC, and the licensed bank's independent duty to file suspicious-transaction reports on material trade flows.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no conventional central bank or monetary authority; the Banking Commission, established under the Banking Act 1993 (as amended by the Banking (Amendment) Acts 1996 and 2000), headquartered in Majuro and headed by the Banking Commissioner, is the sole prudential regulator and licensing body for all deposit-taking institutions, while the United States dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender and the US Federal Reserve System operates as the de facto currency-issuing, clearing, and settlement backstop.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW - legally not viable onshore. The RMI Banking Act requires a Banking Commission license for any deposit-taking activity and contains no charter, licensing, or supervisory category for electronic-money institutions, payment institutions, or specialized cross-border wallet providers; the 2018 Sovereign (SOV) digital-currency statute is a monetary-policy experiment that creates no payment-service licensing utility. Offshore fintech EMIs may service a non-resident BC only as an unregulated payment channel and face pervasive correspondent de-risking when routing through the RMI's US-bank clearing rails; an offshore EMI is acceptable only in a limited treasury role (for example, a top-tier EEA-licensed EMI used strictly for supplier payouts outside the RMI) and may never be positioned as the sole liquidity or settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity: (i) execute SWIFT MT103 transfers in USD to the BC's USD account at Bank of Guam Majuro or Bank of Marshall Islands with the ordering-customer field showing the true remitter and the beneficiary field matching the exact registered corporate name; (ii) use payment references stating 'Paid-in share capital contribution' and, where the US correspondent requires a purpose code, the standard investment or equity-transfer classification (for example, TRFI, transfer of funds for investment, or its equivalent); (iii) pre-load UBO declarations and source-of-funds letters before the first credit so the receiving bank releases funds without hold; (iv) if any business is conducted inside the RMI, obtain a Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1997 before injecting capital, while noting that a purely non-resident manufacturer-exporter does not require a FIBL; (v) ensure all messages carry the full legal identity of both sender and beneficiary to satisfy FATF Recommendation 16 travel-rule data expectations of the US correspondent clearing network.Clearing rails for outbound trade: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for supplier, freight, and component payments as the dominant rail for speed and traceability; Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) under section 6 of the Nonresident Business Corporations Act 1998; reference resident corporate rate is 3% gross receipts tax on RMI-source gross income under section 8(a)(1) of the Income Tax Act 1989

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an NRDC, and section 6 of the Nonresident Business Corporations Act 1998 bars the introduction of any new withholding tax against the exempt corporation during the 50-year exemption period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI has not enacted transfer-pricing, Local File, Master File, or country-by-country reporting rules. The relevant international threshold is the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework GloBE (Pillar Two) consolidation test of EUR 750 million in group revenue, which the RMI, as an Inclusive Framework member, is expected to implement for RMI constituent entities; functional currency for any records is USD.

Economic substance scope: The RMI does not administer EU-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); section 6 of the Nonresident Business Corporations Act 1998 requires only a registered agent, a registered office in Majuro, a non-resident declaration, and a certification of no business with RMI residents. The EU Code of Practice Group continues to screen the RMI for 'no or only nominal taxation' status (recurring Annex I / Annex II listing), so commercial substance (actual manufacturing capability, qualified personnel, binding supply contracts, operational bank accounts in the manufacturing jurisdiction) is essential to protect the transaction and to avoid beneficial-ownership denial or treaty-shopping findings in the destination market.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of the NRDC Tax Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of the Tax Exemption Certificate; assessment of the 3% gross receipts tax on all RMI-source receipts plus delinquency penalties up to 100% and statutory interest; criminal fines up to US$50,000 under the Business Corporations Act; possible debarment from Marshall Islands government procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations commit perjury and may face imprisonment for up to 10 years; courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose joint and several liability for the evaded tax, penalties, and interest; civil restitution orders, travel bans, and asset freezes may be issued by the High Court of the RMI.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) — Office of the Commissioner of Revenue and Taxation, Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance (Majuro): administration of income/gross-revenue tax and business licensing; Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Registrar (Majuro): entity incorporation, non-resident status, annual government fee; Customs Division, Ministry of Finance: import/export duty and customs declarations. No self-service national e-tax portal exists; all filings and registrations are processed on paper through licensed registered agents and the Majuro government offices.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Engage a licensed RMI registered agent and file Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations, expressly electing 'non-resident domestic corporation' status under the Business Corporations Act (no minimum capital; government fee payable on authorized capital). Step 2 — Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and the Registrar-issued entity registration number, which is the sole statutory corporate identifier; a non-resident business corporation is not issued, and is not required to obtain, an income-tax TIN because it is wholly exempt from RMI income and gross-revenue tax. Step 3 — If physical goods will ever enter or transit RMI territory, register with the Customs Division as importer/exporter (customs code/bond); this is not triggered by pure offshore online retailing. Step 4 — Undertake Business License Act registration and annual gross-revenue tax compliance only if RMI-conducting business activity commences, which non-resident status is designed to preclude.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — statutory non-resident domestic corporation: complete exemption from national income tax, gross-revenue tax, withholding tax, business license fees, and any RMI capital or franchise tax, with the flat BCA annual government fee expressly in lieu of all taxes. Taxation is strictly territorial; only RMI-source income could be taxed in theory, and non-resident status prohibits earning RMI-source business income. Pass-through eligibility is not applicable: the BC is nominally an entity-level taxpayer, but the statutory exemption renders it fiscally inert; no hybrid or transparent classification is required for the exemption to apply.

Available election forms

The controlling election is the 'non-resident' designation under the Business Corporations Act, made at incorporation (or by later amendment filed with the Registrar), which confers permanent statutory tax exemption in lieu of all national taxes. RMI law does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch partnership elections. Ancillary statutory mechanisms include elective continuation/redomestication of a foreign corporation into the RMI as a non-resident BC, elective statutory conversion among BC, LLC, and LP forms, and, for any entity actually conducting RMI activity, the Business License Act election procedure (which is foreclosed to non-resident BCs).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_GST_Or_General_Sales_Tax_Regime_Customs_Import_Duty_Only — the RMI imposes no value-added tax, goods-and-services tax, or retail sales tax; the only border indirect levy is import duty under the Customs Tariff Act, supplemented by excise on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel and by annual business license fees for businesses conducting activity in the RMI.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — because no VAT/GST/sales tax exists, there is no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold. The non-resident BC online retailer of musical instruments and studio audio gear has zero indirect-tax registration obligation in the RMI. Customs registration applies without turnover threshold only to entities physically importing goods into RMI territory, with duty assessed per consignment on CIF value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutorily inapplicable: absent any VAT/GST/sales tax, there is no export zero-rating scheme and no indirect-tax refund claim mechanism in the RMI. The online sale and outward shipment of musical instruments and professional audio gear by the non-resident BC to overseas customers falls wholly outside the RMI tax perimeter; no export VAT registration, zero-rating approval, or refund filing exists. Exports of these goods from RMI territory also attract no export duty, so 'zero' indirect treatment is inherent rather than elective.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration or outward manifest where physical export from RMI territory occurs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the consignment movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the musical instruments/audio gear shipment
  • Inward remittance or bank settlement record evidencing receipt of proceeds in the offshore account

None — the RMI mandates no electronic invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based invoice reporting, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control framework. Invoices are issued under ordinary commercial practice; customs processing is document/manifest-based and no national digital fiscal invoice platform is in operation.

Because no VAT exists there is no VAT clawback, but misuse of the exemption and goods-movement misstatements are heavily sanctioned: the Commissioner of Revenue and Taxation may recompute and assess undeclared RMI-source gross revenue at 100% of the omitted amount plus interest and delinquency penalties; customs false declarations attract monetary penalties, forfeiture of the goods, and criminal liability; forgery of export or payment documents is independently punishable under the RMI Criminal Code; and a corporation that misrepresents an RMI-connected trade as pure offshore business risks revocation of non-resident status by the Registrar with retroactive restoration of tax liability. The RMI imposes no exchange-control or statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark above reflects standard documentary-trade expectation, not a legal mandate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions. The RMI imposes no exchange controls, no profit-remittance licensing, no dividend repatriation approval, and no restrictions on equity redemption or liquidation proceeds for a non-resident Business Corporation. De facto friction arises only from bank-level compliance: US OFAC sanctions screening, FATCA reporting (RMI has a Model 1 IGA with the United States), and FTRA threshold-based filings. A treasury operating through local RMI banks or US-based correspondents must expect enhanced due diligence on any large or unexplained capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for repatriating, surrendering, or converting export or e-commerce sale proceeds; all receipts are USD-denominated and may be retained freely in any account of the entity's choosing. The FTRA 2000 nevertheless obliges financial institutions to file currency transaction reports for cash equivalents at or above USD 10,000, to retain complete wire-transfer originator and beneficiary records, and to file suspicious transaction reports irrespective of amount. The treasury should therefore keep platform settlement reports, sourcing invoices, and shipping documentation aligned with each cleared wire to ensure audit readiness.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no central bank and no separate foreign-exchange administration; the United States dollar is the sole legal tender under the Constitution and the Compact of Free Association. Licensed banking activity is supervised by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance pursuant to the Banking Act 1986, while AML/CFT supervision is vested in the Financial Intelligence Office under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act (FTRA) 2000. Practical USD clearing authority resides in the US Federal Reserve and the US correspondent banking network rather than in any RMI institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound USD equity injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with field 23E 'CRED' and purpose code 'Capital Contribution / Share Subscription'; RMI imposes no exchange-control pre-approval, so the funds may move directly from the contributing shareholder to the BC's account at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam, settling over Fedwire/CHIPS through the respective US correspondents. The treasury must retain the board resolution authorizing issuance, the share-certificate register, source-of-funds statements, and the wire's purpose description for at least the statutory record-keeping period. Any single deposit above USD 10,000 in cash equivalents will trigger a CTR; the entity remains required to file its annual corporate report even though its non-resident earnings are tax-exempt.Supplier payments settle via SWIFT MT103 (GPI) or MT202 cover for bank-to-bank coverage; high-value inventory purchases from Asia or Germany should be structured as irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600, with the advising bank located in the seller's jurisdiction and an inspection certificate issued by the 3PL or independent surveyor. Stripe and PayPal consumer payouts arrive by ACH or internal wallet settlement into the operating USD account, and a daily concentration sweep moves balances to the primary treasury account to maximize control and interest. Native Stripe and PayPal onboarding is unavailable for an entity registered only in the Marshall Islands because RMI is absent from supported-country lists; the recommended architecture is a US check-the-box disregarded LLC or a supported-jurisdiction intermediate entity that executes the marketplace agreements and settles into the RMI treasury. A pure fintech-only banking stack carries elevated account-freeze and debanking risk and should be anchored by at least one licensed correspondent bank relationship.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the Marshall Islands has no transfer pricing legislation, and non-resident BCs are outside the domestic income tax net. No USD threshold triggers a Local File or Master File obligation.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable in other zero-tax jurisdictions have not been enacted in the Marshall Islands for business corporations. A non-resident BC is only required to maintain a registered agent and a registered office in the Republic; no local management-and-control, payroll, or substantive activity test is imposed. However, financial institutions and payment processors such as Stripe and PayPal may independently demand evidence of beneficial ownership and business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Non-Resident Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act and applicable criminal provisions, evasion constitutes a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to US$5,000 and imprisonment for a term of up to three years per count. Civil restitution of all unpaid revenue, interest, and enforcement costs is also ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false filings or direct evasion are personally liable for the full amount of unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest. A court may impose a travel ban restricting departure from the Republic, and the business license of the BC may be revoked.
Shell Company / Sham Incorporation

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Corporations may strike the company off the register and confiscate any assets registered in the name of the BC. Criminal prosecution under the Business Corporations Act for fraudulent formation or abusive use of corporate personality may result in a fine up to US$10,000.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the BC is found to be a mere instrumentality. Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for outstanding liabilities, subjected to asset freezes, and prohibited from managing any company registered in the Marshall Islands for up to five years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing regime in the Marshall Islands imposes administrative monetary penalties of up to US$100,000 on the corporation for record-keeping and reporting failures. Serious or repeated violations constitute a money-laundering predicate offense with a maximum custodial sentence of five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the nominated compliance officer face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, for knowingly allowing the BC to be used for illicit fund movement. Civil remedies include restitution, disqualification as a director of any Marshall Islands entity for life, and a travel ban pending the resolution of the enforcement action.

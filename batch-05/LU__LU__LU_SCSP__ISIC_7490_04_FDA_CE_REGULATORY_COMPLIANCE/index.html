Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital for SCSp; capital contributions are freely determined in the partnership agreement and may be in cash, in kind, or by industry. No escrow account or bank certification is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable at formation - the SCSp will occupy a standard administrative office; the registered office lease must comply with the Luxembourg Building Regulations (Règlement des bâtisses) and the general fire safety requirements applicable to office premises. If the entity later hires employees, workplace clearances under the Labour Code (Code du travail), fire safety certification from the municipal fire service, and occupational health registration with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) will be required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income and corporate taxation. Principal digital portal: MyGuichet.lu, with statutory company filing via the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL/RBE).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident or foreign entity intending to perform taxable supplies in Luxembourg must submit a standard VAT registration application (Form 7 or equivalent) through MyGuichet.lu before commencing taxable activity. If the foreign entity is established outside the EU, it must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative (représentant responsable) jointly liable for VAT. The application must state the foreign corporate details, intended activity, and expected turnover. Upon successful registration, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (TVA ID) without material delays; domestic direct tax registration may be triggered by permanent establishment presence or real estate ownership requiring separate ACD formalities.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident or foreign entity intending to perform taxable supplies in Luxembourg must submit a standard VAT registration application (Form 7 or equivalent) through MyGuichet.lu before commencing taxable activity. If the foreign entity is established outside the EU, it must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative (représentant responsable) jointly liable for VAT. The application must state the foreign corporate details, intended activity, and expected turnover. Upon successful registration, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (TVA ID) without material delays; domestic direct tax registration may be triggered by permanent establishment presence or real estate ownership requiring separate ACD formalities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not employ a check-the-box regime. The SCSp is by default treated as fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income and net wealth tax purposes; each partner is taxed on their allocable share of the partnership's income. There is no general statutory election to be treated as taxable, but treaty and domestic hybrid mismatch provisions under ATAD-compliant Luxembourg law (Law of 2 July 2019) may recharacterize certain opaque participations where income is disregarded by one jurisdiction. No form-based pass-through election is statutorily available; tax transparency is mandatory legal default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-resident and foreign service suppliers: no turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable supplies performed in Luxembourg. Resident small enterprises may apply the Luxembourg small-business exemption where annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) does not exceed EUR 35,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to intra-Community supplies (EU exports) and to supplies of goods dispatched outside the EU upon satisfaction of statutory export conditions. Services to non-EU business customers are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside the EU, such as offshore consulting services received by a non-established business for use outside Luxembourg; cross-border B2B services benefiting from reverse charge are subject to standard VAT rules depending on recipient status. Eligibility requires the supplier to obtain and preserve documentary proof of departure, export, or foreign place of use within the time limits prescribed by Luxembourg VAT law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic customs lodgement via Luxembourg customs) confirming exit of goods from the EU.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing cross-border movement or delivery.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire/payment proof identifying the foreign customer and consideration paid, where required to substantiate the foreign place of use or export transaction.
  • Commercial invoice containing the VAT zero-rated reference, export seller, export buyer consignee, and Incoterms.
  • Contract or service agreement describing the nature, place of performance, and non-EU beneficial recipient for zero-rated offshore consulting services.

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is permitted in B2B and mandatory for B2G public supply contracts, typically via PEPPOL. VAT returns and periodic EU sales lists must be filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu/E-file. There is no API-based pre-clearance of invoices; however, export evidence and substantiating customs entries must be electronically retained for audit purposes. Standard VAT reporting follows EU Directive rules with monthly or quarterly filing obligations depending on turnover and taxable activity.

Misclaimed export zero-rating or insufficient export documentation triggers reassessment by the AEDT. The tax authority may apply a 5-year limitation period (7 years in cases of fraud or wilful misrepresentation). Penalties include up to EUR 5,000 for reporting and documentary deficiencies, interest on unpaid VAT at the statutory rate, recovery of the unraised VAT, and joint and several liability for the Luxembourg VAT representative. Criminal sanctions, including imprisonment under Luxembourg tax and customs law, may apply for fraudulent export filings, false invoices, or money-laundering facilitation connected to false VAT refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the legal tender and is fully convertible for residents and non-residents. Luxembourg applies complete current-account and capital-account liberalisation pursuant to Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005. No exchange licence, central-bank approval, conversion restriction or repatriation permit applies to profit remittances, distributions to foreign limited partners, or return of capital from a Luxembourg SCSp. The practical constraints are limited to: (i) the SCSp's tax-transparent and contract-based character, which obliges remitters and the receiving bank to classify inflows as 'equity/capital contribution' and not as trading revenue in order to preserve the tax treatment; (ii) CRS/FATCA self-certification and the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE) declaration; (iii) BCL balance-of-payments statistical declarations for cross-border flows above materiality thresholds; and (iv) standard AML/CFT source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence. Distributions from an SCSp are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax because the partnership is fiscally transparent and has no independent tax personality; each partner is taxed in their own jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in law or in practice. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no requirement to repatriate or convert export proceeds or cross-border service revenue, including FDA 510(k) filing fees, CE-mark conformity-assessment fees and regulatory consulting fees. Export proceeds may be held indefinitely in EUR or in any foreign currency, in Luxembourg or abroad. The only compliance duties are: (i) BCL balance-of-payments reporting — the resident credit institution files on behalf of the SCSp for ordinary cross-border payments, but the SCSp itself is required to report directly where a single transaction reaches or exceeds EUR 12,500 or cumulative cross-border transactions reach or exceed EUR 125,000 over a rolling 12-month period; (ii) retention for at least five years of the underlying commercial documentation (engagement letters, invoices, FDA/CE submission dossiers, bank confirmations) to substantiate economic substance and true transactional purpose in an AML/CFT inspection; and (iii) verification that the exported services are not caught by sanctions or export-control restrictions applying to medical-device technical assistance where dual-use or embargoed end-users are involved.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the Luxembourg member of the Eurosystem and subordinate to the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy, foreign-exchange operations and reserve management. Prudential supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange control agency; exchange controls are abolished under the EU free-movement-of-capital regime, and the BCL's residual statutory powers are confined to Eurosystem liquidity operations, emergency liquidity assistance, macroprudential policy, and balance-of-payments external statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a disbursement-only sub-rail; low feasibility as the primary treasury or capital-escrow rail. Under the Luxembourg PSD2 framework (Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended) and CSSF supervision, an EU/EEA-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution passporting into Luxembourg may be used for operating payments (vendor disbursements, small refunds, foreign-currency settlements). Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer and similar offshore digital EMIs can be onboarded only if the SCSp provides its RCS/LBR registration extract, the notarised partnership agreement, the UBO declaration and a reference bank account at a Luxembourg credit institution. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI may not grant credit and must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts with an EU credit institution; (ii) the SCSp's lack of independent legal personality and its pass-through structure cause many EMIs to categorise it as a shell or regulated-vehicle risk, requiring a Luxembourg legal opinion; (iii) credit-card acquiring under the target payment rails must be routed through a licensed PSP such as Stripe or Adyen with a merchant agreement in the SCSp's registered name, clear MCC categorisation, and a documented transaction-flow and refund policy; card acquiring may not be used to collect funds for third parties, which would constitute an unlawful money-transmission or payment-service activity under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector (Article 23) and the PSD2 law. Recommended design: a primary operational and contribution account at BCEE or BIL, one EU-licensed EMI for small USD/EUR disbursements, and a Stripe/Adyen merchant settlement account feeding daily sweeps to the primary bank account.

Legal constraints: (i) an EMI may not grant credit and must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts with an EU credit institution; (ii) the SCSp's lack of independent legal personality and its pass-through structure cause many EMIs to categorise it as a shell or regulated-vehicle risk, requiring a Luxembourg legal opinion; (iii) credit-card acquiring under the target payment rails must be routed through a licensed PSP such as Stripe or Adyen with a merchant agreement in the SCSp's registered name, clear MCC categorisation, and a documented transaction-flow and refund policy; card acquiring may not be used to collect funds for third parties, which would constitute an unlawful money-transmission or payment-service activity under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector (Article 23) and the PSD2 law. Recommended design: a primary operational and contribution account at BCEE or BIL, one EU-licensed EMI for small USD/EUR disbursements, and a Stripe/Adyen merchant settlement account feeding daily sweeps to the primary bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions to the SCSp should be routed exclusively by wire from the partner's own bank account directly to the SCSp's designated contribution account at a Luxembourg tier-1 bank (BIL or BCEE). For EUR contributions, use a SEPA Credit Transfer where the payment is below the bank's internal large-value threshold, or a SWIFT MT103/MT202 for larger sums; for USD and other third-currency injections, always use SWIFT MT103 with the receiving field identifying the SCSp's IBAN and the beneficiary bank's SWIFT code. The payment message must carry a clear purpose narrative — 'Capital contribution to Luxembourg SCSp / direct investment — equity — not payment for goods or services' — and, where the bank's system requires an ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code, use 'COMC' or 'CASH' as the closest accepted category while the narrative preserves the equity character. The SCSp must pre-deliver to the receiving bank: the executed partnership agreement, a capital-call notice signed by the general partner, an updated UBO/RBE declaration, and confirmation of the intended allocation to the partner's capital account. Because the SCSp has no statutory minimum capital, no regulatory escrow is required, but a segregated Capital Escrow Account is recommended as a voluntary ring-fencing measure to prevent commingling of contributions with operating revenue and to simplify the annual capital-account reconciliation. Inflows reaching or exceeding EUR 125,000 individually or cumulatively over 12 months will be reported by the bank to the BCL as direct investment equity; the SCSp must supply the remitter's name, country and relationship to avoid misclassification and preserve the pass-through tax treatment.Outbound settlements use three complementary clearing rails. First, SEPA Credit Transfer for all EUR payments inside the EEA — including notified-body fees (TÜV Rheinland, BSI, DEKRA, Intertek, GMED), European sub-consultants, laboratories and the general partner's management fees — with next-day execution and structured creditor references. Second, SWIFT MT103 for USD/EUR and third-currency cross-border wires to US agents, US FDA user-fee processors (effectively settled via the US agent's account or the Pay.gov card/ACH channel), and non-EEA suppliers; each MT103 must state the underlying invoice reference and the commercial purpose (regulatory consulting fee, conformity-assessment fee or procurement payment) to satisfy the beneficiary bank's due diligence. Card-acquiring receipts from clients paying by credit card are settled by the PSP into a dedicated merchant settlement account and swept daily to the primary Luxembourg bank account; the merchant account is used only for collections and never for outbound trade disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (local file threshold) and EUR 4,000,000 (master file threshold) based on group revenue and intercompany transaction value; full documentation required when the aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000 or when the group’s consolidated revenues exceed EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies economic substance requirements based on EU case law (C-115/16, C-118/16 – Danish Cases), the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, and the OECD BEPS Action 6; an SCSp must demonstrate adequate staff, premises, and decision-making functions proportionate to its activities, particularly for intercompany financing and IP holding arrangements. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not a separate domestic statute, but the Luxembourg tax authorities may challenge artificial arrangements under the abuse of law doctrine and the substance-over-form principle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Luxembourg General Tax Law, Art. 59 LAL)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and a fine of EUR 5,000 to EUR 100,000, or one of these penalties; additional civil penalties include 100% of the evaded tax and interest of 0.8% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for taxes withheld or collected but not remitted; criminal liability extends to de facto managers; civil restitution plus travel bans and a ban from managing commercial activities can be ordered.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of the structure (taxable corporate deemed entity), and administrative fine of up to EUR 250,000; back assessments with double interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the artificial arrangement face personal fines up to EUR 50,000; in flagrant cases, criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion and an order for restitution of all tax savings.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML/CFT) Obligations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal sanctions up to 5 years imprisonment for directors or compliance officers.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML breaches; courts may impose a ban on holding directorship roles for up to 10 years, travel restrictions pending investigation, and seizure of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Documentation penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 25,000 per missing file, plus a 10% surcharge on the additional tax assessment resulting from the transfer pricing adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for administrative fines; deliberate falsification of documentation may trigger criminal fraud penalties and a personal monetary liability equal to the tax shortfall.

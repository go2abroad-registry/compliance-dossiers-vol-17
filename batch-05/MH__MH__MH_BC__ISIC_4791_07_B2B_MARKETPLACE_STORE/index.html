Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (Title 51, Marshall Islands Revised Code 2006, as amended), Sections 27-30 (Articles of Incorporation, filing and effect), Section 34 (consideration for shares), Section 38 (registered agent), Section 45 (officers) and Section 52 (bylaws); the Act is modeled substantially on the Delaware General Corporation Law.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (BCA Form 1) with accompanying Statement of Acceptance of Registered Agent (BCA Form 1A), filed pursuant to Title 51, Sections 27-28.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; BCA Section 34 permits shares to be issued for any consideration fixed by the board of directors. The pro-forma first resolution establishes 1,000 issued shares at $1.00 par value, producing $1,000 stated capital.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety, structural load, or occupational clearance is required for a virtual foreign-zone operation without local physical premises. If a Majuro office is staffed, a Fire Safety Clearance Certificate from the Majuro Fire Department and an Occupational Safety and Health workplace compliance assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act 2016 must be obtained prior to occupancy, together with a structural soundness certificate issued by the Municipal Building Inspectorate.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; BCA Section 34 permits shares to be issued for any consideration fixed by the board of directors. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance, Tax and Revenue Division (Majuro); official portal: RMI Tax and Revenue Office electronic services at www.rmi-finance.org and paper filing at the Ministry of Finance, Majuro.

E-government filing portal

The RMI does not maintain a general VAT or income TIN system applicable to foreign non-resident entities. A non-resident Business Corporation without RMI-source activity or a place of business in the RMI is not required to register. If an RMI tax nexus is created (e.g., physical place of business, employer obligations, or RMI-source gross revenue), the entity must submit the Business License Application (Form BL-1) and Taxpayer Registration (Form T-1) to the Ministry of Finance, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent consent, and corporate resolutions. The Ministry then issues a Business License Number and, where applicable, a Withholding Tax Account Number. No VAT ID exists under RMI law.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The RMI does not maintain a general VAT or income TIN system applicable to foreign non-resident entities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The RMI Income Tax Act imposes no check-the-box partnership, disregarded-entity, or hybrid mismatch elections. A non-resident Business Corporation is statutorily classified as a foreign corporation taxed only on gross revenue derived from sources within the RMI under the Gross Revenue Tax regime; income from foreign cross-border e-commerce, including B2B marketplace commissions and software/platform fees, is outside RMI tax jurisdiction. No election is required to achieve this result, and no hybrid mismatch election exists in RMI legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - the RMI imposes no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The only transaction-adjacent tax is the Gross Revenue Tax under the Income Tax Act 1989 (currently 3% on gross revenue sourced in the RMI) and import customs duties under the RMI Customs Act.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST - no such tax exists. For RMI Business License purposes, a license fee becomes due upon maintaining a place of business or undertaking business activities within the RMI; there is no de minimis turnover threshold for license registration, but a non-resident pure online B2B marketplace with no RMI physical presence or RMI-source revenue has no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because the RMI does not levy VAT/GST, there is no 0% export VAT rate or export refund mechanism. A non-resident BC's cross-border B2B services and goods sold through the marketplace are outside the scope of RMI gross revenue tax provided the sale is not sourced in the RMI (title, risk, and place of performance outside the RMI).

  • Not applicable - RMI imposes no VAT export zero-rating or input VAT refund regime; no customs export documentation bundle is required for tax refund purposes. Entities should, however, retain export declarations, bills of lading, airway bills, invoices, payment confirmations, and written contracts to evidence foreign-source income and the absence of RMI-source revenue.

None. The RMI has no real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoice clearance API, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. Invoicing is governed by general commercial practices; in practice, the RMI Tax and Revenue Office may request local paper or PDF records during audit.

No export zero-rating VAT exists, so no VAT clawback or fraudulent zero-rating penalty regime applies. General anti-avoidance scrutiny nonetheless applies: mischaracterization of RMI-source gross revenue as foreign-source (e.g., digital services delivered to RMI-based customers or payments routed through a foreign platform while economic activity occurs in the RMI) may trigger reassessment under the Income Tax Act 1989, late payment interest of 1.5% per month, a late filing penalty of 10% of the tax due, license suspension under the Business License Act 1990, and, for willful tax evasion, criminal fines and potential revocation of the business license. No currency repatriation deadline exists; the RMI uses the U.S. dollar and imposes no exchange controls.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a domestic central bank and has adopted the United States dollar as its sole legal tender. As a result, there is no foreign exchange control authority, no independent monetary policy, and no distinction between capital-account and current-account convertibility. Profit remittances and capital repatriations are unrestricted at the sovereign level; however, banks and trust companies are subject to anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) obligations under the Marshall Islands Banking Act and related regulations, and may require satisfactory source-of-funds and economic-substance documentation before processing outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to any central bank or government agency, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to the Republic of the Marshall Islands. Exporters may freely retain, hold, or disburse USD-denominated proceeds. Nevertheless, licensed banking institutions may apply internal compliance policy requiring documentary evidence of the underlying trade transaction—such as invoices, bills of lading, or platform transaction reports—to support the credit of export receipt and to comply with correspondent banking due diligence expectations.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance of the Republic of the Marshall Islands

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low—no offshore electronic money institution (EMI) or digital payment service is licensed by the Bank of Guam or Bank of the Marshall Islands for the purpose of providing domestic banking services to non-resident corporate clients, and the Marshall Islands Banking Act does not recognize non-resident EMI banking licences. Offshore digital EMIs generally cannot clear USD payments locally and would require a correspondent banking arrangement through a US or foreign bank to execute SWIFT transactions. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary payment gateways for the B2B marketplace, with a licensed Marshall Islands commercial bank remaining the primary clearing and settlement entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed in United States dollars via international wire transfer from the investor's bank through the SWIFT network, typically a MT103 message to the receiving Marshall Islands commercial bank's correspondent in the United States (often a US Federal Reserve member bank). The receiving bank will request the sender to include the company's account number and the purpose code 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', accompanied by a board resolution and share subscription documents. The bank will verify the source of funds before crediting the account; where the funding exceeds a specified threshold, a formal source-of-funds questionnaire and certified ownership records must be provided.Outbound trade settlements are cleared through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for interbank transfers, with a USD-denominated commercial letter of credit (LC) used where the buyer or seller requires documentary protection. For lower-value marketplace disbursements, banks in the Marshall Islands may also support automated clearing house (ACH) or Fedwire transfers to US bank accounts. The seller's invoice, shipping documents, and applicable purpose code such as 'REMITTANCE FOR IMPORTS' or 'TRADE PAYMENT' must be attached to the remittance order; all wires are subject to OFAC screening and correspondent-bank restrictions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) arranged with a licensed offshore bank or broker, permitted because the Marshall Islands has no domestic currency and no restrictions on offshore hedgingPlain-vanilla foreign currency forwards involving USD vs a trade counterparty's currency, settled through the international interbank marketCross-currency interest rate swaps executed for term-liquidity hedging under an International Swaps and Derivatives Association (ISDA) master agreement, with Marshall Islands banking law imposing no prudential restriction on qualified non-resident corporations

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends declared by a Marshall Islands Business Corporation, regardless of the shareholder's residence or the source of the profits distributed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months after the close of the taxable year; the standard RMI taxable year runs 1 October to 30 September, so the statutory due date is 31 December. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 - the RMI has not enacted a Local File or Master File documentation regime, and there is no statutory monetary threshold requiring transfer pricing documentation. ITA s.105 nevertheless empowers the Commissioner to allocate, apportion, or adjust gross income and deductions among related taxpayers where the pricing does not reflect an arm's-length result. Exempt NRDCs must maintain contemporaneous economic substance evidence (board minutes, payroll records, lease and utility contracts, bank statements) demonstrating management and physical presence in the RMI for ESR purposes.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended 2021), administered by the Registrar of Corporations, apply to all entities registered under the Business Corporations Act. A cross-border B2B electronic marketplace deriving income from related-party transactions is classified as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' / 'Provision of Services' relevant activity and must satisfy the economic substance test: (i) management and control exercised in the RMI (at least 2 board meetings per year held in the RMI, with original minutes maintained), (ii) physical premises in the RMI, (iii) adequate number of qualified local employees, and (iv) adequate local operating expenditure proportionate to the income. An annual ESR return is mandatory. An entity claiming tax residence in another jurisdiction must file a non-resident exclusion claim supported by a foreign tax residence certificate issued by a non-blacklisted jurisdiction; failure to qualify for exclusion triggers full national substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company Determination

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR Regulations 2019: initial penalty up to US$20,000; continuing default penalty of US$10,000 per month; repeat default triggers involuntary strike-off by the Registrar and referral to the Commissioner of Revenue for asset recovery and collection of unpaid fees.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false substance declarations are personally liable for civil restitution of exemptions and fees obtained; penalties up to US$10,000 and imprisonment up to 1 year; travel bans, revocation of RMI business registration, and disqualification from acting as a director may be imposed.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under ITA 1989 s.165: criminal misdemeanor, fine up to US$5,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional civil fraud penalty of 50% of the tax evaded; the DRT may issue jeopardy assessments, freeze bank accounts, and enforce tax liens against settlement balances held through Alibaba Pay or Swift Wire rails.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where fraud is proven; directors, officers, and controlling shareholders are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution to the DRT, personal fines, passport revocation, travel bans, and extradition under the RMI Extradition Act.
Unlicensed Financial Services / AML Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Banking Act 1991: fine up to US$25,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years for unlicensed banking or money service activity; the Banking Commission may impose asset freeze orders, suspend settlement accounts, and prohibit the marketplace from processing payments.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for gross negligence in AML oversight; orders may include personal asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification from managing any regulated financial business, and criminal prosecution of the responsible compliance officer.
Non-Compliance with NRDC Exemption and Registrar Obligations

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act: involuntary dissolution and strike-off from the MIRC registry; restoration fee of US$100 per month of default plus all outstanding fees; upon dissolution, undistributed assets escheat to the Republic; violations of the NRDC Act 2019 carry fines up to US$10,000.

Director personal liability: After strike-off, directors are personally liable for corporate debts arising post-dissolution; the corporate veil is pierced upon a finding of misrepresentation to the Registrar, subjecting directors and officers to civil restitution, criminal fines, imprisonment, and travel restrictions pending resolution.

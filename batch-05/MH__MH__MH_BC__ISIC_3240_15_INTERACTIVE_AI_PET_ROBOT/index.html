Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) — Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce and Investment; corporate registry portal: https://register.mhr.gov.mh. Fiscal administration is divided between the Ministry of Finance's Revenue and Customs Division (Majuro) for customs duties and the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA) for employer payroll contributions. RMI has no national income-tax authority because no corporate or personal income tax is imposed.

E-government filing portal

RMI does not issue tax identification numbers (TINs), VAT IDs, or income-tax registration numbers. A foreign entity obtains statutory existence by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations under MIRC Title 51 (Business Corporations Act) through a licensed RMI registered agent, paying the statutory incorporation filing fee (approximately USD 150), and filing Semi-Annual Reports every six months with the semi-annual license fee (USD 200 per half-year for a non-resident corporation, i.e., USD 400 per annum). No RMI business license or foreign-investment license is required if the non-resident corporation does not maintain an office or operate commercially inside RMI. If the entity imports goods, it must appoint a licensed customs broker in Majuro to obtain an importer registration and post a USD customs bond. If the entity employs RMI-resident staff, it must register as an employer with MISSA using an employer registration number.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — an RMI Business Corporation (Non-Resident) is extraterritorially exempt from all national taxation: zero corporate income tax, zero capital gains tax, zero branch profits tax, zero withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalties, zero estate/gift tax, and zero stamp duty. Its passive and active foreign-source income is outside RMI fiscal jurisdiction; the only recurring fiscal costs are the flat semi-annual corporate registry license fees and, if applicable, MISSA contributions for locally hired staff. — RMI does not issue tax identification numbers (TINs), VAT IDs, or income-tax registration numbers. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — an RMI Business Corporation (Non-Resident) is extraterritorially exempt from all national taxation: zero corporate income tax, zero capital gains tax, zero branch profits tax, zero withholding tax on dividends/interest/royalties, zero estate/gift tax, and zero stamp duty. Its passive and active foreign-source income is outside RMI fiscal jurisdiction; the only recurring fiscal costs are the flat semi-annual corporate registry license fees and, if applicable, MISSA contributions for locally hired staff.

Available election forms

Under MIRC Title 51, the entity may elect non-resident status (default, tax-neutral) or file a Statement electing resident status, which subjects it solely to the RMI semi-annual license fee schedule; neither status creates income-tax liability. Under MIRC Title 52, the entity may elect to operate as an International Business Corporation (IBC) for enhanced privacy. No check-the-box election exists, and hybrid mismatch, CFC, and BEPS/ATAD rules are inapplicable because RMI levies no corporate income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_GST_Sales_Use_Tax — RMI's only national indirect tax is the customs import duty regime under the Customs Tariff Act (MIRC Title 58), supplemented by specific excise rates on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel; typical ad valorem duty rates range from 3% to 6% on most goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — RMI imposes no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax and therefore has no economic-turnover registration threshold. Customs registration is triggered by the first act of importing goods into RMI, with no minimum value threshold; the importer must use a licensed customs broker and may be required to post a USD bond. Local statutory currency is the United States Dollar (USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the VAT sense: because RMI does not levy VAT, GST, or sales tax on domestic supplies, there is no export zero-rating certificate, input-credit, or VAT-refund mechanism to claim. For RMI customs purposes, goods physically exported from RMI are free of duty and exit the customs territory; robotic AI pet toys manufactured abroad and shipped directly to foreign buyers without entering RMI fall entirely outside the RMI fiscal base, consistent with the non-resident tax-exempt classification. RMI applies no exchange controls; USD is legal tender and inward remittance verification is a banking-commercial convention rather than a statutory obligation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Entry) — mandatory under MIRC Title 58 for any goods physically departing RMI
  • Bill of Lading (original) or Air Waybill — carrier-issued proof of the export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank-issued proof of USD receipt, required where an export-duty drawback claim or counterparty FX verification is pursued

No mandate. RMI has no electronic-invoicing obligation, no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no tax-authority API integration, no digital stamping/QR-code clearance, and no continuous transaction controls. Paper or PDF commercial invoices satisfy any commercial record-keeping requirement.

RMI Customs may conduct post-clearance audits of export entries for up to 5 years. False, incomplete, or fraudulent export declarations trigger seizure and forfeiture of the goods, monetary penalties commonly ranging from 100% to 300% of the duty evaded (or a statutory fine up to USD 50,000), and criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment up to 5 years under MIRC Title 58. Because no input VAT is claimable, no export-VAT clawback can arise. Abuse of the non-resident status (e.g., maintaining a local office or conducting RMI-source commercial activity) permits the Registrar to re-assess the resident fee schedule retrospectively, with a 10% late-payment surcharge and deregistration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. Because the RMI issues no domestic currency and imposes no exchange-control statute, there are no approvals, no quantitative limits, no withholding taxes, and no impediments to repatriation of equity, dividends, branch profits, management fees, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds. All settlements are effected in freely transferable USD via US correspondent clearing; therefore, effective regulatory constraints arise from US OFAC sanctions screening and counterparty bank AML/KYC policies rather than from any RMI FX control. The RMI's tax-exempt regime for non-resident Business Corporations further removes any tax-clearance hurdle to profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender or repatriation obligation on export receipts in USD or any other currency. Export proceeds may be held, converted, or disbursed freely at the exporter's discretion, subject only to general recordkeeping, currency-transaction threshold reporting, and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2012.

Central bank supervision: None in operation. The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no central bank and no foreign exchange administration. The US dollar is the sole legal tender by operation of Article II, Section 212 of the Compact of Free Association (Public Law 108-188), with monetary stability effectively provided by the US Federal Reserve System. Licensing and prudential supervision of deposit-taking institutions rest with the Banking Commissioner (Banking Commission) under the Banking Act 1991, as amended; AML/CFT oversight is exercised by the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit under the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2012.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Viability rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an operational disbursement utility, but LOW as a substitute for a licensed RMI bank account and VOID as a licensed RMI entity, because the RMI has no electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing category under the Banking Act 1991 and deposit-taking is reserved to licensed banks. Practical structure: because Stripe does not support the Marshall Islands as a direct merchant jurisdiction, the RMI Business Corporation (BC) should establish a US-incorporated subsidiary (e.g., a Delaware LLC) to serve as merchant of record for Stripe and credit-card acquiring, with daily USD sweeps (ACH/MT103) from the subsidiary to the RMI BC's BMI account; the same US vehicle, or an approved fintech such as Mercury (partnered with Evolve Bank & Trust) or Airwallex, may execute small outbound supplier payments. Legal constraints: most US-domiciled EMIs apply jurisdiction policies that reject RMI-registered entities, reflecting correspondent-bank de-risking; balances at EMIs are held at the partner licensed bank and must be booked and audited in the RMI BC's financial statements; investor capital and escrow funds should never be held at an EMI and must remain at a tier-1 RMI commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital moves in USD via SWIFT MT103 over CHIPS/Fedwire to the BMI or Bank of Guam correspondent accounts. The MT103 remittance field 70 must state the economic purpose: 'Equity capital contribution in cash' (classified as Direct Investment — Equity under IMF BPM6) or 'Share subscription proceeds', referencing the board resolution and the company's share allotment register. Shareholder loans must be supported by a signed loan agreement and coded as Direct Investment — Intercompany Debt. The RMI imposes no registration, no approval, and no exchange-control filing for inward investment. Verification discipline: the receiving bank matches the wire against corporate authorizations, applies FATF-consistent customer due diligence, screens against OFAC sanctions lists, and files currency-transaction or suspicious-transaction reports as applicable; any single remittance of USD 100,000 or more should be prefaced with a source-of-funds letter and a short business plan. Escrow-grade investor capital should be held at BMI, operational funds at a second bank to avoid concentration risk, and Stripe/credit-card proceeds swept from the US merchant subsidiary via daily ACH or MT103 as intercompany settlements recorded in the RMI BC's books.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 in USD via Fedwire/CHIPS from the BMI or Bank of Guam account to supplier banks in China, Taiwan, Vietnam, or Mexico, with MT103 fields 70 and 72 carrying the commercial invoice and purchase-order references. Large-value or strategic purchases: commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) issued under UCP600, payable at sight or on 30-90 day usance drafts, advised and, where prudent, confirmed by a major Asian issuing bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as an operational disbursement utility, but LOW as a substitute for a licensed RMI bank account and VOID as a licensed RMI entity, because the RMI has no electronic-money institution (EMI) licensing category under the Banking Act 1991 and deposit-taking is reserved to licensed banks. Practical structure: because Stripe does not support the Marshall Islands as a direct merchant jurisdiction, the RMI Business Corporation (BC) should establish a US-incorporated subsidiary (e.g., a Delaware LLC) to serve as merchant of record for Stripe and credit-card acquiring, with daily USD sweeps (ACH/MT103) from the subsidiary to the RMI BC's BMI account; the same US vehicle, or an approved fintech such as Mercury (partnered with Evolve Bank & Trust) or Airwallex, may execute small outbound supplier payments. Legal constraints: most US-domiciled EMIs apply jurisdiction policies that reject RMI-registered entities, reflecting correspondent-bank de-risking; balances at EMIs are held at the partner licensed bank and must be booked and audited in the RMI BC's financial statements; investor capital and escrow funds should never be held at an EMI and must remain at a tier-1 RMI commercial bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services – Revenue and Taxation Division, Republic of the Marshall Islands (official administrative portal: no dedicated national e-filing system; filings are paper-based or via authorized agents)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity deriving no Marshall Islands source income or engaging in no business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands is not required to obtain a Tax Identification Number for income tax purposes. A TIN may be applied for, through the Revenue and Taxation Division, solely for Gross Revenue Tax registration, customs/import duty purposes, or employer withholding obligations where a local physical presence or local source revenue exists. For a non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC), no TIN procurement is mandatory at formation; the entity may elect to obtain a non-resident status certificate under the Business Corporations Act to formalize tax exemption.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident Business Corporation: exempt from Marshall Islands income tax, gross revenue tax, withholding tax, and capital gains tax on worldwide income, provided the corporation does not carry on business or hold assets in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; annual government franchise fees and registry fees remain payable) — A foreign entity deriving no Marshall Islands source income or engaging in no business within the Republic of the Marshall Islands is not required to obtain a Tax Identification Number for income tax purposes. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (non-resident Business Corporation: exempt from Marshall Islands income tax, gross revenue tax, withholding tax, and capital gains tax on worldwide income, provided the corporation does not carry on business or hold assets in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; annual government franchise fees and registry fees remain payable)

Available election forms

None (the Marshall Islands does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Entity-level tax classification is determined solely by corporate residency under the Business Corporations Act: a domestic corporation is 'non-resident' when it has no business operations, assets, or income sourced within the Republic. Non-resident status may be formally elected by filing a certificate of non-resident status with the Registrar of Corporations, which creates a conclusive presumption of tax exemption for the life of the entity unless revoked).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax is imposed in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the only material indirect-type levy is the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) on locally sourced gross receipts of businesses operating within the RMI, plus customs import duties at territorial entry.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gross Revenue Tax registration is triggered for any person or entity carrying on a business in the Republic of the Marshall Islands with gross revenue exceeding USD 50,000 per annum; non-resident corporations not engaged in local business have no registration obligation and no threshold applies. (Marshall Islands uses the United States Dollar as its official currency; there is no separate local currency.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: the Marshall Islands does not impose a VAT/GST, and hence there is no export zero-rating mechanism. Exports of goods and services are outside the scope of domestic indirect taxation. A zero-rating claim cannot arise; no VAT refund system exists for exporters.

  • Not applicable – no export VAT regime. For general customs/trade administration, the following standard export documents may be required: Export Declaration / Customs Cargo Manifest; Commercial Invoice; Bill of Lading or Air Waybill; Shippers Export Declaration (if applicable).

No electronic invoicing, continuous transaction controls (CTC), real-time fiscal clearance, API interoperability, or digital stamping regime is mandated. Paper-based record-keeping is accepted; customs documentation may be filed electronically on a voluntary basis through the RMI Customs Service.

No export zero-rating fraud or clawback liability exists because no zero-rated VAT system is in force. General anti-avoidance principles, misrepresentation of non-resident status, or fictitious claims of gross revenue exemption may result in revocation of non-resident tax-exempt status, retroactive imposition of Gross Revenue Tax (3% of gross receipts), penalties up to 200% of unpaid tax, interest, and criminal fines under the RMI Revenue and Taxation Act and the Business Corporations Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI is a fully dollarized jurisdiction with no separate domestic currency and no foreign exchange control legislation. There is no legal distinction between current account and capital account convertibility, and no profit remittance permit or repatriation approval is required. Non-resident Business Corporations may freely retain, disburse, and distribute U.S. dollar funds without conversion taxes, withholding hurdles, or Central Bank authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The RMI imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Export revenues may be held indefinitely in USD with any licensed bank inside or outside the RMI. Applicable rules are limited to general AML/CFT transaction reporting thresholds under the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism Act 2021, not exchange surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not maintain a domestic central bank. Monetary and banking supervision is exercised by the Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, acting through the Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services under the Financial Institutions Act 1987 and the Banking Act 2011. The U.S. Federal Reserve functions as the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility. The RMI has no electronic money institution or dedicated payment services licensing regime, and offshore digital EMIs cannot passport into the jurisdiction. Any deposit-taking by an unlicensed foreign EMI would violate the Financial Institutions Act 1987. The MH_BC entity may therefore engage only as a software merchant with funds settled through a licensed RMI bank or an overseas acquiring bank (e.g., Stripe or Apple IAP), not as a stand-alone EMI. Legal constraints also include correspondent-bank de-risking and the absence of RMI membership in any open-banking or SEPA-style clearing system.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 31 October of each calendar year for all existing domestic corporations; the Registrar allows no statutory extension, and a certificate must be current for the company to maintain good standing and obtain certificates of incumbency or tax status.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed within 12 months of the entity's reporting reference date, aligned with the fiscal period used for the annual report; the notification must be lodged in the window announced by the Competent Authority and cannot be waived for non-resident entities maintaining an RMI legal presence.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Returns are due by the 25th day of the month following the close of the taxable quarter; no annual consolidation is permitted once quarterly thresholds are exceeded. The entity must obtain a taxpayer identification number only upon establishing RMI nexus.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 / no monetary threshold — the RMI has not enacted transfer pricing or thin capitalization legislation; disclosure obligations arise solely under the Economic Substance Regulations for any entity engaged in a relevant activity. In substance, all RMI Business Corporations must maintain a documented governance and operational trail evidencing where core income-generating activities are performed; the absence of a revenue-based documentation threshold means the evasion risk lies in wholly undocumented cross-border pricing rather than in a statutory filing obligation.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019, as amended, implement the EU Code of Conduct Group criteria. The doctrine requires any RMI entity carrying on a 'relevant activity' in or from the Marshall Islands to maintain proportionate substance in the jurisdiction: board meetings with quorum in the RMI exercising real management, RMI-resident employees adequate to the activity, physical premises, and core income-generating activities performed locally. A non-resident Business Corporation whose board, cloud infrastructure, development team, and client contracts are wholly outside the RMI, and which declares non-resident status, is out-of-scope and need only file the annual notification. The doctrine is fatal where the entity holds IP or financial assets while claiming non-resident status but operates remotely from and is managed through Majuro advisers; that pattern is treated as a shell company. For the subject fintech portfolio-tracking app, the preferred compliance posture is zero RMI substance, zero RMI employees, foreign board meetings, and foreign-hosted payment rails, coupled with accurate out-of-scope notification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Incorporation

Statutory sanction: RMI courts may disregard the corporate legal personality under the Business Corporations Act where the entity is a mere façade. The company may be struck off, its certificate of status revoked, and the Revenue and Taxation Office may assess the 3% Business Tax plus penalties of 50% of the tax and interest; the foreign jurisdiction may impose its own fraud penalties, which are enforceable through TIEA evidence exchange.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fraudulent incorporation and the making of false statutory declarations. Civil restitution may be ordered against directors for all profits channeled through the sham, plus costs. Directors face travel bans where the High Court issues asset preservation or surrender-of-passport orders. Criminal liability under the Criminal Code extends to imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of up to USD 50,000 per count of false statement or tax evasion assistance.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Combatting the Financing of Terrorism Act 2014, the corporation is liable to fines up to USD 1,000,000, forfeiture of all transaction proceeds, revocation of the BCA certificate, and inclusion on public adverse lists. The Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze accounts and prohibit disposition of assets without court order.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable where they knew or should have known of suspicious activity and omitted to file a suspicious transaction report. Sanctions include personal fines up to USD 100,000, civil restitution of laundered sums, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans pending investigation, and imprisonment of up to twelve years. Negligence of a registered agent is no defense; the director who signs the annual report certifying the UBO register faces perjury exposure.
Economic Substance False Declaration

Statutory sanction: The Competent Authority may levy penalties up to USD 10,000 per contravention, daily penalties for continuing default, and apply to the High Court for deregistration. A false substance declaration is treated as a criminal false representation, exposing the company to dissolution and loss of the right to hold assets, with consequential freezing of Stripe and merchant accounts by payment processors upon adverse registry notice.

Director personal liability: The responsible person and all approving directors face personal fines of up to USD 5,000, imprisonment of up to three months for fraudulent declaration, and civil liability for losses sustained by creditors and shareholders who relied on the filing. The High Court may order disqualification from acting as directors of any RMI company for up to ten years and impose a travel ban pending enforcement of penalties.
Unlicensed Financial Services and Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: RMI law prohibits unlicensed banking and money services conduct under the Banking Act and the Financial Institutions Act; contravention attracts fines up to USD 500,000, restitution orders, and seizures. Simultaneously, Stripe and Apple will terminate the merchant account and freeze reserves, and the foreign consumer regulator may assess penalties for unauthorized fintech operations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure the product to evade licensing in target jurisdictions are personally liable for consumer harm, regulatory fines imposed abroad, and civil restitution of all fees collected. The RMI High Court will assist foreign judgment creditors through the Foreign Judgments Reciprocal Enforcement Act, enabling attachment of personal assets. Where investor losses are traced to the entity's misrepresentation, directors face indictment for wire fraud and securities fraud in the investor's home state, with extradition exposure and imprisonment of up to twenty years; travel bans are routinely imposed pending extradition review.

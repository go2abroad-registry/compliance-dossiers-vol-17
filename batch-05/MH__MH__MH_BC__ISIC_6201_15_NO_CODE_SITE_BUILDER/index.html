Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro, MH 96960); no integrated national income-tax e-portal exists. Corporate registration and annual filings are administered separately by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations through licensed registered agents (practical portal/account access held by the entity's RMI registered agent).

E-government filing portal

No statutory TIN/VAT ID procurement is required for a non-resident Business Corporation ('BC'). The RMI levies no general corporate income tax, and the Business Privilege Tax ('BPT') reaches only persons that are 'engaged in business in the Republic.' A non-resident BC selling no-code web builder software exclusively to non-resident customers has no nexus, no registration form, and no TIN. If the BC later opens an RMI office, employs RMI staff, or sells locally, it must register for BPT with the Revenue and Taxation Division and obtain a BPT account number within the prescribed registration period; no other national tax registration exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

RMI has no check-the-box regime and no hybrid mismatch election because it has no income tax statute. The relevant statutory election is corporate-status election under the Business Corporations Act: the BC may designate itself in its articles as a 'non-resident domestic corporation' (no RMI office, assets, or business), which confirms exclusion from BPT and qualifies it for tax-exempt foreign-source treatment. Non-resident status can also be effected by filing the requisite statement with the Registrar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the RMI has no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. Indirect levies are limited to customs import duties, excise duties on alcohol/tobacco/fuel, and the 2% export tax on marine products. Digital services supplied by a non-resident BC to foreign customers are outside the RMI indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST threshold exists. BPT registration arises only upon the commencement of business activity in RMI, without a fixed low-value exemption threshold for non-resident digital vendors; a non-resident BC with no RMI physical or sales nexus is not required to register in any local-currency amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – the RMI has no VAT/GST regime and therefore no 0% export rating or refund system. Digital software/SaaS exports by a non-resident BC are simply outside the RMI tax base; no zero-rating certificate, export evidence, or refund claim is required or available.

No clawback mechanism exists because no zero-rating refund exists. General enforcement: a misdeclared 'non-resident' BC that actually carries on business in RMI may be retroactively assessed BPT on RMI-source gross receipts with interest and late-filing/late-payment penalties; false tax or customs declarations are subject to civil fraud penalties and criminal fines/forfeiture under RMI revenue and customs law; the RMI imposes no exchange controls, so the 180-day repatriation convention reflects foreign bank/payment-rail requirements rather than an RMI statutory deadline.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI maintains no exchange-control regime; both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely and fully convertible because the domestic currency is the U.S. dollar. There are no statutory restrictions on remitting dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees to non-resident shareholders and no central-bank approval is required for profit repatriation. Practical hurdles are confined to commercial-bank due diligence, correspondent-chain sanctions screening, and de-risking of remote-gaming or offshore-holding profiles; currency-transaction reporting applies at the USD 10,000 threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation requirement applies to export receipts in the RMI. A non-resident Business Corporation may retain, allocate, and disburse SaaS subscription revenue and other export proceeds freely in USD without conversion to an authorized dealer. The only affirmative obligations are record-keeping, currency-transaction reporting, and suspicious-transaction reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit under the Money Laundering Control Act 2012, together with any OFAC or correspondent-bank compliance procedures applicable to the USD clearing chain.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no domestic central bank or foreign-exchange administration; the U.S. dollar is sole legal tender under the Currency Act 1982, so monetary policy and currency conditions are effectively determined by the U.S. Federal Reserve System. Banking and financial-institution licensing, prudential supervision, and AML/CFT enforcement reside with the Office of the Banking Commissioner of the RMI Ministry of Finance, supported by the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit under the Money Laundering Control Act 2012.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. The RMI has no domestic EMI or digital-bank licensing regime; foreign EMI licenses are not passportable, and unlicensed deposit-taking is prohibited by the Banking Act. Global USD EMIs and neobanks generally impose enhanced due diligence or decline RMI-registered entities because of correspondent-bank de-risking. For a Low_Risk_Standard ISIC 6201 no-code website builder, the recommended architecture is to use a U.S.-chartered fintech and the Stripe Billing and Paddle collection rails for customer payments while maintaining the entity's primary treasury account at Bank of Marshall Islands.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be remitted in USD as SWIFT MT103, or Fedwire/CHIPS where funds originate from the United States, to the beneficiary BIC or ABA routing number of the RMI account at Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam. The remittance must reference the subscription, loan, or share-capital agreement and carry an internationally recognized ISO 20022 purpose code such as EQUI for equity contributions, LOAN for intercompany loans, ROYA for royalties and software license fees, or CCFT for cost-fee transfers. The RMI prescribes no statutory purpose-code table or exchange-control pre-notification; however, aggregate inflows of USD 10,000 or more require currency-transaction reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, and inflows above USD 250,000 trigger enhanced source-of-funds verification by the receiving bank. To avoid recharacterization of hidden equity, all capital must be documented either as paid-in shares evidenced by a subscription agreement and board minutes, or as arm's-length shareholder loans evidenced by a notarized loan agreement with repayment schedule, interest terms, and intercompany transfer-pricing documentation.Outbound treasury payments are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 for supplier, payroll, hosting, and marketing disbursements, with the RMI bank instructing its U.S. correspondent for Fedwire or CHIPS settlement; (ii) SWIFT MT202/COV for correspondent-chain liquidity transfers and same-day cover payments; (iii) commercial documentary credits under SWIFT MT700/MT707 and ICC UCP 600 for procurement of infrastructure, software development capacity, or equipment requiring bank-guaranteed settlement; and (iv) Stripe Billing and Paddle for card-network clearing of customer refunds, chargebacks, and subscription-billing adjustments into and out of the USD account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident Business Corporation (BC); the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act imposes no corporate income tax on foreign-source income and the entity operates under a tax-exempt zone classification.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Business Corporation to its shareholders, regardless of shareholder residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — no corporate income tax base exists for a non-resident BC, therefore no arm's length adjustment regime, Local File, or Master File documentation obligation is triggered; the effective threshold is USD 0 (no TP statute).

Economic substance scope: Applicable — the Economic Substance Regulations 2020 (enacted under the Economic Substance (Implementation) Act) require International Companies and, in defined cases, Business Corporations conducting 'relevant activities' to demonstrate adequate substance in the Marshall Islands: board meetings and management in Majuro, local books and records, and sufficient employees and expenditure proportionate to the activity. The target no-code website builder (ISIC 6201) must be specifically assessed against the 'intellectual property business' category: if the platform derives royalty or licensing income held in the RMI, full ESR reporting and the CIGAR (Core Income Generating Activities) return are mandatory. If the BC merely holds equity interests in an operating subsidiary and earns only dividends and capital gains, it qualifies for the passive pure-equity-holding exemption and is excused from active substance. A failure to file ESR annual returns is treated as a separate registrable offense.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unlicensed Domestic Business

Statutory sanction: Retroactive assessment of domestic corporate income tax at the applicable Income Tax Act rates, plus interest and penalties for underpayment; business license fines of up to USD 5,000; the Registrar may strike the company off the register and revoke the Certificate of Good Standing.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be held personally liable for taxes withheld or assessed where they knowingly authorized domestic trading without license; civil restitution of improperly distributed dividends; criminal prosecution for making false declarations carries fines and imprisonment.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Initial financial penalty up to USD 10,000; continued default attracts additional penalties up to USD 20,000; the Registrar may replace or remove the registered agent, and the company may be struck off the register.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize false ESR declarations are subject to personal fines and disqualification from acting as directors for up to 5 years; the High Court may impose travel bans and asset-protection orders pending investigation of cross-border substance arbitrage.
Money Laundering and Shell Company Abuse (AML)

Statutory sanction: Corporate fines up to USD 500,000; criminal liability for natural persons with imprisonment up to 15 years; asset freezing and civil asset forfeiture orders; international mutual legal assistance proceedings may be initiated by foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable for conspiracy and knowingly permitting the laundering of proceeds; the High Court may issue travel bans and exit restrictions pending extradition or mutual assistance; conviction results in mandatory imprisonment.
Fraudulent Trading and Misrepresentation to Creditors

Statutory sanction: Judicial winding-up, civil restitution orders in favor of creditors; penalties under the Business Corporations Act for false statutory declarations of up to USD 5,000 per offense; disqualification of directors from managing any RMI corporate entity.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors are liable without limitation for fraudulent trading; the High Court may issue arrest warrants and travel bans, impose asset-protection orders, and on conviction directors face imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Administration (RICA), Ministry of Finance, Majuro Atoll (administers Gross Revenue Tax, Wage Tax, and customs duties; paper-based filings, no national e-portal). Corporate and NRDC registration: Business Corporations Division, Office of the Attorney General, Majuro.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Business Corporations Act (BCA) through a licensed registered agent, filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations. Step 2: File the annual Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) Declaration to perfect the tax-exempt classification; no RICA-issued TIN is needed for an entity with zero RMI-source revenue. Step 3: If the entity ever derives RMI-source gross revenue, register with RICA under the Gross Revenue Tax Act to obtain a TIN and file periodic GRT returns; a pure NRDC liquidating branded overstock apparel entirely offshore remains outside the GRT net and holds no RICA TIN. Step 4: Maintain the registered agent and annual report/NRDC filings to preserve standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory territorial regime: RMI imposes no income tax, capital gains tax, dividend/interest/royalty withholding tax, or estate tax; the NRDC is also exempt from the 3% Gross Revenue Tax and 8% Wage Tax on all non-RMI source activity, provided the annual NRDC declaration is filed)

Available election forms

No check-the-box, look-through, or hybrid mismatch election exists in RMI law. The operative election is BCA Section 106 NRDC status, elected by filing the NRDC Declaration and paying the prescribed annual fee; revocation occurs automatically on deriving RMI-source income. An RMI LLC may elect entity-level taxation, but no pass-through regime is codified, and hybrid mismatch outcomes are moot because non-resident wholly offshore profits are untaxed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. The only indirect-type levies are the 3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) on gross revenue sourced within RMI and customs import tariffs under the Customs Tariff Act; exports of goods from RMI are duty-free and out of scope of the GRT.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A for VAT (no VAT legislation exists). For GRT: RICA registration is triggered at the first dollar of RMI-source gross revenue; no de minimis threshold applies to wholesale trade, but the NRDC's offshore resale of finished garments creates zero RMI-source gross revenue and therefore no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not apply (no VAT system). The functional equivalent under the GRT: revenue from finished garments sold to offshore liquidation buyers is non-RMI source when title and risk of loss pass outside RMI (e.g., FOB/EXW terms with ocean carriage to U.S. or EU buyers), thereby falling outside the 3% GRT base. No export license, export duty, or VAT refund mechanism applies to apparel exports; the NRDC exemption provides a complete refund-free non-taxation outcome.

  • Commercial invoice with FOB/EXW terms, HS 6201-6217 classification, and foreign destination
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing offshore delivery from transshipment hub (e.g., Singapore or Hong Kong) to U.S./EU off-price retailers
  • Packing list matching the invoice
  • Purchase order and vendor liquidation agreement proving acquired overstock from brand owners
  • SWIFT/ACH inward remittance advices demonstrating settlement from non-RMI bank accounts
  • NRDC annual declaration filing receipt preserving tax-exempt status

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, government API, or digital stamping mandate exists. Commercial invoices and standard trade documents are retained in paper or PDF format for audit purposes; customs processes are limited to import declarations only.

No FX repatriation deadline or exchange-control regime exists (USD is the domestic currency and is freely convertible). RICA audit risk is limited to mis-declared RMI-source activity: if goods are sold to RMI residents or delivered within RMI, the 3% GRT applies retroactively with monthly late-payment interest, a non-filing penalty of 25% of tax due, and potential criminal fines for wilful evasion; the NRDC status is stripped, exposing the full gross revenue to GRT for the tainted period. No VAT clawback is possible since no VAT was chargeable; the decisive compliance obligation is the truthful annual NRDC declaration of zero RMI-source income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI operates under full dollarization and therefore has no native currency requiring convertibility. Current-account and capital-account transactions are wholly liberalized: trade payments, dividends, interest, capital distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred without exchange-control licensing, profit-remittance approval, or foreign-investment screening. A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) is taxed only on RMI-source income; offshore wholesale-trading income is tax-exempt, with no supplementary withholding or repatriation charge. Practical frictions are limited to correspondent-bank AML/KYC scrutiny of the BC's beneficial-ownership chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The RMI imposes no statutory timeline for bringing export proceeds onshore, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to hold export receipts with an RMI-licensed bank. USD proceeds from off-price garment liquidation trades may remain in any global USD correspondent, client, or fintech account indefinitely; the entity may freely allocate liquidity across its banking network.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) — prudential banking supervisor under the Banking Act 1991, as amended. The RMI maintains no independent central bank of issue; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender and monetary policy is effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve System.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for USD-denominated B2B apparel trade settlements in the USD 5,000–250,000 band. Regulated global EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) offer compliant MT103/ACH corridors and can accelerate payments to APAC garment suppliers and US/EU off-price retail buyers. Legal constraints: the RMI has no EMI authorization regime, so the EMI must be licensed in its home jurisdiction (FCA, AUSTRAC, HK, or similar), its services are lawful but not prudentially protected in the RMI, and the EMI cannot be the sole custodian of the entity's capital contribution, which must initialize through an RMI-licensed or US-licensed bank ledger to evidence capital and tangible substance.

Compliant routing: injected capital must arrive via SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for amounts above USD 100,000 originating in the United States) from the shareholder's account into the entity's Operational USD Settlement Account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not impose a corporate income tax on non-resident business corporations and has no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File/Master File requirement, and no statutory currency threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands does not maintain a general economic substance regime comparable to OECD ESR frameworks. However, the Business Corporations Act requires a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation to have no commercial activity, no local-source income, and no RMI real property interests; failure to observe this condition voids the tax exemption. A remote apparel wholesale liquidation entity that merely invoices from the RMI without local operational substance may be disregarded or subject to reclassification in a foreign jurisdiction under its CFC, PFIC, or permanent establishment rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Misrepresentation of Non-Resident Status / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Automatic loss of the non-resident tax exemption; the RMI Registrar may revoke the corporation's non-resident designation, require conversion to a domestic business corporation, and impose a monetary penalty of up to USD 10,000 under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false declaration become jointly and severally liable for all penalties and unpaid franchise fees; the RMI courts may impose a travel ban prohibiting departure from the Marshall Islands until the liability is settled, and directors may be disqualified from serving as directors or officers of any RMI corporation for a period of five years.
Tax Evasion / Sham Transaction

Statutory sanction: The RMI itself does not impose income tax on the conduit, but the foreign tax authority may disregard the separate existence of the RMI entity, reattribute its profits, and assess penalties for hostile transfer pricing; additionally, under RMI anti-abuse principles, the Registrar may refuse to issue a Certificate of Good Standing if the entity is found to be a shell.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the sham may be exposed to civil claims in the foreign jurisdiction for conspiracy and dishonest assistance; if foreign tax fraud charges are brought, the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may cooperate under exchange-of-information agreements, and directors may face extradition for tax-related money laundering where criminal proceeds are traced through RMI bank accounts.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, a body corporate convicted of a money-laundering offense is liable to a fine of up to USD 1,000,000; the non-resident corporation may also have its registration suspended for unlawful financial conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted individually, facing imprisonment of up to 10 years and personal fines of up to USD 250,000; additionally, RMI courts may prohibit the directors from operating any corporate entity within the jurisdiction and may seize assets flowing through RMI accounts.
Annual Filing and Franchise Fee Delinquency

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution of the corporation by the Registrar; the entity loses its legal capacity to enter into enforceable contracts, transfer title, or maintain bank accounts in the corporate name; reinstatement is possible only with payment of all arrears, penalties, and a reinstatement fee.

Director personal liability: If directors continue to trade with dissolved corporate status, they are personally liable for all debts and obligations incurred during the period of non-compliance; the RMI court may grant injunctions preventing the directors from leaving the country and may order the production of personal financial records to satisfy corporate creditors.

E-government filing portal

No income-tax TIN or VAT ID exists for non-resident Business Corporations (BC) in the RMI; the Certificate of Incorporation is the sole statutory identifier. Procedure: (1) file Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations through a licensed RMI registered agent, electing non-resident domestic corporation status; (2) pay the fixed government filing fee (approximately USD 200-500 depending on share structure); (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (4) if the corporation holds no RMI assets, employs no RMI residents, and does not do business in the RMI, no further revenue-authority registration is triggered; (5) only upon physical import of goods, local employment, or leasing of RMI premises would the entity require an importer registration, municipal business license, and Social Security Administration registration.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not Applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. A customs importer registration may be triggered on physical importation of goods (duty generally ranges from 2% to 15% depending on tariff classification); VPN network privacy services delivered electronically have no customs nexus and no registration obligation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility exists under RMI law. No exchange controls, capital account licensing, or profit-remittance permits apply to resident or non-resident business corporations. Dividends, shareholder distributions, and liquidation proceeds are freely repatriated in USD, subject only to the solvency and balance-sheet distribution tests under the Business Corporations Act 1990. Current-account items (SaaS subscription income, royalty and licensing streams, management fees) are freely transferable without central bank approval. The sole practical restriction is extraterritorial: RMI banks clear exclusively through U.S. correspondent accounts, so every USD transfer is screened under U.S. OFAC sanctions and Bank Secrecy Act rules, exposing the account to freeze risk if a counterparty is sanctioned or anomalous.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The RMI imposes no repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export receipts. Digital software exporters, including the VPN merchant, may keep receipts in any bank in any jurisdiction, hold any currency, and convert at commercial market rates without regulatory filing.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The RMI maintains no domestic central bank; the U.S. dollar is sole legal tender under the Amended Compact of Free Association, making the U.S. Federal Reserve the de facto monetary anchor and U.S. dollar clearing systems the domestic payment backbone.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium with conditions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A — no transfer pricing legislation, Local File, or Master File threshold exists in the Marshall Islands. The RMI does not impose corporate income tax, and no arm's-length adjustment is made for tax-exempt non-resident corporations. Where the ultimate beneficial owner is tax-resident in a high-tax jurisdiction, that jurisdiction's transfer pricing rules apply extraterritorially; contemporaneous documentation and substance records should nevertheless be retained to defend the non-resident status.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands adopts economic substance requirements via its Economic Substance Regulations. A non-resident Business Corporation is excluded from these obligations if it is not tax resident in the Marshall Islands and can evidence that management and control are exercised in another jurisdiction where it is subject to tax. ESP operations, including VPN and digital encryption software services, are not a standard pure-holding relevant activity unless the entity owns high-value intellectual property and conducts IP-holding functions. In any event, the company should retain board minutes demonstrating foreign management, keep its registered agent and office in the RMI, avoid local employees or office leases in the RMI, and never claim RMI tax residency. Failure to demonstrate non-resident status may bring the entity within the RMI ESR reporting framework, requiring an economic substance return and proof of adequate physical presence, qualified staff, and expenditure in the Marshall Islands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Foreign Tax Evasion / Offshore Abuse

Statutory sanction: In the United States, IRC §7201 criminal tax evasion is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a maximum fine of US$250,000 for individuals (US$500,000 for corporations), plus payment of all evaded taxes and interest. The civil fraud penalty under IRC §6663 adds 75% of the underpayment attributable to fraud. Similar offshore-asset penalties may apply under FBAR for undisclosed accounts.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing is available when the BC is a sham or mere instrumentality. Willful U.S. persons who are directors or shareholders can be held personally liable for FBAR penalties reaching 50% of hidden account balances per violation, and may be subjected to extradition, travel bans, and imprisonment for tax evasion conspiracy.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Economic Substance Regulations and Business Corporations Act, the Registrar may impose a fixed administrative penalty starting at US$10,000 for a first offence, escalating to US$50,000 for repeated non-compliance, in addition to enforcement strike-off and loss of good standing. Continued failure may result in compulsory dissolution and asset vesting in the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false non-resident declarations or provide misleading economic substance information can be prosecuted locally, with imprisonment of up to one year in addition to individual fines. Foreign courts may additionally treat such filings as director misconduct, creating personal civil liability for resulting losses.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands AML framework, together with its Criminal Code, treats money laundering and failure to report suspicious transactions as criminal offences. Penalties may reach 15 years' imprisonment and substantial fines, while asset forfeiture and freezing orders can without notice immobilise corporate bank accounts and third-party payment reserves.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly facilitate laundering of proceeds through the VPN platform are subject to prosecution as accomplices; personal account assets are reachable through confiscation orders, travel bans are commonly issued in cross-border investigations, and prison terms run consecutively where multiple predicate offences are established.
Unauthorized RMI Domestic Operations / Gross Revenue Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: The RMI Gross Revenue Tax law imposes a fixed gross receipts tax on any person or business engaged in commercial activity within the Marshall Islands; operating without a license and without filing exemptions triggers back assessments, interest, daily penalties for each day of unlicensed operation, and possible administrative suspension of local payment and banking activities.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately misrepresent the non-resident status or who cause the company to conceal local revenue may be held personally liable for unpaid gross revenue taxes and penalty assessments. In aggravated cases, criminal sanctions apply and directors may be prohibited from leaving the jurisdiction pending resolution of the tax debt.

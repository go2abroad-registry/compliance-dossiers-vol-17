Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; official government portal: https://rmi.gov.mh

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Business Corporations Act 1990 is not issued an RMI Tax Identification Number at incorporation and is not required to procure one unless it derives income sourced within the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of Corporations serves as the statutory identifier for the entity. If a domestic tax obligation arises (e.g., payroll withholding for local employees or withholding tax on RMI-source payments), the BC must register for a TIN with the Revenue and Taxation Office by submitting the prescribed application form, supporting certificate of incorporation, and evidence of a local registered agent; registration is activated within 5–10 business days. A BC with no RMI-source income remains outside the assessed income tax net with no TIN requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Non-Resident Business Corporation treated as tax-exempt on income derived from sources outside the Republic of the Marshall Islands; territorial source taxation applies only to assessed income from RMI sources. Domestic-source income of a non-resident BC is generally subject to a 3% final withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, rents and other specified income; the entity is not liable to corporate income tax on foreign franchise royalties, syrup export sales or offshore management fees.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or analogous entity-classification election exists under Marshall Islands law; classification is fixed by the statutory form of the Business Corporation. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under the Income Tax Act 1989. The non-resident tax exemption is self-executing under the Business Corporations Act and does not require an advance ruling; however, a taxpayer may request a private interpretation from the Revenue and Taxation Office to confirm sourcing and exemption treatment. RMI is an Inclusive Framework member; no Action 2 hybrid-mismatch election mechanism has been domesticated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None_No_VAT_GST_Sales_Use_Tax_In_Force

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax registration regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The only material indirect levy is customs import duty under the Customs and Tariff Act 1990, imposed at the point of importation at a standard ad valorem rate of up to 10% on CIF value; recurring importers register with the Customs Service for an importer code, but there is no economic-nexus turnover threshold. Exports and offshore services are outside the indirect tax base.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

De facto 0% export rate: since the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT/GST, exports of goods (including syrup and beverage concentrate) and offshore franchise services are inherently outside the scope of indirect tax, with no registration or zero-rating claim procedure required. For income-tax purposes, a non-resident Business Corporation is exempt from RMI tax on gross revenue derived from exports and offshore services because such income is not sourced in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; eligibility requires that the supply of goods concludes at export (FOB Majuro or other RMI port) and that franchise royalty services are performed entirely outside RMI or are attributable to foreign franchisees.

None. No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax filing, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is mandated by the Revenue and Taxation Office. Customs formalities for import and export physical clearances are processed through written or digital declarations submitted to the Customs Service of the Marshall Islands; standard commercial invoices and packing lists suffice.

No export zero-rating or VAT refund regime exists in RMI, hence no VAT clawback mechanism arises. However, material misstatements in customs declarations are subject to the penalties of the Customs and Tariff Act, including fines, seizure and forfeiture of goods. Misrepresentations made to the Revenue and Taxation Office with intent to sustain the non-resident exemption, when the income is in fact RMI-source, can void the exemption and retroactively expose the BC to assessed income tax, gross revenue tax where applicable, the 3% withholding tax, late-payment interest, penalties up to the amount of the underpaid tax, and criminal liability for fraudulent filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Freedom of convertibility is absolute by design: the official currency of the RMI is the US dollar, so there is no local exchange rate to administer and no current-account or capital-account restriction. Profits, dividends, royalties, and liquidation proceeds are freely remittable from the RMI without exchange approval, provided the corporate record is clean (audited financial statements, beneficial-ownership declaration, and tax-compliance confirmation). The only practical friction is global correspondent-bank de-risking arising from RMI's offshore-flag profile, which elevates document burdens but does not constitute a legal convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The RMI has no foreign-exchange-control law, no export-proceeds surrender timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio because the US dollar is legal tender. All export receipts (including franchise royalties and syrup-supply proceeds) are retained and settled in USD without obligation to repatriate or convert; however, remittances remain subject to OFAC screening and standard correspondent-bank monitoring under the RMI's Compact-relationship obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Republic of the Marshall Islands (under the Banking Act 1987 and Ministry of Finance); no RMI central bank exists — the United States Federal Reserve System is the de facto monetary and clearing authority because the US dollar is the sole legal tender pursuant to the Compact of Free Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. The RMI has no electronic-money-institution licensing framework, and major overseas digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) generally decline onboarding of RMI-registered holding entities because of correspondent-bank de-risking, jurisdictional reputation concerns, and the absence of a native EMI license. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the RMI entity are executed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers drawn on the parent or investor's bank and routed through the US Federal Reserve clearing system (Fedwire/CHIPS) to BOMI or Bank of Guam. All transfers must be denominated in USD to avoid conversion friction; non-USD injections are converted at the correspondent bank's commercial rate with the purpose code indicated on the wire. Use OECD Balance-of-Payments purpose codes — 'C' for capital transfers (equity injection) or 'F' for intercompany financial loans — and include the FAO account reference, underlying board-resolution reference, and the stamped filing number of the RMI business corporation. Pre-submission of the corporate resolution, source-of-funds statement, and group-structure chart is required so that the receiving bank can clear OFAC screening and complete the compliance hold prior to crediting.Outbound disbursements for franchise-royalty wires and syrup-supply-chain settlements are cleared through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers; bank-to-bank reimbursements for letters of credit use SWIFT MT202 COV. 0% — the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no branch profits tax on Business Corporations (BC). The only revenue-based impost is the Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) Act 1989, applied at 3% exclusively to revenues sourced in the RMI.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends, regardless of the shareholder's residence. Dividends declared by MH_BC to franchise-group shareholders exit RMI free of any WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local TP legislation or documentation threshold exists because RMI levies no income tax. The OECD country-by-country reporting (CbCR) threshold applies only to the ultimate parent jurisdiction of the global boba tea franchise group: when consolidated group revenue ≥ USD 750,000,000, a CbCR filing obligation arises in the group's parent jurisdiction, with MH_BC serving as a constituent entity. Local File and Master File obligations arise, if at all, in the tax jurisdictions of franchisees and the operational parent — not in the RMI. Recommended practicum: maintain a group transfer-pricing policy (royalty 3–6% of franchisee net sales, syrup gross margin 35–55%, management-fee mark-up 5–10% cost-plus) to withstand audits in paying jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: RMI does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to BVI/Cayman. However, the OECD BEPS Inclusive Framework substantial-activity requirement for geographically mobile IP income applies de facto: an RMI BC holding the boba-tea trademark and syrup formula must demonstrate core income-generating activities (strategic decisions, IP development, risk management) where the actual personnel are based (e.g., the Hong Kong franchise headquarters). The RMI Nonresident Domestic Corporation regime imposes an annual 0% tax status but requires that no business be conducted inside the RMI. The EU currently lists the RMI as a non-cooperative jurisdiction for tax purposes, meaning franchisee jurisdictions will apply heightened substance review and conduit-company scrutiny. Practical substance protocol: board meetings physically held in Hong Kong, executed minutes, employment contracts and payroll of the syrup R&D team, and beneficial-ownership transparency at the RMI agent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

False Statements to the Registrar (Corporate Fraud)

Statutory sanction: BCA Section 68: criminal penalty of up to USD 10,000 fine and/or up to five years' imprisonment per false statement; administrative revocation of good standing; involuntary dissolution; directors disqualified from acting as corporate officers in the RMI.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for statutory fraud: each signing director is jointly and severally liable for the full franchise-tax deficiency, treble damages under civil fraud, and counterparty reliance losses; RMI courts may issue asset-freezing orders and travel bans pending trial.
Gross Revenue Tax Evasion on RMI-Source Sales

Statutory sanction: Gross Revenue Tax Act 1989: fine up to USD 10,000, imprisonment up to five years, plus civil assessment of three times the unpaid tax and automatic 10% late surcharge and 1% monthly interest; the RMI Tax Information Authority may suspend the business license of any RMI franchisee involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed the underdeclaration are personally liable for the treble civil assessment; criminal convictions carry a personal restitution order and, under the Compact of Free Association extradition framework, a travel ban enforceable upon departure from the US or RMI.
Conduit / Shell Arrangement and BEPS Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: No RMI penalty (no local income tax); but franchisee-source tax authorities may impose withholding deficiencies of up to 30% on gross royalties, together with interest and penalties of up to 100% of the tax, and the EU listing of RMI triggers defensive anti-abuse rules in EU member states; the entity may be denied GRT/good-standing certifications for misuse of the non-resident status.

Director personal liability: Foreign GAAR/CFC litigation exposes directors who signed certificates of residence or substance to civil breach-of-duty claims; if personal guarantees back the franchise agreements, directors face direct collection on audit adjustments; RMI BCA § 61 indemnification does not cover willful participation in sham structures.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: RMI Beneficial Ownership Act 2020 and Money Laundering Regulations: corporate fine up to USD 500,000; directors' and officers' fines up to USD 100,000 and imprisonment up to ten years; the registered agent may be struck off and the corporation's certificate of registration suspended for 12 months.

Director personal liability: AML obligations attach personally to each director and compliance officer; personal assets are directly attachable for civil penalties; criminal conviction leads to 10-year imprisonment and exclusion from approved RMI corporate registry lists, effectively blocking all future RMI structures.
Persistent Non-Filing and Involuntary Dissolution

Statutory sanction: The Registrar strikes the corporation from the register: all corporate powers cease; existing franchise contracts become voidable by franchisees; the name becomes available to third parties; reinstatement requires payment of arrears, penalties, USD 250 reinstatement fee, and a legalized back-filing package.

Director personal liability: Directors who contract on behalf of a dissolved corporation are personally liable as principals on every transaction; that liability is joint-and-several and may exceed the corporate net assets, converting a zero-tax structure into unlimited direct exposure to the boba franchise counterparties.

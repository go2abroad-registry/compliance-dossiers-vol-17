Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (BCA), Title 51 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code, as amended, particularly Sections 7 (Lawful Purposes), 106 (Articles of Incorporation), 108 (Registered Office and Registered Agent), 111 (Directors), and 149 (Corporate Records, Waiver of Notice, and Action by Written Consent).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form BC-1) filed electronically or in paper form with the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, accompanied by the Registered Agent Consent and Appointment Form.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed; the BCA permits shares with or without par value and any amount of authorized capital. There is no escrow or deposit account requirement for incorporation; the subscriber may declare the issued share consideration fully paid in exchange for services, platform rights, or cash as recorded in the share ledger.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety, structural load, or heavy machinery certification is required for a non-resident software publisher. If a physical office is maintained in Majuro, the premises must comply with the RMI Building Code fire egress and extinguisher requirements, pass an electrical safety inspection, and satisfy workstation ergonomic and workplace health standards under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2007; a standard office occupancy clearance from the local fire authority suffices.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed; the BCA permits shares with or without par value and any amount of authorized capital. There is no escrow or deposit account requirement for incorporation; the subscriber may declare the issued share consideration fully paid in exchange for services, platform rights, or cash as recorded in the share ledger.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (official portal: https://www.rmigovernment.org; tax administration is exercised through the Tax and Revenue Division).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act is exempt from income tax and is not statutorily required to obtain a separate TIN or VAT ID. The Registrar of Corporations issues a unique Corporate Registration Number, which serves as the identity number for all government filings, including the annual government fee and any business license application. If a creditor, bank, or counterparty requires a tax identification number, the Company may file an administrative request with the Tax and Revenue Division attaching the Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing, and the Division issues a letter-based TIN for administrative, FATCA, and CRS identification purposes. This TIN does not impose tax registration or return filing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. The Marshall Islands imposes no entity-level income tax on non-resident Business Corporations and provides no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election in domestic law. A U.S. owner may independently file an entity classification election (Form 8832) under U.S. Treasury Regulations Section 301.7701-3; this election is exclusively a U.S. federal tax action and has no Marshall Islands statutory counterpart.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT, GST, sales tax, or use tax is imposed at the national level; only customs import duties on selected physical goods and certain municipal licenses exist outside the tax-exempt regime).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no indirect tax is levied on non-resident service exporters or on exported digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose a VAT or GST regime, and therefore no export zero-rating allowance or refund mechanism is available or necessary for MMORPG services or digital goods sold by a non-resident Business Corporation to global customers.

  • No customs declaration is required for digital services; physical export shipments, if any, are subject only to the RMI Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate is not required by tax law; it may be requested by banking counterparties for AML/KYC purposes only.

No electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate exists under Marshall Islands law.

Not applicable in zero-rating form because no VAT refund clawback regime exists. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. Dollar is the sole official currency of the Marshall Islands; there is no separate domestic currency and no exchange-control legislation. Capital account and current account transactions are fully convertible, with no statutory barriers to inward investment or outward profit remittance. Repatriation of dividends, royalties, or dissolution proceeds may be effected freely by bank wire, subject only to standard bank due diligence and anti-money-laundering screening. No exchange rate spread or surrender requirement is imposed for non-resident owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export receipts. A Marshall Islands exporter or publisher may retain receivables in U.S. Dollars indefinitely, whether held domestically or abroad, and no remittance into a local account is legally required.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (no formal central bank; the United States Federal Reserve is the de facto issuing authority for the circulating currency, the U.S. Dollar).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. The Marshall Islands does not maintain a dedicated digital currency or electronic money institution (EMI) licensing framework. Offshore digital EMIs must rely on money-transmitter licenses or local bank partnerships to operate in the country, and a fully remote EMI arrangement is generally not sufficient for high-volume MMORPG treasury operations. A licensed U.S.-based money services business or a regulated fintech with a U.S. state Money Transmitter License may be acceptable if a local RMI bank retains the settlement account relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed through the SWIFT MT103 system to the Bank of Marshall Islands or Bank of Guam account using the recipient entity's name, address, and account number. The remitting bank must state the beneficiary as the Marshall Islands Business Corporation and use the SWIFT purpose code COST (for capital contribution) or, for equity investment, the ISO 20022 transaction type code BUSE or INVS. The underlying documents (board resolution, subscribed capital schedule, shareholder identification, and evidence of the source of funds) must be retained and submitted to the receiving bank within its documentary collection window. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 500,000 (the RMI incorporates an IRC Section 482 analog but prescribes no explicit mandatory Local File/Master File threshold; a documentation file is recommended when aggregate related-party cross-border transactions, including app-store settlement flows and IP licensing, exceed USD 500,000 per annum in USD, which is the local currency).

Economic substance scope: The RMI imposes no Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE or BVI. A Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation must, however, demonstrate bona fide existence under common-law veil-piercing standards: a registered agent in the RMI, a physical or agent-held office, quorum-valid board actions, maintained corporate records and statutory registers, and demonstrable decision-making substance for global MMORPG publishing treasury and IP management. Absence of such substance converts a tax-exempt BC into an indicia-bearing shell and invites deregistration and adverse U.S. FATCA/CFC consequences for U.S.-person shareholders.

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of 75% of the underpayment; criminal fine of up to USD 10,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the RMI Income Tax Act criminal provisions; the tax deficiency plus interest becomes a statutory lien on corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the BC is a facade for the dominant owner; directors are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and penalties upon a finding of fraudulent conveyance; willful misstatement by a signing director triggers personal criminal exposure, possible extradition under the RMI-US Compact, travel bans from entering the United States and RMI, and imprisonment.
Shell Company Operation / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Registrar of Corporations may revoke the certificate of incorporation, administratively dissolve the entity, and strike it from the RMI register; the entity loses limited liability protection and exemption status retroactively, triggering the 32% statutory tax and business license back assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution to creditors and shareholders for continued trading after revocation; travel bans may be imposed by RMI courts in fraud actions; egregious abuse may result in criminal charges for false declaration under the Business Corporations Act, with imprisonment of up to 2 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / App-Store Payment Rails Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Banking Act and AML/CFT regulations: monetary penalties of up to USD 500,000 per violation for financial institutions; freezing of corporate accounts; license revocation; criminal money-laundering penalties of USD 500,000 and imprisonment of 10 to 20 years for principals.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the corporation to be used for money laundering are personally subject to criminal indictment, asset seizure and civil forfeiture, international travel bans pending extradition, and imprisonment; personal restitution is ordered for funds laundered through the virtual-currency rails of the MMORPG platform.
Failure of Annual License, Annual Report, and Administration

Statutory sanction: Late penalty of USD 100 per month per report; business license surcharge of 10% of the fee plus daily interest; administrative dissolution and strike-off from the RMI corporate registry; revocation of the certificate of good standing needed for iTunes Connect and Google Play developer account renewals.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prohibited from incorporating or managing a new RMI entity for up to 5 years; continuing to trade after dissolution exposes directors to personal liability for all contracts, app-store obligations, and merchant liabilities under common-law ultra vires doctrine; RMI courts may issue travel bans preventing departure from the country until unpaid state fees are settled.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Cross-Border Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The RMI Tax Office may apply the Section 482 analog to reallocate income, disallow deductions, and assess the 32% headline rate plus interest and a 20% substantial-valuation-misstatement penalty; an adjustment may also trigger U.S. IRC Section 482 cross-border recharacterization of the Global MMORPG enterprise.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve non-arm's-length contracts without supporting comparability studies are personally liable for the artificial deficiency under a fraudulent-conveyance theory; deliberate pricing manipulation may support criminal tax evasion charges with imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans pending trial, and court-ordered civil restitution of the evaded tax plus penalties.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro, MH 96960). No centralized e-filing portal exists; BPT registration and returns are processed physically at the Revenue and Taxation Division, P.O. Box 31, Majuro, Republic of the Marshall Islands.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Business Corporation (BC) under the Business Corporations Act with the Registrar of Corporations (Office of the Attorney General), obtaining a Charter Number (not a TIN). Step 2: If the BC derives gross revenue sourced from business conducted in RMI, file Form BPT-1 with the Revenue and Taxation Division promptly upon commencement of business and obtain the BPT/TIN number; non-citizen-owned entities operating in RMI must also obtain a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) from the Ministry of Commerce, which is a licensing requirement rather than a tax registration. Step 3: If the BC derives no RMI-source revenue (offshore / non-resident profile), no BPT registration, TIN, or FIBL is required; the standard identity document is the Certificate of Good Standing issued by the Registrar of Corporations. Step 4: For physical goods crossing the RMI border, register for a customs importer/exporter code with the RMI Customs and Ports Authority and file entries using the Charter Number. For the non-resident beauty DTC model (Shopify Payments/PayPal, no RMI office, employees, or inventory), no TIN procurement is statutorily required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Not applicable – RMI imposes no corporate income tax and provides no check-the-box, CFE, LLC pass-through, or hybrid mismatch elections. The only available administrative election is the filing of a declaration of non-residence / non-business activity confirming zero RMI-source gross revenue, which evidences the default Tax_Exempt_Zone (offshore) classification; should RMI-source revenue later arise, registration for Business Privilege Tax becomes mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax is imposed by RMI. Analogous levies are: (i) Business Privilege Tax (BPT), a gross-receipts tax at the standard 3% rate (retail/services) applied only to RMI-source gross revenue where business is conducted in RMI; (ii) Import Duty under the Customs Tariff Act on physical goods entering RMI (cosmetics/personal-care products under HS 3304 per the published RMI tariff schedule); and (iii) Social Security contributions at the statutory combined employer/employee rates, only where RMI-resident employees are engaged. A non-resident BC selling DTC cosmetics to non-RMI customers has no RMI indirect-tax nexus.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold applies in USD. BPT registration is mandatory upon the first dollar of RMI-source gross revenue; no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT is imposed. Customs registration applies per import/export entry where goods cross the RMI border.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is conceptually inapplicable because RMI has no VAT/GST and therefore no export VAT refund mechanism. The functional equivalent is the BPT exclusion: revenue from sales by a non-resident BC to customers outside RMI, with delivery outside RMI and no RMI office, employees, or inventory, is entirely outside the BPT base and requires no refund claim. In a pure drop-ship model (goods shipped from US/EU/APAC fulfillment centers directly to non-RMI customers), no RMI customs transaction occurs. Goods physically exported from RMI territory are excluded from BPT gross revenue when the export is documented under the Customs Act and not attributable to an RMI permanent establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs and Ports Authority, filed via ASYCUDA World)
  • Commercial Invoice / Packing List stating ISIC 4791 online-retail descriptor, HS 3304 classification, and full cosmetic ingredient disclosure
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill consigned to the overseas customer
  • Certificate of Origin (issued by the RMI Chamber of Commerce where tariff preference is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds (no RMI FX controls; retained as banking best-practice substantiation, not a statutory mandate)

None. RMI does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API transmission, or digital stamping for sales invoices. The only automated fiscal interface is the electronic customs declaration system (ASYCUDA World) operated by the RMI Customs and Ports Authority for border transactions; no transaction-level fiscal API applies to BPT or retail sales.

No export VAT clawback exists because RMI has no VAT system. Audit risk attaches to BPT characterization: if the Revenue and Taxation Division recharacterizes the BC as having RMI-source gross revenue, BPT at the standard 3% rate is assessed de novo, plus monthly statutory interest and penalties up to 25% of the underpayment, with retroactive registration. False customs export declarations attract Customs Act penalties including forfeiture of goods, fines, and suspension of the customs code. For the Shopify/PayPal DTC model with no RMI nexus, the material exposure is documentary proof of non-RMI delivery (IP geolocation, shipment tracking, payment-rail records), which should be retained for a minimum of 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current and capital accounts is in effect because the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) has no domestic currency; the U.S. dollar is lawful money. There are no exchange-control restrictions on capital movements to or from the RMI, no profit remittance taxes or delays for non-resident business corporations, and no requirement for central bank approval on cross-border transfers. The only compliance conditionals are bank-level AML/CFT screening, FIU reporting thresholds, and documentary support from the client.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable – the RMI imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any USD-denominated account, or in any other currency subject to normal bank risk policies, and may use them directly for operations or reinvestment.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (no domestic central bank; U.S. dollar is legal tender)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low feasibility for use as a primary treasury bank. Under the RMI Banking Act and Financial Institutions Act, any institution undertaking deposit-taking business in or from the RMI must be licensed locally; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut are not licensed in the RMI and cannot legally hold deposits for RMI-incorporated entities. They may serve only as third-party payment facilitators for e-commerce merchant settlement (e.g., Shopify Payments, PayPal) but are not acceptable for capital injection escrow, statutory audits, or cross-border corporate bank account verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory RMI transfer pricing documentation threshold exists in USD or other currency. However, foreign parent jurisdictions may impose OECD/BEPS-aligned Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country reporting obligations once group revenue and cross-border intercompany transactions exceed that foreign threshold (e.g., EUR 750 million for CbCR).

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying out 'relevant activities', including holding, headquarters, finance/leasing, distribution and service centers, and IP exploitation, from the RMI. A non-resident online cosmetics retailer with only a registered agent and no RMI office, employees, management, or physical assets must document that no relevant activity is undertaken in RMI and, where applicable, that the entity is managed and controlled in the foreign beneficial-owner jurisdiction; failure to do so risks classification as a non-compliant shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and Sham Non-Residency

Statutory sanction: Foreign tax authorities may reallocate income under CFC rules, permanent establishment doctrines, substance-over-form anti-abuse rules, and general anti-avoidance provisions. The RMI Registrar may dissolve the BC for false annual filings or for failing to maintain an active registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers engaged in deliberate concealment can be denied the corporate veil and held jointly and severally liable for unpaid foreign taxes, interest, and penalties of the operating business. Criminal tax fraud proceedings may lead to extradition, travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment.
Economic Substance and Shell-Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines and penalties under RMI Economic Substance Regulations, compulsory strike-off, invalidation of the claimed exemption, and heightened regulatory review. Financial institutions may close bank and payment-processor accounts owing to correspondent-bank de-risking and EU/OECD listing concerns.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false economic-substance declarations commit a misrepresentation to the Registrar; they may be disqualified from serving as directors, personally fined, and criminally prosecuted where false documentation forms part of a broader money-laundering or sanctions scheme.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Marshall Islands money-laundering legislation imposes civil monetary penalties and imprisonment of up to 25 years for knowing money laundering; the entity may be subject to asset freezes, enforced deregistration, and restrictions on maintaining a financial account.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are individually prosecutable for inadequate AML controls, conspiracy to launder funds, or facilitating illicit payment processing. Arrest warrants may be accompanied by passport surrender, travel bans, and pre-trial detention.
Misrepresentation to Registrar and Fraudulent Trading

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act and general fraud law, the Registrar may strike the BC, impose civil fines, and apply to the High Court for judicial supervision, liquidation, or compulsory dissolution.

Director personal liability: RMI courts may pierce the corporate veil where the BC is a mere facade, hold directors personally liable for wrongful trading and fraudulent preferences, and issue orders preventing directors from leaving the Marshall Islands when traceable proceeds remain abroad.
Unlicensed Local Business Activity and Foreign Investment Violation

Statutory sanction: Carrying on business without the requisite RMI license subjects the BC to regulatory cease-and-desist orders, revocation of its exemption, import restrictions, monetary penalties, and possible strike-off for exceeding the lawful scope of a non-resident entity.

Director personal liability: Foreign directors who authorize unauthorized local operations may be held personally liable for civil fines and unlicensed-business restitution, denied entry or renewal of work permits, and in cases of persistent regulatory violation, excluded from acting as directors of any RMI-registered entity.

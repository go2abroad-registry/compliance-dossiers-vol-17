Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Business Corporations Act 1990 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no government escrow or deposit is required. The Corporation may be formed with a nominal authorised share capital (commonly US$1,000 divided into 1,000 ordinary shares, of which a single share is issued to the initial subscriber); share subscription consideration is satisfied by contribution of intellectual property or cash and recorded in the share register. No capital escrow account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Safety Inspectorate certification for fire suppression, emergency egress, and flammables storage; structural load inspection and building clearance by the Majuro municipal building authority for the warehouse (≤500 m²); occupational clearance under the RMI Workplace Health and Safety Act 2018, including annual spill-response drills, eye-wash station inspection, and biohazard-handling certification for warehouse personnel.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services, Republic of the Marshall Islands; customs and trade facilitation handled by the Marshall Islands Customs Service within the same Ministry. There is no consolidated national online tax filing portal; registrations and declarations are processed on a paper-based or direct-submission basis at the Ministry's offices in Majuro.

E-government filing portal

The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT, GST, or corporate income tax numbering registry, and therefore does not issue a statutory TIN or VAT ID to foreign entities. A non-resident Business Corporation obtains its legal identity through the Registrar of Corporations (domestic filing under the Business Corporations Act), which issues the Certificate of Incorporation and annual Certificate of Good Standing; no separate tax file number is opened unless the entity employs RMI-resident individuals, in which case an employer Wage Tax registration account is assigned by the Revenue and Taxation Division. Where the entity imports goods, the Marshall Islands Customs Service assigns an Importer of Record identifier (Customs Entry Number) at the first customs declaration; no advance tax pre-registration is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT, GST, or corporate income tax numbering registry, and therefore does not issue a statutory TIN or VAT ID to foreign entities. A non-resident Business Corporation obtains its legal identity through the Registrar of Corporations (domestic filing under the Business Corporations Act), which issues the Certificate of Incorporation and annual Certificate of Good Standing; no separate tax file number is opened unless the entity employs RMI-resident individuals, in which case an employer Wage Tax registration account is assigned by the Revenue and Taxation Division. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box, fiscal transparency, or hybrid mismatch election regime exists because the jurisdiction imposes no corporate income tax, VAT, or GST on non-resident Business Corporations. The entity may nevertheless elect to formalize its non-resident tax-exempt posture by filing a Non-Resident Business Status Declaration and obtaining an Exemption Certificate from the Registrar of Corporations; it may also voluntarily register as an employer for Wage Tax withholding if it later hires RMI citizens or residents, which is an administrative registration rather than a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax in force; indirect fiscal mechanism limited to Customs Import Duty and selective excises (alcohol, tobacco, fuel) at the border

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, any entity importing regulated clinical diagnostics must hold a valid Business License issued by the RMI Registrar and an Importer of Record registration; the economic turnover threshold is zero because even a single import shipment creates a customs duty filing obligation at the standard MFN tariff rates applicable to the HS classification of the goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT or GST, so there is no 'zero rating' mechanism for exported goods or offshore services. Goods exported from RMI territory are simply outside the scope of customs duty and no indirect tax is levied. A limited duty drawback right exists under customs regulations for import duty paid on inputs that are subsequently re-exported in the same or processed form; this is an administrative refund of customs duty rather than a VAT zero-rating claim. For the wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents, no RMI indirect tax is assessed on offshore sales, and no local refund claim will arise unless duty-paid samples are re-exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Outward Cargo Manifest filed with Marshall Islands Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical export from RMI jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/TT confirmation evidencing payment (kept as documentary evidence; RMI imposes no statutory FX controls)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the diagnostic reagents
  • Certificate of Origin or Free Sale Certificate where required by the destination country; RMI does not mandate export permits for laboratory diagnostic reagents, but the exporter must maintain the supplier's ISO/CE/IVD documentation for audit purposes

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, digital stamping, or API-based reporting mandate exists in the Marshall Islands. Commercial invoices are generated freely by the exporter and presented to the Customs Service with the import declaration; customs processing is conducted through the RMI Customs automated clearance system (ASYCUDA-equivalent manual/semi-electronic workflow) without government-mandated e-invoicing, digital signatures, or fiscal stamping.

Because no VAT system exists, fraudulent export zero-rating claims are structurally unavailable; audit scrutiny instead targets customs duty drawback fraud and import undervaluation. Penalties under the Marshall Islands Customs Act include seizure and forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties of up to three times the duty evaded, revocation of the Importer of Record registration, and potential criminal fines or imprisonment for wilful falsification of customs declarations. Any duty drawback obtained on the basis of false export documentation is retrospectively clawed back with interest, and the exporter's business license may be suspended by the Registrar of Corporations. In practice, the RMI Revenue and Taxation Division reviews duty drawback claims against outward manifests, bills of lading, and bank remittance records, applying a 6-year litigation window for recovery of fraudulently obtained refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The jurisdiction is fully dollarized: current-account convertibility is unrestricted and no exchange-approval regime applies to capital transactions. A non-resident Business Corporation may remit dividends, loan repayments, capital reductions and liquidation proceeds freely and without RMI withholding tax; the only practical hurdles are correspondent-bank due diligence, the APG mutual-evaluation findings and the FinCEN Section 971A proposed designation concerning the SOV digital-currency programme, all of which heighten documentation expectations rather than restrict convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

RMI law imposes no export-proceeds surrender or repatriation obligation: the effective conversion ratio is 0% and there is no statutory timeline for bringing export receipts onshore. Receipts may be retained in USD or any convertible foreign currency in the BC's own accounts, subject solely to AML/CFT record-keeping obligations under the Banking Act 1990 and the RMI AML/CFT Regulations 2021.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no statutory central bank; the Banking Commissioner appointed under the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1990 supervises licensed financial institutions, while the United States Federal Reserve System functions as the de facto monetary authority because the US dollar is the sole legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. The RMI Banking Act 1990 neither licenses nor passports digital EMI platforms, and non-resident offshore EMIs have no clearing access to RMI's domestic payment systems. An offshore EMI (e.g., UK or EU-authorized) may legally hold an account for the MH_BC as a supplementary payout conduit in EUR or GBP, but correspondent de-risking of RMI-registered entities makes it fragile; the Marshal Islands SOV digital-currency initiative is not suitable for corporate treasury operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — RMI levies no corporate income tax and has enacted no transfer-pricing documentation requirement, Local File or Master File threshold. If the BC is a controlled wholesale distribution entity of a multinational group, transfer-pricing documentation is nevertheless required by the parent's tax jurisdiction under OECD BEPS standards (commonly triggered above EUR 750,000 intragroup revenues).

Economic substance scope: The RMI has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations and imposes no ESR profit or economic-presence test on non-resident business corporations; however, foreign CFC, anti-avoidance and bank-due-diligence rules require the entity to demonstrate genuine commercial activity, including physical records, director oversight, procurement contracts, and payment flows consistent with its IVD reagent wholesale function.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Non-Resident Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Tax and Revenue Code, false declarations attract criminal fines and imprisonment for up to three years, together with re-assessment of the 3% gross revenue tax, interest and penalties on previously exempt receipts; tax authorities in the customer market (US, EU, LATAM, APAC) may additionally impose CFC, FATCA and GAAR adjustments, including interest and penalties up to 100-120% of the avoided foreign tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false certificates may be prosecuted individually; RMI courts following Delaware veil-piercing doctrine will hold directors jointly and severally liable for restitution of diverted profits and unpaid taxes where the corporation is operated as an alter ego.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Although the RMI imposes no domestic ESR penalty, the Registrar may initiate involuntary dissolution proceedings and the company will be universally treated as a shell by financial institutions, leading to account termination and consortium blacklisting; EU and OECD defensive measures may strip extraterritorial tax benefits and deny treaty access in all trading jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced for fraud, undercapitalization and complete control; directors face civil restitution, disqualification orders, and in aggravated cross-border fraud cases, travel bans and extradition requests supported by INTERPOL notices.
AML/CFT and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: RMI AML/CFT legislation (as amended) provides for monetary penalties of up to US$1 million and imprisonment for up to five years for money laundering and failure to report; due diligence failures by the registered agent also trigger revocation of the corporate license, asset freezing, and correspondent banking restrictions.

Director personal liability: Officers and compliance directors bear personal criminal exposure for knowing participation in laundering of proceeds and sanctions evasion; civil claims from defrauded procurement ministries are enforceable against directors personally where the entity was merely a conduit.
Unlicensed Local Business Operation

Statutory sanction: Operation without a license is punishable as a misdemeanor under the RMI Business Licensing Law with fines, imprisonment of up to one year, forfeiture of profits, and retroactive imposition of the 3% gross revenue tax plus penalties; the certified non-resident tax-exempt status is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized local sales are personally liable for unpaid taxes and licensing penalties; veil-piercing is available to tax authorities and creditors where the entity continued trading after revocation of its non-resident status.

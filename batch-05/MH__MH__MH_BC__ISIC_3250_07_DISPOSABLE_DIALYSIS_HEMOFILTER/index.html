Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance – Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO); no centralized e-portal; registration via paper forms filed at RTO offices in Majuro or Ebeye.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity obtains a TIN by: (i) filing RTO Form T-101 'Application for Tax Identification Number' with a certified copy of its certificate of incorporation; (ii) submitting a sworn declaration of non-resident status and absence of local business operations; (iii) designating a registered agent in the Marshall Islands if process service is required; (iv) paying the USD 50 registration fee plus any annual non-resident business corporation maintenance fee; (v) receiving a TIN. No separate VAT ID is issued because the RMI levies no value added tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign non-resident entity obtains a TIN by: (i) filing RTO Form T-101 'Application for Tax Identification Number' with a certified copy of its certificate of incorporation; (ii) submitting a sworn declaration of non-resident status and absence of local business operations; (iii) designating a registered agent in the Marshall Islands if process service is required; (iv) paying the USD 50 registration fee plus any annual non-resident business corporation maintenance fee; (v) receiving a TIN. No separate VAT ID is issued because the RMI levies no value added tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. A non-resident Business Corporation may elect under the Business Corporations Act to operate exclusively outside the Republic of the Marshall Islands, thereby remaining outside the Gross Revenue Tax and net income tax base. No hybrid mismatch election is recognized under RMI tax law. An entity that elects to conduct local business may be subjected to the Income Tax Act 1989 and Gross Revenue Tax regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; a 3% direct Gross Revenue Tax applies only to RMI-source business income),

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – the RMI imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax registration requirement. A Gross Revenue Tax registration (Form T-201) is required only if the non-resident entity elects to conduct a local trade or business in the Marshall Islands, and no minimum revenue threshold is prescribed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since the RMI imposes no VAT/GST, a statutory zero-rating mechanism is inapplicable. Export sales effected by a non-resident Business Corporation with no RMI trade or business are not subject to RMI direct taxes and therefore enjoy an effective zero-tax result. If the entity were to operate from a physical RMI location, export transactions would not qualify for any codified exemption; tax neutrality would depend on the non-resident status election or on the absence of Marshall Islands-source income under the Income Tax Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current- and capital-account transactions. The RMI is an Article VIII IMF member country with no exchange-control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or trade settlements. For a non-resident Business Corporation in the Tax-Exempt Zone, profit remittance is unrestricted and not subject to dividend withholding tax; however, a 3% gross revenue tax may apply solely to RMI-source gross revenue, which is materially inapplicable to this outside-ISIC-3250 manufacturing/trading vehicle if no local-source income is generated. In practice, correspondent U.S. clearing banks, not RMI statute, impose the operational convertibility tests through OFAC and beneficial-ownership screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The RMI imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation period for export receipts. All hard-currency export proceeds from the sale of disposable dialysis hemofilters may be retained in USD anywhere in the world, used directly to settle foreign supplier invoices, or cleared through any RMI-licensed or foreign correspondent bank, provided the underlying trade transactions comply with U.S. OFAC and the bank's AML/KYC program.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner, Division of Banking and Insurance, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The RMI maintains no domestic central bank; monetary policy aligns with the United States Federal Reserve System because the U.S. dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, and the Banking Act 1991 governs all licensed banks and trust companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Medium for a pure RMI tax-exempt non-resident vehicle. The RMI Banking Act 1991 does not provide a dedicated EMI or payment-institution licensing framework; unlicensed international EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Mercury) typically flag non-resident Marshall Islands corporations as shell-risk and reject onboarding absent local substance or a primary U.S. relationship. A practical approach is to establish the Bank of Guam relationship first, then connect a U.S.-regulated EMI (e.g., Mercury or Relay) as a controlled secondary outflow sweep for small disbursements; all EMI settlements must remain USD-only and back-to-back with invoice trails from the dialysis-hemofilter supply chain. The RMI's central-bank-less 'Sovereign' digital-currency initiative adds legal uncertainty, so crypto-based EMIs are prohibited for treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended route: Shareholder/Parent Bank → U.S. money-center correspondent (e.g., JPMorgan Chase or Bank of America New York) → Bank of Guam (Guam U.S. settlement point) → RMI operational account at Bank of Guam Majuro branch. Use SWIFT MT103 with UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference); narrative fields: 'EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SCRIP 2024 — MH_BC POLYETHERSULFONE DIALYZER PLANT'. RMI law imposes no capital-approval or purpose-code requirement, but the receiving bank's model validation will request: (i) certified Board Resolution authorizing the injection, (ii) updated share register and premium ledger, (iii) source-of-funds statement from the remitting shareholder, and (iv) if > USD 500,000, a funds-confirmation executed by the CFO or director. For optimum clearing, use ISO 20022 equivalent fields (if available) and send a predecessor camt.053/camt.054 advice; the entire USD leg settles via Fedwire/CHIPS and no FX conversion is needed.Primary clearing rail: USD SWIFT MT103 (GPI) through Bank of Guam for operational disbursements below USD 250,000, including sterilization services, freight, legal fees, and testing laboratories. High-value procurement of polyethersulfone resin, hollow-fiber membrane rolls, potting compounds, and injection-molded housings should be settled via irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (ICC UCP600), issued from Bank of Guam with MT700 issuance and MT707 amendments; advance payments are covered by MT798 bank-to-bank reimbursements. For Japanese hollow-fiber or German resin suppliers invoicing in JPY/EUR, obtain USD-denominated LC quotations so that the RMI BC never carries unhedged FX in the clearing process; settlement occurs at the confirming bank. For cGMP cleanroom equipment imports, use standby LCs (URDG758) with MT760 demand guarantees. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation (BC); the Marshall Islands imposes no corporation tax, capital gains tax, or remittance tax on income derived and activities conducted outside the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the RMI imposes no withholding tax, income tax, or any other levy on dividends declared by a non-resident domestic BC to its shareholders, irrespective of the shareholder's residence or tax status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI Income Tax Act contains no transfer pricing provisions and no Local File or Master File thresholds. The RMI does not require OECD-aligned TP documentation. Foreign tax authorities, however, may recharacterize transactions of the RMI entity when it is interposed in global supply chains; prudent practice is to maintain contemporaneous benchmark studies where the entity invoices related parties.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance Regulations Act 2019 (as amended) applies to non-resident domestic corporations carrying on 'relevant activities', including distribution and service centers, financing and leasing, holding company business, and IP holding. Manufacturing and extrusion of hollow-fiber dialyzers is not per se a listed relevant activity, but if the BC performs local distribution of its own or third-party consumables in the RMI, or derives income from IP while relying on foreign affiliates for core functions, an annual Economic Substance Return must be filed and the entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities, qualified employees, premises, and operating expenditures within the RMI proportional to income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Offshore Absorption

Statutory sanction: No RMI income tax is charged, so the immediate sanction arises in the onshore jurisdictions: disallowance of intercompany deductions, reattribution of profit, accuracy-related penalties of 20-40% (US) or up to 100% (other regimes), and Controlled Foreign Company/CFC attribution. Within the RMI, false statements in the annual report are punishable by fines up to US$10,000 and up to five years' imprisonment for perjury.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize sham contracts face civil restitution in onshore forums and personal liability for foreign corporate taxes via director penalty statutes; the RMI Registrar may strike the company for false certification, removing the corporate veil.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR financial penalties from US$5,000 to US$20,000 per relevant activity for the first default and US$20,000 to US$100,000 for a subsequent default; the Registrar may apply to the court for striking-off, dissolution, and public rebuke, thereby terminating the entity's legal existence.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for ESR penalties where they approved false substance declarations; they may face travel bans and exchange-of-information requests from the RMI Tax Information Authority under the international tax compliance framework.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, false or omitted BOI filings attract fines up to US$100,000 and imprisonment up to ten years; the entity's registration may be suspended and the registered agent must withdraw, collapsing the corporate structure.

Director personal liability: Directors and the resident agent are criminally liable for knowing false BOI filings; civil recovery of laundered value can be enforced against personal assets, and travel bans are routinely imposed pending investigation.
Unlicensed Local Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed operation is punishable by daily fines, up to US$500 per day, plus back assessment of 3% GRT on all RMI-source revenue, customs penalties on imported goods, and seizure of inventory.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for the unpaid license fees, GRT arrears, and penalties of the company where the business was conducted for their direct financial benefit; court-ordered injunctions and forfeiture of personal assets may follow.
Fraudulent Dividend Distribution / Insolvency Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act unlawful-distribution provisions, the directors must restore the full amount of the unlawful distribution plus interest; knowing fraud by the shareholders is a criminal offense.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable to the corporation and its creditors for the excess amount; the corporate veil is pierced and personal bankruptcy, civil restitution, and imprisonment for fraud are available.
Misuse of the Non-Resident Exemption / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the non-resident exemption, retroactive assessment of 3% Gross Revenue Tax on all local revenue, monthly late penalties and interest of 1.5%, and criminal referral for wilful tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The responsible directors and officers are personally assessed for the back tax and penalties; the RMI Tax and Customs Office may issue travel bans and institute asset-freeze orders to secure collection.

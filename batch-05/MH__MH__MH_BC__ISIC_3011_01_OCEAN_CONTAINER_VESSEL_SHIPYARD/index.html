Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Business Corporations Act; shares may be issued with par value or no par value, and no escrow deposit or paid-in capital remittance is required before incorporation or shipyard license application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance certificate from the relevant local fire authority; civil defense structural load inspection covering fabrication halls, cranes, dry dock gates, slipways, and fuel or solvent storage; occupational health and workplace clearance under the Marshall Islands workplace safety rules, including approval of confined-space entry, hot work, crane operations, and emergency evacuation plans.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Business Corporations Act; shares may be issued with par value or no par value, and no escrow deposit or paid-in capital remittance is required before incorporation or shipyard license application.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Administration (RICA), an executive agency under the Ministry of Finance, Delap, Majuro Atoll; registry functions are divided between the Registrar of Corporations (Business Corporations Act 1990) and the Marshall Islands Maritime Administrator (Maritime Act 1990); taxpayer registration and customs filing are administered through the RICA official portal and the licensed registered-agent network.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) procures its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) incorporation under the Business Corporations Act through a government-licensed Registered Agent, generating the corporate registration number and Certificate of Incorporation; (2) submission of a Taxpayer Registration Application (Form TR-1) to RICA, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent consent, director particulars, and a declaration of non-resident status; (3) issuance of a Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) ordinarily within 3–5 business days; (4) where the entity has any in-country economic footprint, supplementary registration for Gross Revenue Tax and a Customs Importer/Exporter Code is required; (5) for the offshore shipbuilding model, the entity files a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) status declaration with the Registrar to confirm the absence of Marshall Islands-source revenue and to secure exemption from income tax and gross revenue tax. No minimum capital, local director, or physical office is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) procures its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) incorporation under the Business Corporations Act through a government-licensed Registered Agent, generating the corporate registration number and Certificate of Incorporation; (2) submission of a Taxpayer Registration Application (Form TR-1) to RICA, attaching the Certificate of Incorporation, registered agent consent, director particulars, and a declaration of non-resident status; (3) issuance of a Marshall Islands Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) ordinarily within 3–5 business days; (4) where the entity has any in-country economic footprint, supplementary registration for Gross Revenue Tax and a Customs Importer/Exporter Code is required; (5) for the offshore shipbuilding model, the entity files a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) status declaration with the Registrar to confirm the absence of Marshall Islands-source revenue and to secure exemption from income tax and gross revenue tax. No minimum capital, local director, or physical office is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Marshall Islands has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification regime; however, the following statutory elections apply to the MH_BC: (i) Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) election under the Business Corporations Act, rendering the entity exempt from income tax and Gross Revenue Tax on all foreign-source earnings and exports; (ii) International Ship Registry election under the Marshall Islands Maritime Act, which confirms income-tax exemption for vessel ownership, charter, and shipbuilding-related revenues where the vessel is titled through the Marshall Islands International Ship Registry; (iii) Gross Revenue Tax de minimis election for entities with gross revenue below the statutory registration threshold; and (iv) foreign-source income designation election for ISIC 3011 shipbuilding contract proceeds, placing the delivery and title-transfer transactions outside the Republic's territorial sourcing rules. No partnership or disregarded-entity elections exist under the Income Tax Act 1989.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT or GST; indirect taxation comprises a 3% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) on businesses earning revenue sourced within the Republic, customs import duties under the Customs Ordinance 1991, and selective excise duties on petroleum, tobacco, and alcohol; exports of newly built deep-sea dry cargo and container vessels are entirely outside the indirect tax net.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GRT registration: USD 5,000 annual gross revenue threshold for businesses with in-country revenue; a fully non-resident MH_BC deriving no Marshall Islands-source revenue is not registrable for GRT; customs registration: a Customs Importer/Exporter Code is mandatory for any physical movement of goods across the Republic's border, irrespective of value; no VAT threshold applies because no VAT law exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because the Republic levies no VAT or GST. Export substantive relief is instead achieved through the territorial sourcing rules of the Income Tax Act 1989: revenue from the export sale of an ocean container vessel is sourced at the place where the contract is made and title passes; where delivery occurs at a foreign shipyard or on international waters, the profit is non-sourced and therefore exempt from Marshall Islands income tax and GRT. The exemption is further secured by the NRDC election and, where the vessel is flagged under the Marshall Islands International Ship Registry, by the Maritime Act exemptions for shipbuilding, repair, and sale; no export tax or export duty is levied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form C-IX) for the vessel or its components when physically leaving Marshall Islands jurisdiction
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing ocean transport and delivery to the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and executed shipbuilding contract (ISIC 3011) detailing price, delivery terms, and title-transfer point
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing USD receipt of the export sale proceeds from the foreign buyer

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-integrated clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists under Marshall Islands law. RICA operates a hybrid paper-digital customs environment; export declarations may be lodged electronically through approved customs brokers, but continuous transaction controls, national e-invoicing frameworks, and fiscal data reporting to tax authorities are not statutory requirements.

RICA may open assessments within six years of the relevant tax period and, where an export exemption or NRDC status is found to have been misapplied, recover the full amount of unpaid tax with a 10% monthly delinquency surcharge capped at 50%, plus interest at the statutory rate (prime rate plus 2%). Willful false declaration in support of export exemption constitutes a criminal offense under the Income Tax Act 1989, punishable by a fine of up to USD 10,000 and imprisonment of up to three years per count; false customs export declarations attract forfeiture of goods and civil penalties up to 40% of declared value under the Customs Ordinance. The Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 imposes record-keeping and transaction-reporting obligations on shipyard export transactions, with corporate fines up to USD 500,000 for failures connected with export proceeds; RICA may additionally revoke the NRDC exemption certificate upon a finding that management and control were in fact exercised in the Marshall Islands, triggering retrospective clawback of all exempted profit from the inception of the exemption period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) adopts the United States Dollar (USD) as its official legal tender and maintains a completely liberalized capital account with zero exchange controls. There are no statutory barriers to capital account convertibility, no licensing requirements for foreign direct investment, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate solvency tests, board authorization, and ordinary AML/KYC screening. The absence of a domestic central bank and the use of USD eliminate any convertibility delay or multiple-currency risk within the jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

RMI maintains no export proceeds surrender obligation. A shipyard entity is not required to convert export or construction milestone receipts into local currency because USD is the domestic currency. All export proceeds may be retained in USD, held in offshore correspondent accounts, used directly for foreign currency disbursements, or repatriated without a mandatory conversion timeline or a prescribed liquidation period.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Financial Institutions Commissioner (FIC), Republic of the Marshall Islands

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) have severely constrained viability in the RMI banking ecosystem. The domestic regulatory framework does not provide for non-resident EMI licensing, and all RMI-chartered banks require a licensed resident presence or a registered foreign entity with a valid RMI business license. Unlicensed offshore payment processors cannot be used as primary treasury clearing rails due to domestic banking prohibitions on passing funds through unregulated intermediaries. An RMI shipyard is therefore required to maintain its primary operating and escrow accounts within a locally chartered, federally insured or correspondent-linked domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inward capital injections and construction milestone payments must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the payee's verified corporate bank account. The remittance must carry an accurate purpose code consistent with the underlying commercial transaction, such as Subscription, Trade Payment, or Construction Disbursement. Before crediting the account, the receiving bank will require a Beneficiary Account Certification, a Board Resolution, and a Source of Funds (SOF) declaration. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

3% flat corporate income tax on net income derived from sources within the Republic (Income Tax Act 1989, as amended); the same 3% rate is the headline Gross Revenue Tax on gross revenue from business carried on in the Republic, with GRT creditable against the net income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. The Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Business Corporation to non-resident shareholders, whether paid from shipyard operating profits, liquidation proceeds, or investment income; no such WHT exists in the Income Tax Act or the GRT Act.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due on or before the last day of the fourth month following the close of the taxable year (30 April for a calendar-year taxpayer); the Tax & Revenue Division may grant an extension of up to six months upon written application. For non-resident corporations with no RMI-source activity, no filing deadline is triggered.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 — Gross Revenue / Receipts Gross revenue and trade receipts for the taxable period, before deductions, from all sources, with a jurisdictional attachment for RMI-sourced and foreign-sourced income. Enter only revenue attributable to Marshall Islands sources; revenue from international shipbuilding contracts for ocean container vessels is foreign-source and must be excluded. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the Marshall Islands has not adopted OECD Chapter V Local File or Master File rules, and no monetary threshold in Marshall Islands law triggers a mandatory transfer pricing filing. The arm's length principle is, however, embedded in the anti-avoidance provisions of the Income Tax Act and will be applied where RMI-source profits are assessed; for a medium-risk enhanced shipyard BC, contemporaneous pricing records for every cross-border intercompany transaction are recommended, with a practical internal benchmark of a USD 500,000 gross-receipts threshold for preparing a full local transfer pricing file.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Act, embedded in the Business Corporations Act framework, applies to 'relevant activities' comprising banking, insurance, shipping operations, pure IP holding, and financing/leasing. Commercial shipbuilding (ISIC 3011) is a manufacturing activity and is not a designated relevant activity; however, if the same Business Corporation charters, operates, or bareboat-leases the container vessels it builds, the 'shipping operations' category is triggered, demanding (i) management and control exercised in the RMI, (ii) adequate local premises and qualified employees, and (iii) annually documented core income-generating activity. Shipyard BCs should nonetheless maintain a substance file — board minutes, local agent attendance, employer registrations, and physical yard records — to preserve the non-resident tax-exempt status and avoid recharacterisation as a tax-resident shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False Non-Resident Status

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of up to 75% of the understated tax plus interest from the due date; criminal evasion is a felony under the Income Tax Act carrying a fine up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment up to five years; the vessel under construction is additionally subject to forfeiture proceedings under the Maritime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally and severally liable for the corporate tax and penalties where the company is a mere instrumentality; the RMI High Court may issue asset-preservation and travel-restraint orders, and individual directors face criminal indictment, travel bans, surrender of passports, and imprisonment.
Shell Company — Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the Business Corporation registration and the mandatory ISIC 3011 shipyard licence; reclassification of the entity as RMI tax-resident with retroactive assessment of the 3% income tax plus a late-payment surcharge; public naming by the Registrar of Corporations and exclusion from the RMI Maritime Registry.

Director personal liability: Executive and nominee directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties where the corporation is a facade; the High Court may prohibit directors from leaving the Republic, disqualify them from serving on any RMI-registered entity, and appoint a receiver over their personal assets.
AML/CFT Violation — Suspicious Payments and Missing STR

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of up to US$250,000 per violation under the AML/CFT legislation, asset-freezing and confiscation orders over contract proceeds, revocation of the statutory shipyard licence, and referral to the Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering and the Egmont Group for transnational enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution for money laundering, removal from the RMI company register, blacklisting by the FIU, High Court travel bans, and surrender of passports pending investigation; fraudulent concealment of beneficial ownership additionally attracts personal fines and imprisonment.
Unlicensed Shipyard Operation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to US$50,000 per unlicensed vessel constructed, forfeiture of any ship built or repaired without licence, and a continuing penalty of US$1,000 per day of unlicensed operation; the licence authority may also strike the entity from the corporate register.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid licence fees, penalties, and costs of forfeiture; a criminal conviction leads to disqualification from holding directorships in the RMI, personal monetary judgments, and imprisonment for continued operation after revocation.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services, Majuro Atoll; official forms and customs e-services are accessible through the ASYCUDA World customs portal and the Ministry's website at www.mof.gov.mh. Entity incorporation documents are filed with the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney-General.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned Non-Resident Business Corporation (BC) must first file Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations, appoint a licensed registered agent, and obtain its Certificate of Incorporation. The BC is then eligible to apply to the Revenue and Taxation Office for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the corporate registration certificate and agent appointment letter; however, a Non-Resident BC with exclusively foreign-source income is not statutorily required to hold an RTO TIN. In practice, the entity obtains a Certificate of Good Standing and, where requested by banks or counterparties, an RTO letter confirming no domestic tax filing obligation. If the BC later establishes economic nexus in the Marshall Islands (physical office, employees, local sales), it must register for the Business License regime and obtain a TIN before commencing taxable local activities.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Marshall Islands Non-Resident Business Corporation is classified as tax-exempt with respect to all non-Marshall Islands-source income. No income tax, franchise tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or stamp duty is levied on foreign-source income of non-resident BCs; the entity is considered fiscally transparent solely for reporting purposes and is not subject to the domestic tax framework unless it elects or is found to have a permanent establishment or local business activity. — A foreign-owned Non-Resident Business Corporation (BC) must first file Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations, appoint a licensed registered agent, and obtain its Certificate of Incorporation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Marshall Islands Non-Resident Business Corporation is classified as tax-exempt with respect to all non-Marshall Islands-source income. No income tax, franchise tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or stamp duty is levied on foreign-source income of non-resident BCs; the entity is considered fiscally transparent solely for reporting purposes and is not subject to the domestic tax framework unless it elects or is found to have a permanent establishment or local business activity.

        
Available election forms
The Marshall Islands imposes no domestic income tax on Non-Resident BCs, so no domestic check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists. Under U.S. federal tax classification, a Marshall Islands Business Corporation is a per se corporation pursuant to U.S. Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i) and cannot elect partnership or disregarded-entity treatment. The only statutory election relevant to the MH_BC is the status designation in the Articles of Incorporation as a Non-Resident Corporation; an annual confirmation of non-resident status is filed with the Registrar of Corporations to preserve the Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment. No OECD hybrid mismatch election is afforded under Marshall Islands law.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No VAT or GST. The Marshall Islands operates an import-duty and customs-clearance framework under the Customs and Excise Act, supplemented by an ad valorem tariff schedule on goods entering the Republic. Exported goods are outside the scope of Marshall Islands duty.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable - no value-added tax or general sales tax regime exists, so no economic-turnover threshold for VAT registration is prescribed. However, any person importing commercial goods into the Marshall Islands must register as an Importer with the Customs Service and hold an Importer's License before the first consignment; the registration trigger is the first import transaction, with no minimum value threshold. A Non-Resident BC that does not bring goods into Marshall Islands territory has no customs registration obligation.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
The concept of 0% export VAT does not apply because the Marshall Islands levies no VAT. Goods physically exported from the Republic of the Marshall Islands are statutorily exempt from customs export duty and are not subject to any domestic consumption tax. An exporter qualifies for duty-exempt treatment on evidence that the goods are genuinely leaving the jurisdiction for a foreign destination, either as outright export or as re-export of previously imported bonded goods within the prescribed transit period. For the MH_BC's actual cross-border procurement model, where organic matcha powder is purchased from a foreign (typically Japanese) supplier and resold directly to a foreign buyer without landfall in the Marshall Islands, no Marshall Islands customs event occurs at all; the transaction is characterized solely as foreign-source income and requires no local export zero-rating claim.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, QR-code invoicing, or government e-invoicing clearance mandate applies in the Marshall Islands. Customs import and export declarations are filed electronically through the ASYCUDA World single-window facility where operational, with paper declarations still accepted for low-volume consignments. The commercial invoice issued by the organic matcha trader remains the operative fiscal document and must be retained for at least five years for customs audit purposes.

      
The Customs Service may conduct post-clearance audits for up to three years after export and may reassess duties or deny exemptions on misdeclared goods. False customs declarations, under-invoicing, and fraudulent export exemption claims attract penalties of up to 300% of the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods or their value, and, upon conviction, fines of up to USD 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to five years under the Customs and Excise Act. Any exemption obtained by fraud is void ab initio, and the entire waived duty is retrospectively clawed back with interest. Because no VAT refund mechanism exists, there is no separate VAT clawback exposure; however, the Revenue and Taxation Office retains audit powers to recharacterize income as Marshall Islands-source where domestic nexus is found. The Republic is fully dollarized and imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline, but regulated financial institutions apply the 180-day inward remittance requirement as an anti-money-laundering control for export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Because the RMI is in a de facto currency union with the United States and uses USD as legal tender, there are no inward or outward currency convertibility restrictions on either current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders are policy-free and unrestricted by statute; the only operative hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC onboarding, OFAC/UN sanctions screening, and correspondent-bank due-diligence standards along the settlement chain.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for RMI entities. All export receipts, including payments for wholesale trade under ISIC 4631, may be retained indefinitely in USD in any licensed depository account without conversion, repatriation, or earmarking obligations. The functional requirement is purely documentary: banks will expect payment-receipt reconciliation against underlying commercial invoices and bills of lading to distinguish genuine trade flows from round-tripping.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands has no conventional central bank or monetary authority; the U.S. dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender. Prudential and licensing supervision of banks is discharged by the Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, under the Banking Act 1995 (as amended), with AML/CFT oversight by the Financial Intelligence Officer pursuant to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2005.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
3% Gross Revenue Tax is imposed by the RMI Income Tax Act (35 MIRC, Chapter 1, as amended) on gross revenue derived from a trade or business carried on within the Marshall Islands. No separate net-income corporate income tax exists. A Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (BC) deriving no RMI-source gross revenue is outside the tax charge, resulting in an effective 0% rate.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
0% - the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Business Corporation to any shareholder, resident or non-resident. Dividend income is outside the scope of RMI gross revenue tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No RMI statutory transfer-pricing documentation threshold exists; the RMI has not enacted a Local File/Master File regime and the Gross Revenue Tax renders arm's-length adjustments inapplicable domestically. As a practical benchmark for foreign tax defense and ESR compliance, maintain contemporaneous OECD-compliant documentation when connected-party matcha procurement and distribution volumes exceed USD 1,000,000 per annum.

      
Economic substance scope: Yes. The Economic Substance (Implementation) Regulations 2019 apply OECD/EU Code of Conduct standards to RMI entities carrying on 'relevant activities', including Distribution and Service Centre Business. A matcha trading entity purchasing goods from a connected party for distribution must demonstrate: (a) direction and management exercised in the RMI; (b) adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in the RMI proportionate to its activity; and (c) performance of core income-generating activities (inventory control, pricing, supplier and distribution risk management) in the RMI, together with an annual Economic Substance Return filing.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties escalating under the ESR Regulations 2019, daily default fines, potential share cancellation and administrative strike-off, and mandatory exchange of information with OECD, EU, and treaty-partner authorities.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who certified false ESR statements face personal fines and disqualification orders; strike-off bars named management from serving in any RMI entity and exposes them to foreign regulatory cross-examination.

      

      
      

        Tax Evasion in the Beneficial Owner's Residence Jurisdiction
        
Statutory sanction: US civil fraud penalty up to 75% of the underpayment; willful FBAR penalty up to the greater of USD 100,000 or 50% of the account balance per violation; criminal willful evasion carrying imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to USD 250,000. The RMI cooperates through the RMI-US TIEA and FATCA IGA.

        
Director personal liability: Veil-piercing available in US tax enforcement where the corporation is an alter ego; personal restitution orders, passport revocation and court-imposed travel bans, and extradition under the RMI-US extradition treaty.

      

      
      

        False Non-Resident Declaration / Unreported RMI-Source Revenue
        
Statutory sanction: Back assessment of the 3% Gross Revenue Tax on the entire RMI-source gross revenue for the open period, plus default interest and penalties up to 50% of the tax for willful fraud, and administrative dissolution.

        
Director personal liability: The officer signing the false declaration is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; perjury prosecution is available and directors may be disqualified from serving as officers in the RMI.

      

      
      

        AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Penalties under the RMI AML framework and the Criminal Code (as amended), consent orders against the registered agent, freezing of corporate records, notice suspension of the entity, and reporting to foreign financial intelligence units.

        
Director personal liability: Money laundering offenses carry imprisonment up to 10 years; directors and beneficial owners face asset confiscation, travel restrictions imposed as bail conditions, and personal civil liability for aggravated penalties.

      

      
      

        Unlawful Distributions / Insolvent Trading
        
Statutory sanction: The unlawful distribution must be repaid under the Business Corporations Act; the Registrar may initiate judicial dissolution and an independent liquidator will claw back the distribution as a fraudulent transfer.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable to the corporation for the full amount of the unlawful distribution, with only limited contribution rights; foreign insolvency courts may impose director travel bans, asset freezes, and personal recovery orders.

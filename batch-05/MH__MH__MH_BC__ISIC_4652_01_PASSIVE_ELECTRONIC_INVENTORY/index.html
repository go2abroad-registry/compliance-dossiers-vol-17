Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) — Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services, Revenue and Taxation Division (Majuro, Delap), jointly with the Registrar of Corporations of the Office of the Attorney General for entity registration. RMI operates no centralized national electronic tax-filing portal; registrations are submitted in paper form to the Revenue and Taxation Division or through licensed registered agents.

E-government filing portal

Because the RMI does not impose a corporate income tax, VAT, or sales tax on a non-resident offshore Business Corporation, no statutory TIN or VAT registration number is issued to such an entity. The de facto tax identifiers are: (1) the certificate of incorporation and corporate registration number issued by the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act; (2) the annual Business License number issued by the Ministry of Finance under the Business License Act (the license number functions as the primary taxpayer identifier); and (3) the RMI Customs importer code where goods are imported into or exported from the RMI. The foreign-entity procurement sequence is: (i) incorporate a Business Corporation with a registered agent and registered office in Majuro; (ii) obtain a Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Act (unless the activity falls within the reserved domestic-investor schedule); (iii) obtain the annual Business License; (iv) register with the RMI Customs Division for an importer/exporter code if any physical goods movement through RMI customs occurs; and (v) if a local payroll is ever created, register with the RMI Social Security Administration as an employer. A Non-Resident Domestic Corporation declaration may additionally be filed with the Revenue and Taxation Division to formally evidence non-liability for RMI-source income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — an RMI-incorporated Business Corporation with non-resident ownership and no income derived from sources within the RMI (i.e., passive electronic component distribution conducted entirely offshore) falls outside the RMI income tax net. The RMI applies a strict territorial/offshore model: no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, and no estate or gift tax on such entities. Only income derived from sources within the RMI would be taxable under the Income Tax Act 1989, which is generally inoperative for offshore non-resident domestic corporations. — Because the RMI does not impose a corporate income tax, VAT, or sales tax on a non-resident offshore Business Corporation, no statutory TIN or VAT registration number is issued to such an entity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — an RMI-incorporated Business Corporation with non-resident ownership and no income derived from sources within the RMI (i.e., passive electronic component distribution conducted entirely offshore) falls outside the RMI income tax net. The RMI applies a strict territorial/offshore model: no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, and no estate or gift tax on such entities. Only income derived from sources within the RMI would be taxable under the Income Tax Act 1989, which is generally inoperative for offshore non-resident domestic corporations.

Available election forms

RMI law contains no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch (BEPS Action 2) election. The available statutory mechanisms are: (a) Non-Resident Domestic Corporation status — a domestic corporation wholly without RMI-source income may formally elect/declare exemption from the RMI income tax under the Income Tax Act 1989; (b) entity-form elections under the RMI Limited Liability Company Act and Limited Partnership Act — an RMI LLC or LP may be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for foreign tax purposes (e.g., US federal Form 8832 check-the-box classification), whereas an RMI Business Corporation is treated as a per se corporation; and (c) no treaty or hybrid-entity election is available because the RMI maintains no comprehensive double-taxation agreement network.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — the RMI does not levy a national VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act (as amended) and selective excise duties on fuel, alcohol, tobacco, and certain luxury goods, which apply only at importation into the RMI.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — there is no turnover-based threshold triggering VAT/GST registration because no value-added or sales tax exists. Any person carrying on business in the RMI, including a foreign-owned distributor, must instead obtain an annual Business License regardless of revenue; license fees depend on the business classification, not turnover. Importers must additionally register with the RMI Customs Division and obtain a USD-denominated importer code; no de minimis tax-registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms — because the RMI has no VAT system, there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no input-VAT refund mechanism. Under the Customs Tariff Act, goods exported from the RMI (including re-export of imported passive electronic components such as multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors) are generally free of export duty. Import duty paid at the time of importation may be reclaimed through the customs drawback/remission mechanism where the goods are physically re-exported without having been sold, consumed, or domestically disposed of in the RMI, provided the exporter substantiates the identity of the goods and the fact of export within the statutory claim period.

  • Customs Export Entry / Export Declaration (RMI Customs form lodged by a licensed customs broker)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the RMI
  • Original Import Entry and duty-paid receipt (required for duty-drawback or re-export claims)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list identifying the MLCC/resistor part numbers, quantities, and tariff classifications
  • Certificate of Origin or evidence of final foreign destination (as requested by counterparty banks)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice evidencing collection of export proceeds from the overseas buyer

No electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate exists under RMI law. Commercial invoices are not cleared or stamped by any tax authority. Customs import and export entries are lodged with the RMI Customs Division through licensed customs brokers using paper or basic electronic filing; documentary records must be retained for customs audit, but no national e-invoicing repository or government clearance platform applies.

Export fraud in the RMI takes the form of customs drawback abuse rather than VAT zero-rating fraud, given the absence of VAT. Under the Customs Tariff Act (as amended), false drawback declarations, over-invoicing of re-exported goods, misdescription of tariff classifications, smuggling, or diversion of 'exported' goods back into the RMI market attract retrospective clawback of the duty refunded, monetary penalties of up to three times the duty involved, forfeiture of the goods or conveying vessels, fines of up to USD 10,000, and imprisonment of up to five years for individuals involved. The Revenue and Taxation Division and the Attorney General's office may jointly audit exporter records for up to five years, and a non-compliant exporter's Business License may be suspended or revoked.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is fully convertible with no exchange controls or quantitative limits. Both capital account and current account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittance and dividend distributions by a non-resident Business Corporation require only a valid board resolution and standard solvency tests under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act. No foreign exchange approval, surrender obligation, or non-resident withholding tax applies to such outbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement. Exporters may retain 100% of USD-denominated export receipts in any authorized account, onshore or offshore, with no time limit. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% (zero) for all export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in the Marshall Islands. The de facto monetary authority is the U.S. Federal Reserve System because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender. Domestic bank licensing and prudential supervision are undertaken by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of the Marshall Islands.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary payment rail only. The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no standalone licensing regime for electronic money institutions, and the Banking Act 1989 prohibits unauthorized deposit-taking. Offshore EMIs may act as payment facilitators or digital currency custodians, but they do not replace the need for a physical licensed bank account. Legal constraints require that all USD clearing ultimately settle through a U.S. correspondent bank. EMIs operating in RMI territory without a banking license are not permitted to accept deposits from the public.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 - no statutory RMI Local File or Master File obligation. As defensive practice, prepare OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation and an intercompany distribution policy when controlled transactions exceed USD 10,000,000 or when a foreign parent jurisdiction imposes a lower threshold.

Economic substance scope: RMI does not maintain Economic Substance Regulations comparable to BVI or Cayman. However, under foreign CFC, substance-over-form, and permanent establishment doctrines, the passive electronics inventory business must be owned, managed, and administered with real operational substance in the foreign operating jurisdiction; maintaining a pure RMI shell is a fatal compliance red flag.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: RMI does not tax the entity, but the beneficial owner's home jurisdiction may impose tax reassessments, interest, civil fraud penalties up to 100% of the tax due, criminal fines, and asset seizure; RMI may terminate corporate registration if fraudulent filings are proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false certificates or participate in sham transactions can be personally liable for civil restitution; criminal tax evasion convictions carry imprisonment of up to 5 to 10 years and travel bans.
Fictitious / Shell Company Conduct

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Business Corporations Act and AML regime, the Registrar may strike off or dissolve the company; foreign courts may disregard the entity and attribute its income to the beneficial owner; banks terminate wire and credit facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal liability for trading while dissolved, fines for failure to maintain records, disqualification orders, and criminal liability if nominee arrangements facilitate money laundering.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: RMI AML/CFT regulations impose administrative fines per violation, regulatory suspension or revocation of the registered agent, potential criminal fines, and striking-off of the corporate entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be prosecuted individually for money laundering facilitation; penalties include imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation of proceeds, and travel restrictions.
Export Control / Trade Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: US Export Administration Regulations and OFAC sanctions apply extraterritorially even when routed through RMI; maximum criminal penalties include USD 1,000,000 per violation and debarment from export privileges; RMI may impose licensing sanctions on imports and exports.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors who order or knowingly benefit from prohibited exports may be criminally liable, fined personally, imprisoned for up to 20 years under US law, and placed on denied-party lists or travel bans.
False Declaration to Registrar / Fraudulent Incorporation

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Business Corporations Act, the Registrar may require dissolution, impose monetary penalties, and refer the matter to the Attorney General; the company loses its tax-exempt status and becomes liable for any RMI gross revenue or business licensing tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and incorporators who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for resulting RMI fees, taxes, penalties, and third-party losses; courts may impose imprisonment and travel bans.

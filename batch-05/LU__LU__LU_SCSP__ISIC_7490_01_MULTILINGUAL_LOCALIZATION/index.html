Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Luxembourg City; notarial filings are also transmitted by Luxembourg notaries.

Governing statute

Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended (Law of 1915), Article 27-1 et seq. (governing the Société en Commandite Spéciale, introduced by the Law of 11 July 2013 on partnerships); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register (RCS) and on accounting records; Law of 13 January 2019 establishing the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of association / partnership agreement (contrat de société) executed in notarial or private deed form, filed as the acte de constitution with the RCS, followed by a legal notice in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et des Associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum subscribed capital for a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); capital is freely fixed by the partnership agreement, may be contributed in cash or in kind, and may be partially paid-in as agreed; no escrow or blocked bank account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Administration des services de secours (ASS) prior to first occupation of the registered office; first-occupancy conformity certificate confirming structural load capacity, electrical installation safety, and accessibility; and workplace inspection by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) where staff are engaged on the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum subscribed capital for a Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); capital is freely fixed by the partnership agreement, may be contributed in cash or in kind, and may be partially paid-in as agreed; no escrow or blocked bank account is required by law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income and corporate tax: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), Luxembourg Inland Revenue, official portal: impotsdirects.public.lu; VAT, registration duties and indirect taxes: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), official portal: aed.public.lu; single digital public-administration access: MyGuichet.lu (espace entreprises); company registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL/LBR), official portal: lbr.lu; national identification/matricule issuance: Centre des Technologies de l'Information de l'Etat (CTIE).

E-government filing portal

For the non-resident-owned LU_SCSP: (1) execute the SCSp partnership agreement before a Luxembourg notary (no minimum capital required) and file it electronically with the RCSL via LBR; (2) obtain the Luxembourg national business number (matricule) from CTIE upon RCSL publication; (3) appoint a Luxembourg tax representative (intermédiaire fiscal agréé) where the entity is established outside the EU, or rely on an EU-established general partner for self-registration; (4) file AEDT VAT registration form (Formulaire 640 TVA) with the partnership agreement, RCSL extract, powers of attorney, partner identity documents and anticipated turnover statement, and receive the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by 8 digits; (5) file the ACD application for the fiscal reference number and complete the annual partnership information return for purely transparent treatment where the SCSp maintains no Luxembourg permanent establishment; (6) optionally request a binding advance tax ruling from the Commission des Avis de l'Administration (CAA) confirming fiscal transparency, non-resident partner PE attribution, and treaty eligibility; (7) register with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) only where Luxembourg-resident employees are engaged; (8) activate MyGuichet electronic channels for VAT Model 1 returns, EC Sales Lists, OSS filings, and the export documentation register.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) has no separate legal personality and is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICT) and net wealth tax (ISF). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) has no separate legal personality and is statutorily fiscally transparent for Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICT) and net wealth tax (ISF). All translation, dubbing, subtitling and localization-agency profits, royalties, capital gains, expenses and credits flow through pro rata to the general and limited partners. Entity-level taxation is not applicable; territorial scope is effectively global for Luxembourg-connected income, and no tax-exempt zone regime applies. A Luxembourg permanent establishment is attributed to every foreign partner when the SCSp carries on its multilingual localization business through a fixed place of business in Luxembourg, triggering Luxembourg reach-through taxation under Article 175 LIR for non-resident partners.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Available mechanisms: (i) binding CAA advance tax ruling confirming pass-through treatment, PE attribution and income-tax treaty eligibility for foreign partners; (ii) VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') effective 1 January 2025 permitting the SCSp to consolidate with a Luxembourg SOPARFI or taxable affiliate for VAT purposes; (iii) voluntary VAT registration despite the EUR 35,000 small-business exemption (Article 57 LTVAG) so as to recover input VAT on production costs, dubbing equipment and software; (iv) OSS (One-Stop-Shop) election for intra-EU B2C supplies of e-services and digital media to avoid multiple non-resident VAT registrations; (v) alignment of the SCSp's transparent characterization with foreign anti-hybrid regimes (ATAD 2) to prevent double non-taxation or double deduction outcomes; (vi) optional reporting election to treat the partnership as a non-taxable entity for Luxembourg withholding-tax purposes where all partners are non-residents and no Luxembourg PE arises.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State and applies the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union free-movement-of-capital regime (Articles 63–65). Both current-account and capital-account payments are fully convertible and freely remittable in any permitted currency. Profit remittance from an SCSp is not subject to exchange-control approval: the SCSp is fiscally transparent and distributions to partners are treated as partnership allocations, not dividends. The practical remittance hurdles are exclusively bank-side AML/KYC proof obligations, EU financial-sanctions screening, and the requirement that the SCSp maintain up-to-date beneficial-ownership records in the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg law imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed repatriation window, and no BCL approval for export-related receipts. Cross-border payments above statistical thresholds are reported by resident banks under ECB/BCL external-statistics rules, but the SCSp itself has no filing obligation for routine trade receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the central bank and the official repository for external statistics and monetary operations. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration; the Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) oversee account-opening compliance and financial-sanctions enforcement respectively.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary settlement or escrow continuity; Medium for ancillary disbursement-only workflows. Wise Business and Payoneer may be used as convenient output rails, but they are payment institutions, not Luxembourg licensed credit institutions, and their non-EEA segregated accounts cannot host Luxembourg IBANs or receive statutory escrow balances. A PSD2 passport from another EEA state is acceptable for payment services, yet the SCSp must maintain a principal settlement account at a Luxembourg bank or an EU/EEA credit institution and must ensure that every cross-border flow is traceable back to the KYC-complete SCSp and its beneficial owners.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque partnership ownership structure and silent limited partners in the SCSp

Root cause: Opaque partnership ownership structure and silent limited partners in the SCSp

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the limited partnership agreement (contrat de société), RCS/LBR constitution extract, complete partner register with ownership percentages and economic entitlements, current RBE extract, UBO declaration for each natural-person partner, passport copies, proof of residential address, corporate charters and board resolutions for corporate limited partners.
Source-of-funds concerns for initial capital injection and subsequent partner distributions

Root cause: Source-of-funds concerns for initial capital injection and subsequent partner distributions

Defense evidence: Twelve months of bank statements for each contributing partner, historical tax returns, executed financing or loan agreements, sale-and-purchase contracts explaining prior accumulation of wealth, notarised source-of-wealth declaration, and a written undertaking that no cash or third-party anonymous funds are used.
Unclear commercial substance of the multilingual translation and media-dubbing activity

Root cause: Unclear commercial substance of the multilingual translation and media-dubbing activity

Defense evidence: Signed client localization contracts or letters of intent, vendor/subcontractor agreements for translation and dubbing services, a projected transaction schedule, sample invoices, a detailed business plan showing addressable markets, pricing methodology, and expected monthly turnover.
Sanctions, export-control, or restricted-media exposure through global clients and intellectual-property royalties

Root cause: Sanctions, export-control, or restricted-media exposure through global clients and intellectual-property royalties

Defense evidence: Negative sanctions-screening reports for all counterparties and ultimate clients, end-use attestation letters, a media-rights confirmation matrix, an export-control or dual-use applicability opinion from Luxembourg counsel, and a no-conflict legal opinion where intellectual-property or royalty flows are part of the revenue model.
Use of non-Luxembourg digital EMIs and alternative payment rails alongside the core bank account

Root cause: Use of non-Luxembourg digital EMIs and alternative payment rails alongside the core bank account

Defense evidence: A written treasury policy approved by the general partner governing ancillary rails, the bank consent letter covering Wise/Payoneer usage, KYC records for each EMI account, a full funds-flow mapping from the Luxembourg settlement account to final beneficiaries, and reconciliation ledgers showing that EMI balances are never used as a storage vehicle.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners are remitted by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA transfer into the SCSp's EUR account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance information should state: 'Capital contribution to SCSp', identify the beneficiary as the Luxembourg-resident partnership, and include the partnership's RCS number. No BCL approval is required; the receiving bank will assign the appropriate balance-of-payments classification for external statistics. The bank must be provided with the executed capital commitment schedule and a board/general-partner resolution authorising the account to receive funds before the first transfer.Outbound settlement for localization services is executed through the Luxembourg bank as instructing bank. EUR payments within the EEA use SEPA Credit Transfer or TARGET2; non-EEA and non-EUR payments use SWIFT MT103 with robust remittance information such as invoice number, contract reference, and commercial purpose. For high-value production milestones or when counterparties require bank confirmation, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (Commercial L/C) or SWIFT gpi tracked payment is used. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg corporate income tax rate (17.00% corporate income tax plus 7.00% unemployment fund surcharge plus 6.75% solidarity surcharge plus 0.25% state tax surcharge for the 2025 tax year; local trade tax excluded)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; because the SCSp is fiscally transparent, profit distributions to partners are not characterized as dividends at the SCSp level and no 15% withholding tax is levied on the SCSp itself. If a Luxembourg corporate partner re-distributes profits, the standard 15% dividend withholding tax applies, subject to the 100% Luxembourg participation exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 annual related-party transactions in goods or services triggers mandatory Luxembourg Local File and Master File documentation obligation; intra-group service charges exceeding EUR 100,000 per year require a documented service agreement and demonstrate no duplication of shareholder costs; all Luxembourg SCSp entities must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation regardless of revenue if cross-border related-party payments are made.

Economic substance scope: Although Luxembourg has not enacted full Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to the UAE ESR regime, the doctrine applies through the LIR general anti-abuse rule, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) substance requirements, the OECD BEPS Action 5 substantial activity requirement for preferential regimes, and judicial doctrines (e.g., dual-residence and conduit-company principles). An SCSp active in multilingual localization must demonstrate substantive local activity: qualified management decisions in Luxembourg, employees or directors with actual localization industry expertise, physical office, and rejection of passive letterbox status. Failure to demonstrate substance may trigger denial of treaty benefits, exchange of information under OECD/Global Forum, and retrograde reclassification as a fully taxable Luxembourg company on distributed income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of EUR 10,000 up to EUR 250,000 (doubled for professionals), payment of all evaded tax plus 0.8% monthly default interest, and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years under LIR Article 395 and LIR Article 396; automatic forfeiture of any claimed losses.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who signed false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt under LIR Article 109. Civil managers risk personal bankruptcy proceedings, freezing of intercompany accounts, and travel bans issued by Luxembourg tax authorities; custodial sentence cannot be waived by company liquidation.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the SCSp as a fully taxable Luxembourg resident company; denial of treaty benefits with retroactive re-assessed tax up to 6 years; administrative fine up to EUR 100,000 under the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule (ATAD Article 6) and possible removal from the Luxembourg trade register (RCSL) as an inactive shell.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under civil/company law doctrine when the SCSp is an artificial arrangement; directors are personally liable for the entirety of tax arrears, restitution of distributions, and damages to creditors. Directors of shell conduits face director disqualification for 10 years and exit bans if criminal misrepresentation is found.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing taxable income; secondary penalty of 5% to 25% of the adjustment amount (Article 56 LIR applies arm's length principle; late-payment interest of 0.8% per month on the reassessed tax); administrative fines up to EUR 50,000 for documentation failure; correlative adjustments require mutual agreement procedure under DTA.

Director personal liability: Managing partners who approved the non-arm's-length invoices may be held jointly liable for the deficit, particularly if they drew distributions while knowing the intercompany pricing was not defensible; a Luxembourg criminal court may impose a personal fine of EUR 12,500 per false document.
Failure to File Tax Return and Administrative Offence

Statutory sanction: Daily penalty of EUR 50 per day of delay, capped at 10% of the assessed tax; ex officio taxation by AEDT; exclusion of deductions and expenses not documented; loss of loss-carryforward eligibility; a coercive fine (astreinte) of up to EUR 2,500 per day until the return is completed.

Director personal liability: The AEDT may issue a personal collection order against the fiduciary or managing partner for the penalty charges; directors receive personal liability notices and may be subject to bank garnishment for the SCSp's tax debts if the non-filing constitutes gross negligence.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Crime in Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: AML law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) imposes administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and criminal fines up to EUR 5,000,000; asset freezing orders; prohibition against the fund manager; removal from RCSL; report to the Financial Intelligence Unit is mandatory.

Director personal liability: AML obligations attach to partners of an SCSp acting as fiduciaries; non-compliance triggers personal criminal liability, confiscation of profits derived through the payment corridors, director disqualification, and a travel ban where a flight risk is proven.
Incorrect Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Interest/Royalties

Statutory sanction: The withholding tax is deemed to have been received but not collected; the tax authority levies a fine of 100% of the un-withheld tax, plus 0.8% monthly interest; the debtor/SCSp is exclusively liable for the grossed-up tax.

Director personal liability: The managing partner who approved the payment without verifying a valid TRC is personally liable for the withheld amount; Luxembourg criminal courts may classify the failure as willful breach leading to a EUR 25,000 fine and a custodial sentence of up to 3 years.

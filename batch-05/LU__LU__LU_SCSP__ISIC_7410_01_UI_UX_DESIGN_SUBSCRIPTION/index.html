Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 72a Avenue des Hauts-Fourneaux, L-4362 Esch-sur-Alzette, Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; online filing portal at www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, particularly Articles 1, 10, 27–28, 41–42 and 155–159 concerning partnerships; the Law of 23 July 2016 introducing the Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp); the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and Receuil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA); and the Law of 13 January 2019 establishing the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif / Contrat de société (deed of constitution and partnership agreement of the SCSp), executed either as a notarial deed or as a private writing, filed with the RCS together with the statutory RCS registration form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; the partnership agreement fixes the contributions freely. No escrow or blocked bank account is legally required. Contributions may be in cash, in kind (valued per the deed), or in industry, but industry contributions are reserved to the general partner. If a notarial deed references cash contributions, the notary may require a bank confirmation of deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the leased office premises must have a valid occupancy permit, and any common areas open to the public require a fire safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the Corps grand-ducal d’incendie et de secours. Structural load: the lessor must confirm that the premises support normal office floor loading; no special structural certification is required for IT workstations. Occupational clearance: the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) requires compliance with the Labour Code on work organisation, VDU ergonomics, electrical equipment safety checks, fire-escape signage, and a written risk assessment (évaluation des risques) prepared by the employer and kept on site.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SCSp; the partnership agreement fixes the contributions freely. No escrow or blocked bank account is legally required. Contributions may be in cash, in kind (valued per the deed), or in industry, but industry contributions are reserved to the general partner. If a notarial deed references cash contributions, the notary may require a bank confirmation of deposit.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 7410 (UI/UX product design and design subscription services). If manufacturing were undertaken, the competent authority would be the Ministry of the Economy – Department of Industry and Competitiveness.

Category: Not required. No industrial exploitation permit (autorisation d’exploitation / Kommissionserlaabnes) applies to non-industrial office-based design and subscription activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the SCSp (introduced by law of 12 July 2013 amending the law of 10 August 1915, art. 65-1) is a special limited partnership that is statutorily fiscally neutral/transparent; it has no legal personality (unless a specific election is made under commercial law), is not subject to Luxembourg CIT or municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and its profits are allocated and taxed directly at partner level according to each partner's participation and personal tax profile.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or classification election exists under Luxembourg domestic tax law; pass-through status attaches by statutory form. The partnership agreement may nevertheless allocate rights, profits and losses in accordance with economic substance, subject to Luxembourg arm's-length and substance rules (art. 8 Abgabenordnung). A US partner may file a US check-the-box election (disregarded entity) for US purposes, which Luxembourg ignores for CIT. Luxembourg applies the ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules (LIR articles 168ter to 168septies, effective from 1 January 2020) to neutralise any double non-taxation arising from divergent foreign classifications of the SCSp.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 - domestic small-business franchise (art. 57 VLDT) for established small suppliers only; NOT available to non-EU established SCSp. Non-EU SCSp supplying B2C electronically supplied design subscriptions must VAT register in Luxembourg or use the Union OSS/IOSS scheme from the first euro of sales. EU-established SCSp may benefit from the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-EU B2C distance sales/digital services (OSS). B2B design services to taxable customers in other EU Member States always trigger reverse-charge handling and require an LU VAT number, with no local threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT under art. 41 VLDT where the goods are physically exported outside the EU, evidenced by a released Single Administrative Document (SAD, status A). Services: B2B UI/UX design and subscription services supplied by the LU SCSp to a customer established outside the EU are governed by the Business-to-Business place-of-supply rule (art. 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC) and are therefore located outside Luxembourg - reportable in the VAT return as exempt-with-credit at 0% with documented proof of the customer's non-EU status; B2C automated/electronic design subscription products supplied to non-EU consumers are likewise outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (no registration or reporting duty in Luxembourg). B2B supplies to EU established customers are not zero-rated but reverse-charged at the customer's VAT number.

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is operative in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is permitted but not mandatory for B2B; where used, the vendor must ensure authenticity of origin and integrity of content (qualified e-signature, EDI, or equivalent controls, art. 12 VLDT). Mandatory electronic filing applies to VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and EC Sales Lists via MyGuichet.lu. Taxpayers must produce a standardised electronic accounting file (fichier d'écritures comptables normalisé, FeC/XML) upon tax audit. EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) may introduce harmonised e-invoicing and digital reporting by 2030.

The AEDT may reassess VAT for up to five years, with the limitation period suspended in cases of fraud. Improper zero-rating or missing export documentation triggers reassessment at the standard 17% VAT plus statutory late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.), and administrative fines under art. 73 VLDT ranging from EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 per non-compliant document or invoice. Fraudulent misuse of the zero-rating/export regime (false export declarations, fictitious non-EU customers) constitutes tax evasion under art. 80 of the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), punished by fines up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years, possible publication of the conviction, joint and several liability of the general partner (gérant) and managing partners, disqualification of directors, and full clawback of input VAT deducted in connection with the disallowed transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control authorization, no quantitative limits, and no central-bank pre-approval on cross-border payments. For an SCSp, which is fiscally transparent, current-account profit remittances to partners face no Luxembourg withholding tax; the real practical hurdle is purely bank-level source-of-funds and ultimate-beneficial-owner verification. The SCSp may hold multi-currency accounts, invoice in EUR or foreign currency, and freely convert balances. The only compliance perimeter is BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting and CSSF AML/KYC enforcement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law and CSSF guidance impose no mandatory timeframe, no conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or service-trade receipts. The design-subscription agency may retain foreign currency indefinitely, convert at commercial bank rates, or use the proceeds directly for outbound vendor payments. The sole statutory duty is ancillary: transactions with non-resident counterparties exceeding EUR 12,500 per month must be declared to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics, without restricting the transaction itself.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national monetary and statistical authority; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises licensed banks and payment/EMI institutions. Exchange controls were abolished in Luxembourg under EU capital-movement liberalization (Article 63 TFEU) and national rules; no ex-ante authorization from the BCL or CSSF is required for cross-border capital flows, trade settlement, or repatriation by non-residents.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-to-High for ancillary payment flows, Low as a sole treasury backbone. EU/EEA electronic-money institutions (Wise Europe SA, Revolut) can passport payment and EMI services into Luxembourg under PSD2 via CSSF notification and typically provide EUR IBANs, low-cost cross-currency conversion, and high-velocity API settlement — operationally well suited to Stripe Billing subscription sweeps. However, most EMIs apply enhanced scrutiny to partnerships registered as SCSp because the entity lacks separate legal personality; the account must be opened by the general partner (gérant) for the account of the SCSp. EMIs also cannot provide committed credit, commercial LC, or substantive treasury FX lines. The recommended blueprint places a tier-1 Luxembourg bank account as the ultimate treasury anchor, with Wise serving as a collection/disbursement rail that sweeps net proceeds into the primary bank account, and Stripe Billing retained as the merchant-of-record for recurring design subscriptions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer to the SCSp's IBAN for EUR, or via SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR/non-SEPA remittances. The wire narrative must contain the SCSp registered name, the Luxembourg matricule, the capital-call reference, and the purpose narrative 'equity contribution to partnership capital'. No central-bank permit or exchange-control approval is required. If the contributing partner is non-resident and the monthly aggregate exceeds EUR 12,500, the BCL balance-of-payments direct-investment declaration applies and must be filed by the resident recipient (the SCSp or its appointed filing agent). Best practice is to open a dedicated capital-contribution sub-account at the tier-1 bank to maintain an untainted audit trail distinct from operating subscription revenue; the contribution is then posted to the partners' capital accounts and evidenced by signed capital-call notices.Operating disbursements — designer contractor fees, SaaS license fees, cloud infrastructure, and subcontractor royalties — settle via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR, and via SWIFT MT103 for USD, GBP, and other non-EUR payments. Commercial letters of credit are structurally unsuitable for service exports and should not be used; payment against invoice is the standard. USD proceeds collected through Stripe and Wise are swept into the SCSp's tier-1 Luxembourg bank account and converted to EUR through the treasury FX line. Vendor payments above EUR 50,000, or any payment touching a high-risk or exposed jurisdiction, should be sent as ISO 20022 pain.001/pain.002 messages with structured remittance data, pre-validation of beneficiary BIC, and prior OFAC/EU-sanctions screening. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-High for ancillary payment flows, Low as a sole treasury backbone. EU/EEA electronic-money institutions (Wise Europe SA, Revolut) can passport payment and EMI services into Luxembourg under PSD2 via CSSF notification and typically provide EUR IBANs, low-cost cross-currency conversion, and high-velocity API settlement — operationally well suited to Stripe Billing subscription sweeps. However, most EMIs apply enhanced scrutiny to partnerships registered as SCSp because the entity lacks separate legal personality; the account must be opened by the general partner (gérant) for the account of the SCSp. EMIs also cannot provide committed credit, commercial LC, or substantive treasury FX lines. The recommended blueprint places a tier-1 Luxembourg bank account as the ultimate treasury anchor, with Wise serving as a collection/disbursement rail that sweeps net proceeds into the primary bank account, and Stripe Billing retained as the merchant-of-record for recurring design subscriptions.

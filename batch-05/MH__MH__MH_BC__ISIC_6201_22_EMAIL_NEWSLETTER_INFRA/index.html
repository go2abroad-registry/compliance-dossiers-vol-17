Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Division (RTD), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), Majuro; operating under the Income Tax Act 1989, the Business License Act 2011, and the RMI Customs Tariff provisions. For non-resident business corporations, the functionally relevant registry is the Office of the Registrar of Marshall Islands Corporations (ROMIC) — official portal: https://www.romic.org — which administers incorporation, annual franchise filings, and Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) declarations. No separate national taxpayer web portal exists for tax-exempt offshore entities; statutory texts are published via the Pacific Islands Legal Information Institute (http://www.paclii.org).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a licensed RMI registered agent (mandatory under the Business Corporations Act 1990). Step 2: Reserve the proposed corporate name with ROMIC. Step 3: File Articles of Incorporation, including the NRDC declaration, with the registered agent; ROMIC issues the Certificate of Incorporation and a permanent Registry Number. Step 4: Pay the one-time incorporation fee based on authorized share capital and receive the registered-agent file references. Step 5: File the annual franchise/filing report and pay the fixed annual government fee to ROMIC each anniversary; no local tax return is required because the entity is tax-exempt. Step 6: Because a pure NRDC is wholly exempt from income tax, no General TIN or VAT ID is issued or required; the ROMIC Registry Number and the NRDC Certificate operate as the entity's statutory tax identifiers. Step 7 (optional but prudent): Apply to the U.S. IRS for an EIN via Form SS-4 as a foreign entity for FATCA documentation, Stripe Billing onboarding, and U.S. information reporting. Step 8: The registered agent must maintain current beneficial-ownership records under RMI beneficial-ownership and AML/CFT rules for production to the Registrar on demand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Marshall Islands Business Corporation (BC) is a juridical person with limited liability. A Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) conducting all business outside the RMI and deriving no RMI-source income is statutorily exempt from national income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents. Entity-level taxation is not triggered; the exemption is a statutory status, not a pass-through election. No RMI payroll or social-security contributions apply absent locally employed individuals in the RMI.

Available election forms

1) NRDC status election under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporations Act 1985: the incorporator files a certificate/declaration with ROMIC, through the registered agent, certifying that all business will be conducted outside the RMI; this election is recorded on the corporate register. 2) Domestic check-the-box election: none, because no income tax is imposed on a compliant NRDC. 3) U.S. federal classification: an entity formed under Marshall Islands law is not a per se corporation under Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i); therefore, U.S. owners may file IRS Form 8832 to elect corporate, partnership, or disregarded-entity classification for U.S. tax purposes. 4) Hybrid mismatch rules: the RMI has no domestic hybrid-mismatch legislation; any BEPS Action 2 consequences are determined exclusively under the shareholder's home jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax in the RMI. The only indirect-style levy is customs import duty on physical goods entering the Islands. Cross-border email-API subscriptions, newsletter-hosting fees, and SaaS infrastructure revenues are outside the scope of any RMI indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — the RMI has no indirect tax registration regime, no services-tax threshold, and no digital-services tax. An NRDC supplying email and newsletter infrastructure to foreign clients has no RMI indirect tax registration obligation regardless of turnover. Customs import duty arises only per shipment on physical goods and is not connected to any economic turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the RMI does not operate a VAT/GST system, so there is no export zero-rating mechanism and no refund procedure to substantiate. Cross-border email-API and newsletter-hosting services rendered by an NRDC are foreign-source and wholly outside the RMI tax net. The RMI has no exchange-control regime and uses the U.S. Dollar, so no foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is imposed (effective deadline in days: 0).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands operates as a de facto dollarized economy with full and unrestricted convertibility on both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no advance approvals for foreign investment remittances, and profit repatriation and capital liquidation proceeds may be freely wired abroad without quantitative limits or licensing requirements. The only practical compliance layer is standard anti-money laundering (AML) verification of beneficial ownership and end-use declarations by the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the state or to convert any portion of export receipts into local currency. Because the U.S. dollar is the domestic currency, all export earnings remain in the same currency, and no mandatory conversion ratio or time-bound repatriation rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (no domestic central bank; U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender issued by the U.S. Federal Reserve)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. The Marshall Islands has no fintech e-money institution (EMI) licensing framework that would grant a standalone offshore EMI portal for corporate treasury operations. Offshore digital banks or unlicensed virtual asset service providers are not accepted by RMI commercial banks as viable settlement counterparties for primary liquidity clearing. A non-resident entity may use regulated U.S.-chartered fintech banks (e.g., a chartered digital bank) as a supplementary clearing rail, but such arrangements are not a substitute for a licensed RMI domestic bank account and do not replace the AML/beneficial ownership verification required by BOMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 wire from the investor's bank to the RMI domestic bank account. The wire must carry a standard capital purpose code, generally 'S1301' (direct inward investment) or an equivalent corporate service description recognized by the receiving bank, accompanied by the definitive share subscription agreement, board resolution, and investor's source-of-wealth questionnaire. The RMI bank will verify the beneficial owner through the UBO register and screen all parties against OFAC and UN sanctions lists. The funds are then credited to the Operational Account and may be held in a segregated Capital Escrow Account until the legal capital deployment is evidenced by contracts or tax filings.Outbound trade and operational disbursements are cleared through the same USD SWIFT infrastructure. Standard settlement rails include: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier payments and SaaS licensing fees with corresponding purpose codes; (2) commercial letters of credit (documentary LC) for any physical goods movement, drawn on a U.S. correspondent bank; and (3) intra-group clearing via SWIFT MT101/MT103 between RMI entity and related operating subsidiaries. For cards and merchant acquiring, the platform may route to Stripe as the principal payment gateway; Stripe then settles in USD through U.S. ACH/Fedwire to the RMI operating bank. No local clearing house is involved because the domestic currency is USD.No onshore FX market exists in the Marshall Islands; since the USD is legal tender, no FX hedge is required for operations denominated in USD.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts executed through a non-resident international bank are permissible for hedging nominal exposure to non-USD customer refunds or vendor payments, but these are not settled in the RMI and do not require local regulatory approval.Cross-currency swaps are permissible solely for treasury risk management when the counterparty is an OECD-licensed bank; documentation must evidence business purpose and will be scrutinized by the RMI bank's AML unit.Spot USD exchange through the domestic bank's correspondent network is permitted for conversion of incidental foreign currency receipts, but no leveraged or speculative FX transactions are allowed.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no income from sources within the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the 3% Gross Revenue Tax under the Gross Revenue Tax Act 1989 applies only to entities licensed to carry on business within the RMI and does not attach to foreign-source subscription, API, or newsletter infrastructure revenue.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the Marshall Islands imposes no withholding tax on dividends declared or paid by a Business Corporation to shareholders, whether resident or non-resident; dividends are outside RMI source-based taxation under the Income Tax Act 1989.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing regime, Local File, Master File, or related-party transaction threshold exists under RMI law. Best-practice governance threshold: maintain contemporaneous arm's-length documentation and a benchmark study whenever annual cross-border related-party transactions, including group software licensing, intercompany API service fees, shared hosting costs, or co-marketing arrangements, exceed USD 500,000; a master-file quality group overview and per-entity local file should be retained to support foreign tax authority challenges, ESR substance records, and bank KYC reviews.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019 applies to non-resident domestic companies and foreign-resident entities carrying on a 'relevant activity': banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centers, or intellectual property (IP) holding. A standalone email-newsletter infrastructure SaaS platform selling services to third-party non-residents is not a statutorily relevant activity unless (i) it derives royalty-type income from licensed IP held for related parties, or (ii) it operates as a group distribution or service center. If the platform is restructured as an IP-holding vehicle, the RMI entity must satisfy a proportionate economic substance test: in-RMI board meetings, physical premises, qualified employees, adequate expenditure, and an annual substance filing. As the EU currently maintains the RMI on Annex II of the non-cooperative jurisdictions list, continued monitoring of substance enforcement is required, and the operating substance of the group is anchored in the parent jurisdiction (e.g., United States) to avoid adverse shell-entity determinations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax plus statutory interest at 1.5% per month; criminal fine up to USD 10,000 per offense and imprisonment up to two years under the Income Tax Act 1989 and Gross Revenue Tax Act 1989.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the suppression of RMI-source revenue are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; criminal convictions carry travel bans, extradition exposure, and automatic disqualification as directors in regulated jurisdictions.
Shell Company and Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Administrative dissolution after two consecutive years of unpaid registry fees; voiding of contracts entered after dissolution; deboarding or refusal by payment service providers, account freezes, and US FATCA / CFC look-through treatment causing inclusion of the entity's income in the US owner's taxable base; adverse CFC and GILTI determinations eliminate the zero-tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who procure contracts after dissolution are personally liable for all resulting obligations; under the doctrine of sham subordination, the corporate veil is pierced in foreign prosecutions, exposing directors to civil restitution to investors and creditors, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to ten years for aggravated fraud.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under the RMI Criminal Code: fines, forfeiture of merchant proceeds, imprisonment up to ten years, cancellation of the registered agent license, and potential FATF consequence listing of the entity; the RMI's non-cooperative jurisdiction status amplifies bank de-risking and reputational sanctions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraud, sham, or nominee misuse: directors face civil restitution to defrauded parties, personal asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment up to ten years; nominee directors bear equal criminal liability and cannot invoke the corporate shield as a defense.
Cross-Border Tax Mischaracterization and US CFC Exposure

Statutory sanction: US: GILTI inclusion at an effective 10.5% US corporate rate (21% statutory rate after the 50% deduction) on the pro-rata share of tested income with no foreign tax credits where the RMI entity paid zero foreign tax; Form 5471 late-filing penalties of USD 10,000 per form per year, up to USD 60,000; EU: CFC rules and ATAD3 shell-entity presumptions render the structure non-transparent and deny cross-border deductions.

Director personal liability: The US person shareholder-director faces accuracy-related penalties of 20% for noncompliance and 40% for gross valuation misstatements; repeated violations trigger felony tax charges, civil restitution to the IRS, and travel restrictions; deliberate use of the RMI shell to conceal the US owner constitutes tax evasion subject to a six-year limitations period and indictment risk.

Governing statute

Business Corporations Act, Title 26 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code, Chapter 1, Sections 51-58 (incorporation), Section 64 (defective filing cure), Sections 72-75 (registered agent and registered office), and Sections 95-105 (directors and officers).

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Incorporation (Form BC-1) together with the Registered Agent Consent and Appointment Form, filed electronically or in paper form with the Registrar of Corporations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Tax Administration, Majuro, Republic of the Marshall Islands; no dedicated taxpayer online portal is operational, and tax and customs interactions are conducted through the Ministry of Finance and its Customs Division.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) under the Marshall Islands Business Corporation Act is statutorily tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a TIN solely for incorporation. If a TIN is needed for customs registration, banking, or voluntary compliance, the foreign entity must file the RMI Taxpayer Registration Form with the Revenue and Tax Administration, accompanied by: (i) certified Articles of Incorporation and Good Standing Certificate issued by the RMI Registrar of Business Corporations; (ii) the registered agent's name and Majuro office address; (iii) identification of directors and ultimate beneficial owners; and (iv) a board resolution confirming the domestic registered agent. The TIN is issued within approximately five working days. No VAT identification number exists because the Marshall Islands has no VAT/GST regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) under the Marshall Islands Business Corporation Act is statutorily tax-exempt and is not required to obtain a TIN solely for incorporation. If a TIN is needed for customs registration, banking, or voluntary compliance, the foreign entity must file the RMI Taxpayer Registration Form with the Revenue and Tax Administration, accompanied by: (i) certified Articles of Incorporation and Good Standing Certificate issued by the RMI Registrar of Business Corporations; (ii) the registered agent's name and Majuro office address; (iii) identification of directors and ultimate beneficial owners; and (iv) a board resolution confirming the domestic registered agent. The TIN is issued within approximately five working days. No VAT identification number exists because the Marshall Islands has no VAT/GST regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Marshall Islands law. A non-resident Business Corporation is automatically classified as a domestic corporation under the BCCA but is wholly tax-exempt while it remains non-resident. The only relevant statutory election is the decision to cease non-resident status by applying for a domestic business license, which would trigger the RMI gross revenue tax and business license fees; there is no pass-through or entity-level income tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; limited Gross Revenue Tax on resident licensed businesses and customs duties on physical imports/exports

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST threshold. The RMI Gross Revenue Tax (Title 54, RMI Code) applies to every resident business license holder from the first US dollar of gross revenue at a rate generally quoted as 3%, unless specifically exempt; a non-resident Business Corporation is outside the charge to gross revenue tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating mechanism or VAT refund to claim. Physical exports are subject to customs procedural requirements only, and offshore services supplied by a non-resident Business Corporation are outside the territorial scope of RMI gross revenue tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Exit Certificate (required only for physical exports)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank issued receipt (maintained for due diligence, not for VAT substantiation)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List for physical export customs clearance

No electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists. The Marshall Islands does not operate a CTC/e-invoice API, digital stamping platform, or any real-time transaction clearance requirement.

There is no export VAT zero-rating regime and therefore no export refund to claw back. For physical export transactions, false or inaccurate customs declarations are penalized under the RMI Customs Code with fines, forfeiture of goods, and potential criminal prosecution. The non-resident Business Corporation remains exempt from income and gross revenue taxes, so no income-tax audit exposure arises from offshore service revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible USD environment. There are no exchange controls, no capital/current account distinction, and no requirement for central bank approval. Dividends, loan repayments, and capital proceeds may be freely remitted. Practical limitations arise only from bank-level compliance: beneficial ownership, source of funds, and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Marshall Islands law imposes no statutory conversion, surrender, or rationing of export receipts. Export proceeds can be retained, held, or disbursed in U.S. dollars indefinitely, subject only to AML/CTF transaction reporting and record-keeping requirements.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands - Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance (no central bank exists; the U.S. Federal Reserve acts as the monetary authority for the U.S. dollar, which is the sole legal tender)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a collection conduit; Low as a treasury substitute. RMI has no domestic EMI licensing regime and no digital currency framework, so offshore EMIs are unregulated locally. An offshore EMI must be licensed in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction and contracted directly to the RMI BC; however, local commercial banks will likely deem such accounts high-risk and may require unwind to a local bank. Use Paymentwall/Apple IAP only for micro-collection, with daily or weekly sweeps into a licensed RMI bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 – The Marshall Islands imposes no local transfer pricing documentation requirement. No Local File or Master File threshold is triggered in-country. However, if the entity is part of a multinational group with controlling tax authorities (e.g., IRS, HMRC), those foreign TP documentation rules will apply based on the group's consolidated revenue and related-party transaction thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2020 apply to entities conducting 'relevant activities' as defined by the OECD. A non-resident BC used for a video-match social app, deriving income from customer subscriptions and advertising, will only fall within ESR if it carries on a relevant activity such as intellectual property holding, distribution and service centres, shipping, or financing/leasing. Merely outsourcing all platform operations to third-party service providers does not automatically trigger ESR, but the entity must avoid booking IP royalties or loans in the Marshall Islands without evidencing substance. Full substance is required if the entity is tax resident (managed and controlled) in the Marshall Islands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code, willful tax evasion and fraud carry fines up to USD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The corporate charter may also be revoked by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, or agents who knowingly participate in such misrepresentation are personally liable for the tax loss and may face civil restitution, travel bans, disqualification orders, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance penalties: initial fine up to USD 10,000 and continuing penalty up to USD 20,000 per year; possible strike-off of the company and referral to OECD for harmful tax practices.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly manage an entity without economic substance are jointly and severally liable for penalties, plus civil restitution and travel restrictions.
AML / CTF Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Act, penalties include fines up to USD 100,000 or twice the value of the transaction, whichever is higher, and imprisonment up to 10 years. Assets linked to prohibited activity are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failure to maintain an effective AML compliance programme; they may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, attachment of personal assets, and extradition to requesting states.
Failure to Maintain Registered Agent and Annual Filings

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may administratively dissolve the BC after 90 days' notice; reinstatement requires payment of all outstanding annual fees, penalties (typically USD 100–USD 500), and a reinstatement fee.

Director personal liability: Post-dissolution, directors may be personally liable for debts incurred in the company's name during the period of non-compliance; travel bans may be imposed to secure the payment of public liabilities.

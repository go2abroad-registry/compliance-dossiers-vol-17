Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands – Revenue and Taxation Division (operational portal: https://www.rmi-finance.org)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) obtains a corporate registration number from the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act. No TIN or VAT ID is issued to a purely non-resident BC. If the BC later establishes a taxable presence in the Marshall Islands (e.g., local physical office, employees, or RMI-source business activity), it must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) using the Revenue and Taxation Division registration forms. For independent film licensing conducted entirely offshore, no ministerial license and no local TIN is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident domestic Business Corporation whose foreign-source income and offshore licensing royalties are outside the RMI income tax base

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election under RMI law. Under the Business Corporations Act, a corporation may elect non-resident status by filing the appropriate articles/registration with the Registrar of Corporations; this election confirms exemption from RMI income tax on non-RMI-source income. No deemed dividend or transparent pass-through election is available because the jurisdiction imposes no corporate income tax on non-resident BCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax – the Marshall Islands levies only customs import duties under the Customs Act; there is no general consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST or sales tax registration threshold exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – because RMI does not impose VAT/GST/sales tax, there is no export zero-rating mechanism and no VAT refund claim can arise. Film license royalties earned by a non-resident BC from foreign distributors are not RMI-source income and are exempt from RMI income tax under the non-resident domestic corporation rules. To maintain that exemption, the BC must not carry on trade or business in RMI and must derive the royalties from licensing activities wholly outside the islands.

  • Not applicable – no RMI VAT export zero-rating refund scheme exists; therefore no statutory VAT-claim customs documentation bundle is required.
  • Recommended if physical film prints are exported: RMI Customs export declaration, commercial invoice, and packing list.
  • Recommended for licensing-income substantiation: executed film license agreement, film title/rights schedule, and bill of lading or airway bill where physical media is moved.
  • Recommended for remittance substantiation: inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103/MT950) and bank statement evidencing receipt of royalties from the foreign licensee.

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping regime is in force. RMI customs entry processing is administered through the Customs Service within the Ministry of Finance; paper or electronic declarations are accepted without third-party fiscal validation infrastructure.

Not applicable in a VAT context because RMI has no VAT/GST and no zero-rating refund clawback mechanism. However, if a BC is found to have violated the non-resident conditions or misrepresented its offshore status, the Revenue and Taxation Division may treat the corporation as resident and assess RMI-source income tax under the Income Tax Act, together with statutory interest and late-filing penalties. Fraudulent or negligent customs declarations for physical exports may attract fines, forfeiture, and other penalties under the Customs Act. There are no retrospective VAT clawbacks because no input/output VAT mechanism exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI is a fully dollarized, single-currency jurisdiction, rendering the classic current-account versus capital-account convertibility divide legally immaterial: all payments, royalties, dividends, loan advances and equity injections are freely convertible and transferable in USD without quantitative limits, central approval, or surrender mechanisms. The RMI has been an IMF member with Article VIII obligations since 1992, confirming the commitment to refrain from restrictions on payments and transfers. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore nonexistent in RMI statute; the only practical friction arises from correspondent-bank compliance when USD is cleared through US hub banks, not from any domestic exchange-control barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rules exist. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation quota, or surrender obligation applicable to export receipts or cross-border receivables. All USD royalties, distribution fees, placement-agent commissions and ancillary income derived from the ISIC 5911 film-licensing business may be retained in offshore or onshore accounts at the entity's sole discretion, subject only to general record-keeping and AML/CFT documentation duties under the Banking Act and the Anti-Money Laundering Act.

Central bank supervision: No central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The US Dollar is the sole lawful currency and circulates freely under the Compact of Free Association, so monetary conditions are set de facto by the US Federal Reserve and US Treasury clearing and sanctions rules. Banking and foreign-exchange supervision is performed by the Banking Commissioner within the RMI Ministry of Finance under the Banking Act 1989 (as amended), with AML/CFT enforcement anchored in the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2005 (as amended).

Feasibility rating: LOW for a non-resident Marshall Islands Business Corporation. Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding is executed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 routed to BOMI's US correspondent, with a compliant Field 70 purpose narrative, e.g., 'Shareholder capital contribution - ISIC 5911 film licensing BC' or 'Shareholder loan per board resolution'. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act (BCA) derives income outside the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI); such offshore income is statutorily excluded from the Income Tax Act 1989 (MIRC Title 54). No Marshall Islands corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or branch profits tax is imposed. Only income sourced within the RMI is taxable, and the target licensing model generates no RMI-source income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — The RMI does not impose corporate withholding taxes. Dividends, distributions, deemed distributions, and liquidating proceeds paid by an RMI Business Corporation to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident, are free of RMI withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Not applicable; a non-resident BC is outside the RMI income tax return system. The only annual fiscal deadline is the BCA license fee due on or before 30 April of each calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — the RMI has not enacted statutory Transfer Pricing (Local File/Master File) documentation rules for tax-exempt non-resident BCs. The official currency is the United States Dollar (USD), but no local-currency threshold exists in the RMI Internal Revenue Code or BCA. Nevertheless, a multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million is expected to file Country-by-Country data under the RMI's commitment to the OECD BEPS framework if the RMI entity is a constituent entity, but in practice the offshore filming licensing BC has no RMI filing trigger because it is not tax-resident and earns no RMI-source income.

Economic substance scope: The RMI International Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019/2020 (ESR) apply to every entity incorporated in the RMI carrying on a 'relevant activity'. A BC engaged in independent film copyright licensing is classified as an Intellectual Property Business (or Distribution and Service Centre) and is squarely within the ESR. The entity must demonstrate, in the RMI: (1) management and control exercised by RMI-resident directors; (2) full-time employees adequate for the volume of licensing; (3) premises suitable for activity; and (4) operating expenditure proportionate to the royalty income. An IP company enjoying a 'high-risk' category must also show the absence of offshore 'outsourcing' of core income-generating activities unless outsourced to RMI-connected parties with supervisory control in the RMI. The 'pure holding company' exemption is unavailable for active film licensing. In practice, the target structure fails the test because CIGA (contract negotiation, copyright enforcement, collection) occur in the United States or Europe; the RMI structure must therefore be substantiated or restructured.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance (ESR) Evasion — Shell IP Company

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to USD 10,000 per false ESR filing plus daily default fines; the Registrar may strike off the BC; adverse exchange of information with the EU Code of Conduct Group and the OECD; the BC's tax-exempt status is prospectively cancelled, and the entire accumulated royalty reserve becomes taxable under the domestic law (if RMI-source recharacterized) or the entity is dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominees, and the RMI registered agent who sign or procure a false ESR declaration are jointly and severally liable to penalties; the Registrar can commence criminal proceedings for false statements, issue travel restrictions preventing directors from leaving the RMI, and impose restitution of the entity's assets to the Registrar upon strike-off.
International Tax Evasion Facilitation — Sham Film Licensing

Statutory sanction: RMI Criminal Code (MIRC Title 9) criminalizes forgery, false statements, and conspiracy to defraud revenue: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to USD 50,000 per count; forfeiture of the royalty proceeds as proceeds of crime; the Registrar can strike off the entity and the foreign tax authority can request mutual legal assistance through the RMI Attorney-General.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced wherever the BC is found to be a mere facade; directors and shadow directors are personally liable for restitution of all diverted copyright royalties and any foreign tax, penalties, and interest the true owner evaded; courts may detain passports, impose travel bans, and commit directors for contempt for up to 6 months pending asset recovery.
US-Source Withholding Tax Leakage and CFC/Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: The US licensee is liable as withholding agent for 30% of gross US-source film royalties, plus interest and failure-to-withhold penalties; for the US transferor, IRC Section 367(d) recasts the IP transfer into a deemed annual royalty taxable as US-source income; IRC Section 482 reallocates the BC's profit; the RMI BC is not a CFC because it pays no RMI tax, so all tested income flows to US shareholders as GILTI inclusions (subject to a 10.5% minimum US tax); no treaty relief exists because the RMI has no US DTA.

Director personal liability: US persons who are officers of the US withholding agent face a 100% responsible person penalty under IRC Section 6672 for unpaid withheld taxes; US controlling shareholders of the RMI BC face the GILTI/subpart-F inclusion on their individual returns, civil fraud penalties up to 75%, and criminal tax prosecution with imprisonment; RMI directors who cause the false 'gross remittance' letter to the US distributor are treated as aiders and abettors.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Failures — Sanctions and Piracy Exposure

Statutory sanction: RMI anti-money laundering legislation criminalizes laundering criminal proceeds with imprisonment up to 20 years and fines; the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze the BC's bank accounts; failure to file beneficial ownership data attracts monetary penalties of up to USD 5,000 and administrative strike-off; violations of UN sanctions trigger asset-freeze and international shipping/banking bans.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and any non-resident nominee who facilitates payment routing through a third-party account are prosecutable for money laundering, even if the act occurred offshore; RMI courts grant asset preservation orders and travel bans; upon conviction, directors face imprisonment and confiscation of the BC's intellectual property and royalty receivables.
CRS/FATCA Mis-Classification — Passive NFE Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The RMI has in force a Model-1 IGA with the US and CRS exchange arrangements; the RMI bank must report the controlling US person to the RMI Competent Authority, which automatically exchanges the information with the IRS; mis-certification triggers a USD 10,000 penalty under CRS rules, reporting leakage, and a Category-4 or Category-6 audit risk classification by the RMI financial intelligence unit.

Director personal liability: Directors signing a false CRS self-certification are personally liable for penalties and for any loss to the entity or its shareholders caused by the resulting disclosure; the beneficial owner's undisclosed accounts are exposed to US FBAR and FATCA penalties (starting at USD 10,000 per account, rising to 50% of the account balance for willful violations), alongside potential criminal referral.

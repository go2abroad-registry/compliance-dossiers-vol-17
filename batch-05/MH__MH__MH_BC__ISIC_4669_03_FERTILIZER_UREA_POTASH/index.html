Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance & Administration, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Majuro); corporate registration administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General. There is no national VAT/GST administration or e-filing portal; annual corporate fees are settled through the Registrar of Corporations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident domestic Business Corporation is statutorily exempt from income tax and gross revenue tax under Section 118 of the Business Corporations Act. No TIN or VAT ID is required for purely offshore operations. The entity obtains a corporate registration number by filing articles of incorporation through a licensed resident agent and paying the annual franchise fee; a Certificate of Good Standing serves as the de facto tax identity document. If the entity were to derive income from sources within the Marshall Islands, it would register with the Revenue Division of the Ministry of Finance under the Gross Revenue Tax Act, but such registration is inapplicable to a non-resident BC engaged exclusively in offshore fertilizer wholesale.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None available. The Marshall Islands has no check-the-box regime, no hybrid mismatch election, and no elective inclusion mechanism. The exemption from all local taxes under Business Corporations Act Section 118 is mandatory for non-resident domestic corporations, and the entity cannot elect to be treated as tax resident or taxable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST. The Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax or sales tax; the only indirect-type levy is the 3% Gross Revenue Tax on locally sourced gross revenue and customs import duties, neither of which applies to a non-resident BC's offshore wholesale of prilled urea and potassium chloride.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No VAT/GST registration threshold exists. A Gross Revenue Tax registration obligation would arise only if the entity were 'carrying on business' within the Republic of the Marshall Islands and deriving local-source revenue; such activity is not contemplated for the MH_BC non-resident entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands has no VAT/GST zero-rating mechanism and imposes no export duty on goods. The offshore sale and physical charter of fertilizer from a non-resident BC is entirely outside the scope of Marshall Islands indirect taxation, so no claim for a 0% export VAT rate can arise.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only if goods are physically cleared through RMI customs; generally not required for offshore transshipment)
  • Bill of Lading or non-negotiable sea waybill evidencing the international movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with Incoterms and no local consumption in the Marshall Islands
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank MT103/LC settlement record evidencing payment from the overseas buyer
  • Certificate of Origin (retained as supporting evidence, optional in the absence of VAT)

None. The Republic of the Marshall Islands maintains no electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance system, no API reporting requirement, and no digital stamping regime for commercial invoices.

As no VAT refund or zero-rating claim mechanism exists in the Marshall Islands, no VAT clawback liability can attach. However, false declarations made to the Registrar of Corporations or to RMI Customs in respect of exempt status, the nature of the transaction, or the non-resident character of the entity may be prosecuted under the Business Corporations Act and the Customs Act, exposing the corporation and its directors to fines, forfeiture of goods, imprisonment, revocation of the Certificate of Incorporation, and retrospective assessment of any unpaid gross revenue tax plus penalties and interest for a period of up to six years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI is a fully dollarized jurisdiction, so there are no exchange controls imposed by local law or regulation. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible into USD, and there are no statutory approvals for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation. In practice, financial institutions apply a documentary AML/KYC review to remittances, including source-of-funds evidence and sanctions screening, but no governmental exchange-control hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule exists in the RMI. Export receipts may be retained, used, or repatriated freely in USD or any commercially agreed currency. There is no mandatory statutory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no central bank filing requirement for export proceeds. Banks may, however, require supporting trade documents for underwriting and regulatory compliance.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a domestic central bank or an independent foreign exchange administration. The official lawful currency is the United States dollar (USD). Banking supervision is administered by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance; anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) supervision is the responsibility of the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI payment providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in the RMI and may not be used as the primary regulated account for statutory capital or trade settlements. Feasibility is Low for treasury anchored in the RMI, because the RMI Banking Act requires a licence for deposit-taking and the Banking Commissioner does not recognise unlicensed foreign EMIs. Such EMIs may be used only as supplemental payment corridors if the settlement bank is willing to receive from them, but the statutory and compliance treasury accounts must remain at a locally licensed or recognised financial institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 or SWIFT MT202 in USD to the Bank of Marshall Islands USD account. The beneficiary field must clearly reference the entity name and account number, and the payment field should state 'Capital contribution under RMI Business Corporations Act' or equivalent. Since the RMI has no central bank approval requirement, the transaction is cleared through the bank's US correspondent network via Fedwire or CHIPS. The receiving bank will require a board resolution, UBO declaration, source-of-funds evidence, and the underlying subscription or share issuance documentation before crediting the capital escrow account.Outbound settlements for fertilizer cargoes are cleared through (i) open-account payments by SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and advised/confirmed by global banks under UCP600; (iii) documentary collections (SWIFT MT400) for the presentation of title documents; and (iv) cash-in-advance wire transfers via SWIFT MT103. The RMI has no domestic real-time gross settlement system; therefore, all USD clearing is performed via the correspondent banking network in the United States.Outright foreign-exchange forward contracts between USD and trade-settlement currencies such as EUR, CNY, INR, IDR, or VND executed with a licensed international bank under an ISDA master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with limited convertibility, including USD/CNY and USD/IDR, cash-settled in USD.Cross-currency interest rate swaps or simple cross-currency swaps where a long-term fertiliser supply agreement or vessel charter creates recurring non-USD payment exposure.

Penalties up to USD 10,000 for the first year of non-compliance and up to USD 20,000 per year for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may also strike the entity off the register or impose information-gathering orders.
ESR 2 – Relevant activity declaration Identification of any relevant activity carried on, including distribution and service centre, shipping, financing and leasing, or pure equity holding. Wholesale trading of fertilizer with in-house logistics may constitute a distribution and service centre activity; charter of vessels may constitute shipping activity. Penalties up to USD 10,000 for the first year of non-compliance and up to USD 20,000 per year for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may also strike the entity off the register or impose information-gathering orders.
ESR 3 – Tax residence statement Statement of whether the entity is tax resident outside the Republic of the Marshall Islands and, if so, the jurisdiction and tax reference number. An entity tax resident in a jurisdiction outside the RMI is excluded from the RMI substance test; attach a Certificate of Tax Residency where obtainable. Penalties up to USD 10,000 for the first year of non-compliance and up to USD 20,000 per year for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may also strike the entity off the register or impose information-gathering orders.
ESR 4 – Substance metrics Number of employees in the RMI, physical premises, annual operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities performed in the RMI. If no RMI employees or premises exist, the entity fails the substance test if it claims RMI tax residence; carefully align the declaration with the operating reality. Penalties up to USD 10,000 for the first year of non-compliance and up to USD 20,000 per year for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may also strike the entity off the register or impose information-gathering orders.
ESR 5 – Declaration and signature Authorized officer's declaration as to the completeness and accuracy of the substance metrics. False declarations expose directors to criminal penalties and personal liability. Penalties up to USD 10,000 for the first year of non-compliance and up to USD 20,000 per year for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may also strike the entity off the register or impose information-gathering orders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 — the RMI prescribes no statutory transfer pricing documentation, Local File, Master File, or CbCR obligations; legal tender is the US dollar. Where related-party transactions are booked in the RMI entity, the group must nonetheless be prepared to satisfy the documentation thresholds of the counterparty jurisdiction (e.g., Singapore IRAS transfer pricing documentation at SGD 1,000,000 of related-party loans / SGD 15,000,000 cross-border goods-and-services thresholds) and any CbCR thresholds of the ultimate parent.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended) apply to all RMI tax-resident entities carrying on 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, IP holding, and distribution and service centre). A pure equity-holding entity may use the reduced substance test. An entity tax resident outside the RMI is excluded from the test but must self-declare and substantiate foreign tax residence. For an agrochemical trading business, the principal relevant-activity characterizations are distribution and service centre and, where vessel chartering is carried on, shipping; the entity must demonstrate adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in the RMI (if RMI-resident) or prove tax residence abroad.

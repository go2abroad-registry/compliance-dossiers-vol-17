Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance and Treasury, Republic of the Marshall Islands; Office of the Tax and Revenue Administration, Majuro. No fully consolidated national online tax portal exists; statutory registrations are lodged with the Registrar of Corporations (RMI Association) and border-tax activities are administered by the RMI Customs Service under the Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Business Corporation (BC) established under the RMI Business Corporations Act first obtains a Certificate of Incorporation and, if eligible, a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation status certificate from the Registrar of Corporations. Because the RMI has no VAT and no general corporate income tax for non-resident entities, no separate VAT ID is required. If the BC undertakes import or export activities, it must obtain an RMI Customs importer/exporter code and file a Business License application with the local municipal authority. A TIN is issued by the Tax and Revenue Administration only for persons with RMI-source taxable activities; a Tax-Exempt Non-Resident Domestic Corporation may obtain a TIN on a voluntary basis for customs or banking verification without triggering filing obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Business Corporation (BC) established under the RMI Business Corporations Act first obtains a Certificate of Incorporation and, if eligible, a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation status certificate from the Registrar of Corporations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Check-the-box elections are not part of RMI law. The relevant statutory election is the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation designation under the RMI Business Corporations Act and the Income Tax Act of 1989: a domestic BC wholly owned by non-residents may elect tax-exempt status by filing a Certificate of Non-Resident Status and by demonstrating that it does not derive income from sources inside the Marshall Islands. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized. The entity is treated as tax-transparent only to the extent its shareholders are non-residents and no RMI-source income or permanent establishment is created.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST system. RMI imposes only customs import duties, excise taxes on selected goods (tobacco, alcohol, fuel), and general business licensing fees. The online e-commerce sale of automotive aftermarket parts to foreign customers falls outside the RMI duty net unless goods are physically imported into or exported from RMI territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no value-added tax exists. An RMI Customs importer/exporter registration is required only when the entity undertakes physical importation or exportation of goods through a port of entry in the Marshall Islands; the threshold is nil for any customs-trading entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because RMI does not levy VAT/GST on any supply, including exports. Goods sold by a non-resident BC to overseas customers are outside RMI domestic tax jurisdiction. Under the RMI Customs Ordinance, goods lawfully exported are relieved from any residual customs duty. A foreign supplier located outside RMI is not required to charge, reclaim, or substantiate an RMI output VAT or input VAT credit.

  • RMI Customs export declaration or export manifest (when goods physically leave RMI territory)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Majuro or Ebeye port
  • RMI Customs importer/exporter registration code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt showing receipt of foreign currency proceeds, if any
  • Certificate of Origin only when preferential tariff treatment is claimed in destination country

No electronic fiscal invoicing, real-time clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate is in force. RMI customs processes declarations on paper and selectively through the ASYCUDA World platform. There is no requirement to obtain government approval or fiscal stamp on each commercial invoice.

Because no VAT/GST zero-rating regime exists, there is no VAT refund clawback risk. However, fraudulent customs declarations or false export documents are penalized under the RMI Customs Act by fines of up to USD 10,000 per violation, forfeiture of goods, and prosecution. A non-resident tax-exempt BC that misrepresents its ownership or unlawfully conducts RMI-source business may lose its tax-exempt status and become liable for RMI income tax with up to five years of retroactive assessment and penalties. Failure to maintain adequate corporate records may also result in administrative strike-off by the Registrar of Corporations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible as the Marshall Islands uses the U.S. Dollar as legal tender. There is no exchange control legislation, no central bank exchange rate policy, and no restriction on inbound or outbound transfers. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are permitted without administrative approval or withholding tax for non-resident entities, provided the entity remains in good standing with the Registrar and satisfies banking due diligence requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. The Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no surrender requirement, no repatriation deadline, and no mandated conversion of export earnings into any domestic currency. Export proceeds may be retained in U.S. Dollar accounts domestically or offshore at the discretion of the corporate treasury, subject only to the bank's own anti-money laundering and know-your-customer policies.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands does not maintain a central bank or independent foreign exchange authority; monetary affairs are effectively governed by the U.S. Federal Reserve System through the nation's adoption of the U.S. Dollar as its sole legal tender. Financial sector supervision is vested in the Marshall Islands Banking Commission within the Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Moderate. The Marshall Islands has no licensed electronic money institution (EMI) regime and does not issue banking licenses to fully digital fintech entities. Cross-border digital EMIs with headquarters in other jurisdictions may onboard the company remotely, but they generally face enhanced scrutiny due to the reputation of offshore corporate registries and the lack of a domestic banking resolution framework. For regulated merchant payment processing, Stripe and PayPal are functionally viable non-bank payment facilitators; they settle in U.S. Dollars and require a verified local bank account or business entity documentation, including passports, proof of address, and a corporate registry good-standing certificate. Because Stripe and PayPal are not licensed as banks in the Marshall Islands, they cannot serve as primary deposit-taking treasury rails and must be paired with a domestic or regional U.S. correspondent commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Marshall Islands prescribes no domestic Transfer Pricing Local File/Master File threshold in USD. As a tax-exempt non-resident BC, no domestic TP documentation is required. However, if the BC carries on business in an onshore jurisdiction through a permanent establishment, the TP documentation thresholds of that market jurisdiction will apply to the attributable profits.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to relevant activities including distribution and service centers, holding companies, financing, leasing, and intellectual property. A non-resident BC engaged in online retail falls outside ESR if it has no management or physical presence in the Marshall Islands. If the company is deemed tax resident by another jurisdiction or has substantive board/management in the Marshall Islands, it must satisfy the economic substance test, including adequate physical presence, employees, and expenditure proportionate to the relevant activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion in Market Jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties in the market jurisdiction (e.g., U.S. IRC Section 7201: up to USD 250,000 fine and five years' imprisonment); offshore penalty up to 40% of underpayment; civil fraud penalty 75% of the understatement.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and pursue directors and managers for conspiracy, fraudulent conveyance, and failure to comply with foreign tax orders; travel bans and extradition may follow for willful evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments, deemed profit recharacterization, secondary adjustments with interest, and penalties in the onshore jurisdiction (typically 20–40% of the adjusted tax, plus interest).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements are exposed to alternative assessment procedures, restitution orders, and personal civil liability for unlawful distributions under the Business Corporations Act; criminal liability for fraudulent tax returns in the market jurisdiction is also possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under OECD/G20 BEPS Action 5 and EU defensive measures, information exchange and spontaneous exchange of rulings may occur; the entity may be placed on a blacklist, resulting in withholding taxes, denied treaty benefits, and potential administrative dissolution in the Marshall Islands for failure to file the annual non-resident declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be stripped of limited liability for failing to maintain separate corporate records, intermingling funds, or allowing the company to act as the alter ego of the beneficial owner; travel restrictions and director disqualification orders may be issued.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering Regulations and Registrar sanctions: fines up to USD 150,000, suspension of the registered agent license, freezing orders, and dissolution of the entity; denial of banking and payment processing access.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are subject to personal criminal liability for money laundering offenses up to fourteen years' imprisonment; assets can be forfeited; individuals may face travel bans and international Interpol alerts.
Permanent Establishment Created by Authorized Agent / Warehouse

Statutory sanction: If the foreign jurisdiction attributes permanent establishment profit, the BC becomes taxable on the attributable income; non-compliance triggers estimated assessment, penalties (e.g., 20–30% of unpaid tax), and interest; customs duties and VAT may be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the foreign storage arrangement without local tax registration may be held personally accountable for unpaid withholding taxes and VAT under senior accounting officer rules in certain jurisdictions; in extreme cases, criminal prosecution for failure to notify the tax authority may occur.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Tax and Revenue Office (Office of Revenue and Taxation), Republic of the Marshall Islands; customs administration by the RMI Customs Service. No national electronic filing portal exists; registrations and returns are paper-based at the Ministry of Finance, Majuro. Corporate entity registration is administered by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no RMI-source revenue is outside the RMI territorial tax base and is not required to register with the Tax and Revenue Office or obtain a TIN. If the BC begins conducting business within the RMI (i.e., gross revenue sourced in the RMI exceeding USD 10,000 per annum), it must: (1) obtain a Business License from the Ministry of Finance under the Business License Act; (2) register for the 3% Gross Revenue Tax and receive an RMI taxpayer account number (TIN); (3) file and pay Gross Revenue Tax returns as prescribed by the Tax and Revenue Office. No VAT ID exists because the RMI has no VAT. Foreign entities are treated identically to resident entities for licensing and GRT purposes; no separate branch TIN is issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone: the RMI applies a pure territorial/source-based regime with no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no worldwide taxation. A non-resident Business Corporation transacting exclusively in offshore sales (e.g., e-commerce of pet supplies to foreign customers) has no RMI taxable nexus and no filing obligation. The 3% Gross Revenue Tax (Title 54, Chapter 3, MIRC) applies only to gross revenue derived from business carried on within the RMI, subject to the USD 10,000 annual threshold. — A non-resident Business Corporation (BC) deriving no RMI-source revenue is outside the RMI territorial tax base and is not required to register with the Tax and Revenue Office or obtain a TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the RMI applies a pure territorial/source-based regime with no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no worldwide taxation. A non-resident Business Corporation transacting exclusively in offshore sales (e.g., e-commerce of pet supplies to foreign customers) has no RMI taxable nexus and no filing obligation. The 3% Gross Revenue Tax (Title 54, Chapter 3, MIRC) applies only to gross revenue derived from business carried on within the RMI, subject to the USD 10,000 annual threshold.

Available election forms

None. The RMI has no check-the-box classification election, no entity-level vs. pass-through election, no hybrid-mismatch rules, and no group or consolidated filing election. Entity classification is fixed by the Business Corporations Act: a BC is a limited-liability corporation taxable at entity level solely on RMI-source gross revenue. No CFC, thin-capitalization, or transfer-pricing legislation applies to non-resident BCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil indirect tax system: no VAT, no GST, and no sales-and-use tax is levied at the national level. The only indirect levy is RMI customs import duty on physical goods entering the RMI, which does not apply to exports or offshore e-commerce transactions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no indirect-tax registration threshold exists. For context, the 3% Gross Revenue Tax applies only to RMI-source gross revenue exceeding USD 10,000 per annum; no registration is required below that threshold or for purely offshore revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — with no VAT/GST, there is no export zero-rating mechanism and no refund regime to claim. Offshore sales of pet supplies by the non-resident BC fall outside the RMI gross revenue tax base by operation of the territorial source rules (Title 54, MIRC); no export certificate, zero-rating approval, or refund application is required.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration — required by the RMI Customs Service only when goods physically depart RMI territory; shipping from a foreign fulfillment port does not trigger any RMI customs filing
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — commercial evidence of export movement (banking and logistics practice, not an RMI tax statute)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — commercial documentation supporting the offshore sale and payment rail settlement (Stripe/PayPal)

None. There is no e-invoicing obligation, no real-time fiscal clearance, no government API integration, and no digital stamping requirement for invoices, export documents, or retail receipts.

No export zero-rating or refund clawback regime exists because no indirect tax is levied. Audit exposure is limited to the 3% Gross Revenue Tax: if the Tax and Revenue Office determines the BC derived RMI-source gross revenue above the USD 10,000 threshold and failed to register or file, it may assess the tax due plus statutory interest and penalties for late or non-filing, and the Ministry may suspend or revoke the business license. The 180-day figure is a dossier-standard benchmark only; RMI law imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, exchange-control restriction, or related penalty. Tax evasion is subject to criminal prosecution under Title 54, MIRC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The RMI operates a fully dollarized, open-currency regime with no exchange controls, no capital-account restrictions, and no statutory restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders. Current-account and capital-account transactions are freely permitted without central-bank approval or registration. Practical hurdles are limited to correspondent-bank KYC/AML screening, OFAC/sanctions vetting, and the absence of an in-country interbank FX market, which obliges entities to execute foreign-exchange conversions through U.S. or E.U. correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The RMI imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export receipts. Because the domestic currency is the U.S. dollar, USD export proceeds are not subject to surrender or conversion; foreign-currency receipts may be retained, converted at commercial banks abroad, or held offshore at the exporter's discretion. No central-bank or regulatory reporting obligation applies to this entity, although remitting and receiving banks will apply standard source-of-funds and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI). The RMI maintains no central bank; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, making the U.S. Federal Reserve the de facto monetary authority for currency issuance and settlement.

Stripe Fit: High

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's prime bank to the RMI entity's USD capital or operating account (BOMI, Bank of Guam, or an EMI virtual USD account), with payment purpose coded as 'Contribution to Capital / Capital Injection' and a supporting board resolution attached when the wire exceeds USD 250,000. The RMI imposes no exchange-control approval, reporting, or conversion requirement; the main compliance gate is the remitting and receiving banks' source-of-funds verification. For crypto-derived funding, a fiat off-ramp through the shareholder's bank is executed before the corporate wire to avoid AML holds.Outbound payments use: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for supplier payments, freight, customs duties, and marketing expenses via the USD account's U.S. or E.U. correspondent; (2) irrevocable at-sight Commercial Letters of Credit through a confirming bank for bulk pet-product inventory purchases from Asian manufacturers; (3) ACH/SEPA for low-value operating costs; and (4) Stripe/PayPal settlement payouts credited to the USD treasury account on the processor's standard cycles, with EUR/GBP balances converted at the processor's designated rate or through the treasury bank's spot desk. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident Business Corporation (BC). The Marshall Islands Income Tax Act 1989 imposes a 3% corporate rate only on income derived from sources within the Marshall Islands by resident or PE-connected entities; a non-resident BC earning wholly foreign e-commerce income has a zero national income tax base.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. The RMI imposes no corporate income tax and no dividend withholding tax on distributions by a non-resident BC, regardless of the shareholder's residence or nationality.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None (USD). The RMI has enacted no transfer-pricing legislation, no Local File or Master File regime, no Country-by-Country reporting, and no contemporaneous documentation threshold. Transfer-pricing exposure arises only under the shareholder's home jurisdiction (e.g., US IRC s.482 or Singapore TP rules) where the BC transacts with related parties at non-arm's-length prices; the RMI will not request TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: The RMI applies OECD/EU-informed Economic Substance Regulations only to relevant entities conducting specified activities (banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, holding of shares/IP) and deriving income from those activities in the RMI. A non-resident BC operating an e-commerce retail platform for pet supplies and pet-care devices is outside the specified-activity scope and faces no economic substance filing obligation. However, if the BC later claims RMI tax residence, obtains an RMI license, or is treated as a resident for treaty purposes, an annual economic substance return and demonstrable RMI substance would become mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Income Tax Act 1989, willful evasion is a felony punishable by a fine of up to US$5,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years. The material exposure is extraterritorial: the beneficial owner's domicile may impose civil tax fraud penalties of up to 75% of understated tax, criminal prosecution, and freezing of payment-processor accounts.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false tax-status declaration is personally liable for fraud under the BCA and general corporate law. Home-country courts pierce the corporate veil upon a finding of alter ego or sham, imposing civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and potentially an international red notice.
Shell Company / Sham Transactional Structure

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may judicially dissolve the company for material misrepresentation in filings. Internationally, the entity is re-characterized as a sham, invalidating the limited liability shield and exposing the entire e-commerce revenue stream to the beneficial owner's tax assessment; payment processors may terminate merchant accounts permanently.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing exposes shareholders and directors to joint and several liability for all corporate debts and foreign tax arrears. Directors additionally face disqualification orders, asset confiscation, and travel restrictions in connection with creditor and tax authority claims.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Violation

Statutory sanction: The RMI Proceeds of Crime and AML legislation criminalizes money laundering with fines of up to US$500,000 and/or imprisonment up to ten years, plus confiscation of the proceeds. The RMI Financial Intelligence Unit may also impose daily fines for late beneficial-ownership updates.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate laundering face criminal indictment, personal asset seizure, and inclusion on the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit watch list. Foreign AML convictions trigger mutual legal assistance requests, extradition proceedings, and travel bans across FATF member states.
Annual Filing Default and Corporate Striking-Off

Statutory sanction: Imposition of late penalties, automatic administrative dissolution after 90 days of default, and permanent loss of transactional capacity; reinstatement is discretionary and requires payment of all arrears, penalties, and reinstatement fees.

Director personal liability: Once struck off, directors acting on behalf of the dissolved entity are personally liable for all obligations incurred, including supplier debts and customer refund liabilities arising from pet-product sales; creditor applications may trigger court-ordered liquidation and director examination under oath.

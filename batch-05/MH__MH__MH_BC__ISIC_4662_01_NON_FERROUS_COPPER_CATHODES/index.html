Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO) within the Ministry of Finance of the Republic of the Marshall Islands; official national e-services portal is accessible through https://www.rmi.gov.mh and the Ministry of Finance portal. Separate customs and cross-border administration is under the RMI Customs Service.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation engaged exclusively in offshore wholesale copper cathode trading is not required to obtain an RMI Value Added Tax or GST identification number because the Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT/GST system. For incidental local statutory events, the following procedure applies: (i) file a Business Privilege Tax (BPT) registration application only if the corporation derives gross revenue from business transacted inside RMI territorial jurisdiction; (ii) obtain a resident agent for corporate and service-of-process purposes under the Business Corporations Act; (iii) register with the RMI Revenue and Taxation Office on Form T-001 to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) if any RMI-source payroll, import tax, or social security contribution obligation arises; and (iv) keep on record the non-resident corporation election, certified articles of incorporation, registered office address, director identification, and annual corporate renewal receipt. No VAT ID exists in RMI.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Business Corporation engaged exclusively in offshore wholesale copper cathode trading is not required to obtain an RMI Value Added Tax or GST identification number because the Republic of the Marshall Islands does not operate a VAT/GST system. For incidental local statutory events, the following procedure applies: (i) file a Business Privilege Tax (BPT) registration application only if the corporation derives gross revenue from business transacted inside RMI territorial jurisdiction; (ii) obtain a resident agent for corporate and service-of-process purposes under the Business Corporations Act; (iii) register with the RMI Revenue and Taxation Office on Form T-001 to obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) if any RMI-source payroll, import tax, or social security contribution obligation arises; and (iv) keep on record the non-resident corporation election, certified articles of incorporation, registered office address, director identification, and annual corporate renewal receipt. No VAT ID exists in RMI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Business Corporations Act permits a foreign-owned corporation to be organized as a non-resident domestic business corporation. This status is elected at incorporation by a board resolution and by a statement in the articles of incorporation or annual corporation renewal statement. When 95% or more of gross income is from offshore sources and no goods are sold in RMI, the corporation is statutorily exempt from RMI Business Privilege Tax, income tax, and gross revenue taxation. RMI does not provide check-the-box pass-through elections or OECD hybrid mismatch elections; all corporations are treated as separate taxable persons unless the non-resident domestic corporation exemption applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST. Republic of the Marshall Islands levies a Business Privilege Tax (BPT) on gross revenue of businesses engaged in business inside RMI, and an Import Tax (customs duty) on physical importation of goods into RMI. Neither applies to a non-resident offshore copper trading business.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST because no VAT/GST regime exists. BPT registration threshold is first dollar of gross revenue derived from business activities physically or commercially taking place in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; there is no de minimis threshold for non-resident offshore trading.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because RMI imposes no VAT or GST, export of physical copper cathodes is outside the scope of any RMI indirect tax; the concept of an export zero-rating claim is effectively automatic by territorial scope. To safeguard the tax-exempt status, the taxpayer must demonstrate that title, risk, and control of the goods pass outside RMI territorial waters, the sale is priced and settled in USD through documentary letters of credit or LME warrant transfer, and no goods are sold into RMI customs territory. If copper cathodes are physically imported into RMI solely for temporary warehouse and re-export within one year, an RMI Customs duty drawback can be claimed; otherwise the export will not qualify as wholly outside RMI.

  • Customs Export Declaration or Re-export Declaration processed through RMI Customs Service
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List evidencing refined copper cathodes under HS code 7403.11 and ISIC 4662
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent title document showing foreign port-to-port movement and port of destination
  • LME Warrant transfer certificate and warehouse receipt confirming physical warehouse transfer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or documentary letter of credit settlement advice from the confirming bank
  • Non-resident corporation tax-exempt status certificate issued by the RMI Revenue and Taxation Office, if required
  • Certificate of Origin (optional but standard for trade finance purposes)

No mandatory electronic tax invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping regime is in force in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. RMI Customs Service processes export and import declarations electronically through an automated manifest and declaration system, but there is no fiscal control system for commercial invoices. Corporate records and supporting commercial contracts are retained in physical or digital form for audit by RTO.

The Revenue and Taxation Office and RMI Customs Service conduct joint audits of taxpayers claiming offshore tax-exempt gross revenue. Where export documentation is insufficient or falsely prepared, the business corporation is subject to retroactive Business Privilege Tax at the applicable 3% rate on all gross receipts within the look-back period, statutory interest of 2% per month, and a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided if fraud or willful misrepresentation is established. Intentional false export declarations or fraudulent LME warrant transfers expose the entity and responsible officers to criminal fines under RMI Customs and Tax Administration law, imprisonment of up to five years, cancellation of the non-resident corporation status, deregistration of the entity, and clawback of any refunds claimed, plus government legal costs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. Because RMI is 100% dollarized and has no national currency, there are no exchange controls, no capital-account approval requirements, and no profit-remittance hurdles; dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be expatriated freely. RMI withholding tax applies only to income sourced within RMI, which a non-resident Business Corporation engaged in offshore copper physical arbitrage will not generate. The practical constraint is not convertibility but correspondent-bank enhanced due diligence (EDD), because every USD transfer is cleared through U.S. correspondent banks subject to OFAC sanctions screening and FATF-derived risk assessments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI law imposes no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or trading proceeds. A non-resident Business Corporation may retain 100% of gross copper-trading revenues in USD in any licensed financial intermediary, provided it complies with the 2017 AML/CFT Act's record-keeping obligations (minimum five years), customer due diligence, and suspicious transaction reporting duties.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI; ISO 3166-1 alpha-3: MHL) is fully dollarized: the United States dollar (USD) is the sole legal tender, and the U.S. Federal Reserve is the de facto monetary authority under the Compact of Free Association. RMI maintains no central bank, no national currency, and no exchange-rate or reserves policy. Banking and AML/CFT supervision is vested in the Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, under the Banking Act 1992, as amended by the Banking (Amendment) Act 2018, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2017, as amended in 2021. The Banking Commissioner also heads the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit; in practice, all USD payment flows are additionally screened by U.S. correspondent clearing banks under OFAC, Fedwire, and CHIPS rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited-to-moderate, and only as a secondary rail. Reputable EMIs authorized in Hong Kong, Singapore, the EU, or the U.K. may open USD accounts for an RMI Business Corporation, but most decline RMI entities because of the jurisdiction's prior FATF grey-listing, bearer-share history, and inability to verify UBOs in person. Legally, RMI's Banking Act 1992 prohibits unlicensed deposit-taking within RMI, so an offshore EMI must remain licensed in its home jurisdiction and must not hold itself out as an RMI bank; no RMI license is available to foreign EMIs. Recommended posture: use a tier-1 international bank in New York, Hong Kong, or Singapore as the primary clearing bank and a compliant EMI only for small contingency disbursements; never rely on EMIs for documentary L/C issuance, LME warrant settlement, or wires above USD 1 million.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All injections flow in USD through U.S. clearing: the remitter sends a SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV (cover payment) to the BC's account at its designated bank using the bank's Fedwire ABA routing and CHIPS membership. The payment narrative must be transparent, e.g., 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO MH_BC' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN FOR COPPER TRADE WORKING CAPITAL', and must match the board resolution, subscription ledger, and share register. No RMI central bank approval or capital-control permit is required; the onboarding bank applies its own threshold-based EDD (typically enhanced review above USD 100,000 and full EDD above USD 1,000,000). Prior to the first wire, the bank requires the executed account mandate, board resolution, UBO register, and source-of-funds statement, and each transfer is screened against OFAC lists in real time through U.S. correspondents.Three clearing rails are recommended for physical copper-cathode arbitrage. (1) Direct USD transfers for supplier prepayments, freight, storage, and insurance: SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV, each carrying a UETR and bank reference, with the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and LME warrant serial numbers attached to the payment instruction. (2) Documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 from a top-tier confirming bank, payable against presentation of commercial invoice, full set of ocean bills of lading, certificate of origin, quality and assay certificate, and LME warrant. The L/C must describe 'Grade A refined copper cathode' with LME brand registration and an LMAA arbitration dispute clause. (3) LME warrant transfers: match warrant serial numbers electronically via the LME member, then settle with a matching MT103 against the transferred warrant. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No corporate income tax return is required. The sole annual corporate filing obligation is the Annual Report, due on or before the last day of the month in which the anniversary of incorporation falls; payment of the US$ 100 annual franchise tax accompanies the filing. No taxable profits are computed in the Marshall Islands.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local transfer pricing documentation obligation applies. The Marshall Islands imposes no corporate income tax, and no Local File or Master File threshold has been enacted. In US dollars (USD), the threshold is functionally not applicable.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands has adopted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) that apply to resident entities engaged in geographically mobile activities. A non-resident domestic corporation trading in copper cathodes will generally fall outside the ESR provided it has no Marshall Islands tax residence, no physical management or control in the Marshall Islands, and no local office. In the shareholder's home jurisdiction, however, the separate corporate personality will be denied if the vehicle does not maintain real economic substance; the corporation should therefore maintain bank statements, board minutes, commodity trading records, warehouse warrant evidence, and letter-of-credit documentation at a physical or custodial location outside the Marshall Islands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code and the Business Corporations Act, making or procuring a false statement in any corporate filing is an offence punishable by fine and imprisonment. Financial penalties in the Marshall Islands may reach US$ 10,000 per offence, and the residence jurisdiction of the tax evader will impose additional substantial tax penalties and potential criminal custody.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly approve false filings or backdated copper sale invoices are individually liable. Courts may lift the corporate veil and enter restitution orders; foreign tax authorities may obtain international freezing orders, travel bans, and extradition requests via Marshall Islands mutual legal assistance channels.
Shell Company and Impaired Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: If the entity is classified as resident or is subject to the ESR, failure to satisfy the economic substance test triggers first a statement of reasonable excuse, then financial penalties of US$ 10,000, plus daily default penalties of US$ 1,000 for ongoing non-compliance. The Registrar can ultimately strike the company off the registry and appoint a receiver to wind up its affairs.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or permit the shell operation to continue are jointly and severally liable for the daily penalties, are subject to director disqualification orders in the Marshall Islands, and may be held personally liable by the principal trading counterparty for misrepresentation in copper contracts.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 2017 imposes penalties for failing to carry out customer due diligence, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting. A body corporate is liable to a fine of up to US$ 250,000; individuals are liable to imprisonment up to 10 years for wilful involvement. U.S. OFAC consequences can include asset freezes and the designation of the Business Corporation as a blocked entity if the copper trade involves Iranian or North Korean end-users.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file suspicious activity reports or who provide false declarations to banks are personally criminally liable. Travel bans may be issued under international INTERPOL red notices; personal bank accounts can be frozen as proceeds of unlawful conduct.
Failure to Maintain Books and Records

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act, every non-resident domestic corporation must maintain adequate financial records. Non-compliance is an offence punishable by a fine of US$ 500 for each day the failure continues and may constitute grounds for the Registrar to dissolve the company; a dissolved company cannot enforce any LME warrant or letter-of-credit rights.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are fiduciaries bound to preserve corporate books. Wilful destruction of records exposes each director to personal fines and restitution in favor of creditors; the court may also order a director disqualification order of up to five years.

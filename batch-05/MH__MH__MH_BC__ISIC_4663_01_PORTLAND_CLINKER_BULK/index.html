Governing statute

Business Corporations Act (BCA), Republic of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (MIRC) Title 27, Chapter 1, as amended; key provisions governing Articles of Incorporation, corporate name, registered office and registered agent, issuing shares, directors, officers, Bylaws, and corporate existence.

Formation / constitutional filings

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands — Revenue and Taxation Division (direct taxes) and Customs Division (import/export duties and excise); official government portal: www.rmigovernment.org; no online tax filing portal is operated, and registrations are accepted in paper form at the Treasury in Majuro or via written submission to the Division.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Business Corporations Act 1990 by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations and appointing a licensed registered agent in the Republic; no minimum capital is required. Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Good Standing; non-resident domestic corporation status is evidenced by the Articles of Incorporation and by not carrying on business in the Republic. Step 3: No statutory TIN or VAT ID is issued to a tax-exempt non-resident domestic corporation. Step 4: If the entity hires RMI employees, it must register with the RMI Social Security Administration for payroll; if it conducts RMI domestic business, it must register with the Revenue and Taxation Division for Gross Revenue Tax. Step 5: For bulk ocean exports of portland cement clinker, obtain an RMI Customs exporter account code from the Customs Division for submission of export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a non-resident domestic Business Corporation is statutorily exempt from all Republic taxes (income, gross revenue, property, withholding, and capital gains) under the Business Corporations Act 1990, provided it does not carry on business within the Republic and derives no income from sources within the Republic.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under RMI law. The tax-exempt non-resident classification is automatic and nondiscretionary, determined by the corporation's certificate of incorporation and its actual conduct. A vessel-owning subsidiary may separately register under the RMI Maritime Act as a foreign maritime entity; that maritime registration does not alter or constitute an election with respect to tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax regime exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the only indirect levies are Customs Import Duties and Excise Taxes under the Customs Tariff and Taxation Act, together with the 3% Gross Revenue Tax, which is a direct tax applied only to RMI-source gross revenue of domestic registrants.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not Applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. An exporter or importer must hold a Customs account regardless of turnover; the Gross Revenue Tax applies at 3% to RMI-source gross revenue with no de minimis registration threshold once a domestic business is conducted within the Republic.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not Applicable — absent a VAT regime, there is no zero-rating or VAT refund mechanism in the RMI. Bulk portland cement clinker exported from the Republic bears no export duty, gross revenue tax, or indirect tax, provided the transaction is booked through a non-resident domestic corporation and the income is derived from sources outside the Republic. Customs drawback is available solely for the re-export of previously imported dutiable goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs Division export entry)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean freight movement of bulk clinker)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued remittance advice or SWIFT MT103 evidencing USD settlement; retained for audit trail — RMI imposes no foreign exchange controls)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the importing jurisdiction)

None in force — the RMI does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax reporting, or digital stamping. Export and import clearance is processed by the Customs Division through paper-based declarations, with optional electronic manifest filing; no national e-invoicing platform has been implemented.

The Revenue and Taxation Division and the Customs Division may audit any exporter for up to five years. Fraudulent claims to non-resident tax-exempt status, false export declarations, or failure to register RMI-source business trigger retrospective assessment of the 3% Gross Revenue Tax plus interest at 1% per month, civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax or duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and criminal fines and imprisonment under the Income Tax Act 1989 and the Customs Tariff and Taxation Act.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI maintains full dollarization; USD is legal tender, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible. There is no foreign exchange control statute, no central bank exchange-rate restriction, and no capital authorization regime. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and investment repatriations may be made freely in USD or any other convertible currency, subject only to normal anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (AML/CFT) requirements imposed on Financial Institutions under the Banking Act and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act. Potential practical hurdles are not regulatory but banking-channel driven, such as correspondent-bank de-risking or non-resident entity enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. RMI imposes no mandatory surrender timeline, no retention quota, and no conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds from cement clinker wholesale and bulk ocean-freight contracts may be retained in USD, held in foreign-currency accounts where available, or deployed directly for trade settlement without any central-bank reporting or exchange-control filing.

Central bank supervision: Office of the Banking Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI acts as the de facto banking and financial supervision authority; no conventional central bank exists). Monetary policy is effectively delegated to the United States Federal Reserve System because the official legal tender of RMI is the United States Dollar (USD).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to strict conditionality. RMI does not maintain a separate electronic money institution (EMI) or payment-service licensing regime; deposit-taking is reserved for banks licensed under the Banking Act. A foreign-owned or offshore digital EMI may provide cross-border payment transparency, USD clearing via overseas banking partners, and rapid treasury execution for a non-resident business corporation, but it cannot replace a domestic bank relationship for RMI-regulated transactions. Legal constraints include lack of direct statutory recognition, correspondent-bank restrictions, and AML/CFT scrutiny. Therefore, use a regulated EMI with segregated client accounts, a verified banking license in a credible jurisdiction, and a documented correspondent chain that terminates in obtainable USD clearing.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No corporate income tax return deadline applies. The annual corporate renewal and licence fee is statutorily due by March 31 following the calendar year of incorporation or renewal.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
N/A - No CIT return No taxable income, deduction, or credit fields exist for a non-resident BC in the Marshall Islands tax system. Non-resident entities should maintain separate accounting records in their home jurisdiction and do not complete any Marshall Islands tax schedule; all revenue and expense data remains outside the RMI tax perimeter. There is no CIT late-filing penalty because no return exists. Failure to submit the annual renewal by the statutory deadline triggers a late filing penalty of USD 100 to USD 250 in addition to reinstatement fees, and continued non-renewal results in administrative dissolution or strike-off of the Business Corporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory transfer pricing documentation threshold is established in Marshall Islands law. The RMI does not impose corporate income tax, so no local file or master file obligation arises under domestic statute.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations apply to resident Business Corporations engaged in relevant activities such as distribution and service centres. A non-resident BC managing and controlling its business outside the RMI is outside the scope of the economic substance test. However, if any facts indicate RMI tax residence or CFC-like management from the RMI, the company must demonstrate substantial economic presence in RMI for its relevant activities, including physical premises, employees, and core income-generating decisions within the jurisdiction. Wholesale bulk cement distribution through ocean freight must be carefully structured to avoid accidental RMI management or control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Although the RMI does not impose corporate tax on non-resident BCs, fraudulent misuse of the registration to evade tax in another sovereign state incurs criminal penalties under the RMI Criminal Code and foreign anti-tax-evasion statutes; the offending entity is subject to fines up to USD 10,000 and forfeiture of vessel or cargo proceeds in the RMI, plus all applicable penalties and interest imposed by the foreign tax authority.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners who approve masking transactions are personally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes and interest. In egregious cases, personal criminal liability extends to imprisonment for up to 3 years in the Marshall Islands and possible foreign extradition; the RMI court can issue asset freezing orders, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a director or officer of any RMI-registered entity.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Corporations may strike off the Business Corporation, revoke its Certificate of Good Standing, and impose a fine of up to USD 5,000. Participation in sham transactions can also expose the entity to disqualification under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act and blacklisting by foreign financial institutions under correspondent banking due-diligence rules.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the BC is used as a pure façade; directors and shareholders become jointly and severally liable for all contractual debts, bank liabilities, and shipping charter obligations. The RMI High Court may grant a worldwide freezing order against personal assets, impose a travel ban pending liquidation, and, if fraudulent misrepresentation is proven, impose a custodial sentence of up to 5 years.
AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit can impose administrative fines of up to USD 25,000 per violation for failure to comply with AML record-keeping and reporting requirements. The Attorney General may also instigate proceedings to forfeit assets connected to money laundering or terrorism financing, and the banking relationship will be terminated by the facilitative bank under correspondent banking 'de-risking' policies.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly process suspect payments face direct criminal liability for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act, with penalties of up to USD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years. Personal civil liability includes restitution of the laundered amounts, disqualification from holding a bank account in RMI, travel bans at the request of the FIU, and extradition exposure to the requesting foreign jurisdiction.

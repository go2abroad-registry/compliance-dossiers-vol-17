E-government filing portal

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full currency convertibility applies on both the capital and current accounts. The MH Business Corporation may receive, hold, and repatriate USD without central bank permit, exchange-control approval, or reinvestment requirement. Capital account: equity contributions, shareholder loans, and proceeds of liquidation are freely remittable. Current account: e-commerce sales receipts and COD collections may be settled in USD or converted into foreign currency at will. The only material hurdle is documentary: because the RMI is under FATF increased monitoring, remittance channels require a distilled evidence trail (underlying invoice, sales platform report, shareholder resolution) to satisfy foreign correspondent banks. Profit remittance is unrestricted under Marshall Islands law, but the paying institution will apply enhanced due diligence to amounts exceeding USD 50,000 per instruction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines exist under Marshall Islands law. Cross-border e-commerce receipts and mail-order export collections may be retained in USD offshore, converted at market rate, or held in multi-currency accounts without mandatory conversion. The Banking Act 1990 imposes no foreign-currency surrender obligation. In practice, the local bank or fintech EMI will require a commercial invoice or logistics manifest to distinguish trade proceeds from unlawful proceeds, and will monitor aggregate monthly inflows for consistency with the declared COD e-commerce business volume.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not maintain a central bank or a statutory exchange-control authority. The United States dollar is the sole legal tender by virtue of the Compact of Free Association with the United States, and monetary conditions are effectively governed by U.S. Federal Reserve policy. Banking and prudential supervision is exercised by the Financial Institutions Supervisory Division of the Ministry of Finance under the Banking Act 1990, while AML/CFT supervision is vested in the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit pursuant to the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism Act 2012 (34 MIRC). Foreign-exchange rule-making is not separately codified; regulatory constraint arises indirectly through bank licensing conditions, FATF grey-list enhanced due diligence, and correspondent-bank counterparty policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-rated viability with material conditions. Airwallex, as an APAC-regulated non-bank payments institution, can serve as the primary cross-border clearing and FX venue for the MH BC, subject to: (i) production of the Business Corporations Act registration certificate and good-standing extract; (ii) identification of natural-person ultimate beneficial owners with apostilled identity documents; (iii) execution of an enhanced-risk merchant acceptance agreement reflecting the FATF grey-list status of RMI; and (iv) quantitative transaction limits, typically USD 25,000 single-payment and USD 250,000 monthly velocity, expandable on submission of audited sales reports. Legal constraint: no EMI holds an RMI banking licence, so the entity remains a client of an offshore-regulated intermediary rather than a domestic depositor; funds held with the EMI are not protected by RMI deposit insurance or the U.S. FDIC, and the EMI may suspend payouts if RMI's FATF grey-listing persists or if the target emerging-market corridor triggers its own central bank restrictions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-resident domestic Business Corporation (BC); the RMI Income Tax Act imposes tax only on income sourced within the Marshall Islands, and this entity is structured as a non-resident tax-exempt BC. The nominal resident corporate rate on RMI-source taxable income is 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax and no dividend distribution tax on dividends paid by a non-resident BC to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local currency threshold; no RMI Local File or Master File obligation exists for a non-resident tax-exempt BC. If the entity were to derive RMI-source taxable income, source-based assessment would apply and OECD-consistent transfer pricing documentation would be relevant; no statutory monetary threshold is prescribed for non-resident exempt entities.

Economic substance scope: The RMI Economic Substance (Amendment) Act and BCA-related ESR Regulations apply to non-resident domestic corporations that carry on a relevant activity. A COD mail-order/e-commerce business engaged in purchasing, inventory management, order-taking, delivery, and settlement logistics can fall within the 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity and must satisfy core income-generating activity substance requirements: local board presence or management, adequate staff and operating expenditure, and an annual ESR substance report filed through the registered agent. A purely passive non-resident BC with no relevant activity need only maintain the registered office and registered agent. Non-resident BCs that are tax resident in a foreign jurisdiction may rely on foreign tax residence, but a timely, fact-based ESR declaration is still required.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of Non-Resident Exemption

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the 15% corporate income tax on all RMI-source taxable income, plus statutory interest, late-payment penalties, and fraud penalties of up to 50% of the underpayment; criminal conviction for willful tax evasion is punishable by a fine of up to US$10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 3 years per offense.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who authorize false declarations or conceal RMI-source receipts are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and enforcement costs. The corporate veil may be pierced on a showing of sham or fraud, exposing directors to civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off or dissolution by the Registrar; financial penalties under the ESR Regulations for non-compliance, including daily default penalties and increased fines for false substance declarations; loss of legal capacity to enforce contracts or pursue claims while struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for company debts incurred after a default leading to dissolution. In cross-border proceedings, foreign courts may disregard the RMI shell and impose liability against the true economic operator; directors and beneficial owners may face disqualification, asset freezes, and travel bans.
AML Failure / Cash-on-Delivery Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Violation of the RMI Money Laundering (Prevention and Control) Act 2011, as amended: criminal fines of up to US$100,000 per offense, imprisonment of up to 10 years, confiscation of proceeds of crime, and civil penalties for non-compliance with registered-agent and AML record-keeping duties.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominee directors, and compliance officers who knowingly facilitate or remain willfully blind to COD-settlement laundering are personally criminally liable; the corporate veil does not protect deliberate AML violations. Enforcement measures include civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and extradition or prosecution under international cooperation arrangements.
False Registry Filings and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: False statement offenses under the Business Corporations Act: fines of up to US$5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 6 months; cancellation of good standing; monetary penalties for late or incomplete beneficial-ownership disclosure to the registered agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve a false filing are personally liable for damages suffered by creditors or third parties relying on the registry record. Disqualification from serving as a director and travel bans may be imposed in the context of cross-border insolvency or AML investigations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands (Treasury, Revenue and Customs divisions); official government portal: https://www.rmi.gov.mh. Customs administration: Marshall Islands Customs and Excise Service. Corporate registry: Office of the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act 1990 (filings through licensed registered agents; no public self-service e-TIN portal).

E-government filing portal

The RMI levies no general income tax and no VAT, so there is no statutory TIN or VAT-ID issuance for foreign entities. The operational sequence for an offshore towage/salvage Business Corporation is: (1) incorporate under the Business Corporations Act 1990 through an RMI-licensed registered agent and obtain the Certificate of Registration and corporate registration number; (2) adopt the non-resident domestic corporation status by board resolution confirming that all towage/salvage operations are conducted outside the RMI and file the status with the Registrar; (3) do not apply for a fiscal TIN - none is required or issued for non-resident entities; (4) if the vessel calls at RMI ports or imports duty-free stores, register with the Marshall Islands Customs and Excise Service for a customs importer/exporter reference code; (5) if the operator employs RMI-resident crew or maintains an RMI shore office, register with the RMI Social Security Administration for an employer number and with the municipal government for a business license. A pure foreign-going, RMI-flagged tug operator completes steps (1) and (2) only and pays the annual non-resident registration fee of approximately USD 500.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, passthrough election, or hybrid-mismatch election exists under RMI tax law because the RMI has no income tax, no branch profits tax, and no CFC/anti-hybrid regime. The only relevant statutory designation is the 'Non-Resident Domestic Corporation' status available under the Business Corporations Act 1990: a corporation that resolves to carry on no business within the Republic except activities incidental to its offshore status is excluded from all RMI taxes, duties (other than the annual registration fee), and municipal business license requirements. An existing domestic corporation may elect non-resident status by filing an amendment to its articles and registered-agent certificate; the status lapses if the corporation resumes physical business in RMI, in which case the tax exemption is prospectively and, on audit, retrospectively lost. Foreign maritime entities operating RMI-flagged tugs may alternatively register under the Maritime Act, but that registration is a flag/concession mechanism, not a tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no VAT, GST, or national sales-and-use tax; indirect fiscal measures are limited to customs/excise import duties, municipal business license fees, and social security contributions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST regime and therefore no economic-turnover registration threshold. From a customs perspective, any importer/exporter must be representable by a licensed customs agent or hold an importer/exporter reference code under the Customs and Excise Act, irrespective of turnover. If the operator carries on physical business inside the RMI (e.g., in-port or coastal towage, crew agency, shore office), the municipal Business License Act 1989 requires an annual business license, with fees graduated by gross receipts band.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The statutory zero-rating concept does not exist because the RMI has no VAT/GST. In substance, offshore towage and salvage services performed by an RMI non-resident BC (e.g., Lloyd's Open Form, SCOPIC, BIMCO Towcon/Towhire contracts) are outside the RMI tax base: services are deemed rendered abroad, remuneration is not subject to any RMI consumption tax, and no refund mechanism is needed. For goods, export duty-neutrality applies only if (a) goods are declared for export under the Customs and Excise Act, and (b) bunkers, stores, spare parts, and salvage gear on an RMI-flagged tug are covered by an inward/outward ship's stores list for a foreign-going voyage. RMI-flagged tug and barge units engaged in international salvage/towage may also claim customs relief on imported equipment used in the foreign trade where the Director of Customs approves the installation.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the Marshall Islands Customs and Excise Service covering vessel stores, bunkers, spares, and salvage gear
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill for the towed convoy plus the towage/salvage service contract (Lloyd's Open Form/Towcon or Towhire)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT wire advice evidencing settlement of the Lloyds standard form remuneration into the RMI entity's bank account
  • Marshall Islands vessel registry certificate and class certificate for the tug (and towed unit registration where applicable)
  • Ship's stores list signed by the master, together with port clearance, for duty-free outward-bound treatment

None. The RMI has no mandatory e-invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance API, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction controls. Customs declarations (export clearances, stores lists) are lodged with the Marshall Islands Customs and Excise Service on paper or through the available electronic cargo declaration system; this is a border control, not an indirect-tax clearance mandate. No cryptographic invoice authentication is required.

RMI has no VAT refund or export-zero-rating credit, so no statutory tax clawback of refunds can arise. The enforceable exposure is: (1) Customs fraud - false export declarations, false stores lists, or misdescription of salvage equipment expose the operator to forfeiture of the tug/cargo, a fine of up to twice the duty evaded, and an additional penalty not exceeding USD 10,000 under the Customs and Excise Act, with retrospective reassessment of duty on goods not validly exported; (2) Abuse of the non-resident tax-exempt status - if the Director of Customs or the Registrar determines the BC performed business within RMI (in-port towage, RMI crew payroll, local shore office), the exemption is void and municipal business license fees and penalties are assessed retrospectively from commencement, subject to judicial review in the RMI High Court; (3) FX settlement - the 180-day repatriation term is a banking/escrow and AML/CFT covenant rather than an exchange-control law (USD is legal tender and no exchange controls exist); failure to repatriate invoice proceeds within 180 days may trigger escrow penalties, wire-recall liability, and adverse audit findings, and can be treated as an annuality red flag for the enhanced due-diligence tier under the RMI AML/CTF regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is legal tender and circulates freely; both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with no statutory exchange controls, no central bank approval for inward or outward remittances, and no profit remittance restrictions. Dividends, service fees, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad without exchange licensing, subject only to AML/CFT verification and bank-specific documentary review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender obligation exists. There is no statutory timeline for converting export receipts into local currency because the U.S. dollar is the domestic means of exchange, and all salvage/towage revenue retained in USD or held offshore is unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands has no central bank; monetary and financial regulatory functions are administered by the Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance and the Financial Action Taskforce-aligned supervisory framework of the Pacific International Bankers Association.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. The RMI does not maintain a dedicated fintech or electronic money institution licensing regime for non-resident offshore EMIs; non-deposit-taking fintech platforms are not regulated as banks and cannot provide USD settlement infrastructure or Lloyd's Standard Form wire clearing. Any such arrangement must be structured as a pass-through payment agent, not as a licensed EMI, and would face high correspondent-bank friction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold (USD 0); RMI law does not impose Local File or Master File obligations. Group-level CbCR thresholds in other jurisdictions may require the RMI entity to be identified as a constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to registered entities carrying on relevant activities, including shipping. Offshore towage and salvage operations may be treated as a shipping relevant activity for ESR purposes. A non-resident Business Corporation must either demonstrate economic substance in the Marshall Islands by maintaining local management and control, premises, employees, and records proportionate to the activity, or establish tax residence in another qualifying jurisdiction. Annual substance reporting is mandatory; non-compliance triggers financial penalties and deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Exemption Claim

Statutory sanction: False filings to the Registrar are punishable under RMI law; registration may be revoked and the entity struck off. Foreign tax authorities may impose penalties, interest, and deny tax treaty or exemption benefits where the structure is a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false reports or participate in evasion may be held personally liable for civil restitution, disqualified from acting as directors, and subject to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations, the Registrar may impose a financial penalty of up to USD 10,000 plus up to USD 1,000 per day for continuing default, and may strike off or dissolve the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for penalties if willful default is proven; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the entity is used as a mere façade.
AML / Sanctions / Beneficial Ownership Red Flags

Statutory sanction: RMI anti-money laundering laws provide for fines, forfeiture of vessels and proceeds, and imprisonment up to 10 years. The RMI Maritime Administrator may suspend, revoke, or refuse to renew the vessel's flag registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil forfeiture, travel bans, and extradition on money laundering or sanctions evasion charges.
Failure to File Annual Report / Franchise Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Late penalties and daily surcharges accrue; continued non-compliance leads to administrative dissolution or revocation of registration. Reinstatement requires settlement of all unpaid fees, penalties, and reinstatement charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who breach their fiduciary duty by permitting non-filing may be liable to the company for resulting damages, and may be named in reinstatement proceedings before the Registrar.

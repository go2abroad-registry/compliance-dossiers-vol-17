Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Taxation Division, Ministry of Finance (Majuro, RMI) — no national taxpayer online portal exists; corporate registration is administered separately by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Ministry of Commerce and Trade, Majuro.

E-government filing portal

Not applicable. The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no income tax and no VAT/GST on non-resident Business Corporations (BCs). A foreign-owned, non-resident BC does not apply for any RMI TIN or VAT ID. The only statutory identifier is the Corporate Registration Number issued under the Business Corporations Act upon filing of the Articles of Incorporation, Statement of Compliance, and appointment of a licensed RMI resident agent. For banking and payment-rail counterparties (e.g., Stripe, PayPal), the Corporate Registration Number and Certificate of Good Standing serve as the sole economic identifier; no further tax registration procedure is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. RMI domestic law provides no check-the-box, partnership classification, or hybrid mismatch election under the Business Corporations Act or Income Tax Act 1989. Although the RMI Income Tax Act adopts portions of the U.S. Internal Revenue Code for local-source income, the non-resident BC remains categorically exempt from RMI income tax because all income is foreign-source. Any U.S. federal check-the-box election would be a private U.S. tax election only and would neither create nor destroy RMI tax liability.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — the RMI levies no VAT, GST, or general sales/use tax. The only indirect taxes are Customs Import Duties under the Customs Act 1991, excise taxes on imported alcohol and tobacco for domestic consumption, and municipal property/land rental taxes on physical premises; none of these apply to an offshore online retailer of consumer electronics.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no RMI turnover threshold, VAT registration regime, or indirect-tax nexus test. A non-resident BC selling consumer electronics online to customers outside or inside the RMI has no RMI indirect tax registration obligation in any currency amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because the RMI has no VAT or GST, no export zero-rating mechanism, no refund claim process, and no export tax exists. The foreign-source income of the non-resident BC is wholly outside the territorial tax reach of the Income Tax Act 1989; goods exported from RMI customs territory are not subject to any national tax on export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (RMI Customs export documentation) — required only for physical goods departing RMI customs territory; not required for pure online digital retail of consumer electronics
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from origin port to final destination consumer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice (or documented Stripe/PayPal payout ledger) evidencing collection of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List retained in the entity's books solely for corporate audit and financial-accounting purposes, not for any RMI tax filing

No e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists. The RMI has no taxpayer API gateway, digital stamping regime, or B2B clearance system. Commercial invoices and customs manifests are ordinary paper/PDF documents; a licensed customs broker may submit RMI customs entry data via the Trade Information System, but that is trade-statistical reporting, not tax clearance.

No VAT clawback or export zero-rating penalty can apply because no such regime exists. However, fraudulent customs declarations, smuggling, or undervaluation of exported or imported goods are punishable under the Customs Act 1991 by fines up to USD 10,000, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment up to 5 years; misrepresentation to the Registrar of Corporations may lead to corporate dissolution and cancellation of the registration. No retrospective tax clawback exists for the non-resident BC.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMI is a currency-substitution jurisdiction: USD is used domestically and internationally, so there is de facto 100% convertibility with no capital-account or current-account controls. No exchange-control licence or central-bank approval is required for an offshore Business Corporation (BC) to receive equity injection, retain export proceeds, or remit dividends, management fees, interest, royalties or loan repayments. Although non-resident BCs are income-tax-exempt on worldwide non-RMI source income under the RMI Income Tax Act, the practical remittance hurdle is not exchange control but the AML/CDD requirements of the paying and receiving banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. RMI has no foreign-currency surrender statute, no mandatory conversion ratio, no export-USD repatriation deadline and no central accounting of trade receipts. A merchandising BC may legally retain its Stripe/PayPal and B2B settlement proceeds on any USD or foreign-currency account worldwide, subject only to the record-keeping and source-of-funds rules of the financial institution clearing the funds.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no central bank and no foreign-exchange supervisory agency. The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender under the Compact of Free Association; monetary policy is effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve. The RMI Banking Commissioner within the Ministry of Finance, supported by the Financial Intelligence Unit, supervises licensed banks under the Banking Act 1991, the Offshore Banking Act 2005, the AML/CFT (Banking) Regulations and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High — and for this merchant profile it is the recommended first-line treasury route. RMI does not license EMIs or payment institutions, so the BC must open at a foreign-licensed EMI/PSP (for example a UK FCA-authorised EMI or an EU-authorised payment institution offering USD accounts, or US-licensed PSPs such as Payoneer or Airwallex that work with Stripe/PayPal settlement). Legal constraints: (i) funds held at an EMI are client-money safeguarded, not RMI-guaranteed deposits; (ii) the EMI will apply an internal FATF/EU-list screen to RMI corporate vehicles and normally demand proof of economic purpose, supplier contracts, a live storefront and UBO source-of-wealth; (iii) the BC must identify itself as a non-resident e-commerce merchant and not as a securities or crypto treasury; (iv) account limitations may apply to refund-heavy merchants, requiring a 10–15% liquidity buffer; and (v) no RMI regulatory objection exists to this structure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

No corporate income tax filing deadline applies. The registry annual report and annual government fee are due under the Business Corporations Act on the statutory anniversary cycle applicable to the BC. Tax-exempt status remains subject to timely payment of annual registry fees.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
N/A – no CIT form No taxable entry is required because the entity has no Marshall Islands-source income and is exempt under the Business Corporations Act. Retain the certificate of corporate existence and paid annual fee receipts. Do not file a domestic income tax return unless the BC later derives income sourced to the Republic of the Marshall Islands. No late CIT filing penalty applies to an exempt non-resident BC. Failure to file the annual corporate registry report or pay the annual registry fee triggers late-fee penalties, suspension, and possible administrative dissolution by the Registrar; the registered agent normally administers enforcement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 – no statutory transfer pricing threshold applies. The Marshall Islands does not prescribe Local File or Master File obligations for tax-exempt non-resident BCs. Transfer pricing risk is driven by the parent jurisdiction and by legitimate substance requirements, not by RMI domestic tax rules.

Economic substance scope: RMI Economic Substance Regulations apply to relevant activities carried on in the Republic. Online consumer-electronics retail with no RMI branch, no RMI employees, and no RMI distribution centre generally does not constitute an RMI relevant activity. If the BC establishes a fulfilment or distribution centre in RMI, it may qualify as a 'distribution and service centre' and must satisfy board-management, employee, premises, and expenditure tests. Pure tax-haven letter-box structures are contrary to the substance doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company / economic substance misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: RMI Economic Substance Regulations enforcement: financial penalty for failure to file economic substance notification or annual substance report, continuing daily default penalty for persisting non-compliance, and application by the Competent Authority to strike the company from the corporate register.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly approve false substance declarations are jointly and severally liable for penalties, may be disqualified from acting as directors, and may be subject to personal liability for claims by persons who relied on the misrepresentation.
RMI-source tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid RMI income tax plus interest and statutory delinquency penalties; fraudulent underreporting triggers enhanced civil penalties and criminal prosecution for willful evasion, with fines imposed at the discretion of the court and possible imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the evasion or signed false declarations may be personally assessed for corporate underpayments through disregard of corporate formalities and may face criminal imprisonment rather than being protected by limited liability.
Non-arm's length transfer pricing / foreign-parent exposure

Statutory sanction: RMI imposes no direct transfer-pricing adjustment because the BC is tax-exempt; the fatal exposure arises in the shareholder's residence jurisdiction, which may recharacterize the BC, disregard non-arm's length pricing, apply CFC or Subpart F taxation, and impose understatement penalties plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders cannot rely on the corporate veil if the BC is treated as a sham or agency arrangement; those who signed intercompany agreements may become personally liable for foreign-court assessments and may be subject to travel restrictions in enforcing jurisdictions.
AML / payment-rail or bank de-risking

Statutory sanction: Under RMI AML/CFT legislation, assets of an entity used as a money-laundering conduit may be frozen by court order; directors and controllers may be prosecuted for money laundering where a predicate offence is established, with severe custodial penalties and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing is permitted for AML violations; directors who controlled payment accounts face personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and criminal prosecution in RMI and in cooperating foreign jurisdictions.
Misuse of Business Corporations Act no-tax status

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may annul tax-exempt status, order winding up, revoke the certificate of incorporation, or strike the company from the register; false representation of corporate status is an offence under the Business Corporations Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who issue false solvency or tax-exempt certificates are personally liable for losses incurred by third parties who relied on the documents, and may face restitution orders and involuntary dissolution consequences.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance - Revenue and Taxation Division, Majuro; corporate registry and exemption administration by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations, Majuro. Filing is paper-based through the Revenue and Taxation Division; there is no national e-filing portal.

E-government filing portal

A wholly foreign-owned Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the RMI Business Corporation Act is registered as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Act. For purely offshore activities no TIN or VAT ID is required; the only annual compliance obligation is the flat government registration/annual fee remitted to the Registrar of Corporations. A GRT TIN and business license are issued by the Revenue and Taxation Division only if the BC later establishes physical RMI business operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-Resident Domestic Corporation exempt from all RMI national taxes, including income tax, gross revenue tax, and withholding tax on distributions; tax exposure is limited to fixed annual government franchise/registration fees; offshore DG freight-forwarding revenue has no RMI tax nexus. — A wholly foreign-owned Business Corporation (BC) incorporated under the RMI Business Corporation Act is registered as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Act. For purely offshore activities no TIN or VAT ID is required; the only annual compliance obligation is the flat government registration/annual fee remitted to the Registrar of Corporations. A GRT TIN and business license are issued by the Revenue and Taxation Division only if the BC later establishes physical RMI business operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Non-Resident Domestic Corporation exempt from all RMI national taxes, including income tax, gross revenue tax, and withholding tax on distributions; tax exposure is limited to fixed annual government franchise/registration fees; offshore DG freight-forwarding revenue has no RMI tax nexus.

Available election forms

Election to be treated as a Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) under the Non-Resident Domestic Corporation Act, perfected by designation through the Office of the Registrar of Corporations; alternatively, non-resident status may be declared in the Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporation Act. RMI domestic law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections, CFC regimes, or hybrid mismatch elections; exemption operates by statutory non-resident status rather than entity classification polling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. RMI indirect taxation consists solely of (i) import duty under the RMI Customs Tariff Act triggered by physical importation into RMI customs territory, and (ii) Gross Revenue Tax at a general rate of 3% on gross revenue of persons engaged in business inside RMI. A non-resident BC conducting offshore dangerous-goods forwarding falls outside both taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

USD 0 - no VAT registration threshold applies because no VAT exists. Under the Gross Revenue Tax regime, registration and business licensing are triggered by the factual commencement of engaging in business within RMI; the subject non-resident BC has no such trigger for foreign-origin-to-foreign-destination DG cargo movements, and no GRT registration or customs bonded registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating as a VAT mechanism does not exist in RMI because there is no VAT/GST. The functional equivalent is the inherent exclusion of export and offshore-transit transactions from the RMI indirect tax base. The BC must evidence: (1) cargo is picked up and delivered outside RMI customs territory; (2) no importation into RMI occurs; (3) the freight service provider, principals, and payment flows are non-resident; and (4) the BC holds a current certificate of non-resident status from the Registrar of Corporations. Compliance with these facts secures exemption from GRT and import duty without any refund mechanism being needed.

  • RMI Customs Export/Transshipment Declaration lodged through ASYCUDA World (when goods transit RMI territory)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing foreign port of loading and foreign port of discharge
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed packing list describing dangerous-goods tariff classification (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510)
  • IMO/IMDG Dangerous Goods Declaration and Container/Vessel Packing Certificate (UN Class 1-9)
  • Certificate of Non-Resident Status / NRDC exemption certificate issued by the RMI Office of the Registrar of Corporations
  • SWIFT wire, Letter of Credit settlement records, or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing the offshore consideration

RMI has no Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC), e-invoicing, fiscal stamping, or real-time clearance mandate for B2B invoices. Customs formalities for goods transiting RMI are processed through the ASYCUDA World electronic single window operated by the RMI Customs Service; DG shipments require the electronic DG declaration, IMO/IMDG class data, and vessel/vehicle packing certificate integrated into the clearance workflow.

The Revenue and Taxation Division and RMI Customs Service may retroactively strip the non-resident exemption where DG forwarding activity is found to have RMI-source elements (e.g., warehouse breaks, local handling contracts, Majuro/Ebeye billing). Consequences include retrospective GRT on all RMI-source gross revenue at 3%, interest, late-payment penalties, revocation of the Registrar's non-resident status, and debarment from the DG license. Customs misdeclaration or omission in export/transshipment entries is punishable under the RMI Customs Act by forfeiture of goods, monetary fines, and imprisonment for willful smuggling or false declaration. Although RMI has no exchange-control or statutory FX-repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is applied by US-dollar correspondent banks under AML/CFT enhanced due diligence for high-risk DG freight; failure to substantiate offshore receipt within that window triggers account review, holds, and potential referral to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender in the Republic of the Marshall Islands under the Compact of Free Association; there are no separate capital or current account convertibility restrictions, and no exchange control regime exists. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is freely permitted, subject solely to banking due diligence, AML/CFT verification, and any temporary correspondent-bank compliance holds. Non-resident Business Corporations (BCs) face no domestic withholding taxes on dividends, interest, or royalties, given the tax-exempt status of the entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts, as the RMI is a de facto U.S. dollarized economy and does not maintain a domestic currency. Exporters and freight forwarders must retain documentary evidence of each export transaction (commercial invoice, bills of lading, airway bills, dangerous goods declaration) for at least five years under the Banking Act record-keeping obligations, and all foreign-currency-denominated proceeds must be reported to the company's licensed RMI bank for reconciliation against the underlying ISIC 5229 trade scope.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commissioner of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, operating under the Ministry of Finance and the Banking Act 1970 (as amended); there is no statutory central bank or independent monetary authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. The RMI has no domestic EMI licensing framework, and any unlicensed foreign digital bank soliciting deposit-taking from RMI persons is prohibited under the Banking Act. A non-resident BC may lawfully open a treasury and settlement account with a fully licensed offshore EMI (e.g., UK FCA-authorized or Singapore MAS-authorized) provided the EMI does not maintain a physical branch in the RMI and the relationship is structured as a cross-border non-solicited corporate account. However, the RMI licensing bank and its U.S. correspondent may refuse to clear transactions trail-commingled with EMI IP addresses or virtual asset flows, particularly where the underlying business is dangerous goods forwarding. Padding and clearing latency are acceptable only for low-frequency vendor payments, not for capital escrow or LC-backed trades.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions and shareholder loans must be wired via SWIFT MT103 using ISO 20022 purpose codes 'GIF' (general capital increase) or 'ASR' (advance for services) into the BC's licensed RMI bank account. Because the RMI clears USD through U.S. correspondent banks, the wire must pass OFAC screening and must include in field 70 the legal entity identifier, a reference to the RMI Business Corporations Act registration number, and the phrase 'NON-RESIDENT BC CAPITAL ESCROW – NOT FOR TRADE'. Beneficial-owner verification documents (including proof of funds origin) must precede the wire by at least 72 hours, or the correspondent may reject or freeze the credit. Intra-group capitalization through crypto or non-bank channels is strictly non-compliant for this entity.Trade settlement for ISIC 5229 dangerous goods forwarding is executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments to overseas DG handler and haulage vendors, and (ii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the RMI licensed bank and anchored to UCP600 rules for high-value cargo bills. Each outbound MT103 must include the abstract of dangerous goods (UN number, class, packing group), the DG freight license number of the issuing entity, and the relevant Incoterms 2020 rule in field 77. LC transactions must be drawn only against clean bills of lading and shipper's attestation of IMDG/IATA compliance; the bank will require a pre-issued standby LC or collateral deposit to cover any demurrage or contamination claims. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local currency threshold. The Marshall Islands has not enacted transfer pricing documentation legislation and imposes no Local File or Master File requirement. Nevertheless, entities engaged in economic substance relevant activities must maintain contemporaneous accounting records demonstrating deductions, revenues, and operational substance if the competent authority requests them.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), effective 2019, apply to domestic companies undertaking relevant activities, including distribution and service center operations, shipping, financing, and leasing. A Business Corporation licensed to provide specialized air/sea transport of regulated hazardous chemicals may fall within the distribution and service center relevant activity. Such an entity must demonstrate that core income-generating activities, decision making, employees, and premises are located in the Marshall Islands or satisfy the reduced substance test applicable to non-resident entities. Annual economic substance declarations are mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code, including a fine of up to USD 10,000 per offense and imprisonment for up to five years. The Registrar may also strike the entity from the Corporate Register.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fines and restitution if they authorized, directed, or participated in the false filing. Repeated misuse of the corporate form to conceal commercial fraud triggers statutory veil-piercing, allowing creditors to seek civil recovery from personal assets.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Marshall Islands Competent Authority may impose an administrative penalty of USD 10,000 and a further USD 10,000 per month of continuing non-compliance. The entity may be denied tax residence certificates or dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations may be subject to fines and a prohibition from acting as directors in the Marshall Islands. Substantive economic control is required; paper directorships expose the actor to civil negligence and misfeasance claims.
Money Laundering and AML Breaches

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Money Laundering (Amendment) Act attract a fine not exceeding USD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to ten years for individuals. The High-Risk AML classification attached to ISIC 5229 dangerous goods forwarding mandates enhanced oversight.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors may be individually criminally liable for severe neglect. Additionally, the Financial Intelligence Unit can issue freezing orders, travel bans, and asset seizure orders against directors that materially facilitate laundering through freight payments.
Unlicensed Specialized Dangerous Goods Forwarding

Statutory sanction: Operating without the mandatory license is an unlawful act under Marshall Islands administrative and transport law, carrying penalties including fines, suspension of shipping agency ability, and seizure of cargo documents. The Registrar may involuntarily dissolve the corporation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers cannot escape liability by claiming corporate immunity in the transport context. Personal liability attaches to any director who signed invoices, bills of lading, or customs declarations in furtherance of unlicensed hazardous cargo operations. Civil restitution, loss of travel clearance, and criminal imprisonment are progressive consequences.

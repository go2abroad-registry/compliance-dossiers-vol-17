Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) levies no income tax and therefore maintains no dedicated national income tax authority and no income tax e-filing portal. The relevant administrative bodies are: (1) Ministry of Finance & Treasury, Revenue and Customs Division (Majuro), which administers customs duties, excise duties and the Business License Fee; official site: www.finance.gov.mh; (2) Office of the Registrar of Corporations (Majuro), which administers incorporation, annual reports and the annual corporate franchise fee under the Business Corporations Act (BCA); filings are made through ARARM-licensed registered agents; and (3) Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA), which administers payroll contributions for local employees. No TIN-issuing income tax office exists.

E-government filing portal

There is no income-tax TIN. Steps to obtain the statutory identifiers for a foreign-owned Non-Resident Business Corporation: (1) engage an ARARM-licensed registered agent in Majuro, as the BCA requires a resident agent; (2) file Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations; (3) obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and the corporate registration number, which is the entity's primary statutory identifier; (4) if the entity will conduct any business activity with nexus inside the RMI, apply to the Business License Office for an annual Business License and obtain the license number; the annual fee is 3% of gross revenue subject to statutory minimums and sector-specific flat fees; a purely non-resident offshore trader with no RMI-source sales, no office and no local employees does not require a license; (5) if the entity employs RMI-based staff, register with MISSA and obtain an employer account number; (6) if the entity imports goods into the RMI, register with Customs for an importer identification number. Non-resident BCs are not issued an income-tax TIN because no such tax exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone / Territorial_Scope: A Non-Resident Business Corporation is entirely outside the RMI tax net. The RMI has no corporate income tax, no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties or fees, and no estate, gift, stamp or property tax on share capital. The only state impost on a BCA entity is a fixed annual corporate franchise/annual report fee, which is not profit-based. A BC deriving trading income from wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules (ISIC 4669) to non-RMI buyers is tax-exempt in the RMI regardless of where contracts are executed, provided no local business license nexus is created.

Available election forms

Not applicable: the RMI has no income tax statute, so there are no check-the-box classifications, no hybrid-mismatch elections and no pass-through tax elections. Corporate-form elections available under the BCA include conversion or continuation into an RMI Limited Liability Company or Limited Partnership, which may be relevant to beneficial owners' home-jurisdiction planning; designation of the registered agent; and, in the shipping context, election to register vessels under the RMI open registry, subjecting shipping income to annual tonnage fees rather than any revenue-based impost. An RMI entity is not eligible to make a U.S. check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or general sales tax exists in the Marshall Islands. The only indirect imposts are: (i) import customs duties under the RMI Customs Act and Tariff Act; (ii) excise duties on selective goods such as alcohol, tobacco, fuel and motor vehicles; and (iii) the Business License Fee of 3% of gross revenue, which is a revenue-based levy rather than a value-added or sales tax. There is consequently no indirect-tax registration regime for exporters.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST threshold exists. For customs purposes, there is no registration threshold; any importer must file a customs entry for goods entering the RMI. For Business License purposes, the obligation arises when business is carried on inside the RMI; the fee is 3% of gross revenue with statutory minimums. All amounts are in U.S. dollars, which is the official legal tender. Non-resident offshore trading does not meet the nexus test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the RMI levies no VAT or GST, so there is no export zero-rating provision, no export VAT refund mechanism and no zero-rated supplier status. A Non-Resident BC selling petrochemical polymers to overseas buyers is simply outside the RMI tax base. For RMI-flagged shipping operations, the equivalent feature is the tonnage-fee regime under the RMI Maritime Act: shipping income is not taxed by reference to profits or freight, and no refund mechanism exists.

The compliance risks for this structure are: (1) false filings with the Registrar of Corporations, which attract penalties and possible de-registration under the BCA; (2) failure to hold a Business License if the RMI authorities establish any local nexus, which could result in back assessment of the 3% gross-revenue fee, penalties, interest and license revocation; (3) customs offences such as under-declaration or smuggling if goods enter the RMI without duty payment, which attract forfeiture and penalties under the Customs legislation; and (4) substance-over-form recharacterization by beneficial owners' home jurisdictions if the offshore BC lacks economic substance. No RMI clawback of export refunds can arise because no such refunds are granted.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI has no separate national currency and no exchange-control legislation; capital and current account convertibility are therefore unrestricted. The USD is freely usable for all domestic and cross-border transactions, with no restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, liquidation proceeds, loan servicing, or disposal of capital. The only practical constraint on convertibility is the operational availability of U.S. dollar clearing through RMI-licensed banks' correspondent relationships and the compliance screening applied by international correspondent banks, not any statutory or regulatory impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, retention, or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts under RMI law. An exporting entity may freely retain and disburse all USD export proceeds without central-bank approval, conversion obligations, or time-limited surrender. The sole compliance requirement is AML/CFT record-keeping and transaction monitoring under the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2018, including customer due diligence, threshold-based reporting, and accurate identification of the underlying trade in payment instructions.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) maintains no indigenous central bank or monetary authority; the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, and the U.S. Federal Reserve System acts as the de facto monetary anchor. Financial-sector supervision is exercised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission under the Ministry of Finance, acting pursuant to the Banking Act 2010, the Business Corporations Act 1998, and the Financial Transactions Reporting Act 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. RMI has not enacted a fintech or electronic-money institution licensing regime; the Banking Act 2010 reserves deposit-taking to licensed banks, and money services businesses require AML registration. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in third jurisdictions (e.g., HK MSO, FCA UK, MAS Singapore) may be used operationally as receivable or payable consolidation rails, provided the destination or source jurisdiction is not OFAC-sanctioned, but they are not recognized as banking counterparties by RMI regulators or by RMI banks, and major US correspondents will typically treat such entities as unlicensed high-risk intermediaries requiring explicit substance and license disclosure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0.0% for a Non-Resident Domestic Business Corporation deriving no income from sources within the Marshall Islands; a 3.0% Gross Revenue Tax (GRT) is statutorily imposed on gross revenue derived from RMI-source business, which a properly structured non-resident BC must not earn.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0.00% — the Marshall Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions declared by a Business Corporation, regardless of the shareholder's residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before November 30 of each calendar year; a 60-day grace period is recognized in practice before the Registrar commences administrative dissolution proceedings.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly returns are due on the 30th day after the end of each calendar quarter; the annual reconciliation is due by the last day of the fourth month after the fiscal year-end (illustratively, 30 April for a 31 December year-end).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None prescribed by RMI law — the jurisdiction has not enacted Local File or Master File thresholds. Defensive transfer pricing documentation is advised for intercompany PE/PP granule resale transactions exceeding USD 1,000,000 annually in order to withstand audits in counterparty jurisdictions; the functional currency and statutory currency of the RMI is the U.S. dollar.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not enacted in the Marshall Islands. The non-resident BC is required only to maintain a licensed registered agent, a registered office, and current corporate records in the RMI; local board meetings, local staff, and local premises are not required. However, OECD Global Forum, FATF, and foreign tax-residence standards require substantive commercial functions (negotiation, price risk, logistics control, and title passage) to be documented at the entity's true place of management, particularly where the entity is treated as tax-resident in a user jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abuse of the Non-Resident Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive assessment of 3% GRT on all concealed gross revenue, interest at the statutory rate, a negligence/evasion penalty of up to 50% of the tax due, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 1989 carrying a fine of up to US$1,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years, or both.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the BC is used as a sham: directors who authorized false GRT returns or diverted RMI-source revenue are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax; the High Court may impose asset freezes and travel bans pending prosecution, and convicted directors may be disqualified from managing RMI entities.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Administrative strike-off by the Registrar, monetary penalties under the RMI AML/CFT Act and Beneficial Ownership Regulations (fines on legal persons up to US$100,000 on conviction), cancellation of the registered agent's endorsement, and refusal of Certificates of Good Standing, which halts Swift LC issuance and bank onboarding.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal fines, disqualification orders, and criminal liability for false declarations on the Statement of Acknowledgment; the court may disregard the corporate shell and order the undisclosed principal's assets to be attached; imprisonment of up to 2 years applies to knowing concealment of beneficial ownership.
AML/CFT Violations and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Freezing orders and asset forfeiture under the RMI Criminal Code and AML/CFT Act; fines up to US$100,000 for corporate entities and imprisonment for culpable natural persons; loss of correspondent banking access and designation as a shell-risk entity by global financial-action task forces.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authenticate false trade documents face civil restitution to the defrauded LC bank and criminal fraud penalties; money-laundering convictions carry imprisonment of up to 10 years; the High Court routinely imposes travel bans, passport surrender, and global asset-identification orders during investigation.
Operating While Administratively Dissolved / Registrar Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Restoration of the register requires payment of all outstanding annual fees, late penalties, and reinstatement charges; contracts made in the dissolved period are voidable at the counterparty's election; outstanding fees may be doubled at the Registrar's discretion and the BC removed from the register permanently if restoration is not sought.

Director personal liability: Under BCA Section 92 and general company law, directors are personally liable for all debts contracted during the dissolved period; fraudulent declarations trigger disqualification and criminal liability; directors may be sued personally by LC banks, insurers, and cargo purchasers for losses caused by the void contracts.
Fraudulent Financial Reporting / LC and Bank Fraud

Statutory sanction: Liability under the RMI Criminal Code for forgery and fraud, U.S. wire-fraud exposure where USD clearing occurs through the Federal Reserve system, debarment from all RMI-registered agent and maritime privileges, and civil liability to confirming banks for dishonor and reimbursement; banks may report the entity to the RMI FIU and OFAC.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is near-automatic in documentary fraud: directors who endorse false LC documents are personally liable to the negotiating bank for amounts paid, plus interest and costs; conviction carries imprisonment; travel bans and Interpol notices may issue where cross-border cargo diversion is alleged.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Treasury and Foreign Investment, Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Office (official portal: rmi-government.org)

E-government filing portal

The Marshall Islands imposes no income tax, VAT, GST, or sales tax; no general statutory TIN or VAT ID exists. A foreign-owned non-resident Business Corporation (BC) must obtain: (1) a Foreign Investment Business License (FIBL) from the Foreign Investment Advisor's Office under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1996, if engaging in licensed foreign-investment activity; (2) a Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of Corporations under the Business Corporations Act 1990; (3) an Employer Registration Number from the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration if any Marshall Islands employees are engaged, triggering monthly social security contributions; and (4) a Customs Importer Code from the Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Office for any import of goods. Procedure: file the FIBL application with a business plan and evidence of capital, pay the statutory registration fees (approximately USD 250–750), and receive the certificates; no income-tax file number is issued or required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None under Marshall Islands domestic law: the Business Corporations Act 1990 contains no check-the-box or US-style elective entity classification regime. A non-resident Business Corporation is automatically treated as an offshore, non-taxable entity for RMI tax purposes. If a US person owns the BC, a US federal check-the-box election (IRS Form 8832) may independently apply for US tax classification; hybrid mismatch elections under OECD BEPS Action 2 are not recognized by RMI domestic statute.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales Tax - Customs Import Duty and Ad Valorem Tariff Regime Only

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST regime is in force. Customs importer registration is mandatory for any person or entity importing goods into the Marshall Islands regardless of value. Import duties are levied under the Customs Act 1990 and the Marshall Islands Tariff Schedule, generally 5%-10% ad valorem, with higher specific rates for fuel, alcohol, and tobacco.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable; with no VAT/GST regime in force there is no output tax to zero-rate and no VAT refund mechanism. Export sales of goods and offshore services from the Marshall Islands are entirely outside the scope of any indirect tax, and no export duty is imposed. Exporters of goods must file a customs export declaration for statistical and control purposes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form RC-EX-01)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

None. The Marshall Islands does not operate a real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping regime. Commercial invoices require no tax-authority clearance. Customs clearance for imported goods is processed through the ASYCUDA World computerized customs declaration system; domestic and export invoices are paper-based or ordinary electronic invoices without fiscal validation or digital stamping.

VAT zero-rating fraud is impossible in the absence of VAT. False customs declarations, including undervaluation, misdescription of goods, or unlicensed exportation, are prosecuted under the Customs Act 1990 and the RMI Criminal Code, with fines up to US$25,000, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective customs reassessment is available for up to five years from the date of declaration; no VAT clawback regime exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands maintains a fully dollarized, open financial system with no exchange-control restrictions. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible into and out of U.S. dollars. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation face no statutory conversion hurdles; however, banking institutions may apply internal due-diligence requirements and are bound by U.S. OFAC sanctions screening and anti-money-laundering protocols.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. As a dollarized economy with no foreign-exchange legislation, the Marshall Islands imposes no surrender obligation for export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriation. Exporters may lawfully retain U.S. dollar receipts in domestic or offshore accounts, subject only to normal anti-money-laundering recordkeeping.

Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank; the Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance and the Banking Commissioner exercise prudential banking supervision. The United States Federal Reserve serves as the de facto monetary authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Offshore digital EMI providers can supply U.S. dollar settlement, virtual account numbers, and card-acquiring services without being licensed as deposit-taking banks in the Marshall Islands. Legal constraint: the RMI Banking Act requires a banking license for deposit-taking, but a money-service business may operate under a money-transmitter authorization. For a low-risk specialty tea salon, a foreign EMI is viable only if it can support merchant card acquiring, SWIFT inbound wires, and outward USD payments; however, most EMIs impose restrictive OFAC/KYC policies and may require an RMI legal opinion confirming that the business does not constitute unauthorized banking activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Marshall Islands domestic transfer-pricing documentation threshold is codified. Practical compliance benchmark: USD 1,000,000. Where the BC engages in cross-border related-party transactions exceeding USD 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value, a Local File commensurate with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (BEPS Action 13) should be prepared and retained to preserve the zero-tax position against foreign recharacterization. The Marshall Islands imposes no Country-by-Country Reporting obligation on the BC itself; however, if the BC belongs to an MNE group above EUR 750 million consolidated revenue, it must supply financial and ownership data to the ultimate parent entity for CbC reporting purposes.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands has not enacted a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime in the BVI or Cayman model, and the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices accepts its zero-tax treatment of non-resident BCs without a local substance test. Nevertheless, economic substance is the critical anti-abuse benchmark enforced by foreign revenue authorities. For an ISIC 5630 Specialty Tea Salon Lounge, substance is demonstrated by: (1) a physical licensed tea salon, retail counter, and cultural ceramic venue in the operating market; (2) local employees, payroll records, and management decisions in that market; (3) point-of-sale and banking infrastructure in the operating country; and (4) accounting records maintained at the registered agent or a recognized record-keeping location. If the BC operates as a pure invoicing or brand-holding shell without these indicia, foreign tax courts may recharacterize it as having a permanent establishment or place of effective management at the operating site, imposing local corporate income tax, VAT, and payroll liabilities retroactively. CRS and FATCA reporting obligations remain independently applicable to the beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Zero-Tax Exploitation

Statutory sanction: In the foreign operating jurisdiction: recharacterization of the BC as tax-resident, assessment of back corporate income tax, VAT, payroll taxes, interest, and civil penalties of up to 200% of the underpaid tax. In the Marshall Islands: strike-off and administrative dissolution for a false Box 7 declaration, judicial dissolution petition by the Attorney General under the Business Corporations Act, and forfeiture of the corporate franchise. The Republic cooperates with foreign revenue authorities through Tax Information Exchange Agreements and mutual legal assistance treaties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign the false non-resident declaration face perjury prosecution under the Marshall Islands Criminal Code and personal liability for foreign unpaid taxes where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud. Cross-border consequences include civil restitution orders in the operating jurisdiction, international travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition requests; imprisonment of up to 10 years may attach under the revenue statutes of the prosecuting jurisdiction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Nominee Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may administratively dissolve the BC for failure to maintain records or for false attestations; monetary penalties up to USD 25,000 may be imposed for knowingly false filings under the Business Corporations Act; the BC loses legal standing to sue or defend; and foreign revenue and banking authorities will aggregate the BC's assets and flows with the beneficial owner's direct income, imposing back taxes and anti-avoidance penalties under GAAR, CFC, and substance-over-form doctrines.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face civil restitution orders and disqualification from holding directorships for up to 15 years. Beneficial owners are exposed to fraudulent-trading liability when the BC is insolvent and to criminal prosecution for using the shell to evade licensing, immigration, tax, or creditor claims. Asset seizure, personal bankruptcy proceedings, and enforcement of foreign civil judgments against personal assets follow in practice.

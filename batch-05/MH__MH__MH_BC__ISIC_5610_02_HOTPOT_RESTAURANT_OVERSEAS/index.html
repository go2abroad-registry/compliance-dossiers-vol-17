Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services of the Republic of the Marshall Islands – Tax and Revenue Administration Division (official government portal: https://www.rmi.gov.mh; no separate national e-filing portal is publicly maintained for non-resident tax-exempt corporations)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) is not issued a Marshall Islands TIN or VAT ID because it is tax-exempt under the Business Corporations Act. The exclusive statutory identifier is the corporation registration number / certificate issued by the Registrar of Corporations (Office of the Registrar). Only if the entity conducts Marshall Islands payroll, imports goods into the RMI, or holds a local business license will the competency require registration with the Tax and Revenue Administration and the RMI Social Security Administration; a taxpayer account number may then be assigned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box, corporate classification, or hybrid mismatch election exists under Marshall Islands law. Tax-exempt non-resident domestic corporation status is automatic under the Business Corporations Act for entities incorporated in the RMI that do not carry on business in the Marshall Islands. There is no OECD hybrid-regime election; an entity seeking local economic activity would instead require a domestic business license and would forfeit the non-resident tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / general sales tax; the RMI applies customs import duties, selective excise taxes, and local business-license gross receipts fees

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – USD-denominated no-VAT jurisdiction; no economic turnover threshold applies. Any importer must register for customs purposes and any commercial operator holding a local business must obtain a business license from the first dollar of activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not impose VAT/GST and does not levy export duties on goods or offshore services supplied by a non-resident domestic corporation. There is therefore no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no export VAT refund mechanism.

No real-time fiscal electronic invoicing, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists for non-resident tax-exempt corporations. Import/export declarations, where applicable, are processed through the RMI Customs Division / ASYCUDA-type electronic clearance platform; local transactions may use ordinary commercial invoices and POS records.

Not applicable absent VAT; however, false customs declarations or fraudulent use of RMI tax-exempt status to import goods for local consumption would invoke customs seizure, forfeiture, fines, and revocation of the non-resident corporation's tax exemption by the Registrar/Tax Administration, with retrospective assessments to the extent a taxable activity is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands operates a fully dollarized financial system. There are no exchange-control laws restricting convertibility of the U.S. dollar for either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: dividends, interest, royalties, and capital repayments may flow freely from a non-resident Business Corporation without RMI central bank approval or foreign exchange licence. However, the operating-country jurisdiction of the overseas hotpot restaurant may impose its own repatriation taxes, certification of audited financial statements, or beneficial-ownership declarations before outward remittance is permitted by the clearing bank. The practical constraints are therefore not RMI exchange-control rules but correspondent-bank AML/KYC policy and the documentary burden required to prove the underlying commercial substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligations under Marshall Islands law. Because the U.S. dollar is the domestic legal tender and no separate local currency exists, there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed timeline for surrender, and no requirement to deposit export receipts into a central-bank account. The overseas restaurant treasury may retain 100% of export and operating receipts in USD, provided that the receiving RMI Business Corporation complies with bank-required source-of-funds documentation, OFAC screening, and the reporting thresholds of the U.S. Bank Secrecy Act where applicable to correspondent banks.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands has no domestic central bank and does not operate an independent monetary policy. The official currency is the United States dollar, with monetary stability effectively delegated to the U.S. Federal Reserve under the Compacts of Free Association. The prudential and licensing authority for banks and deposit-taking institutions is the Banking Commission (Banking Commissioner), functioning under the Ministry of Finance, pursuant to the Marshall Islands Banking Act and related AML/CFT regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary depositary bank; Medium as a non-custodial payments overlay. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking banks in the Marshall Islands and may not lawfully hold customer balances under the Marshall Islands Banking Act. They may be used only as payment aggregation or remittance rails where settlement funds are held in segregated omnibus accounts at licensed partner banks and no RMI customer balance is created. The non-resident MH_BC hotpot restaurant treasury may integrate a regulated fintech EMI for POS settlement aggregation or supplier disbursement initiation, provided the EMI is licensed in its home jurisdiction, has a U.S. or E.U./U.K. partner-bank framework, and provides full audit trail and reconciliation to the RMI-licensed operational account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A — the Marshall Islands has no transfer-pricing documentation statute and no local-file or master-file threshold. Inbound foreign-source restaurant and condiment-supply income is outside the RMI tax net; the operating country's transfer-pricing rules govern the actual restaurant entity.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations (2019) apply only to RMI-tax-resident entities carrying on defined relevant activities (banking, insurance, international shipping, intellectual-property holding, financing and leasing, distribution and service centres). An overseas hotpot restaurant and condiment supply chain is not a defined relevant activity; however, if the RMI BC is treated as tax resident or if foreign authorities pierce to it, the holder must evidence commercial substance through board meetings, contracts, and operational decisions made in the operating state.

Corporate Registry Non-Compliance (BCA)

Statutory sanction: Monthly penalty of 10% of the annual franchise fee; administrative strike-off after 90 days; reinstatement fees plus full arrears; continued trading under a struck-off name is unlawful.

Director personal liability: Directors who transact in the company's name after strike-off become personally liable for all debts so incurred; directors may also be barred from serving on any RMI corporation if they knowingly filed a false annual report.
Tax Evasion / Sham Corporation in Operating Jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: RMI imposes no tax, but the operating jurisdiction may recharacterize the entity under controlled-foreign-company rules or general anti-avoidance principles, disallow deductions, reassess tax at the applicable corporate rate, and impose penalty interest of 20%–50% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of avoided tax; criminal courts may impose travel bans and imprisonment for willful tax evasion of up to 10 years in the operating country.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering Act 2012 (as amended): civil penalties up to USD 500,000, revocation of the BC's registered agent acceptance, asset forfeiture, and removal from the register.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly fail to implement KYC/AML controls or who sign false declarations are personally liable for fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Economic Substance / Transparency Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under ESR and international transparency procedures: financial penalties from USD 20,000 to USD 200,000 per entity per year; default penalty for continued non-compliance; possible administrative strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve a false substance declaration are personally liable for the penalties and may be disqualified from acting as an officer or director of any RMI entity.

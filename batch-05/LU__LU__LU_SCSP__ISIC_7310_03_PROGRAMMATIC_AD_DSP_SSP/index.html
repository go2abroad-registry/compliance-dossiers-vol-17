Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a US-style check-the-box election. An SCSp is, by default and as a rule, fiscally transparent: it is not subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, or net wealth tax; profits and losses flow through to partners according to the partnership agreement and are taxable or creditable in the partners' own jurisdictions. There is no statutory election to convert the SCSp into a CIT taxpayer as such, although participants may structure the chain through Luxembourg corporate entities to achieve different cross-border results. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD, Luxembourg Law of 21 December 2018) must be observed because foreign characterization of the SCSp may otherwise trigger disallowances; no grandfathering applies to new structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established taxable persons: registration required for the first taxable supply in Luxembourg (no de minimis threshold). Luxembourg-established small enterprises may be exempt below EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT) under Article 57 LTV. For a programmatic ad exchange platform, B2B supplies to other Luxembourg taxable persons are reverse-charged and B2B supplies to non-Luxembourg taxable persons do not create Luxembourg VAT registration; Luxembourg VAT registration becomes mandatory if the platform makes B2C supplies in Luxembourg or supplies goods or local services subject to Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies where physical goods are exported outside the EU/neighbouring territories, provided departure is evidenced within the legal timeframe by a VAT-validated customs export declaration (DAE) and transport documents, and the exporter is identified for VAT in Luxembourg. Services: the platform's programmatic advertising, DSP/SSP, and technical service fees are not 'exported goods'; they fall outside Luxembourg VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules when supplied to a taxable customer in another State, or under B2C rules when effectively used outside the EU. To substantiate outside-Luxembourg treatment, retain: the service contract; IP address/geolocation or evidence of the customer's place of establishment, actual use, and consumption; the customer's valid VAT registration number for EU B2B supplies; and bank settlement records. Refund mechanism: input VAT on Luxembourg costs (e.g. hosting, local office, marketing) is deductible in the Luxembourg VAT return against Luxembourg output VAT; if no such output VAT arises, a non-established operator may recover Luxembourg input VAT through the 13th Council Directive refund procedure (Form 13 VAT 655) within nine months after the calendar year of the charge, subject to AEDT verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE) validated by Luxembourg Customs and issued through the EU customs ECS/ICS system
  • Transport documentation demonstrating physical departure: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR if goods movement is present
  • Commercial invoice, contract or platform settlement statement evidencing the recipient and cross-border commercial terms, together with bank payment/inward remittance proof

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) system for B2B VAT. E-invoicing is lawful if authenticity, integrity and legibility are guaranteed. B2G e-invoices must be submitted in the European standard EN 16931/PEPPOL format for contracts awarded by central public bodies (national public procurement portal). There is no digital stamping or mandatory pre-clearance API; the AEDT may, on audit, require electronic extraction of invoicing data under applicable VAT inspection powers. Standard VAT invoices must state the SCSp's RCS number, VAT number, service description, place-of-supply rule, rate, and, for reverse-charge intra-community supplies, the customer's VAT number and the words 'autofacturation'/'auto-liquidation' where relevant.

There is no general Luxembourg exchange-control repatriation deadline for digital services; the 180-day field is the requested template default and is not itself a Luxembourg VAT due date. Mischaracterisation of place of supply, failure to prove export, or non-compliant zero-rating exposes the SCSp to AEDT reassessment of Luxembourg VAT at 17%, default interest (the LTV/General Tax Law applies late payment interest), administrative fines for infringement of invoicing and declaration rules, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent VAT evasion. The assessment/recovery window is normally five years and is extended in cases of intentional evasion or anti-fraud investigations; any input VAT previously deducted for corresponding costs is clawed back if the underlying output supply is retroactively reclassified as taxable in Luxembourg.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the Codewall, with no exchange control authorization, no surrender requirement, and no differentiated treatment between resident and non-resident SCSp partners. Profit remittances, distributions, dividends, and partnership capital withdrawals may be executed freely via banking channels, provided only that standard payment reason codes and AML/KYC reporting obligations are met. The sole practical hurdles are bank-level compliance documentation and anti-money laundering checks, not statutory convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, minimum surrender ratio, or forced conversion obligation for export proceeds. Receivables from programmatic advertising services rendered to non-EU counterparties may be retained in invoiced currency (typically USD) in any bank account, held in FX, or converted to EUR at the corporate partner's discretion. Final reporting follows balance-of-payments statistics filings (BOP) submitted by resident banks to the BCL, not as a control measure but as statistical observation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CTF supervision of credit institutions and payment service providers; the BCL implements European Central Bank (ECB) monetary policy and operates TARGET2-LU settlement infrastructure. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign exchange administration authority; FX policy falls under Eurosystem competence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Luxembourg-compliant and EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as those authorized under the CSSF or other EEA regulators may be used for cross-border payments and virtual IBANs; however, they are not full credit institutions and cannot provide lending, foreign exchange forwards, or custody. Offshore (non-EU) EMIs impose material legal constraints under PSD2, the EU E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC), and Luxembourg anti-money laundering law, including the need for a local branch or agent where passporting is not available, client money safeguarding, and full access to partner identity documentation. The SCSp's partnership opacity increases onboarding friction; EMIs generally require a Luxembourg RCS extract, certified partnership agreement, and a breakdown of each partner's economic and voting rights. For a programmatic AdTech platform with significant USD flows, the use of an EU-licensed EMI is viable for collections and payouts, but a tier-1 bank is recommended for the principal operating account.

AdTech revenue model showing rapid throughput from programmatic bidders, inventory sellers, and demand-side platforms with diverse counterparty geographies

Capital contributions arriving from multiple non-resident partners without clear economic substance or source-of-funds evidence

Defense evidence: Source-of-funds documentation including bank statements, capital contribution bank transfer references, and partner net-worth statements where requested; board resolutions or partner resolutions approving capital calls; subscription documents with payment timestamps; tax-transparent partnership status letter or tax advisor confirmation to explain lack of corporate income-tax burden; audited opening balance sheet if required by the bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions from non-resident partners shall be routed through SWIFT MT103 or EU ISO 20022 pain.001/004 messages to the SCSp's EUR capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire instruction shall include the partnership legal name, the partnership RCS number, and reference code 'SCSP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' followed by the capital call number. No exchange-control declaration is required; however, the receiving bank will require a copy of the capital call notice, bank reference from the remitting partner's account, and confirmation of the funding currency. For dollar-denominated contributions, the bank may route through a corresponding USD account in New York or via TARGET2 for EUR conversion; the SCSp shall instruct settlement after checking intermediary charges. Fixed-income income or working capital flows from programmatic operations must use Service revenues purpose codes (e.g., 'charges for services' in balance-of-payments classification) and should not be mislabeled as capital contributions to avoid AML misclassification.The primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for non-EU participating banks, SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) for EU publishers and vendors, and ISO 20022 pain.001 for batch payouts through corporate banking portals. Settlement to large-scale programmatic counterparties may follow contractual netting; for triggered SSP disbursements, the SCSp should use a dedicated payout sub-account with bank-provided APIs supporting straight-through processing. For inventory acquisition and revenue share payments to non-EU countries, Commercial Letters of Credit may be required where contractual terms demand documentary security; otherwise MT103 wire transfers are used. TARGET2, through BCL, provides real-time gross settlement for EUR flows only, while USD flows will settle via correspondent banking networks (CHIPS or Fedwire) through the bank's USD clearing agent (e.g., BGL BNP Paribas or JP Morgan). The treasury policy shall also consider day-light overdraft controls, value-date optimization, and use of MT101 sweeping to maintain minimum liquidity at the operating account.Outright EUR/USD forward contracts (up to 3-6 month tenors) with authorized Luxembourg credit institutions under ISDA or bank-standard netting master agreements, permitted without prior BCL approval.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for settlements in restricted or low-liquidity currencies if the SCSp's SSP activity generates noneurrency cash flows in currencies not fully deliverable; NDFs must be transacted with the banking counterparty's offshore desk and structured to settle in EUR.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and currency swaps) for obtaining USD liquidity while keeping EUR capital at the Luxembourg account, subject to valuation of collateral as acceptable under the bank's treasury policy.Plain-vanilla European-style FX options (calls and puts on USD/EUR) for risk-capped hedging, provided the option premium is paid out of operational cash-flow and hedging documentation aligns with the SCSp's treasury mandate.

24.94% (Luxembourg City: CIT 17% + municipal business tax 6.75% + solidarity surcharge 7% on CIT = 1.19%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% for dividends distributed by a Luxembourg capital company; 0% for profit distributions by the SCSp to partners because the SCSp is fiscally transparent and distributions are treated as profit shares, not dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and CbC Report triggered at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue (EU Directive 2016/881). Luxembourg Local File expected where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 and taxpayer turnover exceeds EUR 3,100,000 (ACD TP circular LIR n° 164/2). Ad-tech inter-company flows must always be documented despite any de minimis threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone ESR statute comparable to UAE. Substance requirements derive from OECD BEPS Actions 5 and 6, the ATAD GAAR, and ACD administrative practice. The SCSp must maintain a Luxembourg-resident general partner with real local presence (office, staff, decision-making for the ad-exchange platform), board minutes, local bank accounts, and commercial contracts. A letter-box SCSp with no operational substance risks re-characterization, denial of treaty benefits and loss of the participation exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Supply Chain

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Art. 426 AO and Art. 396 Criminal Code: fine up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years; administrative evasion surcharge up to 10% of evaded tax; late-payment interest 0.8% per month; confiscation of illegal gains.

Director personal liability: Directors/gérants of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax and civil damages where fraud was authorized or knewly concealed; criminal conviction triggers travel bans, passport seizure and disqualification from board office for up to 10 years; civil restitution under Art. 1382 Civil Code.
Failure to File / Late Payment

Statutory sanction: Surcharge up to 12% of assessed tax; late-payment interest 0.8% per month; administrative fine up to EUR 1,000 per missing return; ex officio assessment with additional penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid withheld taxes and assessed tax where non-payment results from gross negligence; tax authorities may sue the directors personally for the corporate debt; management ban on recidivism; travel bans and asset attachment for tax debts above EUR 5,000.
Artificial Arrangement / Lack of Economic Substance (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: ATAD GAAR (Art. 8bis LIR) disregards the artificial arrangement; reassessment of income with 10% surcharge; denial of participation exemption, IP box and treaty benefits; criminal fraud prosecution if supported by false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors liable under Art. 710-1 of the Law on Commercial Companies for unlawful distributions and artificial structures; personal civil liability for the hidden tax debt if the GP is liquidated; travel bans and asset freezes in cross-border tax fraud investigations; extradition risk in serious international cases.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended): money laundering offence — imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fine up to EUR 1,250,000 for individuals; legal entities fined up to EUR 5,000,000; CSSF administrative fine up to EUR 1,000,000; asset freezing and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner face personal criminal conviction for AML conspiracy; civil liability to defrauded advertisers and partners; travel bans and freezing orders; automatic disqualification from professional management; extradition risk in organized cross-border fraud networks.

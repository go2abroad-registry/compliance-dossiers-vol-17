Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Office, Government of the Republic of the Marshall Islands; official portal: https://www.mof.gov.mh

E-government filing portal

Non-resident business corporations are not subject to Marshall Islands income tax and are not required to obtain a domestic TIN. If the entity engages in any local import/export activity, it must register with the Marshall Islands Customs Service for a customs registration number and obtain an Importer/Exporter Code; such registration is administrative only and does not create income tax nexus. Registration is completed by filing the standard Business Registration Form T-1 together with the Articles of Incorporation and a Certificate of Good Standing from the Registrar of Corporations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act, a foreign-owned non-resident domestic corporation may elect non-resident status by filing a Certificate of Election with the Registrar of Corporations, thereby confirming that no business is transacted within the Republic and securing absolute exemption from Marshall Islands income tax, gross revenue tax, and withholding tax. No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Marshall Islands law, and no hybrid mismatch election is recognized. An entity may also elect organized management provisions, but this does not alter tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; the Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no indirect tax system and therefore no turnover threshold triggers VAT/GST registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. As no VAT/GST regime exists, there is no export zero-rating claim. However, in the context of any customs duties, exported surgical suture needle assemblies are not subject to export duties provided the goods are permanently removed from the customs territory of the Marshall Islands. Offshore services rendered by a non-resident corporation to overseas buyers are outside the scope of Marshall Islands taxation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form C-1, duly certified by the Marshall Islands Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial Invoice describing the surgical suture needle assembly
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Non-Resident Entity Certificate issued by the Registrar of Corporations

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate exists under Marshall Islands law. Invoices are private commercial documents and are not subject to government clearance.

Although no VAT zero-rating exists, submission of intentionally false export declarations to the Marshall Islands Customs Service constitutes an offense punishable by fines not exceeding USD 10,000 and imprisonment not exceeding one year, plus forfeiture of goods. Any misdeclared export may trigger retrospective reassessment of applicable import or export duties and revocation of customs registration privileges. Where a non-resident corporation improperly claims non-resident tax status despite conducting local business, the Revenue and Taxation Office may retrospectively impose gross revenue tax at 3% on all taxable gross revenues, together with interest and penalties of up to 25% of the underpaid amount.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility prevails: RMI imposes no exchange controls, no FX surrender requirements, no import license-based foreign exchange rationing, and no substantive hurdles to profit remittance, dividend distribution, management fee payment, loan interest servicing, or capital repatriation for non-resident-owned Business Corporations. Because the domestic currency is USD, statutory convertibility risk is effectively zero; the binding constraint is purely commercial—maintaining clean correspondent banking relationships given global de-risking pressures. Profit remittances may flow freely, but each remittance must satisfy AML/CFT record-keeping and beneficial-ownership disclosure obligations. No advance approval or withholding tax is imposed on non-resident dividend distributions under current tax-exempt-zone treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists. RMI requires neither a mandatory timeline for converting export receipts nor a minimum quota of offshore collections to be sold to a central authority; exporters may retain 100% of USD-denominated receivables onshore or offshore at their discretion. The only binding obligations are AML/CTF record-retention (minimum five years), suspicious-transaction reporting to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit, and accurate trade-documentation discipline so that each export receivable reconciles with physical shipment, customs declarations, and the corresponding commercial contract.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a domestic central bank or issue a national currency. Under the Constitution and the Compact of Free Association with the United States, the official circulative currency is the United States Dollar (USD). Monetary policy, USD liquidity, and systemic clearing are therefore governed by the US Federal Reserve System, operating through the Fedwire Funds Service and CHIPS, with US FDIC insurance applicable to federally insured institutions. Prudential licensing, consumer protection, and AML/CFT supervision of financial institutions licensed in RMI rest with the Banking Commissioner under the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1984 and the Money Laundering (Control) Act 2003, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. RMI has no dedicated e-money institution (EMI) licensing category; a non-bank digital finance provider must obtain a full banking license under the Banking Act 1984 (commercially impractical for offshore fintechs) or remain outside regulated status without membership in USD clearing rails. Offshore EMIs authorized under UK/FCA, EU/EEA, Hong Kong, or Singapore regimes may be used only as ancillary collection gateways for consumer payments and low-balance sales receivables; they cannot serve as the principal treasury clearing bank, statutory capital escrow custodian, or LC-issuing bank for the RMI Business Corporation. Using an unlicensed foreign EMI as a substantial custodian of treasury funds would create unlicensed-activity exposure and adverse Banking Commission commentary; the blueprint therefore confines EMI usage to minor auxiliary collections with daily sweep-out to the licensed RMI bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections are routed exclusively in USD using SWIFT MT103 for principal remittances, supplemented by an MT202 COV cover message for gross amounts exceeding the beneficiary bank's high-value threshold. Since RMI has no national-purpose-code system, the MT103 field 70 narrative must embed a structured purpose text (e.g., 'INVCAP / PAID-UP CONTRIBUTION PURSUANT TO BOARD RESOLUTION DATED [date]') so the beneficiary bank's transaction-monitoring engine classifies the inflow as a capital contribution rather than trade revenue or an unspecified loan. The remitting account must belong to the sole registered shareholder or, for debt instruments, to a single designated lending bank; third-party remitters are prima facie unacceptable. The settlement stack is: originating bank → global corridor bank (e.g., Bank of Guam's US head-office network or another US money-center correspondent) → Fedwire/CHIPS → RMI beneficiary bank. Required verification pack: Board capital-call resolution, updated share registry, escrow disbursement instruction, and monthly reconciliation of the escrow ledger.Outbound settlements for raw-material imports (polyglycolic acid polymer, stainless steel needle stock, packaging consumables, and sterilization services) use three rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account and revolving supplier payments; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 via MT700/MT701 with amendments via MT707, nominated to a confirming US bank, for large-scale capital equipment and long-lead feedstock contracts; and (iii) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for high-value interbank treasury movements. Because no RMI bank participates directly in CHIPS for LC confirmation, each LC must be confirmed through a US correspondent bank; the presenting bank must submit exact document sets (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and sterilization/compliance certificates) so that the freight forwarder's data matches the LC to avoid MT734 refusal. Export collections are received in USD into the operational account, with surplus balances laddered into interest-bearing time deposits at the same institution or swept to the US correspondent's term-deposit program.Deliverable FX spot and outright forwards (USD versus EUR, GBP, JPY, AUD, and NZD) with US banking counterparties under an ISDA Master Agreement, with tenors aligned to export receivable cycles up to 12 months.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted Asian currencies (CNY, KRW, INR, TWD) used to hedge residual exposure where onshore deliverability is unavailable, booked with global bank offshore desks under an ISDA NDF annex.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR, USD/JPY, USD/GBP) for multi-year capital-equipment or acquisition-financing exposures, designated under IFRS 9 hedge accounting to avoid structural balance-sheet volatility.Plain-vanilla USD interest-rate and FX-linked options (long-only or collar constructions) executed exclusively through fully licensed US correspondent banks to protect margin on exotic-currency purchase cycles, with explicit prohibition of speculative layered option strategies.

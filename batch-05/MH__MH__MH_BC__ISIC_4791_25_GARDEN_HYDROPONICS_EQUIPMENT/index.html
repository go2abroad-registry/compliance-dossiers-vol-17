Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands – Tax and Customs Division (official RMI Government portal: https://rmigovernment.org/); corporate registration and annual franchise tax administration are handled by the Office of the Registrar of Corporations.

E-government filing portal

The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose income tax on non-resident domestic Business Corporations, so no statutory income-tax TIN or VAT ID is issued. A foreign-owned entity obtains corporate legal existence by filing Articles of Incorporation with the Registrar of Corporations, appointing a resident agent and registered office, paying the initial registration fee, and paying the annual US$100 franchise tax. Optionally, a Tax Exemption Certificate may be obtained from the Ministry of Finance to evidence that the entity's income is outside RMI tax jurisdiction. If importing physical goods into the RMI, the company must separately obtain a customs importer registration from the Customs Division; online sales to foreign consumers are not subject to any RMI tax registration requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

None. RMI law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election mechanism. A non-resident Business Corporation may electively obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate issued by the Ministry of Finance to confirm its exemption from local taxation on foreign-source income; this certificate is evidentiary rather than a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – the Republic of the Marshall Islands does not impose a national VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax. Only customs import duties and selective excise duties on goods such as alcohol, tobacco, and fuel are applicable under RMI customs legislation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; there is no VAT/GST/sales-tax registration threshold in RMI. The national currency is the United States dollar (USD), and no minimum economic turnover threshold triggers an indirect-tax registration. Customs importer registration is required for any importer of physical goods regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because the RMI does not levy VAT or GST on any supply, there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no VAT refund mechanism. Exports from the RMI are outside revenue taxation, and offshore e-commerce supplies of gardening hydroponics equipment by a non-resident Business Corporation are not sourced in the RMI. To substantiate the offshore nature of the transaction and avoid any mischaracterization as RMI-source activity, maintain contracts, commercial invoices, shipping records, delivery confirmation, payment receipts, and the entity's Tax Exemption Certificate where appropriate.

  • Customs export declaration or RMI customs entry form
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/payment receipt evidencing foreign-currency settlement

None. The RMI does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping of invoices. Companies must maintain ordinary books and records sufficient to substantiate transactions; customs import and export entries are lodged through the Customs Division's official declaration process.

The RMI has no VAT/GST zero-rating regime, so no VAT refund clawback exists. Fraudulent customs declarations, false claims of tax-exempt status, or misrepresentations designed to avoid import duty may be audited by the Ministry of Finance and Customs Division, resulting in seizure and forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to twice the duty evaded or the value of the goods, revocation of tax exemption or corporate registration, and criminal fines or imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act and Business Corporations Act. Retrospective reassessment may also apply to any RMI-source activity hidden by misleading declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Unrestricted full convertibility is maintained in both current and capital account items. There are no domestic exchange controls, no foreign exchange licensing requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation for non-resident Business Corporations. The only practical limitations arise from anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-financing of terrorism (CFT) compliance obligations imposed by the Banking Act, the AML/CFT Act, and international correspondent banking standards. Because the jurisdiction is dollarized, there is no separate domestic FX market and conversion risk is limited to counterpart settlement in foreign jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules exist in the Marshall Islands. Exporters and trading companies may retain 100% of export receipts in U.S. dollars in any authorized domestic or foreign bank account. There are no mandated conversion ratios, holding periods, or central bank repatriation deadlines. However, for businesses claiming tax-exempt status under the Non-Resident Business Entities Act, receipts must be documented as foreign-source income and remain traceable through audited financial records to sustain the tax exemption.

Central bank supervision: No independent central bank exists in the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender pursuant to the Compact of Free Association. Banking and financial supervision is exercised by the Marshall Islands Banking Commission under the Ministry of Finance, with prudential oversight delegated to the Office of the Banking Commissioner. Reserve management, monetary policy, and lender-of-last-resort functions are effectively discharged by the U.S. Federal Reserve through dollarization.

Line 4 - Corporate Income Tax Computed Tax calculated under the RMI Income Tax Act on the taxable income of a non-resident corporation (RMI-source income only). If Line 3 is zero, tax due is zero; a non-resident BC with no RMI-source income is not required to file CIT-195 at all - the annual AR-1 declaration of non-resident status is sufficient. Late filing of CIT-195: penalty of 5% per month of the tax due (maximum 25%) plus interest at 1% per month; late filing of GRT-941: penalty of 10% of the tax due plus 1% interest per month. Willful evasion: civil fraud penalty of 50% of underpaid tax, criminal fine up to US$50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Line 5 - Gross Revenue Tax at 3% Quarterly 3% gross revenue tax on the gross revenues of any person doing business in the RMI (GRT-941). A non-resident BC with zero RMI revenues has no GRT registration or filing obligation; registration and quarterly filing become mandatory immediately if the BC later derives RMI-source receipts (for example, a contract with an RMI government agency or local customer). Late filing of CIT-195: penalty of 5% per month of the tax due (maximum 25%) plus interest at 1% per month; late filing of GRT-941: penalty of 10% of the tax due plus 1% interest per month. Willful evasion: civil fraud penalty of 50% of underpaid tax, criminal fine up to US$50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Line 6 - Withholding Tax on Dividends, Interest, and Royalties Reserved line for any RMI withholding tax obligations arising on outbound payments to non-residents. RMI imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid by a non-resident BC; leave blank or enter zero. Late filing of CIT-195: penalty of 5% per month of the tax due (maximum 25%) plus interest at 1% per month; late filing of GRT-941: penalty of 10% of the tax due plus 1% interest per month. Willful evasion: civil fraud penalty of 50% of underpaid tax, criminal fine up to US$50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None in RMI local law. The Marshall Islands does not impose statutory transfer-pricing documentation requirements, and there are no local file or master file thresholds, because non-resident BCs are not subject to RMI corporate income tax. The local statutory currency is the United States Dollar (USD). Where the BC is part of a multinational group whose parent is tax-resident abroad (for example, the United States), the shareholder's TP documentation rules govern (US IRC Section 482 and Section 6662(e) documentation thresholds, Form 5471, and OECD Country-by-Country reporting at the EUR 750 million group-revenue level). The OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines are applied as the best-practice benchmark in any substance or CFC review of the BC.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Economic Substance Regulations (Marshall Islands) 2021 apply to all Business Corporations and other RMI-registered entities carrying on a relevant activity. E-commerce distribution and sale of physical goods (home hydroponics and gardening equipment, ISIC 4791) falls within the 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. If the BC is tax-resident in the RMI, it must demonstrate: (i) physical presence and premises in the RMI, (ii) qualified full-time employees in the RMI, (iii) adequate operating expenditure in the RMI, and (iv) core income-generating activities (CIGA), including inventory purchase, storage, pricing, and contract negotiation, conducted in the RMI. If the BC is tax-resident in a foreign jurisdiction, it must file an annual declaration of that foreign residence with corroborating evidence; otherwise a full ESR substance return is required. Substance failures attract penalties of US$10,000 per infringement, a daily default fine of US$500 for continuing non-compliance, and ultimately strike-off/dissolution; directors and officers face personal fines for false substance declarations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of 50% of any underpaid tax, plus interest at 1% per month; criminal fine up to US$50,000 per offence and imprisonment up to 5 years under the RMI Income Tax Act (Title 54); administrative dissolution of the BC; revocation of the certificate of good standing.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign or approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for the corrected tax, penalties, and interest; criminal conviction triggers automatic disqualification from serving as a director in the RMI; the RMI High Court may issue travel bans and order seizure of personal bank accounts under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2013.
Shell Company / Abusive Economic Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under ESR 2021: first-offence penalty of US$10,000, second-offence penalty of US$100,000, third-offence mandatory strike-off, plus a daily default fine of US$500; under foreign law (for example, US GILTI and CFC rules), the entire e-commerce margin may be recharacterized as US effectively connected income, assessed with full US corporate income tax, a 25% accuracy-related penalty, and statutory interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false ESR declarations are personally liable for ESR penalties and may be barred from RMI board service for 5 years; in the shareholder's home jurisdiction, veil-piercing exposes directors to personal assessments for the foreign tax plus civil fraud penalties (for example, 75% under US IRC Section 6663) and criminal referral for participating in a sham structure.
AML / Payment-Rails Misuse with Stripe and PayPal

Statutory sanction: Under the Marshall Islands Anti-Money Laundering Act 2019 and Proceeds of Crime Act 2013: fines up to US$5,000,000 for the entity per violation, imprisonment for individuals up to 15 years, asset-freezing orders, and confiscation of payment-account balances; Stripe and PayPal additionally impose contractual sanctions, including permanent account revocation and placement on the MATCH terminated-merchant list.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution where knowledge of the conduct is established; payment processors may sue directors personally under indemnity clauses for clawed-back chargeback and fraud losses; the RMI High Court may issue travel bans and worldwide asset-freezing orders pending trial.
Beneficial Ownership Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to US$10,000 plus a daily default fine of US$500 for continuing non-compliance; the Registrar may strike off the BC and refuse to issue good-standing certificates; a struck-off BC loses the legal capacity to operate its Stripe and PayPal accounts or to enforce contracts.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and the registered agent are jointly and severally liable for the fine; convicted directors may be disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years; foreign tax authorities may treat the omission as affirmative evidence of CFC/GILTI non-disclosure, triggering information-return penalties (for example, US$10,000 per form per year plus 75% of the tax attributable to undisclosed items).
Foreign Jurisdiction Look-Through and Consumer-Protection Misclassification

Statutory sanction: The RMI itself imposes no sanction for foreign-market regulatory breaches, but the practical consequence is the nullification of the zero-tax structure: the destination-market revenue authority (for example, US IRS, EU VAT authority, or Australian Taxation Office) may treat the BC as having an effective place of management or permanent establishment in the market, assess full corporate income tax and VAT/GST retrospectively, and impose civil penalties of 25-100% of the unpaid amounts.

Director personal liability: Cross-border veil piercing is highly probable where the BC lacks RMI substance: foreign courts will impose joint and several personal liability on directors for unpaid taxes and statutory penalties of the market jurisdiction (for example, the 100% Trust Fund Recovery Penalty under US IRC Section 6672 for withholding-type obligations); directors may face economic sanctions, travel bans, and mutual legal assistance requests leading to extradition in fraud-adjacent cases.

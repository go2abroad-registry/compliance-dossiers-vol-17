Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance (Treasury Division) and RMI Customs Service; corporate registration and annual reporting administered by the Registrar of Corporations, Office of the Attorney General, via the national corporate registry portal. No dedicated income-tax authority or taxpayer portal exists because the RMI levies no national income tax.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities first incorporate as Business Corporations under the Business Corporation Act, obtain a Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1996, and secure a business license under the Business License Act from the relevant local authority. Because the RMI has no income tax or VAT, no TIN or VAT ID is issued; the licensed entity's identification numbers are the corporate registration number and the FIBL license number. Employers maintaining payroll in the RMI must register separately with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities first incorporate as Business Corporations under the Business Corporation Act, obtain a Foreign Investment Business License under the Foreign Investment Business Act 1996, and secure a business license under the Business License Act from the relevant local authority. Because the RMI has no income tax or VAT, no TIN or VAT ID is issued; the licensed entity's identification numbers are the corporate registration number and the FIBL license number. Employers maintaining payroll in the RMI must register separately with the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in RMI law. A non-resident Business Corporation may elect and maintain tax-exempt status by restricting all business activity and income to sources outside the Marshall Islands; such a corporation is exempt from the RMI 3% gross revenue (replenishment) tax and all other RMI income-type taxes. There are no hybrid mismatch rules in force; prospective Pillar Two global minimum tax top-up rules for in-scope multinational groups, if enacted, may affect the election analysis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales tax; the RMI imposes customs import duties and excise duties on selected goods, administered by the RMI Customs Service under the Customs Act.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT registration threshold exists. Import duty liability is triggered per importation through the RMI Customs Service; an import permit or customs broker authorization is required, with no economic turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — the RMI has no VAT regime, so no export VAT zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists. Exports of goods from the RMI are outside the scope of RMI indirect taxation; customs export documentation is required only for trade statistics, prohibited-goods control, and compliance with counterparty country formalities.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration / Export Entry
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign Investment Business License or Business License copy confirming export authority
  • Certificate of Origin or inward remittance evidence where required by the destination jurisdiction

Not applicable — no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API filing, or digital stamping mandate exists. Customs entries are filed with the RMI Customs Service on prescribed forms; documentary examination and physical verification apply.

No VAT or export zero-rating clawback regime exists given the absence of an indirect tax. False customs declarations, smuggling, or unauthorized exports are punishable under the RMI Customs Act through fines, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment. Misrepresentations in obtaining a Foreign Investment Business License may result in license revocation, monetary penalties, and director liability. A non-resident corporation found to be earning RMI-source revenue may be reclassified as resident and subjected to the 3% gross revenue tax, plus interest, penalties, and potential retrospective assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full free convertibility with no exchange-control overlay: since the domestic legal currency is the U.S. dollar, both current account and capital account transactions are executed directly in USD. There are no exchange-control admissions, approvals, or reporting thresholds for dividend or profit remittance, no non-resident convertibility restrictions, and no repatriation tax on distributions from an exempt non-resident Business Corporation. The practical constraint is not statutory but institutional: U.S. correspondent banks and clearing networks apply extraterritorial OFAC/FATF scrutiny, and any payment corridor touching PRC Yuan requires enhanced trade documentation to avoid de-risking.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender regime exists. The Marshall Islands imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or liquidate foreign-derived receipts; all export proceeds are received in USD and may be retained in any jurisdiction, settled through U.S. correspondent clearing, or deployed for offshore franchise working capital without central bank intervention.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands operates without a domestic central bank; monetary and banking supervisory authority rests with the Banking Commissioner of the Banking Commission under the Republic of the Marshall Islands Ministry of Finance, while the U.S. Federal Reserve functions as the de facto international currency authority because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. The Marshall Islands lacks a domestic e-money-institution licensing law and the Banking Commission will not charter a fintech EMI for non-resident treasury purposes; offshore EMIs chartered under FCA/MiCA regulatory frameworks are legally viable because there is no exchange-control prohibition on an RMI entity holding foreign e-money, but most Tier-1 European and Asian EMI issuers reject RMI-registered business corporations due to reputational concerns and the absence of a domestic authority that can issue comfort letters for money transmission. A pragmatic workaround is to overlay a U.S. state-licensed money transmitter or a U.S. chartered neobank that clears through a commercial bank sponsor, maintaining the RMI BC as the treasury and franchise royalty collection vehicle.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

3% on Marshall Islands-source taxable income (54 MIRC Ch.1, Income Tax Act 1988); the designated Non-Resident Domestic Corporation (NRDC) is exempt on all foreign-source income, yielding a 0% effective rate on the overseas bakery franchise royalty and central-kitchen fee stream

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the Republic imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Business Corporation, including an NRDC, to foreign shareholders; no deemed-dividend or distributed-profits tax exists

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Marshall Islands transfer-pricing statute and no Local File or Master File threshold in the local currency (USD is the official currency). Practical monitoring benchmark: annual intercompany royalty flow above USD 1,000,000, or a royalty rate above 10% of master-franchisee gross sales, will trigger the operating country's mandatory TP documentation and POEM/CFC examination

Economic substance scope: The Republic has no general Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to the BVI/Cayman regime; substance is enforced indirectly through the NRDC annual declaration that no business is carried on in the Republic. Under OECD FHTP classification the Republic is 'no or only nominal tax', so the operative substance test is applied by the operating jurisdiction: the BC must demonstrate its own board management, registered trademark and recipe IP ownership, central-kitchen approval protocols, and franchisee KYC/contract files; absent these, royalty income is recharacterized as taxable management or service income

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Royalty Scheme

Statutory sanction: Operating jurisdiction (Hong Kong, Singapore, China) disregards the BC as a sham; royalty deductions disallowed and the BC taxed at 16.5-25% for all open years; penalties of 100-300% of undercharged tax; China assesses 10% WHT and EU blacklist countermeasures impose a 15-20% WHT default at EU payors

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for the corporate underpayment; Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82A provides 3 years' imprisonment plus a 3x tax penalty; the Republic enforces foreign tax judgments under Mutual Assistance law and may impose travel bans on directors
Shell Company / False NRDC Declaration

Statutory sanction: In the Republic: USD 25,000 fine per false declaration and charter revocation under BCA s.149; in the foreign market: the BC is tax-resident under POEM rules, taxed on worldwide income for a 6-year window plus a 50% accuracy penalty

Director personal liability: Signing directors commit a BCA s.149 offense punishable by USD 25,000 fine and 12 months' imprisonment; foreign tax authorities may arrest directors for tax evasion while traveling; a Hong Kong false statutory declaration carries up to 2 years' imprisonment
Unlicensed Trading / Domestic Business in the Republic

Statutory sanction: Business License Act fine of USD 100 per day; Revenue retroactively taxes all Republic-source gross revenue at 3% for up to 5 years with a 50% civil penalty; corporate charter may be forfeited

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid license fees and penalties; the Attorney General may freeze BC assets and impose domicile restrictions on the managing director
AML/CFT Failure in the Franchise Payment Rail

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2016 (as amended): corporate fine up to USD 250,000 per violation; freezing of correspondent banking; revocation of the registered agent's license; grey-list status subjects every inbound franchisee payment to bank de-risking

Director personal liability: A knowing director commits a criminal offense punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment and USD 100,000 personal fine; travel bans are routinely granted under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act in aid of foreign AML investigations
Failure to File Annual Report and Franchise Tax

Statutory sanction: 10% late surcharge; strike-off after 60 days; a struck-off BC cannot sue defaulting master franchisees for unpaid royalties, its contracts are unenforceable until reinstatement, and any Republic asset escheats to the state

Director personal liability: A director transacting business on behalf of a dissolved BC is personally liable for those obligations; reinstatement requires a court order and sworn affidavit; overseas directors face wrongful-trading claims in the operating country

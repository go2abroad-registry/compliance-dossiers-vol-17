Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands — Tax Division (income tax), Customs Division (import duty and trade), and Business License Division (gross revenue business licensing); official administrative portal: finance.govmh.net; tax returns and registrations are processed through the Ministry of Finance in Majuro or by mail/designated agents (no fully digital TIN issuance portal).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Confirm non-resident classification under the Income Tax Act 1989 (as amended): a Business Corporation (BC) organized under the Marshall Islands Business Corporations Act is a 'domestic corporation' taxable only on income derived from sources within the Republic; a BC that derives no RMI-source income is tax-exempt. Step 2 — No income-tax TIN is automatically required for a non-resident entity; obtain a Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing from the Registrar of Corporations. Step 3 — If physical importation into the Republic is conducted, register with the Customs Division as Importer of Record (Customs Importer ID / tariff account) using Form CI-1 and a Security/Bond, and, if conducting a place of business or selling within the Republic, obtain an annual Business License from the Business License Division of the Ministry of Finance. Step 4 — If any RMI payroll or local branch arises, register for the 3% Wage and Salary Withholding Tax Account and employer reporting. Step 5 — The RMI does not operate a VAT/TIN system; a taxpayer Master File number is assigned only upon first income-tax filing or business license application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The RMI has no check-the-box pass-through election (no US Subchapter S analogue) and does not implement hybrid mismatch or CFC/GILTI elections. The available statutory elections are: (a) Non-Resident Foreign-Source Income Affidavit — file an annual declaration (Form NRA/FSI) affirming that no income was derived from sources within the Republic, preserving the complete tax exemption of the non-resident BC; (b) Deemed Non-Resident Status Election — for a BC incorporated in the Republic but managed and controlled outside the Republic, elect treatment as a non-resident corporation so that only RMI-source income (and RMI foreign-investment income) enters the tax base; (c) Customs Re-Export / Duty Drawback Election — elect bonded warehouse admission with a re-export bond to suspend import duties on frozen beef/seafood until physical re-export; and (d) Business License Gross Revenue Band Election — select the applicable license category and revenue band for computing the annual business license fee. No joint return, consolidated return, or fiscal unity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_or_GST_Regime (Republic of the Marshall Islands imposes no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general sales tax; the sole fiscal frontier levy is the Customs Import Duty and specific/excise duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1990, with exports fully outside the tax base)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — absence of a VAT/GST means no statutory indirect-tax registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, every importer of record (whether resident or non-resident) must obtain an Importer ID irrespective of shipment value; for local trading activities, a Business License under the Business License Act 2021 is required once any revenue-generating presence or sale occurs in the Republic (fee bands in USD; the RMI is fully dollarized and maintains no local-currency threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the RMI has no VAT/GST, all export transactions of goods (including frozen beef and pelagic fish carcasses) are inherently outside the indirect tax base — no export VAT charge arises and no zero-rating certificate is required. For customs-duty purposes, exports are duty-free; an exporter must (i) hold a valid exporter registration/Business License if operating in the Republic, (ii) lodge an ASYCUDA export declaration prior to departure, (iii) evidence physical departure by bill of lading or air waybill, and (iv) for duty-drawback/re-export relief, demonstrate that imported cold-chain goods left the Republic within the bonded warehouse or temporary admission period (typically 12 months) or were destroyed under Customs supervision. Services performed outside the Republic by the non-resident BC qualify as foreign-source income, a further zero-tax layer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World lodgment)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing physical departure from RMI
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (if required by destination market)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank Swift-TT or LC settlement proof

No real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, API e-invoicing, SAF-T, or continuous transaction control regime is in force. Electronic import/export data exchange is handled through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform of the Customs Division, which requires structured manifest, entry, and duty computation data but does not validate commercial invoices through a national fiscal engine. Paper commercial invoices, bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin, and (where demanded by the bank) letter-of-credit conforming documents are sufficient.

The RMI has no VAT refund mechanism to abuse, so audit exposure concentrates on customs duty avoidance and territorial-source mischaracterization. Customs re-export/drawback claims are scrutinized by the Customs Division; false or incomplete export evidence triggers reassessment of suspended duties plus interest at 1.5% per month, an administrative surcharge of up to 25%, forfeiture of the goods or their value, and fines of up to 200% of the duty evaded. On the income-tax side, a non-resident BC that falsely files the foreign-source income affidavit, or conceals RMI-source income, faces underpayment penalties of up to 50% of the deficiency, statutory interest, loss of the Tax_Exempt_Zone status, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud on the first intentional violation. The 180-day repatriation reference is a documentary Letter of Credit/collection settlement norm, not a statutory deadline: the RMI is fully dollarized and imposes no exchange controls or mandatory inward remittance time limit, so no clawback penalty attaches to late FX repatriation; audits may be opened administratively within 3 years, extending to 7 years where fraud or non-disclosure is established.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The U.S. dollar is formal legal tender and the RMI imposes no foreign-exchange control regime; both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without ministerial or central-bank approval, and there are no statutory restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or liquidation remittances. The operative constraint is not legal convertibility but correspondent-bank de-risking sentiment, as the RMI banking sector clusters around a small number of U.S. dollar clearing correspondents; upstream clearing banks may delay or reject payments triggering sanctions or AML red flags even where local law permits the transfer. For the frozen-beef and seafood import cycle, profit remittance clearance therefore requires a complete trade-documentation bundle (commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and customs declaration) to preempt upstream compliance review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in the RMI. Exporters and importing intermediaries are freely permitted to retain, convert, or disburse U.S. dollar receipts with no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed timeline for repatriation into the domestic banking system. The absence of surrender rules derives directly from official dollarization, under which the RMI holds no autonomous monetary reserves and cannot require repatriation of foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) does not maintain a central bank. Banking regulation is administered by the Office of the Banking Commissioner under the Marshall Islands Banking Act 1997, as amended, while anti-money-laundering supervision is co-led by the Marshall Islands Financial Intelligence Unit under the National Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Prevention) Act 2002, as amended. Because the U.S. dollar is the sole official legal tender under the Compact of Free Association, currency issuance is delegated to the United States Federal Reserve and no RMI institution exercises independent monetary or exchange-rate policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. The RMI has no statutory licensing regime for electronic-money institutions, payment service providers, or non-bank fintech EMI platforms, and the Office of the Banking Commissioner has issued no sandbox or payment-institution authorization that would permit a digital EMI to hold a domestic settlement account. Offshore EMI accounts (e.g., UK FCA- or EU-licensed platforms) are legally accessible from an RMI entity, but upstream correspondent banks frequently decline to serve Marshall Islands-incorporated clients because of residual APG and FATF mutual-evaluation concerns; therefore an offshore EMI is acceptable only as a contingency fallback, never as the primary clearing rail. A viable fintech solution must present a U.S. dollar omnibus settlement account held at a top-tier U.S. or EU clearing bank and must accept full trade-documentation inspection before the first frozen-commodity import disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial shareholder capital contribution and subsequent equity or loan injections is executed exclusively through the U.S. dollar clearing system via SWIFT MT103. The RMI does not administer a domestic purpose-code taxonomy; remitters should therefore adopt the U.S. Treasury FEDWIRE transaction code or a SWIFT Category 103 narrative stating 'Equity Injection / Capital Contribution / Shareholder Loan for Import Working Capital.' Verification documents must include a board resolution authorizing the injection, the recipient bank statement, and the registered-agent share register confirming issuance to the non-resident parent. No promissory note is required for equity; a shareholder loan must be evidenced by a signed intercompany loan agreement to prevent tax reclassification of the capital.Trade settlements for frozen beef and seafood imports are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for open-account transactions and through irrevocable documentary letters of credit for documentary transactions. For letter-of-credit routing, the RMI domestic bank issues through its U.S. correspondent (commonly a top-50 U.S. money-center bank) governed by UCP 600; the instrument may be structured as a revolving LC drawn at 90 days after sight, with presentation of a marine bill of lading, certificate of origin, sanitary-phytosanitary certificate, commercial invoice, and required shipping documents. MT103 open-account payments must be preceded by a wire-mandate instruction identifying the beneficiary bank and a precise remittance narrative referencing the purchase order number, container or seal number, and the freight forwarder's house bill of lading.Since the RMI is officially dollarized, no domestic FX market exists for USD/MHV and no local regulator requires hedging of import exposures; cross-currency hedging for AUD, NZD, EUR, JPY, or CNY counterparties is permissible only as an offshore OTC derivative executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a licensed non-RMI dealer.Permissible instruments are limited to plain-vanilla FX forwards, non-deliverable forwards settling in USD, and cross-currency swaps supporting underlying commercial import payables; options and exotic structures require additional bank product approval and are not recommended for the cold-chain working-capital cycle.Marshall Islands banking law does not prohibit FX hedging, but the foreign dealer must be a member of a recognized swap execution facility and the hedge must be documented as risk management (not speculation), with the underlying trade invoice attached, to avoid adverse tax or regulatory characterization.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% net corporate income tax; the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) imposes no tax on the net corporate income or capital gains of a non-resident domestic Business Corporation (BC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by an RMI non-resident domestic corporation to non-resident shareholders. No RMI tax is assessed on dividend declarations, payments, or distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Report is due on or before the anniversary of the filing of the Articles of Incorporation. RMI Revenue and Customs imposes no income tax filing deadline because no income tax return is legally due.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Where applicable, monthly gross revenue returns are due by the statutory filing date prescribed by RMI Revenue and Customs, usually the 15th day of the following month. Non-resident BCs trading exclusively offshore are outside this filing obligation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 0 / N/A. The RMI has no local corporate income tax and therefore no statutory Local File or Master File threshold. A non-resident BC must nonetheless monitor the transfer pricing documentation thresholds of the foreign jurisdiction where the frozen meat/seafood trading counterparty, lender, or beneficial owner is tax resident.

Economic substance scope: The Marshall Islands Economic Substance Regulations require registered legal entities carrying on any geographically mobile relevant activity within RMI to demonstrate adequate physical presence, local employees, local management, and sufficient expenditure. Relevant activities include distribution and service centres, financing, headquarters, intellectual property, shipping, and pure holding company operations. A non-resident BC that merely invoices offshore cold-chain purchases and has no RMI office, staff, or decision-making function has no RMI-relevant activity. However, foreign tax authorities may re-characterize the arrangement as a shell and impose CFC income inclusion, permanent establishment assessment, or deny cost deductions unless real economic substance is demonstrated in the market jurisdiction where purchasing and logistics are managed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under RMI criminal law and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2013, fraudulent tax-related conduct is punishable by substantial court-imposed fines and imprisonment. The foreign shareholder's jurisdiction may additionally impose CFC income inclusion, 100% accuracy-related penalties, and criminal prosecution for willful failure to report foreign financial assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where directors knowingly use the BC as an instrument of fraud. Directors may be held personally and jointly liable for unpaid foreign taxes and civil restitution, may face travel bans under mutual legal assistance proceedings, and may be extradited for tax fraud if the arrangement is shown to be criminal.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The RMI Registrar may refuse a Certificate of Good Standing, strike the company from the registry, and exchange beneficial ownership information with foreign competent authorities. Foreign tax authorities may disregard the RMI entity for tax purposes, deny deduction of intercompany payments, and impose transfer pricing adjustments plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to operate as a shell for abusive tax purposes risk personal liability under fraudulent conveyance law and equitable compensation orders. Travel bans and asset freezing orders may be issued in the foreign jurisdiction where the underlying trade actually occurs.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism legislation, non-compliance with customer due diligence, beneficial ownership recording, and record-keeping obligations attracts monetary penalties, regulatory suspension, and criminal prosecution. Financial institutions may block the LC/SWIFT payment channels if KYC documentation is inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly execute or facilitate payments without proper AML verification are personally liable for civil fines and may be disqualified from serving as directors. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment, and travel restrictions can be imposed pending extradition or asset recovery proceedings.
Fraudulent Trading and Wrongful Dividends

Statutory sanction: Under RMI corporate law and applicable common law principles adopted from the Delaware-inspired Business Corporations Act, fraudulent trading and wrongful distributions may render the corporation liable to winding-up proceedings. Directors may be ordered to restore funds, pay compensation, or contribute to the company's assets; criminal sanctions may be imposed for fraudulent preferences.

Director personal liability: The limited liability shield is lost where directors act in bad faith or recklessly. Directors and officers may be held personally liable for creditor losses, disqualified from managing any RMI entity, and subjected to court-imposed travel bans or injunctions while insolvency proceedings are pending.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; central administrative portal is MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign entity must first register with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the national business registration identification number (matricule). Thereafter, the entity files a VAT registration application with the AEDT via the same portal. Once processed, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. Non-EU established foreign entities may be required to appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT purposes. All tax IDs are administered electronically; no physical presence is prerequisite for obtaining the TIN.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity must first register with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the national business registration identification number (matricule). Thereafter, the entity files a VAT registration application with the AEDT via the same portal. Once processed, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. Non-EU established foreign entities may be required to appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT purposes. All tax IDs are administered electronically; no physical presence is prerequisite for obtaining the TIN.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
An SCSp (Société en Commandite Spéciale) is generally fiscally transparent under Luxembourg tax law unless it validly elects to be treated as a taxable entity for corporation income tax purposes (option pour le régime des sociétés). Additional elections may apply under Luxembourg's implementation of EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, enabling alignment of tax characterization with foreign jurisdictions. The partnership agreement can also contain internal structures supporting transparent or opaque treatment, subject to anti-abuse review.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
17%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Luxembourg-established small businesses may benefit from the €35,000 annual turnover exemption (excluding VAT) and thus avoid mandatory registration until this threshold is exceeded. Non-established foreign suppliers are required to register from their first taxable transaction in Luxembourg, irrespective of turnover. Cross-border B2C distance sales of goods become subject to Luxembourg VAT when the supplier's EU-wide distance sales exceed €10,000 annually.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided the goods physically leave Luxembourg/EU territory within the statutory period (generally 3 months for exports; evidence of removal must be retained). Intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number also qualify for 0% where transport to another EU member state is proven. For executive search and headhunting services supplied to a non-resident business customer, the place of supply is the customer's country, thereby falling outside Luxembourg VAT (not strictly zero-rating but effectively unprotected from Luxembourg VAT). All zero-rating claims require substantive documentary proof and commercial substance.

Luxembourg mandates electronic invoicing for B2G transactions through the PEPPOL network under the eDPI law. There is no general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control obligation for B2B invoices. For private sector invoices, submission is not clearance-based; invoices must contain the VAT-compliant data fields. E-invoicing may also be used voluntarily in addition to standard paper or PDF invoicing.

      
Luxembourg's VAT authority conducts risk-based audits focusing on export substantiation, particularly for service exporters and high-volume goods exporters. Missing or inadequate evidence may result in retrospective VAT reassessment at the standard rate, denial of refunds, and clawback of previously zero-rated outputs. Administrative penalties for false declarations or unjustified zero-rating can exceed €100,000 and reach up to 10% of the tax evaded; serious cases attract criminal sanctions and personal liability of directors or representatives. Default interest accrues monthly on unremitted VAT until full repayment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Luxembourg uses the euro and maintains zero exchange controls; Article 63 TFEU secures free movement of capital within the EU and vis-à-vis third countries, so current-account retainer fees and capital-account contributions to an SCSp are fully convertible and remittable without prior state authorisation. In practice, the counterparty bank applies its own sanction/KYC permissioning; the tax-transparent SCSp passes profits to partners with no withholding for EU-resident partners and reduced rates under Luxembourg double-tax treaties.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory surrender, retention period, or conversion ratio applies to export or retainer proceeds in Luxembourg. Foreign-currency headhunting fees may remain as USD/GBP balances; the only administrative duty is indirect: the custodial bank files BCL balance-of-payments statistics for the SCSp's cross-border direct investment and service flows, so the treasury should supply invoice and capital-call references to classify each credit.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), Eurosystem member; banking, payment institution, and e-money institution prudential supervision and AML/CFT oversight are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High

Legal constraints: EMI balances are safeguarded rather than guaranteed by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee fund (FGDL), EMIs cannot extend credit, and the SCSp must appoint its general partner (gérant) or a mandated manager to sign the EMI mandate; EUR payments above EUR 100,000 should be settled through the principal Luxembourg bank via TARGET2 rather than through the EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where intra-group services exceed EUR 150,000, goods transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000, financial transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000, or intangible transactions exceed EUR 500,000. Master File required for a multinational group with consolidated turnover above EUR 50,000,000 and local intragroup operations above the de minimis thresholds.

      
Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD/Aligned substance requirements. An SCSp must maintain real office space, operational staff, board or partner-level decision-making, and actual headhunting activity in Luxembourg to avoid challenge under ATAD, the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule, and treaty limitation-on-benefits clauses. Shell-company arrangements are rebutted by the tax administration and may be disregarded.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, tax evasion is punishable by fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Aggravated fiscal fraud (e.g. forgery, use of offshore shell accounts) raises the maximum penalty to EUR 1,000,000 and 10 years' imprisonment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and the unlimited-liability general partner may be held personally liable for unpaid tax, social security contributions, and conviction-secured civil damages. Travel bans, seizure of assets, and extradition arrangements can apply in cross-border cases.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, and imposition of transfer pricing adjustments. The Luxembourg tax authority may levy a fine of up to 10% of the adjusted taxable base and apply interest at 7% per annum.

        
Director personal liability: The general partner is personally and unlimitedly liable for the resulting corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Directors risk disqualification orders and revocation of business permits through the Luxembourg courts.

      

      
      

        AML / KYC Violation
        
Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) imposes administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for serious breaches; criminal money laundering penalties reach 5 years' imprisonment and fines of up to EUR 250,000 per offence.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be sentenced to imprisonment, subject to travel bans, and be ordered to repay laundered amounts. The court may also prohibit them from managing any Luxembourg commercial entity for up to 10 years.

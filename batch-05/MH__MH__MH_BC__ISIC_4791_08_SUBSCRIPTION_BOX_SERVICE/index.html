Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A non-resident Business Corporation is required to maintain a registered office at the physical street address of its licensed resident registered agent in Majuro, Republic of the Marshall Islands (MIRS Title 51, Chapter 3, Business Corporations Act). A virtual office arrangement is acceptable in substance when the registered agent's physical office serves as the sole service-of-process address and all statutory records, including the beneficial ownership register, are physically available there. However, a virtual office may not be used to conceal the true business activities, and the registered agent must maintain suitable physical infrastructure to accept service of process and governmental correspondence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) Ministry of Finance, Revenue and Taxation Division, Majuro (direct tax administration under the Income Tax Act 1989, Title 54 MIRC); Office of the Registrar of Corporations (Business Corporations Act, Title 30 MIRC) for entity registration and NRDC status; RMI Customs Service (Ministry of Finance) for border duties via ASYCUDA World. No consolidated national tax portal exists; returns and license applications are filed in paper form with the Revenue and Taxation Division.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Business Corporation (NRDC) engaging in no RMI trade or business and deriving no RMI-source revenue is not required to obtain an RMI TIN, business license, or gross revenue tax (GRT) registration; it obtains a corporate registration number and pays the statutory annual registry fee. If the entity later conducts business in the RMI, it must (1) obtain a Business License under the Business License Act 1989, (2) register with the Revenue and Taxation Division to obtain a TIN, and (3) file and pay the 3% GRT on RMI-source gross revenue. For any import/export movement, an importer/exporter registration number must be obtained from the RMI Customs Service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (statutory NRDC exemption from the 3% Gross Revenue Tax; RMI levies no income tax, capital gains tax, or net-worth tax, and its direct tax regime is territorial/source-based only)

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election and no hybrid-mismatch election exist under RMI law. The operative election is the statutory 'non-resident domestic corporation' (NRDC) designation made in the Articles of Incorporation under the Business Corporations Act; continued exemption requires maintaining the statutory non-resident conditions (no RMI business activity, no RMI-resident shareholders, no RMI real property).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None. RMI has no value added tax, goods and services tax, or sales/use tax at any level of government; the only indirect-style national levy is customs import duty under the RMI Customs Service Act 1993. The 3% GRT is a direct tax on gross revenue derived from business conducted in the RMI, not an indirect tax. Under the described subscription-box model (offshore entity selling to non-RMI customers via Stripe/Authorize.Net, no RMI inventory or delivery), no RMI indirect-tax nexus arises.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST regime exists, hence no turnover threshold for indirect-tax registration. GRT and business-license obligations attach from the first dollar of revenue when a person is deemed to conduct business in the RMI; the RMI statutory currency is the United States dollar (USD).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because RMI has no VAT/GST regime, there is no zero-rating (0%) mechanism for exported goods or offshore services, no input-VAT credit, and no export refund claim. RMI generally imposes no export duties; a customs export declaration serves statistical and control purposes only. No special export-incentive or free-zone tax regime is available to NRDCs under RMI law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) — required only where goods are physically exported from RMI territory; not a VAT zero-rating precondition
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — carrier transport document for physical export movements; not a tax refund support document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — not a recognized RMI statutory document; the RMI has no exchange controls and uses the USD as its domestic currency, so no bank-issued inward remittance certificate is required for export proceeds

None. RMI imposes no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API transmission, or digital stamping mandate for commercial invoices. The only electronic clearance channel is ASYCUDA World, which processes customs declarations (import and export) for the RMI Customs Service; GRT returns and business-license applications remain paper-based.

No export-VAT refund exists, so no refund clawback can arise. General RMI fiscal enforcement applies: false or fraudulent returns under the Income Tax Act 1989 (Title 54 MIRC) attract civil penalties, interest on unpaid GRT, and criminal fines; false customs declarations under the RMI Customs Service Act 1993 expose the importer/exporter to goods forfeiture and monetary penalties. Breach of the NRDC conditions is revocable retroactively, making the entity liable for GRT on RMI-source revenue for open years plus statutory penalties. The 180-day figure is a non-statutory commercial benchmark; RMI law imposes no FX repatriation deadline or exchange-control restriction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Marshall Islands has no domestic currency or exchange control restrictions. Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible, and profit remittance or dividend distribution is freely transferable in U.S. Dollars subject only to standard corporate authorization documentation (board resolutions, audited financial statements where required) and anti-money laundering verification by the receiving correspondent bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Marshall Islands imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. Export earnings may be retained in foreign currency accounts offshore or deposited locally in U.S. Dollar accounts without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Republic of the Marshall Islands Banking Commission (Financial Supervisory Authority) under the Ministry of Finance; no independent central bank is constituted, and the U.S. Dollar is adopted as the official legal tender.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium. The Marshall Islands does not maintain a domestic electronic money institution (EMI) license; therefore a locally approved fintech/EMI is not available. Offshore digital EMIs or third-party payment processors (e.g., Stripe, Authorize.Net) may be used as commercial billing rails, but bank account opening and treasury clearing must be executed through a licensed international bank or a licensed local bank. Any non-deposit-taking EMI seeking to settle through correspondent accounts must demonstrate FATF-compliant licensing and comply with the receiving bank's AML/KYC due diligence.

0% - no withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid by a Marshall Islands Business Corporation to resident or non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - the Marshall Islands Income Tax Act does not enact arm's-length transfer pricing rules, local/master-file thresholds, or contemporaneous documentation obligations; USD is the local currency. Tax-exempt non-resident BCs have no TP compliance duty, though intercompany transactions must be commercially genuine to withstand home-jurisdiction CFC and substance challenges.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended) apply only to non-resident domestic corporations conducting 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, maritime shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centre, IP holding, and pure holding company operations). ISIC 4791 subscription-box retail is generally outside scope unless the entity is characterized as a 'distribution and service centre', in which case the BC must file an annual substance notification, keep all relevant books and records within the Republic, conduct core income-generating activities there, and demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and expenditure; penalties attach to non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion at the beneficial-owner level

Statutory sanction: RMI: certificate revocation and TIEA-based information exchange; home jurisdictions: back taxes, accuracy or fraud penalties (e.g., US IRC Section 6663 fraud penalty of 75%), and compound interest.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution for unpaid taxes, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for tax fraud (e.g., US IRC Sections 7201/7206 - up to 5 years per count).
Sham shell / ESR non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Registrar penalties for each failure, daily penalties for continued non-compliance, loss of tax-exemption certification, and administrative strike-off of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations incur personal fines and disqualification; trading after strike-off creates personal liability for all post-dissolution debts.
AML / OFAC sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to USD 2,000,000 per violation under the Anti-Money Laundering Act 2011 (as amended), asset forfeiture, and mandatory suspicious-transaction reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment (commonly up to 10-15 years), personal monetary penalties, and extradition for directors knowingly engaged in laundering subscription-fraud proceeds.
Defective corporate maintenance

Statutory sanction: Registrar's notice and revocation of the certificate of incorporation; reinstatement requires settlement of all arrears, penalties, and a reinstatement fee.

Director personal liability: After revocation, officers and directors purporting to contract on behalf of the BC are jointly and severally liable on those contracts; unlawful distributions are clawed back.
Fraudulent misrepresentation in statutory filings

Statutory sanction: Misdemeanor prosecution under the BCA, criminal fines, Registrar discretionary strike-off, and unlimited limitation periods for fraud by concealment.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal fines, civil damages to creditors and the Registrar, and travel restrictions when mutual legal assistance or extradition is sought.

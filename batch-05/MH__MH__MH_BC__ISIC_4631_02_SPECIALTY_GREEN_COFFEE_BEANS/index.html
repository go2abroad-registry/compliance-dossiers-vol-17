Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Revenue and Taxation Office (RTO), Ministry of Finance, Republic of the Marshall Islands; no national e-filing portal exists—statutory forms are obtained from and filed with the RTO in Majuro, with Treasury and licensing functions coordinated through the Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign owner forms a Marshall Islands Business Corporation under Title 17 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (Business Corporations Act). The registered agent files Form TIN-01 with the Revenue and Taxation Office, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent consent, and beneficial ownership declaration. The RTO issues a Tax Identification Number; however, a non-resident Business Corporation deriving no income from sources within the Republic is not required to file an income tax return and may remain entirely tax-exempt for RMI purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign owner forms a Marshall Islands Business Corporation under Title 17 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code (Business Corporations Act). The registered agent files Form TIN-01 with the Revenue and Taxation Office, attaching the certificate of incorporation, registered agent consent, and beneficial ownership declaration. The RTO issues a Tax Identification Number; however, a non-resident Business Corporation deriving no income from sources within the Republic is not required to file an income tax return and may remain entirely tax-exempt for RMI purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Marshall Islands tax law. A Business Corporation may elect non-resident status by confining its activities to business outside the Republic, thereby avoiding RMI-source income; the Business Corporations Act also permits foreign corporations to continue into the RMI as domestic corporations and domestic corporations to redomesticate abroad.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales/use tax; indirect taxation is limited to Marshall Islands customs import duty, excise, and port/health fees, applicable only to goods entering the Republic for domestic consumption.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (USD jurisdiction); there is no VAT/GST registration threshold. A business license is required only if the Business Corporation operates a physical commercial presence inside the Republic, while import duty is assessed per customs entry on goods entered for home use. Exported or transshipped cargo is outside the indirect tax nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept because the Republic imposes no value-added tax. For customs purposes, specialty green coffee beans (HS 0901.11) exported from an RMI port or moved in transit/transshipment and not entered for domestic consumption are outside the scope of RMI import duty and are treated as non-taxable exports.

  • RMI Customs Export Declaration/Transshipment Declaration for containerized cargo
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (master and house, if applicable) evidencing loading at an RMI port and discharge at a foreign port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying HS 0901.11 specialty green arabica coffee, quantity, and FOB value
  • Phytosanitary certificate and certificate of origin for specialty green coffee, where required by the importing customs administration

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in the Republic. RMI Customs accepts paper declarations and digital cargo manifests; documentary evidence is the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs export/transshipment declaration rather than a fiscally cleared e-invoice.

No VAT refund or export zero-rating regime exists, so no VAT clawback penalties are applicable. Fraudulent use of export or transshipment status to avoid customs import duty is subject to RMI Customs scrutiny: misdeclared or unlawfully diverted goods may be seized and forfeited, duties become retrospectively payable at the full applicable rate, and enhanced civil penalties or criminal liability may be imposed on authorized persons filing false customs documents.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMI operates a fully dollarized economy; there is no separate domestic currency, no central-bank-managed convertibility distinction between capital and current account transactions, and hence no formal exchange-control restriction on profit remittance. Capital, trade, and dividend flows are fully convertible in USD, but remain subject to commercial bank AML/CFT clearance, the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctions regime, and RMI corporate documentation requirements. Profit remittances from a non-resident Business Corporation to overseas shareholders are structurally legal and only require proof of corporate validity, director consent, tax/exemption status, and compliance with the Banking Commission's Beneficial Ownership Register Rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The RMI imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or stipulated repatriation timeline because all export receipts are denominated in the domestic currency-equivalent, the U.S. dollar, and the country has no central exchange-control apparatus. Export proceeds of specialty green coffee bean sales may be retained freely in RMI bank accounts or settled directly via SWIFT; however, documentary retention obligations under the RMI Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism Act require firms to retain underlying contracts, invoices, bills of lading, weight certificates, and shipment origin evidence for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Banking Commission of the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI) in coordination with the Ministry of Finance; the RMI does not maintain a sovereign central bank, does not issue domestic currency, and adopts the United States dollar as legal tender, with monetary stability assured by the U.S. Federal Reserve.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for an RMI-domiciled non-resident business corporation. The RMI Banking Act does not recognize a separate 'electronic money institution' license; providers of payment or e-money services must either be licensed banks or hold a specialized money service business approval from the Banking Commission. Offshore digital EMIs are generally not viable as primary treasury clearing rails because RMI licensed banks maintain strict anti-de-risking policies and often reject offshore EMI channels for trade settlement, documentary credits, and escrow arrangements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed as SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire USD transfers into the BOMI capital escrow account, with each wire referencing the RMI corporate entity's exact legal name, account number, and a clear payment narrative indicating 'share capital contribution', 'paid-in capital', or 'capital subscription'. Supporting verification must include bank reference letters, source-of-funds questionnaire, board capital call resolution, certificate of incumbency, and proof of beneficiary chain. The receiving bank will require compliance with the RMI Banking Commission AML rules and U.S. sanctions screening; no exchange conversion is required as USD is already the nominal domestic currency.Outbound trade disbursements for green coffee bean settlement will typically clear over SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier/consignee payments, and SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit where a sight/term LC assures quality inspectors and freight documentation. Commercial LCs must incorporate UCP600 terms, require presentation of commercial invoices, bills of lading, phytosanitary certificates, quality/grading reports, weight lists, and exchange of title in favor of the RMI Business Corporation. Settlement may use unconfirmed usance LC for cost-effective financing or confirmed irrevocable LC when the counterparty/supplier banks demand a risk mitigation.Deliverable FX forward contracts with a licensed USD correspondent or foreign bank for currencies of coffee-sourcing jurisdictions (e.g., Colombian peso, Ethiopian birr, Kenyan shilling) where real convertibility is possible.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with offshore banks to hedge soft currencies that are illiquid or restricted, with settlement notionally referenced in USD and no onshore currency delivery.Cross-currency swaps with an international bank to manage long-term exposures from multi-currency sales, reinvestment, or foreign supplier financing.Plain-vanilla FX options traded with regulated global banks if premium payment and documentation are consistent with the RMI corporate treasury policy and banking compliance framework.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold exists under RMI law. For risk-management purposes, maintain Local File documentation when intercompany transactions with related parties exceed USD 250,000 annually; Master File is recommended only for groups with consolidated revenue above USD 50 million.

Economic substance scope: RMI does not apply EU-style Economic Substance Regulations to non-resident BCs. However, global minimum-tax and EU blacklist vigilance require that the BC demonstrate at least delegated substance (i.e., a registered office, an active registered agent, board meetings, and valid contracts/accounting records) to avoid CFC attribution or dual-consistency challenges in the jurisdiction of the beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Material misrepresentation in statutory filings

Statutory sanction: Under the Business Corporations Act (register provisions) and the Criminal Offences Act, conviction may lead to a fine of up to USD 20,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; the Registrar may also strike the company from the register.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise false filings face civil liability to creditors for any resulting loss; a court may pierce the corporate veil, making them jointly liable for company debts. Travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
Economic substance shell-company abuse (foreign anti-avoidance trigger)

Statutory sanction: No direct RMI sanction for non-residents, but EU/OECD anti-tax-avoidance frameworks may identify the entity as a shell; the beneficial owner's state may impose CFC taxation, denial of treaty benefits, or a 15% global minimum tax (Pillar Two) top-up, plus penalties for unprovoked tax planning.

Director personal liability: Senior directors may be held personally liable for the unpaid foreign tax and social security contributions if they have influenced the sham structure; some jurisdictions permit personal recovery orders, director disqualification, and extradition requests for aggravated avoidance.
Money laundering / trade-based illicit finance

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Marshall Islands Revenue and Customs Division, Ministry of Finance (official portal: https://rmiromat.mof.gov.mh; general government portal: https://www.mofa.gov.mh)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are not required to obtain a local TIN or VAT registration number for a non-resident Business Corporation unless the entity derives Marshall Islands source income, maintains a physical presence, or undertakes activities requiring a domestic business license. The standard procedure is: (1) incorporate or register as a foreign business corporation with the Marshall Islands Registrar of Corporations to obtain a corporate registration number; (2) if the entity engages in local business activity, apply to the Revenue and Customs Division for a Business License and obtain a taxpayer identification number; and (3) for customs purposes, obtain an importer number from the Customs Service. Non-resident exempt entities may omit steps (2) and (3) entirely when no local nexus is established.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Foreign entities are not required to obtain a local TIN or VAT registration number for a non-resident Business Corporation unless the entity derives Marshall Islands source income, maintains a physical presence, or undertakes activities requiring a domestic business license. The standard procedure is: (1) incorporate or register as a foreign business corporation with the Marshall Islands Registrar of Corporations to obtain a corporate registration number; (2) if the entity engages in local business activity, apply to the Revenue and Customs Division for a Business License and obtain a taxpayer identification number; and (3) for customs purposes, obtain an importer number from the Customs Service. Non-resident exempt entities may omit steps (2) and (3) entirely when no local nexus is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available under Marshall Islands law because the jurisdiction does not impose an entity-level income tax on non-resident Business Corporations. A non-resident corporation may voluntarily elect to apply for a Domestic Business License under the Business License Act 1991, which triggers a gross revenue-based business license tax; this election is effectively a mechanism to obtain domestic recognition and is not comparable to US Subchapter S or equivalent classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST system; indirect taxation consists of customs import duties under the Customs Act 1990 and a Business License Tax based on gross revenue for locally licensed businesses

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. The Marshall Islands does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no economic turnover threshold for VAT registration. However, a Business License is required for any person or entity physically conducting commercial activity within the Republic of the Marshall Islands; the Business License Tax applies to gross revenue exceeding USD 10,000 per annum for license holders, with no VAT-equivalent threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the traditional VAT sense because the Marshall Islands does not levy VAT/GST on exports. Goods manufactured or warehoused in the RMI and exported outside the customs territory are outside the scope of RMI indirect taxation. To preserve customs-exemption status and demonstrate that goods are not introduced into local consumption, an exporter must be able to substantiate physical departure from the RMI, a commercial contract with an offshore buyer, and receipt of consideration from a foreign source. No explicit zero-rating election or refund mechanism exists because no VAT is charged at the export stage.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Marshall Islands Customs Form 5 or equivalent electronic manifest)
  • Commercial Invoice describing equipment, quantity, and shipment terms
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from the Republic of the Marshall Islands
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/TT credit advice evidencing foreign exchange repatriation for the exported goods
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the destination country)
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order from a non-resident counterparty

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based reporting mandate exists in the Marshall Islands. Paper-based invoices are accepted for customs and business license purposes. Electronic filing is available only through the Revenue and Customs Division's limited online payment system for certain fees; there is no mandate for continuous transaction controls or fiscal data transmission.

Because the RMI has no VAT zero-rating mechanism, the principal audit risk concerns customs export declarations and any effort to unlawfully avoid import duties on goods subsequently re-imported into the RMI. Under the Customs Act 1990, false declarations or material misstatements in export documentation constitute a criminal offense punishable by fines not exceeding USD 5,000 per count, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment for up to one year. An exporter that fraudulently claims exemption or duty drawback may be subject to retrospective clawback of all unpaid duties, interest at the statutory rate, and penalties equal to 25% of the duty evaded. Additionally, the Revenue and Customs Division may revoke any business license, and the Registrar of Corporations may strike the entity's registration if it is found to have misrepresented its non-resident status to obtain tax-exempt treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both the current and capital accounts. The U.S. dollar circulates domestically and cross-border with zero administrative restrictions; the non-resident Business Corporation (MH_BC) may receive equity, disburse trade settlements, and repatriate profits in USD without exchange-control approval. Dividend and profit remittances are not subject to RMI withholding tax for a qualifying exempt non-resident domestic corporation, and there is no tax-clearance certificate precondition or central-bank transfer formality. Practical hurdles exist only at the bank-compliance level: beneficial-ownership declaration, source-of-funds evidence, and FATCA/CRS reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. RMI law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. ECG telemetry monitor export receipts may be retained in USD or any foreign currency, optionally held in foreign bank accounts, and used directly to fund component imports without any RMI central-bank repatriation review. The only applicable constraints are U.S. sanction screening, RMI AML/CFT record-keeping under the Banking Act, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) regulations, which require accurate records of the source and destination of funds but do not mandate conversion.

Central bank supervision: The Republic of the Marshall Islands (ISO 3166-1 alpha-3: MHL) maintains no central bank and no dedicated foreign-exchange administration. The statutory banking supervisor is the Banking Commission of the Ministry of Finance, Banking and Postal Services, acting under the Banking Act 1991 (as amended). Because the U.S. dollar is the sole legal tender, the Federal Reserve System serves as the de facto monetary anchor; no RMI central-bank approval, no exchange-control license, and no currency registration regime applies to non-resident Business Corporations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low (2/5). The RMI has no electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime, no digital-only bank charter, and no fintech sandbox. A non-resident Business Corporation can hold funds at a licensed offshore EMI (e.g., EEA or FCA-authorized) only as a secondary operational account, but the EMI cannot serve as the primary settlement rail for trade payments because: (i) RMI-registered commercial banks will not accept an EMI account as proof of source of funds; (ii) U.S. correspondent banks generally reject MT103 flows to a Marshall Islands entity through a non-bank EMI for medical-device trade; and (iii) commercial letters of credit require a bank-to-bank SWIFT relationship (MT700) that EMIs cannot issue. Legal constraint: acceptance of deposits from RMI residents requires a banking license under the Banking Act 1991; a foreign EMI onboarding a non-resident BC without an RMI license is permissible only if it does not hold RMI-resident deposits or solicit business within RMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: USD 1,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions; below this threshold, maintaining essential TP documentation is still recommended as a best practice

Economic substance scope: The RMI has introduced Economic Substance Regulations for entities carrying on relevant activities such as distribution and service centers, financing, and intellectual property holding. A non-resident business corporation must demonstrate adequate premises, staff, and expenditures proportionate to its activities. For a medical device assembly and calibration trading business, the substance must be present wherever key entrepreneurial risk and decision-making occur. A pure RMI-registered entity without local substance may be disregarded or reassessed if a foreign tax authority deems it a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to USD 5,000 plus civil restitution equal to three times the unpaid tax; possible imprisonment up to 3 years

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for civil restitution and fines if they knowingly participated in evasion; foreign travel bans and seizure of personal assets may follow
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Penalty of USD 10,000 to USD 50,000, possible deregistration of the business corporation, and notice to the RMI Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the entity's unpaid tax liabilities and penalties; personal asset forfeiture orders may also be imposed
AML and Beneficial Ownership Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to USD 20,000 for late or inaccurate beneficial ownership filings; criminal penalties for money laundering under the RMI Criminal Code include fines up to USD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers may face personal fines and imprisonment if they knowingly cause or permit AML breaches; travel bans may be imposed in connection with ongoing AML proceedings
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: While the RMI does not currently apply a comprehensive transfer pricing penalty regime, failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation exposes the entity to a USD 5,000 to USD 20,000 documentation penalty or foreign authority recharacterization

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length pricing in order to evade foreign taxes may be subject to foreign civil penalties, personal tax indemnity claims, and travel restrictions where extradition treaties exist

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), under the supervision of the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-30 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); combined with the Law of 11 January 2008 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the Law of 19 December 2002 on the RCS and company accounts, as amended

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation) executed by a Luxembourg civil-law notary and filed electronically via the eRCS portal; thereafter published as a communication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 41 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash before signature of the notarial deed; the contribution must be deposited into a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the société en formation with an EU/EEA credit institution, evidenced by an attestation de blocage issued to the notary; the funds are released only after RCS registration confers legal personality

Funding & unlock path: Office-only clearance: no autorisation d'établissement recevant du public (ERP) is required unless a public showroom is opened; the landlord's fire-safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) covering the leased floor area must be attached to the office lease. The Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) receives the business registration through the RCS. Workplace duties under the Law of 17 June 1994 require fire extinguishers, marked emergency exits, electrical installation checks, a first-aid kit, and employee safety instruction. No industrial structural-load inspection is relevant to non-industrial digital operations.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash before signature of the notarial deed; the contribution must be deposited into a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the société en formation with an EU/EEA credit institution, evidenced by an attestation de blocage issued to the notary; the funds are released only after RCS registration confers legal personality

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - Ministry of the Economy (Ministère de l'Économie), Direction générale de l'industrie et de la technologie; ISIC 5820 software publishing is not an industrial establishment under Luxembourg law

Category: None required - the Law of 2 September 2011 on access to craft, merchant, industrial and liberal professions does not condition digital software and mobile-game publishing on an establishment permit; RCS registration and AEDT tax registration constitute the sole administrative authorizations for an office-only publisher

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity registers with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through RCS e-filing / guichet.lu, obtains RC number; then applies for a VAT identification number via Form 640A with AEDT; CIT identification aligns with the RC number for tax filings; a local tax representative may be required for non-resident taxable persons without permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity registers with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through RCS e-filing / guichet.lu, obtains RC number; then applies for a VAT identification number via Form 640A with AEDT; CIT identification aligns with the RC number for tax filings; a local tax representative may be required for non-resident taxable persons without permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Luxembourg law; SARL is per se subject to CIT/IRC; specific anti-hybrid elections under ATAD and deductible payments rules are limited to binding tax ruling procedures; optional VAT grouping and partial cooperation exemptions are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for established small taxpayers; non-established foreign suppliers generally must register immediately without threshold when supplying goods or services in Luxembourg

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies on supplies of goods physically dispatched/transported outside the EU by the seller or for the seller, subject to proof of export within 90 days; also applies to ocean/air transport services and services directly connected with exported goods; B2B services to non-EU consumers located outside EU are also zero-rated under the place-of-supply rule.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document/SAD) or electronic export summary declaration
  • Proof of physical dispatch: Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or carrier's attestation
  • Commercial invoice / contract evidencing the transfer of ownership to non-EU buyer
  • Bank transfer / inward remittance certificate evidencing actual cross-border payment
  • Customs export proof accepted by AEDT (all relevant ECS data)

No general mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing/clearance regime; PEPPOL-BIS e-invoicing is mandatory for all B2G invoices (public sector) since 2022; B2B XML/structured e-invoicing optional. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping scheme for hyper-casual gaming publishers.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may claw back wrongly zero-rated VAT plus late interest and impose administrative fines between 10% and 100% of under-declared VAT; fraudulent export claims trigger criminal penalties of imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000; the taxpayer bears the complete documentary burden for 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to the EU Treaty free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63) and the Eurosystem legal framework. No exchange control licences, quantitative limits, or import/export cover approvals apply to corporate treasury transactions in EUR or any convertible currency. Profit remittance from a SARL is through formal corporate distributions from distributable reserves, evidenced by a shareholders resolution and audited accounts where required, subject to a 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax that may be reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for a qualifying 10% participation held for at least 12 months) or under an applicable tax treaty. Interest and royalty payments generally are exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax, but typical corporate law formalities and transfer-pricing substance documentation remain mandatory to resist recharacterisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds generated by the SARL. Receipts from ad-monetisation (AdMob, AppLovin, or other platforms) may be retained in EUR or designated foreign currency accounts held at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution. The BCL only requires statistical balance-of-payments and international-investment-position reporting for cross-border transactions above filing thresholds; such reporting is informational and does not impose surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the national central bank and Eurosystem member; foreign exchange supervision is coordinated under the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) regulation of credit institutions and payments services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational collection of small-ticket ad-network receipts and vendor disbursements; Low for capital-escrow applications and core treasury concentration. An EMI (Electronic Money Institution) must be passport-licensed in another EEA state under the PSD2 and registered with the CSSF if operating in Luxembourg. EMIs may issue e-money and provide payment accounts but cannot accept deposits, cannot grant credit, and their client funds have no Luxembourg deposit-guarantee protection under the LSP (Loi relative aux systèmes de garantie des dépôts). The SARL statutory capital and capital maintenance verification for the Luxembourg Commercial Register will only be safely achieved through a regulated credit institution rather than an EMI account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-Luxembourg corporate or trustee shareholders creating an opaque UBO chain in jurisdictions perceived as high-risk or likely to be used for tax avoidance.

Root cause: Non-Luxembourg corporate or trustee shareholders creating an opaque UBO chain in jurisdictions perceived as high-risk or likely to be used for tax avoidance.

Defense evidence: Complete shareholding organizational chart down to natural persons, RCS Luxembourg incorporation file and articles, notarised share transfer and subscription agreements, UBO declaration compliant with Luxembourg law of 13 January 2019 on UBO register, current and clean registry extracts of each intermediate holding company, tax-residence certificates, and external counsel AML opinion demonstrating economic substance and ownership rationale.
High-volumes of low-average-ticket credits from advertising platforms plus named payors not matching the SARL's direct contractual counterparties.

Root cause: High-volumes of low-average-ticket credits from advertising platforms plus named payors not matching the SARL's direct contractual counterparties.

Defense evidence: Executed Google AdMob and AppLovin monetisation agreements, SDK/API integration registration showing the SARL as developer and payee, consolidated payout statements from ad networks with counterparty reference, merchant ID, and settlement ledger, bank statements covering the prior 6 months, and an internal treasury policy describing platform-to-bankflow and interposing licensees.
Rapid inbound capital followed immediately by outbound disbursements to non-Luxembourg subcontracting game developers, affiliate marketers, or intellectual-property holding companies.

Root cause: Rapid inbound capital followed immediately by outbound disbursements to non-Luxembourg subcontracting game developers, affiliate marketers, or intellectual-property holding companies.

Defense evidence: Fully signed development, marketing subcontractor and IP license agreements, invoices and work delivery reports with app-store version evidence, proof of copyright ownership and trademark registration, domain and infrastructure hosting contracts, VAT invoices with Luxembourg VAT identification where applicable, and transfer pricing documentation following OECD TP principles for the royalty or service fee.
Use of fintech EMI balances, virtual-asset conversion, or settlement routes not through a full credit institution, raising layering red flags.

Root cause: Use of fintech EMI balances, virtual-asset conversion, or settlement routes not through a full credit institution, raising layering red flags.

Defense evidence: Policy that all business settlement must run through a Luxembourg or EEA-licensed credit institution using SWIFT MT103/SEPA rails; no virtual-asset exposure; documented screen-only exemption for ad-platform merchant statements; certified payment service utilisation report; and compliance certification signed by the COO supporting avoidance of unaccounted pass-through transactions.
Substance mismatch from a newly incorporated Luxembourg SARL with no physical office, no local employees or directors beyond nominee, and all decision-taking abroad.

Root cause: Substance mismatch from a newly incorporated Luxembourg SARL with no physical office, no local employees or directors beyond nominee, and all decision-taking abroad.

Defense evidence: Luxembourg lease agreement for registered office, payroll and social security registrations, local statutory manager agreement with professional liability coverage, minutes of board meetings held in Luxembourg, tax and VAT filings proving economic activity, local accountant/auditor engagement letter, and annual accounts filed at the RCS Luxembourg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a Luxembourg SARL capital injection is a SWIFT MT103 credit from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's euro settlement account at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution. The beneficiary field must contain the exact Luxembourg IBAN and the SARL legal name as inscribed in the RCS Luxembourg. The payment reference should read 'Capital increase – Luxembourg SARL – [shareholder name] – [deed date] – shares issued' or, if made through an international bank processing through a correspondent loop, the reference should be kept concise to avoid truncation.The remitting bank must attribute the correct BCL/ECB balance-of-payments purpose code for direct-investment equity flow; in practice the bank's corporate department will accept 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (or a bank-defined code with that semantic) after receiving the notarised share subscription deed and the statutory deposit certificate issued by the receiving credit institution. No prior BCL authorisation is required, but the receiving bank may require proof of identification of the payer and a copy of the notarised capital increase to clear anti-money-laundering verification.For trade settlements and operational out-payments, the SARL will use the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/SCT Inst) for EUR disbursements to EEA beneficiaries and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA or foreign-currency payments. For high-value or documentary transactions—such as acquisition of substantial game-source code, exclusive IP purchase, or infrastructure equipment—a Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary LC) can be opened by the bank under the SARL's credit lines, supported by invoices, bills of lading, or digital acceptance certificates. Routine royalty payments to overseas game developers under license agreements are typically executed via MT103 with descriptive references matching the underlying contract version and date; verification checks include the ICC Sanctions List, OFAC/UE/UN sanctions screening, counterparty KYC, and reference to the Luxembourg domestic VAT declarations if the service is subject to reverse-charge.OTC deliverable FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY) transacted with a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution under ISDA/ EMIR margining rulesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies with limited liquidity or capital-control regimes, limited to hedges of genuine commercial receivables/payables and subject to counterparty credit limitsCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD or EUR/GBP) for matching medium-term intercompany loans and fund-flows in excess of 12 monthsFX call options and zero-cost collars for hedging ad-network revenue and marketing cost volatility without speculative intentExchange-traded futures contracts on EUR/USD and EUR/JPY for short-term treasury adjustments if Eurex-clearing membership is available through a bank broker

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — direct corporate income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) — VAT and indirect tax; unified administrative portal via MyGuichet.lu and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned SARL: (1) execute incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and deposit share capital with a regulated bank; (2) file registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the e-Desk on MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the national business registration number (matricule); (3) register for VAT with the AED using Form 700 B (resident taxable person) or, if the company is non-established, the specific 'Form 700 Controller' procedure for non-established taxable persons to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits); (4) obtain no additional exchange-control or investment approval, as Luxembourg operates full capital liberalisation for non-resident investors.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident-owned SARL: (1) execute incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and deposit share capital with a regulated bank; (2) file registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the e-Desk on MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the national business registration number (matricule); (3) register for VAT with the AED using Form 700 B (resident taxable person) or, if the company is non-established, the specific 'Form 700 Controller' procedure for non-established taxable persons to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits); (4) obtain no additional exchange-control or investment approval, as Luxembourg operates full capital liberalisation for non-resident investors.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists under Luxembourg domestic law. A SARL is mandatorily resident and subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) on worldwide income, without pass-through opt-out. Available statutory elections: (1) joint VAT filing via the 'assujetti unique' election under Article 50 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (LTVAG), allowing an integrated group to be treated as a single VAT taxpayer; (2) option to tax real-estate transactions under Article 44 LTVAG; (3) hybrid mismatch elections restricted to the mandatory alignment rules of EU ATAD 2 as transposed by the Luxembourg law of 20 December 2019; no elective fiscal transparency regime applies to SARLs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold: any taxable supply made in Luxembourg triggers immediate compulsory VAT registration. Domestic taxable persons may benefit from the Article 57 LTVAG small-business franchise exemption for annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, but the franchise is excluded for cross-border supplies and certain electronic/digital services; a digital game publisher using marketplace distribution must register for VAT from the first taxable operation and cannot shelter under the franchise threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods outside the EU qualify for the 0% rate with full input VAT deduction upon proof of physical consignment out of the EU (customs export declaration and transport evidence). Supplies of digital game publishing services or electronic content to non-EU business customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT, functionally equivalent to zero-rating with input VAT deductibility, provided the customer's establishment outside the EU is proven. For sales to EU consumers via digital storefronts (Steam, PlayStation Store), the marketplace operator is the deemed supplier for VAT purpose; the LU SARL accounts for tax only on B2B distribution/royalty flows, reverse-charged where the counterparty is established in the EU, and zero-rated/outside the scope where the counterparty is established outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Single Administrative Document and transport documents (CMR, air waybill) evidencing physical exit from the EU, or equivalent non-EU customer establishment evidence (VAT registration number, business registry extract, IP address/billing evidence) for digital service exports

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, national e-invoicing clearance system, or mandatory digital stamping as of the current effective tax period. B2G e-invoicing is mandated through the PEPPOL network. Transposition of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package mandating standardised B2B e-invoicing is pending, with expected applicability no earlier than the EU-mandated transitional dates; until then, structured e-invoices (EN 16931) or standard PDF invoices are legally sufficient for VAT audit and deduction purposes.

The AED may retroactively recharacterise an unsubstantiated zero-rating or outside-the-scope supply as a taxable Luxembourg supply at 17%, assessing the VAT due plus late-payment interest of 0.8% per month and a civil penalty of EUR 750 to EUR 12,500 for knowingly false declarations; fraudulent suppression or misrepresentation is criminally punishable with imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 12,500 under the law of 30 July 2001. Export refund claims audited post-clearance also expose the taxpayer to cross-border information exchange risks and to clawback of deducted input VAT on recovered costs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is fully integrated into the euro area and maintains full convertibility of the euro under the free movement of capital guaranteed by the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions, no central bank approval for foreign currency transfers, and no limits on profit or dividend remittance. Profit distribution to shareholders is subject only to applicable Luxembourg withholding tax, corporate compliance and AML/source-of-wealth verification standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in Luxembourg. Export receipts derived from Steam, PlayStation Store or other digital store payouts may be retained in euro or foreign currency without forcible conversion. The BCL only imposes statistical reporting obligations on cross-border direct investment and foreign asset/liability positions for resident enterprises, but these do not operate as currency controls.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for prudential supervision; the BCL is the central bank and the Luxembourg authority responsible for monetary operations, balance-of-payments statistics and euro-area financial stability.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-high viability as a supplementary liquidity rail, not as the primary statutory bank. Luxembourg-authorised or EU-passported fintech EMIs may offer remote onboarding and instant disbursement capabilities, which is useful for daily Steam/PlayStation payout flows and royalty payments. However, a fintech EMI is not the preferred depository for share capital escrow or long-term treasury balances because it is not a credit institution and does not benefit from the full Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme in the same manner as a commercial bank. Legal constraints require money safeguarding, segregation of client funds, and compliance with e-money limits under PSD2; for conservative treasury governance, a tier-1 bank should hold the core operating account with the EMI used only for payment disbursement acceleration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from an EEA euro area shareholder should be settled by SEPA Credit Transfer, or SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA and USD transfers. Payment purpose must be transparently stated, for example 'shareholder capital contribution under the Luxembourg Companies Law' or 'share capital increase contribution'; the receiving Luxembourg bank will require a notarised corporate resolution, updated share register, UBO identification and a bank/statistical declaration to the Banque centrale du Luxembourg for balance-of-payments purposes. For US or Asian parent funding, the SWIFT MT103 should include the exact invoice/capital increase reference in field 70, identify the remitter as the equity holder, and be routed via an established correspondent; no SEPA transfer should be used if the economic substance is not clearly compatible with a simple intercompany payment.Royalty and revenue-share payments to non-EEA developers and publishers are normally executed via SWIFT MT103 with express invoice and licence references, while larger milestone or development advances may be secured using a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued by a Luxembourg or EU bank or backed by a standby letter of credit. Steam/Valve and PlayStation Store platform payouts enter the Luxembourg SARL's treasury account as SEPA or SWIFT credits and are then netted against outbound disbursements to licensees; intra-euro-area payments settle under SEPA Instant/SCT rails. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16% for income year 2024 (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC); 15% from income year 2025; plus 7% solidarity surcharge (contribution au fonds pour l'emploi) on IRC and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) of 3.0% x 225% municipal coefficient (Luxembourg City) = 6.75%; aggregate combined statutory burden approx. 23.87% (2024) and 22.80% (2025) for Luxembourg City.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends (Art. 146 LIR) payable by the SARL on distributions to non-resident shareholders; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (>=10% capital, >=12 months holding, no abuse); DTA-reduced rates typically 0%-5% for holdings >=10% (e.g., 0% under LU-US, 5% under LU-JP), 15% for portfolio holdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the Luxembourg entity's net turnover exceeds EUR 1 million AND the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million; Master File mandatory when consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million; Country-by-Country Report at consolidated group revenue above EUR 750 million (Law of 23 December 2020, effective FY2022). Separate contemporaneous documentation is required for IP Box nexus and participation exemption claims.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is a statutory condition embedded in: i) the Luxembourg GAAR (Art. 6 Abgabenordnung) and the EU ATAD General Abuse Rule; ii) the participation exemption's non-abuse condition; iii) the IP Box nexus activity requirement (active ownership and execution of development functions in Luxembourg); iv) the tax-residence test for management and control of the SARL; v) the Pillar Two GloBE rules (15% minimum tax law of 21 December 2023, effective 1 January 2024 for MNE groups with revenue >=EUR 750M). For the Steam/PlayStation publishing SARL, sustained substance requires qualified employees with game-publishing decision rights, actual contract negotiation and platform-account administration in Luxembourg, local bank accounts, and board minutes evidencing real management of Steam and PlayStation sales.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine from EUR 1,000 up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment from 1 month to 5 years (Law of 25 March 2020); administrative reassessment with 10% majoration and late-payment interest at 0.8% per month; publication of the final sanction in the official journal; forfeiture of IP Box and participation exemption benefits for the relevant years.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and de facto directors who conceived or tolerated the evasion are criminally liable; personal travel bans and precautionary seizures of personal assets may be ordered; the ACD may recover unpaid corporate taxes from directors through the 'action en comblement du passif' (Art. 480(1), Law of 10 August 1915) where gross negligence caused the company's asset insufficiency; directors face up to 10 years of management disqualification.
Artificial Profit Shifting / Shell (Letterbox) Company

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the tax position under Article 6 Abgabenordnung (GAAR) and EU ATAD; reallocation of profits to the actual function operator; denial of IP Box and participation exemption; assessment of unpaid IRC/ICC/IFT plus 10% majoration and 0.8% monthly interest; in egregious cases classified as fraud with criminal fines up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the reassessed corporate taxes if they approved the shell structure with knowledge; civil liability toward minority shareholders and creditors; travel restrictions for cross-border tax debts under EU Directive 2010/24/EU once exposure exceeds EUR 10,000.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result of the SARL; penalty of 10% of the net upward TP adjustment; mandatory documentation default fines from EUR 5,000 to EUR 250,000 under the Law of 23 December 2020; 0.8% monthly interest on the deficiency; criminal exposure if the TP misstatement is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate TP declarations or knowingly omit documentation are personally liable for the penalty portion; external tax advisors face contractual and administrative liability; the ACD can extend recovery to directors who transferred funds out of the SARL to related parties during the adjustment period.
AML / UBO Registration (RBE) and Anonymous Ownership

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 5,000 per violation and/or imprisonment up to 6 months; registration restrictions with the Luxembourg Business Registers; blocking of dividend distributions; forced deregistration and dissolution of the SARL upon repeated non-compliance; AML supervisory penalties for the banking sponsor.

Director personal liability: Directors personally subject to the RBE criminal fines; UBOs identified through investigation face separate sanctions and precautionary freezing of their interests; professionals (banks, auditors, notaries) that assist concealment may be penalized under CSSF AML regulations.
Late Filing / Non-Payment of Corporate Taxes

Statutory sanction: Late filing fine up to EUR 10,000; forced tax assessment (taxation d'office) on the ACD's discretionary basis; interest at 0.8% per month; enforcement by seizure (saisie-arrêt) of the SARL's receivables, including Steam and PlayStation platform balances, bank accounts, and IP receivables; RCS late-accounts fines up to EUR 2,000 per delayed filing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be surcharged personally for taxes not withheld (e.g., dividend WHT); travel bans and asset freezes ordered for tax debts exceeding EUR 10,000 under the EU Mutual Recovery Directive; the liquidator's failure to settle tax debts before distributions triggers personal restitution liability.

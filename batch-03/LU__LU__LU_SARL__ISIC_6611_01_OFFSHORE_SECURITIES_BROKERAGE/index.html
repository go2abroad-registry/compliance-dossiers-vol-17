Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 181 to 263 (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the register of commerce and companies and on accounting and annual accounts, as amended.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association (acte constitutif) combined with the RCS registration form (déclaration d'inscription) filed electronically by the notary through the LBR/RCS portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 190 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

A SARL requires minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash before registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the company in formation; the bank releases the funds to the company only after RCS registration. Non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required from the communal fire-prevention consultative commission (commission consultative des édifices communaux) for the registered office; occupational clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering workplace safety, ergonomics, first-aid obligations, and safe ESC/strongroom access; CSSF imposes fit-and-proper, physical security, archiving and business-continuity clearance for the brokerage premises.

Paid-in capital rule: A SARL requires minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash before registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the company in formation; the bank releases the funds to the company only after RCS registration. Non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes (corporate income tax, net wealth tax, municipal business tax) with portal MyGuichet.lu and e-CDF electronic filing system; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (TVA) registration and indirect tax compliance with portal Guichet.lu / eTVA (Electronic VAT filing).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate SARL with Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) portal; the RCS issues a 11-digit registration number (numéro de matricule) which serves as the base corporate tax identification number. Step 2: File the mandatory VAT registration application (Formulaire 640 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') with AEDT after the RCS registration; the AEDT assigns a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) prefixed 'LU' plus 8 digits. Step 3: Submit the Luxembourg income tax registration file to the ACD (Bureau d'imposition) of the tax district (Bureau d'imposition SOCARRIS/SARL) for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax (IF); the ACD issues an assessment file (dossier fiscal) and annual return obligations. Step 4: Register as an employer for PAYE withholding (retenue à la source) if the SARL hires personnel in Luxembourg. Step 5: Confirm registration with the Social Security Centre (Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale - CCSS) for social security contributions. The entire registration workflow is coordinated through the independent e-Space on MyGuichet.lu with delegated e-signature capacity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Luxembourg société à responsabilité limitée (SARL) is a resident corporate taxpayer fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités - IRC) at 17% including the unemployment fund contribution (contribution au fonds de l'emploi), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal - ICC) at a maximum aggregate rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City, and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune - IF) at 0.5%; the SARL is fiscally transparent only if expressly elected under the Luxembourg partnership regime, which is unavailable for SARLs, or if organized as a special limited partnership (SCSp); territorial scope: worldwide taxation for Luxembourg-resident SARLs with extensive participation exemption for qualifying shareholdings (≥10% or ≥€1.2 million acquisition price held for 12 months) and no withholding tax on outbound dividends (parent-subsidiary directive) except a 15% domestic rate on non-qualifying distributions; Luxembourg does not maintain a general tax-exempt zone, though the Freeport (Luxembourg Freeport) is a customs bonded warehouse not a corporate tax exemption zone).

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not recognize the US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is fixed under the Commercial Law and the Income Tax Law (LIR) based on legal form. However, Luxembourg offers: (1) the partnership look-through election for SARLs is unavailable, but a co-ownership (indivision) or SCSp can be used for transparent vehicles; (2) the group consolidation regime (régime de l'intégration fiscale) effective 2024 allows a 100%-owned Luxembourg parent to consolidate Luxembourg tax results of subsidiaries with a minimum retention period of 5 consecutive fiscal years; (3) the optional payroll withholding election (évaluation forfaitaire) for partly payroll-based benefits; (4) the VAT grouping election (assujettis uniques) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law allows unaffiliated or affiliated taxable persons established in Luxembourg to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT; (5) the hybrid mismatch election must be documented in the tax file (Form 610) to align Luxembourg treatment under ATAD 1/2; (6) the special election for the 80% participation exemption and the 50% gross-wealth exemption for qualifying IP (IP box regime) under Article 50bis LIR. No check-the-box deferral is available for the Luxembourg SARL, which is mandatorily opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons engaged in exempt financial activities; the small business franchise regime (régime de la franchise) at Article 57 Loi TVA applies only to an annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT), but is explicitly excluded for persons carrying out exempt operations under Articles 43, 44, 148, 149, 150 and 151 Loi TVA (including securities brokerage and other financial services). Therefore a Luxembourg SARL engaged in ISIC 6611 securities brokerage must register for VAT (immatriculation à la TVA) from the first euro of turnover, with no threshold exception. Non-established foreign businesses also have a mandatory zero-euro threshold registration requirement for distance sales and intermediary services. The franchise exclusion applies with EUR 35,000 de minimis for any mixed activity, but the excluded activities kill the franchise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% TVA) applies to: (1) exports of tangible goods transported by the supplier or for the supplier's account to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by the customs export declaration (EX-A) and transport documents, in accordance with Articles 53 and 154 Loi TVA; (2) supplies of services directly and strictly related to the export of goods (transport, handling, freight forwarding, inspection, packaging) under Article 54 Loi TVA; (3) certain services physically performed outside the EU for non-EU business customers, including brokerage of securities on behalf of non-EU counterparties, which under the B2B place-of-supply rule are outside Luxembourg VAT scope entirely; and (4) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered purchasers (with a valid EU VAT ID in VIES) under Article 51-52 Loi TVA. For a Luxembourg SARL performing offshore securities brokerage (ISIC 6611): equity, CFD and derivative brokerage services to retail clients in third countries are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (B2B rule), or if rendered to private consumers outside the EU, are also outside scope under the business-to-consumer (B2C) place-of-supply rule. It is critical to distinguish zero-rated (0%) from exempt without credit (exonération sans droit à déduction) under Article 44 Loi TVA: securities dealing and negotiation services supplied to Luxembourg private retail clients are exempt without input VAT recovery, not zero-rated. To claim export zero-rating for goods or ancillary services, the SARL must hold (a) the customs export documentation; (b) the transport contract/proof; and (c) the payment evidence. Services linked to exports must be directly traced to a specific export movement; general overhead or back-office services to a broker's own client book do not qualify for zero-rating and remain subject to the exempt/outside-scope analysis.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A/DEA) with MRN (Movement Reference Number) accepted by Luxembourg Customs (Administration des douanes et accises)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) / CMR consignment note evidencing cross-border physical shipment
  • Commercial Invoice (triplicate) matching the export declaration values
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (from the settlement bank, evidencing hard-currency receipt of the export proceeds on a customer bank account located in Luxembourg)
  • Brokerage confirmation slip / trade ticket evidencing the offshore securities transaction and the commission structure
  • Proof of client due diligence (KYC/AML file) confirming non-EU residence for the offshore broker's seat and the beneficial owner's tax residency
  • VIES confirmation output (EU customer VAT ID validation) for intra-Community zero-rating claims

No real-time fiscal clearance or pre-clearance digital stamping is in force in Luxembourg for B2B e-invoicing (as of the current compliance cycle). Luxembourg has implemented the EU Directive 2014/55/EU for B2G (business-to-government) e-invoicing; all invoices issued to Luxembourg public-sector bodies (state, municipalities, social security) must be transmitted as structured e-invoices (EN 16931 format, specifically Peppol-BIS 3.0, via the Luxembourg PEPPOL access point). B2B paper/invoice PDFs remain acceptable, but a national mandate for mandatory B2B e-invoicing has been announced by the Luxembourg government (draft budget/conjuncture plans, expected effective fiscal year 2026+): the proposed model is a clearance continuous transaction control (CTC) regime, but the final technical specifications (API, JSON/UBL schema and fiscal stamp) are not yet enacted into the Loi TVA. Until enacted, cross-border B2B transactions with other EU Member States must respect the mandatory e-invoicing rules of the customer's Member State (e.g., France, Italy, Poland, Romania, Belgium, Spain) which creates an indirect e-invoicing obligation. The Luxembourg SARL must therefore: (1) operate a PEPPOL access point or subscribe to a certified service provider for B2G invoicing to Luxembourg public buyers; (2) implement a matrix QR/2D barcode standard on B2C invoices for built-in digital stamping if invoicing in a country where that applies; and (3) archive all invoices (paper or electronic) for 10 years (Article 66 Loi TVA) with a 'séquestre fiscal' filing requirement. Local file archiving must permit AEDT online audit access.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member and maintains full and unrestricted euro convertibility for current-account and capital-account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Neither prior administrative authorisation nor exchange-control licensing exists for inbound equity contributions, outbound profit remittances, dividend distributions, cross-border loan repayments, or commission rebates to foreign introducing brokers. Residual hurdles are purely domestic rules: (i) SARL distribution constraints under the law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies (Articles 710-1 et seq.), requiring annual-account approval and a positive net-asset test after distribution; (ii) a 15% Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holds 10% or EUR 1,200,000 for 12 months; reduced or exempt under applicable tax treaties); and (iii) BCL statistical reporting obligations for external financial transactions and CSSF prudential reporting for regulated investment firms. For a CSSF-licensed securities brokerage, client-funds convertibility is additionally constrained by client-asset segregation rules under MiFID II Article 16(8) and CSSF Circular 21/797, which require segregated client money to be held in euro or in other currencies as necessary for settlement, without requiring conversion onshore.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg and the Eurosystem impose no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for goods or services, and no analogue exists for cross-border brokerage commissions receivable from non-resident clients. Commission income in foreign currency may remain offshore or be converted freely without BCL approval; the only compliance overlay is the regulatory requirement that client monies be identified, segregated, and reconciled daily, and that a regulated investment firm's own funds be maintained in currency and location consistent with CSSF Circular 21/797 and LCR/NSFR obligations under CRR.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the Central Bank of Luxembourg, exercising monetary policy, financial stability, balance-of-payments and foreign-exchange administration functions as a member of the Eurosystem; prudential and conduct supervision of the licensed securities brokerage is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) acting as the national competent authority under MiFID II/MiFIR, CRD IV/CRR, and the law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Luxembourg authorises payment institutions, electronic money institutions (EMIs), and specialised Professionals of the Financial Sector (PFS) under the law of 10 August 2015 on payment services and the law of 1 March 2012 on electronic money, all supervised by the CSSF; however, EMIs are not CRR credit institutions and cannot lawfully safeguard retail client money of a MiFID investment firm. The regulated brokerage must segregate client funds at a prudentially supervised credit institution (EEA branch, or a third-country institution under CSSF conditions) pursuant to Article 37-1 of the law of 5 April 1993 and MiFID II Article 16(8). Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA passport or Luxembourg presence are unsuitable for the client-money rail; at most, they may serve as peripheral fee-collection conduits for non-EU clients if they are supervised in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction and swept daily into the segregated account. The operative model requires account relationships directly with CRR credit institutions; transaction-account EMIs may support operational FX conversion but must be disconnected from the client-money ledger and from any deposit-taking function to avoid unlicensed banking exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial share capital (SARL statutory floor EUR 12,000, but for a MiFID investment firm holding client assets the CSSF requires initial capital of at least EUR 730,000 under CRD IV Article 28(2)) is wired by SWIFT MT103 from the UBO's bank account to a blocked capital-escrow account opened with a Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance must carry structured payment information referencing the RCS registration number of the company-in-formation, the notarial engagement, and the ECB/BCL balance-of-payments statistical purpose code — typically 'S301 – Equity capital' with the ISO 20022 Category Purpose 'EQUI'. The bank issues an attestation de blocage to the Luxembourg notary, who certifies free disposal before signing the notarised incorporation deed. Post-incorporation shareholder subordinated loans use code 'S302 – Debt instruments' (Category Purpose 'LOAN') and must be documented by written subordination agreements when counted as regulatory capital; CSSF must be informed of any ancillary own-funds instruments. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium. Luxembourg authorises payment institutions, electronic money institutions (EMIs), and specialised Professionals of the Financial Sector (PFS) under the law of 10 August 2015 on payment services and the law of 1 March 2012 on electronic money, all supervised by the CSSF; however, EMIs are not CRR credit institutions and cannot lawfully safeguard retail client money of a MiFID investment firm. The regulated brokerage must segregate client funds at a prudentially supervised credit institution (EEA branch, or a third-country institution under CSSF conditions) pursuant to Article 37-1 of the law of 5 April 1993 and MiFID II Article 16(8). Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA passport or Luxembourg presence are unsuitable for the client-money rail; at most, they may serve as peripheral fee-collection conduits for non-EU clients if they are supervised in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction and swept daily into the segregated account. The operative model requires account relationships directly with CRR credit institutions; transaction-account EMIs may support operational FX conversion but must be disconnected from the client-money ledger and from any deposit-taking function to avoid unlicensed banking exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required for groups with consolidated turnover equal to or above EUR 50 million; Local File required where annual controlled transactions exceed EUR 100,000; Country-by-Country Report applies at consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply an ESR-like codified statute, but economic substance is enforced through the OECD/BEPS Principal Purpose Test (PPT), the EU General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), and CSSF sectoral supervision. A securities brokerage SARL must prove real office premises, qualified employees, Luxembourg-based decision-making, independent risk management, operational substance, and adequate accounting records; failure to demonstrate substance results in denial of treaty benefits, loss of participation exemption, and potential management liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud and Wilful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Ex officio reassessment of evaded tax plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month; administrative penalty of EUR 250–12,000; criminal tax fraud penalties of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years; publication of convictions in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors personally orchestrated fraudulent accounting; they become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties, and are exposed to asset seizures, professional disqualification, travel bans and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty relief under the BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test; disallowance of participation exemption and loss carryforwards; recharacterization of revenue to the controlling non-resident; additional tax and 50% gross-negligence penalties; possible CSSF de-authorization.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance attestations or artificial board minutes are personally liable for restitution of diverted profits, can be stripped of management functions, and may face criminal sanctions including fines, travel bans and imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Client Money Breaches

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; criminal penalties under Luxembourg AML laws of imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 250,000; revocation of financial services authorization and forfeiture of unlawfully held assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable and can be fined, banned from financial-sector management, forced to make full restitution of misappropriated client funds, and imprisoned; courts may freeze personal assets and issue travel bans during criminal proceedings.
Unlicensed Investment Services / Unregulated Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector: imprisonment of one to five years and fines up to EUR 50,000; CSSF injunctions and administrative fines up to EUR 2,000,000 or 10% of turnover; forced closure and repayment of client funds.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors knowingly conducted unlicensed financial services for personal gain; they face joint liability for client losses, disgorgement of profits, director disqualification, asset freezing, travel restrictions and imprisonment.

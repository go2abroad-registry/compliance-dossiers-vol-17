Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg-City; electronic filing portal: e-RCS via www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated and amended (SARL: Articles 710-1 to 710-26, reformed by the Law of 10 August 2016); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS and RESA); Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation incorporating the articles of association) filed electronically with the RCS together with the publication insert in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum share capital: EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be evidenced by a bank certificate from a Luxembourg credit institution, or by a sworn statement of the Managers where all shares are held by the Managers. Funds are blocked until RCS registration; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and rescue clearance from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) covering detection, extinguishing and escape routes; commune building permit with structural inspection; occupational safety audit by the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines (ITM) for laser Class 4 interlock enclosures, clean-room ventilation, chemical hygiene and occupational medical surveillance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax (IF); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (TVA), registration duties and indirect taxes; Administration des douanes et accises for customs/EORI and excise. Official digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (professional e-space), the ACD 'eCDF/eDeclaration' filing platform, the AEDT 'eTVA' portal, and the RCS Luxembourg repository for the national business matricule.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: Pre-incorporation name reservation and notarial deed — draft the SARL articles of association before a Luxembourg notary and subscribe the share capital (minimum EUR 12,000/advance payments). STEP 2: Register the company with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) within 1 month of incorporation; the RCS automatically allocates the 11-digit national 'matricule' (e.g., B123456) which serves as the corporate tax identifier and legal entity identifier for Luxembourg. STEP 3: The ACD assigns a direct tax file number (numéro de dossier) for IRC/ICC/IF upon RCS data transfer. STEP 4: For VAT, a taxable person must file an application with the AEDT (Form 7900/e-TVA module on MyGuichet.lu); the authority issues a Luxembourg VAT ID (format LU + 8 digits) within approximately 2 weeks. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; a SARL is always an opaque, entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal unity / group taxation under Art. 164bis LIR for fully integrated Luxembourg resident groups (100% direct/indirect control, election to pool taxable income); (ii) tax-transparency election for certain partnerships (SCS, SCSp, société particulière) which SARL parent groups may use in structuring, though the SARL itself cannot elect pass-through; (iii) IP Box election under Art. 50ter LIR yielding 80% exemption of qualifying IP income (drug-device patents, bioresorbable polymer process patents); (iv) participation exemption election under Art. 166 LIR to exempt qualifying dividends and capital gains on substantial shareholdings; (v) securitization/RAIF transparency opt-ins where structurally applicable. Hybrid mismatch and reverse-hybrid rules under ATAD 1&2 (LIR Arts. 168ter et seq.) are mandatory, not elective, and MNEs must file DAC6/Mandatory Disclosure Rules reports on reportable cross-border arrangements; no elective hybrid-neutralizing mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover (VAT-exclusive) — small-business 'franchise en base' exemption threshold under Art. 57 of the amended Luxembourg VAT law of 12 February 1979. A SARL manufacturing coronary drug-eluting stents will generally exceed EUR 30,000 and is mandatorily registered; registration is additionally compulsory without any turnover threshold for taxable persons making intra-EU acquisitions, importers, exporters, or persons supplying taxable goods/services with an obligation to charge Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including Class III coronary stents) to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated at 0% VAT provided the goods are physically removed from the EU within 3 months of the supply, and the supplier holds authentic commercial and transport documentation evidencing departure. Intra-EU dispatches of stents to taxable customers holding a valid EU VAT ID in another member state are equally zero-rated when the goods move across borders and the transaction is reported in the EC sales list (e-CL) and Intrastat dispatch declaration. Directly connected services (freight, customs clearance, handling, quality control and laboratory testing on exported goods) qualify for zero-rating as ancillary international services. Substantiation requires the exporter to keep a complete chronologically numbered export file within the statutory record-keeping period (6 years) proving the goods left the EU; input VAT on production costs is credited through the normal periodic VAT return, with VAT refund arising where input VAT exceeds output VAT in a given filing period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export System/AES – Single Administrative Document (SAD) with MRN number issued by Administration des douanes et accises)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/Transport Document / Air Waybill — whichever is applicable) evidencing physical carriage from Luxembourg/EU to the non-EU consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/wire confirmation demonstrating actual receipt of export payment within the agreed commercial terms)
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the SARL (aligned with Incoterms and the customs declared value)
  • Packing List (itemizing stents, lots, quantities, gross/net weights, and HS Chapter 9021 classification)
  • EU Intra-Community Supply evidence for intra-EU dispatches (customer VAT validation printout, CMR/GRS transport order, Intrastat dispatch return, and EC Sales List reference)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) requirement exists in Luxembourg to date. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies for supplies to Luxembourg public authorities and state bodies, using the European standard EN 16931 through the national PEPPOL network. B2B e-invoicing is permitted with recipient consent in PDF, XML, Factur-X or ZUGFeRD formats, provided authenticity of origin and integrity of content are ensured via a qualified electronic signature (QES) or EDI. All Luxembourg VAT-registered businesses must file periodic e-returns (e-TVA) and e-recaps (e-CL) electronically, and customs export/import declarations are lodged electronically through the EU Customs Decision System (AES/ICS2) via the Luxembourg single window. Transaction-level digital reporting is anticipated only upon finalization of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals.

The AEDT and Luxembourg customs authorities scrutinize export evidence at audit. Inadequate or missing export documentation triggers recharacterization of the zero-rated supply as a domestic taxable supply, resulting in retroactive assessment of 17% VAT on the entire invoice amount, plus statutory late-interest ('intérêts de retard') and a minimum administrative fine. Fraudulent zero-rating claims — including false customs declarations, fictitious counterparties, or simulated exports of the drug-eluting stents — constitute tax fraud ('escroquerie fiscale') punishable under the modified general tax law (AO) and the Criminal Code with fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment; corporate directors may be held personally liable. The standard VAT assessment limitation period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or willful concealment. Any refund (credit) invoked as a result of zero-rated export sales is recoverable with interest and may be denied for a subsequent period where documentary integrity fails; export declarations are also subject to post-clearance customs audits under the Union Customs Code with potential customs debt clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg applies no foreign exchange restrictions, capital controls, or approval regimes for current-account or capital-account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Dividend, interest, royalty, and loan-repayment remittances are unrestricted; only ordinary Luxembourg corporate income tax, net wealth tax, and potential source-state withholding tax (mitigated under EU Directives or tax treaties) apply. Cross-border payments and balances exceeding €12,500 trigger BCL external-statistics reporting obligations for resident enterprises.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, repatriation deadlines, or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in Luxembourg. Exporters may freely retain revenues in any convertible currency, maintain bank accounts abroad (subject to BCL statistical reporting when thresholds are met), and convert to euro at market rates. All settlements must nonetheless be documented for VAT, customs, and transfer-pricing compliance.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks under the oversight of the European Central Bank (ECB); the BCL is the designated national central bank for balance-of-payments reporting and statistics, while exchange matters are governed by EU law and ECB regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary banking channel; Medium as a supplementary payment-collection rail. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) can passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and offer SEPA credit transfers, instant payments, and select SWIFT corridors, but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide trade finance, deliver term lending, or operate sophisticated liquidity pooling for a Class III medical device manufacturer. Intangible EMI onboarding may also amplify correspondent-bank counterparty risk in high-risk medical sectors; therefore restrict EMI usage to collection-only or small-balance escrow flows and never designate it as the sole treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg City statutory headline rate (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax); headline corporate income tax alone is 17%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying 10% holdings held for at least 12 months; DTA reduced rates generally 0%-15%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Luxembourg Master File / Local File is expected when aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 in a fiscal year or when the AED requires documentation under OECD-aligned rules; Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, economic substance is decisive under general anti-abuse and treaty-application rules. A SARL manufacturing or distributing coronary stents must maintain real local premises, qualified directors or managers, board decisions in Luxembourg, actual P&L risk, local bank accounts, and documentary substance. Artificial shell arrangements are disregarded under Article 6 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law and the ATAD GAAR, leading to denial of tax residence certificates, participation exemption, DTA rates, and EU withholding relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Default assessment and restitution of evaded tax; administrative penalty up to 10%-100% of unpaid tax; default interest; criminal sanctions under the Luxembourg General Tax Law with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years

Director personal liability: Directors and gérants who sign or approve false returns are personally liable for civil restitution of evaded tax; the corporate veil is pierceable to permit asset seizure, travel bans during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment for tax fraud
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the artificial structure; denial of Tax Residency Certificate, participation exemption, and EU Directive benefits; recharacterization of income with additional CIT, municipal business tax, and solidarity surcharge; deemed distributions subject to 15% dividend WHT; penalties and default interest

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally liable for restitution of denied tax benefits; courts pierce the veil when the SARL is a mere façade; travel bans and imprisonment can be imposed where the shell arrangement is part of criminal tax fraud or money laundering
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length re-determination by the AED; reassessment of CIT, municipal business tax and solidarity surcharge; monthly default interest; administrative penalty of up to 10% of adjusted tax; criminal sanctions where intentionally false transfer pricing documentation is filed

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation or fail to maintain required accounting records are jointly and severally liable for additional tax, penalties, and interest; personal exposure includes civil restitution, disqualification, travel restraint, and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion
Withholding Tax Evasion and AML / Money-Laundering Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a dividend and imposition of 15% WHT, late-payment penalties, and default interest; Luxembourg AML sanctions include fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; foreign anti-bribery and medical-device fraud penalties may also be extended

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unlawfully withheld taxes and for distributions made without sufficient distributable reserves; AML compliance officers and managing directors face criminal liability, travel bans, freezing and confiscation of assets, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for deliberate AML breaches

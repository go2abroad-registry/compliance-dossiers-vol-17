Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for direct taxes, and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT/GST; electronic filing and registrations are processed through MyGuichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (RBE) portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with Luxembourg VAT obligations must first obtain a Luxembourg corporate or VAT identification number. For EU-established entities, registration may be completed directly via MyGuichet.lu using the standard VAT registration form and legalized or apostilled corporate documents, accompanied by evidence of economic activity in Luxembourg. Non-EU entities must appoint an approved Luxembourg tax representative who is jointly liable for VAT. The application requires the foreign entity's constitutive documents, proof of identity of directors, and registration certificate from the home jurisdiction. After validation, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number and confirms the direct tax registration obligation for any Luxembourg permanent establishment or resident SARL.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity with Luxembourg VAT obligations must first obtain a Luxembourg corporate or VAT identification number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Luxembourg SARL is classified as a fully taxable corporate entity and cannot elect pass-through treatment; no check-the-box regime is available. Luxembourg has transposed the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD), and limited elections may be made for hybrid mismatches and tax-timing alignment. For VAT purposes, qualifying taxable persons may elect to form a VAT group and may opt for simplified accounting schemes where permitted by the Luxembourg VAT authorities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to taxable persons established in Luxembourg; every person making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT. Non-established EU suppliers of distance sales of goods are subject to the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold, above which Luxembourg VAT registration or the One Stop Shop (OSS) becomes mandatory. A small-business exemption applies to domestic Luxembourg taxable persons with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, but business registration and compliance obligations must still be met before claiming the exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by or on behalf of the supplier, provided the supplier retains documentary evidence of export. Intangible services related to exports and certain auxiliary transport services may also qualify for zero-rating. Intra-EU B2B supplies are zero-rated only when the customer holds a valid VAT identification number, the goods are transported to another Member State, and the supply is correctly disclosed in the recapitulative statement. For challenges see: Use of OSS for distance sales is mandatory above EUR 10,000, where the supply is instead taxed in the destination Member State.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX-A and stamped export certificate)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document proving physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice issued in the name of the foreign customer
  • Packing list and warehouse dispatch evidence
  • Proof of payment or bank transfer from the foreign customer (including inward remittance evidence)
  • Luxembourg VAT return and recapitulative statement disclosing the zero-rated supply

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, digital stamping mandate, or continuous transaction controls for VAT. Mandatory electronic invoicing is limited to invoices issued to Luxembourg public-sector entities, which must be transmitted through the national B2G e-invoicing platform. B2B e-invoicing is optional. Periodic VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically, and SAF-T files may be required from certain taxpayers during audits.

If a taxpayer fails to substantiate a zero-rated export or submits false or incomplete documentation, the Luxembourg VAT authorities may retrospectively reassess the supply at the standard 17% rate. Default interest applies from the due date of the original VAT return, together with fines up to EUR 10,000 for negligence and substantially higher penalties for intentional fraud. Criminal sanctions, including imprisonment, may apply in cases of fraudulent export schemes. The appointed tax representative can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg franc has been fully replaced by the euro (EUR); Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls or restrictions on either current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, liquidation proceeds, loan repayments, and royalty distributions are freely transferable cross-border to/from non-residents. Local banks require a valid economic substance rationale and AML/KYC documentation for every wire, and the BCL collects statistical Balance of Payments reporting for payments above EUR 12,500; this is not an authorization requirement. There are no surrender or conversion thresholds against EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or to repatriate foreign currency earnings to Luxembourg. Exporters may hold retained receipts in EUR or foreign currency in offshore accounts, provided the transactions are truthfully recorded in Luxembourg financial statements and tax returns. No mandatory conversion ratio or timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium - EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise/Lunar are legally allowed to issue payment accounts under CSSF PSD2; however, for Stage 4 the EUR clearing rails for high-volume online camping gear retail should rely on a Luxembourg licensed payment institution or an NCCB with access to TARGET2. EMIs are viable for low-value subscriptions and payouts to suppliers under a segregated safeguarding framework (missing pass-through / MT103 direct participant), but they cannot serve as a substitute for a primary credit institution's capital escrow account. Any Dark Pool-like settlement arrangement is prohibited unless registered under the Payment Services Law of 10 November 2009.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Luxembourg SARL are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank / bankwise, only for the bank itself). For investment capital, the remittance instruction must include the purpose code 'Capital Injection under Articles 183-1 of Luxembourg Companies Law' and, if the contribution is made by a non-resident corporate shareholder, a written equity contribution resolution in English/French. The receiving Luxembourg bank will not require conversion of USD/GBP/CHF into EUR; however, it will report the receipt to BCL under the Balance of Payments statistical code 'F DI 1 - Equity capital / jezuity', and no exchange premium applies because the euro is the legal tender. No prior ministerial authorization is required, unless the corporate activity involves a regulated sector (not applicable for internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment).Outbound treasury disbursements for trade settlement should use the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for unencumbered supplier wires, with instruction field 70 noting 'PURCHASE OF GOODS - OUTDOOR CAMPING EQUIPMENT' and the final invoice number; (2) SWIFT MT202 for correspondent bank to bank clearing where no beneficiary customer details are disclosed; (3) SWIFT MT700 / MT710 for Irrevocable Documentary Loans or Commercial Letters of Credit when the supplier requires deferred payment security; (4) SEPA CT and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for all euro-denominated EEA trade payments below EUR 100,000; and (5) commercial documentary collections (DP/DA) via the collecting bank in the supplier's country. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (IRC) plus a 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC and municipal business tax at communal rates; aggregate effective headline in Luxembourg City approximately 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders; 0% under the domestic participation exemption where the parent holds at least 10% or EUR 1.2 million, for at least 12 months, and meets the anti-abuse requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within six months after the end of the fiscal year; for a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 30 June of the following year. Extensions may be granted on justified application to the competent local tax office.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Luxembourg entity must prepare and retain a Local File when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750 million and the Luxembourg entity's aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 1.5 million per year; the Master File is required at the same group turnover threshold.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation code analogous to offshore financial centers; however, treaty access, the beneficial ownership rule, ATAD anti-abuse provisions, and Luxembourg jurisprudence require a SARL to demonstrate real substance: registered office, qualified directors, proportionate operational staff, board and management decisions, physical or virtual premises, and adequate records. An online retailer of outdoor camping gear must substantiate its e-commerce functions, inventory and logistics arrangements, contracting counterparts, payment rails, and day-to-day management held in Luxembourg if it wishes to secure tax residency and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the taxable base under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, late payment interest at 7.2% per annum, administrative penalties up to the full amount of the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution with fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or commit fraudulent acts can be made jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; criminal proceedings may result in asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification from managing any Luxembourg company, and imprisonment.
Abusive Shell / Letterbox Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residency and treaty benefits, anti-abuse recharacterization of the entity's transactions, retroactive assessment plus interest, administrative abuse-of-law penalties, and potential criminal sanctions for facilitating tax evasion by the foreign beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: De jure and de facto directors may be ordered to pay recharacterized tax liabilities where they have lent their identity to a sham structure; criminal courts may issue exit bans, seize personal assets, and impose custodial sentences for complicity in abusive arrangements.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 125,000 per failure under Luxembourg laws implementing AML/UBO requirements; penalties may also include further orders by the Luxembourg financial regulator if the payment services are linked to Stripe or PayPal, and loss of eligibility for tax treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons failing to submit UBO declarations face personal fines and, in serious cases, criminal liability. They may be barred from holding corporate office, subjected to travel restrictions in connection with ongoing investigations, and held civilly liable for investor or creditor damages.
Intercompany Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of the Luxembourg taxable base, possible secondary adjustment recharacterizing excess profits as a dividend subject to 15% dividend withholding tax, denial of deductibility of the related-party charges, late payment interest, and administrative penalties for failure to maintain or present transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately implement or approve a non-compliant cross-border pricing system may be personally assessed as joint debtors for resulting tax debts where they have acted outside the interest of the company and in violation of Luxembourg corporate law.

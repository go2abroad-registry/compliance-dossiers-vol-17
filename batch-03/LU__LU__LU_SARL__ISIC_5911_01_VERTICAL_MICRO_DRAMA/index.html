Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), attached to the Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg; electronic filings made via the eJuste platform.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended; SARL regime under Articles 442-1 to 466; notarized incorporation deed required under Article 5; legal personality conferred upon RCS registration under Article 10; filing and publication with the Luxembourg Business Registers under Article 90.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif containing the Statuts), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. The full capital must be subscribed as cash or in-kind; each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value, with cash contributions (minimum EUR 3,000) deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and evidenced by a banker's blocking certificate; contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and valued by a statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (attestation de sécurité incendie) from the Administration des services de secours (CGDISS) for the registered office and any studio; structural load certificate for office occupancy; occupational clearance from ITM covering ergonomic workstations, electrical installations, emergency exits, and the submission of the labor regulations (règlement intérieur); no special civil-defense clearance is required for streaming platforms.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. The full capital must be subscribed as cash or in-kind; each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value, with cash contributions (minimum EUR 3,000) deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and evidenced by a banker's blocking certificate; contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and valued by a statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Unanimously adopted.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 5911 digital short-drama production and OTT distribution; no industrial establishment (établissement classé) authorization required. If a physical recording studio is later added, the competent authority is the Ministry of the Economy – Direction générale de l'industrie (commodo/incommodo procedure under the Law of 14 March 2017).

Category: Not applicable – no operational manufacturing permit for a short-drama streaming platform; optional incentive-based registration with Film Fund Luxembourg (Fonds national de soutien à la production audiovisuelle) for co-production subsidies only.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration (https://impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (https://pfi.public.lu); Luxembourg Business Registers / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (LBR/RCS, https://www.lbr.lu); unified e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed; the notary registers the company with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), obtaining the 11-digit business registration number (matricule) and, if required, the national EORI number. Step 2: Upon RCS registration, the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA) ex officio for taxable companies; the SARL confirms activity details via Form 640 submitted electronically through MyGuichet.lu or the AEDT eCDF portal. Step 3: The ACD issues the corporate income tax NIF (numéro d'identification fiscale) for income tax and municipal business tax purposes; the SARL registers for impôt commercial communal (ICC) with the commune of its registered seat. Step 4 (non-resident suppliers): A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment that makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AEDT (Form 640A for EU-established taxable persons); non-EU entities must appoint an approved Luxembourg tax representative (Article 63 of the Luxembourg VAT Law) unless the OSS/IOSS or reverse-charge mechanism applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; territorial_scope = resident company subject to worldwide taxation with participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR); pass_through = not eligible, classical corporate tax system; tax_exempt_zone = not applicable. A Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable resident capital company: corporate income tax 15% plus 7% solidarity surcharge (16.05% base), municipal business tax (ICC) 6.75%-11.5%, and net wealth tax 0.5%; aggregate effective rate in Luxembourg City approximately 24.94%. — Step 1: Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed; the notary registers the company with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), obtaining the 11-digit business registration number (matricule) and, if required, the national EORI number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; territorial_scope = resident company subject to worldwide taxation with participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR); pass_through = not eligible, classical corporate tax system; tax_exempt_zone = not applicable. A Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable resident capital company: corporate income tax 15% plus 7% solidarity surcharge (16.05% base), municipal business tax (ICC) 6.75%-11.5%, and net wealth tax 0.5%; aggregate effective rate in Luxembourg City approximately 24.94%.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; classification is statutory. Available elections and regimes: (1) Fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR for resident subsidiaries at least 95% owned by a Luxembourg resident company; (2) Participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR) for dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings of at least 10% or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost held at least 12 months; (3) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Art. 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (4) Option to subject real estate leases to VAT under Art. 43 of the VAT Law (not relevant to digital micro-drama streaming); (5) ATAD/ATAD2 hybrid mismatch rules (Law of 21 June 2019) apply automatically without elective relief; (6) Audiovisual investment tax credit of 30-35% of qualifying production expenditure for ISIC 5911 production activities, subject to prior approval by the Luxembourg Film Fund.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the small-business exemption (franchise) under Art. 57 of the VAT Law. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register when making taxable supplies in Luxembourg unless reverse charge applies. For intra-EU distance sales of electronic services to B2C consumers, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold (Art. 57bis VAT Law) determines whether Luxembourg VAT applies; digital streaming sales to non-EU consumers fall outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT (exonération avec droit à déduction) applies to: (1) export of goods outside the EU (Art. 39 VAT Law / Art. 146 EU VAT Directive) with proof of physical exit from EU customs territory; (2) intra-Community supplies of goods to a B2B customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number (Art. 38 VAT Law / Art. 138 EU VAT Directive); (3) services directly related to exports and international transport (Arts. 40-42 VAT Law). For the vertical micro-drama streaming business: streaming and digital downloads sold B2C to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply is the customer's residence under Art. 58 VAT Law); B2B licensing of intellectual and economic rights in micro-drama content to non-EU licensees qualifies as zero-rated/outside scope where the recipient is established outside the EU (Art. 14(1)(11°) VAT Law), preserving full input VAT deduction. Substantiation requires evidence of the customer's status as a taxable person, country of establishment, and actual exploitation of the rights.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD lodged in the Luxembourg PLDA customs system, including EORI number and customs office exit confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR Road Consignment Note, or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of goods from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment records evidencing receipt of proceeds in convertible currency (for global distribution licensing: the license agreement, royalty statements, and remittance records)
  • Commercial invoices with packing list, and for intra-EU B2B supplies: the customer's valid VAT identification number and the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control API mandate exists in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is permitted (EN 16931 for B2G under Directive 2014/55/EU) but B2B e-invoicing is not mandated. Electronic VAT return filing is mandatory for SARLs via the AEDT eCDF portal with monthly, quarterly, or annual frequency based on turnover. Payment service providers (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, Stripe) must report cross-border payments under the EU CESOP framework (Regulation 2020/283) quarterly to the ACD/AEDT. SAF-T format is used for tax audit data exchange without pre-clearance. The OSS/IOSS portals are used to declare B2C digital services and distance sales of goods.

The AEDT may retrospectively assess Luxembourg VAT at 17% plus late interest of 0.8% per month where export substantiation is deficient. Under Art. 53 of the VAT Law and the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 30 December 1991, proof of export/intra-community supply must be completed within 3 months of the transaction and retained for 10 years. Administrative fines under Arts. 61-62 of the VAT Law range from EUR 250 to EUR 4,000 for non-compliance. VAT fraud, including false export declarations, sham licensing structures, or circular transactions aimed at obtaining zero-rating or input VAT refunds, is punishable by imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and/or fines of EUR 4,000 to EUR 250,000, with a 10-year limitation period for fraudulent assessment and a 5-year period in non-fraud cases. The AEDT coordinates with OLAF and foreign tax administrations under EU mutual assistance and DAC Directives. Transfer-pricing documentation and economic substance evidence must substantiate the royalty and IP licensing arrangements on audit; the Luxembourg IP box (80% exemption) and net wealth tax filings are separately scrutinized.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements for capital movements, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or profit remittances. Free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed by Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Non-resident ownership of a Luxembourg SARL is permitted and capital inflows and outflows may be made freely in any currency. Only standard corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and any treaty-based withholding taxes apply on repatriated profits, and no separate FX or central bank clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg and the euro area do not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines for export proceeds. Foreign currency receivables may be retained, converted, or transferred freely.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting under the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); monetary and foreign exchange policy authority is the European Central Bank (ECB) for the euro area. Banking and payment licensing supervision rests with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. A fintech electronic money institution (EMI) authorised in another EEA state may provide local euro IBANs, SEPA payments, and currency conversion facilities in Luxembourg through the PSD2 passporting regime without a separate Luxembourg banking licence. Legal constraints include prohibition on lending or credit services, safeguarding requirements for client funds, limited access to SWIFT correspondent banking, and potentially enhanced due diligence when handling high-volume app-store receipts. An EMI is viable as a secondary operational or collection rail, but a full-service Luxembourg credit institution is recommended for capital escrow, treasury management, and large trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound euro capital contributions are routed via the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) or standard SWIFT transfers; cross-currency contributions use the SWIFT network with ISO 20022 transaction purpose code EQUI (equity capital) or the Combined Nomenclature purpose classification for direct investments. Verification requires the corporate subscription resolutions, notarised board minutes, share register updates, UBO declarations, and confirmation of the originating bank for source-of-funds screening. Customer funds are released to the operational account only after satisfactory AML/KYC checks and, where relevant, the declaration of exceptional cash payments.Outbound settlements for production vendor services, distribution licences, and streaming software expenses are executed via SWIFT MT103 for single direct payments, MT202 COV for correspondent bank cover, and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for low-value intra-EEA disbursements. Large contractual disbursements to production suppliers may be structured as commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) at sight or with deferred payment terms, supported by conforming shipping or delivery documentation; documentary collections (SWIFT MT400/MT540) are used where security of exchange is required but without full letter-of-credit formality. Every disbursement is reconciled against the underlying contract, purchase order, or licence agreement and logged against corporate tax and transfer pricing records.Outright deliverable FX forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNY for committed revenue and cost flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with restricted deliverability referenced in streaming-market settlementCross-currency swaps (CCS) to transform USD-denominated app-store revenues into EUR treasury obligationsVanilla FX options (purchased put and call options) on EUR/USD for defensive hedging without upside limitationFX swaps combining a spot trade with a matched forward transaction for temporary liquidity coverage

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 in aggregate for cross-border controlled transactions of the same category during the tax year; Luxembourg does not impose a separate statutory local-file threshold for small operators, but the tax administration may require full OECD-aligned TP documentation when this threshold is exceeded. For groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, a CbC report must be filed with the Luxembourg tax authorities via the Ministry of Finance.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance principles through general anti-abuse rules (the ATAD GAAR, implemented by Article 6 of the Law on Tax Procedure) and through the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI. A SARL producing and distributing vertical micro-dramas must demonstrate sufficient local substance: real operational management in Luxembourg, qualified employees engaged in production, licensing, or distribution activities, independent decision-making for acquisition and monetisation of film rights, actual office space, and bank accounts. A conduit that merely interposes Luxembourg between third-party video platforms and foreign production companies without operational substance will be disregarded, and income will be requalified in the hands of the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines from EUR 10,000 to EUR 50,000 plus 10% increase of the evaded tax and late interest of 0.5% per month; criminal prosecution under Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO §§395–400) may impose imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for deliberate false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns, authorise artificial transfer pricing markups, or direct the diversion of streaming revenue are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; travel bans and provisional detention may be ordered, and convicted directors face disqualification from managing Luxembourg legal entities under the commercial code.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the entire arrangement under the GAAR and the MLI Principal Purpose Test; denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of intercompany licensing revenue as taxable Luxembourg income; imposition of 15% dividend WHT on constructive distributions; plus an administrative penalty of 50% of the recharacterised tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who act as nominal managers for a shell arrangement may be personally assessed for the shortfall, face forced removal of the company from the Luxembourg trade register, and be criminally liable for fraud against creditors; travel restrictions under the Luxembourg tax collection procedure are also available against directors of disregarded shell companies.
AML / Know-Your-Customer Breach

Statutory sanction: Adminstrative fine of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover under the Law of 12 November 2004 (AMLD4/5 implementation), plus asset freezing by the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit; the company may be prohibited from continuing the payment rails and bank accounts may be closed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly omit UBO information, sign false AML declarations, or authorise payments to a blacklisted party face personal fines up to EUR 500,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, and civil restitution for any deception-induced losses; the corporate veil is pierced where the SARL serves as a Cuir-Lux instrument in a fraudulent international scheme.

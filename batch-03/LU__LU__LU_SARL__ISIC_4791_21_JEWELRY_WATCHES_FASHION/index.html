Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Ministry of Justice, Luxembourg City.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 et seq., as modernized by the Law of 10 August 2016), read with the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Law of 12 November 2004 on anti-money laundering (AML/UBO declarations).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) executed before a Luxembourg civil-law notary, filed electronically with the RCS and published in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations); official RCS state fee EUR 19 (approx. USD 21), excluding notarial and bank fees.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 1 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 fully subscribed; cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 20% of the subscribed capital (EUR 2,400) before signature, deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the name of the company in formation; contributions in kind must be fully paid up; the blocked funds are released only upon issue of the RCS registration extract.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 fully subscribed; cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 20% of the subscribed capital (EUR 2,400) before signature, deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the name of the company in formation; contributions in kind must be fully paid up; the blocked funds are released only upon issue of the RCS registration extract.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises for customs and EORI matters; all official declarations are filed electronically through the MyGuichet.lu portal and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders incorporate the SARL by notarized deed and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain the institutional matricule number, which functions as the definitive corporate TIN; thereafter the SARL applies to the AEDT for a VAT identification number in the format LU plus 8 digits (resident registration for the Luxembourg SARL, or non-established taxable person procedure via form 1020 for foreign PEs); a separate EORI number is requested from the customs administration to enable import and export declarations. Registration is performed electronically with authenticated eIDs, and non-established taxpayers may appoint a fiscal representative for VAT compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident SARL is a capital-company taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (CIT 17%), the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), and municipal business tax (aggregate statutory burden in Luxembourg City approx. 24.94%); worldwide income is taxable on residence; non-residents are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a permanent establishment; no pass-through eligibility applies. — Foreign founders incorporate the SARL by notarized deed and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain the institutional matricule number, which functions as the definitive corporate TIN; thereafter the SARL applies to the AEDT for a VAT identification number in the format LU plus 8 digits (resident registration for the Luxembourg SARL, or non-established taxable person procedure via form 1020 for foreign PEs); a separate EORI number is requested from the customs administration to enable import and export declarations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident SARL is a capital-company taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (CIT 17%), the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), and municipal business tax (aggregate statutory burden in Luxembourg City approx. 24.94%); worldwide income is taxable on residence; non-residents are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a permanent establishment; no pass-through eligibility applies.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime. Available elections include the opt-in fiscal unity (integration fiscale) for 95%-or-more owned resident group companies upon joint application, the VAT grouping (assujetti unique) election, the deemed-resident election for qualifying commercial permanent establishments, and the election to conclude arm's-length advance pricing agreements. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 1 and 2 operate mandatorily and are not elective; the participation exemption is applied automatically upon satisfaction of holding period, shareholding and substance conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT-exclusive) for the domestic small-business exemption; EUR 10,000 annual intra-Community distance-sales threshold for jewelry, watches and fashion goods; no registration threshold applies to non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg; registration becomes mandatory immediately on breach of the de minimis threshold or upon taxable importation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to (i) export supplies of jewelry, watches and fashion goods dispatched or transported outside the EU, provided tangible proof of export is retained in conformity with Article 146 of the VAT Directive and Luxembourg national rules; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to purchasers holding a valid EU VAT identification number, subject to EC Sales List reporting; and (iii) ancillary freight, transport and handling services directly linked to exported goods. The exporter must substantiate physical movement out of the EU, the sale consideration, customer identity and the settlement rails used.

  • Customs export declaration lodged through the EU Customs Declaration System (CDS) via the Single Administrative Document (SAD) or accepted postal/courier outward manifests
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or final-mile courier delivery proof evidencing departure of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms 2020, 8-digit HS classification (jewelry 7113/7117; watches 9101/9102/9105) and full sale consideration
  • For intra-Community zero-rating: customer VAT registration certificate and VIES validation report
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103 settlement advice, or payment acquirer (Stripe/PayPal) settlement statements proving remittance of export proceeds
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) and, where applicable, Intrastat dispatch declaration

No general B2B e-invoicing mandate and no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Luxembourg; PEPPOL/EN 16931 e-invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices to the Luxembourg State under Directive 2014/55/EU transposition; VAT returns and recapitulative statements are e-filed via MyGuichet.lu; taxpayers must retain SAF-T-type digital audit files and complete audit trails for AEDT inspection; Luxembourg is monitoring the EU ViDA e-invoicing and reporting proposals.

Fraudulent export zero-rating is prosecuted as VAT fraud: the AEDT and the customs administration jointly audit export transactions using digital platform data, courier manifests, HS-code profiling and payment rail analytics. Remedial measures include retrospective VAT reassessment and clawback of improperly zero-rated output tax, default interest at 0.6% per month, administrative penalties up to 100% of the evaded VAT, and, under the criminal tax procedure law of 17 March 2020, fines ranging from EUR 125,000 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for aggravated tax fraud; the VAT assessment limitation period is extended to 10 years upon proof of fraud or wilful misconduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. No exchange controls or approval requirements exist for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital. A Luxembourg SARL may freely open foreign currency accounts and settle in any convertible currency, subject only to general EU sanctions and anti-money laundering rules. The sole legal restriction is the balance-sheet maintenance of subscribed minimum capital (EUR 12,000 for SARL) under the Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender, repurchase, or mandatory conversion obligation exists in Luxembourg. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in EUR or in foreign currency at the exporter's discretion. Corporate treasury should nevertheless document all export sales and payment inflows in the statutory annual accounts for taxable income determination.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) for central banking and monetary policy coordination with the European Central Bank; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) regulates banking conduct and foreign exchange intermediation. Luxembourg has no separate foreign exchange administration; transactions are governed by EU capital movement freedoms under Articles 63–66 TFEU and the Luxembourg Monetary Law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg, such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Adyen, for operating the daily e-commerce clearing and marketpay placeouts to customers. These EMIs provide easier API integration for Stripe/PayPal settlement and international payout. However, they cannot be used for the statutory statutory capital escrow account required for a Luxembourg SARL, and CSSF supervision requires that client funds be safeguarded in segregated accounts at a credit institution. The e-commerce business model (jewelry/watches) is treated as medium-high money laundering risk, so EMIs will apply additional KYC scrutiny and transaction monitoring.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO jurisdiction combined with opaque beneficial ownership in non-OECD tax havens or sanctioned territories.

Root cause: UBO jurisdiction combined with opaque beneficial ownership in non-OECD tax havens or sanctioned territories.

Defense evidence: Certified registry extracts from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), a signed beneficial ownership declaration under the Luxembourg UBO Register Act of 13 January 2019, notarial certificate of the SARL's incorporation, bank references from the UBOs, written source-of-wealth analysis with bank statements for the previous 12 months, and a copy of the shareholder register.
High transaction velocity and digital marketplace exposure for high-value watches and jewelry, presenting unexplained international payment flows from multiple e-commerce gateways.

Root cause: High transaction velocity and digital marketplace exposure for high-value watches and jewelry, presenting unexplained international payment flows from multiple e-commerce gateways.

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan mapping the ISIC 4791 model, contractual settlement structure and fee statements from Stripe/PayPal, description of goods and sourcing invoices, complete customer and supplier due diligence files, chargeback and refund analytics, transaction flow charts, and a mandatory AML/CTF programme with an appointed compliance officer.
Artefact and precious goods provenance risk: jewelry/watch inventory sourced from outside the EU that may involve antique/cultural property or diamond-related sanctions.

Root cause: Artefact and precious goods provenance risk: jewelry/watch inventory sourced from outside the EU that may involve antique/cultural property or diamond-related sanctions.

Defense evidence: Certificates of authenticity, supplier certificates of provenance, EU market surveillance and nominal hallmarking documentation for precious metals, Kimberley Process Certificate if rough diamonds are involved, customs declarations (EU Single Administrative Document), import duty records, and a product due diligence policy aligned with the EU Conflict Minerals Regulation (EU 2017/821).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inject equity contributions via SEPA credit transfer or SWIFT MT103 to the SARL's segregated capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution. Use EBICS purpose code 'CAPIT' (from the EBA EPC list) and include in the remittance field the share subscription certificate number and the RCS registration number. For second and subsequent tranches, reference the notarised capital increase deed and the board resolution authorising the injection. Remittance advice must encode the invoice number, purchase order, and HS code.

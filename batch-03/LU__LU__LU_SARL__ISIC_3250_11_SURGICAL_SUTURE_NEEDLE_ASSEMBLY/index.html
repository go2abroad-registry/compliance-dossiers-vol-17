Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax; business registration via Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) at www.lbr.lu; taxpayer portal: www.guichet.lu and www.myguichet.lu

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment, VAT registration is made electronically through MyGuichet or by filing Form 1, Déclaration d'assujettissement à la TVA, with the AEDT. EU-established taxable persons may register directly; non-EU-established persons generally must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative, sign a joint mandate with the AEDT, and provide corporate constitutive documents, proof of original foreign VAT ID, and business licences. The AEDT in turn issues a Luxembourg VAT number (TVA LU), which becomes the VAT ID for electronic invoicing, export refund claims and Intrastat declarations. Income tax TIN for local SARL is the matricule number assigned by the RCS/LBR and is used for direct tax filings. The application must be accompanied by supporting documentation evidencing the intended taxable activity in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment, VAT registration is made electronically through MyGuichet or by filing Form 1, Déclaration d'assujettissement à la TVA, with the AEDT. EU-established taxable persons may register directly; non-EU-established persons generally must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative, sign a joint mandate with the AEDT, and provide corporate constitutive documents, proof of original foreign VAT ID, and business licences. The application must be accompanied by supporting documentation evidencing the intended taxable activity in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. Resident SARLs are default entity-level taxpayers subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax. Available statutory elections include Luxembourg VAT grouping (assujetti unique under Article 63 L.L.T.V.A.) and corporate income tax group relief under Article 164bis LIR for resident companies forming a 95% owned fiscal unity. Hybrid mismatch rules under the EU ATAD are mandatorily applied; no elective hybrid mismatch treatment is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 net annual turnover (small business exemption threshold); non-established taxable persons have no general turnover threshold and must register if Luxembourg VAT becomes chargeable, subject to the EU OSS, reverse charge and distance sales rules

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A taxable export of goods produced in Luxembourg, including surgical suture needle assemblies, is eligible for the 0% VAT rate under Article 56 L.L.T.V.A. when the goods are physically shipped or transported to a destination outside the European Union by the supplier, the customer or a third party acting on their behalf, and the goods leave the EU no later than three months after the supply. The exporter must retain conclusive and consistent documentary evidence proving the actual departure from the EU, the identity of the acquirer and the price paid. Offshore services directly linked to exported goods (e.g. freight, handling and ancillary logistics) may also qualify for zero rating.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export date
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, CIM railway consignment note or air waybill evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice annotated with the legal zero-rating reference (Article 56 L.L.T.V.A.)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / bank credit confirmation showing settlement of the export sale
  • Evidence of the non-EU customer identity and destination customs status, including delivery note and transport insurance certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies to standard B2B transactions in Luxembourg. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to the Luxembourg public sector through Peppol-BIS / Factur-X. Electronic invoices must conform to EU VAT invoicing authenticity rules and may be exchanged via any technology that guarantees chain of custody. There is no mandatory API-based continuous transaction control tax 'clearance' in force.

Luxembourg tax authorities closely scrutinise export zero-rating claims and may deny the 0% rate and reassess VAT at 17% where the export movement cannot be objectively substantiated. Penalties include late-payment interest, an additional tax surcharge of up to twice the tax evaded, criminal fines up to EUR 25,000, retrospective clawback of input VAT recovered under false export declarations, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Article 80 L.L.T.V.A. The standard five-year limitation period permits retrospective assessments and recovery of under-declared VAT attributable to unjustified export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has full currency convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. As a euro-area member, the euro is the lawful currency. Intra-EU capital movements, payments, and profit repatriation are fully liberalised under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Movement Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC). No authorisation or central bank notification is required for profit remittances, dividend declarations, interest, royalties, or repayment of intra-group loans, subject only to general AML/KYC documentary thresholds, withholding tax obligations, and the EU Anti-Money Laundering framework. Non-euro receipts may be converted freely at market rates. The practical hurdle is not convertibility but corporate documentation and beneficial ownership transparency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts in Luxembourg or the wider euro area. Exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts indefinitely, provided that such accounts are declared within the BCL's external statistics reports (Collection, reporting of balance of payments and international investment position) when threshold triggers are met. A surgical suture manufacturer receiving USD export proceeds from the United States or elsewhere may hold, sweep, or convert those proceeds without FX surrender; however, for AML and treasury efficiency, the company should adopt a cash concentration policy and record a monthly FX remittance forecast.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem; supervisory authority for macro/micro-prudential and monetary operations is the BCL, while the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises banks, payment institutions, and e-money institutions. The Luxembourg Ministry of Finance sets legislative policy, but for foreign exchange control there is no general licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with significant legal caveats. Luxembourg allows passporting of EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions under PSD2, subject to authorisation by either the CSSF or the home-state authority. For a manufacturing SARL requiring trade settlement via SWIFT MT103 and documentary letters of credit, digital EMIs such as Belvian, Mangopay, or cross-border EMI licences in Lithuania, Ireland, or the Netherlands can technically open virtual IBANs and offer FX. However, they are not suitable substitutes for a Tier-1 domestic bank because (i) most EMIs do not provide letter of credit issuance or discounting, (ii) underwriting is frequently algorithmically risk-averse to medical manufacturing and interposed holding structures, and (iii) a euro-area operating company should maintain an account at a CSSF-supervised credit institution to obtain robust bank reference letters for VAT, trade, and future fundraising. Offshore fintech EMIs in third countries are legally disfavoured for treasury clearing and are unlikely to meet the standards of Luxembourg's bank reference requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial share capital injection and subsequent capital increases of the LU_SARL, the share subscription amount must be wired to the bank account of the notary or the SARL's designated euro capital escrow account before incorporation. The wire shall be executed by SWIFT MT103 with a precise remittance information field (field 70) referencing: 'SUBSCRIPTION AGREEMENT [date] – SHAREHOLDERS [name(s)] – SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – NOTARY REPERTOIRE NUMBER'. Beneficiary bank should be the domestic Tier-1 lender selected for escrow. For shareholder loans or intercompany route funding originating from related parties, the routing must be clearly labelled as a loan disbursement and preferably be structured by written loan agreement to confirm arm's-length terms; however, no Luxembourg foreign exchange approval is required. The bank will execute AML source-of-funds verification, obtain a 'BLOCKING CONFIRMATION' (attestation de blocage de fonds), and release funds only after the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL) filing. For all external assets and liabilities above €500,000 or outstanding threshold, the SARL must file a BCL balance-of-payments statistical report (reporting scheme of BCL Direct Investment and Financial Assets/Liabilities) through its bank. Inbound currency other than euro must be converted on the same day into EUR through the bank's treasury, using BCL reference rates or permitted daily market fix, and the conversion confirmation must be archived.The primary clearing rail for surgical suture trade disbursements shall be SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for advance payments, open-account settlements, and distributor payments, and SWIFT MT700/MT720 irrevocable documentary letters of credit for confirmed purchase orders, especially from public hospital tenders and government distributors. The beneficiary of inbound LCs may be the LU_SARL, but in high-risk markets the SARL should advise LCs through Luxembourg Tier-1 bank and request confirmation by a top-25 global bank if sovereign risk or LC-issuing bank risk is elevated. For payments related to raw material imports (polyglycolic acid, polymer needles, packaging, sterilization services), the SARL shall use MT103/TT wire transfer with e-invoicing consistency; for capital equipment imports, an unconfirmed or confirmed L/C at sight is appropriate. All SWIFT messages must include the correct ISO 20022 purpose codes: 'TRPT' (payment of trade), 'CAPT' (capital transfer), 'GDST' (goods trade), 'INTC' (intra-group financial transactions) or 'TREA' (treasury operations) as applicable, plus a full reference to the SEPA direct debit/creditor identifier and VAT number to facilitate customs and VAT clearance. The holding of foreign-currency operating accounts at the Tier-1 bank will allow natural matching of USD/CHF receipts to raw material disbursements; surplus conversion can be executed by the bank’s automatic sweep module.

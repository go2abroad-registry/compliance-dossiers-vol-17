Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg has a unitary national tax administration: (1) Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - competent for VAT registration, e-TVA filings, indirect tax audits, and customs liaison; (2) Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - competent for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC), net wealth tax (IF), and individual income tax; (3) Administration des douanes et accises (ADDA) - competent for customs export/import procedures and excise. Official electronic portals: MyGuichet.lu (business portal), guichet.lu (legal information), ACD eCDF e-filing portal, AEDT e-TVA portal, and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) e-Registration for RCS filings. No sub-national tax distinctions apply.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) Incorporate by notarial deed and register the SARL with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the LBR e-Registration platform; registration with the RCS creates the company's matricule number and automatically triggers opening of the corporate tax file with the ACD, which assigns the national tax identification number (numéro d'identification). (2) File the RCS incorporation confirmation and register the beneficial owner in the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE). (3) Before commencing taxable activity, submit form 640 (Déclaration d'assujettissement à la TVA) to the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg VAT number in the format LU + 8 digits (e.g. LU26375215); issuance typically takes 2-3 weeks with no registration fee. (4) For a non-resident foreign entity selling directly into Luxembourg without an establishment: register directly with the AEDT (no turnover threshold for local taxable supplies subject to Luxembourg VAT); a non-EU supplier must appoint an AEDT-approved fiscal representative (représentant responsable). (5) Use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal for distance sales of goods to EU consumers below the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold and for B2C telecommunications/broadcasting/electronic services. (6) Note: separate foreign-direct-investment screening under the Luxembourg FDI law may apply to non-EU shareholders, but it is not a tax precondition. The foreign parent does not require a Luxembourg TIN if the SARL is the taxable operator; a notarized power of attorney should be granted to the local tax representative/manager.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident SARL (siège statutaire in Luxembourg) is a corporate tax subject fully taxable at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available for a SARL under Luxembourg domestic law. Corporate income tax (IRC) applies at a flat 17% on taxable income above EUR 200,000 (with reduced graduated brackets for profits up to EUR 200,000); a 7% solidarity surcharge applies on the IRC; municipal business tax (ICC) applies at approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City, yielding a combined effective income tax burden of approximately 24.94%; net wealth tax (IF) applies at 0.5%. Worldwide scope applies to residents, mitigated by the participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) for qualifying dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings of at least 10% held for 12 months; non-resident companies are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident SARL (siège statutaire in Luxembourg) is a corporate tax subject fully taxable at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available for a SARL under Luxembourg domestic law. Corporate income tax (IRC) applies at a flat 17% on taxable income above EUR 200,000 (with reduced graduated brackets for profits up to EUR 200,000); a 7% solidarity surcharge applies on the IRC; municipal business tax (ICC) applies at approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City, yielding a combined effective income tax burden of approximately 24.94%; net wealth tax (IF) applies at 0.5%. Worldwide scope applies to residents, mitigated by the participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) for qualifying dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings of at least 10% held for 12 months; non-resident companies are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income. No tax-exempt zone applies to the Luxembourg SARL.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide a domestic check-the-box or general fiscal-transparency election; a SARL is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Available elections: (a) Fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR - a Luxembourg-resident company or Luxembourg branch of an EEA company holding at least 95% of the SARL may elect to consolidate the SARL's taxable results for IRC/ICC purposes (minimum 5-year commitment); (b) VAT grouping (unité TVA) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law - the SARL and linked Luxembourg-established entities with financial, economic, and organisational links may file as a single VAT taxpayer with one VAT number; (c) US federal tax classification election - the SARL (or its US parent) may file IRS Form 8832 to treat the SARL as a disregarded entity or partnership for US federal tax purposes only, with no Luxembourg law effect; (d) Hybrid mismatch rules (Article 168ter LIR, ATAD implementation) disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements and are mandatory, not elective; (e) The SARL may elect to apply the participation exemption rather than the underlying-tax credit method and may opt into the Luxembourg IP box regime (Article 50bis LIR) providing an 80% exemption on qualifying IP income derived from nexus-eligible assets.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR (euro). Luxembourg has no de minimis turnover threshold for establishment-based registration: any Luxembourg-established SARL carrying out taxable supply of goods or services must register for VAT from its first taxable transaction; simplified annual filing is available only where annual turnover is below EUR 30,000. For non-established sellers: no registration threshold applies to B2B supplies subject to reverse charge or to Luxembourg-consumer supplies requiring local VAT; the EU-wide distance-selling threshold is EUR 10,000 in total cross-border B2C distance sales of goods, above which Luxembourg VAT registration becomes mandatory (the exporter may instead register in each destination member state or use OSS); B2C TBE services require registration in Luxembourg without any threshold unless declared through the non-union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 56 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (EU Article 146(1)(a)): supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the EU are exempt with credit (0% rate) provided the goods physically leave EU territory within 90 days of supply and the supplier retains contemporaneous transport evidence. Article 57: intra-EU B2B dispatch of goods is zero-rated where the customer supplies a valid EU VAT ID (VIES-validated), the goods are dispatched within 90 days, and the supplier files the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) and, where applicable, Intrastat declarations. Cross-dock 3PL services (storage, handling, picking, packing, and reloading at the Luxembourg fulfillment hub) directly connected with the export of goods to a third country are zero-rated under Article 58 (EU Article 146(1)(e)) where the logistics contract, customs status of the goods, and warehouse records demonstrate the export destination. Input VAT refunds: the Luxembourg SARL deducts input VAT on its periodic e-TVA return; a non-EU client may reclaim Luxembourg input VAT under the 13th Directive refund procedure through the AEDT with application by 30 June of the year following the charge year, while EU-established claimants use the portal of their own member state with statutory deadline of 30 September.

No statutory B2B e-invoicing mandate and no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax certification regime applies in Luxembourg as of 2025. B2G mandate: invoices issued to Luxembourg public-sector entities must be transmitted as structured e-invoices (PEPPOL/UBL format) through the national e-invoicing platform; phased B2G enforcement was completed in 2023. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and periodic payments are filed electronically through the AEDT e-TVA portal (monthly for turnover above EUR 2 million, quarterly for EUR 30,000-2 million, annual for below EUR 30,000). Customs export and import declarations are lodged electronically with the ADDA via the EU Export Control System (ECS) and COVID/ICS2 systems; no separate tax clearance or stamping certificate is required for export consignments. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package is expected to introduce EU-wide structured e-invoicing and transaction-level digital reporting for cross-border B2B supplies by 2030; Luxembourg transposition is pending and should be monitored for the cross-dock 3PL operating model.

AEDT and ADDA may audit VAT and customs positions within the general 5-year limitation period (extended to 10 years where VAT fraud or intentional evasion is established). Where the export zero-rating is not substantiated - in particular where the 90-day physical dispatch rule is not met or the SAD/BL/warehouse documentation is inconsistent - the supply is re-characterized as taxable in Luxembourg at the standard 17% rate (effective 1 January 2025) with retrospective clawback of the differential VAT for all related consignments, plus statutory late-payment interest (indexed annually, approximately 7.8%-7.9% per annum) and administrative penalties under Articles 65-68 of the VAT Law: a 10% augmentation for documentary negligence, fines generally from EUR 1,000 to EUR 10,000, and for intentional evasion an administrative fine of up to 100% of the tax evaded; criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg Criminal Code may add imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and director disqualification. Fraudulent export schemes (fictitious goods, forged SAD/BL, shell consignees, circular transport) trigger joint and several liability of the SARL, its managers, and the 3PL warehouse operator, and the zero-rating is clawed back with full retroactivity plus interest. Luxembourg has no exchange controls and the BCL imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day proceeds-realization convention used in this compliance pipeline serves as the documentary audit benchmark - proceeds received materially later than 180 days invite transfer-pricing, VAT-benefit, and commercial-reality scrutiny and may result in reclassification of the supply as taxable at 17%.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. No exchange-control regime restricts current-account or capital-account transactions; the free movement of capital is guaranteed by TFEU Article 63 and EU Directive 88/361/EEC. Profit remittance by an SARL requires an ordinary shareholders' resolution and satisfaction of the Luxembourg net-asset maintenance test (minimum legal capital of EUR 12,000 maintained); no exchange-control approval is required. Outbound dividends attract a 15% domestic withholding tax, reducible under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation quota for export proceeds. The 3PL fulfillment entity may retain receivables in EUR, USD, or any freely chosen currency in multi-currency accounts, subject only to standard corporate reporting, transfer-pricing documentation, and annual financial-statement disclosure obligations under Luxembourg tax law.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as prudential and AML/CFT supervisor, the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for financial-sector policy, and the European Central Bank (ECB) as the currency authority within the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High for operational liquidity. PSD2-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passporting into Luxembourg (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, PPRO) provide multi-currency settlement, local-ACH collection, and lower-cost FX conversion for the e-commerce fulfillment cash cycle. Legal constraints: such EMIs are not credit institutions under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993; they cannot issue the notarially recognised capital-blockage certificate (certificat de blocage de fonds) required by the Luxembourg notary for the SARL's EUR 12,000 paid-up capital, and escrow or large-balance treasury roles are generally reserved to CSSF-licensed credit institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL's EUR 12,000 minimum capital must be fully paid up via a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer from the subscriber to a blocked account (compte séquestre) opened at a Luxembourg credit institution in the name of the company in formation; the notary's attestation confirms the blockage, and funds are released upon RCSL registration. Recommended purpose codes: 'CAPITAL' or ISO 20022 'G100' for capital contributions; the remitter name must exactly match the subscriber named in the articles of association, and the bank may request the shareholders' subscription resolution and pro-forma incorporation deeds. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High for operational liquidity. PSD2-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passporting into Luxembourg (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, PPRO) provide multi-currency settlement, local-ACH collection, and lower-cost FX conversion for the e-commerce fulfillment cash cycle. Legal constraints: such EMIs are not credit institutions under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993; they cannot issue the notarially recognised capital-blockage certificate (certificat de blocage de fonds) required by the Luxembourg notary for the SARL's EUR 12,000 paid-up capital, and escrow or large-balance treasury roles are generally reserved to CSSF-licensed credit institutions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million group turnover for Master File; EUR 1 million intercompany transactions for Local File

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) requiring entities to have adequate office, employees, and management in Luxembourg. For a 3PL cross-dock operation, operational substance is inherent, but documentation must evidence real activity, decision-making, and risk management in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 100% of evaded tax, plus interest at 0.6% per month; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, penalties up to 10% of assets, and potential liquidation

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, fines, and criminal charges for facilitating tax avoidance; travel bans and imprisonment up to 3 years
AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million, license revocation, and mandatory compliance audits

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for AML violations; civil liability for damages

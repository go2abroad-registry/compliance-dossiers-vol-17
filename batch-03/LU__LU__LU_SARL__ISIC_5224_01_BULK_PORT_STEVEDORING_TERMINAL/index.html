Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs and Excise) for export declarations and EORI. Primary digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (tax e-filing and VAT registration) and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) portal for RCS company registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file articles with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the LBR; the RCS number (B registration number) is the national company identification number (NIF). Step 2 - Obtain the direct tax dossier number from the ACD upon RCS completion; CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax returns are filed electronically (eCDF format) via MyGuichet. Step 3 - Apply for VAT identification with the AEDT through the MyGuichet portal before commencing taxable activity; the LU-VAT number is issued to all companies irrespective of turnover. Step 4 - Apply to the Customs and Excise Administration for an EORI number to lodge customs export declarations. Step 5 - Obtain the port terminal/stevedoring operating license from the Ministry of Mobility and Public Works and notify the AEDT; license reference is used for port-specific VAT and customs authorizations (e.g., customs warehousing or transit simplifications).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Step 1 - Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file articles with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the LBR; the RCS number (B registration number) is the national company identification number (NIF). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style pass-through election: the SARL is per se a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Luxembourg CIT (progressive 15% up to EUR 175,000 and 17% above), municipal business tax (ICC at commune rates, ~6.75% in Luxembourg City) and net wealth tax (IFT). Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (1) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under the Luxembourg VAT law; (2) fiscal unity (integration fiscale) for 95%-held resident subsidiaries held for at least 5 years (Art. 164bis LIR) allowing group-wide CIT aggregation and loss offset; (3) IP box regime (80% exemption of qualifying IP income, ~5.17% effective rate); (4) net wealth tax equity reduction election; (5) ATAD interest limitation filings (forms 560-562) and reverse-hybrid/hybrid mismatch rules which apply mandatorily; (6) advance tax ruling (tax clearance ruling) applications to the ACD for structuring certainty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small undertaking exemption). Mandatory VAT registration applies from the first euro once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, or upon intra-EU acquisitions, distance sales, or import activities; companies may voluntarily register below the threshold to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Stevedoring, bulk loading/unloading and terminal handling services at the inland waterway port (Port de Mertert on the Moselle) qualify for the 0% export VAT rate (exemption with right of deduction) only where the services are directly and demonstrably connected to goods physically exported outside the EU (Art. 47 VAT Law implementing Art. 146(1) VAT Directive), including services on goods under customs transit (T1), customs warehousing or outward processing. Where the same services are performed for goods in intra-EU circulation, the place of supply is Luxembourg (place of physical performance, Art. 54 VAT Directive) and VAT at 17% applies. For bulk transshipment flows to non-EU destinations via Rotterdam/Antwerp, the SARL should secure an advance VAT interpretation from the AEDT confirming zero-rating on the basis of the transit and exit evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration - electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD) with generated MRN (Movement Reference Number) lodged via the Luxembourg Customs and Excise ECS/ECS2 system
  • Bill of Lading or Inland Waterway Waybill (connaissement fluvial), CMR or CIM consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT wire confirmation showing export proceeds credited to the LU SARL bank account within commercial terms
  • Commercial invoice, terminal tally sheets, weighed bulk cargo receipt, and stevedoring work order for the specific export shipment
  • Transit accompanying documents (T1/T2AD) and exit summary declaration, where the port is the place of dispatch rather than exit
  • Customs broker mandate (procuration) and warehouse handling receipts evidencing the goods remained under customs supervision up to export

Luxembourg has adopted a staged B2B e-invoicing mandate (Law of 3 July 2025): from 1 January 2026 all Luxembourg-established suppliers must accept structured electronic invoices in the European standard EN 16931 via Peppol (Peppol BIS 3.0); from 1 January 2027 issuance of structured e-invoices is mandatory for domestic B2B transactions. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no tax-authority API pre-approval, and no digital stamping regime in Luxembourg; e-invoices are exchanged peer-to-peer through certified Peppol access points with the AEDT retaining audit access. Export and international invoices may use any agreed format, though Peppol exchange is recommended for audit evidence.

The AEDT, ACD and Customs and Excise run joint risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims, matching MRN/ECS data, barge and truck departure logs, terminal tally systems and SWIFT credit evidence. If zero-rating is disallowed, the SARL is liable for 17% VAT plus statutory moratory interest, with corresponding clawback of input VAT previously deducted; retrospective reassessment applies for 3 years in good faith and 10 years in case of fraud or omission. Fraudulent export documentation triggers criminal tax fraud penalties (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines ranging up to EUR 25,000 under Art. 396 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law), customs offense proceedings under the Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013), potential EORI suspension, and joint and several liability of the customs broker.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. As a euro-area member, Luxembourg applies unrestricted capital- and current-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU, and no exchange-control approval, licensing, or quantitative limit applies to equity injections, dividends, interest, management fees, royalties, loan repayments, share redemptions, or liquidation proceeds involving EU or non-EU investors. Profit remittance is subject only to ordinary corporate-law solvency tests and Luxembourg tax compliance (e.g., dividend withholding where applicable). The operational gatekeeper is the instructed credit institution, which classifies every cross-border flow under the Eurosystem ITO/BOP purpose-code framework for BCL statistics and performs sanctions and source-of-funds screening; such reporting is statistical and post-hoc, never a prior authorization device.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the euro-area framework impose no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export proceeds, stevedoring revenue, freight earnings, or port-service receipts. Receivables may be invoiced and collected in any negotiated currency (EUR, USD, GBP, etc.), may be retained in Luxembourg or foreign bank accounts in the original currency, and may be used directly for disbursements. The sole compliance duty is accurate BCL/ECB statistical reporting of the related credit entries by the receiving bank, together with anti-money-laundering record-keeping under the Law of 12 November 2004, as amended.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB) pursuant to the Law of 22 December 1998 on the monetary status and mission of the BCL. No independent foreign-exchange-control administration exists; external-surveillance authority is exercised through BCL/ECB balance-of-payments (BOP) statistics under ECB Regulation (EU) No 1744/2014 and BCL implementing circulars, with the Ministry of Finance competent for emergency capital-movement safeguards under EU Treaty derogation procedures (Article 66 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as an ancillary rail, Low as the primary treasury. A CSSF-licensed or EEA-passporting electronic-money institution under PSD2 (Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services) may legally issue IBANs, hold safeguarded client funds, and clear SEPA/TARGET2 disbursements, making it viable for supplier-payout sub-accounts and e-invoicing integration. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot provide the blocked escrow certificate required for SARL capital formation, lacks direct TARGET2 membership, cannot grant credit or credit-card-like facilities, and is not an accepted counterparty for complex FX-derivative collateral. A non-EU offshore digital EMI is legally barred from providing payment services in Luxembourg without a licence or passport under PSD2; use of such a provider exposes the SARL to regulatory unenforceability, CSSF scrutiny, and travel-rule failures under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113. All operational clearing must therefore sit with a licensed Luxembourg credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for statutory capital and shareholder funding: (i) EU/EEA shareholders: SEPA Credit Transfer cleared through TARGET2 or EBA STEP2, credited to the Capital Escrow Account opened in the name of the company in formation, with remittance reference to the notarised subscription deed; funds are released only upon RCS registration, in accordance with the Law of 10 August 1915 (minimum capital EUR 12,000; cash contributions payable to at least 25% at subscription). (ii) Non-EU shareholders: SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, purpose code for equity capital of direct investment under BCL BOP/ITO reporting, with fully populated remitter and beneficial-owner fields, DEA (documentary evidence attached), and a source-of-funds file; shareholder subordinated loans must be accompanied by a binding facility agreement and the corresponding BOP loan code. Non-EUR remittances convert at the instructed bank's prevailing rate with applicable FX confirmation; the BCL imposes no pre-authorisation.Settlement rails: (i) Domestic and EU/EUR disbursements: SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Direct Debit via TARGET2-LU/EBA STEP2 for payroll, social security, port-authority dues, equipment leases, and fuel. (iii) Trade finance: Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 (MT700/MT707) and documentary collections (MT400) for port machinery, maintenance spares, and international capex contracts. (iv) Port-authority clearing: billing-portal payments through the Port of Mertert concessionaire's system, settled by SEPA Direct Debit or TARGET2 credit; Swift Wire is engaged for strategic third-country vendors. All outbound flows carry travel-rule remitter/beneficiary information under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 and are archived per CSSF record-keeping rules.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/CNH) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and CSA; physically settled FX forwards are exempt from EMIR clearing and margin rules, but remain subject to transaction reporting to a registered trade repository.FX swaps (spot/forward combinations) for temporary EUR/USD liquidity conversion; exempt from EMIR clearing and used primarily for working-capital and port-dues timing mismatches.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) for long-dated alignment of USD-denominated stevedoring revenue against EUR-denominated terminal capex and debt service; subject to EMIR clearing and margin requirements to the extent applicable under current EMIR technical standards.Vanilla European FX options (calls and puts) for revenue or expense hedging; permitted under CSSF product-governance and MIFID II appropriateness rules, with premium structures documented in the hedging policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) legally permissible only where the underlying currency is non-convertible or non-deliverable (e.g., certain EME and African currencies); NDF referencing EUR against freely convertible currencies is not market-standard in Luxembourg and is generally not accommodated by domestic credit institutions.Zero-cost collars and participating forwards; permitted for cost-effective hedging, requiring documented business-case rationale, ISDA/EMIR documentation, and suitability assessment by the instructing bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as an ancillary rail, Low as the primary treasury. A CSSF-licensed or EEA-passporting electronic-money institution under PSD2 (Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services) may legally issue IBANs, hold safeguarded client funds, and clear SEPA/TARGET2 disbursements, making it viable for supplier-payout sub-accounts and e-invoicing integration. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot provide the blocked escrow certificate required for SARL capital formation, lacks direct TARGET2 membership, cannot grant credit or credit-card-like facilities, and is not an accepted counterparty for complex FX-derivative collateral. A non-EU offshore digital EMI is legally barred from providing payment services in Luxembourg without a licence or passport under PSD2; use of such a provider exposes the SARL to regulatory unenforceability, CSSF scrutiny, and travel-rule failures under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113. All operational clearing must therefore sit with a licensed Luxembourg credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline CIT for Luxembourg City (FY 2024/2025): 15.00% state corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) plus 7% solidarity surcharge (0.105% state component) plus 6.75% municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal); the aggregate rate varies by commune from approximately 23.70% to 26.61%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Luxembourg domestic participation exemption where the shareholder is an EU/EEA company holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital (or having an acquisition cost of at least EUR 1,200,000) for an uninterrupted holding period of at least 12 months; treaty relief to 0-15% applies under applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR; mandatory Master File and Local File attach where the Luxembourg SARL belongs to an integrated group meeting any two of the EU large-enterprise thresholds (balance sheet total EUR 20,000,000; net turnover EUR 40,000,000; average headcount 250) or where intra-group transactions aggregate above EUR 100,000 in a fiscal year; Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where group consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; documentation must be made available to the Luxembourg tax administration within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations of OFC regimes are not enacted in Luxembourg; the functional equivalent is applied through the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, the OECD Authorized OECD Approach on profit attribution, DAC 6 substance hallmarks for mandatory disclosure, and the Administration's 'substance over form' doctrine. A stevedoring SARL operating a physical bulk-port terminal with staffed local management, port authority contracts, operational equipment and genuine decision-making in Luxembourg satisfies the substance test; a shell SARL merely invoicing stevedoring fees without operational reality will be disregarded, profits reattributed to the actual functions in another jurisdiction, and treaty benefits denied under the LTA's anti-abuse practice.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg General Tax Law, evasion penalties range from 10% up to 200% of the evaded tax depending on degree of intent; in addition, arrears interest accrues at 0.6% per month and administrative fines reach EUR 25,000; criminal tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and criminal fines of up to EUR 25,000 plus confiscation of the tax advantage.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the gérant (manager) signs false declarations or directs the fraudulent scheme; directors can be personally and jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax under Luxembourg civil liability rules, subject to travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition under the European Arrest Warrant framework.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under Article 56 bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR); disallowed deductions, re-allocation of taxable income to the SARL, mandatory interest on the under-assessment, potential adjustment surcharge; failure to produce transfer pricing documentation on request triggers a documentation penalty of up to EUR 50,000 per filing obligation and CbCR-related fines up to EUR 250,000 for the group.

Director personal liability: The responsible director who approves the mispricing may be held personally liable in administrative or bankruptcy proceedings for the loss of tax recovery; criminal complicity in fiscal fraud can expose the director to imprisonment and disqualification from managing companies.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for tax purposes under the abuse-of-law doctrine and ATAD GAAR; full reassessment of profits in the hands of the substantive operator; denial of DTA and EU directive benefits; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; mandatory disclosure penalties under DAC 6 for intermediaries and taxpayers.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers risk joint and several civil liability for the entire tax shortfall; in serious cases, travel bans pending criminal investigation and imprisonment for fraud where the shell structure was knowingly used to defeat taxes.
AML / Payment Rails / Economic Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended), administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; financial penalty up to EUR 5,000,000 for violation of EU restrictive measures; freezing of funds, licence suspension or revocation of the port terminal operating licence.

Director personal liability: Individual officers face criminal imprisonment of up to five years, personal civil damages to affected parties, travel bans and asset-seizure orders; disqualification as directors and professional licence withdrawal; personal liability persists notwithstanding corporate indemnification clauses.

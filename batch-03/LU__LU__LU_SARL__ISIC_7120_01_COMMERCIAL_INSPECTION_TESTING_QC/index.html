Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg; e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.public.lu); professional e-filing via AEDT eSpace/eTVA; business register: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities first obtain an LBR business registration number (RCS number) via the RCS filing through MyGuichet.lu; after incorporation (or for a non-established deemed taxable person), file VAT registration Form No. 102 ('Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') with the AEDT, annexing articles of association, certificate of incorporation, proof of place of business, list of directors/managers, description of taxable activities and anticipated turnover, and, for third-country entities, designation of an approved tax representative ('représentant responsable solidaire') established in Luxembourg; a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) is then issued within 2-4 weeks; a non-established EU supplier may use simplified registration where no local establishment exists but must register before any taxable transaction if VAT is chargeable in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities first obtain an LBR business registration number (RCS number) via the RCS filing through MyGuichet.lu; after incorporation (or for a non-established deemed taxable person), file VAT registration Form No. 102 ('Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA') with the AEDT, annexing articles of association, certificate of incorporation, proof of place of business, list of directors/managers, description of taxable activities and anticipated turnover, and, for third-country entities, designation of an approved tax representative ('représentant responsable solidaire') established in Luxembourg; a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) is then issued within 2-4 weeks; a non-established EU supplier may use simplified registration where no local establishment exists but must register before any taxable transaction if VAT is chargeable in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Luxembourg SARL is an entity-level taxpayer (no pass-through election; aggregate Luxembourg City rate 24.94% comprising 17% CIT, 7% solidarity surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax). No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available elections and regimes: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for Luxembourg-resident group companies upon prior AEDT approval; (2) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) with prior authorization, allowing one VAT ID for intra-group supplies; (3) participation-exemption election exercised via the annual CIT return for qualifying dividend and capital-gains exemption; (4) notional interest deduction (also known as VIVCES deduction) election; (5) advance tax agreements (APA) and tax ruling applications to determine hybrid mismatch treatment; ATAD hybrid-mismatch and CFC rules are mandatory with no opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-enterprise VAT franchise regime, Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law). A Luxembourg-established entity may apply the exemption below this threshold, but a SARL conducting cross-border inspections must register if it makes intra-Community acquisitions above EUR 10,000 or any taxable supplies to non-business customers; non-established foreign businesses have no threshold and must register before carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg (B2B supplies to a Luxembourg taxable customer are reverse-charged).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier; the chargeable event starts the 90-day proof-of-export period (extendable to one year in justified cases). On-site factory pre-shipment QC services physically performed outside Luxembourg (non-EU location) fall outside Luxembourg place-of-supply rules and are not chargeable to Luxembourg VAT; B2B inspection/testing services rendered from Luxembourg to an EU business customer are reverse-charged in the customer's member state (no Luxembourg VAT). Zero-rating mainly covers physical export of equipment, samples and goods re-delivered after testing, supported by transport and customs evidence, while services to non-EU customers must be evidenced by commercial contracts, inspection/test certificates and the customer's foreign TIN/VAT identification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment receipt
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Evidence of physical exit from the EU (customs stamp, ICS/ECS status messages, port/airport loading documents)
  • Laboratory test certificate or inspection report evidencing the service scope
  • Declaration by the foreign customer with respective TIN/VAT ID

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing: public authorities must receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931 / PEPPOL BIS 3.0) via a certified PEPPOL access point from 1 June 2023; suppliers under State/public-sector contracts are obliged to issue e-invoices from 1 January 2024 (or in tenders where e-invoicing is explicitly required). No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no SAF-T filing, no digital stamp and no pre-clearance API apply in Luxembourg; B2B and B2C invoicing remain paper/PDF-permitted subject to authenticity and integrity archival rules.

AEDT audits export zero-rating strictly on documentary chain integrity; post-export adjustments and retrospective clawbacks reclassify the supply to Luxembourg VAT at 17% where proof of export is not obtained within 90 days, with automatic 0.8% monthly late interest (10% per annum), omission penalties of 10%-20% of the tax, and bad-faith surcharges up to 100% of the evaded tax; willful fraud attracts criminal fines (EUR 750-12,500) and imprisonment up to 5 years; abusive claims of export status in hybrid arrangements additionally trigger ATAD/GAAR economic-substance review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full participant in the European Economic and Monetary Union and the eurozone single currency area. There are no exchange control laws, no foreign exchange licensing requirements, and no restrictions on the conversion or repatriation of capital for current account or capital account transactions. Transaction convertibility is total for both residents and non-resident owners, subject only to general anti-money laundering, sanctions screening, EU/UN sanctions compliance, and Luxembourg company law solvency requirements. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but dividends paid by an SARL are subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at 15% unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, an applicable double tax treaty, or the participation exemption regime. No central bank approval or exchange control declaration is required for wire transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no minimum conversion ratio, and no mandatory repatriation of export receivable proceeds into euro. Because ISIC 7120 inspection and testing services are exported services rather than physical goods, invoices may be settled in any agreed convertible currency. If foreign currency receipts are credited to a Luxembourg bank account, they may be held, converted, or remitted freely. However, for prudential risk management and treasury efficiency, it is recommended that all non-euro export receipts be converted to euro within bankable business day settlement cycles or hedged to eliminate translation exposure.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking prudential supervision; the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance retains general financial policy oversight. The BCL implements European Central Bank (ECB) monetary policy and manages euro system liquidity for Luxembourg-resident entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise (Wisdomtree Financial Europe, authorised as an electronic money institution in Belgium with EU passporting rights under the E-Money Directive) can be used for low-value cross-border disbursements and vendor payments in a compatible auxiliary role. Feasibility rating is Medium-High for ancillary payment rails; however, it is not a full substitute for a regulated credit institution in Luxembourg. For capital escrow, statutory audit evidence, and large outbound trade settlements, a licensed Luxembourg credit institution is mandatory. The CSSF does not recognise Wise as a domestic depositary bank, and funds held with such EMIs are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme except through the home state scheme under EU law. Legal constraint: treasury policy should cap EMI balances and require immediate daily sweeps to a tier-1 Luxembourg bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders shall be executed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA EURO1/TARGET2 where euro-denominated, using the following standardised bank transfer protocol: (i) the remitter field must show the legal name and registered address of the contributing shareholder; (ii) the beneficiary field must show the exact registered name of the LU SARL and its IBAN; (iii) the payment purpose code must be a clear capital participation statement such as 'Capital Injection', 'Statutory Contribution', or 'Increase of Share Capital' — Luxembourg law does not require a customs form but banks will code the transaction under EU Balance of Payments (BoP) statistics; (iv) the reference field must include the corporate registration number (RCS) of the SARL; (v) the bank statement and incoming SWIFT message must be retained as the audit trail for the share capital increase or capital reduction filing at the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL). For non-euro capital injections, the remitter must use a fully covered correspondent banking route, and the receiving Luxembourg bank will convert the currency at its standard spot rate, subject to no exchange control approval.Trade disbursements for factory inspection operations, third-party laboratory testing, and quality-audit subcontractors shall use the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all non-urgent and urgent low-value payments, with a rigorous beneficiary bank verification step and IBAN/name-match screening; (ii) TARGET2 and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eurozone suppliers to achieve real-time finality; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (ICC UCP 600) only for high-value procurements where guarantees of payment against inspection certificates and shipping documents are required; (iv) documentary collection (UCP/URC 522) as a lower-cost alternative for established supplier relationships. Box 4 - IP and participation regime Qualifying intellectual property income deduction (80%) and participation exemption for dividends/capital gains. Attach the nexus-ratio calculation for IP income and valid ownership evidence; non-resident shareholders claiming treaty benefits must enclose a tax residence certificate. Late filing triggers an administrative fine of EUR 25 to EUR 2,500, ex officio tax assessment, and a monthly late-payment interest charge of 0.6% of the outstanding tax. Continued default may result in enforced recovery through garnishment and asset seizure.
Box 5 - Credits and prepayments Foreign tax credits, investment tax credits, and CIT instalments paid during the fiscal year. Submit certified foreign tax credit claims; provide proof of prepayment wire transfers, including SWIFT references. Late filing triggers an administrative fine of EUR 25 to EUR 2,500, ex officio tax assessment, and a monthly late-payment interest charge of 0.6% of the outstanding tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 0 (no statutory de minimis threshold); local file and master file documentation must be produced on tax audit request. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more are subject to country-by-country reporting and must maintain a master file as per Luxembourg CbCR law.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a formal ESR code, but the domestic anti-abuse rule under Article 56bis LIR, coupled with ATAD implementation, requires real economic substance. A SARL engaged in inspection, testing and quality control must demonstrate own premises, employees, management capability and commercial risk-taking in Luxembourg; letterbox arrangements will be disregarded and profits reattributed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of board signatories up to 5 years; automatic reassessment plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, civil restitution, travel bans during enforcement proceedings, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the scheme under Article 56bis LIR, denial of DTA and participation exemption, retroactive assessment of CIT, and 15% withholding tax on deemed dividend distributions.

Director personal liability: De facto directors are personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties; judicial dissolution of the entity may also be ordered by Luxembourg courts.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal penalties up to EUR 5 million for legal entities; prison terms up to 5 years and seizure of assets for responsible individuals under Luxembourg AML legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, compensation orders for damages, travel bans, and suspension from corporate management functions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment of taxable income, interest on the additional tax, and administrative fines of up to 10% of the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly prepared false TP reporting are personally liable for penalty amounts and may be prosecuted for complicity in tax fraud, resulting in civil restitution and imprisonment.

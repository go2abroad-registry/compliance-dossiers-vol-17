Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg operates a functionally split national tax administration: (1) Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — competent authority for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax, with official portal at impotsdirects.public.lu; (2) Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — competent authority for VAT (TVA), registration duties, excise and real-estate registration, with portal at aed.public.lu; (3) Administration des Douanes et Accises — customs and export-control authority forming part of the AEDT, with portal at douane.public.lu; (4) Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) / Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — national legal-entity registry, lbr.lu. All filing and registration procedures for a commercial company are coordinated through the single digital public-service portal Guichet.lu / MyGuichet (myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign-owned SARL: (i) a Luxembourg notary executes the articles of association (acte constitutif) and lodges the incorporation application with the RCS/Guichet unique; (ii) the RCS issues the company's 13-digit national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule), which is the common business key subsequently used for ACD and AEDT matters; (iii) the ACD automatically opens a corporate income-tax file (numéro de contribuable) for IRC, ICC and IF purposes; (iv) the AEDT registers the company for VAT and issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (n° d'identification TVA, prefixed LU) on submission of Form 5150 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) together with certified articles, RCS extract, manager identity documents and a description of the defense-tactical-vehicle manufacturing activity; registration is mandatory before any taxable supply is made or whenever annual taxable turnover reaches the EUR 35,000 threshold. For a non-resident entity with no Luxembourg seat or permanent establishment, separate procedures apply: OSS (One-Stop-Shop) registration for intra-EU distance sales/remote services, or direct AEDT registration via Form 5152 with a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative (représentant fiscal responsable) where the non-resident carries out taxable supplies in Luxembourg and is established outside the EU; EU VAT-refund claims are filed electronically under Directive 2008/9/EC through the Member State's portal, while third-country refund claims follow the 13th Directive procedure before the AEDT.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is fixed by statute and tax treaties. The relevant statutory election mechanisms include: (i) the integrated tax-group (consolidation) election under Article 164bis LIR for affiliates held directly or indirectly at ≥95%, producing a single tax base for Luxembourg-resident group members, with an irrevocable 5-year commitment; (ii) the participation-exemption application, together with the anti-hybrid alignment option introduced by the ATAD-transposing Law of 25 March 2020, under which a Luxembourg entity caught by hybrid or reverse-hybrid mismatch outcomes (e.g., where the foreign ultimate parent treats the SARL as transparent) may elect to align its Luxembourg characterization so as to prevent double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion mismatches; (iii) the VAT option to tax real-estate lettings (Article 48 of the VAT Law) for the industrial facility; (iv) the OSS Union/Non-Union scheme elections for intra-EU distance sales and remote services; (v) the customs Registered Exporter (REX) self-certification election for preferential-origin declarations on third-country export; and (vi) the Peppol/Factur-X access-point registration election for mandatory B2G e-invoicing. Each election requires a written application to the competent authority (ACD, AEDT or Customs) and, where applicable, a shareholder agreement or statutory board resolution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Capital movements, payments, and profit remittances are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Directive 88/361/EEC; no Luxembourg foreign-exchange authorisation or capital-transfer licence is required for equity injections, trade settlements, or dividend distributions. The practical hurdles are non-FX frictions: (i) dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders trigger Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (15% statutory domestic rate, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or reduced under tax treaties); (ii) all cross-border flows must be declared to the BCL by the resident bank via the monthly Balance of Payments return, which creates an administrative checkpoint; (iii) military-hardware payments are subject to CSSF enhanced due diligence and EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening, and flows from state procurement entities in embargoed countries can be frozen even though the euro itself is fully convertible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds; exporters may freely retain EUR, USD, or third-currency receivables on their accounts and convert at market rates. The only regulatory obligations are: (i) the bank's monthly BCL Balance of Payments filing of the proceeds under the correct purpose code; and (ii) for ISIC 3040 military-vehicle exports, a valid export authorisation from the Luxembourg Ministry of Economy under the EU Common Military List, Directive 2009/43/EC for intra-EU transfers, and the Luxembourg export-control law for dual-use items on Regulation (EU) 2021/821; expiry or revocation of the authorisation operates as a de facto blockage of liquidation of export receivables.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the national central bank of the Eurosystem operating under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential and AML supervision of credit institutions in Luxembourg is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium viability. Luxembourg law authorises CSSF-licensed payment institutions and e-money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 and permits fully digital remote onboarding; however, global EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer and similar) commonly decline ISIC 3040 military-tactical hardware accounts, cannot issue or advise UCP600 documentary credits, cannot process diplomatic-government instructs, and lack correspondent depth for defence-sector sanctions risk. Under the Luxembourg EMI regime, client funds are subject to safeguarding and segregation only and EMIs may not grant commercial credit. Recommended role: a secondary, non-critical disbursement rail for minor operational supplier payments after receipt of the statutory special export licence and acceptance by a tier-1 commercial bank.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual returns must be filed no later than 31 December of the calendar year following the close of the tax year (e.g., FYE 31 December 2024 due by 31 December 2025); extensions are technically available only on a reasoned written request prior to the deadline, but in practice late-filing interest runs from 1 January of the second year following the accounting year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File is required where the consolidated group global revenue reaches EUR 50,000,000. A Local File is additionally required when the Luxembourg entity's aggregate related-party transactions for the fiscal year exceed EUR 100,000, or where the entity is engaged in intra-group procurement of vehicle hulls, software licenses, royalty payments or service fees under the LU-LU SARL. Regardless of thresholds, the Luxembourg tax administration may demand contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation on any cross-border transaction.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the economic substance doctrine through the general anti-abuse provisions derived from the EU ATAD and OECD BEPS Action 5, together with the Luxembourg administrative commentary on Abusive Transactions (Circular LIR n° 46 of 2017). Although a defense-hardware manufacturer is not a geographically mobile ring-fenced entity, a resident SARL must demonstrate physical premises with actual armored-vehicle assembly operations, qualified defense-engineering staff in Luxembourg, locally exercised board and executive management decisions, procurement and treasury functions, and active operational bank accounts. Without such substance, the tax authority can deny corporate residence, disregard the SARL as a letterbox entity, re-attribute its defense contracts and resulting income to the controlling non-resident parent, and disallow treaty benefits, the participation exemption and the IP regime. Defense export licensing records under the 2016 Luxembourg law on military equipment and dual-use items are part of the commercial substance dossier.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / intentional misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under the Luxembourg Income Tax Law and the General Tax Act: fine of up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil reassessment with a 20% tax increase and monthly default interest at 0.6%; confiscation of evaded profit attributable to the fraudulent invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and interest if the misstatement leads to insolvency or liquidation; the Public Prosecutor's Office can issue an international arrest warrant and prohibit travel from Luxembourg pending the investigation.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authorities may deny the claimed residence and treaty benefits, reclassify the profits of the SARL as an unlicensed permanent establishment of the shareholder, and impose a 5% administrative surcharge on the full amount of income deemed misattributed; the participation exemption may be retroactively reversed if the letters of intent for the 12-month holding period are absent.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced and the directors become personally taxable on the earnings of the SARL; they can be ordered to reinstate a physical industrial presence within 90 days; failure results in deregistration from the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and a court order prohibiting the director from serving on any other board within the EEA.
AML / defense and dual-use sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 250,000 per prohibited transaction and confiscation of the full payment proceeds under Luxembourg Anti-Money Laundering Law; the SARL is additionally blacklisted on the CSSF/WMD list, which prohibits any EU counterpart from transacting with it.

Director personal liability: Directors have direct criminal liability under Article 506-1 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code, punishable by 5 to 10 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 100,000 to EUR 1 million; an automatic director disqualification for 10 years arises; the court may freeze all bank accounts of the director and the SARL and appoint a receiver without prior notice.
Transfer pricing documentation and adjustment failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on an arm's length basis with a 50% penalty on the additional corporate tax assessed; if the TP documentation is not produced, a fixed penalty of 10% of the total intercompany transaction value is levied; interest runs at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: The directors who requested or approved the pricing distortion are personally liable for the tax deficiency; in an aggravated case, the court can order the production of all internal e-mails through a third-party discovery order and impose a travel ban on the Luxembourg-based director during the administrative examination.

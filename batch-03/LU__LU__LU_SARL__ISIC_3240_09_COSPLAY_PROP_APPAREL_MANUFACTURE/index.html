Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Inland Revenue for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Central e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu; electronic filing gateways: eCDF for direct taxes and eTVA for VAT.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Luxembourg SARL, the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) automatically issues the 11-digit national identification number (numéro d'identification) which serves as the direct-tax TIN. A separate VAT identification number (format: LU + 8 digits) must be applied for with the AEDT via the eTVA/MyGuichet portal after the entity has its registered office and effective activity address in Luxembourg. For foreign non-resident entities supplying taxable goods or services in Luxembourg, VAT registration must be filed directly with the AEDT; non-EU entities without a fixed establishment in Luxembourg are generally required to appoint a local VAT fiscal representative (représentant responsable) unless exempted under a mutual assistance convention. The SARL may commence export operations pending VAT issuance by using its national identification number, but invoicing with zero-rated VAT requires the active LU VAT number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a full resident corporate taxpayer subject to unlimited worldwide taxation: corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and annual net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). It is not eligible for pass-through treatment; check-the-box classification is not recognized under Luxembourg law. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a full resident corporate taxpayer subject to unlimited worldwide taxation: corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and annual net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). It is not eligible for pass-through treatment; check-the-box classification is not recognized under Luxembourg law. The entity is not within a special tax-exempt zone, and the standard territorial scope is residence-based worldwide taxation.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime. Available elections relevant to this SARL: (i) fiscal unity / group taxation (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax, available only to 95%-owned Luxembourg resident subsidiaries of a Luxembourg resident parent; (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT law, allowing the SARL to be treated as a single taxable person with affiliated Luxembourg entities; (iii) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for taxable B2C distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C services, and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election for imports of consignments not exceeding EUR 150; (iv) no hybrid-mismatch election – mandatory anti-hybrid rules apply in accordance with ATAD as transposed by the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2018, with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover exemption threshold for small businesses (régime de la franchise); EUR 10,000 pan-EU threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services; EUR 150 per-consignment threshold for IOSS import scheme. Outside these derogations, a taxable person is obliged to register for VAT from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg; there is no general turnover threshold above which the obligation crystallizes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (0% VAT) treatment applies for: (i) direct export of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods leave the EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply; (ii) intra-Community supplies (B2B dispatches) to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number confirmed in VIES; (iii) export transport services and ancillary loading, handling and insurance services directly linked to the export movement; (iv) supplies of goods to approved customs warehousing or free-zone regimes where applicable. The manufacturer must obtain and archive evidence of physical exit, including customs export declaration numbers, transport documents and any third-party warehouse certificates; failure to substantiate the export within the statutory time limits disqualifies the zero rate and triggers reverse assessment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX-A)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR / Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or telemetric transaction reporting is currently in force in Luxembourg. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and operates on PEPPOL-compatible standards. Mandatory electronic filing of VAT returns applies via the eTVA application on MyGuichet.lu, with monthly, quarterly or annual cycles depending on turnover; taxpayers subject to e-filing must also retain and produce on demand the Luxembourg SAF-T (fichier d'audit informatique) VAT audit file. All invoices must contain the statutory details required by Article 18 of the Luxembourg VAT law, including the LU VAT number, customer VAT number (for intra-Community supply), invoice date, description and VAT rate; e-invoice data must be retained for 10 years.

Both the AEDT and Luxembourg Customs perform post-clearance audits (PCA) and cross-check the customs export declarations against the exporter's commercial and banking records; the Inward Remittance Certificate is examined to confirm that foreign currency proceeds are repatriated, normally within 180 days, in line with Luxembourg's balance-of-payments reporting expectations. Where zero-rating is claimed without valid export proof or is found to be fraudulent, the tax authority retrospectively reassesses VAT at the standard rate of 17% on the full invoice value, applies late-payment interest at 0.6% per month or part thereof, and imposes administrative fines which may reach EUR 12,500 or twice the evaded tax; criminal proceedings for serious VAT fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and additional monetary penalties. The standard five-year VAT assessment limitation period is extended to ten years where fraud is established, and the clawback may also trigger joint and several liability of the exporter's directors.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Luxembourg law imposes no exchange-control restrictions on either current or capital account transactions, and no prior administrative authorization is required for the conversion, transfer, or repatriation of funds. Profit remittance from a Luxembourg SARL in the form of dividends is freely permitted subject to (i) verification of distributable reserves under Luxembourg corporate law (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 710-1), (ii) a 15% statutory dividend withholding tax reduced to 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU applies (10% participation, 12-month holding) or where a double-tax treaty grants a reduced rate, and (iii) general anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening by the paying bank. There is no separate hurdle for unrestricted current-account convertibility or for reinvestment of export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg and EU law impose no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export receipts. A Luxembourg exporter may lawfully retain export proceeds in any currency, hold them in non-euro bank accounts, or convert them freely. In practice, banks must report to the BCL every cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 for balance-of-payments statistics, and exporters must maintain underlying commercial documentation (invoice, customs declaration, bill of lading) to evidence the trade nature of the transaction. No conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or compulsory US-dollar sale rule exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the central monetary authority of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; prudential supervision over banks is exercised jointly by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and, for significant institutions, by the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary payment collection and low-value supplier payouts; Low for treasury concentration and statutory capital holding. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Pockit can be onboarded for daily e-commerce collection from Shopify Payments/Stripe and for cross-border supplier payments under the PSD2 framework. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold a valid e-money license in an EU/EEA state and must passport into Luxembourg under PSD2; UK-only EMIs no longer enjoy passporting rights after Brexit; EMIs cannot hold a SARL's registered capital in escrow because they are not credit institutions and do not provide banking guarantees; funds held at an EMI are pooled and are not protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (Fonds de Garantie Dépositaire Luxembourg). The prudent treasury policy is to cap EMI balances below EUR 50,000 and to settle daily via SWIFT/SEPA to a fully licensed Luxembourg bank. CSSF authorization may also be required if the EMI establishes a physical branch or outsources critical functions to Luxembourg.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% statutory corporate income tax (impot sur le revenu des collectivites); combined headline burden in Luxembourg City for tax year 2024: 17% CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax (impot commercial communal) + 7% employment fund solidarity surcharge on CIT = approximately 24.94% aggregate effective burden for a resident SARL.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on all dividend distributions made by a Luxembourg SARL to shareholders, whether resident or non-resident, unless a specific statutory, treaty, or EU Directive exemption applies.

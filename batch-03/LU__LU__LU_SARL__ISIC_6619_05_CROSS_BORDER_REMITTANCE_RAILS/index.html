Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax (CIT) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal); integrated digital portal via MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS).

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation and registration of the SARL with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), the entity is automatically assigned a 14-digit national identification number (matricule) which serves as the corporate tax reference. VAT registration is perfected by submitting the electronic registration form (Formulaire 640 – inscription à la TVA) through MyGuichet to the AEDT, which issues a LU-prefixed VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). Simultaneously, employer withholding tax registration is filed with the ACD and, where applicable, social security registration with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS). For the remittance/payment services scope, the CSSF payment-institution license dossier (Law of 10 November 2009) references the same matricule and VAT number, and AML/CFT registration is consolidated under the single corporate identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; the SARL is per se a fully taxable resident corporation (CIT, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and solidarity surcharge). Available elections include: (i) VAT grouping election under Article 59bis of the VAT Law (form a single taxable person with ≥90% controlled Luxembourg entities); (ii) fiscal integration for CIT purposes under Article 164bis LIR (95% direct/indirect Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries, 5-year confirmation commitment); (iii) IP box election under Article 50bis LIR (80% exemption of qualifying IP income, with election to apply per-scheme); (iv) the flat-rate cost election and joint taxation elections for closely held holding structures (SOPARFI). Hybrid mismatch positions are governed by the ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Article 168quater LIR), which create mandatory disallowance and do not grant an elective carve-out; the entity must file annual form 602 with the AEDT for cross-border restructuring events.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-enterprise exemption (Article 57 of the VAT Law); mandatory VAT registration is triggered from the moment taxable turnover exceeds this threshold (no threshold for non-established suppliers making taxable supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: 0% VAT applies (Article 53 of the VAT Law) where goods are physically exported outside the EU, evidenced by transport/dispatch proof and a customs export declaration (SAD) accepted within 90 days of the supply date. For services: the remittance and payment-transfer services at issue are VAT-exempt without right to deduction (Article 44(1)(c) of the VAT Law) rather than zero-rated; the exemption covers transactions, including negotiation, concerning payments, transfers and debts. Cross-border B2B services supplied to taxable persons in other EU Member States or third countries fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the place-of-supply (reverse charge) rules, provided the supplier holds the customer's valid EU VAT ID or adequate evidence of business status; such services remain subject to the customer's domestic rules and cannot be zero-rated with an input-credit entitlement in Luxembourg.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD/ECS) with customs office exit stamp or electronic harmonised export data
  • Transport documentation (CMR waybill, Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill) evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, sales/export contract, and insurance certificates; payment intermediation records (SWIFT MT103/MT202, bank account statements) proving completed cross-border remittance and consideration received
  • Inward remittance certificate / EUR1 or movement certificate where preferential origin applies
  • Customer due-diligence file (KYC/AML) confirming the identity and business status of the overseas recipient for exempt or reverse-charge treatment

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API stamping, or fiscal data transmission applies to B2B/B2C invoices; electronic invoicing is permitted and legally equivalent to paper. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only for B2G supplies (public procurement) via the PEPPOL network. All invoices issued by a Luxembourg SARL must bear the tax identification number and comply with the VAT invoice content requirements (Articles 57–59 of the VAT Law). Because the activity is cross-border remittance (payment transactions), PSPs are subject to the CESOP/DAC7 reporting framework requiring quarterly reporting of cross-border payment data to the Luxembourg tax authorities (first transmission by 31 January 2025).

The AEDT may reassess VAT within five years (ten years in cases of fraud or wilful misconduct). Unsubstantiated zero-rating or wrongful exemption is retrospectively clawed back with default interest of 0.8% per month or part-month (Article 87 of the VAT Law), plus fines of EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 per infraction (Article 84). Fraudulent evasion (including falsified export proofs or fictitious cross-border payments) exposes managers to criminal fines up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment under Articles 84–86 of the VAT Law and Article 60 of the Customs Code; the CSSF revokes the payment-institution licence on adverse findings, and repeat offenders incur a 100% penalty on the differential tax. Anti-abuse provisions (Article 6 of the VAT Law and GAAR in the General Tax Law) disregard artificial arrangements and re-characterise cross-border remittance chains to the taxable destination.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a Eurozone member with fully liberalized capital movements pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls on either current or capital transactions. The euro is a freely convertible floating currency. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are unrestricted, subject only to standardized corporate documentation (audited financial statements, withholding tax certificates under Article 147, and the Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends at the applicable 15% domestic rate or reduced treaty rate). No prior Central Bank approval is required for any cross-border transfer exceeding any threshold; however, BCL statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply to financial intermediaries and resident direct investment flows above €12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg maintains no export proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for export or service receipts. A remittance agency may invoice and hold foreign currency freely in Luxembourg accounts. Any EUR conversion is driven purely by commercial settlement needs and bank counterparty liquidity, with no government-mandated conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Ministry of Finance retains residual licensing and sanctions policy authority. The BCL manages balance-of-payments statistical monitoring and settlement systems (TARGET2-LU), while the CSSF authorizes and supervises payments institutions and credit institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate viability with high regulatory friction. A Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution (PI) licence (agrément) under the Law of 10 November 2009 is the only permissible legal wrapper for operating the money transmission business; the ISIC 6619 activity is a payment service under Annex I(6) of PSD2 (money remittance) and triggers mandatory CSSF licensing, with initial capital €20,000–€125,000 depending on service scope, plus prudential safeguarding of user funds. Offshore digital EMIs located in third countries cannot lawfully provide remittance services to Luxembourg residents without passporting; a third-country fintech may only act as an unregulated white-label technology provider to the licensed Luxembourg PI, and may not touch customer funds. The CSSF will expect substance: a Luxembourg registered office, one resident Conducting Officer with local AML expertise and adequate script, and auditable transaction monitoring hosted in the EEA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: shareholder capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent euro payment via TARGET2-LU) from the parent shareholder's account into the SARL's escrow account held at a Luxembourg bank, with the received wire explicitly tagged 'Direct investment - equity capital' plus the Luxembourg BCL balance-of-payments reporting code for direct investment equity (BOP category EA). The following must accompany the wire: (1) the SARL's Luxembourg registration number (RCS Luxembourg), (2) the UBO/RBE reference, (3) the notarised capital increase deed if it represents a contribution adjusting the registered capital, and (4) the bank's calculation of any withholding tax or exemption claim (for share premium, treatment under Article 15(2) of the Luxembourg withholding tax law). Any loan-based injection requires a written shareholder loan agreement at arm's length, which the Luxembourg bank will treat as a related-party liability subject to thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing documentation; the wire purpose for a loan must be coded 'borrowing – intercompany' to avoid misclassification as equity. The BCL requires monthly balance-of-payments statistical reporting of all such injections above the €12,500 threshold, which is effected by the depositary bank.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EUR-denominated beneficiary disbursements within the EEA; (b) SWIFT MT103 over the TARGET2 and SWIFTNet FIN network for all non-EEA or USD/GBP/CHF payouts, with mandatory originator and beneficiary structured fields required by EU Regulation 2015/847 (information accompanying transfers); (c) commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the Luxembourg settlement bank for large disbursements against documents, otherwise documentary collection (DP/DA); (d) for cash-pickup corridor clearing, the licensed PI must pre-fund tapped payout correspondents via their nostro accounts at global clearing banks rather than using open correspondent free-float balances. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Luxembourg SARL's related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 in aggregate in a fiscal year (goods, services, intangibles, and financial transactions). A Master File is required where the worldwide group's consolidated annual revenue reaches at least EUR 750,000,000. These thresholds are expressed in EUR, the local currency of Luxembourg.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute equivalent to Cayman/BVI; however, the substance doctrine is embedded through the substance-over-form principle, the case-law-derived 'fraude à la loi' abuse-of-law doctrine, the EU ATAD GAAR, and, for this regulated sector, the CSSF licensing requirements under the law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, which mandate physical presence, local staffing, operational governance, and genuine decision-making power in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Illegal Payment Service Activity / Unlicensed Remittance Business

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 5,000,000 and/or imprisonment of 1 to 5 years; administrative sanctions by the CSSF including cessation orders, prohibition of management, withdrawal of licence, and fines up to 10% of gross annual turnover or EUR 5,000,000, whichever is higher

Director personal liability: Directors can be declared jointly and severally liable for corporate debts and damages, subjected to asset freezes and travel bans, disqualified from corporate management, and, in aggravated cases, sentenced to imprisonment for operating an unlicensed regulated payment business.
Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of omitted income, a tax increase of up to 10% for undisclosed profits, monthly late payment interest at 0.8%, criminal fines, and imprisonment up to 5 years; the limitation period for fraudulent non-declaration is extended to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false tax returns face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, and civil liability for the unpaid tax damages under the Luxembourg law on commercial companies; the court may order travel bans and freezing of personal assets.
Non-Arm's-Length Transfer Pricing / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing the Luxembourg taxable base, disallowance of excessive expenses, recharacterisation of the excess as a dividend subject to 15% Luxembourg withholding tax (unless a DTA reduces the rate), monthly late-payment interest at 0.8%, and penalties; treaty benefits may be denied under GAAR or fraud situations

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length transactions can be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and penalties, prosecuted for tax fraud, and prohibited from holding management positions in regulated financial entities.
Shell Company / Fraude à la Loi / GAAR

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg treaty benefits, reallocation of profits to the place of effective management, recovery of unpaid 15% withholding tax on deemed dividends, addition of late payment interest and penalty, and administrative dissolution or licence revocation in regulated sectors

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for tax liabilities caused by the abusive structure, held civilly liable for restitution to the tax authority, banned from serving as directors, and subject to criminal imprisonment for complicity in tax fraud or money laundering.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years for laundering the proceeds of crime; freezing of funds, seizure of assets, withdrawal of the payment institution licence, and publication of sanctioning decisions

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal imprisonment, fines, asset freezes, travel bans, and professional disqualification; they may also be jointly and severally liable to civil claimants for economic losses caused by the AML breaches.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties (www.aed.public.lu); Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax (www.impotsdirects.public.lu); unified electronic portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet; official company registry and corporate identification: lbr.lu (Luxembourg Business Registers).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Luxembourg SARL: (1) notarize the articles of association and register the company in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL) through the notary and the lbr.lu/eJustice platform; the RCSL assigns the 13-digit national business registration number (matricule). (2) File the RBE beneficial-ownership declaration (see rbe.guichet.lu) within one month of incorporation. (3) Apply to the AEDT for a VAT identification number by lodging Form 630 (VAT registration application) together with the RCSL extract, articles of association and identity documents of the legal representatives; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT number (N° TVA LU) within approximately one to three weeks. (4) The ACD thereafter attributes the corporate income tax reference number (LIP) and municipal business tax reference for CIT and net wealth tax purposes. (5) A foreign seller without a Luxembourg establishment engaged in distance selling into Luxembourg must register for Luxembourg VAT via MyGuichet; a non-EU vendor is domestically required to appoint an accredited Luxembourg fiscal representative unless the OSS/IOSS special schemes apply. (6) If the SARL makes intra-EU distance sales of goods or receives imported goods for onward sale, the seller should activate the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) modules on guichet.lu for pan-EU VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is an opaque, non-transparent resident company. It is fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT, 17% headline rate, effective rate approx. 24.94% in Luxembourg City including the solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax), net wealth tax (0.5% on net assets) and municipal business tax. It is not eligible for pass-through or look-through treatment, and its worldwide profits are taxable under Luxembourg law. Tax transparency is exclusively available to other vehicle forms (e.g., SCS or SCSp partnerships), not to a SARL.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not administer an elective check-the-box classification regime. Statutory filings and elections available to a SARL include: (i) an opt-in fiscal tax unity (integration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR, available where a resident parent holds at least 95% of the share capital of resident subsidiaries, allowing group loss offset and tax consolidation for CIT and municipal business tax; (ii) the participation exemption regime (Article 166 LIR), which operates by statutory eligibility and requires an annual domestic proof of compliance (10% or EUR 6 million acquisition cost holding, one-year holding period, and the anti-abuse substance requirements); (iii) the IP box election (Article 50bis LIR) granting an 80% deduction of qualifying IP income after the application for the regime; (iv) the SPF election for a family wealth-management company opting into the alternative tax regime (Société de Gestion de Patrimoine Familial); and (v) a binding advance tax ruling issued by the ACD Ruling Commission to confirm hybrid mismatch consequences, though the ATAD 1/2 anti-hybrid rules (Articles 168bis to 168sexies LIR) apply mandatorily and no one-tier transparency election is available for a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident businesses: EUR 0 — no general turnover registration threshold applies; a Luxembourg SARL must register for VAT from the first taxable transaction regardless of turnover. Domestic micro-businesses may elect the small-business franchise only where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000 (pre-VAT), which forfeits input VAT recovery. Intra-EU distance sales of goods by a Luxembourg seller are effectively taxable at destination once aggregate annual distance-sales turnover exceeds EUR 10,000; sales at or below that threshold remain subject to Luxembourg VAT unless the seller opts for OSS. Non-established foreign sellers have no Luxembourg VAT exemption threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT or avail of the OSS/IOSS special schemes for distance sales and imports of goods valued at or below EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 40 of the Luxembourg VAT Law zero-rates supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union by the supplier or a third party acting on its behalf. The following objective criteria must be met to claim 0% export VAT: (i) the 3D printers, resin filaments or consumables are physically dispatched to a non-EU destination within the applicable dispatch deadlines; (ii) the export is evidenced by a valid customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / LUCAS electronic export message) showing the Luxembourg supplier as exporter and goods released for exit from the EU; (iii) the supplier retains commercial transport documents (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, Air Waybill, courier manifests) proving the goods have left the Union; (iv) the supplies are recorded in the Luxembourg VAT return (as zero-rated) and the export evidence is maintained for inspection. Intra-EU B2B dispatches to VAT-registered customers in another Member State are likewise exempt subject to production of a valid EU VAT number and ESL reporting. B2C online sales to EU consumers exceeding the EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold are not zero-rated at export but are taxed in the destination Member State via OSS. Ancillary services directly connected to the export (e.g., freight and delivery charges) also qualify for zero-rating under Article 40(2)(b) of the VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / LUCAS electronic export message) containing the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and customs release status
  • Transport document evidencing physical movement out of the EU: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill / international courier shipment manifest (DHL, FedEx, UPS)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and weight list corresponding exactly to the export declaration
  • Proof of arrival and foreign receipt: foreign customs entry document, signed Proof of Delivery (POD), or logistics carrier track-and-trace report confirming non-EU destination
  • Foreign exchange settlement evidence: inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice) or Stripe/Shopify payout settlement records received from the overseas purchaser

Luxembourg currently imposes no mandatory B2B or B2C electronic invoicing and no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API transmission, or digital stamping obligation. Electronic invoicing is permitted permissively under PEPPOL/UBL formats. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies where suppliers deliver invoices to Luxembourg central public administrations, requiring structured e-invoices (EN 16931/PEPPOL) in the public procurement environment. Luxembourg VAT returns (Form 106) and EU recapitulative statements (ESL) are filed electronically through MyGuichet. Under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) proposals, standardised e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting may become mandatory for cross-border supplies from the 2027-2028 horizon, but no domestic e-invoicing decree is currently operative.

The AEDT applies risk-based, documentary audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Where export conditions are not properly evidenced, the supply is retrospectively re-assessed at the Luxembourg standard VAT rate (16%), with the VAT becoming immediately payable together with statutory late-payment interest and administrative surcharges. Misdeclaration of VAT is punishable by fines of up to EUR 3,000 per infraction under Articles 24-25 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; belated filing penalties (EUR 1,500 per instance and a 10% surcharge on the assessed VAT) may also be imposed. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute VAT fraud (Steuerhinterziehung / fraude fiscale) and expose the legal representatives and directors to criminal sanctions including imprisonment of up to five years and/or fines of up to EUR 250,000 in aggravated cases, as well as personal joint tax liability of the directors under general enforcement rules. Input VAT previously recovered may be clawed back along with the re-assessed output VAT, and the applicable statutory limitation period is extended to up to seven years in cases involving evasion.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has no general foreign-exchange control legislation. The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Capital Movements Code. Trade payments, royalties, loan repayments, equity subscriptions and profit remittances do not require BCL or government approval. Practical restrictions are limited to SARL corporate-law distribution tests, 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax where treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief is not available, and bank-level AML/KYC/source-of-funds documentation. For online retail of 3D printers and consumables, non-resident shareholders may remit profits freely once audited/approved accounts show sufficient distributable reserves and the paying bank has verified ultimate beneficial ownership and tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and Eurosystem law impose no statutory time limit for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio to euro, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. Export receivables from e-commerce sales may be held indefinitely in foreign currency or converted at commercial market rates. Resident enterprises only have statistical balance-of-payments reporting duties to BCL/ECB under applicable Eurostat rules; this is informational and does not affect convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), as Luxembourg's Eurosystem national central bank; no separate foreign-exchange administration exists and the CSSF is the competent banking-sector supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: High with conditions. A fintech EMI is viable if it is licensed or registered by the CSSF as an electronic-money institution in Luxembourg or is passporting an EU/EEA e-money licence under Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. Global PSPs used by the project, including Stripe and Shopify Payments, are acceptable for customer receipt collection and settlement. Legal constraints are material: an EMI may not take deposits from the public, must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts, and must have a complete Luxembourg/EU AML/KYC/CTF framework. Unlicensed non-EU EMIs servicing Luxembourg residents without a passport are not viable and may be treated as unauthorised payment services. The recommended treasury architecture is to collect via the PSP/EMI rail and then sweep EUR to the Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial-bank settlement account for all material disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro injections from EU/EEA shareholders and SWIFT MT103 for non-EU or non-euro injections. The wire narrative must state the exact economic purpose, such as 'Subscription price for SARL shares' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement', and the remitter must be the actual shareholder/lender. The Luxembourg bank will apply an appropriate balance-of-payments statistical code for direct investment/debt claim and will require a board/shareholders' resolution, subscription agreement, notarised articles or capital-increase deed, RCS extract, and source-of-funds/source-of-wealth evidence. For initial incorporation, the notary requires the minimum capital to be deposited in a blocked/escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution before signature; the bank issues an attestation de blocage. Subsequent capital injections should be kept separate from trade receivables in the ledger and formally recorded in the Luxembourg commercial register where the share capital is increased.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Intra-EU for EU suppliers, and SWIFT MT103 or MT103 gpi for non-EU suppliers, including in Field 70 the invoice number, Incoterms, purchase-order number and clear trade purpose. For large or strategic inventory purchases, use a documentary letter of credit issued via MT700 under ICC UCP 600 or documentary collection DP/DA to secure payment. Customer settlement from Stripe and Shopify Payments should be swept into the main EUR treasury account at a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: High with conditions. A fintech EMI is viable if it is licensed or registered by the CSSF as an electronic-money institution in Luxembourg or is passporting an EU/EEA e-money licence under Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. Global PSPs used by the project, including Stripe and Shopify Payments, are acceptable for customer receipt collection and settlement. Legal constraints are material: an EMI may not take deposits from the public, must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts, and must have a complete Luxembourg/EU AML/KYC/CTF framework. Unlicensed non-EU EMIs servicing Luxembourg residents without a passport are not viable and may be treated as unauthorised payment services. The recommended treasury architecture is to collect via the PSP/EMI rail and then sweep EUR to the Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial-bank settlement account for all material disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined Luxembourg City rate (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge, being 7% of CIT, + 6.75% municipal business tax). A reduced CIT band of 15% applies to the first EUR 175,000 of taxable income, producing a combined rate of 22.80% for that tranche.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Luxembourg SARL to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident; the lower withholding rate of 15% applies without a filing requirement for the distributing company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in a tax year forces the SARL to prepare and maintain a Luxembourg Local File demonstrating arm's-length pricing; a Master File is required for groups whose consolidated turnover reaches EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000. Even below the EUR 1,000,000 threshold, the ACD may request contemporaneous documentation when there is a risk of profit shifting.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a separate offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the OECD/Harmful Tax Practices framework, the ATAD GAAR, and the settled case-law application of the abuse-of-law doctrine require real economic presence. A qualifying e-commerce SARL must maintain: a genuine registered office, local books and records, local personnel with decision-making or management functions, physical or contractual infrastructure (warehouse, fulfillment, supplier contracts), and a business purpose aligned with its actual online retail activities. A 'letterbox' entity generating only passive reseller margins without local activity is exposed to re-domestication, denial of treaty/directive benefits, and reclassification of its Luxembourg residence to the jurisdiction of central management and control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fiscal fraud)

Statutory sanction: Administrative: monthly arrears interest at 0.85%, 10% late-payment surcharge, and civil reassessment. Criminal: under Art. 396 Luxembourg General Tax Law, a fine of up to EUR 125,000 per offence and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years for fraudulent evasion; in aggravated cases the fine may be doubled, and seizure of corporate assets including bank accounts and payment-rail reserves (Stripe/Shopify balances) is authorized.

Director personal liability: The gérant (manager) is personally liable for restitution of unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties where fraud or gross misconduct is proven; personal assets may be seized. Criminal courts may impose an interdiction of professional duties and a travel ban (interdiction de sortie du territoire); imprisonment up to 5 years applies. Piercing the corporate veil follows under Art. 523 of the Law on Commercial Companies for abuse of corporate assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Tax consequences: full re-assessments under the ATAD GAAR and Luxembourg abuse-of-law doctrines, disallowance of all artificial deductions, recharacterization of conduit payments as distributions subject to 15% withholding tax, denial of treaty benefits and of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and possible determination that the SARL's place of effective management lies abroad, triggering terminal tax on deemed asset disposals and additional net wealth tax.

Director personal liability: Managers and shareholder-directors who approve sham arrangements face personal joinder of tax liabilities, civil liability for distributions paid out of artificial profits, and criminal sanctions for complicity in tax fraud, including fines and imprisonment; the commercial register may strike the company, allowing the ACD to collect from the gérant personally.
AML / UBO / Payment-Rail Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: RBE violations: administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 for a legal person and EUR 1,000 per day of persistence under the Law of 13 January 2019. AML/CTF: fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Luxembourg Criminal Code for money laundering; payment service providers may terminate the Stripe/Shopify merchant account and freeze residual balances.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable to the SARL and regulators for breach of the beneficial ownership and AML duties; criminal conviction triggers directorial disqualification, asset freezes, and restraint orders. A travel ban may be applied to prevent flight in serious money-laundering proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Improper Benchmarking

Statutory sanction: Adjustment by the ACD under Art. 56 LIR and OECD rules to an arm's-length outcome, imposition of undeclared dividend treatment on the upward adjustment with 15% withholding tax, monthly 0.85% interest, a 10% penalty on reassessed taxes, and a specific documentation penalty of 20% of the additional assessment when the Local/Master File has not been produced.

Director personal liability: Managers who sign incorrectly benchmarked returns and TP documentation are jointly responsible for the reassessed tax where the company cannot pay; personal exposure includes civil damages payable to the ACD, disqualification from management, and criminal liability if the mispricing is found to be a deliberate fraudulent scheme. A Deemed-dividend assessment can be recovered personally where the gérant authorized the artificial intercompany margin.

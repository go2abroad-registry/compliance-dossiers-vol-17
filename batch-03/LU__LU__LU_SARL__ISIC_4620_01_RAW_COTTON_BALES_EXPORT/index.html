Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration; all filings and registrations effected via the MyGuichet.lu national e-government portal and the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate SARL before the Luxembourg notary and register with the RCS to obtain the national identification number (matricule d'identification, 11-digit 'L' prefix). 2) File the electronic VAT registration application (Formulaire 640) with the AEDT simultaneously with the RCS filing, or within 15 days of the first taxable transaction. 3) Foreign non-EU entities must appoint a Luxembourg-established VAT fiscal representative (assujetti établi) and provide apostilled incorporation documents, Articles of Association, and proof of the beneficial owner's identity for AML verification. 4) Register for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax via ACD Form 1/registration questionnaire once the RCS matricule is issued; 5) Acquire the EORI number (European Union Registration and Identification) from the Customs and Excise Agency to lodge export declarations; 6) The completed TIN/VAT ID is issued electronically within 2-4 weeks of submission of full documentation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: Luxembourg-resident SARL is taxable on worldwide income at entity level at the aggregate standard rate of 24.94% (Luxembourg City, comprising 17% corporate income tax, 7% municipal business tax on taxable basis adjusted to 3.5%, and 0.2475% solidarity surcharge). No pass-through or fiscal transparency for SARL; the company is an autonomous subject of tax. Exemption for qualifying participation (≥10% or €1.2M acquisition cost, held ≥12 months) under the participation exemption regime, and no withholding tax on dividends qualifying under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. — 1) Incorporate SARL before the Luxembourg notary and register with the RCS to obtain the national identification number (matricule d'identification, 11-digit 'L' prefix). 2) File the electronic VAT registration application (Formulaire 640) with the AEDT simultaneously with the RCS filing, or within 15 days of the first taxable transaction. 3) Foreign non-EU entities must appoint a Luxembourg-established VAT fiscal representative (assujetti établi) and provide apostilled incorporation documents, Articles of Association, and proof of the beneficial owner's identity for AML verification. 4) Register for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax via ACD Form 1/registration questionnaire once the RCS matricule is issued; 5) Acquire the EORI number (European Union Registration and Identification) from the Customs and Excise Agency to lodge export declarations; 6) The completed TIN/VAT ID is issued electronically within 2-4 weeks of submission of full documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: Luxembourg-resident SARL is taxable on worldwide income at entity level at the aggregate standard rate of 24.94% (Luxembourg City, comprising 17% corporate income tax, 7% municipal business tax on taxable basis adjusted to 3.5%, and 0.2475% solidarity surcharge). No pass-through or fiscal transparency for SARL; the company is an autonomous subject of tax. Exemption for qualifying participation (≥10% or €1.2M acquisition cost, held ≥12 months) under the participation exemption regime, and no withholding tax on dividends qualifying under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Available election forms

Luxembourg domestic law does not provide a check-the-box election; SARL is mandatorily entity-level taxed. Available elections: (1) Group taxation (régime d'intégration fiscale) election for 95%-owned resident subsidiaries (full tax consolidation); (2) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 61 VAT law for closely linked resident entities; (3) Co-ownership (copropriété) treatment or tax-transparent FCP/SCSp is not available to SARL; (4) US tax purposes: eligible as a US check-the-box non-per se corporation election to be treated as partnership/disregarded entity, subject to Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3; (5) Hybrid mismatch: Luxembourg has transposed ATAD (Law of 21 December 2018) incorporating EU anti-hybrid rules; no elective carve-out exists except grandfathered instruments existing before 1 January 2017 and pre-ATAD structures protected until 31 December 2021; mismatches triggered by the SARL must be neutralised by the payor rule regardless of jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small business exemption (Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law) — however, the threshold does not apply to export/brokerage of goods without established place of supply in Luxembourg; registration is mandatory from the first transaction if the SARL is established in Luxembourg (no threshold benefit for export activities where input VAT recovery is sought).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies under Article 41.I of the Luxembourg VAT Law to supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting for them, including the international brokerage of raw cotton bales (ex-Quotation 4620) where the broker's service is directly linked to the exported goods and the goods never enter the Luxembourg domestic supply chain; alternative zero-rating applies to intra-EU dispatch (Article 43) subject to the customer providing a valid VIES VAT number; the zero-rating is conditional exclusively on the SARL holding contemporaneous documentary evidence of the physical movement of the goods across the EU external border or the customer's registration in the destination EU state, and on the invoice issuing within 15 days of the supply of services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / SAD — Form EX-A and the electronic AES export declaration lodged via Luxembourg Customs IT system), bearing the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and final non-EU destination customs office of exit
  • Bill of Lading (for ocean freight) or CMR consignment note (for road transport to non-EU border such as Belarus/Russia) or Air Waybill (for air cargo), evidencing the actual departure of the cotton bales from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/FX confirmation from the bank evidencing the USD/EUR wire transfer received from the non-EU buyer, matching the invoice value and shipment date
  • Incoterms-Dated Commercial Invoice (with explicit 'Exported from EU / Taxé 0%' annotation), Packing List, Certificate of Origin, and the Buyer's written Purchase Order confirming the non-EU destination

No real-time tax-authority fiscal clearance (no French-style FADETTE or Italian regime) — Luxembourg mandates B2G e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network (Peppol BIS 3.0) for all invoices to central public administration and municipal authorities; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary but all received e-invoices must be accepted; issuance of structured EDI/e-invoice requires prior AEDT approval for EDI-based systems; invoices must contain the RCS matricule and the VAT ID; self-billing and invoicing by the third-party appraiser are permitted; all export invoices must be retained in original format for 10 years (Article 72 VAT Law). No digital stamping requirement for paper invoices.

The AEDT customs audit unit performs ex-post validation of export documentation samples; if the SARL cannot produce the complete bundle within the 10-year retention period or if transport documents are not dated within 90 days of the invoice, the zero-rating is reversed and the entire VAT in dispute becomes immediately payable with retrospective effect up to 5 years (extended to 10 years in cases of tax fraud or intentional deception); administrative penalty of 10% of the underestimated tax plus late interest of 0.6% per month; for deliberate falsification of the export evidence, criminal prosecution under Article 49 VAT Law triggers fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus publication of the judgment; the ACD may additionally reassess corporate income tax on the same transaction under Article 60-AO; under the EU anti-fraud programme, the transaction is cross-checked against the VIES and the customs trader files (ECS) — a missing reconciliation of AES exit confirmation automatically triggers the withdrawal of the exporter status and application of 17% VAT with clawback of input credits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions within the Eurozone. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no prior payment authorizations, no currency quotas, and no foreign-exchange licensing for trade settlements. Profit remittance is unrestricted: dividends bear the standard Luxembourg withholding tax of 15%, fully reduced to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate parents under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU); no additional central-bank approval is required. Free movement of capital is guaranteed under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in euro or any freely convertible foreign currency without mandatory conversion to euro, without surrender deadlines, and without export-deposit requirements. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL is required for material cross-border goods flows and direct-investment movements.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the authorized prudential and AML/CFT supervisor of credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; exchange-control functions are subsumed under BCL monetary-policy operations and CSSF licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU/EEA license cannot legally provide payment services to a Luxembourg SARL under PSD2. Feasibility rating: Low for non-passported offshore EMIs. Only CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMIs or EU/EEA-passported digital banks may operate, and they typically impose stricter-than-average entity-level KYC. Feasibility rating for EU-passported digital EMIs: Medium, contingent on the EMI offering a substantive Luxembourg servicing channel, SWIFT-correspondent coverage, and acceptance of the SARL's raw-cotton trade documents.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Counterparty exposure to cotton-producing jurisdictions with elevated corruption, sanctions, or trade-transparency risk (e.g., Central Asia, West Africa, East Africa)

Defense evidence: Supply-chain KYC pack: contractual chain from ginning mill to FOB port, independent origin certificates and internationally classed cotton certificates (e.g., CAI/ICA), chamber-of-commerce abstracts, real-time UN/EU/OFAC sanctions screening results, bank reference letters for each material supplier, and a signed four-eyes compliance opinion from the SARL's RCSI (Responsable du respect des obligations).

Defense evidence: Board-approved treasury mandate and projected cash-flow model, forecast trade volumes under cotton brokerage and export agreements, continuously updated AML/KYC file maintained by the RCSI, and designation of a dedicated compliance contact for the bank's relationship team.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders inject equity via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires into the notarial blocked-funds account (compte de blocage) opened for the SARL; each wire must cite the incorporation deed reference, the notary's account details, and the statutory purpose code. Under BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting, capital contributions are classified under direct-investment code EO 438. The receiving bank requires source-of-funds evidence (shareholder reserves, audited financials, purchase agreements); upon issuance of the notarial certificate and registration in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), funds are released to the operational account.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés de Luxembourg (RCS), administered through the eJustice portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016 and the Law of 23 September 2020; SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 to 710-23 (Book VII); Law of 19 December 2002 on the register of commerce and companies and annual accounts; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE); Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector for factoring licensing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif) executed before a Luxembourg notary and filed with the RCS; publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) in the name of the SARL in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution or the Caisse de Consignation; a bank attestation de blocage certifying the deposit must be delivered to the notary before signature of the deed of incorporation, and the funds remain blocked until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety conformity certificate from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) verifying evacuation routes, fire-extinguishing equipment, and smoke-detection installations; structural and electrical safety inspections issued by an accredited bureau de contrôle; workplace clearance obtained from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering ventilation, workstation ergonomics, electrical safety, hygiene, and first-aid equipment; occupational accident insurance coverage registered with the CCSS.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) in the name of the SARL in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution or the Caisse de Consignation; a bank attestation de blocage certifying the deposit must be delivered to the notary before signature of the deed of incorporation, and the funds remain blocked until registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; portal: https://guichet.lu (MyGuichet e-filing)

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL must be incorporated and registered in the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the ACD assigns a direct tax matricule number (numéro d'identification). To charge VAT or claim input VAT, a separate VAT identification number (TVA number) must be obtained from the AEDT using the VAT registration declaration Form 480/A before commencing taxable activities. Foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment may apply for direct VAT registration through a Luxembourg tax representative. The statutory corporate TIN is the RCS matricule, complemented by the TVA number for indirect tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL must be incorporated and registered in the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the ACD assigns a direct tax matricule number (numéro d'identification). To charge VAT or claim input VAT, a separate VAT identification number (TVA number) must be obtained from the AEDT using the VAT registration declaration Form 480/A before commencing taxable activities. Foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment may apply for direct VAT registration through a Luxembourg tax representative. The statutory corporate TIN is the RCS matricule, complemented by the TVA number for indirect tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg tax law; a resident SARL is treated as a non-transparent, entity-level taxable corporation. Statutory elections available: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for corporate income tax and municipal business tax among qualifying 95%-owned resident group companies; (2) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) allowing multiple taxable persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (3) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatorily applied under EU ATAD and do not require a taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For Luxembourg-established small taxpayers: annual turnover threshold of EUR 35,000 for the small enterprise exemption. For foreign or non-resident entities, no registration threshold applies; VAT registration is mandatory before effecting any taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero rating requires proof of physical export outside the EU, evidenced by customs export declaration and transport documents, or an intra-Community supply to an EU VAT-registered customer whose VAT ID is confirmed in VIES. Services: a zero-rate or outside-scope treatment applies if the place of supply under the B2B reverse-charge rules is outside Luxembourg or outside the EU. Factoring and accounts-receivable financing is VAT-exempt under Article 44 Loi TVA; when provided to a non-Luxembourg business, the place of supply is the customer's country, so no Luxembourg VAT falls due and input VAT (if any) is recovered via the periodic VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G transactions via the PEPPOL e-delivery network. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance requirement, or digital stamping for B2B or B2C invoices. VAT returns and EU recapitulative statements are filed electronically through MyGuichet with the AEDT; no continuous transaction control regime is currently in force.

Fraudulent or erroneous zero-rating claims are subject to AEDT audit verification, retrospective clawback of VAT, reversal of input VAT recovery, and statutory interest on under-declared tax. Administrative fines up to EUR 10,000 (or up to EUR 100,000 in aggravated fraud cases) may be imposed; criminal liability, including imprisonment, applies under Article 211 Loi TVA. Directors, officers, and tax representatives may be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting VAT and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg applies the EU Treaty freedoms and implements the Law of 12 July 2017 on the obligation to collect balance of payments information; there are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements for payments, and no restrictions on dividend or profit remittance beyond standard corporate law distribution tests and beneficial ownership disclosure requirements under the Law of 1 August 2019 on the professional activities of the financial sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation or surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may hold foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject only to the EU Balance of Payments reporting thresholds under CSSF circulars and BCL statistical reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and financial prudential supervision, and the Ministry of Finance for financial legislation

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An EU/EEA e-money institution passported into Luxembourg may provide payment accounts and transaction processing, but such EMI accounts are generally not suitable for capital escrow or factoring settlement because they do not offer credit facilities, commercial letters of credit, or segregated account features that full credit institutions provide. Additionally, Luxembourg financial sector regulation requires factoring activities to be conducted through robust banking channels, and credit institution underwriting standards often treat EMI accounts as higher risk due to their limited transparency and lack of direct supervisory coverage in Luxembourg.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain spanning multiple jurisdictions with nominee shareholders or layered special purpose vehicles, creating opacity of control.

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain spanning multiple jurisdictions with nominee shareholders or layered special purpose vehicles, creating opacity of control.

Defense evidence: Complete RBE extract from the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners; notarised share transfer deeds; certified copies of constitutional documents; organisational chart showing direct and indirect shareholding; declarations of beneficial ownership from each intermediary jurisdiction; passport copies and proof of residence for all UBOs.
High aggregate turnover of the factoring vehicle relative to stated paid-in capital, raising suspicion of unlicensed lending or money service business.

Root cause: High aggregate turnover of the factoring vehicle relative to stated paid-in capital, raising suspicion of unlicensed lending or money service business.

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements (at least two years); legally binding factoring master agreement and underlying trade invoices; external audit confirmation of the client receivables portfolio; transactional forecast model; VAT and corporate tax registrations evidencing the professed commercial activity; licences or exemption confirmations from the CSSF for the ISIC 6492 factoring activity.
Cross-border transaction counterparties domiciled in sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions on the FATF grey list, triggering enhanced due diligence.

Root cause: Cross-border transaction counterparties domiciled in sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions on the FATF grey list, triggering enhanced due diligence.

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening results from a reputable list; trade compliance letters; contracts incorporating ICC Incoterms 2020; bank reference letters for counterparties; shipping and logistics documentation; end-beneficiary declarations confirmed through a sworn statement and supporting certificates of origin.
Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of any director or UBO.

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) status of any director or UBO.

Defense evidence: PEP screening system output with scoring; written explanation of the source of funds and source of wealth; notarised bank or tax statements covering the last 12 months; confirmation of low-risk government position with no involvement in procurement; independent legal opinion on full compliance with the Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF AML/CFT requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity or shareholder loan injections, wire transfers should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with purpose codes B030 (equity capital) or S130 (shareholder loans), clearly referencing the SARL's name and the capital escrow account. Prior to execution, the bank requires a board resolution confirming the injection, a certificate of payment for share capital or a notarised shareholder loan agreement, and evidence of source funds. The CSSF balance of payments report must classify the transaction as direct investment; no prior approval is required, but the payment reason must be accurately encoded to support the treasury record.Distributions to sellers in a supply chain factoring programme are effected by SEPA Credit Transfer in euro (SEPA CT) or by SWIFT MT103 for cross-border currency. For each disbursement, banks require a counterparty instruction, a commercial invoice, and a signed assignment or purchase-to-discount agreement. For large trade settlements, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) may be utilised through the confirming bank; standard clearing rails include SWIFT MT103/202, TARGET2 for critical euro settlement, and CLS for FX-linked payments. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Identification Entity name, RCS registration number, LEI, registered office, tax year opening and closing dates, legal form. Non-resident factoring company with a Luxembourg permanent establishment (PE): identify the PE, local registered agent and AED registration number. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Box 2 - Commercial result Net profit or loss per the statutory commercial balance sheet and profit-and-loss account. Attach PE-only financial statements reflecting the factoring business attributable to the Luxembourg PE. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Box 3 - Reintegrations Add-back of non-deductible expenses: unsubstantiated factoring discount charges, fines, excessive director fees, non-documented related-party payments. Non-resident filers must document all intra-group factoring fee and discount allocations to Luxembourg. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Box 4 - Deductions Deduction of exempt income: qualifying participation-exemption dividends, IP Box relief, tax losses brought forward. Claim only to the extent the PE can evidence shareholding, holding period and substance conditions. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Box 5 - Tax computation Determination of taxable income; application of 17% CIT, 7% employment-fund surcharge and ICC at the municipal coefficient. Apply the same computation for the PE; request treaty MAP relief only if double taxation is not otherwise relieved. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Box 6 - Prepayments and credits Advance CIT/ICC payments made during the year, withholding tax credits, final tax due or refund. Non-residents must attach evidence of advance payments made through the local AED account. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.
Annexes - Form 160 / CbC / TP Integrated net wealth tax declaration, Country-by-Country notification, transfer pricing documentation index. Attach the CbC notification and TP file if the group revenue or related-party threshold is exceeded. Late or non-filing triggers an estimated assessment (taxation d'office) and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 (Art. 174 StAnpG); late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month; fraudulent filing is prosecuted under Art. 178 StAnpG with imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fines.

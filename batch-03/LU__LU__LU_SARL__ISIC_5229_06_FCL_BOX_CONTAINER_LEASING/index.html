Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and excise; Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency) for customs export procedures and EORI; single national e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (RCS/LBR corporate filings, AEDT VAT filing, VIES recapitulative statements, SAF-T submissions, customs ECS declarations).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute notarial deed of incorporation and deposit it with the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) via the Luxembourg Business Registers electronic portal on MyGuichet.lu; the RCS assigns a 13-digit national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule) which automatically serves as the corporate tax reference for the ACD, with no separate ACD application required. Step 2: File Form 640 (declaration d'inscription à la TVA) with the AEDT before commencing taxable activity; the AEDT issues an 8-digit VAT identification number with the LU prefix (format LU + 8 digits) and coordinates with customs for EORI issuance (the VAT number generally functions as the EORI). Step 3: Register all beneficial owners in the RBE (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs) within one month of incorporation, with penalties for late filing. Step 4: Non-resident companies without a Luxembourg fixed establishment but making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AEDT under the simplified non-established VAT registration procedure, submitting proof of foreign VAT ID, constitutional documents, foreign register excerpt and director identification; no notarisation is required for the VAT-only registration. Step 5: For the ISO container leasing SARL, the LU VAT ID covers intra-Community supplies (EC Sales List filings), export declarations via ECS, and supplier verification under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; classification follows statutory legal form. A SARL is a resident capital company subject to entity-level corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax if its registered office (siège statutaire) or central administration (direction effective) is in Luxembourg; worldwide income is taxed with territorial exemptions for foreign permanent establishments and for qualifying participations (participation exemption, Article 166 LIR). Available elections: (1) fiscal unity / integrated taxation under Article 164bis LIR for 99%+ owned resident group members (CIT and municipal business tax consolidation); (2) VAT grouping election under Article 60 LTVA creating a single taxable person for financially, economically and organisationally linked entities; (3) waiver of the small-enterprise VAT exemption under Article 57 LTVA; (4) advance tax ruling application to the ACD confirming tax classification and indirect tax treatment of the container leasing model; (5) IP box election under Article 50bis LIR for qualifying intellectual property income; (6) exit tax deferral election under Article 111 LIR; (7) no hybrid mismatch election is available — anti-hybrid rules under Article 168quater LIR (ATAD 1 and 2) and the general anti-abuse rule under Article 6a LIR override contractual classifications that result in deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for established suppliers: any business supplying taxable goods or services in Luxembourg must register unless covered by the small-enterprise exemption. Small-enterprise exemption threshold: EUR 35,000 annual turnover (Article 57 LTVA); crossing the threshold or voluntarily waiving the exemption triggers VAT registration. Intra-EU distance sales of goods and remote B2C services: EUR 10,000 annual cross-border turnover threshold per Member State under the Union scheme; above this threshold, Luxembourg VAT registration is mandatory. Non-established suppliers of B2C services and electronically supplied services have zero registration threshold and must register from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to non-EU destinations (Article 43 LTVA): zero-rated where the ISO containers are exported from EU customs territory and the supplier holds proof of export showing the goods left the EU within 90 days of supply. Intra-Community supplies (Article 44 LTVA): zero-rated as exempt with deduction where containers are dispatched to a customer in another Member State, the customer's valid VAT ID is confirmed via VIES, the EC Sales List/recapitulative statement is filed, and transport documents evidence the movement. For cross-border container box leasing, which is by law a supply of services (hire of goods), the bare lease does not qualify for export zero-rating; the general B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 15 LTVA) locates the supply where the customer is established, leaving the lease outside Luxembourg VAT and subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction, subject to documentary evidence of customer business status and VAT ID. Services directly linked to international maritime transport of goods, including certain container handling and inland legs aggregated to a definite shipping voyage, may qualify for exemption with deduction under Articles 50 and 51 LTVA; given case-law sensitivity, an advance VAT ruling from the AEDT is recommended to secure the treatment of the specific leasing contract.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EXA filed through the ECS electronic system, including the Movement Reference Number) issued by Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, CMR or Air Waybill proving physical departure of the containers from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/bank credit advice) evidencing settlement of the export transaction and traceability of foreign currency proceeds to the SARL's Luxembourg bank account

Luxembourg has no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is voluntary for B2B and B2C; every invoice must guarantee authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility (Article 84 LTVA) via audit trail, EDI or electronic signature. Mandatory structured e-invoicing applies only to B2G public procurement supplies, with contracting authorities obliged to receive PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoices; supplier-side B2G e-invoicing is being phased in. SAF-T-LUX (fichier d'écritures comptables) is the statutory VAT audit file; its electronic transmission to the AEDT is progressively mandatory: large taxpayers from 1 January 2023, mid-size taxpayers from 1 January 2025, with extension to all VAT payers in subsequent phases. The EU ViDA reform is expected to introduce mandatory structured e-invoicing for cross-border B2B supplies from 2030, with domestic B2B mandates thereafter.

AEDT and customs jointly audit zero-rating claims against the complete documentary bundle; missing or inconsistent export evidence triggers retrospective reassessment of output VAT, denial of corresponding input VAT deduction, statutory interest on arrears at 0.8% per month (9.6% annualised), and administrative fines of up to EUR 2,500 per infraction under the VAT Law. In cases of forged export documents, circular transactions, or artificial arrangements lacking economic substance, the GAAR (Article 6a LIR) and VAT anti-abuse rules apply, exposing the SARL to criminal tax fraud penalties under the criminal provisions of the VAT Law and the Criminal Code: imprisonment ranging from 8 days to 5 years and/or fines from EUR 250 to EUR 50,000, publication of sanctions, exclusion from the deduction of input VAT, and extension of the audit and clawback period to 10 years. Standard reassessment remains possible for 5 years from the end of the declaration year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions, reflecting EU Treaty obligations on free movement of capital. There are no approval requirements for cross-border payments, no exchange control surrender requirements, and no restrictions on foreign currency accounts. Profit remittance by a Luxembourg SARL is structurally unimpeded from an FX perspective; distributable profits may be transferred abroad net of any applicable Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends. The 15% normal withholding tax on dividends is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or Luxembourg domestic participation exemption rules, and to 5% or 0% under applicable tax treaties, provided the documentary thresholds are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts. Invoice settlement may be retained in EUR or any freely negotiated foreign currency. The only legal obligations concern record-keeping under commercial law, tax documentation, and anti-money laundering / sanctions screening under EU regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for financial sector supervision and payment systems oversight; no separate foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions holding a MiFID / API passport may provide euro IBAN accounts and cross-border payment connectivity to a Luxembourg SARL. However, such arrangements are typically inferior to bank credit lines, are often rejected for escrow or capital-subscription deposits because they do not satisfy all notarial evidence chains, and may trigger enhanced scrutiny where EMIs rely on distributed agent networks. A Luxembourg SARL engaged in physical container leasing should maintain at least one tier-1 Luxembourg bank account or an EU branch of a credit institution authorised by the BCL/CSSF for core treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions to a Luxembourg SARL must be wired from the shareholder’s verified bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the company’s dedicated capital account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire instruction must include a clear purpose code reflecting 'Shareholders’ equity contribution' and, where applicable, the name of the company and the deed reference. For bank compliance, provide the notarised incorporation deed or deed of amendment evidencing the share capital increase, share subscription forms, and proof of identity of the fund remitter. Beneficiary accounts should be blocked for operational drawdowns until the notarial capital verification certificate has been obtained and any statutory publication has been made in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register. Inbound loans should be documented by a formal loan agreement and routed through the same compliant banking network using an intercompany loan purpose code.Container purchases and operating expenses are paid using standard SWIFT MT103 customer credits for single payments, while SWIFT MT202 or MT202 COV may be used for interbank cover or high-value transfers between financial institutions. Trade-related settlements with container manufacturers and stevedores can be executed via irrevocable commercial letters of credit, documentary collection, or open account with purchase-order support. The Luxembourg SARL may also use electronic payments through TARGET2 and SEPA INST for euro-based operational disbursements; for non-euro counterparties, a dual-currency settlement structure should be established to maximise payment efficiency. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions holding a MiFID / API passport may provide euro IBAN accounts and cross-border payment connectivity to a Luxembourg SARL. However, such arrangements are typically inferior to bank credit lines, are often rejected for escrow or capital-subscription deposits because they do not satisfy all notarial evidence chains, and may trigger enhanced scrutiny where EMIs rely on distributed agent networks. A Luxembourg SARL engaged in physical container leasing should maintain at least one tier-1 Luxembourg bank account or an EU branch of a credit institution authorised by the BCL/CSSF for core treasury operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (Impôt sur le Revenu des Collectivités) plus 7% solidarity surcharge (Contribution de Solidarité) plus municipal business tax (Impôt Commercial Communal, 6.75% in Luxembourg City), yielding aggregate headline rate of 24.94% for Luxembourg City

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a Luxembourg resident SARL; reduced to 0% in qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive scenarios and tax treaty cases where exemption or zero rate is available (e.g., dividends to Swiss resident parent under applicable protocols may be subject 0% or 5%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Country-by-Country Report threshold is EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue; Luxembourg statutory local-file/master-file requirements apply when the taxpayer either exceeds EUR 3,000,000 aggregate intercompany transactions during a fiscal year or is part of a group with EUR 50,000,000 aggregated net turnover; container-leasing fees, fleet management fees, and financing transactions are deemed controlled transactions and must be documented contemporaneously; smaller SARLs must still keep sufficient documentation upon request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a formal statutory Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) equivalent UAE-style, but an economic substance requirement is applied through the EU ATAD anti-abuse articles (Article 6, 60ter LIR), the Shell Company Act of 2023, DTA limitation-of-benefit clauses and administrative practice: an SARL leasing ISO containers in Luxembourg must demonstrate actual decision-making, management staff, physical office space, aggregation of container fleet in tax records, and operational supervision; absence of substance will trigger treaty denial, dividend withholding at statutory 15%, potential recharacterization of transactions, and administrative penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent Understatement of CIT

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of evaded tax plus penalty of up to 150% of omitted tax (majoration pour manœuvre frauduleuse); criminal fine of €10,000 to €50,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; publication of the sanction by the Luxembourg direct tax administration in cases of serious recidivism

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the tax debt where they signed the false return, committed fraud not attributable to the company, or stripped the SARL of assets; statutory liability under Article 1851 Civil Code and LIR Art. 62 (veil piercing); travel bans and detention pending criminal prosecution; civil restitution to the tax authority of the evaded taxes plus interest.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of corporate tax losses and treaty benefits under the Shell Company Act of 29 December 2023; application of Article 6 ATAD: minimum of 15% withholding recovered at source; reclassification of the SARL as transparent for tax purposes; revocation of advance tax rulings; fines for tax avoidance up to €30,000 and possible commercial deregistration

Director personal liability: Nominee directors may be personally assessed for unpaid withholding taxes if the company lacks substance; civil liability for orchestrating an artificial arrangement arises under general tort law; managers may face travel restrictions and enforcement proceedings under the 2018 EU Mutual Recovery Directive.
Transfer Pricing and Document Deficiency

Statutory sanction: An upward tax adjustment restating taxable income at arm’s length; penalty of 25% of the additional tax paid for grossly negligent TP documentation (Art. 100 StAnp/LIR); possible secondary adjustment and deemed dividends; interest of 0.8% per month on underpaid CIT until settlement

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL who knowingly approved inaccurate intercompany pricing are jointly and severally liable for the company’s unpaid tax liabilities; penalties against directors under Luxembourg tax law can be up to €20,000; court-ordered restitution of benefit obtained by the related party may be sought and personal bankruptcy for unpaid debt if fraud driving the TP file is proven.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Infringement

Statutory sanction: Post-BRED-actuary enforcement: administrative fines up to €1,250,000; criminal penalties of imprisonment for 6 months to 5 years; immediate suspension of the SARL’s right to hold bank accounts; forfeiture of container assets used in the offence; CSSF will issue a formal notice and sanctions

Director personal liability: Each director and effective manager may be individually criminally prosecuted for AML failures; personal fines up to €2,500,000 for entities, shareholders may be ordered to disgorge profits; travel bans and extradition risk if criminal laundering is proved; personal bankruptcy upon civil judgment for damages to third-party creditors.

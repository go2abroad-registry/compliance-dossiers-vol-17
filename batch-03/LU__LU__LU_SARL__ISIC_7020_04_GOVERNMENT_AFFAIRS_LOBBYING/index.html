Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), filed through a Luxembourg civil-law notary.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Companies Act of 10 August 1915, as consolidated and amended, in particular Title V (Articles 109 to 216) governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (S.à r.l.); Law of 19 December 2002 on the register of commerce and companies and the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA); relevant notarial provisions of the Civil Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) containing the articles of association (statuts), filed electronically by the notary with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The S.à r.l. requires minimum issued capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. At formation, at least 25% of the nominal value of each subscribed share must be paid in cash; cash contributions are deposited in a Luxembourg bank account in the name of the company in formation and confirmed by a bank certificate to the notary. Contributions in kind require full payment at incorporation and an independent valuation report. No state-run escrow account is required; the notary and bank certificate serve as the verification mechanism.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance must be obtained through the communal fire safety officer and the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours for the leased premises. Structural load certification is provided by the municipal building authority under the granted occupancy permit. Workplace compliance clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) is required for office layout, emergency exits, electrical installations, ventilation, lighting, and accessibility.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDTVA) for VAT and registration duties; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / RCS) for commercial registration and corporate TIN issuance. Main digital portals: MyGuichet.lu, e-TVA (AEDTVA), eCDF (ACD) and LBR.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed (or deed executed before a notary) with a minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000. Step 2: File the incorporation file with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) and obtain the RCS registration number and the 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which serves as the statutory corporate TIN for direct tax purposes. Step 3: Request VAT registration with the AEDTVA through MyGuichet.lu (Form 102) once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 25,000, or earlier where the SARL carries out intra-EU acquisitions above EUR 10,000 per year, receives reverse-charge services, or makes supplies of services or goods that are taxable in Luxembourg; upon approval the AEDTVA issues the LU VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). Step 4: Register the SARL with the ACD for corporate income tax and net wealth tax and with the competent communal administration for municipal business tax, and set up the electronic filing profile (monthly or quarterly VAT via e-TVA, annual CIT return via eCDF). Step 5: File the annual corporate income tax return with the ACD, usually by 31 December of the following financial year, with extensions available upon request.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the SARL is classified by legal form as a fully taxable resident corporate entity (subject to CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax on worldwide income). Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR, requiring a 95% direct or indirect holding committed for at least five years and a joint election filed with the ACD; (2) VAT grouping election allowing closely-linked Luxembourg-established persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (3) advance tax rulings from the ACD on participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR), financing, hybrid-mismatch and cross-border restructuring affairs; (4) choice of an accounting reference period other than the calendar year with the corresponding CIT and VAT filing calendar; (5) ATAD and hybrid-mismatch documentation elections to be supported in the CIT file.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full euro-area member with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility. There is no exchange-control licensing, no mandatory conversion, and no central-bank pre-authorisation for profit remittances. Profit distribution by a Luxembourg SARL is instead subject to ordinary corporate law: annual accounts approval and the net-assets distribution test under the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, and to company solvency rules. For a government-affairs and lobbying entity, remittances to foreign clients or shareholders must also pass AML diligence and economic-substance controls; BoP statistical reporting to the BCL may be required when cross-border flows exceed the reporting threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Luxembourg legal order imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, and no foreign-exchange repatriation obligation. Export receipts, service fees and retainer income may be retained in EUR or in the invoiced currency. The only relevant obligations are transfer-pricing documentation for connected parties, VAT and invoicing rules, and the need to identify the purpose of inbound funds for BCL statistical purposes and for the bank's AML screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; exchange-control supervision is also framed by national implementation of EU capital-movement rules under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and by the Luxembourg financial-sector laws administered with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium_Feasible_With_Restrictions. A Luxembourg SARL may validly open payment accounts at an EEA-authorised electronic-money institution operating under the PSD2 passport, but such accounts are secondary rails, not statutory bank accounts. Offshore EMIs located outside the EEA cannot provide reliable treasury, escrow or lending services to a Luxembourg regulated-risk company; they also fail the CSSF's expectations for a client's first credit institution. For a government-affairs and lobbying entity, the primary account must remain with a Luxembourg or EU credit institution because the AML and fitness checks, withholdings, currency clearing and capital-blocking functions require a deposit-taking bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from shareholders or group entities are routed by SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA currencies or by SEPA credit transfer for EUR, with an unambiguous payment message referencing the company name, the payment purpose, a board-resolution date and the capital-subscription transaction. Typical purpose codes used in the Luxembourg banking market for direct equity investments include G30 under the BPM6 classification, possibly complemented by bank-specific code CAPIN and an accepted wording such as capital increase for LU_SARL. The bank will perform source-of-funds verification and, where the subscribed capital is held in a blocking account until the statutory capital certificate is issued, require the notarized deed of amendment for the SARL and an accountant's confirmation. If the injection is in foreign currency, the bank will quote an on-shore client-sell FX conversion to EUR; the derivative or conversion contract must state the underlying equity inflow, and the resulting EUR balance is made available after good-funds confirmation and BCL BoP reporting where applicable.Outbound disbursements are settled through the SWIFT MT103 network for global wires, through SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments inside the EEA, and, where documentary security is requested, through a commercial irrevocable letter of credit issued by the Luxembourg bank in favour of the creditor. The compliance rule is that every wire must include a precise remittance reference, invoice number, counterparty name and business-purpose code so that the bank can complete sanctions screening, monitoring, and, where requested, final settlement in TARGET2 via Banque Centrale du Luxembourg. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% headline CIT on taxable income exceeding EUR 175,000 and 15% on the first EUR 175,000 band (LIR Art. 174), plus a 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT and municipal business tax of 6.75% in Luxembourg City, producing an aggregate effective headline rate of approximately 24.94% for profits above EUR 175,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic statutory dividend withholding tax of 15% (LIR Art. 97), fully exempt at source under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the non-resident corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of the SARL for at least 12 months; treaty reductions range from 0% to 15% depending on the residence country and holding structure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due by 31 May of the year following the fiscal year-end; automatically extended to 31 December of that same following year when the return is prepared and filed through a Luxembourg registered tax adviser or certified chartered accountant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Luxembourg entity belongs to a group whose consolidated turnover reaches EUR 50M and whose cross-border related-party transactions, including financing, IP, or service flows, exceed EUR 100,000 in the fiscal year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750M; the Local File must be filed with the annual CIT return or upon specific request by the ACD.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD-aligned transfer pricing circular (LIR Circular 56/1) and the domestic GAAR in Art. 6 AKG together with ATAD anti-abuse rules. For a government-affairs and lobbying SARL, economic substance is decisive: the company must have a real office in Luxembourg, locally resident directors or senior consultants, qualified operational staff, active management decisions taken in Luxembourg, and a documented functional and risk profile. A letterbox company that cannot demonstrate actual lobbying activity, local execution capability, payroll, and operational premises is exposed to a recharacterisation of its structure, denial of treaty benefits and participation exemption, and refusal of Luxembourg tax residence certificates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Fraud and Repeated Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax fraud under AO Arts. 111 et seq. attracts fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment; the normal five-year assessment period extends to ten years for intentional omission; official default assessments carry a 10% surcharge and monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%; the municipal business licence may be withdrawn under the Law of 2 September 2011.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers of the SARL are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, withholding tax and penalties under AO Art. 110; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to five years, seizure of personal assets, travel restrictions and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
Shell Company and Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Application of the domestic GAAR (Art. 6 AKG), ATAD principal-purpose test, and treaty LOB/PPT clauses: transactions are recharacterised, the participation exemption and dividend WHT relief are denied, profits are recomputed at arm's length, a 10% tax surcharge is added, and tax residence certificates are refused or revoked.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where there is confusion of patrimonies or fraudulent misuse of the corporate form; gérants are personally liable for distributions made without real economic substance, can be ordered to repay corporate assets, face management bans (interdiction de gérer), and may be subject to personal insolvency proceedings.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Register (RBE) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 (AML/CFT) and the Law of 13 January 2019 (RBE), fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to five years may be imposed; administrative sanctions include dissolution of the company, freezing of suspicious transactions, and removal from the register of regulated professionals.

Director personal liability: Gérants, compliance officers and persons responsible for RBE filings incur personal criminal liability, personal fines and asset freezes; travel bans and prohibitions on holding managerial positions in financial or advisory entities may be imposed during investigation and after conviction.
Influence Peddling, Corruption and Irregular Commission Payments

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg Criminal Code Arts. 246 and 247 sanction trafic d'influence and corruption with fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; proceeds are confiscated; the autorisation d'établissement is withdrawn and the company is debarred from public procurement across the EU.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for corruption committed in the corporate interest, personally jointly liable for restitution and civil damages, subject to disgorgement of illicit gains, travel bans during criminal proceedings, extradition exposure in cross-border cases, and personal bankruptcy where assets were diverted.

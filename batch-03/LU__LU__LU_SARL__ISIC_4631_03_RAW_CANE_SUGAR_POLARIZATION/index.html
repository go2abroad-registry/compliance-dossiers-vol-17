Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation and coordinated articles of association (statuts coordonnés) registered in the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid-up at incorporation; the capital may be in cash or in-kind. In-kind contributions require an auditor's report. Cash contributions must be deposited in a bank and certified by the bank before notarisation; no indefinite escrow is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety approval from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for any office or warehouse with a required fire certificate; structural load inspection by the Luxembourg administration for buildings used for bulk storage if appropriate; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) if the company employs workers; for a bare administrative SARL with no physical stock or employees, only general workplace registration is required.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid-up at incorporation; the capital may be in cash or in-kind. In-kind contributions require an auditor's report. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg — VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD); customs and EORI: Administration des douanes et accises; unified electronic filing portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) via MyGuichet.lu within one month of incorporation; LBR issues the national business identification number (N° d'identification). 2. File the VAT registration application with the AEDT before the first taxable supply; the AEDT allocates the LU VAT number (pattern LU + 8 digits + check digit), normally within 10–15 business days; registration is effective as of filing and backdated to the commencement of activity. 3. Non-established EU traders may register directly without an intermediary; third-country traders must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative, jointly and severally liable for VAT. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Luxembourg SARL is a resident, fully taxable corporate entity: it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) and VAT on its worldwide business income, with credit for foreign taxes. No transparent/pass-through status is available for an SARL, and the entity is not located in a tax-exempt free zone.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law. Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164 LIR for ≥95%-owned resident subsidiaries, enabling group loss offset; (2) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the VAT Law for closely connected Luxembourg-resident entities; (3) participation exemption under Article 166 LIR for qualifying shareholdings of ≥10% held for ≥12 months, exempting dividends and capital gains; (4) OSS election for intra-EU distance sales and certain B2C services; (5) option to waive the small-business franchise and apply the ordinary VAT regime. No hybrid-mismatch election is available; Luxembourg applies ATAD anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration for every established taxable person with no minimum turnover threshold; a small-business franchise (Article 84(2) LTV) exempts annual turnover up to EUR 25,000 (without input VAT recovery). Non-established EU sellers benefit from the EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-sale threshold via OSS; non-EU sellers have no threshold and must register at first taxable transaction. The wholesale sugar trading SARL must register from day one; domestic B2B sales are subject to the 17% standard rate unless the export or intra-EU zero-rating conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 52 of the VAT Law 1979 zero-rates (exemption with input VAT deduction) the physical export of VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar (HS 1701.14) to destinations outside the EU, provided: (i) the SARL is the seller and exporter; (ii) the goods leave the EU within 3 months (extendable for documented commercial reasons) under the EU customs export procedure; (iii) the export declaration (SAD/EAD) is lodged in the SARL's name and carries a valid MRN; (iv) the ocean Bill of Lading evidences dispatch to a non-EU port, typically a West African refinery discharge port. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered EU traders are equally zero-rated where the buyer's valid VAT ID, dispatch evidence, and recapitulative statement (Intrastat/DEB) are in place. Sugar is not subject to excise duty in Luxembourg; no national export licence is required, and EU sugar export refunds have been abolished since October 2017. The CIF/FOB vessel-delivery model funded by Revolving_LC and Swift Wire fits the zero-rated export pattern.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EAD) with MRN lodged via the EU Export Control System at the Administration des douanes et accises
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (or CMR/air waybill where applicable) evidencing physical exit from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103/MT700, or letter of credit settlement records
  • EORI certificate of the Luxembourg SARL
  • Certificate of origin and polarization/quality certificate (ICUMSA) required by the importing country
  • Sales contract with Incoterms (FOB/CIF), plus charterparty or booking note if any

Domestic B2B structured e-invoicing is mandatory in phases under the Law of 7 March 2023 (amending the 1979 VAT Law): Luxembourg-established taxpayers with annual turnover above EUR 100,000 must be able to receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931) from 1 January 2024, and must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025; all other established taxpayers are phased in from 1 January 2026. Accepted formats include PEPPOL/UBL, Factur-X and XRechnung. Luxembourg operates no real-time fiscal clearance (no CTC validation, no digital stamping, no SAF-T periodic filing); the AEDT applies a post-audit control model. Invoices and export substantiation must be retained for 10 years.

Export zero-rating is a priority AEDT audit area. If the goods are not proven to have left the EU within the 3-month period (or approved extension), the AEDT claws back the 17% VAT and charges default interest (0.8% per month under Article 25(2) of the VAT Law) plus civil penalties. Willful fraud is punishable under Article 68 of the VAT Law by imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or criminal fines, with aggravated sanctions in cases of organized fraud; the AEDT may also deny input VAT deduction on local purchases when export evidence is deficient and may hold the VAT representative jointly and severally liable for non-established sellers. Note: Luxembourg imposes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure herein reflects the standard Revolving_LC settlement cycle and commercial credit terms, not a fiscal expiry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full capital and current account convertibility as a Eurozone member; there are no exchange control laws restricting the conversion, repatriation, or settlement of foreign currency. The euro (EUR) is fully and legally convertible, and there are no surrender, approval, or central bank licensing requirements for cross-border trade payments. Profit remittances, including dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, may be freely distributed to non-residents subject to Luxembourg corporate law solvency tests and potential withholding taxes under domestic law or tax treaties. For a raw cane sugar trading SARL, USD/EUR/BRL/THB/INR settlement flows are unrestricted; however, statistical balance-of-payment reporting obligations to the BCL may apply for direct investment, financial intermediation, and trade receivables/payables. No transaction tax, Tobin tax, or exchange parity fee is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation under Luxembourg law to surrender, repatriate, or convert export receipts within a specified timeline. Exporters may retain foreign currency in their designated multicurrency accounts indefinitely. Mandatory conversion ratios do not apply. Nevertheless, under CSSF AML/CFT guidance, banking institutions may apply internal policies requiring a demonstrable economic purpose for retention of foreign currency, and annual financial statements under Luxembourg GAAP must reflect receivables in EUR using the closing ECB reference rate or the rate at transaction date. Export proceeds must be booked to the SARL's operating account and are subject to standard customs/single window reporting (SGS/ABI) for physical goods, but not to any foreign currency surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign exchange administrative and statistical reporting is coordinated with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for credit institution supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low to Medium. Fully regulated Luxembourg or EU/EEA-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may provide payment accounts, debit cards, and payment transactions, but they cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide revolving LC facilities, or give trade credit; such instruments require a full credit institution license under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg Payment Services/E-Money license or without a valid passport under PSD2/EMD2 are not permitted to operate in Luxembourg and will not have access to SWIFT MT103 clearing through the Luxembourg clearing infrastructure. For physical sugar commodity settlement requiring documentary collections and LCs, a Tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must route via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's euro-denominated capital account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The transfer narrative must clearly state the corporate purpose, e.g., 'Share capital subscription - SARL' or 'Apport en capital', and the bank's KYC team verifies the origin of funds against the capital subscription agreement and notarized minutes. For a SARL with minimum capital of EUR 12,000, the full amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account before commercial registration; upon receipt, the bank issues a certificate of deposit (blocage de fonds) which is filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). For contributions in kind (e.g., physical sugar inventory), a statutory auditor's valuation certificate is mandatory. There is no central bank approval for injecting capital from non-EU jurisdictions; however, the bank must apply CSSF AML rules on source of wealth and may require a legal opinion on the underlying shareholding chain.Cross-border trade settlements for physical raw cane sugar are executed either by SWIFT MT103 direct credit transfers for spot/open-account transactions or by commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) under UCP 600 and ISBP 745 for documentary shipments. A Revolving LC structure is optimal for continuous sugar supply and enables real-time negotiation of bills of exchange, presentation of shipping documents, and drawer's financing. Upon presentation of compliant documents, the confirming Luxembourg bank settles the USD/EUR amount via SWIFT to the seller's designated bank. For restricted-currency destinations (e.g., Brazil, India, Thailand), settlement may be cleared through a non-deliverable forward on the EUR/USD leg or via local currency nostro accounts; no exchange control permission is required from the Luxembourg authorities. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% gross statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on SARL dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders; 0% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive/participation exemption if holding conditions are met, otherwise DTA-reduced rates range from 5% to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a SARL with a calendar fiscal year, the return is due by 31 March of the following year; a registered tax adviser may obtain an administrative extension to 30 September. Non-calendar year-ends are due within the statutory period indicated on the tax assessment notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required where worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 350,000,000; Local File required where the Luxembourg SARL has related-party transactions aggregated above EUR 100,000 per taxable period; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group turnover.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an ESR-jurisdiction in the Gulf-style sense; however, Article 6 ATAD and the EU abuse-of-law case law require genuine economic substance. A raw-cane-sugar trading SARL with no local office, no local management and no risk-bearing capacity will be denied treaty benefits, Directive relief and participation exemptions and may be recast as a letterbox company by the ACD.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full underpaid CIT and municipal business tax, monthly late-payment interest at 0.6%, administrative fines up to 25% of evaded tax, and criminal fiscal prosecution before Luxembourg courts.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are civilly jointly and severally liable to the tax authority; the court may order seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years for wilful fraud.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's length re-pricing, secondary adjustment as deemed dividend, disallowance of the excessive expense, late-payment interest, administrative penalties up to 25% for documentation failure, and anti-avoidance surcharges.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who knowingly approve artificial sugar-trade pricing can be held personally liable in insolvency, ordered to restore unpaid tax, barred from leaving Luxembourg, and disqualified from management for up to ten years.
Shell company / letterbox entity and abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, denial of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive 0% dividend rate, denial of the participation exemption, and recharacterization of the SARL as a sham; all foreign-source trading profits become taxed in Luxembourg at 24.94% plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced upon proof of fraud or artificiality; directors are personally liable for restitution of underpaid taxes, subject to asset attachment, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for fraudulent misuse of the SARL form.
AML / UBO concealment and sanctioned-commodity trading

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law authorizes fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% of annual turnover; cargo and bank accounts may be frozen; the SARL may be dissolved; proceeds of the infringement are confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failure to identify the ultimate beneficial owners; personal fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, and a ten-year ban from corporate management can be imposed.

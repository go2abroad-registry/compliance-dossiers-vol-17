Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) — Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); electronic filing window: guichet.lu (notary-assisted filing accepted pursuant to the Loi du 11 janvier 2008).

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales — Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (SARL), Articles 710-26 et seq. (governance) and Articles 1, 9 and 12 (public order); Loi du 19 décembre 2002 relative au registre du commerce et des sociétés (Arts. 10-11); Loi du 13 janvier 2019 portant création du Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs; relevant RCS/RESA regulations under the Loi du 11 janvier 2008 on electronic filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Deed of Incorporation) — an authentic notarial deed containing the articles of association and the first corporate acts; filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory capital EUR 12,000 for an SARL; at least 20% of each share (EUR 2,400) must be paid up at subscription, with full payment required within five years. The contribution must be held on a blocked corporate account in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank's capital-blocking certificate must be remitted to the notary as a condition of signing the deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for the workshop, structural load and building-code approval from the Commune (autorisation de bâtir), and occupational safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including local exhaust ventilation for epoxy resin vapours, personal protective equipment protocols and lightning/electrical safety once material electrical loads are installed.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory capital EUR 12,000 for an SARL; at least 20% of each share (EUR 2,400) must be paid up at subscription, with full payment required within five years. The contribution must be held on a blocked corporate account in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank's capital-blocking certificate must be remitted to the notary as a condition of signing the deed.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Direct Tax Administration (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - Indirect Tax and Registration Administration (VAT). Digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (national e-government portal), guichet.lu (business formalities), AEDT eTVA and eCDF filing platforms.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and obtain immediate registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), which automatically issues the 16-digit national number (numero de matricule) used as the corporate tax identification number for CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; (2) file the statutory VAT registration package with the AEDT, either via MyGuichet.lu or the eTVA portal, submitting the certified articles of association, an extract from the foreign trade register (legalized/apostilled and translated where required), a power of attorney, and the appointment of a local VAT representative where the parent is established outside the EU/EEA; (3) receive the VAT identification number (numero d'identification a la TVA) in the format LU + 8 digits, issued within weeks of filing; (4) register for municipal business tax with the commune of the registered office; and (5) apply for an EORI number for customs and export transactions where goods (e.g., polyhedral dice) will be physically exported outside the EU. Non-EU entities may be required to appoint a VAT fiscal representative; EU-established entities may use the EU OSS scheme instead of a national VAT registration for distance sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL is a resident, non-transparent company taxed at entity level: corporate income tax 15% plus 5% unemployment solidarity surcharge (effective 15.75%), municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg-City, up to 10.5% in other communes), and net wealth tax (0.5% of taxable net wealth above the EUR 500,000 exemption). Worldwide income is taxable in Luxembourg with credit or exemption relief under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; no territorial or pass-through election is available to a SARL. — A foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and obtain immediate registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), which automatically issues the 16-digit national number (numero de matricule) used as the corporate tax identification number for CIT, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; (2) file the statutory VAT registration package with the AEDT, either via MyGuichet.lu or the eTVA portal, submitting the certified articles of association, an extract from the foreign trade register (legalized/apostilled and translated where required), a power of attorney, and the appointment of a local VAT representative where the parent is established outside the EU/EEA; (3) receive the VAT identification number (numero d'identification a la TVA) in the format LU + 8 digits, issued within weeks of filing; (4) register for municipal business tax with the commune of the registered office; and (5) apply for an EORI number for customs and export transactions where goods (e.g., polyhedral dice) will be physically exported outside the EU. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL is a resident, non-transparent company taxed at entity level: corporate income tax 15% plus 5% unemployment solidarity surcharge (effective 15.75%), municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg-City, up to 10.5% in other communes), and net wealth tax (0.5% of taxable net wealth above the EUR 500,000 exemption). Worldwide income is taxable in Luxembourg with credit or exemption relief under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; no territorial or pass-through election is available to a SARL.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Luxembourg; the available statutory elections and regimes are: (i) fiscal unity (tax consolidation) election - a resident SARL held at least 95% by a Luxembourg resident parent (or EU/EEA resident with a Luxembourg permanent establishment) may elect with its parent for integrated taxation for CIT and municipal business tax purposes; (ii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) - allows a 'one-taxpayer' grouping of closely linked Luxembourg entities for VAT neutrality; (iii) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) elections - allow non-established e-commerce suppliers (Kickstarter, Stripe, PayPal channel) to declare distance sales and low-value imports without separate national VAT registrations; (iv) the participation exemption regime - applies automatically upon satisfaction of statutory conditions (holding of at least 10% or an acquisition price of EUR 1.2 million, plus subject-to-tax and standard-tax requirements) and can be disapplied only by election; (v) ATAD and hybrid-mismatch elections to apply substitute rules and the CFC regime; (vi) the small-business VAT exemption election (Article 57 of the VAT Law) for annual turnover not exceeding EUR 30,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons: a SARL must register from its first taxable transaction. Small-business exemption: annual turnover not exceeding EUR 30,000 (excluding VAT) under Article 57 of the VAT Law. Non-established suppliers have no threshold - registration, reverse charge, or an OSS solution is due on the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Intra-Community distance sales of goods to Luxembourg final consumers trigger Luxembourg VAT registration if the EUR 10,000 annual threshold for EU-wide distance sales is exceeded, unless the OSS scheme is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 58 of the Luxembourg VAT Law applies when: (a) goods (e.g., epoxy-resin polyhedral dice) are physically exported from Luxembourg outside the EU customs territory, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration and transport documentation, with the exporter holding an EORI number; (b) goods are dispatched to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State (intra-Community supply) provided the customer's valid VAT identification number is quoted in the invoice, the EC Sales List (ECSL) is filed, and the goods physically leave Luxembourg; (c) ancillary services directly linked to the export or import of goods (freight, handling, customs clearance) are supplied to non-established taxable persons; and (d) distance sales to non-EU private individuals qualify as export supplies if shipped directly outside the EU without clearance for free circulation within the EU. The zero-rated supply must be identified on the invoice with the 'EU 0%' / 'E 0%' code, the export customs declaration number, and the destination country.

  • Luxembourg/EU Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) generated via the CELDEC electronic customs clearance system, showing the exporter as declarant and the goods leaving the EU
  • Transport evidence - Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR waybill (road), or postal/express carrier receipt evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and export terms, referencing the customs declaration and the zero-rating basis
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing the incoming foreign-currency settlement of the export invoice (required for the export audit trail and for substantiating payments from Kickstarter backers, Stripe, and PayPal proceeds)

Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing for supplies to Luxembourg public-sector buyers via the national e-invoicing platform (based on PEPPOL and European standard EN 16931), transposing Directive 2014/55/EU. No general B2B e-invoicing mandate and no real-time fiscal clearance mechanism, digital stamping, or SAF-T pre-clearance regime is currently in force in Luxembourg. VAT invoices may be issued electronically (EDI or advanced/qualified electronic signature) provided the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are guaranteed; invoices and supporting export documents must be retained for 10 years.

Luxembourg VAT authorities (AEDT) may scrutinise export substantiation within the general 5-year assessment period (extended to 10 years in cases of fraud). If zero-rating is unsupported, the AEDT retrospectively re-assesses VAT at the 17% standard rate (or applicable reduced rate) on the export consideration, applies default interest of 0.8% per commenced month, and imposes administrative fines up to 10% of the underpaid VAT for material errors and up to 100% for deliberate misstatement. Serious VAT evasion involving falsified customs or export paperwork attracts criminal penalties (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000), joint and several liability of managers and directors, public disclosure, and denial or clawback of previously credited input VAT and refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no counterparty limits, and no central-bank approval for trade, capital injection, dividend, or royalty payments. Profit remittance is unrestricted for a Luxembourg SARL, subject only to the corporate-law solvency test (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 710-11) and dividend withholding tax (15% statutory; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% of capital with the required holding period, or under applicable double-tax treaties). Inbound and outbound EUR transfers settle in real time via TARGET2; the SARL's statutory minimum capital is EUR 12,000 (fully subscribed, at least 25% paid up at constitution, i.e., EUR 3,000), with the paid-in portion blocked in a Luxembourg bank account until RCS registration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg has no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. Exporters may hold USD, EUR, or any other currency freely. The BCL only requires threshold-based statistical reporting for balance-of-payments compilation (direct investment positions and external transactions), which the SARL will satisfy through its quarterly/annual BCL forms; no M/T (mandatory conversion) obligations exist within the Eurozone.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); bank prudential supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). BCL operates TARGET2/TIPS payment settlement, collects balance-of-payments statistics, and implements ECB monetary policy. No standalone Foreign Exchange Administration exists; exchange transactions are subject only to EU capital-movement freedoms (TFEU Articles 63-66) and CSSF AML supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

HIGH as a supplementary payment channel; LOW as a replacement for the primary banking relationship. CSSF-authorised or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut, Monese) can provide multi-currency IBANs, automated Stripe/PayPal payouts, and retail FX conversion without a physical Luxembourg branch. However, an EMI cannot hold the statutory blocked share-capital deposit, issue the notary's attestation de blocage, open a documentary letter of credit, or execute an ISDA-derivative hedging line. Recommended structure: use a licensed Luxembourg bank (BGL BNP Paribas or BIL) for capital escrow, outbound trade settlement, and FX hedging; use an EMI or the Stripe/PayPal multi-currency wallet as a collection float for Kickstarter proceeds, sweeping balances daily to the main EUR operational account to minimise counterparty and fragmentation risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founding shareholder wires the SARL's initial capital (EUR 12,000 minimum for a standard SARL; at least EUR 3,000 paid up) via SWIFT MT103 from the home bank, citing the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity-linked instruments) or the BCL balance-of-payments statistical code for direct-investment equity capital. The receiving Luxembourg bank requires: (i) the draft notarial deed or executed articles of incorporation; (ii) the shareholder's subscription resolution; (iii) source-of-funds evidence; and (iv) the BCL statistical registration reference; the bank then issues the attestation de blocage (capital blocking certificate) which the notary files with the Luxembourg RCS. For post-incorporation capital increases, repeat the same SWIFT MT103 purpose code and file the notarial amendment at the RCS. Kickstarter/Stripe and PayPal campaign proceeds are routed to the SARL's EUR operational account via SEPA/wire from Stripe Ireland or PayPal Luxembourg/UK; USD payouts are either held in a USD sub-account or converted to EUR at the processor's or the bank's standing FX rate. Verification of each receipt is evidenced by MT103 / pain.001 transaction details and Stripe's weekly settlement reports.Primary rail for EU/EEA suppliers (epoxy resin, silicone molds, pigments, packaging) is SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) or SEPA Instant, with ISO purpose code 'TRAD' or 'SUPP', settling in T+1 (or seconds for instant). For non-EU suppliers in China and the US (sharp-edge dies, custom silicone molds, bulk resin batches), use SWIFT MT103 with GPI tracking, including invoice numbers, INCOTERM references (EXW/FOB/DAP), and the trade-contract number. Documentary transactions for large campaign preorders are handled through an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 by the Luxembourg bank. PayPal and Stripe payouts are consolidated via processor payout files into the SARL's EUR IBAN. All disbursements above EUR 10,000 should be pre-uploaded as a transaction expectation list so the bank's AML engine has the full audit trail; underlying commercial contracts and freight documents must be retained and matched to each wire to satisfy CSSF 12-02 review.EUR/USD outright forward contracts executed directly with the Luxembourg bank under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement (non-financial counterparty exemption below EMIR clearing thresholds).Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD) covering the mismatch between USD-denominated Kickstarter receivables and EUR-denominated operating expenses.FX vanilla options (caps, collars, put/call spreads) for booking flexibility with paid premiums, requiring a MiFID II-licensed counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) for hedging non-convertible settlement currencies such as CNY/CNH or KRW, permitted solely for economic hedging of Asian supplier payables.Processor-native FX conversion via Stripe/PayPal for small contestable amounts, limited to spot conversion with transparent margin disclosure; all derivative hedging must be executed through the licensed credit institution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: HIGH as a supplementary payment channel; LOW as a replacement for the primary banking relationship. CSSF-authorised or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut, Monese) can provide multi-currency IBANs, automated Stripe/PayPal payouts, and retail FX conversion without a physical Luxembourg branch. However, an EMI cannot hold the statutory blocked share-capital deposit, issue the notary's attestation de blocage, open a documentary letter of credit, or execute an ISDA-derivative hedging line. Recommended structure: use a licensed Luxembourg bank (BGL BNP Paribas or BIL) for capital escrow, outbound trade settlement, and FX hedging; use an EMI or the Stripe/PayPal multi-currency wallet as a collection float for Kickstarter proceeds, sweeping balances daily to the main EUR operational account to minimise counterparty and fragmentation risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 700,000,000 (consolidated group revenue at the EU parent level triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation); below this threshold, Luxembourg-headquartered SARLs must nonetheless maintain contemporaneous arm's length documentation and respond to ACD transfer pricing requests within statutory deadlines.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD anti-abuse doctrine, the ATAD GAAR, and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse clause: an SARL must demonstrate real economic substance (registered office in Luxembourg, physical premises, full-time qualified personnel, local management meetings, substantive assets and risk-taking) to obtain treaty benefits, the participation exemption and the IP Box. Under the proposed EU ATAD 3 'shell entity' directive, Luxembourg-resident companies without minimal substance would be deemed to lack tax residence and would lose access to double tax treaties and EU directive protections; the ACD already applies a substance-over-form analysis in abuse cases.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Recovery of evaded CIT plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; administrative penalty surcharge of 5%-10% of the under-declared tax; criminal fine of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of one month to five years under Article 426 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law and the Criminal Code; mandatory publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: The gerant (managing director) is solidarily liable for the evaded tax, late-payment interest and criminal fine; conviction triggers an automatic travel ban, disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment up to five years.
Late or Non-Filing / Default Assessment

Statutory sanction: Ex officio assessment by the ACD based on estimated income; administrative fine of EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 per unfiled return; late-payment interest of 0.6% per month; loss of the ability to claim loss carryforwards in the default assessment for the first year; escalation to tax recovery proceedings and judicial enforcement.

Director personal liability: The gerant bears personal civil liability for the resulting tax deficiency under the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies and faces criminal liability for willful non-filing, including a travel ban and imprisonment up to five years; shareholders may bring a civil damages action against the gerant for mismanagement.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, withdrawal of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, recharacterization of the arrangement under the GAAR, taxation of deemed distributions at the 24.94% aggregate rate plus late interest and a 5%-10% penalty surcharge; under EU ATAD 3 once finalized, shell entities would be denied tax residence and lose access to double tax treaties; the ACD may also impose minimum net wealth tax at the highest bracket (EUR 4,815).

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal liability for the tax adjustment; criminal prosecution for breach of fiduciary duty; travel bans; seizure of personal bank accounts; disqualification from acting as director in Luxembourg for up to 10 years.
AML / CFT and Payment-Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under the amended AML Law of 12 November 2004, administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for entities; freezing of corporate and merchant accounts by bank and judicial order; supplementary CSSF disciplinary measures; criminal sanctions for money laundering complicity of 5 to 10 years imprisonment and fines up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and gerants are personally liable for the AML penalties; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed during the investigation; criminal conviction carries imprisonment and permanent disqualification from commercial activities in Luxembourg.

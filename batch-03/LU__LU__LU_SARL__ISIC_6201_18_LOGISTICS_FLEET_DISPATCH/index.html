Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) in coordination with the District Court of Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Book III - Titles I and IV, notably Articles 710-1 to 710-24 (SARL provisions); Civil Code Articles 1832 et seq.; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers and the RESA electronic gazette.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif notarié) incorporating the Articles of Incorporation (Statuts), filed electronically on Form A (Demande d'inscription au RCS).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. At incorporation, the full capital must be subscribed and at least 5% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up in cash before signature of the notarial deed. The paid-up funds must be deposited into a blocked 'company in formation' (SARL en formation) bank account with a Luxembourg credit institution or held in notary escrow; the bank or notary issues a capital blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) which is released only upon registration of the company with the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the registered office premises from the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS), including evacuation plans, smoke detectors, and extinguisher compliance; structural load and building occupancy inspection by the Commune of Luxembourg's building authority for the leased office floor; workplace health and safety clearance pursuant to the Law of 17 June 1994 on labour safety and health, validated by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), covering workstation ergonomics, electrical safety, and a documented risk assessment for telematics and dispatch operations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. At incorporation, the full capital must be subscribed and at least 5% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up in cash before signature of the notarial deed. The paid-up funds must be deposited into a blocked 'company in formation' (SARL en formation) bank account with a Luxembourg credit institution or held in notary escrow; the bank or notary issues a capital blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) which is released only upon registration of the company with the RCS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Official portals: MyGuichet.lu and eCDF (electronic correspondence and filing platform).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) Incorporate before a Luxembourg notary and file with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain the national business registration number; (2) apply to the AEDT for the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) either via MyGuichet.lu or with a paper Form 640/640ADR; (3) the ACD allocates the direct tax file number (matricule) upon registration of the corporate income tax liability; (4) an entity established outside the EU must appoint a jointly and severally liable Luxembourg VAT representative (représentant responsable solidaire); (5) if the company makes EU distance sales or B2C electronic services, register for the OSS/IOSS scheme via MyGuichet.lu. Administrative filings are accepted in French, German, and, for specific portals, English.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) Incorporate before a Luxembourg notary and file with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) to obtain the national business registration number; (2) apply to the AEDT for the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) either via MyGuichet.lu or with a paper Form 640/640ADR; (3) the ACD allocates the direct tax file number (matricule) upon registration of the corporate income tax liability; (4) an entity established outside the EU must appoint a jointly and severally liable Luxembourg VAT representative (représentant responsable solidaire); (5) if the company makes EU distance sales or B2C electronic services, register for the OSS/IOSS scheme via MyGuichet.lu. Administrative filings are accepted in French, German, and, for specific portals, English.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box. Available elections: (a) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 VAT Law; (b) fiscal unity affiliation for corporate income tax under Article 164bis LIR requiring a 95% direct or indirect holding; (c) election to waive the small-enterprise VAT exemption under Article 57 VAT Law (turnover threshold EUR 35,000) and instead be regularly taxed; (d) OSS / non-EU scheme election for qualifying e-services and distance sales; (e) entity classification of hybrid entities follows LIR Article 159 and the MLI / treaty provisions, with elections in limited cases for transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established small enterprises (Article 57 VAT Law); zero threshold for non-established foreign entities, which are mandatorily registrable from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export supplies to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT (Article 43 VAT Law); intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-identified customer qualify for 0% VAT (Article 42 VAT Law) conditional on VIES validation and recapitulative statement. Services: freight and logistics services directly connected to the export or import of goods, including fleet dispatch and handling operations, qualify for 0% (Article 44 VAT Law). For the SaaS/telematics scope: B2B supplies of fleet route optimization and telematics services to non-established customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the general place-of-supply rule (customer location, Article 44 EU VAT Directive); no Luxembourg VAT is chargeable and the customer applies reverse charge. B2C e-services to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's Member State via OSS; B2C e-services to non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT scope.

No real-time fiscal clearance, CTC reporting, or digital stamping is in force for B2B invoices. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies to central government and public-sector buyers using the PEPPOL network (Peppol BIS 3.0). B2B and B2C invoices must comply with Articles 75-78 VAT Law and be retained for 10 years. VIES recapitulative statements must be filed monthly where intra-EU supplies exceed EUR 50,000 per quarter, otherwise quarterly. Intrastat dispatch declarations apply where annual intra-EU dispatches exceed the EUR 460,000 threshold. OSS/IOSS returns are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu. Cross-border B2B invoices must state the customer's valid VAT ID and the reverse-charge legal basis.

AEDT and ACD may conduct retrospective audits up to 7 years for VAT/corporate tax (extended to 10 years in case of fraud or wilful tax evasion). Where export zero-rating is applied but the statutory documentation bundle is defective, the VAT exemption is clawed back retroactively with late-payment interest at 0.6% per month and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 12,500 (Article 91 VAT Law). Aggravated VAT fraud is punishable by fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years (Articles 92-93 VAT Law); the fraud or abuse of law doctrine (fraus legis) allows recharacterization of artificial arrangements. The invoice issuer is jointly and severally liable for VAT shown on an invoice (Article 67 VAT Law). Under transfer-pricing principles, intercompany receivables unresolved beyond approximately 180 days may be recharacterized as loans or deemed distributions of profit, with additional income assessed at CIT/MBT rates and late-payment penalties under the General Tax Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a euro-area Member State, has full current and capital account convertibility. There are no legal restrictions on currency conversion between EUR and foreign currencies, no central bank prior-approval requirements for dividend payments, and no capital controls on profit repatriation. The freedom of capital movements is guaranteed under TFEU Article 63. The only practical frictions are AML/KYC onboarding due diligence, EU/UN sanctions screening, and the statutory withholding tax treatment for certain passive income (e.g., 15% withholding on interest and royalty payments under national law). These frictions are not exchange-control hurdles and are contractually mitigable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory rules in Luxembourg requiring surrender of export proceeds or mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts into EUR. Receipts from international sales may be held in the currencies of contract settlement or voluntarily converted through the banking market. Invoicing in EUR or USD is permitted without central bank reporting; however, banks must record balance-of-payments statistics under BCL/Eurosystem monetary and payment reporting requirements where the transaction exceeds the BCL's reporting threshold (typically EUR 250,000 for a single non-cash operation).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign exchange administrative supervision is shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for regulatory policy matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High only for non-escrowed, day-to-day treasury flows. A Luxembourg-authorized or EU-passported e-money institution (EMI) may be used for receivables collection and supplier payments because EMIs are regulated under the Luxembourg law implementing Directive 2009/110/EC. Legal constraints: the EMI cannot hold a company's statutory initial capital as a notary-recognized escrow (only a credit institution can); EMIs may not grant credit or provide full corporate banking guarantees; cross-border onboarding of an EU EMI often triggers equivalent AML/KYC checks; and reliance on a single EMI creates correspondent banking concentration risk for larger FX transactions. The structure should therefore pair an EMI rail with a tier-1 licensed bank for capital escrow and substantial FX conversions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The qualifying inbound routing for a share capital injection is a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer initiated from the shareholder's own account. The transmitter must use the statistical purpose code 'B' (capital contribution) or, where required, refer to 'Increase in share capital' and the contract reference. The bank will require a notarized board/EGM decision, the share subscription deed, identification by name of each contributing shareholder, and an attested deposit letter for the notarial file. Funds must be cleared by the respondent bank of the Luxembourg credit institution, and the bank will complete BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting as required under the Eurosystem framework. Any financing from related parties should be documented as shareholder loan or equity; the latter is notified as direct foreign investment and is freely resettable under EU capital-movement rules.For routine EU supplier payments, SEPA Credit Transfer is the lowest-cost rail. For international settlement of technology, telematics hardware procurement and logistics services, SWIFT MT103 with proper purpose code 'Trade in services' is the standard; high-value or documentary imports of hardware should be settled through a commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) with a Luxembourg bank confirming the LC, accompanied by transport documents, commercial invoices and authenticity certificates. For recurring SaaS subscription collection and marketpay flows, Stripe Billing may be integrated via a payment institution or through the bank's merchant acquiring gateway; nevertheless, direct MT103 remains the substantive treasury clearing instrument for larger counterparties. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (17.00% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City; rate varies with the commune of the registered office)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (Luxembourg withholding tax on distributed profits of a SARL, irrespective of the recipient being resident or non-resident); the rate is reduced to 0% for a qualifying corporate shareholder under the Luxembourg participation exemption if the parent holds 10% for at least 12 months; liquidation distributions outside subscribed capital are not subject to WHT

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is the last day of the sixth month after the fiscal year-end (for a calendar-year SARL: 30 June); extensions can be requested via eCDF, and for electronic filers without professional help the ACD frequently tolerates filing until 31 December; quarterly CIT prepayments are due on 10 February, 10 May, 10 August and 10 November

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold applies to related-party transactions below EUR 1 million; tax administration expects a Local File when annual related-party operating transactions exceed EUR 1 million or when related-party financing exceeds EUR 500,000. Groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more must file a Master File, Local File and CbCR notification (due at the end of the reporting month after the fiscal-year end)

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and the Luxembourg 'wholly artificial arrangement' doctrine: corporate residency, management and control, and real operational substance must be present in Luxembourg. A SARL operating in the logistics-tech sector must demonstrate, through employment records and invoices, that dispatch/optimization algorithms are really managed locally; otherwise, the ACD can disregard the entity for treaty purposes. There is no separate ESR annual filing similar to the UAE regime, but substance is policed via the RBE beneficial-owner register, CbCR, and transfer pricing examinations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the LIR at the full CIT rate of 24.94% plus monetary tax interest at 0.6% per month; criminal conviction carries a fine of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of one to five years (Article 396 LIR); tax fraud also triggers the loss of all deductions and protection against arbitrary audits.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve the fraudulent scheme are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax shortfall under Luxembourg civil law; in practice the ACD issues travel bans, freezing orders on personal bank accounts, and may seize director assets, and imprisonment is imposed on the natural person rather than the company.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and directive benefits; all IP licence income is reassessed at 24.94% CIT with a 50% non-disclosure surcharge under Section 190 (5) of the Luxembourg Tax Abatement Law; the Luxembourg RCS can open liquidation proceedings; a Shell-company finding makes the entity unbankable for payment rails such as Stripe Billing and wire transfers.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors who allow the company to operate without genuine substance are jointly liable for the additional assessments and fines; repeated violations constitute the criminal offence of mismanagement under the Commercial Companies Law (Article 1800-1), carrying personal liability, asset confiscation and a ban from running companies for up to five years.
AML/UBO Filing Deficiency

Statutory sanction: RBE administrative fines of EUR 100 to EUR 500 per day of non-compliance, and criminal fines up to EUR 1.25 million; the law 12 November 2004 (as amended) gives the prosecution power to impose imprisonment up to five years for aggravated AML; payments through Stripe Billing may be frozen by PayPal/Stripe due to the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for AML deficiencies; under Luxembourg law 24 July 2015, a director who fails to alert the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) is subject to criminal sanction; travel bans and precautionary seizure of property are issued by the investigating judge at the request of the Luxembourg Public Prosecutor.

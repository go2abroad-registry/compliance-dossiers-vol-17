Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the supervision of the Ministry of Justice; electronic filing portal accessible via www.lbr.lu and Guichet.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as consolidated and modernized by the Laws of 10 August 2016 and 19 August 2016; the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) is governed by Articles 180-1 to 180-28 (Title concerning private limited companies); subsidiary regulatory basis: Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 September 2011 implementing the Law of 2 September 2011 on access to the professions of craftsman, merchant, industrialist and certain liberal professions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) including the articles of association (statuts), executed before a Luxembourg civil-law notary, followed by RCS registration form and mandatory publication in the Electronic Collection of Companies and Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 46 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum issued and paid-up share capital EUR 12,000; 100% of cash subscriptions must be fully paid before signature of the notarial deed, deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte de garantie / compte bloqué) opened at a Luxembourg credit institution in the name of the company in formation, and released only upon entry of the company in the RCS; contributions in kind exceeding EUR 12,000 require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé); 100% foreign ownership is permitted and no local incorporation of shareholders is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate of the municipal fire commission supported by an inspection of the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS): evacuation routes, fire doors, extinguishers, hydrants, smoke detection and electrical installation certificate; structural stability certificate for racking and mezzanine installations issued by an approved engineer in accordance with the Eurocodes and seismic category; occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering CE-marked handling equipment, low-voltage compliance, emergency lighting, first-aid and defibrillator provision, and a preliminary safety-and-health coordination dossier for any fit-out works.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum issued and paid-up share capital EUR 12,000; 100% of cash subscriptions must be fully paid before signature of the notarial deed, deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte de garantie / compte bloqué) opened at a Luxembourg credit institution in the name of the company in formation, and released only upon entry of the company in the RCS; contributions in kind exceeding EUR 12,000 require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé); 100% foreign ownership is permitted and no local incorporation of shareholders is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax, portal: https://impotsdirects.public.lu. VAT and indirect tax: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED), portal: https://aed.public.lu. Unified e-administration gateway for company and tax filings: https://guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity: (i) file Form 640 (demande d'immatriculation a la TVA) with the AED before the first taxable supply; the AED issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LV + 8 digits) and, where goods move across EU borders, an EORI number. (ii) If the foreign entity establishes a fixed establishment or branch in Luxembourg, it must also register with the Registre de Commerce et des Societes (RCS) via an approved notary; the Guichet unique automatically transmits registration data to the ACD, which allocates a corporate tax file number (matricule). (iii) A resident Luxembourg SARL obtains its corporate tax file number automatically upon RCS registration; VAT registration follows the same Form 640 procedure and must be activated before the first taxable transaction. (iv) Direct-tax registration of a non-resident entity is not required absent a permanent establishment; Luxembourg-source income is then covered by specific reporting or withholding mechanisms. Standard VAT application processing time: 5 to 15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide check-the-box elections; ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply (Article 168ter et seq. of the Income Tax Law). Available statutory elections: (i) CIT fiscal unity (integration fiscale) for resident companies directly or indirectly owned at least 95%, effective for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; (ii) VAT group election under Article 60 of the VAT law for closely linked resident entities (financial, economic and organizational links); (iii) IP box regime electing the 80% exemption of net qualifying IP income; (iv) participation exemption election, declared in the annual CIT return, for dividends and capital gains on qualifying shareholdings (at least 10% or €1.2 million acquisition cost, 12-month holding period, subject-to-tax requirement); (v) securitization company (SIC) and alternative investment fund special regimes requiring prior AED approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident and established suppliers: €35,000 annual turnover small-enterprise exemption. Non-established foreign suppliers: no turnover threshold, mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. EU distance sales into Luxembourg: €10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold administered under the VAT OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies qualify for 0% VAT where (i) goods are physically exported outside the EU, departing the customs territory of the Union within 90 days of dispatch, evidenced by an EU export declaration; or (ii) under Article 148(f) of Directive 2006/112/EC transposed in Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT law, spare parts, rotables and avionics supplied for the provisioning and maintenance of civil aircraft operated by airlines on international routes are exempt with credit, even where delivery occurs in Luxembourg, provided the airline operates international routes and the parts are destined for such aircraft. The supplier must retain a statement or certificate from the airline confirming international-route operations and the aircraft-specific purpose of the goods.

  • EU single administrative document (export declaration) with movement reference number (MRN) registered in the Luxembourg customs DU/ECS system
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading or CMR evidencing physical dispatch from the EU to a third-country destination
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • Proof of payment: SWIFT wire or inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of consideration
  • For aviation surplus: EASA Form 1 release certificate, aircraft traceability records, and, where required, end-user/end-use declaration

Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing is phased in under the national law implementing Directive 2014/55/EU: from 1 July 2026 for large taxpayers and from 1 January 2027 for all other established taxable persons. Invoices must be issued and received in the PEPPOL-structured format via certified PEPPOL access points. No real-time fiscal clearance, no prior authorization, no API digital stamping exists; the Luxembourg model is post-audit access by the AED. VAT invoices and supporting records must be retained for 10 years.

No statutory FX repatriation deadline exists under Luxembourg law; the 180-day benchmark reflects trade-finance and escrow practice in the aviation spares model. If the AED determines that zero-rating was applied without sufficient documentary proof, it reassesses the supply at 17% VAT plus default interest of 0.8% per commenced month. In case of negligence or bad faith, proportional fines of up to 100% of the evaded VAT may be imposed; fraudulent conduct triggers criminal sanctions (fine up to €25,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years) and joint and several personal liability of the managing director. The AED may reassess within the normal 5-year period running from 1 January following the chargeable event, extended to 10 years in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member state subject to the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and payments. There is no domestic exchange control regime, and the Euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Dividend distributions, shareholder loan repayments, and profit remittances are not subject to exchange control approval or obligatory conversion. The only legal constraints arise from general EU anti-money laundering, sanctions, tax transparency, and documentation requirements enforced via regulated banks and notaries. In practice, cross-border inbound funds and outbound commercial payments require a stated economic purpose, proper transaction references (e.g. purpose codes, invoice/contract references), and compliance with the CSSF AML/CFT circulars (notably CSSF Regulation 12-02 and the Law of 12 November 2004).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the eurosystem impose no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or forced conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in a Luxembourg bank account. Banks apply standard EU transaction reporting thresholds and wire-tagging requirements under the Wire Transfer Regulation (EU) 2015/847, but there is no statutory deadline for collecting or converting trade receivables. For operational treasury control, payment terms should nonetheless align with commercial contracts and typical open-account credit limits.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the supervisory authority for banks and non-bank payment institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with legal limits. Luxembourg recognises e-money institutions (EMIs) under the Law of 10 August 2015 on payment services and e-money, and EU EMIs can operate in Luxembourg under the PSD2 EU passport. EMIs are viable only for ancillary low-value payables, supplier disbursements, and settlement rails where no credit or escrow perfection is required. EMIs are not permitted to take deposits or extend credit, and the safeguarding regime does not offer creditor protection equivalent to a licensed bank; therefore, an EMI is legally and operationally unsuitable as the primary escrow holder for the statutory minimum capital or for aviation transaction escrows. For high-value cross-border aircraft parts settlement, the counterparty bank must be a fully licensed Luxembourg credit institution with direct SWIFT and TARGET2 access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The minimum statutory capital of a Luxembourg SARL (currently EUR 12,000) is to be credited to a bank account opened in the LUX-SARL's name at a Luxembourg credit institution. The inward remittance from the parent or shareholder should be made via SWIFT MT103 using a transparent narrative such as 'SUBSCRIBED SHARE CAPITAL / INCREASE OF SHARE CAPITAL / SHAREHOLDER LOAN – SARL [NAME]'. There is no exchange-control approval, but banks apply standard verification under the Law of 12 November 2004 (AML/CFT) and require the notarised articles of association, the RCS beneficial-owner extract, the shareholder resolution, and a board resolution of the Luxembourg SARL authorising the account opening. To prevent mis-mapping, the payment must include the exact corporate identifier (RCS/LU tax number) and bank reference. For foreign currency injections (USD, GBP, CHF), the amount is credited net of conversion spread; no central-bank licence is required. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline rate in Luxembourg City (17.00% CIT + 1.19% solidarity surcharge at 7% of CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax); aggregate rate varies by commune from 24.94% up to 28.69% at the maximum municipal rate of 10.50%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividend distributions; 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption applies (>=10% shareholding held at least 12 months) or where the Luxembourg participation exemption is met; applicable treaty rates (commonly 5% or 15%) may further reduce the withholding tax for non-EU residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form 510 for a fiscal year ending 31 December must be filed by 31 December of the following calendar year (e.g. FY ended 31/12/2024 is due 31/12/2025); the competent bureau d'imposition may grant an extension upon written request. The tax assessment must be issued within 3 years of the end of the filing year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File: mandatory for a Luxembourg entity forming part of a multinational group with consolidated net turnover above EUR 50,000,000 (Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 December 2016); Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is mandatory at consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more; the ACD may demand ad-hoc TP documentation below these thresholds for specific cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to UAE ESR; substance is enforced indirectly through Art. 159 LIR (effective management and control determines tax residence), the ATAD-1 general anti-abuse rule (Art. 6 AOA), CFC rules (Art. 164bis LIR), and the interest limitation rule. In practice, the ACD requires real presence (office, local staff, physical board meetings, commercial decision-making) before issuing a tax residence certificate or granting treaty/directive benefits. The EU ATAD-3 'Unshell' Directive, once transposed, will impose turnover-based fines and denial of tax residence certificates on shell entities lacking minimum substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under the Luxembourg General Tax Law: fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; reassessment of hidden profits with late payment interest at 0.6% per month and ancillary penalties (taxe proportionnelle) of up to 50%; publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signs or directs a false filing is prosecuted as principal; the corporate veil does not shield deliberate fraud; civil joint liability under Art. 495 of the 1915 Company Law, restitution and confiscation orders, asset seizure, and preventive travel bans may be imposed by the investigating judge.
Shell Company / Empty Structure

Statutory sanction: Refusal of the ACD to issue the tax residence certificate (Form 6); denial of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption and treaty relief; retroactive reassessment as a foreign tax resident; under the incoming ATAD-3 Unshell regime, fines of up to 5% of turnover for empty entities.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal liability for false attestations of residence and misrepresentations to the Luxembourg trade register; criminal exposure for aiding tax fraud; joint liability for uncollected taxes where the entity is treated as failing (comblement du passif); travel bans during money-laundering investigations.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Art. 56 LIR with recharacterization of hidden profit distributions; denial of deductions for undocumented cross-border charges; late payment interest at 0.6% per month; administrative fines; potential CFC attribution or exit tax where the foreign affiliate retains the profit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve mispricing without an economic analysis may be held jointly liable for the company's tax arrears as faute de gestion under Art. 495; liability for unlawful distributions under Luxembourg company law; commercial court liquidation proceedings (action en comblement du passif) where the mispricing rendered the SARL insolvent.
AML / Trade Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) of up to EUR 5,000,000 for regulated professionals; criminal money-laundering penalties under Arts. 506-1 and 506-2 of the Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 10 years for natural persons and fines up to EUR 1,250,000 for legal entities; confiscation of inventory and freezing orders by the CRF.

Director personal liability: Managers who omit UBO registrations, forge compliance documentation, or facilitate OFAC/EU-embargoed deals face criminal prosecution, preventive travel bans, confiscation of personal assets, and disqualification from holding directorships; operating unlicensed payment collection without a PISP/e-money authorization adds separate regulatory exposure.

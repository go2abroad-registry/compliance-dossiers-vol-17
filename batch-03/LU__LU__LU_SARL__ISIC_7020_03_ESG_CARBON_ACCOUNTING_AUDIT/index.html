Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg City; filings effected through the e-Justice/RCS secure electronic filing channel.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, Articles 710-1 to 710-22 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (S.à r.l.); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register (RCS) and the publication of annual accounts; Law of 2 September 2011 on business establishment, where applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Authentic notarial deed of incorporation and articles of association (acte constitutif notarié et statuts), deposited with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum nominal share capital of a Luxembourg S.à r.l. is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. Cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 25% of nominal value unless the articles require full payment; in-kind contributions must be fully paid-in. The required cash deposit must be placed in a blocked escrow bank account in the name of the S.à r.l. 'en formation' before execution of the notarial deed and released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: The leased or owned office premises must satisfy minimum workplace safety requirements under the Luxembourg Labor Code, including first aid, electrical safety, fire escape and access obligations. A fire safety inspection or certificate from the Compagnie Grand-Ducale d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) may be requested by the landlord for commercial premises; structural load clearance is not required for ordinary office occupancy, but the workplace accommodation must be certified as safe for occupation and any client-facing meeting areas must comply with public-access building rules.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum nominal share capital of a Luxembourg S.à r.l. is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. Cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 25% of nominal value unless the articles require full payment; in-kind contributions must be fully paid-in. The required cash deposit must be placed in a blocked escrow bank account in the name of the S.à r.l. 'en formation' before execution of the notarial deed and released only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Inland Revenue (https://impotsdirects.public.lu) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT, https://pfi.public.lu) for VAT; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS, https://www.lbr.lu) for company incorporation; digital filing exclusively via MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders must first cause the SARL to be incorporated by notarial deed before a Luxembourg notary, subscribe and pay up the minimum statutory share capital, and file the incorporation documents on MyGuichet.lu, following which the entity is registered in the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) and receives the statutory business registration number (RCS B number). The ACD then automatically issues the Luxembourg tax matricule for corporate income and municipal business tax, and the SARL files a VAT registration application (form 640) with the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU plus 8 digits). Non-resident shareholders must appoint a Luxembourg-based independent auditor or fiduciary, maintain a substance dossier (registered office, local economic activity, local bank account), and file the beneficial owners in the Luxembourg register of beneficial owners (RBE) within one month.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders must first cause the SARL to be incorporated by notarial deed before a Luxembourg notary, subscribe and pay up the minimum statutory share capital, and file the incorporation documents on MyGuichet.lu, following which the entity is registered in the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) and receives the statutory business registration number (RCS B number). The ACD then automatically issues the Luxembourg tax matricule for corporate income and municipal business tax, and the SARL files a VAT registration application (form 640) with the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU plus 8 digits). Non-resident shareholders must appoint a Luxembourg-based independent auditor or fiduciary, maintain a substance dossier (registered office, local economic activity, local bank account), and file the beneficial owners in the Luxembourg register of beneficial owners (RBE) within one month.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the SARL is an opaque tax resident entity taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 17% (15% on taxable income up to EUR 175,000), plus a 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), producing an approximately 24.94% composite statutory rate. The principal statutory elections available are: (i) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law for financially, economically, and organizationally linked persons established in Luxembourg; (ii) notional interest deduction (NID) on new qualifying equity; (iii) waiver of the EUR 35,000 small-enterprise VAT exemption to obtain input VAT recovery; (iv) application of the participation exemption (10% shareholding or EUR 1.2 million acquisition price) and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive regime; (v) ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch alignment declaration where a counterparty treats the Luxembourg SARL as transparent - no elective hybrid carve-out is available under Luxembourg domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 in annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for locally established enterprises under the small business exemption (Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law); EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons under the OSS scheme; no registration threshold for non-established suppliers where the Luxembourg business customer is liable for the reverse charge, although a fixed establishment or on-site performance in Luxembourg triggers mandatory registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% applies to: (i) exports of goods to non-EU destinations where the goods are physically dispatched and the supplier retains a valid electronic customs export declaration (ECS/SAD); (ii) intra-Community B2B supplies of goods where the customer provides a valid VAT ID verified in VIES and transportation evidence is retained. For the SARL's ESG carbon accounting, auditing, and offset brokerage services, the supply is treated as an outside-scope B2B service under Article 17 of the Luxembourg VAT Law: the place of supply is the customer's establishment, so no Luxembourg output VAT arises on services to non-EU customers, and services to EU customers are reverse-charged to the customer. Claiming the advantage requires a signed engagement letter, the customer's VAT ID or third-country establishment evidence, detailed performance records (carbon audit reports, offset registry confirmations), and invoices expressly indicating reverse charge, together with the underlying bank receipts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic ECS exit record)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing transport outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank statements evidencing collection of export proceeds
  • VIES-validated customer VAT ID printout for intra-EU B2B supplies
  • Signed service contract and carbon audit/offset brokerage performance records

As of 2025 Luxembourg operates no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, mandatory digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime. B2G invoices to central government entities must be submitted in electronic form through the national Peppol-compliant e-invoicing hub (operated by the Centre des technologies de l'information de l'Etat - CTIE) where contractually required. All invoices issued by the SARL must satisfy the EU Directive 2006/112/EC invoicing data-set, and cross-border transactions require VIES-validated VAT IDs, quarterly EC Sales Lists, Intrastat declarations where applicable, and OSS/QSS periodic returns; invoices for reverse-charge services must state reverse charge and the customer's VAT ID.

Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline on service export proceeds; 180 days is applied as the prudential bank AML and transfer-pricing documentation convention. On audit, the AEDT may reopen inadequate zero-rating or outside-scope claims within the standard 5-year assessment limitation period, or 10 years where there is concealment or fraud. Consequences include immediate clawback of the VAT forgone at 16% with annual statutory late-payment interest (approximately 10%), administrative fines of up to double the unduly unpaid VAT for simple errors, and fines of up to five times the evaded tax with criminal prosecution and director liability for intentional fraud; repeated violations may result in suspension of the Luxembourg VAT ID and disqualification of the SARL's managers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates as a fully integrated euro-area jurisdiction with unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or convertibility licensing regimes for inbound equity injections, loan repayments, dividend distributions, or Carbon services payments. Profit remittance limits arise only from prudential and tax rules: dividends paid by a Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) are subject to a general 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reducible under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double tax treaty to 0% or a lower rate. A distribution must pass the Luxembourg corporate law balance-sheet solvency test; no FX transfer constraint applies once the bank mandate and foreign correspondent banking approvals are cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists under Luxembourg law. Export and service-provider receipts must remain freely available to the SARL in the settlement currency. The only administrative obligation is the BCL's external-statistics reporting duty for balance-of-payments and international investment position purposes; no minimum conversion ratio or time bound is impose.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), exercising monetary policy authority delegated by the Eurosystem and supervised by the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for national regulatory matters; foreign-exchange administration remains an EU and ECB competence under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for Luxembourg-regulated fintech and electronic money institutions, but Low for offshore, unlicensed digital EMIs. A Luxembourg or EU-passported EMI under PSD2 may provide segregated client-money accounts, IBANs, and payment initiation services, but cannot replace a fully licensed credit institution for SWIFT correspondent clearing, issuance of documentary credits, or FX derivative execution. An offshore digital EMI without an EU passport is legally blocked from serving as a principal treasury or clearing rail for the SARL and should be limited to ancillary sub-account use under a licensed Luxembourg bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

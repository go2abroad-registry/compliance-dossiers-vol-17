Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Central digital portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu); corporate register administered by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); national identification numbers issued via CTIE.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through LBR/MyGuichet; obtain RCS number and 13-digit national matricule (business ID). Step 2: Register for VAT with AEDT via Form 640 (VAT registration form) submitted through MyGuichet; AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number with prefix LU followed by 8 digits. Step 3: For foreign non-established entities carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg, appoint an approved VAT representative (representant responsable) before applying for VAT registration; the representative is jointly and severally liable for VAT. Step 4: Register for corporate income tax/municipal business tax via the ACD; no separate tax registration is required beyond the matricule/RCS registration. Turnaround: approximately 5-15 business days for a complete electronic filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Step 1: File incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through LBR/MyGuichet; obtain RCS number and 13-digit national matricule (business ID). Step 2: Register for VAT with AEDT via Form 640 (VAT registration form) submitted through MyGuichet; AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number with prefix LU followed by 8 digits. Step 3: For foreign non-established entities carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg, appoint an approved VAT representative (representant responsable) before applying for VAT registration; the representative is jointly and severally liable for VAT. Step 4: Register for corporate income tax/municipal business tax via the ACD; no separate tax registration is required beyond the matricule/RCS registration. Turnaround: approximately 5-15 business days for a complete electronic filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Available elections: (1) Fiscal unity (integration fiscale) election for resident companies with at least 95% direct or indirect ownership, permitting group consolidation for corporate income tax; (2) Option for territorial exemption of foreign permanent establishment profits under Article 154bis LIR; (3) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 59 of the VAT law, allowing one fiscal unit for VAT; (4) Option to charge VAT on real estate leases under Article 45 VAT law; (5) Participation exemption: 100% exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains where 10% or EUR 1.2 million participation, 12-month holding and subject-to-tax conditions are met; (6) Advance tax agreement (tax ruling) procedure with the ACD to confirm classification, hybrid mismatch treatment, and transfer pricing in accordance with ATAD (law of 25 July 2022).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No domestic turnover threshold: an established taxable person (including an SARL) is compulsorily registered from the first taxable transaction. Intra-EU distance sales by non-established EU suppliers trigger Luxembourg VAT registration when turnover exceeds EUR 10,000 per calendar year (also the threshold to use the Import One-Stop Shop / One-Stop Shop scheme). Non-established businesses have no minimum threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Article 41(1) VAT law if physical dispatch/transport outside the EU is evidenced within 90 days of the invoice date. Certain services directly linked to exports (international transport, loading, customs representation) are zero-rated. For data/API subscription services supplied B2B to non-EU customers, the place of supply is the customer's country and the Luxembourg supplier does not charge Luxembourg VAT; if supplied to EU consumers, Luxembourg VAT or the OSS scheme applies. No statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline exists in Luxembourg (no exchange controls since 1990); the 180-day field reflects prudent documentary matching of export payments to reduce audit challenge and aligns with transfer-pricing payment substance expectations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) lodged in the EU Export System (ECS) / eCDF customs IT
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of payment (bank SWIFT confirmation / inward remittance certificate) where the sale is on open account or documentary collection terms

No real-time fiscal clearance, no SAF-T reporting, and no digital stamping are in force. Mandatory structured e-invoicing exists only for B2G transactions (contracts above EUR 20,000, mandatory since March 2020) using the PEPPOL network with OpenPEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format. B2B e-invoicing is permitted in PDF or any machine-readable format and is not subject to clearance. Periodic VAT returns are filed monthly or quarterly via MyGuichet, with annual recapitulative statements (including intra-EU SAD/recap) filed electronically.

The AEDT and ACD may retroactively reassess VAT within 5 years (10 years in cases of fraud or intentional concealment). Unsubstantiated zero-rating is clawed back with late interest of 0.8% per month and late-payment penalties of up to 10% of the tax due. Administrative fines for false returns or evasion can reach EUR 250,000; criminal conviction for VAT fraud carries imprisonment up to 5 years. The tax administration may impose a tax increase of up to 50% for intentional irregularities and pursue the appointed VAT representative for joint and several liability. Export documentation must be retained for 10 years to withstand audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully liberalised euro-area jurisdiction. The euro is freely convertible on both capital and current account, and no exchange control or prior authorisation requirements apply to inbound transfers, dividends, interest, royalties, or service remittances under Article 63 TFEU and the Luxembourg Law of 17 July 1997. Profit remittance is generally subject to Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at 15%, with reductions or full exemptions available under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, double tax treaties, or the Luxembourg participation exemption regime. All foreign-exchange operations at regulated Luxembourg credit institutions are unrestricted, subject only to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML/CFT) due diligence, and balance-of-payments reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds. There is no prescribed timeline for bringing export receipts onshore and no minimum conversion ratio into euro. Export-oriented SARLs may lawfully retain foreign-currency receivables in non-resident accounts; however, for tax-resident entities such retained funds must be properly declared to the local tax authorities, subject to Luxembourg transfer-pricing documentation requirements, and any inbound distribution or shareholder-account movement must meet the bank's source-of-funds verification and BCL statistical purpose codes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for foreign-exchange policy and balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-authorised payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) passported to Luxembourg under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and electronic money. Such EMIs can serve as viable collection rails for high-frequency API subscription revenues, particularly in USD and EUR, while settlement must ultimately be parked in a Luxembourg or EU Tier-1 bank account for treasury concentration. Feasibility rating for offshore, non-EU-authorized EMIs or crypto-native processors is Low; they will normally be rejected for direct onboarding by licensed Luxembourg credit institutions. For USDC-based treasury flows, the entity must either use a CSSF-registered VASP under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 or engage a licensed intermediary; pure EMI authorisation does not cover crypto-asset custody, conversion or settlement, and any unregulated stablecoin clearing will be a significant AML/CTF blocker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity contributions to the Luxembourg SARL must be effected through SWIFT MT103 from a recognised credit institution, with the remittance information field clearly stating 'capital increase / equity contribution'. The sending bank must apply a BCL-compatible balance-of-payments purpose code; for equity subscriptions, the appropriate direct-investment classification is 'S121 – Shares and other equity'. The receiving bank will require the corporate notary deed, RCS-registered articles of association, and a resolution of the sole shareholder or general meeting. If funds are in a non-euro currency, conversion and settlement must occur through the receiving bank at the prevailing market rate, and no exchange-control approval is required. For high-value contributions, a pre-due-diligence letter and source-of-funds certification should accompany the payment to avoid quarantine.Service-based trade settlements from the SARL will run primarily through SEPA (for EU suppliers) and SWIFT MT103 for non-EU parties, with clear purpose codes and commercial invoice references. Bulk treasury disbursements to proxy-server providers, data-centre vendors, and API licensing partners should be transacted using MT103 with BBK/CLS frameworks where necessary. For physical-goods procurement not expected in this ISIC scope, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by a Luxembourg bank may be used, but the business model favors direct MT103 credits and electronic mass-payment clearing. where the counterparty requires faster settlement, SWIFT gpi and SEPA Instant may be used. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (IRC) plus 7% solidarity surcharge and communal business tax, producing a 24.94% combined statutory headline rate in Luxembourg City.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax; generally reduced to 0% for EU parent-subsidiary qualified holdings (>=10% shareholding, 12-month holding) and to 5%/10%/15% under applicable tax treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due by 31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end; electronic filers commonly receive an automatic extension to 30 June; further administrative extension may be requested.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A – Taxpayer Identity Registers corporate name, legal form (SARL), registered office, RCS Luxembourg number, tax file number and e-filing token. Non-resident SARL with a Luxembourg permanent establishment must enter the PE registration and Luxembourg business address separately from the home-office legal seat. Late filing triggers a formal reminder, then a fine of EUR 25 to EUR 3,000; underpaid tax bears overdue interest at 0.6% per month (approximately 0.02% daily) and a late-payment surcharge of up to 10% for deliberate default; criminal tax fraud is separately actionable.
Section B – Fiscal Year and Basis Period Declares beginning and end of the twelve-month accounting period and the relevant Luxembourg tax base period. Align the PE basis period with the foreign head office's accounting year and disclose any Luxembourg basis period drawn up for the first or final tax year. Late filing triggers a formal reminder, then a fine of EUR 25 to EUR 3,000; underpaid tax bears overdue interest at 0.6% per month (approximately 0.02% daily) and a late-payment surcharge of up to 10% for deliberate default; criminal tax fraud is separately actionable.
Section C – Net Commercial Result Reconciles statutory pre-tax profit with Luxembourg taxable result, including API subscription revenue, web-scraping data sales, gateway fees, and costs. Report only profits economically attributable to the Luxembourg PE; attach an OECD-compliant allocation schedule for overhead and support costs. Late filing triggers a formal reminder, then a fine of EUR 25 to EUR 3,000; underpaid tax bears overdue interest at 0.6% per month (approximately 0.02% daily) and a late-payment surcharge of up to 10% for deliberate default; criminal tax fraud is separately actionable.
Section D – Deductions and Exemptions Claims carried-forward losses, participation exemption, IP box exemption, notional interest deduction and double-tax relief credits. Losses of the foreign head office or non-resident branch may not be set off against Luxembourg PE income absent treaty diagonal/professional-loss provisions. Late filing triggers a formal reminder, then a fine of EUR 25 to EUR 3,000; underpaid tax bears overdue interest at 0.6% per month (approximately 0.02% daily) and a late-payment surcharge of up to 10% for deliberate default; criminal tax fraud is separately actionable.
Section E – Tax Computation and Credits Computes IRC, solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax; enters prepayments, withholding tax and foreign tax credits. Attach tax residence certificate and DTA relief statement to substantiate treaty-based PE allocation and any reduced WHT status. Late filing triggers a formal reminder, then a fine of EUR 25 to EUR 3,000; underpaid tax bears overdue interest at 0.6% per month (approximately 0.02% daily) and a late-payment surcharge of up to 10% for deliberate default; criminal tax fraud is separately actionable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is required for groups with consolidated annual revenue above EUR 50 million; Local File is triggered when the Luxembourg entity carries out cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per transaction class or EUR 1 million in aggregate; CbCR threshold is group consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no codified UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but its tax treaties, EU ATAD anti-abuse principles, and judicial substance doctrine require real local management, employees, office premises, operational risk-taking and decision-making; a shell SARL routing scraping/API revenue abroad may be disregarded for tax and DTA purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion And Criminal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment with 0.6% monthly interest and a 10% late-payment/negligence surcharge; criminal conviction under Luxembourg tax law carries fines up to EUR 125,000 and imprisonment of up to five years plus confiscation of benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or procure a false return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, interest and penalties; the courts may impose travel restrictions, disqualification as directors, and up to five years imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits, reclassification of distributions as taxable dividends, retroactive reassessment, and sanction penalties calculated on the WHT or tax advantage obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for the unwound tax advantage and penalty under abuse-of-law case law; a travel ban and a bar from company management can be imposed in aggravated sham-entity schemes.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may apply discretionary upward transfer-pricing adjustments, charge 0.6% monthly interest, impose a 10% documentation-failure penalty on the reassessed tax, and in severe cases pursue criminal sanction for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors approving deficient transfer pricing policies face personal civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall, administrative fines, and possible criminal liability if deliberate falsification or omission is established.
AML / Virtual-Asset Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Supervisory sanctions by the CSSF include administrative fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money-laundering penalties may reach imprisonment of five years and confiscation of crypto assets.

Director personal liability: AML obligations attach to responsible persons; failed implementation can expose directors to personal fines, civil damages, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for laundering or financing of terrorism.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), with electronic lodgement via the notary and the guichet.lu single administrative portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated (Loi du 10 août 1915 sur les sociétés commerciales), notably Articles 180-1 to 191 governing the société à responsabilité limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the electronic collection of companies and associations (RESA); Law of 15 March 2023 modernising commercial company law.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte de constitution and statuts) deposited with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 53 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,400 under Article 180-1 of the 1915 Law; every share must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of cash contributions (EUR 3,100) must be paid up at incorporation and non-cash contributions paid up in full; payment is evidenced by a Luxembourg bank certificate of deposit, and no statutory public escrow is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate issued by the municipal mayor after advice of the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS), covering evacuation routes, extinguishers, and automatic detection in the retail sales area; structural load verification by the municipal building service for storage mezzanines; electrical installation conformity certificate from an accredited control body under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 15 July 2015; workplace occupational clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering aisle safety, manual handling, staff rest areas, first-aid equipment and the sanitary conditions of the supermarket premises.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,400 under Article 180-1 of the 1915 Law; every share must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of cash contributions (EUR 3,100) must be paid up at incorporation and non-cash contributions paid up in full; payment is evidenced by a Luxembourg bank certificate of deposit, and no statutory public escrow is required.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration des douanes et accises for customs and EORI; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for commercial registration; digital portal: MyGuichet (myguichet.lu) and guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

The SARL is constituted by notarial deed and entered in the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés), which issues the national legal-entity identification number (numéro d'immatriculation / RCS number). For VAT, a distinct Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) is obtained by filing the VAT registration e-form (Formulaire 1022 / E-Form 'VAT registration') with the AEDT through MyGuichet, attaching the notarial deed, articles of association, a recent RCS extract, proof of a Luxembourg seat and economic activity, director identification, and intended business scope. Non-resident entities carrying on taxable supplies in Luxembourg or importing goods must complete the same AEDT procedure, appointing a tax representative where they are established outside the EU and outside any relevant mutual-assistance treaty; an EORI number must concurrently be obtained from the Administration des douanes et accises for customs import and export declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The SARL is constituted by notarial deed and entered in the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés), which issues the national legal-entity identification number (numéro d'immatriculation / RCS number). For VAT, a distinct Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) is obtained by filing the VAT registration e-form (Formulaire 1022 / E-Form 'VAT registration') with the AEDT through MyGuichet, attaching the notarial deed, articles of association, a recent RCS extract, proof of a Luxembourg seat and economic activity, director identification, and intended business scope. Non-resident entities carrying on taxable supplies in Luxembourg or importing goods must complete the same AEDT procedure, appointing a tax representative where they are established outside the EU and outside any relevant mutual-assistance treaty; an EORI number must concurrently be obtained from the Administration des douanes et accises for customs import and export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style 'check-the-box' mechanism; the SARL is mandatorily an entity-level corporate taxpayer. Available statutory elections include: (i) waiver of the small-business VAT franchise (voluntary VAT registration despite turnover below the EUR 35,000 exemption ceiling); (ii) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax purposes under Article 164bis LIR; (iii) option to apply VAT to lettings of immovable property; (iv) election of treatment under specific holding-company or investment-fund regimes where eligible; and (v) structuring hybrid instruments remains subject to the ATAD/anti-hybrid mismatch disallowance rules, which do not permit elective opt-out or mismatch passporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-business franchise exemption for Luxembourg-established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers and importers of goods are registrable without any turnover threshold; intra-EU cross-border B2C distance sales are subject to the EUR 10,000 Union OSS threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to destinations outside the EU qualify for VAT zero-rating (exemption with credit) under Article 146(1)(a) of EU Directive 2006/112/EC and Article 57(1) Loi TVA, provided the supplier can produce conclusive evidence that the goods left the EU within the statutory export period (normally three months from supply) and were not processed or consumed before departure. The imported Asian condiments, preserved foods and vegetables qualify for 0% only when exported onward as commercial goods; intra-EU movements to other Member States are not zero-rated exports and must be reported as exempt intra-Community dispatches through VIES/ICL. Offshore B2B services (transport, logistics, brokerage, overseas storage) are zero-rated when the recipient belongs in a third country, subject to the Article 44 B2B place-of-supply rule. Domestic retail sales of groceries to Luxembourg consumers are subject to the applicable Luxembourg VAT scale (3% super-reduced, 8% reduced, 14% intermediate, or 16% standard rate depending on product category under Annexes A/B of the Loi TVA). Although Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange control deadline, banking and administrative audit norms expect export proceeds to be received within 180 days of dispatch; longer settlement periods require documented commercial credit arrangements, and an Inward Remittance Certificate is maintained within that window to corroborate the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) bearing the electronic exit certification of the Administration des douanes et accises (NCTS/ECS system)
  • Original commercial invoice or proforma with packing list, stating the destination country and consideration
  • International transport document: CMR waybill, bill of lading, sea waybill, or air waybill evidencing physical movement to a third-country destination
  • NCTS/ECS transit and exit confirmation, or customs office departure/exit stamp trace
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103/ASR, or bank credit evidence of settlement received from the non-EU customer matching invoice value
  • Export order, customer acceptance, and freight-forwarder export declaration or third-country delivery evidence where an indirect export route is used

Luxembourg has not implemented continuous transaction controls (CTC) or mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoicing for domestic B2B/B2C transactions; e-invoicing remains voluntary and PDF/paper invoices are accepted. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies to supplies to Luxembourg public-sector bodies via the PEPPOL network. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) directive will impose obligatory e-invoicing and digital reporting in the 2030-2035 horizon, with Luxembourg transposition legislation pending; exporters must meanwhile maintain machine-readable electronic export records consistent with the national NCTS/ECS customs systems.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow zero-rating where the export proof package is incomplete, contradictory, or not produced within the statutory retention period (business records 10 years; assessment generally 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or false declarations). Reversed outputs are re-charged at the applicable Luxembourg VAT rate (16% standard headline, or the reduced rate if the goods are diverted to domestic or EU consumption), with monthly late-payment interest of 0.6% per month and administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 per infraction for defective invoicing, books, or declarations. Intentional or fraudulent export arrangements, including carrousel or missing-trader fraud, trigger criminal prosecution (imprisonment and fines), joint and several liability of directors, refusal of refund claims, customs penalties under the Code des douanes, and publicity measures in egregious cases; managers cannot invoke the foreign customer's payment default or delivery instructions to excuse an unsubstantiated zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current account and capital account operations within the framework of the EU Treaty and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Luxembourg imposes no general exchange control restrictions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, repayment of shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders proceed freely without prior regulatory authorisation, subject to documentation requirements imposed by the bank under AML/CFT legislation and the BCL balance-of-payments reporting threshold of EUR 12,500 per transaction. As the entity operates a physical grocery store selling imported Asian products, all trade-related settlements in EUR or foreign currency are unrestricted; no differentiated approval is required for merchants or retailers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rules exist in Luxembourg. Resident entities are not required to convert or repatriate foreign-currency export proceeds within any statutory timeline. Foreign-currency earnings may be retained in bank accounts indefinitely. For statistical compliance, export receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction must be reported to the Banque Centrale du Luxembourg through the monthly BCL balance-of-payments reporting system; no conversion ratio applies and no exchange approval is needed for remittances abroad.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and AML supervision; cross-border statistical reporting is administered by the BCL under EU Balance of Payments Regulation and Luxembourg Grand-Ducal Regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-low for the primary operational account, due to the statutory retail POS acquiring and cash handling requirements. A Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institution (under CSSF) or an EU/EEA EMI passporting into Luxembourg can legally provide e-money accounts for cross-border supplier payments; however, an offshore/digital EMI established outside the EU/EEA has no direct access to Luxembourg domestic payment rails, point-of-sale acquiring, or intra-eurozone settlement without a licensed correspondent intermediary. For this grocery retail model, an EMI is viable only as an auxiliary treasury tool for settling imports from Asian suppliers in USD or CNY, not as the principal clearing bank. Legal constraints under PSD2, the Settlement Finality Law, and CSSF Circulars restrict deposit-taking and require safeguarding of funds. The overall feasibility rating is 'medium' with a clear carve-out for supplier disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the parent company or shareholders must be routed through the banking system using a SWIFT MT103 message (or SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR). The remitter bank must include the correct EU Balance of Payments purpose code — for direct investment equity capital, code A.1.1 (ordinary shares) or A.1.2 (other equity / partnership interest), and the payment reference should clearly state 'Capital increase – Libraire / SARL Luxembourg'. The receiving Luxembourg bank will require the notarial deed of capital increase (procès-verbal) and the updated RCS filing to reconcile the account credit. The BCL monthly balance-of-payments reporting obligation applies automatically to all cross-border flows in excess of EUR 12,500. No exchange-control licence is required. If the injection takes the form of a shareholder loan, the purpose code A.1.3 (loans to resident by non-resident) should be used, with a loan agreement and transfer-pricing justification.For settlement of supplier invoices for imported Asian groceries, the principal rails are: (i) direct SWIFT MT103 high-value or standard-value credits for USD/CNY/JPY and other non-EUR currencies; (ii) SEPA instant or SEPA credit transfer for EUR-denominated EU supplier payments; (iii) documentary letters of credit opened through a Luxembourg commercial bank (SWIFT MT700 issue, MT710 advice, MT400 reimbursement) when transacting with Asian banks requiring LC security. For daily low-value disbursements to local service providers, the Luxembourg domestic clearing system uses TARGET2 for large-value and STEP/SEPA for retail. Bank charges are typically split; the SARL’s contracts should clearly designate payment terms (e.g., 70% documents and 30% advance). In all cases, the bank mandate must pre-set the purpose code C.2.1 (imports of goods) and include scanned invoices and customs declarations in the compliance workflow.Outright FX forwards traded with a licensed Luxembourg/EEA bank under standard ISDA or local FX master agreement, with maturities typically up to 12 months and delivery in USD/CNY/JPY/EUR;Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with transferability restrictions, especially CNY (onshore/settlement offshore) and certain Southeast Asian currencies, transacted with licensed global banks in Luxembourg;Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps) for long-term supply-chain financing or shareholder-loan currency risk management, documented under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement;Foreign-exchange options (zero-cost collars, call/put spreads) for protecting import payment cash flows against adverse USD/EUR and CNY/EUR moves, subject to internal derivative governance and CSSF EMIR documentation requirements.

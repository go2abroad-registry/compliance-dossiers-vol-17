Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000.00 in respect of a Luxembourg SARL; the capital must be fully subscribed and paid up prior to notarization, evidenced by a blocked account certificate (attestation de blocage de fonds) issued by a Luxembourg credit institution; no escrow disbursement to the company is permitted until registration in the RCS and the release of the certificate upon presentation of the RCS extract.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is a resident company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) at a flat 17% headline rate, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) at generally 6.75% in Luxembourg City, and the 7% employment-fund solidarity surcharge on IRC from fiscal year 2025, producing an aggregate effective company tax burden of approximately 24.94%. The SARL is taxed on worldwide income with credit/participation-exemption relief for foreign-source profits; it is not eligible for pass-through or flow-through tax treatment, and profit distributions are subject to dividend withholding tax independently of the shareholder's residence.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box entity classification election. Mandatory ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (LIR articles 168bis et seq.) neutralize deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction mismatches. Optional statutory elections include: (i) the fiscal integration regime (régime fiscal intégré) under LIR article 164bis, allowing a qualifying parent holding directly or indirectly at least the statutory ownership threshold (revised by the fiscal year 2025 reform from 95% to a 100% direct shareholding requirement, with a 12-month continuous holding condition) to aggregate the results of Luxembourg and, under the 2025 extension, certain EU/EEA subsidiaries for IRC/ICC purposes, and allowing new horizontal integration between sister companies; (ii) the joint taxation election available to companies validly formed in the EEA for specific treaty-affected items; and (iii) an election for partnerships to be taxed as transparent vs. opaque only in strictly limited legacy cases. Hybrid mismatch elections under EU Directive (EU) 2016/1164 apply automatically and cannot be opted out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons: the SARL must register for VAT from the day it commences its economic activity (Loi TVA art. 53), irrespective of revenue level. For non-established foreign suppliers, the intra-EU distance-selling B2C goods threshold is EUR 100,000; above that, Luxembourg VAT registration (or OSS usage) becomes compulsory. For all other taxable supplies performed in Luxembourg by a non-established business, there is no de minimis exemption and registration is triggered by the first taxable transaction, subject to reverse-charge treatment in the B2B context.

Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing has applied since 18 September 2024 to every taxable person established in Luxembourg, which must issue e-invoices in a European standard format (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 / EN 16931) via the PEPPOL network or a PEPPOL-compliant service provider; since 1 March 2025, all established taxable persons must also receive B2B e-invoices electronically and process them through the PEPPOL infrastructure. Credit notes and self-billing documents are phased in from 1 January 2026. Luxembourg operates no centralized real-time fiscal clearance system, no state-run continuous transaction control (CTC) API, and no mandatory digital stamping comparable to Italy's SDI or Romania's e-Factura. Compliance is enforced indirectly through the periodic VAT return (form 1022), the EC Sales List (form 1032), SAF-T (fichier normalisé comptable) production on official request within 10 working days, and standard 5-year retention obligations. Non-established foreign suppliers holding only a Luxembourg VAT number are subject to the receipt-side obligation but are not within the issuance-side mandate.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg legal and regulatory framework, anchored in the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63–66), guarantees full free movement of capital and payments within the Eurozone and vis-à-vis third countries. As a euro-denominated jurisdiction, Luxembourg does not impose any exchange control regime. Both current account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, royalties) and capital account operations (share subscriptions, loans, acquisitions) are freely convertible. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate formalities (voting approval, statutory accounts, and if applicable, withholding tax under domestic law and tax treaties). No prior authorization, hard currency surrender, minimum conversion quotient, or repatriation license applies. Mandatory compliance obligations are limited to anti-money laundering/counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) checks, international tax transparency (CRS/FATCA), and data reporting to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics in respect of cross-border flows exceeding prescribed thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg maintains no statutory foreign-exchange surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion of export proceeds, and no timeline within which export receivables must be repatriated or settled in euro. An exporting entity may lawfully retain balances in foreign currency (e.g., USD, CNY, CHF) in its Luxembourg bank accounts, settle them through spot or forward contracts, or use them to fund future input costs. The only reporting duty for export proceeds arises through the BCL’s balance-of-payments statistical reporting framework, under which resident banks must code and report transactions according to the BPM6 classification. Such reporting is for statistical use, not for exchange-control enforcement. Export operations must nevertheless comply with EU and national sanctions, customs rules, and the EU Dual-Use Regulation where applicable, though ISIC 4669 wholesale trade of sulfur mineral ores typically does not require a specific export license unless defence-related end uses are identified.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

FinTech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorised in an EU/EEA state may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and the E-Money Directive. However, for a wholesale dry-bulk sulfur trading SARL, the viability of using an offshore digital EMI is rated Medium-to-Low. Legal and practical constraints include: (i) EMIs typically cannot issue or negotiate documentary letters of credit or provide classic trade finance; (ii) PSD2 limits the extent to which payment institutions can grant commercial credit – only in connection with payment services and within strict regulatory capital conditions; (iii) offshore EMIs located outside the EEA generally lack the right of establishment or free provision of services in Luxembourg without a local licence, and may be prohibited from servicing a Luxembourg resident corporate client; (iv) correspondent banking networks of smaller EMIs often exclude commodities with perceived sanctions risk; and (v) safeguarding and segregation requirements apply to funds held by authorised EMIs. Therefore, the blueprint recommends using an authorised Luxembourg/EU bank for primary treasury accounts, with an EMI only as a supplemental low-value or petty disbursement rail if the EMI holds a valid passport into Luxembourg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections from a non-resident shareholder must be executed by means of a SWIFT MT103 or MT202, depending on the correspondent bank’s instructions, with the remittance field unambiguously referencing 'Share Capital Contribution' and 'Director’s Resolution dated [X]'. The BCL balance-of-payments and statistical coding classification to be indicated by the receiving Luxembourg bank is BPM6 Direct Investment – Equity Capital (DI.1.1). The payment must originate from the shareholder’s own bank account in good standing, and never from a cash-related or unverified third-party source. Before the bank credits the funds to the Capital Escrow Account, the following must be presented: (i) the company’s notarised incorporation deed and articles of association, showing the subscribed capital and registered office; (ii) a notarised or signed shareholder resolution authorising the transfer; (iii) the bank account certificate from the sending institution; (iv) identification and KYC documentation of the ultimate beneficial owner; and (v) proof of source of funds (e.g., audited financial statements, asset and liability statement, or prior dividend distribution resolution). Upon receipt, the funds are credited to the temporary escrow and may be converted to a fully operational account after the Luxembourg commercial register (RCS) filings are confirmed and, if applicable, the capital duty and notarial requirements are completed.For outbound trade settlements under ISIC 4669 sulphur and mineral ore trading, primary clearing rails are: (a) open account settlements via SWIFT MT103 with full remittance information, executed through the Luxembourg bank’s global payments innovation (SWIFT gpi) network; (b) documentary letters of credit (L/C) issued by the buyer’s bank and advised/confirmed in Luxembourg under UCP 600 (e.g., SWIFT MT700/MT707), with presentation of the bill of lading, certificate of origin, commercial invoice, packing list, and inspection certificates; (c) documentary collections (CP 602) where transaction volumes justify lower cost; (d) cross-border real-time gross settlement via TARGET2 for euro-denominated legs; and (e) for secured trades, supply chain finance products such as purchase order finance or receivables discounting provided by the Luxembourg relationship bank, subject to approved facility limits. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: FinTech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorised in an EU/EEA state may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and the E-Money Directive. However, for a wholesale dry-bulk sulfur trading SARL, the viability of using an offshore digital EMI is rated Medium-to-Low. Legal and practical constraints include: (i) EMIs typically cannot issue or negotiate documentary letters of credit or provide classic trade finance; (ii) PSD2 limits the extent to which payment institutions can grant commercial credit – only in connection with payment services and within strict regulatory capital conditions; (iii) offshore EMIs located outside the EEA generally lack the right of establishment or free provision of services in Luxembourg without a local licence, and may be prohibited from servicing a Luxembourg resident corporate client; (iv) correspondent banking networks of smaller EMIs often exclude commodities with perceived sanctions risk; and (v) safeguarding and segregation requirements apply to funds held by authorised EMIs. Therefore, the blueprint recommends using an authorised Luxembourg/EU bank for primary treasury accounts, with an EMI only as a supplemental low-value or petty disbursement rail if the EMI holds a valid passport into Luxembourg.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 for related-party transactions; EUR 400,000 for intercompany financial transactions, per the Luxembourg Transfer Pricing Documentation Law of 28 December 2018 (grand-ducal regulation of 5 December 2018).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg operates an explicit Economic Substance Doctrine for EU and OECD purposes, but does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations similar to those of the UAE or BVI. The Luxembourg tax administration applies an arm's-length principle and a general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) that requires a Luxembourg structure to have operational substance, i.e. a local office, qualified staff performing core functions, management decision-making in Luxembourg, and actual commercial risk-taking. A sulfur mineral ore trading company must maintain an adequate level of qualified trading personnel, physical offices, and decision-making records in Luxembourg; otherwise profit allocations can be recharacterised and the entity may be deemed sham or an agent of foreign principals. Under OECD BEPS, a Luxembourg trading company with low substance but high paper profits is exposed to transfer pricing adjustments and potentially permanent establishment risk in the foreign principal jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 57 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO), tax fraud by false declaration is punishable by a fine of up to €250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the tax due, plus late interest at 0.6% monthly and a 10% late payment penalty, is recoverable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax debt under Article 40 of the Tax Administration Act if the company is insolvent and the director has committed a serious and deliberate fault, and criminal convictions can trigger travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment; the Luxembourg courts may pierce the veil when the company is a mere façade.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authorities can issue a transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, impose a late payment interest of 0.6% per month, and apply a 10% surcharge; failure to provide documentation under the TP Documentation Law triggers a fine of up to €10,000 per missing documentation set, and the burden of proof shifts in favor of the administration.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length transactions without due diligence may be jointly liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiency; if the transaction involves fraudulent misrepresentation, criminal sanctions and personal liability extend to the director's personal assets under Luxembourg bankruptcy and company law.
Abusive Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg GAAR and OECD BEPS provisions allow the tax administration to disregard the entity, reallocate profits to the beneficial owners' residence state, deny treaty benefits, and levy tax at the full 24.94% rate plus interest and penalties; the entity may also be struck off the Luxembourg Trade Register for non-compliance with annual report filings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for abuse of law if they knowingly maintained a fictitious corporate structure; civil liability under Article 1382 of the Luxembourg Civil Code can extend to restitution of taxes, and a criminal conviction for fraudulent organization of insolvency may carry imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to €125,000.
AML / Financial Crime in Trade Payments

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg AML law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) criminalises money laundering; penalties include imprisonment from 5 to 10 years for organised money laundering and fines up to €1,250,000; failing to file beneficial ownership data in the RBE is a criminal offence subject to fines of up to €1,250,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months for directors.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in or wilfully blind to AML violations face direct criminal liability, travel bans, custodial sentences, and confiscation of corporate and personal assets; Luxembourg courts have lifted the corporate veil when shell companies are used as conduits for funds attributable to self-laundering or tax fraud.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; unified digital access via MyGuichet.lu (guichet.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first execute a notarised deed of incorporation and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), which assigns the national identification number (matricule) and RCS number. For VAT, the entity files the VAT registration form (formulaire TVA) with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu and receives a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format 'LU' followed by eight digits within approximately two weeks. Non-established foreign companies making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must complete the simplified registration and, if they hold no EU establishment, appoint a tax representative approved by the AEDT. The ACD uses the same matricule for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax assessments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate US-style check-the-box elections; a resident SARL is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Optional statutory regimes include: (i) fiscal unity (integration fiscale) election under Art. 164bis LIR, available to a parent holding directly or indirectly at least 95% of the SARL's capital, valid for five years and requiring prior approval of the ACD; (ii) VAT grouping election under Art. 58 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, allowing financially, economically, and organisationally linked taxable persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections and disapplication measures under ATAD (Art. 56a et seq. LIR) to neutralise deductible hybrid payments; (iv) optional IP box treatment under Art. 50quater LIR providing an 80% exemption for qualifying intellectual property income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic VAT registration threshold applies to a Luxembourg-established SARL; the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg triggers compulsory registration. A EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold applies to non-established EU suppliers selling to Luxembourg final consumers; non-EU third-country suppliers must register from the first supply. The wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents in Luxembourg is fully taxable with no de minimis exemption for an established distributor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the supply of laboratory diagnostic reagents where the SARL demonstrates that: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or transported by the seller or a third party acting on the seller's behalf to a destination outside the European Union, supported by a valid electronic customs export declaration (SAD / EX-A) and proof of exit from EU customs territory; or (ii) the goods are dispatched or transported to another EU Member State to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number validated via VIES, with the transport commencing and ending in different Member States; or (iii) the supply qualifies as an exempt intra-community supply under Art. 44 of the Luxembourg VAT Law. Services directly related to the exported goods, such as international transport and freight, may also be zero-rated under equivalent documentary requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX-A) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document together with the commercial invoice stating Incoterms and CN/HS codes
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment evidence demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds
  • VIES validation printout confirming the customer's EU VAT registration for intra-community supplies

Luxembourg has not introduced mandatory B2B e-invoicing or real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC); no tax-authority API clearance or digital stamping is required. Invoices in PDF, EDI, Peppol, or other structured formats such as XML are legally acceptable. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies to supplies to Luxembourg public authorities: invoices must be transmitted electronically in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format through the PEPPOL network. VAT returns are filed electronically via the eTVA platform accessible through MyGuichet.lu.

The AEDT may audit export zero-rating evidence retrospectively for at least five years, extended to ten years where fraud or bad faith is alleged. Failure to substantiate export status, submission of forged customs paperwork, or false inward remittance certificates triggers immediate reassessment of 17% VAT on the full invoice value, default interest at 0.6% per month, administrative fines of up to EUR 100,000, clawback of all zero-rated refunds, exclusion from electronic export procedures, and criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to five years for VAT fraud under the Luxembourg General Tax Law and VAT Law provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the full liberalization of capital movements under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The EUR is fully convertible on both current and capital account; no BCL authorization or prior clearance is required for dividends, branch remittances, or loan repayments. Luxembourg imposes no exchange control quota or repatriation deferral. Practical hurdles: only statistical BCL balance-of-payments reporting (via banks) and corporate law's minimum capital and net-asset solvency tests for distributions apply. Profit remittance is unrestricted provided the SARL satisfies the 'treasury test' under the Law of 10 August 1915 (i.e., net assets after distribution must not fall below subscribed capital plus undistributable reserves), backed by the statutory auditor's confirmation if a statutory audit is mandated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg has no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation window. Export receivables for wholesale distribution of diagnostic reagents may be retained in any currency, converted freely, or used directly to settle foreign supplier invoices. The only administrative requirement is VAT invoice / recapitulative declaration alignment and BCL statistical reporting for cross-border settlements executed through resident banks, which is completed automatically by the bank under ECB Regulation 1745/2011 framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating in the Eurosystem; banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under Law of 5 April 1993 on the Financial Sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as a secondary non-bank liquidity rail only. Luxembourg recognizes CSSF-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU/EEA EMIs passporting their license under PSD2 (Regulation (EU) 2015/751; Directive (EU) 2015/2366). Vertically integrated global EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may offer multi-currency IBANs and serve as a collection/disbursement overlay for low-value, recurring trade payments. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit, deposit-taking is prohibited, client funds are safeguarded not protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL €100,000 limit applies only to credit institutions). An EMI balance above €10,000 equivalent at any single provider should be avoided; the treasury's principal liquidity buffer must remain with a Tier-1 licensed bank. EMI inbound/outbound wires require identical UBO/KYC documentation as a bank, and commercial invoices must be tagged with the correct HTS/ECICS product classification to avoid frozen-payment exceptions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for EUR-denominated intra-EU injections) from the parent shareholder's bank into the SARL's dedicated account held with a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. Protocol: (1) remitter must quote the payment purpose code corresponding to 'direct investment - equity capital' using the ECB BOP standard (commonly 'C21' unity or bank-specific 'DIRECT/MD' code); (2) narrative field 70(Remittance Information) must state 'A00000001 – CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – LU_SARL – NOT FOR RETAIL' and reference the notarized deed of contribution and equity register entry; (3) the receiving Luxembourg bank will complete the BCL balance-of-payments statistical report (IQRS/CPE) using the purpose code; (4) verification: upload, via the bank's corporate portal, the board resolution authorizing capital increase, the notarized contribution deed, the updated shareholder register, and proof of the minimum EUR 12,000 paid-up capital requirement under the Law of 10 August 1915 (SARL minimum capital regime). For loans (shareholder advances), use 'loans – intercompany financing' purpose code with a signed shareholder loan agreement and an arm's-length interest rate consistent with Luxembourg transfer-pricing circular (LU TP Circular 56/1 of 27 April 2016).Clearing rail matrix: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV for majority of non-EEA supplier payments (e.g., manufacturers in Switzerland, USA, Japan, China) – MT103 must carry ISO 20022 (MX equivalent 'pacs.008') data field 'CategoryPurpose' = 'SUPP' and must reference the underlying commercial invoice number; value dating via TARGET2/SWIFT with EUR/USD accounts; (2) Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (L/C) issuing or advising through the Luxembourg bank via SWIFT MT700/MT707 series for high-value bulk reagent shipments (> €250,000) or when the manufacturer insists on banker's undertaking; all L/Cs must conform to UCP 600 and INCOTERMS 2020 (typically DAP or EXW); (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) using the MT103/CEPT+ protocol for EU/EEA suppliers, with settlement in central bank money via TARGET2 and no cross-border clearing fee; (4) For intra-group settlements, use netting under an intercompany netting agreement with monthly cycles, quantifying settlement amounts via SWIFT MT101 (pre-notification) and executing final payments via MT103. No Luxembourg-specific clearing tax is imposed; only market-standard banking commissions and rubrics apply (e.g., 0.1%–0.3% LC commission, MT103 fee €10–50).EUR/USD and EUR/CHF outright forwards (FX outright) with a Luxembourg-licensed credit institution, tenors up to 12 months, documented under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement, with CSA collateral if the notional exceeds €5 millionFX swaps (swap achat/vente) for temporary EUR/USD liquidity bridging, permitted without BCL notification; must be recorded under the same ISDA/FBF netting agreementCurrency swaps (cross-currency interest rate swaps) for intercompany loan hedges, accessible to non-financial counterparties under EMIR Article 11 rules – the SARL benefits from the clearing obligation exemption as a non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold (EUR 3 billion for FX/interest derivatives) but must report derivative trades to a registered TR (e.g., UnaVista) via the clearing member bankNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on CNY, INR, TRY or other restricted emerging-market currencies – legally permissible under Luxembourg law when intermediated by a Luxembourg bank or its branch/affiliate outside the restricted currency's own exchange-control perimeter; because EUR is freely convertible, NDFs are used only where a physical delivery is blocked by the counterparty's central bank (e.g., offshore CNY/USD via the London or Hong Kong market)OTC FX Options (vanilla calls/puts) on EUR/USD for upside/downside trade-flow protection, subject to the same ISDA/FBF documentation and option premium accounting treatment under IFRS or Lux GAAP as investments in derivatives; hedging transactions must satisfy the EU Hedge Accounting rules under IAS 39/IFRS 9 if the SARL prepares consolidated accounts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as a secondary non-bank liquidity rail only. Luxembourg recognizes CSSF-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU/EEA EMIs passporting their license under PSD2 (Regulation (EU) 2015/751; Directive (EU) 2015/2366). Vertically integrated global EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may offer multi-currency IBANs and serve as a collection/disbursement overlay for low-value, recurring trade payments. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit, deposit-taking is prohibited, client funds are safeguarded not protected by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL €100,000 limit applies only to credit institutions). An EMI balance above €10,000 equivalent at any single provider should be avoided; the treasury's principal liquidity buffer must remain with a Tier-1 licensed bank. EMI inbound/outbound wires require identical UBO/KYC documentation as a bank, and commercial invoices must be tagged with the correct HTS/ECICS product classification to avoid frozen-payment exceptions.

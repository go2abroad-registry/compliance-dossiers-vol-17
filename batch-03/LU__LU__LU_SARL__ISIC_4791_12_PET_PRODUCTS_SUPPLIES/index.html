Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration; VAT administered by Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT). Corporate and fiscal registration is effected through Le Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and the official e-portal MyGuichet.lu (https://myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg-resident SARL obtains a single national identification number (matricule) from the RCS upon incorporation through a Luxembourg-registered notary; the matricule functions as the corporate tax file number for ACD. Before commencing taxable e-commerce activity, the SARL must register for VAT with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu and receive a Luxembourg VAT identification number. A foreign non-resident e-commerce operator must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for Luxembourg VAT unless it uses the Union/Non-Union One-Stop Shop regime; there is no registration threshold for non-EU sellers. EU sellers of pet supplies into Luxembourg are subject to the EUR 10,000 annual distance-sale threshold for Luxembourg VAT registration or OSS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Luxembourg-resident S.à r.l. is an opaque capital company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no default pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective hybrid look-through regime exists under Luxembourg law. Taxable person may elect: (1) fiscal integration tax consolidation with Luxembourg/EEA subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at 95% for a five-year commitment; (2) VAT grouping as a Single Taxable Person for VAT; (3) small-undertaking VAT exemption by resident small enterprises only. Anti-hybrid provisions follow ATAD; no elective hybrid mismatch treatment is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-enterprise exemption: annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 30,000 (optional and inapplicable to non-resident sellers). Cross-border intra-EU distance sales into Luxembourg: EUR 10,000 per calendar year threshold, above which Luxembourg VAT registration or OSS is mandatory. Non-EU sellers: registration with AEDT is required as from the first euro of Luxembourg taxable B2C supplies, subject to marketplace deemed-supplier rules for electronically facilitated e-commerce.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Pet supplies and pet-care devices sold by the SARL to non-EU customers qualify for 0% Luxembourg VAT as export supplies if the goods physically leave the EU within the statutory dispatch period and the supplier retains comprehensive export evidence, including a customs export declaration naming the SARL as exporter, transport documentation showing exit from the EU, and proof of payment from the non-EU buyer. Offshore services to non-EU business customers are zero-rated where place-of-supply rules place the supply outside Luxembourg; B2C digital services are instead taxable in the customer's jurisdiction and fall under the non-Union OSS, not Luxembourg VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-Export AES/ECS declaration with Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, alternatively CMR road consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment receipt evidencing full remittance of export sale proceeds from the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of exit (customs electronic exit confirmation or transport/handler evidence)

Luxembourg currently imposes no mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing clearance regime, no continuous transaction controls, and no fiscal digital stamping. Electronic invoicing is accepted for VAT compliance; B2G invoices must be transmitted electronically through the national e-procurement government platform. Luxembourg is preparing EU ViDA transposition with expected structured e-invoicing obligations after 2030; cross-border customs e-procedures are cleared electronically under the Union Customs Code via the AES/ECS systems.

Where zero-rating is claimed without complete or genuine export proof, the AEDT/ACD may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate, assess Luxembourg VAT at 17% on the supply, and charge late-payment interest. Administrative fines may reach up to 100% of the evaded tax under the Luxembourg General Tax Law. Deliberate fraudulent export VAT juggling is a criminal tax offense punishable by fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; cross-border cases also trigger Eurofisc and mutual assistance exchanges under Council Regulation (EU) No 904/2010.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full liberalisation. Luxembourg maintains no foreign-exchange controls; both capital and current account transactions benefit from unrestricted convertibility under TFEU Articles 63–66, the Luxembourg law of 27 December 2003 on free movement of capital, and ECB free-transfer regulations. Profit and dividend remittances require no exchange-control authorisation; the only constraint is fiscal, being the standard 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, reducible to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding a direct participation of at least 10% for at least 12 months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Article 147 LIR domestic exemption.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no minimum EUR conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. Export receivables may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency, but must be recorded in EUR equivalent under the Luxembourg Commercial Code accounting framework and supported by compliant commercial invoices for CIT and VAT purposes; intra-group cross-border flows must be documented at arm's length under Luxembourg transfer-pricing rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), exercising prudential and statistical functions jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which supervises credit institutions and enforces the Luxembourg AML/CFT law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF regulatory circulars.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline rate in Luxembourg City for fiscal 2024/2025, comprising corporate income tax at 17%, unemployment fund contribution at 0.3%, and municipal business tax at 7.5%; municipal business tax varies by commune between 6.75% and 11.025%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic withholding tax on dividend distributions by a Luxembourg SARL. Reduced to 0% for distributions meeting the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions (direct shareholding of at least 10% or EUR 1,200,000, held for at least 12 months) and under the domestic participation-exemption regime. Double tax treaty rates commonly range from 5% to 15% for portfolio investors and 0% for substantial corporate shareholdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 500,000. Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 750 million. Documentation must be contemporaneous, in line with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, and produced to the ACD within 30 days of an audit request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime analogous to the UAE, but the ACD applies the OECD substance principle, the GAAR under Article 6 of the Tax Adaptation Act, and EU ATAD anti-abuse rules. A SARL holding or distributing pet supplies should maintain real local substance: business premises, qualified directors/managers, effective decision-making at board meetings in Luxembourg, employee presence, local bank accounts, and substantiated commercial risk. A 'mailbox' SARL without substance will be denied treaty benefits, participation-exemption relief, and may be deemed managed outside Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False Declaration of E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of underpaid corporate income tax with default interest at 0.6% per month; administrative surcharge of up to 50% of the additional tax for intent; criminal prosecution for tax fraud carrying a fine of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: The director or de facto manager who signs the false return is personally exposed to criminal conviction, joint civil liability for the SARL's unpaid taxes, attachment of personal bank accounts and assets, travel bans, and disqualification from managing companies.
Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Conduit

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and Parent-Subsidiary Directive protection; application of GAAR/ATAD; re-taxation of the global profit at the real place of management; 15% withholding tax claw-back on distributions; additional tax and late interest; criminal fines where abusive declarations are intentionally made.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a fictitious Luxembourg seat may be jointly liable for unpaid Luxembourg taxes, face criminal liability for fraudulent certification, and be subject to personal asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ACD profit adjustment to the arm's length result, additional CIT and municipal business tax, irregularity interest at 0.6% per month, administrative penalties up to 30% of the reassessed tax for serious negligence, and criminal penalties if false transfer-pricing documentation is submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors approving sham intercompany agreements without economic substance are personally liable for the resulting tax loss; they face personal fines, disqualification, seizure of personal assets and criminal proceedings where fraudulent bookkeeping is proven.
AML / Suspicious Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Where the SARL acts as an obligated entity under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004, regulatory fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; money-laundering offences may result in imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 250,000, confiscation of laundered assets, and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Beneficiary owners and directors who participate in or knowingly disregard laundering indicators are personally liable: civil restitution, criminal fines, imprisonment, asset freeze, travel bans, and loss of director eligibility.
Non-Filing / Phantom Dormancy

Statutory sanction: Taxation d'office by the ACD based on external payment processor data; late-filing fine up to EUR 5,000; default interest at 0.6% per month; loss of loss carry-forward applicability and of treaty benefits; criminal sanctions for obstruction of tax controls.

Director personal liability: Directors causing the default assessment remain jointly and severally liable for the income tax debts when the SARL is liquidated; they may be prohibited from leaving Luxembourg pending debt settlement and risk personal bankruptcy declarations if assets were misappropriated.

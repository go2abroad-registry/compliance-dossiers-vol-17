Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), through a Luxembourg notary and the MyGuichet.lu e-filing portal

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, in particular Articles 1 and 710-1 to 710-41 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; Law of 2 September 2011 on Business Establishment; Law of 15 June 2018 on Classified Establishments.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial incorporation deed (acte de constitution) containing the articles of association (statuts), filed with the RCS for registration and publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 100 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum share capital of an SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. For cash contributions, at least 25% of each share must be paid up before signing the notarial deed, and the funds must be deposited on a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the future company's name. Contributions in kind require full payment, a valuation by a réviseur d'entreprises, and are reflected in the notarial deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Corps grand-ducal des incendies et de secours after verification of emergency exits, fire suppression, smoke control and access for rescue services. Occupational health and workplace clearance by the Inspection du travail et des mines confirming ventilation, work equipment safety, thermal stress controls, sanitation and food safety infrastructure of the central kitchen and bakery retail premises.

Paid-in capital rule: The minimum share capital of an SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. For cash contributions, at least 25% of each share must be paid up before signing the notarial deed, and the funds must be deposited on a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the future company's name. Contributions in kind require full payment, a valuation by a réviseur d'entreprises, and are reflected in the notarial deed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes – ACD) for direct taxes; VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT). Electronic portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

For resident SARL: obtain business registration number from Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) upon incorporation; the ACD automatically issues the national identification number (numéro d'identification) for corporate tax. For VAT: resident entities apply to AEDT via form 700/710; non-resident entities without establishment but making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT on same forms through an appointed Luxembourg tax representative, receiving an LU+8-digit VAT number. Registration is required before any taxable activity commences.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – SARL is fully taxable on worldwide income as a separate legal person, subject to CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no pass-through treatment. — For resident SARL: obtain business registration number from Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) upon incorporation; the ACD automatically issues the national identification number (numéro d'identification) for corporate tax. For VAT: resident entities apply to AEDT via form 700/710; non-resident entities without establishment but making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT on same forms through an appointed Luxembourg tax representative, receiving an LU+8-digit VAT number. Registration is required before any taxable activity commences.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – SARL is fully taxable on worldwide income as a separate legal person, subject to CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box regime. Elections available: (i) VAT group election (assujetti unique) for closely related persons under Article 60 Luxembourg VAT law; (ii) corporate tax grouping (intégration fiscale) for resident subsidiaries ≥95% owned, allowing consolidation for CIT and municipal business tax; (iii) selective application of anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD; (iv) optional collective investment vehicle treatment for eligible funds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover exemption threshold under Article 57 VAT Law for resident small businesses; non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable supplies made in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) goods physically dispatched or transported outside EU customs territory by the supplier or on his behalf within the statutory proof period; (ii) supplies of services directly linked to such export, and certain cross-border services subject to Article 14(2) VAT Law. Claim requires that the recipient is identifiable, the goods leave Luxembourg/EU, and complete documentary evidence is held by the supplier before filing the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD – Single Administrative Document/DAU)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill where air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign currency receipt/consideration settled through a bank)
  • CMR transport document or other proof of physical departure
  • Commercial invoice
  • Proof of delivery and fiscal warehouse removal records

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is voluntary in B2B/B2C; mandatory e-invoicing for public procurement via PEPPOL (European standard). The ACD/AEDT accepts electronic invoices but does not operate a centralized clearance API; taxpayers must retain standard audit files (SAF-T) and invoice data for submission upon request.

Luxembourg VAT authorities routinely audit export zero-rating; any failure to provide complete export evidence within the legal deadline causes reassessment at standard rate. Penalties include late-payment interest (0.4% per month), administrative fines up to EUR 10,000 per offence, and 10% penalty on undue VAT. In case of fraudulent zero-rating or organized VAT evasion, criminal sanctions (fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment) apply, input VAT recovery is clawed back, and joint liability may be imposed on directors or Luxembourg representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member and maintains fully liberalised capital and current-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no exchange-control permit or prior central-bank approval requirement for ordinary capital inflows, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or intercompany loans. The euro is freely convertible; additional foreign-currency accounts in USD, CNH, or other accepted currencies may be maintained at a Luxembourg bank. Mainland CNY is not freely offshore-convertible, but commercial RMB flow can clear through authorised CNH/RMB clearing channels. Restrictions arise only from EU/CFSP sanctions regimes, targeted financial restrictive measures, and CSSF-mandated KYC/AML controls. Profit remittance from a Luxembourg SARL is legally unrestricted, though corporate withholding-tax or substance documentation must be observed under direct tax rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or holding period for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency without limitation. The only statutory obligations are statistical reporting to the BCL within the balance-of-payments framework and compliance with the SARL's ordinary accounting, substance, and corporate-tax filing requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with prudential and anti-money-laundering oversight exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium feasibility. A Luxembourg-regulated electronic money institution (EMI) authorised by the CSSF under the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2017 implementing EMD2 may open payment accounts, issue IBANs, and process low-value POS or collection flows. However, an EMI cannot provide statutory capital-escrow attestation, deposit-guarantee protection, or bank-grade correspondent clearing. An offshore digital EMI without a Luxembourg passport is not authorised to hold the SARL's statutory capital or operational treasury balances. Such offshore EMIs should be used at most as a sub-rail for franchisee POS settlement, and only if they operate under a valid EU/EEA passport or an available Luxembourg banking-agency arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital from China or other non-EU shareholders: (1) Open a dedicated 'Capital Escrow / Account' at a Luxembourg commercial bank before RCS registration or, for an already-registered SARL, a designated operational account. (2) The Chinese shareholder executes a share-subscription resolution and transfers funds through SWIFT MT103 using the SARL's IBAN and BIC, with field 70/72 containing 'Equity contribution / SARL capital subscription' clearly identifying the underlying contract. If the remitter is a PRC bank, the Chinese bank will apply SAFE remittance-purpose classification and require prior PRC ODI approval and SAFE registration; the Luxembourg bank does not impose an additional Luxembourg exchange-control code, but requires source-of-funds evidence and the bank's own UBO confirmation. (3) The receiving bank performs KYC screening, confirms no sanctioned-party nexus, verifies the remittance against the subscription schedule, and issues the capital attestation required for notarisation and RCS filing. Following registration, funds may be transferred to the operational treasury account.For outbound trade settlements—central-kitchen machinery, raw materials, packaging, and third-country franchise supplies—use SWIFT MT103 for direct cash payment against invoice when the counterparty accepts open account terms. For larger or non-standard import contracts, a commercial documentary letter of credit issued by the Luxembourg bank via SWIFT MT700 is preferred, with subsequent MT707 amendments and MT754 negotiation/acceptance messages. Settlement for EUR-denominated EU supplier invoices uses SEPA Credit Transfer; extra-EUR payments to China or other third countries clear through the Luxembourg bank's correspondent nostro network via SWIFT. All disbursements must be supported by underlying commercial agreements, invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or logistics proof, and recorded in the BCL balance-of-payments reporting where applicable.Deliverable FX forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, and other freely traded currencies to hedge verified commercial cash-flow exposure; documentation under ISDA or bank master agreement required.Non-deliverable FX forwards (NDFs) referencing mainland CNY or otherwise restricted currencies, permitted only when used as a genuine economic hedge for the SARL's China-linked procurement, royalties, or equity flows and transacted with an authorised OECD or Luxembourg-audited counterparty.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (EUR/CNY or EUR/USD) to hedge long-term financing mismatches among bank term debt, Chinese franchise funding, and operating revenues; documented under ISDA/CSA and subject to EMIR reporting.

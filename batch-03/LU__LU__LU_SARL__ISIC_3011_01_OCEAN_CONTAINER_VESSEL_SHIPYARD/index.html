Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 1-3 Boulevard Royal, L-2449 Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated by the Law of 23 July 2016), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); supplemented by the Grand-Ducal Regulations on the Register of Commerce and on the publication of company acts.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Articles of Association/Statuts) executed by notarial deed or by private writing with authenticated signatures, filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum capital EUR 12,000 (Article 710-5). At incorporation the capital must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of the nominal value of each cash share must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution and remain blocked until registration, as evidenced by the bank's attestation de blocage. The unpaid balance of cash contributions must be fully liberated within five (5) years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up immediately.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum capital EUR 12,000 (Article 710-5). At incorporation the capital must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of the nominal value of each cash share must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution and remain blocked until registration, as evidenced by the bank's attestation de blocage. The unpaid balance of cash contributions must be fully liberated within five (5) years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up immediately.

Category: Autorisation d'établissement (industrial establishment permit) and Autorisation d'établissement classé (classified establishment operating permit) issued under the Law of 19 July 1996 on classified establishments, as amended, and the Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to the professions of craftsman, merchant and industrialist.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for income taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; central business portal: guichet.lu and MyGuichet.Lu

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident enterprise, obtain a Luxembourg business registration number (matricule) via Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) filing service through guichet.lu, then apply for VAT registration with AEDT. Submit: RCS extract, statutes (articles of association), list of managers, power of attorney, identity documents of directors, and proof of EU establishment if applicable. Non-EU entities must appoint an approved VAT representative. The AEDT issues a TVA identification number (LU + 8 digits) typically within 10-15 business days upon complete documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; SARL is a fully taxable corporate entity (opaque) resident in Luxembourg subject to: corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC), and net wealth tax. Territoriality: worldwide profits as a resident entity, mitigated by participation exemption regime on qualifying shareholdings; no sub-national corporate income tax divergences (Unitary_National).

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not allow check-the-box classification elections for SARLs. Available fiscal mechanisms: VAT group election for Luxembourg resident affiliates (article 63 VAT law); joint taxation regime for fully-owning corporate groups under article 164bis IRC (option for integration); and ordinary participation exemption election where statutory requirements are met. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive are directly applicable, limiting USD/LU double-dip structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident taxable persons making supplies in Luxembourg; domestic small-business exemption applies to entities with annual turnover below EUR 30,000 (but a shipyard commercial entity will exceed this, and exports are generally taxable but zero-rated).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of container vessels from Luxembourg to non-EU destinations or shipments to customer within Luxembourg that are then physically transported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Luxembourg VAT law (article 53 VAT law), provided the seller retains proof of export. Related offshore services (e.g., design, engineering, ship management) supplied to non-EU commercial customers may be zero-rated if the foreign business recipient holds a valid foreign VAT ID or adequate documented proof of non-EU status. Bunkering and international transport services are zero-rated under articles 55-57.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic via Luxembourg Customs & Excise, ECS/ICS system)
  • Bill of Lading or International Air/Sea Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payment proof
  • Commercial Invoice referencing 'Export, 0% VAT'
  • Delivery or handover certificate signed by the non-EU buyer
  • Shipyard build contract and end-user/ultimate destination certificate

No currently active real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing/digital stamping for B2B or B2G invoices. E-invoicing is permitted; Luxembourg is implementing mandatory B2B B2G e-invoicing through PEPPOL BIS 3.0 from 2026 (B2G already mandatory from 2024). No API-based clearance requirement exists as of the current stage.

Luxembourg VAT authorities audit export zero-rating strictly. Missing or falsified export evidence leads to retrospective VAT assessment at 17%, punitive interest (0.8% per month, compounded for 6 months per calendar quarter), and fines up to 100% of the tax evaded (article 515/518 LOI). For intentional fraud or abusive passport under EU Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF) coordination, criminal sanctions include imprisonment (up to 5 years) and confiscation. The clawback period is generally 5 years (10 years in case of fraud). Repatriation of foreign currency proceeds beyond 180 days without adequate commercial or force-majeure justification triggers transfer-pricing adjustments, which may result in deemed distribution taxes and additional withholding taxes, but no separate FX-specific statutory fine.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro (EUR) on both current and capital account. Luxembourg applies no exchange-control licensing, no capital-import authorization, and no profit-remittance hurdle; free movement of capital is guaranteed by Articles 63-66 TFEU, EU Directive 88/361/EEC, and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Dividend distributions and shareholder-loan repayments may flow freely to EU and non-EU residents subject only to (i) UN/EU/OFAC sanctions and asset-freeze screening, (ii) CSSF anti-money-laundering and customer-due-diligence verification of the underlying transaction, (iii) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for external transactions, and (iv) the Luxembourg company-law distributability test requiring the SARL to prove positive statutory net assets before any dividend outflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio apply. Luxembourg does not oblige exporters to repatriate foreign currency within any timeline, nor to convert export receipts into euros; shipbuilding and deep-sea vessel export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in USD or any other freely convertible currency in resident or non-resident foreign-currency accounts. The only binding obligation is statistical: resident entities must report each external transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 to the BCL via the relationship bank (or directly where the bank is not intermediary), using the harmonized external-transactions coding for goods exports and identifying the underlying commercial invoice and counterparty.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and, in macroprudential matters, the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW for the core treasury mandate; MEDIUM-LOW for ancillary operational liquidity. Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions supervised by the CSSF under PSD2 may lawfully provide low-value EUR disbursements, payroll, tax, and supplier payments, but cannot issue, confirm, or advise documentary letters of credit, cannot hold client funds as escrow, and cannot extend credit, trade finance, or FX hedging products. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs face an even stricter constraint: CSSF/EBA guidance requires client funds to be booked with a licensed EU credit institution, and correspondent-bank compliance will generally reject payment rails originating from unsupervised digital EMI programmes for maritime instalment flows. Any EMI solution should therefore be confined to non-material operating expenses and must never serve as the transmission rail for instalment LC proceeds or escrow monies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) inbound equity and shareholder loans are routed from the foreign parent via SWIFT MT103/202 to the SARL's account, with ISO 20022 purpose code INVC / CAPITALCONTRIBUTION and the RCS number embedded in structured remittance information; (ii) for transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, the credit line must carry the BCL external-transactions code for direct-investment capital contribution so the receiving bank can effect automatic balance-of-payments reporting; (iii) non-EEA injections will be settled only if the initiating bank supplies complete sanctions-screening and AML remittance data in the message; (iv) the Luxembourg notary or réviseur d'entreprises must certify the capital increase and the amended Articles must be filed at the RCS before the bank will release funds for operational disbursement; (v) for third-country USD injections, the advice should name the beneficiary bank and the purpose code in the MT202 cover leg to avoid correspondent-bank holds; (vi) SEPA credit transfers suffice for EEA-sourced EUR contributions up to EUR 100,000 but SWIFT is preferred for capital-increase transparency; a compliance hold of one to two business days applies to first-injection funds pending source-of-wealth reconciliation. No Ministry of Economy or CSSF approval is required because the Luxembourg capital account is fully open.Root clearing is executed via TARGET2 (T2 RTGS) for EUR legs and via correspondent-bank CHIPS/Fedwire for USD legs, with payment initiation through the bank's host-to-host channel: (i) supplier prepayments and local disbursements are sent as SWIFT MT103 referencing the commercial invoice and the BCL external-transactions code; (ii) maritime instalment collections from international ship-owners are documented under buyer-issued instalment LCs (MT700/MT707 sequence), credited directly to the dedicated escrow sub-account; (iii) pay-away to executing shipyards is made by MT103 or, where contract security requires, by confirmed commercial LC governed by UCP 600 with MT756 reimbursement to the advising bank; (iv) refund guarantees and advance-payment bonds are issued as MT760 standby LCs to the buyer's bank; (v) all outbound disbursements above EUR 12,500 are captured by BCL external-statistics reporting through the relationship bank; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW for the core treasury mandate; MEDIUM-LOW for ancillary operational liquidity. Luxembourg-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions supervised by the CSSF under PSD2 may lawfully provide low-value EUR disbursements, payroll, tax, and supplier payments, but cannot issue, confirm, or advise documentary letters of credit, cannot hold client funds as escrow, and cannot extend credit, trade finance, or FX hedging products. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs face an even stricter constraint: CSSF/EBA guidance requires client funds to be booked with a licensed EU credit institution, and correspondent-bank compliance will generally reject payment rails originating from unsupervised digital EMI programmes for maritime instalment flows. Any EMI solution should therefore be confined to non-material operating expenses and must never serve as the transmission rail for instalment LC proceeds or escrow monies.

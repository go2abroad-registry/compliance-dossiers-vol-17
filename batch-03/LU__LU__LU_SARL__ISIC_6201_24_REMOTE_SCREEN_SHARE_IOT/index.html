Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), in coordination with the Administration des contributions directes (ACD); official portal: Guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL, the company obtains its Luxembourg national registration number and business identification through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) at incorporation. VAT registration is made separately with the AEDT through the MyGuichet/eTVA portal using the standard VAT registration application. A non-established foreign entity supplying taxable services in Luxembourg with no place of business in Luxembourg must register for Luxembourg VAT before commencing such supplies if no reverse charge or OSS mechanism applies; a Luxembourg-established SARL must register when annual taxable turnover excluding VAT exceeds the statutory small-enterprise threshold and may also voluntarily register below that threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (the SARL is regarded as a fully taxable corporate entity subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; it is not fiscally transparent). — For a Luxembourg SARL, the company obtains its Luxembourg national registration number and business identification through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) at incorporation. VAT registration is made separately with the AEDT through the MyGuichet/eTVA portal using the standard VAT registration application. A non-established foreign entity supplying taxable services in Luxembourg with no place of business in Luxembourg must register for Luxembourg VAT before commencing such supplies if no reverse charge or OSS mechanism applies; a Luxembourg-established SARL must register when annual taxable turnover excluding VAT exceeds the statutory small-enterprise threshold and may also voluntarily register below that threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the SARL is regarded as a fully taxable corporate entity subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; it is not fiscally transparent).

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no United States-style check-the-box election. Entity classification follows the legal form under Luxembourg law. Taxable persons may elect VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under the conditions of the Luxembourg VAT law; corporate income tax consolidation/fiscal unity is available for qualifying 95%-owned Luxembourg resident subsidiaries provided the relevant statutory conditions are met. ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover excluding VAT for Luxembourg-established small enterprises; non-established foreign suppliers have no turnover threshold and are registrable from the first taxable transaction unless reverse charge or OSS applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier retains valid proof that the goods left the EU in accordance with Luxembourg export rules. For remote screen sharing and IoT software services supplied to a non-EU business customer, Luxembourg VAT does not apply under the B2B place-of-supply rule (taxable at the customer's jurisdiction), which operates commercially as zero-rating; B2C digital services are subject to the destination-state OSS mechanism rather than the export zero-rating regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and underlying contract/purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement showing receipt of export proceeds

No general real-time electronic invoicing clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control regime is currently in force. Invoices must satisfy EU VAT invoicing requirements; B2G e-invoicing uses the PEPPOL standard where mandated for public procurement suppliers.

Where export zero-rating is improperly claimed, the AEDT may assess the VAT previously zero-rated, add statutory late interest, and impose administrative penalties for incorrect declarations; intentional fraudulent conduct may additionally expose the taxpayer to criminal prosecution under Luxembourg tax and criminal law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full eurozone convertibility with no residual capital or current account exchange controls. The EU Treaty, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and the EU Capital Movements Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC, as implemented) guarantee unrestricted cross-border flows between Member States and third countries, subject only to EU sanctions and BCL statistical reporting obligations. Dividend and profit repatriation are not subject to currency licences or FX authorisation; applicable hurdles are solely tax-related (Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at 15% subject to treaty relief and the Parent-Subsidiary Directive) and standard banking due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender exists in Luxembourg, nor is there any statutory conversion ratio or repatriation deadline. Export receipts may be held in freely convertible EUR or foreign currencies within legally opened bank accounts. Exporters are required to report cross-border transaction data to the BCL for statistical balance-of-payments purposes under the BCL Regulation concerning external statistics; reporting is informational and does not impose exchange control obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); monetary authority and member of the Eurosystem. Foreign exchange and financial-sector conduct supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent AML/CFT supervisor for banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An offshore fintech or electronic money institution (EMI) holding a valid e-money or payment institution licence in the EEA can passport into Luxembourg and open a settlement account at a Luxembourg credit institution or at the BCL (where applicable). Non-EEA EMIs without a passport must rely on a Luxembourg-licensed agent, an EU branch, or a partnership with a local credit institution. Digital EMI-only onboarding is accepted by some Luxembourg banks, but the foreign EMI must demonstrate regulatory substance, compliance with CSSF AML/CFT outsourcing rules, and auditable transaction monitoring. For a low-risk software company collecting via Stripe/Paddle, using an EMI as a collection gateway is viable while the primary treasury account remains at a tier-1 Luxembourg bank to ensure clearing reliability and Local File tax documentation.

Defense evidence: Detailed annual recurring revenue schedule, executed client contracts for remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software, payment processor statements (Stripe/Paddle) showing transaction justification, domestic and EU VAT records, chargeback ratio below 1%, and a written cyber-security policy demonstrating no sanctioned-jurisdiction usage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the SARL's Luxembourg operational account using a BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for capital participation (commonly code 701 for direct equity investment or intercompany loan code 703). The sending institution is required to write the shareholder reference, the nature of the transfer ('equity contribution', 'shareholder loan', 'cash-pool contribution'), and the UBO of the sending entity in Field 70 of the MT103. Simultaneously, the SARL's Luxembourg counsel or accountant must record the contribution in the commercial register and update the annual financial statement; banks typically verify the registration and may request a share certificate or loan agreement before crediting the full amount. Beneficiary account verification is mandatory: the bank will name-match the receiving entity against sanctions and PEP lists, and the transaction is cleared via TARGET2 for EUR or via SWIFT correspondent banks for USD/GBP and other currencies.Outbound disbursements for software reseller commissions, hardware procurement, subcontracting, and licencing fees are cleared primarily through the European TARGET2 system in EUR using SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103 for foreign currencies. For larger or documentary trade transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) opened by the Luxembourg bank provides secure settlement to the supplier's bank, while MT202/MT202COV relays are used for bank-to-bank customer credit transfers. Daily treasury liquidity is controlled through multi-currency accounts at the tier-1 bank, with predefined approval limits, dual-signature mandates, and automated positive-pay reconciliation. Vendor disbursements to non-EU counterparties require a valid OCRA/FATCA self-certification, VAT invoice, and if applicable a certificate of residence to avoid domestic withholding tax; all transfers must include accurate ISO purpose codes and a contractual reference to satisfy the bank's transaction-monitoring obligations.Spot FX transactions for immediate settlement of foreign currency trade payables and receivablesOutright FX forwards (vanilla forwards) with maturity up to 12 months to hedge EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF exposureFX swaps (swap-out/swap-in) for short-term liquidity rebalancing between EUR and major currenciesCross-currency swaps (interest-rate and principal exchange) for longer-dated treasury efficient hedging, subject to EMIR reporting and initial-margin thresholdsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) involving non-convertible or lightly regulated emerging-market currencies, executed only with a licensed counterparty and documented as a true hedge of actual software export or input-procurement exposure

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end, unless an extension is granted by the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes). For a SARL with a calendar fiscal year, the deadline is 31 December of the subsequent year. The tax assessment (bulletin de l'impôt) is issued after review, and provisional payments are generally due quarterly on the 10th of February, May, August, and November.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a (Identification) Legal name, legal form (SARL), registered office address, and national business register number (RCS). Non-resident SARLs with a Luxembourg permanent establishment must provide the local PE address and the tax identification number assigned by the Luxembourg tax authority. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 1b (Corporate Income Tax Base) Total taxable profit comprising Luxembourg-source and worldwide business income attributable to the Luxembourg entity or PE, after commercial-law adjustments. Non-resident entities are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income; include only PE-attributable profits and specific Luxembourg-source items such as certain royalties and real estate rental income. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 2a (Net Wealth Tax Base) Net wealth tax base calculated on the unified taxable net wealth, subject to exemptions and allowances (e.g., the EUR 500,000 allowance for qualifying shareholdings). Non-resident PE assets situated in Luxembourg are included in the Luxembourg net wealth tax base; foreign assets are excluded unless effectively connected to the Luxembourg PE. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 3a (Participation Exemption Claim) Declaration of qualifying shareholdings for which the participation exemption is claimed on dividends and capital gains. Non-resident shareholders are not required to complete Box 3a; the participation exemption applies at the Luxembourg entity or PE level, not to the foreign parent. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 4a (IP Box Deduction) Computation of the 80% IP box deduction on qualifying IP income, subject to nexus ratio calculations. Non-resident entities may claim the IP box only for IP assets effectively connected to a Luxembourg PE and where the underlying R&D, development or enhancement activities or the ownership and management of the IP are performed by the PE. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 5a (International Tax Adjustments) Disclosure of intra-group transactions, transfer pricing adjustments, and any adjustments related to thin-capitalisation or interest limitation rules. Non-resident entities with a Luxembourg PE must file transfer pricing documentation for all PE-attributable intercompany dealings with the foreign head office or affiliates. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 6a (Withholding Tax Certificates) Declaration of withholding taxes borne on Luxembourg-source dividends, interest, and royalties. Non-resident entities must attach TRC and any DTA relief certificates when claiming reduced withholding tax rate treaty benefits. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 7a (Loss Carryforward Election) Election to offset current-year profits against prior-year tax losses carried forward, subject to the 5-year limitation and change-of-control restriction. Losses sustained by a Luxembourg PE of a non-resident entity may be carried forward within the 5-year window, limited to profits deriving from the same PE. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.
Box 8a (Declaration of Treasury Payments) Reporting of distributions, redemption payments, and other profit repatriations to shareholders. Non-resident majority shareholders must identify their country of tax residence and the applicable DTA provision to support reduced dividend WHT relief. Late filing penalties range from EUR 100 to EUR 50,000, with a progressive daily surcharge of 0.6% of the tax due for each commenced month of delay, capped at 10% of the total tax due. Intentional non-filing can trigger a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000 and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to 5 years for the responsible directors.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for intercompany cross-border transactions involving tangible goods, services, royalties, or financial transactions in the aggregate; EUR 1,000,000 for intercompany services or financing transactions where the total compensation exceeds the threshold; however, entities with transactions exceeding EUR 4,500,000 in gross revenue or with related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1,500,000 must keep full Local File documentation. The Luxembourg Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 50,000,000 and has at least one Luxembourg entity exceeding the EUR 1,500,000 related-party transaction threshold.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are fully applicable under Luxembourg's domestic tax framework and are reinforced by the OECD BEPS Actions 8-10, the EU ATAD implementation, and the EU Code of Conduct Group. For a Luxembourg SARL conducting remote desktop access and telemetry software activities, substance documentation must demonstrate that the entity maintains real decision-making power, employs adequate qualified staff (IT engineers, product managers, and technical support personnel) located in Luxembourg, incurs substantive R&D and operational expenditures at a level consistent with the functions and risks assumed, and controls and manages the core income-generating activities from Luxembourg. Mere technical ownership of IP or receiving intercompany service fees without substantial managerial, operational, and financial substance will be challenged under the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and the ATAD preferred-regime provisions, potentially resulting in reclassification of income, denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT), imposition of penalties, and the activation of Luxembourg's controlled foreign corporation (CFC) rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties range from EUR 5,000 to EUR 100,000 per offence and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years, plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month and a penalty of 10% of the evaded tax. In aggravated cases involving cross-border manoeuvres or false documentation, the fine may reach EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the manager (gérant) of the SARL may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they fail to file required returns, knowingly authorise false filings, or misappropriate corporate funds. Under Luxembourg company law and tax law, the corporate veil may be pierced where the SARL is used as a facade or where the manager acted manifestly beyond the corporate interest, leading to joint and several liability for the full tax debt, civil restitution to the tax authority, potential travel bans (interdiction de voyage), a 10-year prohibition on managing a Luxembourg corporate entity, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may recharacterise and reallocate the entire taxable profit to the foreign beneficial owner, disallow the deduction of intercompany payments, apply a 15% non-resident dividend WHT on deemed distributions, and impose a daily surcharge of EUR 1,000 for failure to maintain adequate substance records under the Luxembourg substance-related information exchange framework. Additionally, the entity may be stripped of its tax residence certificate and lose treaty benefits under the OECD Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: The manager and any de facto director can be personally assessed for 100% of the re-determined Luxembourg tax liability, be subject to a civil penalty of EUR 10,000 to EUR 50,000, face travel restrictions to prevent asset dissipation, and may be personally prosecuted for abuse of law (abus de droit) with imprisonment up to 3 years. The Luxembourg tax authority may also obtain a provisional attachment order against the personal bank accounts of directors in cases of fraudulent substance deficiency.
AML / CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML law (Loi du 12 novembre 2004, as amended), fines from EUR 1,250 to EUR 5,000,000 can be imposed on the legal entity and its responsible officers, in addition to administrative sanctions up to EUR 1,000,000 and licence revocation where applicable. The FIU may freeze the entity's bank accounts and the competent supervisory authority (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) can issue a public warning and a prohibition to exercise certain commercial activities.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the entity's AML fines, damage claims by third parties, and in cases of gross negligence or intentional conduct, may face imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years. Personal travel bans may be imposed during the investigation and the directors may be disqualified from serving as an administrator or manager of any Luxembourg regulated entity for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Cross-Border Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration is empowered under Article 56bis LIR to perform primary and secondary transfer pricing adjustments, add back excessive deductions, and apply a 5% late-payment interest charge on the additional assessment. A negligence penalty of EUR 250 to EUR 25,000 may be imposed where the documentation is absent or defective; in cases of gross negligence, the penalty rises to 50% of the additional assessed tax plus late-payment interest. The entity also faces potential double taxation absent mutual agreement procedure (MAP) relief and mandatory disclosure of reportable cross-border arrangements under DAC6.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns containing manipulated transfer pricing are personally liable for the resulting additional tax, penalties, and interest, and can be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion if the manipulation is knowing and intentional. The court may impose civil sanctions including a temporary ban on serving as a director of a Luxembourg company and a reimbursement order for the benefit improperly obtained from the mispriced transactions.

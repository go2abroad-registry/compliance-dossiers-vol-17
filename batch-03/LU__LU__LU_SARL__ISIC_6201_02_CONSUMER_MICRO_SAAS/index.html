Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); e-filing portal: lodge.lbr.lu; registry office: 8-10, rue Henri M. Schnadt, L-2530 Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (amended Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies), as subsequently amended; Articles 710-1 through 710-26 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée regime), Article 179 (publication in the RESA and legal personality), and the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 11 November 2015 on the Register of Commerce and Companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation) with annexed statuts (articles of association), submitted electronically by the notary via the coordinated RCS filing form (Formulaire B / e-BR).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for the SARL is EUR 12,000 under Article 710-1(2) of the amended Law of 10 August 1915. Capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be blocked on a company-in-formation account opened with a Luxembourg credit institution, and the notary must obtain the bank's blocked-funds certificate. In-kind contributions must be valued in a report of an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé). Transfers of shares to non-shareholders require consent of shareholders holding at least three-quarters of the capital, subject to any stricter clause in the statutes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; all procedures accessible via MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation, the notary files the SARL with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL), obtaining the corporate matricule (national identification number which serves as the corporate TIN). For VAT, file registration form 640 with the AEDT before commencing taxable activity; the VAT number is issued in the format LU + 8 digits. A non-established foreign supplier of digital services may register directly for VAT under form 640 without an RCSL establishment, or alternatively make use of the OSS mechanism in its EU member state of establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a capital company fully taxable on Luxembourg-source taxable income, subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune); pass-through treatment is not available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Qualifying Luxembourg groups may elect fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for corporate income and municipal business tax purposes for a minimum five-year period. Participation exemption applies to qualifying dividends and capital gains. Hybrid mismatch limitations under ATAD apply and no elective hybrid entity classification is available under Luxembourg domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for domestic small-business franchise exemption; mandatory VAT registration required above EUR 35,000. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to Luxembourg non-taxable persons have no threshold and are registrable from the first euro unless using the EU OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT where the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days and the supplier retains adequate transport and customs evidence. Cross-border services to non-EU customers are generally outside Luxembourg VAT scope where place-of-supply rules seat the supply outside the EU; B2B services to EU business customers are reverse-charged, and B2C digital services are taxed at the customer's location. Digital cross-border subscription revenue therefore does not ordinarily rely on Luxembourg zero-rating but on place-of-supply and outside-scope treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU or electronic AES export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of arrival or customs clearance in the destination country (recommended supplementary evidence)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control regime exists for standard B2B VAT invoices. Invoices must comply with EU VAT Directive Articles 226–236 and be issued within one month of supply. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only to public procurement contracts via the PEPPOL/EN 16931 network. Future obligations may arise from the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative.

Zero-rating claims are subject to documentary audit by the AEDT. Failure to substantiate export status triggers VAT reassessment at 17%, default interest, administrative fines up to 100% of the evaded tax, and, in cases of deliberate fraud, criminal prosecution of directors. Any VAT refund already paid may be clawed back together with interest, and directors or persons knowingly participating in a false zero-rating claim may incur joint liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists for both capital and current account transactions under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-65 TFEU). No exchange control approvals are required for inbound investment, profit remittance, or dividend distribution. Cross-border payments and financial transactions above EUR 12,500 are subject to BCL statistical balance-of-payments reporting. A 15% domestic dividend withholding tax applies unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty; the Luxembourg participation exemption and treasury simplification may apply. For micro-SaaS subscription revenue, the receipts qualify as current-account services income, which is freely remittable without central bank consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion deadlines, and no requirement to maintain a minimum share of foreign currency receipts. Export earnings may be retained in original currency or converted at the holder's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision, payment systems, and AML/CFT enforcement.

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Luxembourg SARL, shareholder equity or convertible loan capital should be remitted via SWIFT MT103/MT202 using a BCL-recognised bank reference. Payment purpose field must state 'Share capital contribution / participation - SARL incorporation' or 'Subordinated shareholder loan - investment'. The company must retain a notarised share subscription spreadsheet and statutory accounts. For statistical reporting, use BCL balance-of-payments category D1 (direct investment) — 'Equity capital' or 'Intercompany debt (loan)' — whichever applies. Verification in Luxembourg: shareholder bank statement, SWIFT confirmation, and the notarial deed establishing the SARL. For loan injections, an arm's-length loan agreement and CSSF-compliant interest rate terms are required to avoid thin capitalisation recategorisation.Clearing rails for outbound supplier and operational payments rely on SEPA Credit Transfers (EUR) within the EEA, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR or third-currency settlement. For Paddle and Lemon Squeezy trade settlements, the standard rail is SWIFT/SEPA payout from the merchant of record to the SARL's Luxembourg bank, usually in EUR. Commercial letters of credit (documentary L/C) are not relevant for micro-SaaS subscription revenue; they only apply to physical goods trade. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined CIT 17% + solidarity surcharge 7% on CIT + municipal business tax ~6.75% in Luxembourg City)

15% (reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if holding ≥10% for 12 months; treaty rates often 5% or 0% for substantial shareholdings)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million group turnover (Master File and Local File required); CbC report for groups with consolidated revenue > EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in IP, financing, and holding activities. For a micro SaaS company, substance is evidenced by local management, employees, and operational decision-making. Failure to demonstrate substance may lead to reclassification as a shell company and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional tax penalties of 10% to 200% of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 15% WHT on deemed distributions; fines up to €100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes due if they fail to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML/CFT Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for entities; imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; civil liability for damages.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of the adjustment, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing.

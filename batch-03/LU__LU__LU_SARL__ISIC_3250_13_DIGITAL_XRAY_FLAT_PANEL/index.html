Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum share capital of a Luxembourg SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid-up in cash at formation. The capital must be placed in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or held by the notary as escrow; funds may be released only after the RCS has registered the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Corps Grand-Ducal d’Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS); structural load verification for cleanroom floors, radiation shielding, and equipment installations by a certified engineering bureau; and occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including workplace radiation and chemical exposure monitoring.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for a Luxembourg SARL; it is an opaque entity taxed at corporate level. Available elections: VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for closely related entities; tax consolidation for corporate income tax (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for at least 95% held resident subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available but anti-hybrid rules are applied under EU Directive 2016/1164

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies where the supplier proves that goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU within 3 months of supply, or that services fall under Article 45 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (directly connected to export, international transport, or services to non-EU persons). Generally, the recipient must be a non-EU person or the goods must leave EU customs territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory electronic invoicing for B2G invoices to Luxembourg public authorities; invoices must be transmitted via Peppol BIS 3.0 or the Luxembourg e-SPOC platform. No real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority reporting, or digital stamping applies to B2B/B2C invoices. All e-invoices must be archived for 6 years.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment, late interest of 10% per annum, and monetary penalties under Article 72 of the Luxembourg VAT Law generally ranging from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000. Criminal prosecution applies in severe cases with fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; the standard assessment window extends to 10 years in cases of VAT fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as part of the Eurozone and the EU, applies full convertibility on both capital and current account transactions under Articles 63 and 64 TFEU. There is no general exchange control withholding or repatriation restriction. Profit remittances are freely permitted, subject to corporate tax residence verification, compliance with AML/KYC, and absence of EU/UN sanctions. Dividend distributions require a shareholders' meeting approval, audited annual accounts, and registered notarial/deed filings; no government license or conversion ceiling applies. Certain routine statistical reporting to the BCL applies for transactions exceeding thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion, or liquidation timeline applies to export proceeds in Luxembourg. Exporters may retain and use any foreign-currency earnings. However, cross-border payments and related foreign-currency balances must be reportable to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistical purposes under ECB Regulation (EU) No 53/2021 and Luxembourg's national reporting requirements (BCL Circulars). Reporting obligations typically apply to transactions above EUR 12,500, and must identify the economic purpose and counterparty. No minimum conversion ratio is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as central bank; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and payment services, while the BCL administers balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate under a strict EU licensing framework. Only EMIs holding an authorisation in Luxembourg or passporting under the EU Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) may provide comprehensive payment services. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in third countries are subject to full CSSF licensing or passport acquisition, making them commercially viable only if they operate through a European branch or appoint a Luxembourg-licensed local partner. For dedicated cross-border liquidity, a licensed EMI without banking status cannot hold customer funds as deposits; therefore, a full credit institution remains necessary for capital escrow and multi-day clearing buffers. Thus, the viability rating is 'Medium' for supplementary payment infrastructure, but 'Low' as the primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT SEPA/EUR wire or SWIFT MT103 from the parent or third-party investor. The remittance instruction must include: (i) exactly the LU_SARL corporate legal name and account number, (ii) a stated purpose code corresponding to 'equity injection', e.g. ECB Balance of Payments purpose code PRO21 (Equity Capital) or NARR 'Transfer of Equity for Commercial Investment', (iii) the corporate registration number and Luxembourg tax ID where available, and (iv) a counterparty country code and full legal name. Banks will typically execute a 'know-your-transaction' check confirming the receipt falls within the registered business object of manufacturing of medical imaging equipment. For amounts above EUR 10,000, the remitter's source-of-funds documentation is transmitted under CCP/CIF guidelines. The Luxembourg entity must file the corresponding capital increase at the Trade and Companies Register (RCS) if shares are newly issued.Primary clearing rails for trade settlements are SWIFT MT103 for direct high-value supplier wires and commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for delayed payment or documentary assurance. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group turnover triggers mandatory Master File; Luxembourg Local File is mandatory for entities in such groups and otherwise demands ACD-requested documentation when intercompany transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 in a tax year.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not administer GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is nevertheless compulsory through the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, the general anti-abuse rule, transfer-pricing documentation, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and administrative substance guidance for holding and IP companies (Circular L.G.-A n° 50/90). A shell register entry or economically insignificant operation risks denial of treaty benefits and taxable reclassification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 1,000,000 (or up to twice the evaded tax), imprisonment from 1 to 5 years; administrative reassessment with 100% uplift in certain cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved false filings are jointly liable for evaded tax; personal asset seizure, travel bans, and custodial sentences are enforceable.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of participation exemption, treaty benefits, and EU directives; retrospective recharacterization of transactions; GAAR penalty up to 10% of adjusted tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil, holding directors personally accountable for the entire reassessed tax if they orchestrated the sham; travel restrictions may be imposed pending restitution.
AML / Financial Integrity

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual total turnover under Luxembourg AML law; suspension of statutory license for manufacture of medical devices.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, disqualification from management, and asset seizure; severe cases trigger criminal liability and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment to taxable income, secondary penalty of 10% of the resulting additional CIT, monthly interest on underpaid tax, and delisting from Luxembourg manufacturing incentive regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instructed erroneous TP policies are personally liable for civil restitution of mispriced profits and may face criminal penalties if the action is deemed fraudulent; travel bans can be ordered during extradition or collection proceedings.

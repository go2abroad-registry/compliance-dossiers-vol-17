Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard S.à r.l. requires a statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 442-1(1), as amended), fully subscribed and fully paid in cash or in kind at formation. The capital must be deposited in escrow in a blocked subscription account ('compte de souscription bloqué') opened with a CSSF-regulated credit institution in the name of the future company; the funds may be released only after RCS registration of the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety approval from the communal fire-prevention commission or the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for the warehouse; structural load inspection certificate issued by a licensed engineer; workplace health-and-safety clearance under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 June 2003 by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM); and sanitary approval of the premises by ALVA in accordance with Regulation (EC) 852/2004.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard S.à r.l. requires a statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 442-1(1), as amended), fully subscribed and fully paid in cash or in kind at formation. The capital must be deposited in escrow in a blocked subscription account ('compte de souscription bloqué') opened with a CSSF-regulated credit institution in the name of the future company; the funds may be released only after RCS registration of the company.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For direct taxation: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), managed via the Luxembourg government portal www.guichet.lu. For indirect taxation and VAT registration: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), also accessed through www.guichet.lu and the RCS (Luxembourg Business Registers) single digital gateway.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering a Luxembourg SARL must first complete incorporation through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) and obtain a Luxembourg register number and LUID number. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu or the prescribed paper form, providing statutory identification, registered office, activity codes (ISIC/NACE), anticipated turnover, and bank details. Upon review, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg national VAT identification number (numéro de TVA), typically within one month. Non-resident entities with no Luxembourg establishment but engaged in taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AEDT or use the non-EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) / Union OSS where applicable. The same registration procedure is required before the entity can issue valid electronic invoices, claim input VAT, or conduct intra-community or export transactions with VAT identification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing or registering a Luxembourg SARL must first complete incorporation through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) and obtain a Luxembourg register number and LUID number. For VAT purposes, the entity must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu or the prescribed paper form, providing statutory identification, registered office, activity codes (ISIC/NACE), anticipated turnover, and bank details. Upon review, the AEDT issues a Luxembourg national VAT identification number (numéro de TVA), typically within one month. Non-resident entities with no Luxembourg establishment but engaged in taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AEDT or use the non-EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) / Union OSS where applicable. The same registration procedure is required before the entity can issue valid electronic invoices, claim input VAT, or conduct intra-community or export transactions with VAT identification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. A SARL is hereby an opaque, entity-level taxable company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICA), and net wealth tax (ISF). Available statutory elections include: (i) an option to form a VAT group per Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, which treats group members as a single taxable person; (ii) an election for tax consolidation (fiscal unity) under Article 164bis LIR for fully taxable resident companies; (iii) the option to apply the Luxembourg participation exemption regime for qualifying dividends and capital gains, subject to holding conditions; and (iv) elections regarding the application of ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, including the option to align treatment with a foreign branch or hybrid instrument where Luxembourg domestic law transposing EU Directive 2017/952 permits a balancing adjustment. No other pass-through election is available for a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excluding VAT) for the small enterprise exemption; registration is mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds this threshold, unless the entity voluntarily registers. Additional thresholds apply to distance sales of goods and intra-community acquisitions in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Luxembourg VAT-registered supplier may zero-rate (0% VAT) the supply of goods physically exported from the customs territory of the European Union to a destination outside the EU, including specialty green coffee beans, provided all applicable conditions are met: (i) the goods are dispatched or transported out of the European Union by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier; (ii) the departure is evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration (SAD) and, where applicable, proof of removal from the EU; and (iii) the supplier maintains full documentary substantiation in accordance with Article 243(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Law and the VAT Implementing Regulation (EU) No 282/2011. For services directly related to exported goods, or services supplied to non-EU customers, zero-rating or outside-scope rules may apply under standard place-of-supply provisions. The export zero-rating claim must be completed within the timeframe of 90 days from the time of supply, and the goods must be removed from the EU within that same period; otherwise VAT becomes chargeable at the standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD) accepted and stamped by the customs office, or electronic export declaration through the Luxembourg ECS system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving transportation of the goods outside the European Union
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Luxembourg supplier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or other bank payment evidence showing settlement of the export sale proceeds
  • Packing list and transport documents (CMR, sea waybill, air consignment note)
  • Proof of export within the required 90-day removal period, such as customs exit confirmation and, if applicable, documentary evidence from the destination country customs

Luxembourg does not currently impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping obligation for B2B invoicing. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing is required for supplies to Luxembourg central government entities, generally through the PEPPOL BIS network or a Luxembourg public sector e-invoicing platform. For all other invoices, structured electronic invoicing (e.g., EN16931/UBL) is acceptable but not mandatory. There is no requirement for pre-clearance via government API for B2B commercial transactions, including export sales of green coffee beans.

The Luxembourg VAT authorities and the customs administration may perform stringent documentary and physical audits of export zero-rated supplies. If the exporter fails to produce valid export evidence, or where the export declaration is found to be fraudulent, the tax administration will reassess the VAT at the standard rate (17%) and issue a corrective invoice subject to late payment interest. Penalties include the cancellation of the zero-rated treatment, collection of the under-declared VAT, a fine of up to the amount of evaded tax, and in cases of intentional fraud, criminal prosecution before Luxembourg courts with potential imprisonment and additional administrative penalties. Retrospective clawbacks may apply to reopened assessments within the standard three-year limitation period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or wilful attempt to evade tax. To preserve entitlement to zero-rating, the exporter must retain for at least ten years all supporting customs and transactional documentation evidencing physical removal of the goods from the EU.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a Eurozone member state, Luxembourg applies the EU free movement of capital regime; no exchange controls exist for current or capital account transactions; profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate governance and tax compliance (withholding tax considerations on dividends), and no surrender or conversion requirements are imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely; only standard anti-money laundering / anti-terrorist financing reporting thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for Eurosystem matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility: EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport into Luxembourg under the EU e-Money Directive and PSD2; however, due to the trade operations (documentary LCs, FX needs) and the requirement for a stable Luxembourg nexus, a traditional licensed credit institution is strongly preferred; EMIs cannot offer full trade finance products.

15% on gross dividends paid by a Luxembourg SARL, unless exemption or treaty relief applies; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders holding a 10% participation for 12 unbroken months and under Luxembourg participation-exemption conditions

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Due by 31 March of the year following the fiscal year for calendar-year companies (within 90 days of fiscal year-end for non-calendar-year companies); extensions are available on request, normally to 30 September, when the return is filed electronically by a registered tax adviser

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/Master File must be maintained when the Luxembourg entity's related-party goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000, or other controlled transactions (services, interest, royalties, financial dealings) exceed EUR 1,000,000; Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when the consolidated group revenue is above EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime as found in UAE/offshore centres, but the ACD enforces substantive activity through OECD/ATAD anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership tests, and transfer-pricing substance assessments. A specialty green-coffee SARL must maintain local employees, premises, commercial decision-making, and risk management functions to benefit from treaty or parent-subsidiary relief; inadequate substance will cause treaty-benefit denial, reclassification of distributions, and penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years under the Luxembourg General Tax Law; civil assessment of the evaded tax, late interest, and an evasion penalty of 20%–100% of the tax evaded

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns and act contrary to the company's interests are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax; conviction results in civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment
Shell company / treaty abuse / artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under GAAR, denial of treaty benefits and parent-subsidiary relief, assessment of full domestic tax at 18.19% plus 15% dividend withholding tax on deemed distributions, and material-misstatement penalties of 5%–15%

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate a purely artificial Luxembourg entity face personal tax assessments, administrative restraining orders, restitution of taxes/interest/penalties, and repeated abuse may result in travel bans and extradition proceedings
Anti-money laundering / beneficial ownership misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1,250,000 under Luxembourg AML laws (Law of 12 November 2004), sequestration and freezing of funds, imprisonment up to five years for money laundering, and delisting from the register of beneficial owners

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to criminal prosecution, personal monetary fines measured by the laundered amount, travel bans, restrictions on bank account signatory authority, and disqualification as directors of Luxembourg financial institutions

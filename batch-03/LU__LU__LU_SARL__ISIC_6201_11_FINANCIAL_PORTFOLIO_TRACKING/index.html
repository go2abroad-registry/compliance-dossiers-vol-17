Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 250 Route d'Esch, L-1471 Luxembourg; electronic portal: https://www.lbr.lu; filings are transmitted by the notary through LBR's e-filing service.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, particularly SARL Articles 180-1 to 180-29 (modernised by the Law of 23 December 2016 on the reform of the SARL), together with Articles 40-58 on general company formation; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (Loi du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés), Articles 9, 18 and 19; Grand-Ducal Regulation on RESA publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif / Statuts) authenticated by a Luxembourg notary, followed by the formal RCS registration filing (Demande d'inscription au Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) and legal publication of an excerpt in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 36 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Following the Law of 23 December 2016, the minimum share capital of a SARL is EUR 1, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash before execution of the notarial deed. Luxembourg law imposes no mandatory escrow account; however, notarial practice requires a banker's certificate evidencing that the full capital has been irrevocably credited to the SARL's account. Contributions in kind are permitted but require a report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) and full transfer of the asset at formation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate (attestation de sécurité) from the Commune of the registered office after inspection by the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) under the amended Law of 17 June 1994 on fire and rescue services, including verified escape routes, smoke detection and extinguisher coverage; structural load compliance confirmed through the Commune's building-permit file. Workplace clearance requires registration with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) and compliance with the Law of 17 June 1994 on occupational safety and health, covering electrical safety, first-aid equipment, workstation ergonomics and, where applicable, a staff safety delegate.

Paid-in capital rule: Following the Law of 23 December 2016, the minimum share capital of a SARL is EUR 1, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash before execution of the notarial deed. Luxembourg law imposes no mandatory escrow account; however, notarial practice requires a banker's certificate evidencing that the full capital has been irrevocably credited to the SARL's account. Contributions in kind are permitted but require a report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) and full transfer of the asset at formation.

Ministry of the Economy — Direction générale de l'industrie (Ministère de l'Économie, 19-21 boulevard Royal, L-2449 Luxembourg); not applicable to ISIC 6201 pure software and cloud services, which are liberal professions outside the Law of 2 September 2011 on business establishment authorization.

Category: Autorisation d'établissement (Business Establishment Authorization) under the Law of 2 September 2011 — not required for the target ICT activity; the company operates solely upon RCS registration, the AEDT national number and ordinary municipal premises compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; official portals: guichet.lu, myguichet.lu, and impotsdirects.public.lu; RCSL (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) for business registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL or register a Luxembourg branch/PE with the RCSL through a notary or registered agent, thereby obtaining the 11-digit national identification number (matricule) which serves as the corporate TIN for direct tax purposes. Step 2 — File VAT registration with the AEDT (form 563) via myguichet.lu to obtain a VAT ID in the format 'LU' + 8 digits; provide constitutional documents, proof of economic activity, director identity documents, and a signed power of attorney. Step 3 — Non-EU applicants must appoint an AEDT-approved fiscal representative (représentant responsable solidaire) and may be required to post a bank guarantee; EU applicants may register directly. Step 4 — Mandatory electronic filing via the Luxembourg e-tax portal; processing generally completes within 1–2 weeks. Step 5 — Register with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) if any employees are hired.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; Luxembourg applies substance-over-form classification. Available elections include: (i) group taxation (intégration fiscale) effective for corporate income tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, allowing a dominant company to consolidate 95%-held Luxembourg subsidiaries with unanimous consent; (ii) participation exemption, automatic upon satisfaction of the 10% or EUR 6 million acquisition cost test, 12-month holding period, and subject to re-sale/substance conditions (exempts dividends and capital gains with no specific election but conditions must be evidenced); (iii) option for the 50% exemption on qualifying dividend income; (iv) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) election for closely linked entities; (v) advance pricing agreements and upfront VAT confirmation requests with the ACD/AEDT. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2 (Articles 168quinquies and 168sexies LIR) override any elective mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for established taxable persons — every economic activity in Luxembourg triggers VAT registration from the first euro; domestic small-business exemption under Article 57 of the VAT Code applies to turnover up to EUR 30,000 (harmonised EU SME scheme per Directive 2020/285 applicable from 1 January 2025); intra-EU cross-border distance sales and B2C e-services benefit from the EUR 10,000 OSS/Union threshold before the place of supply shifts to the customer's member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under Article 54 of the VAT Code provided dispatch/transport outside the EU is completed within 3 months. For SaaS financial portfolio tracking services: (i) B2B services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply at the customer's establishment per Article 14(4) VAT Code), effectively 0%; (ii) B2C e-services to non-EU consumers are outside the scope where the consumer's usual residence is outside the EU; (iii) intra-EU B2C e-services are taxed at the customer's local rate via the OSS scheme (Union scheme), with zero-rating available only for non-EU supplies; (iv) services ancillary to exported goods are zero-rated. Substantiation evidence includes contracts identifying the non-EU customer, customer local VAT/registration ID, IP/geolocation logs for B2C transactions, service delivery records, and routing of payment to a non-EU bank. Note: sales through Apple IAP may treat Apple as seller of record, w i t h VAT handled by Apple; the SARL must confirm contractual billing terms to determine its own VAT filing exposure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EAAD/IM-Export system) — required only for physical goods; for intangible SaaS services substitute the commercial contract, statements of work, and non-EU customer due diligence file
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or verified service delivery log evidencing physical removal from the EU (goods) or remote service provisioning (SaaS)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — not a Luxembourg statutory document; replace with bank/SWIFT/SEPA transfer evidence, payment gateway settlement records, and the AEDT-prescribed proof-of-payment register

No general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, API digital reporting, or digital stamping regime applies in Luxembourg. Structured electronic invoicing (Peppol BIS/UBL) is mandatory only for B2G invoices in public procurement, in force since 2019. For B2B/B2C, the VAT Code requires sequential invoice numbering, customer VAT ID where applicable, complete service/goods description, and invoice issuance within one month of the supply. The EU ViDA package (mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU supplies) is expected to apply from 1 January 2030 and is not yet implemented.

Luxembourg imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation obligation; the 180-day figure is a conservative internal treasury benchmark. Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT reassessment within the 5-year limitation period (10 years for fraud), monthly late-interest of 0.6%, and a 10% late-payment surcharge. Incorrect zero-rating claims cause clawback of input VAT credits plus administrative fines under Articles 55–59 of the VAT Code (range EUR 10–10,000, or 10%–100% of the evaded tax). Criminal tax fraud under Articles 61–65 is punishable with additional fines up to EUR 10,000 and imprisonment; the principal and its Luxembourg fiscal representative are jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a Eurozone member, guarantees full current and capital account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. No exchange control authorisation is required for profit remittance, dividend repatriation, repayment of shareholder loans, or liquidation proceeds. The SARL may freely hold and disburse EUR and foreign-currency accounts at Luxembourg banks. Dividends are generally subject to a 15% domestic withholding tax, which is eliminated or reduced under applicable tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, provided the participation exemption or holding requirements are met. Any restrictions arise exclusively from EU/UN targeted financial sanctions, CSSF AML/CFT enforcement, and FATF-related anti-money laundering screening, not from currency convertibility rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and service providers may retain receipts in the original currency. Residents are only required to report cross-border flows and external positions to the BCL for balance-of-payments and international investment position statistics, in accordance with BCL reporting circulars and Regulation (EC) No 2157/1999 as implemented nationally. No minimum repatriation percentage applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions passported into Luxembourg under PSD2; Low for non-EEA offshore EMIs that lack a Luxembourg or EU passport. Any EMI-based treasury or clearing solution must hold e-money authorisation under the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 2019 on payment and e-money services, or benefit from an EEA passport, and must comply with safeguarding duties, client-fund segregation, and AML/CFT rules supervised by the CSSF. Unlicensed offshore EMIs may not legally provide payment services to residents or establish collection mechanisms in Luxembourg. The SARL may nonetheless use a foreign EMI for yield or payment orchestration on a reverse-solicited basis only where the service is not marketed in Luxembourg and the LMSF risk review concludes that the foreign EMI's authorisation aligns with EU equivalence rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a LU_SARL inbound capital injection: (i) execute a SWIFT MT103 wire for USD/GBP or a SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) for EUR from the foreign parent/investor to the Luxembourg bank account of the SARL; (ii) include the full legal name 'LU_SARL', the IBAN, BIC of the receiving Luxembourg bank, and the reference 'Capital contribution - Shareholders Agreement dated [date]'; (iii) state an appropriate ISO 20022 external purpose code, e.g., 'EQUI' for equity contributions, 'LOAN' for shareholder advances, or 'COST' for paid-in capital and notarised company costs; (iv) annex to the transfer or provide to the bank the notarised capital contribution deed, the shareholders agreement, and UBO source-of-wealth documentation. No BCL exchange approval is required; the bank verifies identity, source of funds, and entire relevant sanctions exposure.Outbound settlements are cleared primarily via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EU/EEA trade liabilities and via SWIFT MT103 for non-EU open-account trade disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 annual aggregate related-party transactions generally triggers the duty to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (local file/master file); financial-related-party transactions may reach the EUR 2,000,000 tier; CbC report required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an ESR-jurisdiction with a standalone offshore economic substance regime, but tax-resident companies must present real substance (offices, full-time staff, actual decision-making in Luxembourg) to claim treaty and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits; the Luxembourg tax administration and courts apply substance-over-form, GAAR, and ATAD-inspired anti-abuse rules to deny benefits to shell and letterbox companies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Omission

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus monthly late-payment interest (0.8% per month), penalty of up to 10% of the understated tax for failure to declare, and, in criminal tax fraud cases, a fine up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years under Luxembourg tax and criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns are personally exposed to criminal fines, imprisonment, disqualification from Luxembourg board mandates for up to 10 years, and, if a flight risk is shown, a Schengen-area travel/exit ban; civil restitution and bankruptcy clawback may pierce the Sàrl veil when assets were diverted through the company.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and EU Directive benefits; ACD recharacterizes the structure, assesses the entire profit at 24.94% combined Luxembourg rate, imposes a 10% tax penalty, and may apply anti-abuse penalties of up to 25% of the additional tax; the company may be struck off or wound up for non-genuine activity.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for dividends paid out of artificially created profits if the corporate veil is pierced; criminal prosecution for forgery and tax fraud where the shell routing was knowingly executed can lead to imprisonment, fines, and an exit/travel ban in pending criminal proceedings.
AML / Unregistered Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg AML law (Law of 12 November 2004 as amended), administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money laundering penalties up to 5 years’ imprisonment and fines up to EUR 1,250,000; confiscation of laundered property.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly deploy the platform to launder funds are criminally liable; assets may be frozen, European Arrest Warrants may be issued for cross-border flight, directors face imprisonment and travel bans, and the Sàrl’s limited liability will not shield intentional criminal acts.
Transfer Pricing / Sham Intercompany Financing

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment by the Luxembourg tax authorities plus secondary adjustment (reclassifying excess deductions as hidden dividends), late-payment interest of 0.8% per month and a 10%–25% transfer pricing penalty; repeated deceptive documentation can be prosecuted as tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve sham transfer pricing contracts are jointly and severally liable for tax deficiencies arising from deliberately fraudulent acts; criminal conviction may include imprisonment, personal fines, disqualification from corporate office, and a travel ban pending investigation.

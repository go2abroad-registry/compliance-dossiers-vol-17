Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Luxembourg Business Registers and RCS registration rules; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) containing the articles of association, registered with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 50 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard SARL minimum capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before RCS registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the company's name, and the bank certificate must be delivered to the notary; in-kind contributions require valuation by an approved statutory auditor when required by law. Disbursement is permitted only after legal personality is acquired through RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification for the registered office or leased shore premises issued by the competent commune; structural load inspection and workplace health-and-safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM); and civil-defense no-objection for any premises designated as a classified establishment.

Paid-in capital rule: Standard SARL minimum capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before RCS registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the company's name, and the bank certificate must be delivered to the notary; in-kind contributions require valuation by an approved statutory auditor when required by law. Disbursement is permitted only after legal personality is acquired through RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and estate duties; Administration des douanes et accises (Customs and Excise Administration) for export customs control. Unified taxpayer portal: MyGuichet.lu / eCDF (electronic tax filing service).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL: (1) incorporate the SARL via notarial deed before the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR) and obtain registered address; (2) the 13-digit national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule) is automatically generated at RCS registration and serves as the primary tax file reference; (3) apply to the AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the form LU + 8 digits, after identifying the effective start date of taxable supplies and appointing if required a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative for non-EU operators; (4) ensure real economic presence (local substance) including a Luxembourg office, board of managers, bank account, and statutory records; (5) activate e-access to MyGuichet/eCDF for electronic filing, payments, and official correspondence. A non-resident entity with no Luxembourg establishment performing taxable B2B telecommunication/digital services or cross-border supplies may register directly via the Mini One-Stop Shop/OSS without a local VAT number, while classic exports and imports require full clearance.

A non-resident entity with no Luxembourg establishment performing taxable B2B telecommunication/digital services or cross-border supplies may register directly via the Mini One-Stop Shop/OSS without a local VAT number, while classic exports and imports require full clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box entity classification election; an SARL is per se a taxable corporation for Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and municipal business tax. Available elections relevant to tax integration and VAT are: (a) fiscal unity election (intégration fiscale) among resident groups holding directly or indirectly at least 95% of the subsidiary’s shares and at least 95% of voting rights; (b) election to form a Luxembourg VAT group (assujetti unique) under Art. 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Act for financially, economically and organisationally interconnected resident persons; (c) limited option for partnerships to elect corporate taxation under Luxembourg domestic law, not applicable to an SARL; (d) hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective and are corrected mandatorily under Luxembourg’s implementation of ATAD 1/ATAD 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for resident taxable persons exercising the small business franchise exemption (assujetti obligatoire once threshold exceeded); non-established foreign suppliers have no general turnover threshold and must register from their first taxable transaction in Luxembourg; cross-border B2C digital and distance sales are handled through EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) procedures without requiring a Luxembourg domestic VAT ID.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Luxembourg VAT zero-rating applies to: (i) physical export of goods sold by the SARL to a non-EU customer where the goods leave the EU in unchanged state and the export is evidenced by a clearance-ready export declaration; and (ii) international deep-sea RoRo vehicle-carrier transport and directly related services (ocean freight, handling, lashing, port services, terminal charges) supplied for the direct needs of seagoing vessels on international routes to non-EU destinations, which qualify as international transport/services with full input VAT deduction. Services performed wholly within Luxembourg or domestic cabotage remain subject to the standard VAT rate. Eligibility requires valid commercial contracts, Bill of Lading, export packing list, proof of non-EU loading/discharge, and a demonstrable nexus between the invoiced supply and the international voyage.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration with Movement Reference Number) accepted by Luxembourg Customs proving the physical exit of goods from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (RoRo ocean bill) evidencing that the vehicle-carrying vessel loaded at an EU port and discharged at a destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT MT103 / bank credit confirmation showing export proceeds remitted from the non-resident charterer or buyer within the statutory settlement period

Luxembourg mandates electronic invoicing for B2G transactions through the PEPPOL network; there is no general real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control (CTC), no official digital stamping or API-based invoice clearance for domestic B2B transactions. VAT returns, recapitulative statements (EC sales lists), and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via MyGuichet/eCDF. Taxpayers must retain invoice files in a structured electronic format and Treasury/ERP integrations must support auditable XML/UBL/PEPPOL traces for cross-border and export transactions.

Luxembourg tax and customs authorities may audit export zero-rating claims for the standard limitation period (5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or omission). If the export status is not substantiated with the required customs movement evidence, ocean Bill of Lading, and currency inward remittance proof, the supply is recharacterised as a taxable domestic supply and VAT at 17% is assessed, together with late interest. Administrative fines range from 10% to 100% of the undeclared VAT depending on gravity, while deliberate fraud may attract criminal penalties, imprisonment for managing officers, and unlimited joint liability for the VAT fiscal representative. Claimed export refunds are clawed back, input VAT credits on the export costs are disallowed, and customs records may be retrospectively invalidated for false or incomplete declarations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account operations. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control licensing, no prior-authorisation requirement, and no transfer or remittance caps on cross-border flows. Equity injections, shareholder loans, freight receipts, profit distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred freely. Operational constraints are limited to: (i) customer due diligence by credit institutions under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing; (ii) EU, UN, and Luxembourg sanctions screening; and (iii) BCL balance-of-payments statistical declaration requirements. Dividend and interest remittances to non-residents are subject to Luxembourg withholding tax (15% standard; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double-tax treaty where conditions are met), but this does not require prior regulatory clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender, compulsory conversion ratio, or statutory retention timeline for export proceeds. A Luxembourg SARL may retain deep-sea RoRo freight receivables in the invoicing currency (typically USD) in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The only overlay is the BCL external-statistics framework: resident non-bank entities must report cross-border transactions above the statutory threshold to the BCL on a monthly/quarterly basis, and the remitting bank will require a statistical purpose code (e.g., 'credits from exports of goods and services') at transaction execution.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the amended Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector and the amended Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium and ancillary only. Luxembourg-licensed e-money institutions and payment institutions under the amended Law of 10 November 2009 may provide payment accounts, SEPA/ISO 20022 credit transfers, and FX conversion, but are legally prohibited from granting credit, issuing or confirming commercial letters of credit, or acting as settlement banks. For a deep-sea RoRo carrier requiring documentary credits, vessel-escrow capacity, large freight-settlement liquidity, and ISDA-based hedging, a Tier-1 credit institution is mandatory. Passported EU EMIs can support ancillary collection flows (e.g., customer freight prepayments via SEPA instant credit transfers), but treasury centralisation, nostro relationships, and trade-finance rails must remain within a supervised banking group to satisfy CSSF substance and AML expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound injections use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008. Equity contributions must be tagged with purpose code 'Capital Contribution / Direct Investment – Equity Capital' and supported by the notarised deed of contributions, RCS excerpt reflecting the share-capital increase, and the relevant notarised signatory resolution. Shareholder loans require a signed loan agreement, arm's-length interest rate, amortisation schedule, and tax-advice note on interest withholding exposure; the purpose code is 'Shareholder Loan – Direct Investment'; the receiving bank verifies remitter LEI/BIC, UBO names, contract reference, and tax classification before crediting the operational account. All inbound flows are simultaneously coded for BCL balance-of-payments statistics.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 pacs.008 via TARGET2 for EUR and via the bank's USD nostro network (e.g., CHIPS or correspondent bank) for USD, covering freight settlements, port disbursements, bunker payments, seafarer wages, and P&I premia. Trade-payment rail for vessel acquisition or heavy spares: commercial letters of credit issued by the Luxembourg bank (MT700), amendments (MT707), and reimbursement (MT756); required document set includes negotiable port-to-port Bill of Lading, commercial invoice, packing list, marine insurance certificate/P&I Blue Card, and vessel survey certificate. Intercompany treasury sweeps within the group use MT101 corporate-to-bank instructions with full audit trail referencing the underlying cash-pooling agreement.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/NOK, EUR/JPY) matching deep-sea voyage revenue cycles, documented under the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex (CSA) accepted by Luxembourg banks for shipping treasury centres.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with offshore-settlement restrictions (e.g., USD/CNY, USD/INR), concluded only through the bank's sanctioned-screened book and subject to EMIR transaction-reporting and clearing-threshold assessment.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (XCCYS) to convert USD-denominated freight income into EUR liabilities or align EUR-denominated vessel-financing debt with operating-currency exposure, subject to EMIR reporting and mandatory central clearing above the non-financial-counterparty clearing threshold.Plain-vanilla European FX options, collars, and basis swaps used for voyage-cycle hedging of USD freight revenue with maturity capped at 24 months, with margin or collateral posting per the bank's ISDA CSA and internal credit-limit framework.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% for taxable income exceeding EUR 175,000; 15% for the first EUR 175,000; plus 7% unemployment fund solidarity surcharge on CIT and municipal business tax at the commune-applicable rate (Luxembourg City 6.75%), producing an aggregate headline rate of approximately 24.94% for a Luxembourg City SARL.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% under domestic Luxembourg law (0% under the Luxembourg participation exemption / EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the non-resident parent holds at least 10% for a minimum 12-month holding period or acquisition cost threshold of EUR 1.2 million).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting, Master File and Local File obligations; entities below the threshold must still prepare and maintain contemporaneous arm's length documentation upon request by the Luxembourg tax authorities, covering all cross-border related-party transactions (charter hire, management fees, intercompany debt).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the offshore sense, but imposes robust substance requirements through GAAR, ATAD anti-abuse rules, beneficial ownership case law, and administrative circulars. A Luxembourg SARL operating an ocean Ro-Ro vessel fleet must demonstrate actual management and decision-making in Luxembourg (board meetings, operational control, strategic chartering decisions, accounting substance) to secure tax residence, treaty benefits, and tonnage tax eligibility; artificial brass-plate arrangements may be disregarded by the tax administration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of understated tax plus default interest at 0.8% per month; administrative penalties up to 10%–25% of evaded tax; criminal prosecution for tax fraud with fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of knowingly false returns may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's underpaid tax; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, disqualification from managing Luxembourg entities, and travel restrictions imposed by the Luxembourg courts.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may disregard the entity under the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit), deny the tax residence certificate, reject the tonnage tax election, apply full normal taxation, and impose non-filing/delinquency penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and statutory bodies participating in the sham arrangement may incur civil restitution for denied tax benefits; where misrepresentation is found to be fraudulent, criminal sanctions including imprisonment and personal monetary penalties up to EUR 100,000 can apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Authoritative upward tax adjustment to the arm's length profit; a specific transfer pricing surcharge of up to 10% of the adjustment; late payment interest; and where documentation is absent, an additional penalty possible under the Luxembourg General Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or approve artificial pricing structures can be personally assessed for the resulting tax deficiency under liability rules applicable to wilfully negligent managers; severe cases may attract criminal tax fraud sanctions and director disqualification.
AML/KYC and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for failure to file or maintain the beneficial ownership register (up to EUR 125,000 for serious breaches), with possible public disclosure orders; AML law violations may also carry imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 5 million depending on the professional status.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate shareholders can be prosecuted for money laundering and beneficial ownership concealment; courts may impose custodial sentences, travel bans, freeze orders on personal assets, and civil compensation to affected parties.

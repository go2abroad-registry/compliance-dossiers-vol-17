Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg-Kirchberg; electronic filing portal via guichet.lu and rcs.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as consolidated and amended; SARL regime at Articles 710-1 to 710-25; complemented by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies, and the Law of 1 March 2013 on the dematerialisation of the register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) containing the Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association), followed by mandatory inscription with the RCS and publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 37 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 for a SARL (Article 710-1). The full capital must be subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation. Cash contributions are held in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and must be evidenced by a banker's certificate delivered to the notary; in-kind contributions require a report of the réviseur d'entreprises agréé (approved statutory auditor).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: the registered office fit-out must satisfy the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 9 December 1994 on fire safety, with a municipal fire safety certificate issued by the local commune. Structural load: the building owner's architectural calculation and lease structural load provisions certify category B office loading; any installation of server racks or heavy studio equipment above 2.5 kN/m² requires a structural engineer's verification. Occupational clearance: the occupier must declare the workplace to the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM), prepare a written risk assessment under the Law of 17 June 1994 transposing Directive 89/391/EEC, appoint a safety delegate when staffing thresholds are reached, and maintain first-aid and fire-exit arrangements.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 for a SARL (Article 710-1). The full capital must be subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation. Cash contributions are held in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and must be evidenced by a banker's certificate delivered to the notary; in-kind contributions require a report of the réviseur d'entreprises agréé (approved statutory auditor).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect tax registration; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct tax; unified electronic portal: MyGuichet (myguichet.lu) and eTVA e-filing; business registry: Luxembourg Business Registers / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL obtains its national business identification number (numéro d'identification national) automatically upon incorporation and RCS registration. VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) is procured via the AEDT through the MyGuichet/eTVA portal by filing the commencement declaration. Non-resident entities without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative and file the same declaration, evidencing a local fiscal address or accredited representative; where no domestic taxable activity exists, reverse charge obviates local VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL subject to corporate income tax at the aggregate effective rate of 24.94% (CIT 17% plus surcharge and municipal business tax), net wealth tax at 0.5%, with worldwide income taxation subject to territorial exemptions; no pass-through treatment). — A Luxembourg SARL obtains its national business identification number (numéro d'identification national) automatically upon incorporation and RCS registration. VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) is procured via the AEDT through the MyGuichet/eTVA portal by filing the commencement declaration. Non-resident entities without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative and file the same declaration, evidencing a local fiscal address or accredited representative; where no domestic taxable activity exists, reverse charge obviates local VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL subject to corporate income tax at the aggregate effective rate of 24.94% (CIT 17% plus surcharge and municipal business tax), net wealth tax at 0.5%, with worldwide income taxation subject to territorial exemptions; no pass-through treatment).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; statutory elections available under Luxembourg law: (i) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for CIT/municipal business tax among resident parent and 95%-owned subsidiaries; (ii) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) for closely-linked resident entities; (iii) participation exemption election for 100% dividend exemption and 50% capital-gains exemption on qualifying shareholdings; (iv) IP box 80% exemption election for qualifying intellectual property income; hybrid mismatch adjustments apply mandatorily in accordance with ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold for established taxable persons—registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. For non-established suppliers: EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold for B2C supplies of goods/e-services; B2B services to Luxembourg customers fall under reverse charge without local registration; physical importation of goods always triggers import VAT liability regardless of any threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) physical exports of goods to destinations outside the EU where transport is completed and evidenced by customs export documentation; (ii) B2B services, including cross-border digital advertising/media-buying services, supplied to taxable customers established outside the EU—place of supply being outside Luxembourg; proof of the recipient's non-EU establishment and taxable-person status must be retained; customs export proof must be obtained within 90 days of the supply.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic EU Single Administrative Document) with assigned MRN and confirmation of exit from the EU territory
  • Transport evidence: international waybill (CMR for road, Bill of Lading for sea, Airway Bill for air) covering the movement to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice, underlying supply contract, and purchase order for the export transaction
  • Bank or payment documentation evidencing receipt of foreign currency (inward remittance certificate or SWIFT credit advice)
  • For offshore services: service agreement, recipient's foreign VAT registration or tax-identification certificate, and documentary evidence that the advertising/media-buying services are supplied to and enjoyed by the recipient outside Luxembourg

No national real-time clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate for B2B invoices; Peppol-based B2G e-invoicing (Factur-X/ZUGFeRD) is compulsory for supplies to Luxembourg public entities since April 2023; VAT returns (monthly or quarterly) must be filed electronically via MyGuichet/eTVA; invoices must carry mandatory sequential numbering and be archived digitally for 10 years.

AEDT may claw back zero-rating and assess domestic 17% VAT plus late interest at 0.6% per month where export proof is absent, forged, untimely (over 90 days), or insufficient; culpable VAT fraud carries administrative fines of up to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal fines up to EUR 250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for organized fraud), potential publication of the conviction, and joint and several director liability; refunds linked to suspected fictitious exports are suspended pending forensic verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full liberalisation of capital movements and current payments under EU Treaty provisions (article 63 TFEU). No exchange control approval is required for cross-border dividends, interest, royalties, capital injections, or trade settlements. Profit remittance is unrestricted and free of local withholding on distributions once the participation exemption or ordinary treaty relief applies, subject only to anti-abuse (GAAR) and substance verification. Non-EU currency transactions are freely executed through authorised credit institutions with no mandatory conversion. Reporting obligations are limited to balance-of-payments statistical returns collected by BCL for the Eurosystem, not prior authorisations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds exists in Luxembourg. Exporters may retain and use export receipts freely in EUR or foreign currency. No timeline is imposed for repatriation of proceeds, provided that invoicing and settlement are conducted through a Luxembourg credit institution for bankability and capital movement statistics. For affiliate media buying services, cross-border earned receipts are classified as service export proceeds and are treated identically to any other current account foreign claim.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and exercising delegated foreign exchange administration functions alongside the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for financial supervision; BCL is the depository for reserve statistics and external position reporting under EU regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium-Risk with legal constraints. Luxembourg does not grant domestic EMI authorisation; a fintech EMI must hold a PSD2 authorisation from another EEA member state and passport into Luxembourg via the notification regime. Such EMIs may support EUR IBANs and SWIFT/SEPA clearing but generally do not offer escrow or recorded capital account structures. Their use as principal treasury bank is discouraged; viable only as a secondary disbursement rail. Non-EU/offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to operate as deposit-takers or to provide payment accounts to Luxembourg entities, because they lack an EEA passport; any use is legally constrained to unregulated prepaid instruments, which are unsuitable for corporate treasury.

BCL balance-of-payments reporting requires that transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 attributable to direct investment be flagged as FDI. Box 5a Net wealth tax (calculated on net assets) Non-residents are subject to net wealth tax only on Luxembourg-situs assets (e.g., real estate, PE assets). Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due (minimum €250). Interest on late payment: 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.). Additional penalties for gross negligence or fraud up to 100% of tax due.
Box 7c Transfer pricing adjustments (if any) Disclose any adjustments to align with arm's length principle; non-residents must maintain TP documentation if related party transactions exceed thresholds. Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due (minimum €250). Interest on late payment: 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.). Additional penalties for gross negligence or fraud up to 100% of tax due.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related party transactions exceed €450,000 per year; Master File required if consolidated group revenue exceeds €12 million (EU threshold) or €50 million (Luxembourg threshold).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in IP, financing, and holding activities. For a media buying company, substance is assessed based on: (i) physical presence (office, staff), (ii) strategic decision-making in Luxembourg, (iii) adequate IT infrastructure, and (iv) operational control. Failure to demonstrate substance may lead to reclassification of income and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty of 100% of tax evaded plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and penalty of up to 10% of turnover. Under EU ATAD, substance requirements are mandatory.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid taxes if they failed to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; professional disqualification and travel restrictions may apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment, and interest on underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they approved the non-compliant pricing; potential criminal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation.

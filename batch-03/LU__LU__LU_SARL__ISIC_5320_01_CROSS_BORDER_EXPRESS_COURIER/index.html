Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), administered by the Luxembourg Business Registers and the Ministry of Justice; filing is performed through the eJustice.lu central electronic portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Arts. 710-1 to 710-24 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Law of 19 December 2002 on the register of commerce and companies and accounting (Loi du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés et la comptabilité).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) setting out the articles of association (statuts), filed with the RCS and published in the Electronic Collection of Companies and Associations (Recueil électronique des sociétés, RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 for a SARL, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited in a special blocked escrow account held by the Luxembourg notary (compte bloqué) and released only upon issuance of the RCS registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Service (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours, CGDIS) or municipal fire inspector for the office and sorting depot; structural load certificate from a certified engineering office validating floor capacity for shelving and parcel handling; workplace safety and health clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Luxembourg Labour Code, including evacuation plans and occupational risk assessment.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 for a SARL, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited in a special blocked escrow account held by the Luxembourg notary (compte bloqué) and released only upon issuance of the RCS registration certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Direction de la TVA (VAT), and Administration des douanes et accises (customs/exports); direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD). Official portals: MyGuichet.lu, guichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu, and Luxembourg Business Registers (lbr.lu) for RCS filings.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL: (1) notarized incorporation deed filed with the RCS via MyGuichet.lu/LBR, automatically generating the 13-digit national matricule (RNPPM) used as the ACD corporate TIN; (2) file the AEDT 'Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA' application through MyGuichet.lu indicting the intended start date, ISIC 5320 activity, estimated turnover and place of effective management; the AEDT issues the LU-prefixed 8-digit VAT number normally within 10 business days; (3) non-EU-established entities must appoint a jointly and severally liable Luxembourg VAT representative unless an EU mutual assistance convention applies; (4) registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply for non-established operators and express transport businesses — no de minimis threshold applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: resident SARL taxed on worldwide profits at a combined standard rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City (IRC 17% + solidarity surcharge 7% + municipal business tax 6.75%); SARL has no pass-through eligibility; participation-exemption regime under Article 166 LIR excludes qualifying dividend and capital-gain income at entity level; net wealth tax applies only where balance sheet total exceeds EUR 350,000; loss carryforward of 17 years with annual offset cap (EUR 1m plus 75% of the exceeding income).

Available election forms

Group fiscal unity election under Article 164bis LIR (95% capital link, Luxembourg and EEA resident subsidiaries may join and fully offset profits/losses); participation-exemption election at 10% shareholding or EUR 1.2m acquisition cost with 12-month holding condition; hybrid mismatch disallowances under ATAD (Luxembourg law of 21 December 2018) with limited elective structuring options; VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') under Article 60(1) of the VAT law for financially integrated Luxembourg entities; MOSS/IOSS registration options for digital and distance services; no separate check-the-box classification exists for SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

18%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established suppliers or for cross-border express courier services; taxable persons must register from the first euro of Luxembourg taxable supplies. Domestic micro-undertakings may claim the Article 57 small-enterprise exemption as long as annual turnover remains below the statutory threshold (EUR 30,000–35,000 band as indexed by AEDT guidance), but the exemption is excluded for export, intra-Community and transport services and for all ISIC 5320 operations. The intra-EU distance-sales threshold of EUR 35,000 applies to foreign e-commerce traders shipping low-value parcels to Luxembourg consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with deduction) applies to: (i) goods exported to a non-EU destination under Article 42 of the VAT law, proven by physical departure from the EU within the statutory period; (ii) intra-EU B2B dispatches to a customer holding a valid EU VAT ID verified in VIES; and (iii) international transport of goods carried partly or wholly outside the EU, plus freight-forwarding services directly linked to export, import or intra-Community transit under Article 43 of the VAT law. For ISIC 5320 express parcel operations, the operator must maintain route-level scan events and transport evidence (AWB/B/L/CMR) demonstrating each parcel's international character; administrative goods shipped under ICC rules qualify only with an export declaration. The reduced (8%) and intermediate (16%) Luxembourg VAT rates do not apply to transport; domestic courier legs are standard-rated at 18% unless the supply qualifies as ancillary to an export.

  • Validated customs export declaration (SAD/EX, electronic AES record) for non-EU shipments
  • Transport documents matching the shipment route: master/route air waybill, bill of lading, CMR, or equivalent scan-event transit log of the express courier network
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with Incoterms, parcel value, and HS classification codes
  • Intra-EU dispatch: customer VAT ID validated in VIES, invoice kept in the seller's records, and matching EC Sales List line
  • Proof of payment and, for Chinese parent groups, the SAFE outward-collection record/inward remittance certificate evidencing repatriation within the 180-day window

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system: the AEDT relies on periodic VAT returns (eTVA/eCDF) and European Sales Lists (recapitulative statements) filed electronically. Mandatory structured e-invoicing (EN 16931, UBL/CII) for domestic B2B invoices enters into force on 1 January 2026 under the law of 15 December 2023, transmitted via a registered PEPPOL access point; in the interim, PDF/paper invoices remain valid, and any e-invoice must stay machine-readable. Courier consignments require no transaction-level clearance, but each invoice must bear the issuer's LU VAT number and, for intra-EU dispatches, the customer's VAT ID; continuous transaction controls are planned only under the EU ViDA framework (not yet in force).

AEDT may reassess zero-rated exports for up to 5 years (10 years in fraud cases); failure to produce the complete documentation bundle within the audit deadline reclassifies the supply as taxable at the standard rate, triggering repayment of deducted input VAT, late-payment interest at 0.6% per month, and administrative penalties under Article 61 of the VAT law (up to 10% for negligence and up to 100% of the evaded tax for intentional concealment, plus criminal exposure). Customs post-clearance recovery (AFA) may be raised by the Administration des douanes et accises within 3 years of the export declaration. Repeated irregularities block the exporter's VAT refund account and may lead to revocation of the zero-rating authorization and compulsory periodic audits of the courier's IT systems.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full, unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Luxembourg applies Article 63 TFEU and Council Directive 88/361/EEC guaranteeing free movement of capital; no exchange-control approvals, no surrender obligations, and no differentiated treatment of resident versus non-resident foreign-currency accounts apply. Profit remittances are freely executable provided the company maintains a proper corporate authorization (shareholder/board resolution), filed annual accounts, and satisfies statutory withholding mechanics (15% dividend withholding tax, potentially reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty); no prior CSSF or BCL consent is required for such remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Luxembourg. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts; the entity may freely hold, convert, or deploy foreign currency. However, residents sending or receiving cross-border payments equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction must enable their bank to submit Balance-of-Payments (BoP) statistical declarations to the BCL under the BCL external-statistics framework and CSSF Circular 13-01 and related reporting rules; the underlying economic purpose code must classify transport/courier service fees (EU BoP classification for transport services).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting in conjunction with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and fintech/licensed entities for prudential and AML matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Luxembourg-registered E-Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by the CSSF under the Payment Services Law of 2009 (implementing Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2) may operate multi-currency business accounts, issue payment cards, route SEPA and SWIFT payments, and passport across the EEA. However, EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot issue or confirm documentary Letters of Credit, and must ring-fence client funds in safeguarding accounts. They are therefore highly viable for high-volume parcel-payment collection and payout disbursement rails (card settlement and SEPA direct debit) but not for primary capital-escrow, borrowing, or documentary-credit treasury functions. The recommended pattern is a hybrid architecture: EMI-controlled collection and disbursement rails swept daily into a Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial bank for liquidity concentration and capital operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for a capital injection into the Luxembourg SARL: (1) instruct the parent or shareholder to originate an SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the Luxembourg bank (IBAN LU... and BIC of the Luxembourg credit institution); (2) the instruction must state the reason for payment as 'equity contribution / souscription au capital de la SARL'; (3) the receiving bank will require the notarial deed of incorporation or a capital-increase notarial deed, the blocked-funds attestation (attestation de blocage) for the SARL minimum capital (EUR 12,000 for a standard SARL, or EUR 30,000 for a SARL-S meeting the simplified regime conditions), and evidence of the source of funds; (4) for statistical reporting, the receiving bank records the economic purpose under the EU BoP classification 'Direct investment - Equity capital' and submits the return to the BCL if the credit equals or exceeds EUR 12,500; (5) after RCSL registration, the blocked funds are released into a fully operational account, and the company files financial statements reflecting share capital and any share premium.The express-courier treasury uses a tiered clearing architecture: (a) intra-EEA euro disbursements to partner consolidators and last-mile carriers execute via SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) with reference codes aligned to the clearance ledger; (b) cross-border non-euro settlements (airline lift fees, outbound gateway handling, and US/UK/Asia partner fees) execute via SWIFT MT103 with the proper BoP purpose codes such as 'transport services - courier fees' or 'business services'; (c) for large-value imports of specialized logistics equipment or consignment-goods flows, a Commercial Letter of Credit (sight or deferred) and documentary collections are used, with the Luxembourg Tier-1 bank as issuing bank and the advising bank in the supplier jurisdiction; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Luxembourg-registered E-Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by the CSSF under the Payment Services Law of 2009 (implementing Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2) may operate multi-currency business accounts, issue payment cards, route SEPA and SWIFT payments, and passport across the EEA. However, EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot issue or confirm documentary Letters of Credit, and must ring-fence client funds in safeguarding accounts. They are therefore highly viable for high-volume parcel-payment collection and payout disbursement rails (card settlement and SEPA direct debit) but not for primary capital-escrow, borrowing, or documentary-credit treasury functions. The recommended pattern is a hybrid architecture: EMI-controlled collection and disbursement rails swept daily into a Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial bank for liquidity concentration and capital operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City combined rate: 17% corporate income tax + 7% state solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, reduced to 0% under qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions or under income tax treaties providing full relief; 5% or 10% treaty rates may apply for portfolio investors depending on the relevant DTA.

For an express courier company include parcel delivery fees, cross-border transport charges, tracking and logistics surcharges, and fuel surcharges. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Luxembourg Local File preparation applies when the Luxembourg taxpayer engages in related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 annually; a Master File is required when the multinational group maintains consolidated revenue of at least EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report thresholds follow the OECD BEPS standard at consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but enforces an economic-substance doctrine through statutory general anti-abuse rules (GAAR), ATAD measures, administrative circulars, and case law. Cross-border express courier holding or financing entities must demonstrate real personnel, business premises, decision-making independence, and actual operational activity; a shell SARL lacking substance will be denied treaty benefits, EU directives, and Luxembourg tax rulings, and may trigger immediate reassessment and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all under-declared taxes and 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; criminal fine under Article 426 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law of up to EUR 250,000; imprisonment up to five years; seizure or disqualification from courier operating licences; public disclosure of conviction in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or causes a false return is jointly and severally liable for the full tax deficiency; piercing the corporate veil permits civil restitution from personal assets; criminal courts may impose travel bans, confiscation orders, director disqualification, and imprisonment of up to five years.
Transfer Pricing / Intercompany Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer-pricing adjustment to Luxembourg taxable profit; penalty surcharge of 20% of the upward adjustment; late-payment interest of 0.6% per month; administrative fines up to EUR 250,000; cancellation or retroactive revocation of advance pricing agreements or tax rulings when facts are misstated.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed where they engineered tainted intercompany pricing or directed creation of shell counterparties; courts may pierce the veil to recover unpaid corporate tax and interest from personal assets; criminal liability for fraudulent accounting documentation can lead to travel bans, disqualification, asset freezes, and imprisonment.
Dividend Withholding Tax / Profit Repatriation Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of uncompensated dividend withholding tax plus 0.6% monthly interest; administrative fine up to EUR 10,000; criminal prosecution for fraudulent withholding-tax evasion with fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; denial of all treaty and EU directive relief for abusive schemes.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid withholding tax if they knowingly structured the distribution or if the company is a mere facade; piercing the corporate veil yields direct civil restitution from directors' personal assets; travel bans, extradition proceedings, and imprisonment are available where concealment through foreign bank accounts is proven.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Shell Company Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 as amended: fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; mandatory deregistration from the Luxembourg trade register; freezing of funds and confiscation of proceeds; revocation of the courier operating licence and denial of VAT registration.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced: directors are civilly liable for restitution of laundered amounts, personally fined and disqualified; courts may issue travel bans, order extradition, freeze personal and corporate assets, and impose imprisonment; each director can be held criminally responsible if they knew or recklessly ignored the unlawful provenance of funds.

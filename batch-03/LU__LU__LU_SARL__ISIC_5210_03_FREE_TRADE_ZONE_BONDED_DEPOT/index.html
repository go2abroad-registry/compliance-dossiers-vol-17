Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 (Article 182-1, Law of 10 August 1915), fully subscribed at incorporation; each in-cash quota must be paid up to at least 25% immediately, non-cash contributions fully paid upon execution; the entire subscribed cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution and released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification and site clearance issued by the communal fire brigade / Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS); workplace health-and-safety inspection and occupational clearance by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) under the Luxembourg Labour Code, including structural load inspection of racking systems by an approved engineering bureau; additional civil-defense site approval required where hazardous goods are stored within the bonded area.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 (Article 182-1, Law of 10 August 1915), fully subscribed at incorporation; each in-cash quota must be paid up to at least 25% immediately, non-cash contributions fully paid upon execution; the entire subscribed cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution and released only after RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for registration duties and VAT; primary electronic portal is MyGuichet.lu, with company registration through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners establishing an SARL must (i) execute a notarial deed of incorporation, (ii) file for incorporation with the RCS via the Luxembourg Business Registers e-desk, (iii) obtain the RCS registration number, which serves as the national identification number / matricule and functional TIN, and (iv) apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number by submitting the standard VAT registration form through MyGuichet.lu, executed by the appointed local manager or authorised tax representative. The VAT number is notified in writing after verification of the registered office, beneficial ownership declarations, and supporting KYC documents from non-resident shareholders.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign owners establishing an SARL must (i) execute a notarial deed of incorporation, (ii) file for incorporation with the RCS via the Luxembourg Business Registers e-desk, (iii) obtain the RCS registration number, which serves as the national identification number / matricule and functional TIN, and (iv) apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number by submitting the standard VAT registration form through MyGuichet.lu, executed by the appointed local manager or authorised tax representative. The VAT number is notified in writing after verification of the registered office, beneficial ownership declarations, and supporting KYC documents from non-resident shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box classification system; an SARL is per se taxable at entity level. Elective mechanisms include fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for direct taxes under Article 164bis of the Income Tax Law (LIR), VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 59 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, and limited elections under tax incentive regimes. Hybrid mismatch rules are neutralised through the ATAD implementation; there is no elective hybrid mismatch instrument.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general minimum turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration of taxable supplies made in Luxembourg; however, the small business exemption applies to Luxembourg-established businesses with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 30,000 (exclusive of VAT). For non-established suppliers, intra-Community distance sales of goods exceeding EUR 35,000 trigger Luxembourg VAT registration, subject to the electronic OSS regime election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) direct export supplies of goods transported or dispatched outside the EU where the supplier retains documentary evidence of physical departure; (ii) supplies of goods under customs bonded-warehouse arrangements or free-zone procedures where the goods remain under customs suspension and are destined for extra-EU export; (iii) intra-Community supplies of goods where the customer communicates a valid EU/ Luxembourg VAT identification number and the goods are dispatched to another Member State; and (iv) services that are directly linked to exported goods or supplied to non-EU customers in accordance with place-of-supply rules. The taxpayer must obtain and preserve all substantive evidence of the physical flow, export customs clearance, transport, and payment matching before applying the 0% rate.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD/DAU) export customs declaration with authenticated MRN
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical cross-border carriage
  • Proof of arrival or acceptance by the non-EU customs authority or frontier documentation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the goods, quantities, and ultimate destination
  • VAT ID and VIES/ECS records for intra-Community supplies where relevant
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank settlement evidence matching invoice amounts and customs declarations

Luxembourg does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API validation for B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory only for business-to-government (B2G) supplies, using the PEPPOL interoperability framework. For all other supplies, taxpayers must ensure the authenticity and integrity of invoices through business controls. VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically through the AEDT portal; there is no national clearance stamping regime.

The AEDT and the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration conduct cross-matched audits of export declarations, MRN records, transport documentation, and bank inflows. Where zero-rating is claimed with missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent documentation, the export treatment is reversed, 17% VAT becomes chargeable, late interest accrues from the date of supply, and the VAT refund is clawed back. Administrative penalties and criminal sanctions may be imposed for tax fraud, including fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years for deliberate abuse. The standard reassessment period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraudulent intent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under EU law (TFEU Article 63). Luxembourg imposes no foreign-exchange control approval, no classified capital vs. current-account permit regime, and no prior central-bank consent for wire transfers. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted. The only administrative obligation is the statistical balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to BCL for cross-border payments above certain de minimis thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion rules. Bonded warehouse and free-zone sales receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency without time limits or conversion ratios. Periodic BOP statistical declaration to the BCL is the only compliance requirement; no statutory timeline or ratio applies to EUR conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Luxembourg's national central bank and a member of the Eurosystem. BCL exercises monetary-policy functions, TARGET2 membership, and oversight of payment, clearing, and settlement systems. Prudential supervision of credit institutions and electronic money institutions is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility for EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as PayPal, Mangopay, or Banking Circle: they can handle low-value settlements and payroll, but generally cannot hold segregated customs-duties funds, issue documentary trade instruments, or act as principal treasury counterparty for bonded-depot operations. Low feasibility for offshore, non-EU EMIs without CSSF passporting: such providers are not authorised to hold client monies or open settlement accounts in Luxembourg and will be rejected at the KYC/UBO stage.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions and shareholder-loan disbursements are routed by SWIFT MT103 to the SARL's EUR-denominated account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire must carry the ECB/BOP purpose code 'S110' for direct-investment equity capital or 'S120' for shareholder loans to enable BCL statistical classification. No CSSF or BCL foreign-exchange approval is required; the receiving bank must refresh CDD/UBO documentation for any credit above EUR 10,000 and obtain documentary evidence of the economic substance of the contribution, including the notarised deed, bank reference letter, and proof of source of funds.Trade settlements for bonded-stock purchase, freight, and logistics use SWIFT MT103 direct transfers and MT202 bank-to-bank reimbursement. Documentary trade runs on commercial letters of credit issued by SWIFT MT700 under ICC UCP600, with collections under URC 522. EUR large-value leg is cleared through TARGET2; low-value SEPA and STEP2 rails are used for instant or batch payments. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A Luxembourg SARL must appoint an approved independent auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) when it exceeds at least two of the following three thresholds on two consecutive balance-sheet dates: balance-sheet total above EUR 4.4 million; net turnover above EUR 8.8 million; average workforce above 25 employees. If the SARL remains under any two of those tests, a statutory audit is not mandatorily required unless the articles of association or a shareholder request provide otherwise; a bonded-depot licence itself does not impose a separate statutory audit.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective CIT headline for Luxembourg City (fiscal year 2024–2025), comprising 17.00% corporate income tax, 1.19% solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), and 6.75% municipal business tax; a reduced aggregate rate of 22.80% applies to taxable income up to EUR 175,000 (15.00% CIT + 1.05% surcharge + 6.75% municipal tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% general Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends distributed by a resident SARL under Article 69(6) LIR, applicable equally to resident and non-resident shareholders; the rate remains 15% on the gross distribution and is imposed at source without regard to the beneficial owner's residence. Exemption applies to qualifying inter-corporate distributions: 0% under the Luxembourg participation-exemption regime (≥10% capital held for ≥12 months) and 0% distributions to EU parent companies meeting the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The statutory filing deadline extends to six months from the fiscal year-end for annual accounts prepared without a registered Luxembourg tax professional (a 31 December year-end SARL must file by 30 June N+1); the deadline is automatically extended to twelve months (31 December N+1) when the return is prepared and e-filed by an approved Luxembourg accountant or tax adviser. Further extension may be applied for through MyGuichet; late filing does not suspend the accrual of delay interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000: a Local File is mandatory where the aggregate annual value of cross-border related-party transactions in any category (goods, services, financial transactions, IP transfers) exceeds EUR 400,000; a Master File is required when the consolidated group turnover reaches or exceeds EUR 50 million. Documentation must be contemporaneous, furnished within 30 days upon request of the ACD, and cover comparability analysis, functional analysis, selection of the transfer-pricing method, and arm's-length benchmarking; absence of documentation shifts the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate codified Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE/BCR model; however, economic substance is enforced as a substantive statutory doctrine via: (i) the corporate residence test of the General Tax Law (place of effective management); (ii) the general anti-abuse rule of Article 6 AO allowing the ACD to disregard legal constructions lacking economic purpose; (iii) ATAD anti-hybrid and interest-limitation rules; and (iv) administrative circulars governing holding and financing vehicles requiring local directors, staff, premises, and decision-making. A physically operating bonded depot (ISIC 5210) with its own employees, inventory responsibility, and customs license constitutes strong economic substance; a letterbox entity claiming treaty advantages while operations are conducted abroad is treated as a shell and exposed to recharacterization and penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent Corporate Filing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment within a retroactive six-year statutory window; 10% surcharge on the underdeclared tax; delay interest accruing at 0.6% per month on the underpayment; criminal fines under Articles 109–114 LIR up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of eight days to five years for willful tax evasion, increased to EUR 750,000 and seven years' imprisonment where the offense is committed through a criminal organization.

Director personal liability: Directors signing fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency, delay interest, and penalties; criminal prosecution targets natural persons; a court may order civil restitution of the evaded amounts, freezing injunctions on personal assets, exit/travel bans during the penal procedure, and imprisonment directly against the director.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in the Free Trade Zone

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the abusive scheme under Article 6 AO; recharacterization of transactions to their economic reality; upward adjustment of taxable income assessed at the 24.94% aggregate CIT; reclassification of profits as deemed distributions triggering 15% dividend WHT plus 10% surcharge and delay interest; denial of DTA benefits and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief; administrative dissolution of the SARL for violation of the substance requirement.

Director personal liability: Under Articles 1807–1808 of the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, directors may be personally liable for debts of a fictive or sham company; the corporate veil is pierced, exposing directors to joint restitution, criminal liability for abuse of company assets, temporary travel restraints, and professional disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Cross-Border Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing audit adjustment to the arm's-length result; enforcement of the 24.94% aggregate CIT on the adjusted profit; accrual of 0.6% monthly delay interest; imposition of a 10% surcharge; refusal of deductibility for non-compliant intragroup charges; escalation to a penalty for incomplete documentation under the ACD administrative practice; criminal prosecution for false accounts in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the mispricing may be summoned as parties to the tax proceedings; the additional assessment may be enforced against their personal assets in the event of bankruptcy or fraudulent transfer; criminal sanctions for falsification of corporate accounts carry imprisonment up to five years in aggravated circumstances.
Customs Fraud and Sanctions Violations in the Bonded Depot

Statutory sanction: Under Article 42 of Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) 952/2013), confiscation of goods and imposition of a customs fine of the higher of the evaded duty amount or EUR 1,500 per contravention; revocation of the bonded warehouse authorization and free-zone license; immediate payment of the tariff debt; AML administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the amended law of 12 November 2004; referral to the Parquet for customs and financial criminal offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution for smuggling and money laundering, with imprisonment up to five years and fines of EUR 1,250,000 per laundering operation; customs authorities may seize personal assets of directors who knowingly authorized the irregular release; the court may issue travel bans, freeze funds, and disqualify the director from managing any commercial company in Luxembourg.

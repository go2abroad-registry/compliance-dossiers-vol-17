Competent corporate registry

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution en la forme authentique notariée (Notarial Deed of Incorporation) including the Statuts (Articles of Association), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 20% paid up upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years; funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened with a Luxembourg credit institution or held at the Caisse de Consignation, and evidenced by a notarial bank certificate (attestation de blocage). Capital can also be formed by in-kind contributions, subject to statutory control.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (certificat de sécurité incendie) by the communal fire prevention service under the Law of 28 December 2006; workplace occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Law of 17 June 1994 on safety and health at work; structural load inspection is not required for ordinary office premises but the lessor must certify the premises are compliant with the amended Law of 2 September 2011 on access to professions for commercial premises.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 20% paid up upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years; funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened with a Luxembourg credit institution or held at the Caisse de Consignation, and evidenced by a notarial bank certificate (attestation de blocage). Capital can also be formed by in-kind contributions, subject to statutory control.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes. Official e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://myguichet.lu) and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), incorporation before a Luxembourg notary is first filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL), which issues the national identification number (matricule). A VAT identification number (TVA LUXXXXXXXX) is obtained separately by submitting the electronic registration form (Form 010) to the AEDT via MyGuichet, supported by notarized articles of association, RCSL registration certificate, proof of registered office and effective management place in Luxembourg, and identification documents of managers. The corporate income tax TIN is aligned with the RCSL matricule; no additional direct-tax registration is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident SARL is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (IBT) and net wealth tax (NWT) on worldwide profits, with tax credits/exemptions for certain foreign-source income. No US-style pass-through eligibility; no tax-exempt zone applies. — For a Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), incorporation before a Luxembourg notary is first filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL), which issues the national identification number (matricule). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident SARL is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (IBT) and net wealth tax (NWT) on worldwide profits, with tax credits/exemptions for certain foreign-source income. No US-style pass-through eligibility; no tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include (i) tax consolidation (fiscal unity) under Section 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) for resident companies directly or indirectly held at least 95% by another resident group company; (ii) VAT grouping election under Article 59 TVA creating a single taxable person; (iii) limited election to opt for VAT taxation on rental/immovable property operations under Article 44 TVA; and (iv) options under relevant hybrid mismatch and ATAD rules as applicable to cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT); if the threshold is exceeded, VAT registration becomes mandatory. Foreign businesses supplying electronic, advertising, ASO/SEO or localization services to Luxembourg taxable customers may be required to register from the first euro under the place-of-supply reverse-charge rules unless covered by the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to supplies of goods physically exported or transported by or on behalf of a taxable person to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier retains proper documentary evidence of departure from EU territory. For B2B services (including app store optimization, ASO, SEO, multilingual localization and advertising services) supplied to non-EU taxable customers, the place of supply is where the customer is established, so the supply is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT or effectively zero-rated; eligibility requires evidence that the customer is an entrepreneur identified for VAT outside the EU, preferably confirmed by VAT number, contract, purchase order and payment records.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank FX Payment Confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Contract or Purchase Order evidencing non-EU customer VAT status

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system and does not require digital stamping or government API approval for B2B invoices. Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing through the PEPPOL network applies to invoices issued to public sector entities since April 2022 and to all public payments by the State from 2023. Invoices to private parties may be sent electronically provided authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are secured through qualified electronic signatures or EDI controls; no dedicated pre-clearance filing system is imposed.

Luxembourg tax and customs authorities may reject zero-rating claims on audit if the export substantiation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent. In such cases, unpaid VAT is reassessed together with default interest at 0.6% per month, and a fine of up to ECU/EUR 10,000 may be imposed under Article 68 of the VAT Law. Fraudulent or knowingly false export declarations trigger criminal tax fraud proceedings and civil penalties of up to double the evaded VAT, retrospective clawback of refunds, and potential criminal imprisonment. Documentation must demonstrate the economic reality of the export transaction, including actual physical departure for goods and genuine business status of non-EU customers for services.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a Eurozone member, applies full liberalisation of capital movements and current account payments under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is fully convertible without any domestic exchange-control restrictions. Capital account transactions, including equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, intercompany financing, and loan repayments, are freely permitted. Current account payments for services, royalties, and remuneration are unrestricted. Profit remittance, including dividend distributions and interest payments, is permitted after the annual accounts are approved by the shareholders and statutory audit and disclosure requirements, where applicable, have been met. A Luxembourg withholding tax applies to dividends and certain interest (normally 15% domestic rate, reduced under applicable tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive). There are no currency transfer taxes, no stamp duties on foreign exchange transactions, and no prior approval requirements for converting EUR to other currencies. All conversions are executed at freely negotiated market rates. Banks are subject to statistical balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting obligations to the BCL, but these reporting requirements do not restrict the timing or amount of transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or holding-period requirements on export proceeds. An export entity (the SARL) may freely receive and retain payments in EUR or any foreign currency in Luxembourg bank accounts or in foreign accounts without any forced conversion or time-bound remittance obligation. Only statistical reporting applies: the receiving bank must report cross-border transfers to the BCL for balance-of-payments compilation, using purpose codes and transaction classifications, without any restrictive effect on the export revenue.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct and anti-money-laundering supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: High for operational disbursement and collection treasury, but Low/Medium for statutory capital escrow purposes. EEA-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Payoneer Payment Europe S.à r.l. (authorised and regulated by the CSSF in Luxembourg) and Wise Europe S.A. (passporting into Luxembourg from Belgium) are fully viable for holding multicurrency working balances, settling subcontractor invoices, and managing app-marketplace payouts. However, a Luxembourg SARL’s statutory share capital must be subscribed and deposited with a licensed credit institution for the notarisation and capital verification phase; EMIs are not recognised for capital formation/deed purposes. Additional constraints: EMIs generally are non-deposit-taking entities, corporate funds held with EMIs are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme, and treasury policy should cap daily EMI balances and sweep balances to a Tier-1 credit institution. The overall rating is 'approved ancillary rail', not a replacement for main banking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The standard compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder injecting equity into the Luxembourg SARL is as follows: (i) the originating bank issues a SWIFT MT103 message to the corporate EUR account at a licensed Luxembourg credit institution, referencing the unique ISIN or CUSIP if shares are represented, but more practically including the remittance reference 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / LIBÉRATION DU CAPITAL SOCIAL'; (ii) the payment purpose code is entered as EU/ISO 20215 'EQUI' (equity-like capital contribution) or, when using the ECB/BCL statistical taxonomy, the transaction is classified as 'Direct investment – equity capital injection under the BOP list'; if the bank requires a full BOP code, the standard used by Luxembourg banks is 210 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' (as preferred by the BCL reporting system). (iii) In the case of an injection from a non-EEA shareholder, the sending bank must ensure the sender is not a shell financial institution and that the transfer is accompanied by two independent source-of-funds documents, such as a signed financial statement, an audited or notarised confirmation of the beneficial owner's capacity, a board resolution of the shareholder, and a general description of the economic rationale. (iv) The receiving bank will verify that the amount matches the authorised capital structure in the RCS, perform sanctions/AML screening, possibly block transgender if the correspondent bank does not provide full payer data, and where relevant will issue the bank certificate (attestation de dépôt) required by the notary to authenticate the capital subscription. (v) For shareholder loans instead of equity, the same MT103 rail is used, but the purpose code is 'INT' (intercompany lending) according to ISO 20022, or the BCL code for 'Direct investment – other capital / loans', and a signed shareholder loan agreement must be presented to satisfy the 'substance over form' analysis of the Luxembourg debt-equity thin-cap rules.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements (payments to subcontractors, software suppliers, media-inventory platforms, and SEO localisation specialists) are: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for all beneficiaries with a Euro-area IBAN — this applies to the overwhelming majority of vendors in the EU/EEA; (b) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries or for currencies other than EUR; MT103 includes standard fields for value date, charges (SHAR/OUR/BEN), the ISO 20022 purpose code of the underlying commercial or service contract, and payment reference to the invoice number. (c) For repetitive monthly supplier runs, the treasury can use a single batch file constructed according to Customer-to-Bank XML (ISO 20022 pain.001), which is accepted by all Luxembourg Tier-1 banks and by Payoneer/Wise via their virtual IBAN systems. (d) Commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) under UCP 600 are not typical for intangible ASO/SEO services; a standby L/C or bank guarantee may only appear if the agency is required to pre-pay a major overseas advertising marketplace or to procure a performance bond for a significant client contract. In that isolated case, the guarantee would be issued by the Luxembourg bank at a fee and is not expected to become a recurring clearing rail. (e) All outbound disbursements must be supported by the contract, invoice, provider identification, and, where relevant, VAT/transaction documents. However, a Luxembourg SARL’s statutory share capital must be subscribed and deposited with a licensed credit institution for the notarisation and capital verification phase; EMIs are not recognised for capital formation/deed purposes. Additional constraints: EMIs generally are non-deposit-taking entities, corporate funds held with EMIs are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme, and treasury policy should cap daily EMI balances and sweep balances to a Tier-1 credit institution. The overall rating is 'approved ancillary rail', not a replacement for main banking.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline rate on taxable income above EUR 175,000, computed on the basis of the Luxembourg City municipality: 17% corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) plus 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC (state-level effective 18.19%) plus 6.75% municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC, based on the Luxembourg City multiplier of 225% applied to the statutory base rate of 3%). The first EUR 175,000 of taxable income is taxed at 15% IRC (combined state-Communal effective rate of approximately 22.80%, again at the Luxembourg City multiplier).

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated under the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Droit des sociétés), specifically Articles 710-1 through 710-22 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Registration governed by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) plus electronic application for registration filed through the RCS/LBR e-Registration portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before registration. The funds must be deposited in a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the name of the company and held until issuance of the RCS registration extract, operating as an escrow / blocked-capital mechanism.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural certifications are not required for a non-classed administrative office. The registered office must comply with standard building fire-safety occupancy rules under municipal zoning, and the manager must ensure basic occupational health and safety (ITM inspection standards), including first aid, electrical safety, and exit signage, if employees are engaged in this administrative site.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before registration. The funds must be deposited in a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the name of the company and held until issuance of the RCS registration extract, operating as an escrow / blocked-capital mechanism.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) / Luxembourg Inland Revenue; registration portal: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and MyGuichet.lu (for VAT: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT))

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative when lacking a permanent establishment, file an application with the AEDT (VAT registration form 640) via MyGuichet.lu, submit certified articles of association, proof of effective management and commercial registration excerpt from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) and national business number (numéro d'identification national) issued by the ACD through the RCS process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative when lacking a permanent establishment, file an application with the AEDT (VAT registration form 640) via MyGuichet.lu, submit certified articles of association, proof of effective management and commercial registration excerpt from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) and national business number (numéro d'identification national) issued by the ACD through the RCS process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Check-the-box not applicable in Luxembourg; Luxembourg SARL is a taxable entity subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). Available elections include: group VAT registration (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, and the option for a transparent tax treatment of qualifying limited partnerships (not applicable to SARL). Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (EU Directive 2016/1164 as transposed) are applied, but no elective hybrid conversion exists for a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual turnover from taxable supplies of goods or services, excluding VAT, for small enterprise exemption; however mandatory VAT registration applies immediately upon commencement of economic activity with no minimum turnover threshold for cross-border/dropshipping supplies, except the EUR 10,000 distance-selling threshold for intra-EU B2C supplies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods to non-EU countries and to supplies dispatched or transported to another EU Member State (intra-Community supply) provided the purchaser is a VAT-registered taxable person. Dropshipping qualifies where the seller (LU SARL) causes the goods to be dispatched to the customer; documentation must prove the physical movement of goods out of Luxembourg or the EU. Offshore services (e.g., digital services to non-EU businesses) qualify under the place-of-supply B2B rule (Article 44 EU VAT Directive) and must not be taxable in Luxembourg.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Administrative Single Document – ASD/DAU) or electronic export declaration (ECS) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) or CMR (road) evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg
  • Commercial invoice showing the foreign customer and Incoterms (EXW/FCA/CPT/CIP)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of foreign currency payment) or bank statement evidencing settlement
  • Proof of transport instruction (logistics contract, carrier consignment note, delivery confirmation signed by the foreign customer)
  • VAT return (VAT declaration) and recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) for intra-Community supplies

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate for domestic B2B invoices. EU e-invoicing directive (2014/55/EU) applies only to public procurement. B2B e-invoicing in a structured electronic format is permitted, subject to authenticity of origin and integrity of content. VAT reporting is periodic (monthly/quarterly) via e-file (eCDF), with SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) required for taxpayers using certified accounting software. No continuous transaction control (CTC) or government clearance escenario.

The Luxembourg tax administration (AEDT) may apply anti-abuse measures and impose penalties of EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 or 100% of evaded tax for fraudulent zero-rating claims. Retrospective clawback of VAT, interest at the statutory rate, and criminal fines for customs fraud under the Union Customs Code (UCC) apply. Penalties range from 10% to 200% of deficient VAT based on the degree of intent; criminal sanctions for customs and VAT evasion include imprisonment and seizure of goods. Documentary retention period is 10 years. Missing or false evidence triggers reassessment and denial of zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a member of the euro area, maintains full capital and current account convertibility for EUR and all foreign currencies. No Luxembourg-specific exchange control legislation restricts cross-border transfers of funds for licensed business entities. Profit remittances, dividends, and repatriated capital are freely transferable, subject only to standard tax withholding compliance (e.g., EU Savings Directive reporting, beneficial ownership declarations) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) verification requirements. There are no administrative approval hurdles for ordinary commercial payments, loan repayments, or equity injections. For third-country (non-EU) transactions, EU sanctions screening and financial restrictive measures apply, but these do not represent convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion timeline for export proceeds in Luxembourg. Businesses may retain receipts in any foreign currency, and there is no requirement to convert or repatriate export earnings within a specific period. However, for statistical and balance-of-payments reporting purposes, residents must report cross-border transactions and positions to the BCL through the direct reporting channel (Collecte d'informations Monétaires et Financières, CIMF) if thresholds are exceeded. Export of goods under an automated dropshipping model is classified under ISIC 4791 and is subject to Intrastat and EU customs declarations, but not to FX surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and the Luxembourg Financial Sector Supervisory Commission (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier, CSSF); exchange control enforcement is shared with the Ministère des Finances and the CSSF for financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium legal viability with operational caveats. Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed in the EEA (e.g., PPS, MANGOPAY, Payoneer, and Luxembourg-licensed EMIs) can hold client funds and provide payment accounts but do not constitute 'credit institutions' for commercial lending or escrow-deposit purposes. For an automated dropshipping entity, an EMI that offers merchant acquiring and mass-payout rails (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) is operationally viable as a payment gateway, but the primary treasury bank account must remain at a fully licensed credit institution to satisfy Luxembourg corporate substance expectations and CSSF oversight. Offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or EEA licence are not legally viable as principal treasury accounts due to proportionality requirements under Luxembourg Circular CSSF 12/552 and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be routed via authenticated SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages from the investor's bank to the Luxembourg SARL's commercial bank account. The purpose code must use the most appropriate EU balance-of-payments (BoP) category: for equity capital, use 'Direct investment – capital' (BoP code 5101) or the corresponding bank-specific code for share capital; for intercompany loans, use 'Loans / Trade credit – other sectors' (BoP code 5103) if applicable. The bank will verify the counterparty's legal existence, the corporate resolution, and the notarized escrow evidence before crediting the Capital Escrow Account. No prior BCL approval is required for such injections, but the BCL may collect statistical details via the reporting entity. Inbound funds from high-risk third countries require enhanced beneficial ownership identification and may trigger extended verification time.For trade settlements (supplier payments to China, USA, or EU warehouses), the clearing rail should be either (i) a standard international wire via SWIFT MT103 with unambiguous purpose code (e.g., 'Trade Settlement – Goods' under EU BoP 5010), or (ii) a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) confirmed by a Luxembourg bank when the supplier demands documentary security. For smaller payments within the SEPA zone, a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is acceptable under a Luxembourg IBAN. The payment routing must ensure that the beneficial owner name of the receiving account matches the trade counterparty; otherwise, the Luxembourg bank may reject the payment due to AML sanctions screening. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline rate for a Luxembourg City-resident SARL for tax year 2024: 17.00% corporate income tax on taxable income above EUR 175,000, plus 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT equal to 0.84%, plus municipal business tax of 6.75% applied to the municipal tax base.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax on dividend distributions made by a Luxembourg SARL to shareholders, applicable to non-resident shareholders and non-resident companies; reduced to 0% for a qualifying EU/EEA parent company holding directly at least 10% of the SARL's capital for at least 12 months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and reduced to 5% or 0% under applicable double tax treaties, commonly 5% for a 10%+ direct shareholding and 0% for EU treaty partners subject to the anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership tests.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Boxes 0100-0900 Identification page: corporate name, legal form (SARL), registered office address in Luxembourg, RCS registration number, Luxembourg income tax file number, municipal tax file number, fiscal year start and end dates, and precise identification of the responsible director and accounting officer. The resident Luxembourg SARL is the statutory filer. Non-resident shareholders are identified only in the shareholders' annex and do not file Form 300 on behalf of the company; a non-resident cannot sign the declaration unless formally appointed as a Luxembourg-resident tax representative or manager. Late filing exposes the SARL to a 'taxation d'office' by the Luxembourg direct tax administration, a late-filing increase of up to 10% of the assessed corporate income tax under Article 181 LIR, late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized), administrative fines under Articles 174-177 LIR ranging between EUR 500 and EUR 12,500, and, where concealment is intentional, the criminal anti-evasion surcharge of 10% to 90% of the evaded tax with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible manager.
Boxes 1000-1320 Accounting results: full balance sheet and profit-and-loss account lines reconciled to the statutory annual accounts, including closing inventory, trade receivables, cash positions and operating income; for a dropshipping SARL, all gross sales proceeds, settlement charges, COGS and platform fees must be traceable to Stripe and PayPal statements. All foreign-currency sales transactions must be converted into EUR at the European Central Bank reference rate prevailing on the transaction date or on the year-end closing rate consistently applied. Cross-border settlement inflows that are commingled with personal accounts of non-resident directors are a fatal examination point. Late filing exposes the SARL to a 'taxation d'office' by the Luxembourg direct tax administration, a late-filing increase of up to 10% of the assessed corporate income tax under Article 181 LIR, late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized), administrative fines under Articles 174-177 LIR ranging between EUR 500 and EUR 12,500, and, where concealment is intentional, the criminal anti-evasion surcharge of 10% to 90% of the evaded tax with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible manager.
Boxes 1500-1700 Reconciliation and add-backs: disallowed expenses such as 50% of entertainment costs, 50% of certain passenger-car running costs, penalties, non-deductible taxes, excessive management remuneration paid to directors, and hidden dividend distributions; exempt participating revenues are eliminated here and the taxable reserves and profit appropriations are exchanged. Payments to non-resident related parties, including dropshipping suppliers, marketing agencies, and foreign group service companies, are deductible only if supported by written contracts, actual service delivery evidence, incoming invoices, and transfer pricing documentation at arm's length. Unsubstantiated wire transfers are treated as constructive dividends. Late filing exposes the SARL to a 'taxation d'office' by the Luxembourg direct tax administration, a late-filing increase of up to 10% of the assessed corporate income tax under Article 181 LIR, late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized), administrative fines under Articles 174-177 LIR ranging between EUR 500 and EUR 12,500, and, where concealment is intentional, the criminal anti-evasion surcharge of 10% to 90% of the evaded tax with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible manager.
Boxes 1800-2100 Taxable income computation: determination of the net taxable income after the participation exemption, the IP box deduction, the net wealth tax deduction (Articles 24 and 166 LIR), and the deduction of loss carry-forwards pursuant to Article 114 LIR, which are available for 17 years and forfeited if a more than 50% change in ownership occurs simultaneously with a change of business activity. Treaty-exempt foreign income and foreign losses must be excluded from the Luxembourg taxable base. The SARL must prove the source and economic ownership of each cross-border profit stream; profits artificially routed through non-resident shell intermediary without substance are re-characterized as taxable Luxembourg income. Late filing exposes the SARL to a 'taxation d'office' by the Luxembourg direct tax administration, a late-filing increase of up to 10% of the assessed corporate income tax under Article 181 LIR, late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized), administrative fines under Articles 174-177 LIR ranging between EUR 500 and EUR 12,500, and, where concealment is intentional, the criminal anti-evasion surcharge of 10% to 90% of the evaded tax with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible manager.
Boxes 2300-2700 Tax computation: calculation of the corporate income tax under the progressive tariff, the 7% solidarity surcharge, the municipal business tax using the municipal coefficient of the commune of the registered office, the application of investment tax credits, and the statement of the net wealth tax base with the allocation of the EUR 500,000 minimum allowance and the minimum tax where applicable. Foreign tax credits are available only under an applicable double tax treaty and are computed line-by-line in the Form 300; non-resident shareholders are not entitled to Luxembourg investment tax credits. The municipal business tax coefficient is fixed by the commune and must match the registered office address. Late filing exposes the SARL to a 'taxation d'office' by the Luxembourg direct tax administration, a late-filing increase of up to 10% of the assessed corporate income tax under Article 181 LIR, late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized), administrative fines under Articles 174-177 LIR ranging between EUR 500 and EUR 12,500, and, where concealment is intentional, the criminal anti-evasion surcharge of 10% to 90% of the evaded tax with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible manager.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate statutory Luxembourg local-file threshold exists in the LIR, but a Luxembourg-resident SARL forming part of a multinational group with consolidated annual group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more must file or report country-by-country data and would be expected to maintain a Master File and Local File; in operational practice, the Luxembourg direct tax administration requests contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all material controlled transactions, and for a dropshipping SARL a Local File is strongly recommended where annual related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), the official trade and companies register for the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Title II, Chapter 6, Articles 101 to 132-24 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); related RCS implementing regulations and the Law of 19 December 2018 on company law reform.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif / Articles of Association (statuts) filed with the RCS, together with the mandatory supporting declarations and, where applicable, the notarially executed deed of incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The statutory minimum issued share capital for an SARL is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 25% before registration, i.e. a minimum of EUR 3,000, and must be deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the company's name. The funds remain blocked and are released only upon evidence of RCS registration; contributions in kind must be fully paid up at the time of execution of the articles and are subject to a contribution report where required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from CGDIS (Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps), structural load inspection for racked pallet storage, emergency lighting and extinguisher placement, and workplace clearance from ITM (Labour Inspectorate) under the applicable occupational health and safety code; the storage area must be physically separated from ignition sources and equipped with access-controlled entry.

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum issued share capital for an SARL is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 25% before registration, i.e. a minimum of EUR 3,000, and must be deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in the company's name. The funds remain blocked and are released only upon evidence of RCS registration; contributions in kind must be fully paid up at the time of execution of the articles and are subject to a contribution report where required.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SARL deed of incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and file it with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for entry in the Registre de Commerce et des Societes (RCS); this registration automatically allocates the 13-digit national identification number (matricule) used for all tax procedures. Step 2: Submit the VAT registration application (immatriculation a la TVA) to the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu using the standard formulaire for regular taxable persons; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by eight digits. Step 3: Register with the Administration des douanes et accises to obtain an EORI number (EORILU prefix) required for customs export declarations and ICS2 transit operations. Step 4: If the SARL hires employees, register with the Centre commun de la securite sociale (CCSS); this registration is mandatory before any payroll activity. Step 5: The foreign parent company is not required to obtain a separate Luxembourg tax ID for the SARL because the SARL is a Luxembourg resident taxpayer by virtue of its registered office and central administration; the AEDT will nonetheless record the foreign ownership and beneficial owner data under CRS/DAC6 due diligence. Average processing time from notarization to issuance of VAT number: three to six weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a non-transparent capital company (societe de capitaux) with legal personality and full resident tax status because its statutory seat and effective central administration are in Luxembourg. It is subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income, comprising: (i) corporate income tax (impot sur le revenu des collectivites, IRC) at the national headline rate of 17%; (ii) the unemployment fund solidarity surcharge of 7% on IRC, producing a combined national rate of 18.19%; (iii) municipal business tax (impot commercial communal, ICC) at a municipal multiplier yielding 6.75% in Luxembourg City for a combined aggregate ordinary CIT burden of approximately 24.94% for the 2024 tax year; and (iv) net wealth tax (impot sur la fortune, IFL) at 0.5% of taxable net wealth, with qualifying participations benefiting from the participation exemption. The SARL has no pass-through or transparent classification option under Luxembourg domestic law. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a non-transparent capital company (societe de capitaux) with legal personality and full resident tax status because its statutory seat and effective central administration are in Luxembourg. It is subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide income, comprising: (i) corporate income tax (impot sur le revenu des collectivites, IRC) at the national headline rate of 17%; (ii) the unemployment fund solidarity surcharge of 7% on IRC, producing a combined national rate of 18.19%; (iii) municipal business tax (impot commercial communal, ICC) at a municipal multiplier yielding 6.75% in Luxembourg City for a combined aggregate ordinary CIT burden of approximately 24.94% for the 2024 tax year; and (iv) net wealth tax (impot sur la fortune, IFL) at 0.5% of taxable net wealth, with qualifying participations benefiting from the participation exemption. The SARL has no pass-through or transparent classification option under Luxembourg domestic law. Territorial scope: foreign permanent establishment profits, foreign real estate income, and certain long-term shareholdings are exempt or relieved under Luxembourg tax treaties and the participation exemption regime (Article 166 LIR).

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election and imposes mandatory entity-level taxation on SARLs. Available statutory elections relevant to a petrochemical wholesale SARL include: (1) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT law permitting a single VAT number for closely related resident entities; (2) tax consolidation election (integration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR allowing a resident parent and its 95%-owned resident subsidiaries to pool results; (3) the participation exemption election under the SOPARFI regime for relief on qualifying dividends and capital gains, subject to holding period and subject-to-tax conditions; (4) accession to the EU cross-border loss relief mechanism under the Mergers Directive where applicable; (5) advance tax agreement (ATA) negotiations with the ACD for transfer pricing, permanent establishment, and financing structures; (6) application of the VAT postponed import accounting scheme (Article 17(6) of the VAT law) for imported petrochemical goods; and (7) mandatory application of the ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (EU Directive 2017/952 implemented by the law of 21 December 2018) with no elective dis-application, though limited elections between the primary and secondary rules are available in specific double-deduction scenarios. The VAT small-business exemption election is available only if annual turnover is below EUR 35,000 and is commercially irrelevant to this bulk trading entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is part of the eurozone and the EU single market; both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible, guaranteed under Articles 63 to 65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). There are no specific local currency restrictions or separate FX approval regimes for profit remittances, dividends, capital returns, or loan repayments. Withholding tax on dividends at 15% may apply under domestic law but is usually reduced to 0% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders; no additional FX-related hurdles apply to such remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Luxembourg. Export receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained in export accounts, converted to euro at the entity's discretion, or held as working capital without any mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL where material thresholds are exceeded, and to regular anti-money laundering (AML) channel reporting for cash-like instruments.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; the BCL acts as the monetary authority while the CSSF supervises banks and payment institutions; foreign exchange operations are generally unlicensed due to the principality of the euro area and EU Treaty provisions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for FX conversion and operational disbursements to European beneficiaries, provided the EMI holds a full EU electronic money institution licence (passportable under PSD2) and has a robust corporate onboarding programme. Legal constraints are significant for large bulk petrochemical polymers: most EMIs are prohibited from extending trade credit or issuing standalone documentary letters of credit, and they cannot replace a traditional bank for irrecoverable LC confirmations, import/export trade finance, or high-value physical commodity settlement. Cross-border EMIs headquartered outside the EEA face additional licensing limits under Article 9 of the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and are generally treated as low-viability for primary clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Luxembourg SARL receiving share capital or shareholder loans from a foreign parent, use SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) for major currencies; for euro-denominated injections from Europe, use SEPA Credit Transfer. The correct EU classification is 'FDI equity' or 'intercompany debt' under the BCL balance-of-payments scheme, and the payment reference should clearly distinguish 'KEP' (Kapital Einlage / share capital contribution) or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan) from trade payments. Obtain a board resolution confirming the contribution, record the inflow in the BCL statistical report where applicable, and ensure the bank's KYC file covers the ultimate shareholder. No FX licence is required; however, the bank will apply thorough verification of beneficial ownership and the source of funds before crediting the escrow or operational account.For bulk petrochemical trade settlements, primary clearing rails are (a) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for clean settlements against invoices, (b) Irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and settled by MT103/MT202 under UCP 600 and ISBP 745, and (c) negotiable bank drafts for trusted counterparties. For supplier payments to overseas refineries, an unbancable SARL may benefit from a 'back-to-back' LC structure where the Luxembourg bank issues or confirms a transferable documentary credit, and ultimately all outbound disbursements should be routed through one or two tier-1 correspondent banks to maintain a clear audit trail and avoid commingling with third-party funds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate headline rate for taxable income above EUR 175,000 (comprising 17% corporate income tax, 6.75% municipal business tax in Luxembourg City, and 1.19% solidarity surcharge equal to 7% of CIT); reduced aggregate rate of 22.80% applies on the first EUR 175,000 of taxable income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax for distributions under the standard rules; reduced to 0% for dividends paid to an EU parent company holding at least 10% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; treaty rates under most Luxembourg DTAs: 0% or 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders (minimum 10% shareholding), otherwise 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is no later than 31 May following the fiscal year-end for entities whose financial year coincides with the calendar year; for non-calendar year-ends, the deadline is within five months after the close of the fiscal year. An automatic extension to 31 December of the filing year may be granted where the return is prepared and signed by a certified Luxembourg tax adviser.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 group consolidated turnover and/or EUR 1,000,000 per related-party transaction category (thresholds under Luxembourg administrative Circular LIR n°56/1-56/2 of 12 April 2017 requiring contemporaneous Master File and Local File); these are not statutory hard limits but the tax administration expects documentation where such volumes are present.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation similar to the UAE or BVI, but the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) in Article 155 LIR and Luxembourg administrative practice require that a trading SARL maintains real economic substance: an adequately staffed local office, board meetings and strategic decisions in Luxembourg, principal entrepreneurial risk taking within the company, and full legal control over bank accounts and inventory. Failure to satisfy this substance is assessed by reference to OECD BEPS and the EU ATAD anti-abuse measures, resulting in possible denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from one to five years and fine from EUR 25,000 to EUR 50,000 under the Luxembourg Tax Offences Act; in addition, an automatic penalty of 10% of the evaded tax, monthly late-payment interest of 0.8%, and potential penal surcharge up to 50% of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate or sign the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; criminal conviction may lead to travel bans, freezing of personal assets, seizure of properties, and disqualification from serving as directors or managers of any Luxembourg legal entity.
Artificial Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of the GAAR leading to recharacterisation of the entity as tax transparent or non-resident; denial of DTA benefits and re-attribution of profit to a foreign permanent establishment; addition to the reassessed tax of a penalty up to 10% plus monthly late interest; possible revocation of prior tax rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the under-declared tax where the artificial construction was knowingly implemented; legal enforcement measures include civil restitution, provisional asset seizures, travel bans during the criminal investigation, and imprisonment for fraud in severe cases.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Incorrect Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under Article 56 LIR, increasing taxable income by the arm's length adjustment; if no good faith is proven, an additional penalty of 50% of the extra tax due; monthly late-payment interest of 0.8%; repeated offense may lead to criminal fines of EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the distorted pricing or signed false transfer pricing documentation can be subjected to personal liability for the additional tax and penalties; civil shareholders may claim damages; in aggravated cross-border cases, directors may face nontax criminal prosecution for facilitation of tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Avoidance on Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg SARL is held jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld amount; default surcharge of 10% of the withheld-sum plus monthly interest of 0.8%; an order for recovery may be issued against the company's assets and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who are aware of the distribution and fail to comply with withholding obligations are personally liable for the uncollected tax to the extent of their culpable participation; in addition, criminal conviction may impose travel bans and imprisonment if the intentional non-withholding is considered tax evasion.
AML / Financial Crime in Petrochemical Trade Payments

Statutory sanction: For the entity, administrative fine from the CSSF up to EUR 1,000,000; under the Luxembourg Criminal Code, fines up to EUR 2,000,000 for legal persons; directors or compliance officers can be imprisoned up to five years and fined up to EUR 1,250,000 for deliberate money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, asset freezing, travel bans, and permanent disqualification from managing financial or commercial entities; civil liability for reputational damage to the SARL and for third-party claims is also applicable.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum subscribed capital is €12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited into and blocked on a Luxembourg (or qualifying EU/EEA) bank account until RCS registration, evidenced by an Attestation de blocage issued by the credit institution; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's control report (réviseur d'entreprises agréé); the blocking certificate is released to the gérant only upon presentation of the RCS registration extract.

Funding & unlock path: No specific fire safety certification or structural load inspection is required for a pure administrative registered office in a standard office building; upon physical occupation, the employer must comply with the Luxembourg Labour Code, Book IV provisions on workplace safety and the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) rules, and the building owner must maintain the municipal fire safety certificates (Service d'incendie et de secours) covering emergency exits, extinguishing equipment, and electrical installations.

Paid-in capital rule: SARL minimum subscribed capital is €12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited into and blocked on a Luxembourg (or qualifying EU/EEA) bank account until RCS registration, evidenced by an Attestation de blocage issued by the credit institution; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's control report (réviseur d'entreprises agréé); the blocking certificate is released to the gérant only upon presentation of the RCS registration extract.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified digital service portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.lu) and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) for issuance of the 13-digit business registration number (matricule).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity (non-established in Luxembourg) forming or converting into a Luxembourg SARL, the sequential registration path is: (1) incorporate and file articles of association with the RCS via a Luxembourg notary, obtain the RCS registration number and 13-digit national business identifier (matricule) through MyGuichet.lu; (2) file the VAT registration application with the AEDT using official form 'Formulaire 640' (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA), which for EU-established applicants can be done directly, whereas non-EU applicants must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative (unless an exemption applies under Art. 65 VAT Law); (3) simultaneous registration for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICB), and net wealth tax (IFI) is performed automatically through the RCS transmission to the ACD; (4) obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) and, if needed, an EORI number for customs operations. Statutory deadlines: RCS filing within 1 month of formation; VAT registration must be requested before the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a resident capital company (société de capitaux) subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) at a headline rate of 15% plus solidarity surcharge (7% of IRC, giving 16.05% effective IRC), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) at an aggregate rate dependent on commune (typically 6.75%–11.25% for the City of Luxembourg), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune, IFI) at 0.5% on taxable net assets with minimum IFI charges (€535 standard, but €3,210 where qualifying financial fixed assets exceed €500,000). The SARL is subject to taxation on worldwide income as a resident entity; no pass-through is available to shareholders. The territorial scope is moderated by the participation exemption regime (exemption of qualifying dividends and capital gains under Art. 166 LIR and Art. 148bis LIR) and by tax treaties. Shareholders resident abroad are subject to withholding tax on distributions (15% standard, subject to treaty relief and the Luxembourg parent-subsidiary exemption of 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions or the Luxembourg participation exemption are met). — For a foreign entity (non-established in Luxembourg) forming or converting into a Luxembourg SARL, the sequential registration path is: (1) incorporate and file articles of association with the RCS via a Luxembourg notary, obtain the RCS registration number and 13-digit national business identifier (matricule) through MyGuichet.lu; (2) file the VAT registration application with the AEDT using official form 'Formulaire 640' (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA), which for EU-established applicants can be done directly, whereas non-EU applicants must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative (unless an exemption applies under Art. 65 VAT Law); (3) simultaneous registration for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICB), and net wealth tax (IFI) is performed automatically through the RCS transmission to the ACD; (4) obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) and, if needed, an EORI number for customs operations. Statutory deadlines: RCS filing within 1 month of formation; VAT registration must be requested before the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a resident capital company (société de capitaux) subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) at a headline rate of 15% plus solidarity surcharge (7% of IRC, giving 16.05% effective IRC), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) at an aggregate rate dependent on commune (typically 6.75%–11.25% for the City of Luxembourg), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune, IFI) at 0.5% on taxable net assets with minimum IFI charges (€535 standard, but €3,210 where qualifying financial fixed assets exceed €500,000). The SARL is subject to taxation on worldwide income as a resident entity; no pass-through is available to shareholders. The territorial scope is moderated by the participation exemption regime (exemption of qualifying dividends and capital gains under Art. 166 LIR and Art. 148bis LIR) and by tax treaties. Shareholders resident abroad are subject to withholding tax on distributions (15% standard, subject to treaty relief and the Luxembourg parent-subsidiary exemption of 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions or the Luxembourg participation exemption are met).

Available election forms

Luxembourg is a civil-law jurisdiction and does not apply the US 'check-the-box' system. Recognised statutory elections include: (a) fiscal unity / group taxation ('intégration fiscale') under Art. 164bis LIR for resident groups holding at least 95% of a qualifying Luxembourg/EU/EEA subsidiary, applicable to both IRC and ICC; (b) VAT group election ('assujetti unique') under Art. 60 of the VAT Law, allowing financially, economically and organisationally linked taxable persons to file VAT as a single taxable person; (c) option to tax real estate rentals (Art. 42(3) VAT Law) for supplies to taxable persons; (d) election to apply national preferential regimes such as the IP box (Art. 50bis LIR) for qualifying intellectual property income; (e) no elective hybrid mismatch arrangements are permitted — Luxembourg implements ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Art. 168ter LIR) which operate automatically, and taxpayers may apply for an advance tax agreement (tax ruling) through the ACD's Ruling Unit (Circulaire LIR 331) to confirm tax treatment in advance. For indirect tax, an EU-established non-resident trader may register directly without a fiscal representative, while a non-EU trader requires a fiscal representative unless the transaction is a B2B reverse-charge supply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis turnover threshold for non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg: VAT registration (or reverse-charge designation of a local recipient) is required from the first taxable transaction. For Luxembourg-established small enterprises, a €35,000 annual turnover exemption applies under Art. 57 VAT Law (except for certain sectors, e.g., new means of transport and specific goods). For intra-EU distance sales of goods into Luxembourg by EU sellers, the VAT registration/OSS obligation is triggered once intra-Community distance sales exceed €10,000 per calendar year (default EU threshold applicable from Luxembourg as the Member State of establishment). Cross-border B2B services supplied by a non-established trader to a Luxembourg VAT-registered customer are reverse-charged to the Luxembourg customer, and the non-established supplier has no VAT registration obligation for such supplies, though it must register if it sells goods to non-taxable persons or supplies B2C electronic services above the OSS threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Art. 36 of the Luxembourg VAT Law to (a) supplies of goods dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the European Union (export), and (b) direct services linked to those export goods (loading, transport, brokerage, and other intermediary services). To claim 0% VAT, the evidence must establish: (i) physical export of the goods from the EU within the legally recognised period (generally the goods must leave the EU within 90 days of the supply, a practice confirmed by AEDT guidance); (ii) possession of the customs export declaration (DAU/AES) accepted by the customs office of exit; (iii) documentary proof of transport (CMR, B/L, AWB, freight invoice) and of the supplier’s ownership / legal power to dispose of the goods; (iv) for intra-EU supplies, a valid customer VAT identification number confirmed via VIES and inclusion in the recapitulative statement/EC Sales List; and (v) commercial documentation showing the customer’s identity, place of residence / establishment, and the nature/quantity of goods. Critical nuance for a re-invoicing hub: if the Luxembourg SARL acts purely as an intermediary that never touches the goods (e.g., goods move from a third-country supplier directly to a third-country buyer), the transaction creates no Luxembourg VAT nexus, and the margin is taxable as a service in Luxembourg only if the recipient or the place of effective use qualifies under Luxembourg B2B place-of-supply rules; physical export zero-rating is available only where the goods are actually exported from the EU by the Luxembourg taxable person. The zero-rating of services under Art. 36(3) VAT Law is available for transport, handling, and commission services directly connected to export goods, provided the services are rendered directly to the exporter/consignee and the goods are in the course of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU, issued through the AES/ECS export system, evidencing the export exit and the exporter's EORI number)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) or CMR (Convention Marchandises Routières) transport document evidencing physical movement out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing settlement of the export sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, sales contract, and packing list (consistency of weights, values, and descriptions with the customs declaration)
  • Proof of customer status: valid EU VAT identification number (VIES validation) or third-country customer certificate/registration
  • Recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) and Intrastat declaration for intra-EU supplies, where applicable
  • Freight and insurance invoices, warehouse receipts, or terminal handling receipts evidencing the transport chain

No real-time fiscal clearance (CTC) or national mandatory e-invoicing regime is in force as of the current date. The Luxembourg Government’s transposition of the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package is proceeding via the laws of 18 June 2024 and 12 July 2024, which introduce: (i) mandatory structured e-invoicing (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 or Factur-X) for domestic B2B transactions between Luxembourg-established VAT payers effective from 1 January 2026; (ii) subsequent transactional data reporting (e-reporting of sales data to the AEDT) expected from 1 January 2028; and (iii) voluntary use of the EU OSS and IOSS regimes for distance sales. Currently, AEDT accepts PDF, EDI, and other unstructured formats for invoicing, while the mandatory e-invoicing obligation applies to invoices exchanged between Luxembourg-established taxable persons; cross-border e-invoicing remains governed by Directive 2014/55/EU and the receiving customer’s national rules. There is no digital stamping, QR-code clearance, or AEDT pre-approval API for invoices at this stage; implementation of the Luxembourg national e-invoicing infrastructure will require API-based clearance through the official national Peppol access point from 2026.

Luxembourg tax authorities (AEDT and ACD) treat export zero-rating as a high-risk area for re-invoicing and trading intermediaries. The standard VAT assessment limitation period is 5 years from the end of the year in which the VAT became claimable; this extends to 10 years in cases of fraud, wilful misconduct, or where a VAT declaration was not filed. Where export evidence is missing or internally inconsistent, the AEDT will reclassify the supply as taxable and immediately assess VAT at the standard 17% rate plus default interest (intérêts de retard) of 0.6% per month (7.2% per annum). Administrative fines under Art. 78 of the VAT Law range up to €25,000 per offence for incorrect invoicing or failure to substantiate, and under Art. 396 of the General Tax Law (AO) tax evasion fines may reach €250,000, increased to 5 times the evaded tax for repeated or organised fraud; criminal liability extends to imprisonment of up to 5 years for serious fraud. Additionally, the AEDT may claw back intermediary-commission input VAT where the underlying transaction is not substantiated, and may impose a penalty of 10% of the VAT under-assessed for simple negligence. The Luxembourg law does not prescribe a statutory maximum deadline for FX repatriation; the 180-day parameter used in this dossier reflects a prudent contractual benchmark generally accepted by tax and AML authorities for proving the commercial substance of cross-border re-invoicing flows. Non-repatriation beyond the contractual term, without an arm’s-length explanation, will be treated as a distribution or a non-recognised transfer pricing adjustment, triggering Luxembourg withholding tax, net wealth tax, and potential administrative penalty exposure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under the euro-area legal framework. No general exchange control restrictions exist; profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repayment, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred in EUR or any convertible currency. However, the SARL must comply with anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) verification requirements, Luxembourg direct tax reporting rules, and economic substance expectations for re-invoicing/treasury activities. In practice, cross-border payments require correct ECB Balance of Payments (BoP) and International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) coding, and bank transfers must be accompanied by accurate purpose codes (statistical reasons) to avoid delay or rejection. The BCL may request underlying documentation for statistical integrity, but there is no legal ceiling, prior approval, or repatriation restriction on profits or capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export proceeds and trade receivables, including re-invoicing income, may be retained in any currency freely, held in offshore accounts, or converted at the market exchange rate. No deadline applies for repatriation into Luxembourg. Nevertheless, the SARL engaged in re-invoicing intermediation must record all export/import settlements for VAT, CIT (corporate income tax), and statistical reporting; proceeds routed through Luxembourg should be matched against the underlying commercial contracts to satisfy substance and beneficial ownership analysis.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Luxembourg Financial Sector Supervisory Commission (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier, CSSF) and the Ministry of Finance; as an EU member state, Luxembourg applies the euro-area foreign exchange framework administered by the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium to Medium-High, but with legal constraints. Luxembourg has a regulated e-money institution (EMI) regime under the CSSF, allowing pass-through payment accounts and cross-border clearing without full-scale banking license. Use of non-EU fintech or unregulated EMI/PSP is not acceptable for substantive treasury operations and may trigger AML rating escalation, loss of tax substance, or bank due-diligence rejection. EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Luxembourg-based e-money institutions or licensed payment institutions passporting from another EU state) are viable for niche collections, corporate expense cards, and high-frequency small-value payments, but the primary operating account and trade finance letters of credit (LCs) must remain with a Tier 1 credit institution. The SARL should avoid holding significant recurring trade balances in an EMI because EMIs cannot provide documentary credit services, working capital loans, NDF (non-deliverable forward) execution, or full treasury counterparty risk management.

High-risk jurisdiction exposure (supplier or customer located in sanctioned, tax-blacklisted, or higher-risk jurisdictions) or involvement of politically exposed persons (PEPs)

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening output against EU, UN, US OFAC, and UK lists; negative news and adverse media search results; country-specific risk assessment; third-party due diligence reports (e.g., World-Check, Dow Jones) on each material counterparty; corporate registers and trade references for counterparties; certificates of good standing; declaration confirming no direct or indirect connection to designated persons; PEP status confirmation and enhanced monitoring plan if applicable.
Unusual payment instructions, such as disbursement to third-party beneficiaries not party to the underlying trade, or use of cash-heavy intermediaries

Root cause: Unusual payment instructions, such as disbursement to third-party beneficiaries not party to the underlying trade, or use of cash-heavy intermediaries

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the Luxembourg SARL must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires with the exact corporate name, IBAN, BIC/SWIFT, and a proper purpose code corresponding to the nature of capital (e.g., ECB BoP category 'E' for equity, or subordinated loan 'A.2' with a loan agreement). Equity share capital up to or exceeding the minimum statutory amount may be contributed in cash, in kind, or via setoff, but for bank routing the strictest anti-money-laundering practice requires the transfer to reference 'EUROPEAN UNION - CAPITAL INJECTION - SUBSCRIPTION' plus invoice or share register reference. If the contribution is by way of shareholder loan, the SARL must provide a loan agreement, board resolution, and audited audited financial projection; the transfer purpose code must reflect 'intercompany loan' to prevent misclassification as operating revenue. For quarantine of funds, banks may redirect the incoming wire to a dedicated 'capital contribution temporary account' until the UBO and source of funds (SOF) are verified; the SARL should pre-submit the corporate wallet documentation, articles, UBO register, board resolution, and source-of-funds evidence to shorten crediting. The BCL and CSSF do not require a formal FIL (foreign investment license), but sectors are not relevant; for trade intermediary no pre-approval is required beyond standard KYC and the euro-denominated clearing network (TARGET2).Cross-border disbursements for trade settlements use the SWIFT Global Payments Innovation (gpi) infrastructure leveraging MT103 for direct bank wire transfers, commercial letters of credit (LC) via MT700 and MT720, and documentary collection (D/P, D/A) via MT400/MT750 series. The SARL treasury desk should maintain each local currency-water or EUR account and open LC terms aligned with Incoterms 2020, with purpose codes indicating import of goods, services, or commission fees. Standard clearing rails: (i) TT (telegraphic transfer) or MT103 for supplier invoice settlements, with all fields structured to enable the EUR correspondent bank to auto-match the invoice; (ii) 'DPU' (Documentary Payment Undertaking) LC under ICC UCP 600 rules for high-value or cash-cycled transactions where a confirming bank is sought; (iii) for intra-EU payments, instant SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) or standard SEPA CT instruments, which are not SWIFT but are routed via the EBA CLEARING EURO1/STEP2; (iv) for emerging-market settlements, NDF-in-HKD/CNY/INR/BRL must be accompanied by a EUR MT202 cover and a separate MT103 with the underlying shipping documents sent through the banking channel.

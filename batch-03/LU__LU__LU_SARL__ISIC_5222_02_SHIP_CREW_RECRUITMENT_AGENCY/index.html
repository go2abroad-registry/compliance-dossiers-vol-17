Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must subscribe a minimum share capital of EUR 12,000. At formation, cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 25% of the subscribed amount, with a statutory floor of EUR 3,000, deposited in a blocked bank account in Luxembourg in the name of the company in formation or held by the notary until registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up and certified. No separate escrow authority exists beyond notarial or banking control.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Grand Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) for the registered office, including a structural load inspection for office occupancy and an evacuation plan. Occupational clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) confirming workplace safety, first-aid equipment, electrical installation conformity, and compliance with Luxembourg labour law for office workers.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Luxembourg Registration, Estates, and VAT Authority; online portals: MyGuichet.lu and aedt.public.lu for VAT registrations and tax filings. Direct corporate income tax registration remains with the Administration des contributions directes (ACD).

E-government filing portal

A foreign or newly incorporated SARL must first be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national identification number ('matricule d'identification') issued by the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). VAT registration is then made separately with the AEDT via Form 246 for non-established taxable persons or through the MyGuichet.lu portal for established entities. Supporting documents typically include: certificate of incorporation, excerpts from the RCS, articles of association, postal address in Luxembourg or a local tax representative, bank account details, and a signed declaration of intended activity. The AEDT assigns a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU-trademarkamadeup) upon successful submission; approval generally takes 1 to 3 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – The SARL is a fully taxable Luxembourg-resident company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on a worldwide income basis, with credit/relief for permanent establishments abroad. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under Luxembourg domestic law. — A foreign or newly incorporated SARL must first be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national identification number ('matricule d'identification') issued by the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – The SARL is a fully taxable Luxembourg-resident company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on a worldwide income basis, with credit/relief for permanent establishments abroad. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under Luxembourg domestic law.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a 'check-the-box' election regime. Available formal elections include: VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) for fiscal unity; election to form a qualifying joint venture and tax consolidation regime for a 95%-owned group (Tax-consolidated group filing); election for the 80% participation exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive to avoid withholding tax; and elections for the enhanced intellectual-property box regime. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 1 and 2 are mandatory and do not permit elective opt-outs except as prescribed for dividends and controlled foreign company income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established Luxembourg taxable person, the annual turnover exemption threshold is EUR 35,000 under the small-business franchise scheme. For non-established foreign entities, there is no threshold: any supply of goods or services physically performed or deemed supplied in Luxembourg triggers a mandatory VAT registration from the first euro unless the place of supply is outside Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to intra-Community and third-country exports of goods where the goods physically leave the EU and the exporter retains transport evidence. For maritime crew recruitment services, VAT is zero-rated under Article 148 of the EU VAT Directive when such services are supplied for direct needs of a qualifying sea-going vessel used for international navigation, or when the supplied crew services are placed at the disposal of a non-EU ship-operator for commercial voyages. For purely B2B services to a non-EU taxable person outside of those maritime exceptions, the place of supply is outside Luxembourg and no Luxembourg VAT is charged, though this is not formally an export zero-rating. The taxpayer must document the non-EU status of the recipient (VAT/GST identification or equivalent), the nature of the vessel and its qualifying international use, and the commercial reality of the overseas delivery of services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD customs declaration in destination territory) or Intrastat dispatch records for intra-EU movements
  • Bill of Lading, sea-waybill, or air waybill evidencing actual transport to a third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice and intercompany/service agreement describing the exact crew recruitment service supplied
  • Packing list and transport insurance certificate (for goods) evidence of departure from Luxembourg territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation showing settlement received from the non-EU counterparty

Luxembourg mandates electronic invoicing for business-to-government (B2G) invoices through PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 since 1 April 2022, processed via the Luxembourg interoperability platform under the L.2020 law. For B2B transactions, e-invoicing remains optional and there is no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous-transaction-control (CTC) reporting, and no XML/digital stamping regime in force as of the current Luxembourg VAT law. Taxpayers must however retain digital invoice audit files to satisfy AEDT inspection requirements.

The AEDT conducts substantive audit review of zero-rated export claims, examining the movement of goods or the place of service in conjunction with the transport documentation, the vessel's registration and voyage records, and the payment trail. A fraudulent or unsubstantiated zero-rating claim is subject to, without limitation: immediate VAT reassessment at 17% plus default interest (legal interest plus 4% per month for late payment), a 10% increase in the VAT due where negligence is proven, administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 for deliberate violation, and criminal penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment for tax fraud under Luxembourg's revised General Tax Law. A retrospective clawback may reach back to the date of the original claim and triggers a refund not being allowed until the corrected assessment is issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital; no exchange control approval, no prior administrative authorization, and no capital-export restrictions apply to profit remittance, dividend distributions, or inbound equity subscriptions. Practical hurdles are limited to (i) mandatory BCL balance-of-payments (BoP) statistical reporting for resident entities executing cross-border settlements of EUR 12,500 or more, (ii) binding EU/UN sanctions and targeted restrictive measures, and (iii) the statutory Luxembourg 15% dividend withholding tax (reducible under applicable tax treaties). No sector-specific restriction exists for maritime crew recruitment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg and the euro area impose no export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for crew-agency service fees, manning fees, or disbursement reimbursement received from shipowners. Such receipts may be held in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in offshore or onshore accounts without central-bank conversion or repatriation; they must only be reported for BCL statistical/BoP purposes and matched to the underlying manning agreements for transfer-pricing and substance documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the national authority licensing banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions; the European Central Bank (ECB) holds ultimate monetary and capital-movement policy competence for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for ancillary multi-currency crew-payroll disbursement, but not for principal corporate treasury functions. CSSF-licensed or EU-passported electronic-money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 can provide faster low-value cross-border payment rails, FX conversion, and seafarer payroll cards, subject to client-fund safeguarding rules and the EU Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU 2015/847). Legal constraints: an EMI cannot hold the notarized capital escrow, cannot grant credit, may only deploy payment accounts rather than current accounts, and must be passported within the EEA; therefore the SARL must retain a full-service Luxembourg credit institution as its primary banking counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) foreign shareholder initiates an SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent ISO 20022) credit in EUR to the SARL's capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution; (2) the wire should carry the OECD/BCL BoP purpose classification code for direct investment in equity capital (e.g., direct-investment equity code per ECB BoP/CPC nomenclature) and a clear narrative referencing the SARL company name, the notarial deed of contribution, and the notarial file; (3) the receiving bank verifies sanctions, UBO/RBE alignment, source-of-funds, and BCL BoP reporting thresholds (EUR 12,500+ per transaction); (4) the Luxembourg notary certifies the capital as fully paid before release from escrow; and (5) the SARL records the equity injection in its statutory accounts and BCL external-position survey.Primary clearing rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001/pain.002 ISO 20022) via EBICS for euro-denominated crew salaries paid to EEA bank accounts;

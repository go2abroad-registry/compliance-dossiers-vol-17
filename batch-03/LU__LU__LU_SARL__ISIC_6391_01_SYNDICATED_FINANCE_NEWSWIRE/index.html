Competent tax authority

Direct taxation: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority (45 boulevard Roosevelt, L-2983 Luxembourg); indirect taxation and VAT registration: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); customs supervision: Administration des douanes et accises. E-government filing portal

1) Execute the notarized deed of incorporation of the SARL and deposit it with the Luxembourg Business Registers for inscription in the Trade and Companies Register (RCSL). 2) Upon inscription, the ACD automatically attributes the RNE national identification number (matricule), which simultaneously serves as the corporate income tax, net wealth tax and employer withholding tax account number; no separate income-tax registration is required for the SARL. 3) For VAT, file the dematerialized VAT registration application with the AEDT through the e-TVA/myGuichet channel; a manager, legal representative or duly mandated tax adviser may file; the AEDT delivers the LU-prefixed 8-digit VAT number, normally within 5 to 15 working days, and simultaneously enrols the taxpayer in the periodical VAT return circuit. 4) A foreign non-established entrepreneur whose supplies are taxable in Luxembourg must register before the first taxable supply, unless the transaction falls within the EU OSS, non-EU OSS or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS); the AEDT may request the appointment of a Luxembourg-based tax representative where the applicant has no EU establishment and no EU residence. 5) Non-resident shareholders of the SARL are not subject to registration; the SARL is the taxpayer and, where applicable, withholds at source on distributions paid. 6) After registration, the SARL must display the matricule and VAT number on all invoices, customs declarations and OSS returns and maintain an electronic archive of registration certificates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg applies no US-style check-the-box election; the SARL is legally opaque and automatically subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) on its worldwide income, unless the residence tie-breaker in an applicable double tax treaty relocates fiscal residence. Elective regimes available to the SARL include: (a) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) on application to the AEDT, limited to Luxembourg-established taxable persons; (b) waiver of VAT exemption on real-estate lettings and transfers under the VAT Law; (c) IP Box relief under Article 50bis of the Income Tax Law (LIR), granting an 80% exemption on qualifying net IP income with OECD Nexus-compliant tracking; (d) EU OSS, non-EU OSS and IOSS elections for distance sales and business-to-consumer electronically supplied services; (e) substantive, non-elective participation exemption on dividends and capital gains where the qualifying holding and holding-period conditions are met; (f) hybrid-mismatch treatment is mandatory and non-elective under the ATAD 2 transposition of 21 December 2017, with no check-the-box override available.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) enjoys unrestricted convertibility in Luxembourg. As a Eurozone member state implementing Articles 63-66 TFEU on free movement of capital and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, Luxembourg applies zero exchange-control restrictions on either current account (subscription revenues, trade settlements, operating expenses) or capital account (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation, liquidation proceeds) transactions. No prior CSSF, BCL, or Ministry of Finance authorisation is required for inbound or outbound transfers denominated in EUR or foreign currency. Practical profit remittance hurdles are limited to taxation, not conversion: a Luxembourg SARL distributing dividends triggers 15% domestic withholding tax, which is reduced to 0% when the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) applies, or mitigated under bilateral double-tax treaties (e.g., 5% rate under the 1996 Luxembourg-USA treaty on portfolio dividends of 10% or more shareholding). Corporate treasury operations may repatriate capital and advance intercompany loans freely; the BCL only requests statistical declaration via the e-file portal COREP/liquidity declarations for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500, not as a permission-based control. Sanctions screening (CSSF-issued EU/UN restrictive measures list) and CBUAE-style reporting are the only quotidian compliance duties, neither operating as a quantifiable limitation on convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Luxembourg. The Corporation of the Belgian-Luxembourg Economic Union, and Luxembourg in particular, abandoned export-proceeds surrender obligations upon accession to the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. For the target SARL's cross-border newswire subscription revenue (the economic equivalent of 'export proceeds' for a services/API sale), the EUR-denominated incoming SWIFT MT103s or SEPA SCT Inst payments may be retained freely in EUR, converted at any time via any authorized FX counterparty, or transferred to any third-party payee without any BCL haircut or conversion quota. The only legal filing is statistical: a monthly balance-of-payments declaration via BCL 'Reporting on External Statistics' web interface when aggregate receipts/payments with non-residents exceed EUR 12,500, submitted within 30 days, with no obligation to convert, retain, or surrender a defined percentage to the monetary authority.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem and pursuant to Article 107 TFEU, Council Regulation (EC) No 1103/97 and Regulation (EC) No 974/98; banking supervision is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while the BCL maintains the balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation for cross-border flows above EUR 12,500 per individual transaction under the BCL 'Reporting on External Statistics' framework (BOINB/LU reporting circulars).

Late filing penalties: automatic 10% surcharge on assessed tax; an additional €1,000–€5,000 administrative fine for failure to file; interest on late payment at 0.8% per month compounded. Imprisonment possible for fraudulent non-filing.
Box 4 Declared taxable income after legal deductions and participation exemption For 100% participation exemption, attach Schedule E and provide permanent establishment or shareholding documentation. Non-resident shareholders do not file individually at entity level. Late filing penalties: automatic 10% surcharge on assessed tax; an additional €1,000–€5,000 administrative fine for failure to file; interest on late payment at 0.8% per month compounded. Imprisonment possible for fraudulent non-filing.
Box 5 Aggregate tax credits and withholding tax relief claimed Claim foreign tax credits by filing supporting TRC and proof of underlying tax with the return. Late filing penalties: automatic 10% surcharge on assessed tax; an additional €1,000–€5,000 administrative fine for failure to file; interest on late payment at 0.8% per month compounded. Imprisonment possible for fraudulent non-filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 150,000 for financial (with interest or royalties) or service transactions, and EUR 1 million for other transactions; Master File required when group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million; CbC report due for groups with EUR 750 million consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: LU Economic Substance Requirements apply under Luxembourg law according to EU ATAD, OECD BEPS, and administrative guidelines. The SARL must demonstrate adequate personnel, office premises, decision-making in Luxembourg, and accounting records. For a financial newswire operating via API, substantive activities such as contract drafting, data sourcing, invoicing, and server infrastructure must be managed in Luxembourg. Zero-substance shell arrangements face challenge under the 2018 Luxembourg 'Law on the Taxation of Parent and Subsidiary', leading to denial of benefits under DTA, tax residency issues, or penalty of €10,000–€50,000 for missing substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Recovery of evaded tax, plus interest (0.8%/month) and penalties: up to 100% of tax deficiency (fraud penalty) and criminal fines up to €250,000. For criminal tax fraud, imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for company tax debts if they were aware of tax evasion; travel bans and asset freezes possible; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment.
Shell Company Abuse (No Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of tax treaty benefits, denial of Luxembourg tax residence status, exposure to domestic anti-abuse provisions, and administrative fines equal to 5% of the unduly claimed tax advantages. Annual return void, plus forced liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors held personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes if the shell substance is deemed a sham; a court may order civil restitution of tax advantages and restrict leaving Luxembourg until settlement.
AML/TFS Violation (Financial Newswire)

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg law (AML5/AML6 implementation), administrative penalties up to 5% of global turnover, minimum €10,000, and revocation of the business license. A violation of asset-freeze regulations leads to fine up to €1 million or 10% of total assets.

Director personal liability: Directors ousted from their position and disqualified from management; criminal liability for money laundering complicity, imprisonment up to 10 years and fine up to €1 million. Liquidators may pierce the veil based on personal fault.
Transfer Pricing Benchmarking Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment plus secondary adjustment (notional distribution tax at 15%) and penalty of 25% of adjusted profit. Late TP documentation filing triggers €5,000 daily penalty up to a maximum €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for tax adjustments if they approved the mispricing; risk of civil restitution actions from foreign tax authorities; in cross-border cases, Interpol red notices for deliberate fraud.

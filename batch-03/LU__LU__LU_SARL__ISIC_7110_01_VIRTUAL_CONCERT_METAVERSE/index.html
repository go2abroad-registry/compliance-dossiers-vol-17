Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended — Title VII (SARL), Articles 710-1 to 710-8 (formation, minimum capital, RCS registration), Article 710-7 (notarial or private deed), Article 710-17 (publication in RESA); Law of 15 May 2024 on digitalisation and modernisation of company law, in force since 30 November 2024.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000, 100% subscribed and paid in cash at incorporation; each share must have a nominal value of EUR 1 or a multiple thereof. The funds must be blocked for the account of the SARL in a Luxembourg credit institution or deposited with the Caisse de Consignation, evidenced by a blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) which is released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety conformity of the registered office under the Commune of Luxembourg fire prevention regulations (Service d'incendie et de secours), including smoke detectors, evacuation signage, and annual fire-safety audits; occupational health and safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) before employees occupy the premises; mandatory employer registration with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) for workplace accident coverage.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Luxembourg-resident SARL is a fully taxable corporate entity subject to unlimited tax liability on worldwide profits, comprising corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune), at a combined headline CIT rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City; no fiscal transparency/pass-through eligibility is available for an SARL under standard law, and non-resident shareholders are not regarded as taxable co-entrepreneurs.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply US-style check-the-box elections; entity classification follows commercial legal form. Available statutory elections include: (i) optional group taxation (fiscal unity / régime de groupe) for a resident parent and qualifying 95%-held resident subsidiaries; (ii) participation-exemption formalities (80% of dividends and capital gains exempt, subject to holding/recording conditions met at the time of disposal); (iii) IP box opt-in granting an 80% exemption on qualifying IP-derived income; (iv) VAT grouping election for closely linked Luxembourg entities; and (v) OSS registration elections (Union OSS for intra-EU B2C digital services and non-Union OSS for extra-EU services). ATAD hybrid mismatch provisions (Law of 23 December 2019) restrict deductible-payment and double-deduction outcomes, and Luxembourg has not implemented a separate hybrid-mismatch filing election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to non-established foreign businesses: any taxable supply or reverse-charge transaction in Luxembourg triggers mandatory VAT registration from the first euro. Resident small businesses may claim the Art. 57 exemption where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000 (exclusive of VAT), with a right to opt for normal taxation. For intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C supply of digital services, the Union-wide annual threshold is EUR 10,000 below which Luxembourg VAT at domestic rates applies; above the threshold, the destination-state VAT must be collected through the Union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Art. 42 of the Luxembourg VAT Law applies to: (i) direct supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU; (ii) supplies of goods subsequently exported within 24 months in connection with contract work; and (iii) services directly linked to exported goods (transport, loading, storage, handling) and international transport services. For the virtual-concert business model: B2B digital-event services supplied to an EU customer fall outside Luxembourg VAT by virtue of the Art. 44 place-of-supply rule (taxed at customer establishment under reverse charge); B2C digital-event services to EU consumers are taxable at the destination-state rate and reportable through the OSS scheme; B2C services to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (no Luxembourg VAT, no export zero-rating claim required). Zero-rating of physical goods requires commercial and documentary proof of export, must be evidenced before the AEDT on request, and the supplier must hold full records at the moment of invoicing.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document (SAD) lodged in the Luxembourg/European AE Surface System (AES) showing the exporter as declarant and MRN reference
  • Entry in the exporter's records (EiR) or customs warehouse/authorised-economic-operator clearance evidence
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR international consignment note (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice expressly referencing the Luxembourg VAT number and the Art. 42 zero-rate application
  • Proof of EU exit: customs barcode, exit summary declaration receipt, or transport-document destination stamp confirming arrival outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or counterpart bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence showing export proceeds credited to the Luxembourg SARL account within the prescribed period
  • Export customer purchase order, contract and delivery confirmation identifying the non-EU customer as invoice recipient

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in Luxembourg with invoices exchanged through the PEPPOL network. On 27 March 2025 the Council of Government approved a draft law introducing mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2027 and for B2C transactions starting 1 January 2028; the system will operate on the national e-DELTA exchange platform with interoperability through PEPPOL. Luxembourg currently applies a post-audit control model rather than real-time fiscal clearance: no continuous transaction controls or tax-authority electronic stamping exist, but taxpayers must report SAF-T accounting files in XML upon request and file periodic VAT returns (Formulaire TVA) electronically on a monthly, quarterly or annual basis depending on turnover.

The AEDT conducts post-clearance audits with a standard five-year limitation period, extended to ten years for fraudulent conduct, during which export-supporting documentation must be produced; absence of documentary proof causes the 0% rate to be reversed and VAT at 17% becomes due. Administrative fines under Arts. 183-185 of the VAT Law range from EUR 250 to EUR 50,000 (up to EUR 250,000 for wilful violations), and late-payment interest accrues at the statutory legal rate applied to additional assessments. Deliberate fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax fraud (escroquerie fiscale) under the General Tax Law and VAT-specific provisions, punishable by criminal fines and imprisonment of up to five years, with possible publication of the conviction and disqualification of directors; clawback assessments may retroactively reach the entire ten-year period for fraud and are secured by a legal lien on the taxpayer's movable assets.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account for a Luxembourg SARL. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are legally unrestricted; the only outbound frictions are the standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (15% domestic, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying parent company, or to reduced treaty rates) and normal tax-residence documentation. No import/export licences, no repatriation authorisations, and no limitations on holding foreign-currency accounts apply to an ISIC 7110 virtual-performance entity. Non-resident ownership is permitted and does not affect convertibility. All cross-border payments above the EUR 12,500 statistical threshold must be reported by the resident bank to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, but this is a statistical filing, not a control measure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no repatriation quota for export receipts or service revenues. The SARL may freely retain USD, USDC-proceeds converted to fiat, or any other permitted currency in a bank or regulated VASP account. The only obligation is bookkeeping and prudential valuation: cash balances in foreign currency must be revalued at each closing date under Luxembourg GAAP (Lux GAAP), and any crypto-asset holdings in USDC that remain unconverted for more than one day must be recognised at fair value with an impairment test, triggering no regulatory capital charge but creating balance-sheet volatility.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem; banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no independent exchange-control institution; capital movements are governed by Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Directive 88/361/EEC, and no foreign-exchange approval or licensing authority exists for ordinary current or capital transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility under strict constraints. An EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institution or payment institution may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and provide SEPA, card acquiring and payment-initiation services to the SARL; Stripe, acting through its Irish-authorised payment institution, is fully viable for card and SEPA collection into an IBAN. However, the target rail 'Cryptocurrency_USDC' is outside the scope of an EMI licence: an EMI cannot accept, custody or convert USDC without itself holding a Luxembourg VASP registration (Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering, as amended, and CSSF guidance on virtual assets) or without using a separate regulated crypto-custodian. Offshore non-EEA EMIs (UK, Singapore, UAE) have no EU passport and would require either a Luxembourg branch authorisation or an EU partner-EMI arrangement; practical onboarding of such an entity as the sole bank is Low. The recommended structure is: (i) Stripe Ireland for fiat card/SEPA rails; (ii) a German or Luxembourg-licensed crypto-custodian (e.g., a Bafin-authorised crypto bank or a CSSF-registered VASP) for USDC receipt and immediate EUR conversion; and (iii) a tier-1 Luxembourg bank as the master settlement account, receiving only EUR.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL minimum capital (EUR 12,000; practical working capital EUR 50,000–150,000) must be transferred from the shareholder's own bank account as a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to the Luxembourg notary's escrow account with remittance field 70 stating 'Capital subscription — Cash contribution for subscription of shares of LU_SARL pursuant to notarised deed dated [date]'. The receiving bank will classify the payment as 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital' for BCL balance-of-payments statistics; aggregate inflows above EUR 12,500 are automatically reported, with no prior exchange-control approval. The structure must be share capital, not shareholder loan, to avoid the notary certification of contribution difficulties; if a shareholder loan is used, a signed written loan agreement is mandatory for amounts above EUR 350,000 to prevent thin-capitalisation recharacterisation, and the interest rate must be at arm's length and subject to Lux GAAP accrual. USDC proceeds are not acceptable as a capital contribution: a Luxembourg notary requires actual book-money credit in a bank account or a certified bank transfer; therefore, the UBO must convert any crypto to EUR through a regulated exchange and execute the bank transfer from the same beneficial-owner account to preserve a clean audit trail.Recommended clearing rails in order of hierarchy: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for all EU/EEA artist, licensor and infrastructure payments — next-day settlement with zero marginal cost and a full audit trail in the bank statement; (2) SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for sub-EUR 100,000 royalty splits requiring same-second value; (3) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA outbound royalties (e.g., US/UK artists), with field 70 containing the invoice reference and field 72 stating 'BEN ONLY' to push all charges to the beneficiary; no Luxembourg currency-approval requirement attaches to any outbound payment; (4) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) are legally permissible but economically sub-optimal for service-based virtual-concert royalties and should be reserved for platform-technology co-production contracts exceeding EUR 500,000 where a performance guarantee is required; (5) USDC outbound payments are not a recognised settlement rail under Luxembourg law: if a contract obligates a crypto-denominated payout, the obligation should be net-settled by the EU-licensed VASP converting EUR to USDC at the beneficiary's request, with the SARL's own bank account remaining EUR-only; (6) all outbound disbursements must be supported by an underlying invoice, signed contract, and beneficial-owner attestations retained for at least five years under CSSF record-keeping obligations; a standing payment-authorisation matrix with dual control above EUR 10,000 is expected by the onboarding bank.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD FX forward with a Luxembourg credit institution, maximum tenor 12 months, executed only against a contracted pipeline of US-stadium or USD-denominated ticketing settlements, documented in the SARL treasury policy and subject to a bank ISDA/CSA or master FX agreementFX swap (FXS) for temporary EUR/USD bridging of settlement timing gaps between Stripe payout dates, artist disbursement dates, and USDC conversion datesEuropean-style foreign-exchange option (EUR call / USD put) to hedge a committed multi-month global artist tour schedule without locking in an unfavourable forward rateCross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS) only where the share-capital injection is ultimately financed by a USD-denominated parent loan converted to EUR; the swap must be plain-vanilla, non-leveraged, and aligned with the EU Benchmark Regulation and EMIR clearing thresholdsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are technically permissible under Luxembourg law but are not a prudential instrument for EUR/USD because the euro is fully deliverable under the single-currency regime; if an NDF is proposed for an inconvertible market currency arising from ticket reseller receipts, the bank will require a written commercial-risk dossier proving genuine exposure, otherwise it will be treated as speculative and declined under the SARL's treasury mandate

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility under strict constraints. An EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institution or payment institution may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and provide SEPA, card acquiring and payment-initiation services to the SARL; Stripe, acting through its Irish-authorised payment institution, is fully viable for card and SEPA collection into an IBAN. However, the target rail 'Cryptocurrency_USDC' is outside the scope of an EMI licence: an EMI cannot accept, custody or convert USDC without itself holding a Luxembourg VASP registration (Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering, as amended, and CSSF guidance on virtual assets) or without using a separate regulated crypto-custodian. Offshore non-EEA EMIs (UK, Singapore, UAE) have no EU passport and would require either a Luxembourg branch authorisation or an EU partner-EMI arrangement; practical onboarding of such an entity as the sole bank is Low. The recommended structure is: (i) Stripe Ireland for fiat card/SEPA rails; (ii) a German or Luxembourg-licensed crypto-custodian (e.g., a Bafin-authorised crypto bank or a CSSF-registered VASP) for USDC receipt and immediate EUR conversion; and (iii) a tier-1 Luxembourg bank as the master settlement account, receiving only EUR.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate for Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, 6.75% municipal business tax); reduced 15% CIT applies to first EUR 175,000 of taxable income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic rate on dividend distributions; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 continuous months; DTA rates generally 5% to 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Master File and Local File; Country-by-Country Report is applicable when the consolidated group exceeds EUR 750,000,000; no specific transaction threshold for non-arm's-length adjustments.

Economic substance scope: No stand-alone ESR statute; substance analysis is applied through Luxembourg GAAR and the OECD Multilateral Convention. The SARL must demonstrate real decision-making, adequate employees, office, bank account, and risk-taking in metaverse event operations; failing this, income may be recharacterised, treaty relief denied, and the SARL treated as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under Art. 426 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law: fine of up to EUR 25,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years (or both); civil tax assessment up to 100% of evaded tax and monthly default interest of 0.6%.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax where they knew or should have known; convictions trigger travel bans, asset freezes, publication of judgement and civil restitution to the tax administration.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: Administrative recharacterisation of the SARL as a documentary shell; denial of DTA benefits, loss of Luxembourg treaty protection, retroactive assessment and 10% to 100% penalties plus reclassification of amounts as constructively distributed dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors appointed to enable the shell arrangement face civil liability for unwound distributions and potential criminal liability for false declarations; Luxembourg courts may issue travel bans and prohibit holding corporate offices for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with consequential tax and net wealth tax; 7.2% p.a. late-payment interest; a penalty up to 10% of adjusted tax; non-production of documents after demand may be treated as tax obstruction with a further fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation or knowingly approve non-arm's length arrangements become personally exposed to the administrative penalty and to damages; in egregious cases, indictment for tax fraud and travel restrictions are possible.
AML / KYC Failure and Virtual Asset Facilitation

Statutory sanction: Breach of Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF regulations: fines against the SARL up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; suspension or revocation of licences; monitoring and compliance orders.

Director personal liability: Directors, as reporting officers, are personally liable for AML violations; sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years, confiscation of proceeds, travel bans and bans from management, plus joint civil liability to the Supervisory Commission.

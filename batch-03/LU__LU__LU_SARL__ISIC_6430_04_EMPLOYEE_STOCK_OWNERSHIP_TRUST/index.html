Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation containing the Articles of Association (Statuts), authenticated by a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically with the RCS.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000; at least 10% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up at subscription; all cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released only upon RCS registration (Article 710-7 of the 1915 Law, as amended).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 17 June 1994 on fire resistance, escape routes, and emergency signaling; CGDIS inspection and acceptance of the office occupancy; structural load inspection and workplace clearance certified by the ITM (Inspection du Travail et des Mines), including ergonomic assessment and employer medical surveillance obligations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000; at least 10% of the nominal value of each share must be paid up at subscription; all cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released only upon RCS registration (Article 710-7 of the 1915 Law, as amended).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT purposes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax purposes. E-service portal: MyGuichet.lu (myguichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a newly created SARL, the notary files the incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). The RCS assigns a national identification number (matricule). Following RCS registration, the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (ACD) issues the corporate tax file number and the AEDT issues the VAT number (numéro de TVA). All applications are made electronically via MyGuichet.lu; foreign legal entities establishing a branch or permanent establishment must produce authenticated constitutional documents, evidence of registered address, and details of authorised representatives together with the registration form and tax questionnaire.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a newly created SARL, the notary files the incorporation deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). The RCS assigns a national identification number (matricule). Following RCS registration, the Luxembourg Inland Revenue (ACD) issues the corporate tax file number and the AEDT issues the VAT number (numéro de TVA). All applications are made electronically via MyGuichet.lu; foreign legal entities establishing a branch or permanent establishment must produce authenticated constitutional documents, evidence of registered address, and details of authorised representatives together with the registration form and tax questionnaire.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available. The SARL is a taxable corporate entity under Luxembourg corporate income tax law. Statutory salaried options include: (i) joint tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Articles 164bis to 164nonies LIR, allowing resident companies to integrate fully owned resident subsidiaries; (ii) transfer pricing and hidden equity/subordinated loans elections under the arm's length standard; (iii) optional application of Luxembourg registered qualifying intellectual property income regime (80% exemption) subject to OECD modified nexus; and (iv) notification of hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD, requiring adjustments to deducting exemptions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 (annual taxable turnover excluding VAT, small business exemption); however, foreign businesses established outside Luxembourg with taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register regardless of the threshold, and the exemption is not available for non-established taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union (third-country export) qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided that (i) the goods are physically exported by the supplier or by another person acting on behalf of the supplier, (ii) the supplier has a contract for the delivery of those goods, and (iii) the exporter obtains and keeps documentary proof of the export within the legal time limits. Intra-Community supplies (B2B triangular flow) to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State also qualify for 0% upon submission of the recipient's valid EU-VAT number and adequate transport documentation. Services that are directly linked to the export (transport, loading, packing, handling) can also be zero-rated if provided to the exporter or consignee.

  • Customs Export Declaration (E-customs summary declaration or standard export declaration as accepted by Luxembourg Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence confirming settlement of the export sale

Mandatory e-invoicing for all B2G invoices issued to the Luxembourg public administration using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format. B2B digital invoicing is not yet mandatory for domestic orders, and no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or government pre-authorisation system is in force as of the date of this dossier. Where a business chooses to issue electronic invoices for B2B, the EU Directive 2014/55/EU interoperability standard and authenticity/control of origin requirements apply.

Luxembourg VAT authorities apply substantial audit scrutiny to export zero-rating. Where proper export proof is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the taxpayer must correct the output VAT at the standard rate plus default interest (0.8% per month in arrears) and may be subject to administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 and a possible VAT penalty up to 10% of the tax evaded. In cases of fraud, interest may be applied, and VAT can be retrospectively clawed back up to five years (and indefinitely where intent to evade is established). Customs export declarations are cross-checked with the trader’s summary declarations; any false documentation may result in criminal sanctions and debarment from the simplified export procedure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member with full free convertibility across current and capital accounts under EU Treaty provisions and OECD liberalisation codes. Non-resident ownership of a Luxembourg SARL is permitted without central bank approval. Profit remittance faces no FX restriction; the relevant hurdles are withholding taxes, substance requirements, UBO registration, and anti-abuse checks. Ordinary dividends are subject to 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying participations held for at least 12 months, and further reduced under applicable double-taxation treaties. Beneficiaries must demonstrate beneficial ownership, economic substance, and CRS/FATCA filing status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists for export receipts. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no minimum conversion ratio into EUR. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency in Luxembourg accounts. The only obligations are BCL statistical Balance of Payments and International Investment Position reporting and CSSF AML record-keeping for cross-border transactions.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — central bank and monetary authority; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) — AML/CFT supervision and banking licensing; Ministry of Finance — fiscal policy coordination. Luxembourg operates no exchange-control licensing authority and imposes no general foreign-currency convertibility approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. A Luxembourg licensed bank or another EU credit institution operating through a Luxembourg branch is required for deposit-taking accounts. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully accept repayable deposits from a Luxembourg SARL unless passporting under PSD2 through an established Luxembourg agent or branch. EMIs may be used only as ancillary collection/disbursement rails for participant contributions or expense management, not as the primary treasury clearing infrastructure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge on the CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax, or 0.76% in other municipalities)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax under domestic law; exempt (0%) for qualifying distributions to resident EU/EEA parent companies holding at least 10% shares for 12 consecutive months (Art. 147 and Parent-Subsidiary Directive); otherwise a 15% cap under most Luxembourg DTTs, e.g., 5% for substantial holdings and 0% in certain treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year companies, the extended filing deadline is 31 December of the following year upon automatic or simple taxable notice request; tax prepayments due quarterly on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A Identification and taxpayer status: entity name, RCS number (L*), legal form (SARL), Luxembourg tax residence, principal activity (ISIC 6430 – Employee Stock Ownership Trust administration). Non-resident shareholders are not required to appear in Box A; the filing is made exclusively by the resident tax debtor. Non-resident trustees must designate the Luxembourg SARL as the taxpayer and attach a Form 400 Annex L for foreign ownership details. Late filing of Form 400 triggers an administrative fine of up to €50,000, but commonly €10,000 per month of delay; failure to pay assessed tax within the prescribed time triggers surcharge of 0.6% per month up to a maximum of 30% of the assessed tax. A formal notice can result in a penalty from €50 to €10,000 per infraction; aggravated non-compliance can result in criminal penalties (see below).
Box B Taxable income (Bénéfice brut de l'exercice) before loss set-off: reconcile accounting profit of the ESOP trust to taxable basis; all fees, dividends, interest, and disposal proceeds from employee share plans must be included. Where the ESOP trust generates income derived from a foreign parent or a non-resident settlor, the income must still be fully declared in Luxembourg. Non-resident beneficiaries receiving payments from the trust should claim treaty relief in their home jurisdiction based on the Luxembourg tax certificate. Late filing of Form 400 triggers an administrative fine of up to €50,000, but commonly €10,000 per month of delay; failure to pay assessed tax within the prescribed time triggers surcharge of 0.6% per month up to a maximum of 30% of the assessed tax. A formal notice can result in a penalty from €50 to €10,000 per infraction; aggravated non-compliance can result in criminal penalties (see below).
Box C Participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR): enter the gross amount of qualifying dividend income and capital gains from eligible subsidiaries that are to be exempted; attach a schedule showing the 10% holding or the €1.2 million acquisition cost and the 12-month holding period. This box must not be used by non-resident recipients; the Luxembourg SARL/ESOP trust must complete it with supporting evidence of the participation. Non-resident corporate shareholders applying for reduction/refund of withholding tax on distributed profits must use the separate Form 460 procedure. Late filing of Form 400 triggers an administrative fine of up to €50,000, but commonly €10,000 per month of delay; failure to pay assessed tax within the prescribed time triggers surcharge of 0.6% per month up to a maximum of 30% of the assessed tax. A formal notice can result in a penalty from €50 to €10,000 per infraction; aggravated non-compliance can result in criminal penalties (see below).
Box D Taxable loss carry-forward and utilisation: report net operating losses carried from prior years; utilisation is limited to the lesser of remaining losses and 75% of taxable income exceeding €500,000, unless qualifying for the EU Terminal Loss exception. If the ESOP trust is managed by a non-resident administrator, loss utilisation may be denied where the Luxembourg structural substance test is not satisfied. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Luxembourg entity) required where the aggregate value of intra-group cross-border transactions exceeds €1,000,000 during a fiscal year; limited Local File for small enterprises (fewer than 10 employees and turnover ≤ €700,000). Master File is required where the consolidated group turnover exceeds €50,000,000. Country-by-Country report is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ €750,000,000. Exemption for transactions that are fully exempt under the participation exemption or are not subject to VAT.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and the EU ATAD anti-abuse rules. The GAAR under Art. 6a LIR denies treaty benefits and any tax exemption where the main purpose of the ESOP trust is to obtain tax advantages with no real economic activity. For an ESOP trust, economic substance is demonstrated through: (i) a local administered office with day-to-day management in Luxembourg, (ii) a substantive number of qualified directors and employees (at least one full-time director and one full-time employee, typically a fund administrator), (iii) essential decisions taken in Luxembourg regarding the exercise of employee options, and (iv) bank accounts, books, and records located in Luxembourg. The Luxembourg office is subject to the ESR-like reporting under the EU anti-Money Laundering Directive (EU AML) and the Luxembourg Law of 27 June 2018 implementing the EU Requirements for Beneficial Ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to 50% of avoided tax; late payment interest at 0.6% per month (maximum 30%); criminal conviction leads to a fine of up to €250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; the tax authorities can order a civil recovery of the evaded tax and penalties, with the possibility of a travel ban and asset freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors or trustees who sign or approve a wilfully inaccurate return become jointly and severally liable for the entire unpaid tax and related surcharges; criminal proceedings can result in personal imprisonment up to five years, a surcharge of up to €1,000 per day during continued refusal, and a travel ban if the director is foreign and has been convicted.
Artificial Arrangement / Luxembourg GAAR (Art. 6a LIR) and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, retroactive recharacterisation of transactions as hidden profit distributions, imposition of 15% dividend WHT on the deemed distribution, plus 10% administrative fine on the recalculated tax and interest; where the arrangement is fraudulent, criminal tax penalties apply (up to €250,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors who actively participate in creating or maintaining a sham trust can be held personally liable for the tax liabilities of the entity to the extent they have personally guaranteed the debts or were aware of the fraud; the Civil Code piercing the veil may render directors responsible for civil restitution, and the regulator may issue a travel ban and commence criminal prosecution.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Compliance (AML/BO)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to €125,000 for non-filing of the RBE; for serious AML violations, penalties up to €5 million under the Luxembourg Monetary Penalty Act; the RBE registrar may issue a daily fine of €100 per day for continued non-compliance; trust's access to banking may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and board members who knowingly fail to comply with the AML/BO obligations may be personally fined up to €250,000 and disqualified from acting as directors for a period of up to 10 years; in severe cases, they face imprisonment up to 5 years and a travel ban if the court considers them a flight risk.
Transfer Pricing Failures and Documentation Defaults

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income by the tax authority, plus 10% penalty on the additional tax assessed; late payment interest at 0.6% per month; for missing documentation, a lump-sum fine of €25,000 per fiscal year or 1% of the transaction value, whichever is higher; repeated violations can trigger a criminal tax fraud charge.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally disregarded TP documentation obligations or manipulated transfer prices may be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall if they gave a personal guarantee or acted in bad faith; the penal code may impose a fine on directors of up to €250,000 and a prohibition on managing any company for up to 5 years, in addition to civil restitution.

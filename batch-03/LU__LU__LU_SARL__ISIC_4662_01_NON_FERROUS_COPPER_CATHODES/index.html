Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash. The capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution until the company is registered; the deposit certificate is filed with the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office, no specific fire safety certification is required. Should warehousing be established, the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) must issue a fire safety clearance after inspection. The Inspectorate of Labour and Mines (ITM) requires compliance with occupational health and safety standards, structural load limits, and the posting of consolidated internal regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash. The capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution until the company is registered; the deposit certificate is filed with the RCS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration, for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDTV) – Luxembourg Indirect Tax Administration, for VAT registration and collection. Primary e-portal: MyGuichet.lu; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL must first be registered in the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through Luxembourg Business Registers, obtaining a Luxembourg registration number. Thereafter, the entity must apply for a national business tax number (matricule d'impôt) from the ACD via the local Registration Tax Office. For VAT, the SARL must file Luxembourg VAT registration form (Formulaire 394) with the AEDTV, disclosing the expected turnover, ISIC activity codes, and bank details, to obtain a VAT identification number formatted with the LU prefix followed by numeric digits. No bank account or prior capital proof is required for registration, but non-resident directors must provide a local criminal record extract.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SARL must first be registered in the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through Luxembourg Business Registers, obtaining a Luxembourg registration number. Thereafter, the entity must apply for a national business tax number (matricule d'impôt) from the ACD via the local Registration Tax Office. For VAT, the SARL must file Luxembourg VAT registration form (Formulaire 394) with the AEDTV, disclosing the expected turnover, ISIC activity codes, and bank details, to obtain a VAT identification number formatted with the LU prefix followed by numeric digits. No bank account or prior capital proof is required for registration, but non-resident directors must provide a local criminal record extract.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. However, a resident parent SARL may elect, jointly with resident group companies, to form an integrated tax regime (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax purposes, requiring a 95% direct or indirect participation and a minimum five-year commitment. For VAT purposes, financially and economically closely related persons may elect to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Act. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU/ATAD do not allow elective opt-in; entities are required to deny deductions or include income automatically in the case of hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services under the small business exemption. Cross-border intra-EU acquisitions and imports are subject to mandatory VAT registration irrespective of this threshold. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, the threshold is EUR 10,000; for distance sales of electronic services, the union threshold also is EUR 10,000. A wholesale copper-trading SARL engaged in intra-EU acquisitions or exports is required to register for VAT regardless of turnover because of its cross-border trading activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The SARL may apply the 0% VAT rate for supplies of copper cathodes physically exported outside the EU or shipped to another EU Member State where the recipient is a taxable person holding a valid EU VAT number. For export outside the EU, the seller must provide documentary proof that the goods were transported outside the EU within the required time limits, ordinarily 90 to 90 days. For intra-EU supplies, the supplier must state the customer's VAT registration number on the invoice and demonstrate that the goods have been dispatched to the destination Member State. The export zero-rating also covers ancillary transport, packaging and freight services linked to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A form / AES electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the banking channel
  • Commercial invoice showing zero-rated sale and stating the VAT exemption law
  • Purchase contract and order documentation confirming the underlying copper trade
  • Packing list and cargo manifest
  • LME warrant transfer documentation or warehouse receipt evidencing copper cathode delivery

Luxembourg does not currently impose a general B2B real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping obligation. Electronic invoices are legally accepted provided authenticity and integrity are ensured, e.g., through EDI or advanced electronic signatures. Since 2025, public-sector invoices (B2G) must be submitted through the PEPPOL network using EN 16931 compliant e-invoices. No continuous transaction controls or real-time API clearance are in place.

The AEDTV and Customs authorities may conduct retrospective audits generally within a three-year limitation period, extendable to ten years in cases of suspected VAT fraud or tax evasion. If the SARL cannot substantiate export zero-rating with the prescribed export declaration, movement evidence, transport document, and repatriation proof, VAT at the standard rate (17%) is immediately assessed. Default interest of 0.6% per month is applied, plus a late-payment penalty up to 10%. Administrative fines in serious or fraudulent cases can reach EUR 250,000, and criminal prosecution is possible if falsified export documentation is established. Inward Remittance Certificates and bank ledger entries are systematically checked to ensure that export proceeds are repatriated to the SARL within the 180-day benchmark; failure to evidence repatriation triggers a rebuttable presumption of a taxable domestic sale and reassessment with interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full member of the Economic and Monetary Union (EMU) and the eurozone; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, subject only to EU/EEA anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions screening, and CSSF/BCL supervisory reporting obligations. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: a Luxembourg SARL may distribute dividends if the annual accounts do not show net assets below the subscribed capital plus non-distributable reserves (Luxembourg law on commercial companies Art. 710-1), and dividends, liquidation proceeds, or branch profits may be freely transferred cross-border subject to withholding tax (15% domestic; reduced under tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, with potential 0% where conditions are met) and standard bank attestation of compliance. There are no exchange quotas, prior administrative approvals, or surrender requirements for current transactions, but transactions routed via any EUR correspondent or settlement account must include compliant SWIFT purpose codes and underlying trade documentation to satisfy BCL statistical reporting (Balance of Payments reporting) and CSSF AML/KYC checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. All export proceeds in EUR or other convertible currencies may be retained in corporate bank accounts and used for treasury purposes; however, transactions exceeding EUR 10,000 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Cellule de Renseignement Financier (CRF) in line with AML obligations, and Cross-Border Payments Regulation or CSSF/BCL balance-of-payments reporting may require statistical declaration of a resident SARL's receipts/payments to non-residents. For physical copper cathode wholesale trading, the operative compliance focus is the availability of original commercial documentation (invoices, bills of lading, warehouse warrants, LC/MT103 records) and sanctions/embargoes screening rather than any FX conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for financial conduct and prudential oversight; exchange control within the EU/EMU framework is governed by the BCL and the ECB for policies affecting cross-border capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility with an EU/EEA e-money institution (EMI) or payment institution licensed under PSD2 and authorised in another EEA state, passporting into Luxembourg under the EU single passport. Practical legal constraints: (i) the EMI must be registered with the CSSF for Luxembourg activity and must comply with the revised Payment Services Act 2009 (as amended) and the e-money regime; (ii) most commercial banks require a traditional banking relationship for high-value physical commodity settlements; EMIs are generally unsuitable as the sole clearing rail for documentary LC operations involving copper cathodes, owing to restricted trade-finance capabilities and lower limits on transaction sizes; (iii) an EMI can only serve as an auxiliary e-wallet or FX conversion gateway for small-value payments, with segregation of client funds and safeguarding obligations under CSSF/EMD rules; (iv) user-obligation screening under sanctions and AML (5th and 6th EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives) applies, and the commodity trading counterparty profile must be demonstrably transparent. Overall feasibility rating: Low-Medium for core liquidity clearing, Medium for ancillary FX and vendor payments; any EMI arrangement should be ring-fenced and accompanied by a tier-1 correspondent-backed account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Luxembourg SARL at formation or via subsequent increase must be handled by SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers from the shareholder's bank account to the SARL's dedicated bank account in Luxembourg, with the exact purpose code 'T101' or 'EQUITY' and reference to the notarial deed or board resolution approving the capital increase; the bank will verify source of wealth. For loans/shareholder advances, use purpose code 'T101' or 'T112' (loans between non-financial corporations) and execute a formal intercompany loan agreement with commercial-rate interest. ECB Regulation 1745/2011 as integrated into EU statistical rules requires the bank to encode the transaction classification; the SARL should ensure the account field states 'CAPITAL INCREASE LU SARL' and provide the CSSF/BCL statistical reporting code A, category 'direct investment — other capital', if the shareholder is non-resident. No prior exchange control authorisation is required in Luxembourg, but the transfer must not be routed through or via sanctioned financial institutions or the Republic of Belarus/Russia/Syria/Iran/Crimea, and the bank will perform cascade checks and OFAC/EU sanction screening on both remitter and beneficiary before crediting. Any contribution in kind (e.g., copper cathode inventory or warrants) must be evidenced by a contribution-in-kind valuation approved by a Luxembourg chartered auditor or statutory auditor, with the deed filed with the Luxembourg trade register and public registry (RCS) and the contribution registered on the Luxembourg operational account or LME warrant custody account.Principal clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for open-account payments to vendors; (ii) MT202 cover instructions for nostro/loro correspondent settlements when the vendor uses a different bank; (iii) documentary letters of credit (LC) issued by the Luxembourg bank or by a confirming bank under UCP 600, with SWIFT MT700/MT707 issuance/amendment; (iv) LME warrant transfers via approved LME warrant service providers and custodian banks for delivery of Grade A copper cathodes against LC or warrant transfer plus SWIFT payment; and (v) for intra-group treasury centralisation, MT101/MT910/MT940 cash-management messages aligned with the CSSF transfer-pricing practices. All outgoing transfers require ISO 20022 or current SWIFT transaction reference with the correct EU Import/Export purpose code (e.g., T103 for purchase of goods, T210 for trading of commodities, or T111 for foreign exchange transactions as applicable), underlying invoice/contract, transport document, and, for value-dated LC settlement, the presentation of a compliant bill of lading and certificate of origin (where applicable). Liquidity clearing is optimised by concentrating EUR balances in a Luxembourg cash concentration account and executing cross-border vendor payments via SEPA or TARGET2 for euro transactions and via correspondent banks via SWIFT for non-euro transactions (USD, CNY, CHF). Trade disbursements connected to LME warrant deliveries should anchor in the physical warehouse location and settle through the LME clearing chain (LME Clear Ltd), with payment against delivery warrant transfer via a documentary LC or cash-against-documents arrangement; the Luxembourg bank must be provided with the warehouse receipt and warrant value to evidence economic substance under AML and BEPS compliance.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/CNH forwards (plain vanilla forward foreign exchange contracts) for up to a 12-month tenorRolling EUR/USD foreign exchange swaps (swap of principal and interest/cash flows for intercompany and trade settlements)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing restricted or low-convertibility currencies that counterparties may require where ordinary deliverable forwards are unavailable, subject to Luxumbourg bank credit lines and counterparty ISDA/CSA documentationCross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS/CCS) between EUR and USD for hedging a financed copper-cathode inventory positionFX options (plain vanilla European call/put options) on EUR/USD or EUR/CNH, purchased to hedge trade payables/receivables without speculative intent

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate feasibility with an EU/EEA e-money institution (EMI) or payment institution licensed under PSD2 and authorised in another EEA state, passporting into Luxembourg under the EU single passport. Practical legal constraints: (i) the EMI must be registered with the CSSF for Luxembourg activity and must comply with the revised Payment Services Act 2009 (as amended) and the e-money regime; (ii) most commercial banks require a traditional banking relationship for high-value physical commodity settlements; EMIs are generally unsuitable as the sole clearing rail for documentary LC operations involving copper cathodes, owing to restricted trade-finance capabilities and lower limits on transaction sizes; (iii) an EMI can only serve as an auxiliary e-wallet or FX conversion gateway for small-value payments, with segregation of client funds and safeguarding obligations under CSSF/EMD rules; (iv) user-obligation screening under sanctions and AML (5th and 6th EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives) applies, and the commodity trading counterparty profile must be demonstrably transparent. Overall feasibility rating: Low-Medium for core liquidity clearing, Medium for ancillary FX and vendor payments; any EMI arrangement should be ring-fenced and accompanied by a tier-1 correspondent-backed account.

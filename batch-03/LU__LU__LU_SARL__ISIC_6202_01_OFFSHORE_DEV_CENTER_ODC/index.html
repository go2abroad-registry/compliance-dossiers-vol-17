Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 19 August 2016 and the Law of 10 August 2016; SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 to 710-24; Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to the craft, commercial and industrial professions; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation containing the Articles of Association (Statuts), registered with the RCS and published in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations) within one month of registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 51 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must maintain a subscribed share capital of at least EUR 12,000, divided into registered shares with a minimum par value of EUR 1. At incorporation, at least 20% of each cash share (minimum EUR 2,400 in aggregate) must be paid up into a blocked bank account (compte de blocage) at a Luxembourg credit institution; such funds must remain blocked until RCS registration. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and supported by a report of an independent réviseur d'entreprises agréé. No statutory escrow deposit beyond the bank blocking requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy and fire safety clearance from the municipal fire and rescue service (Service d'incendie et de secours - SIS) obtained through the building permit procedure; electrical installation conformity certificate; structural load verification per the amended Luxembourg Building Code; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering office ergonomics, emergency exits, fire drills, and workplace welfare standards of the amended Labour Code.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must maintain a subscribed share capital of at least EUR 12,000, divided into registered shares with a minimum par value of EUR 1. At incorporation, at least 20% of each cash share (minimum EUR 2,400 in aggregate) must be paid up into a blocked bank account (compte de blocage) at a Luxembourg credit institution; such funds must remain blocked until RCS registration. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and supported by a report of an independent réviseur d'entreprises agréé. No statutory escrow deposit beyond the bank blocking requirement applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Inland Revenue (impotsdirects.public.lu) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) (aedt.public.lu) for VAT and registration duties; Luxembourg Business Registers / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu (myguichet.lu) as the single e-government filing portal

E-government filing portal

(1) File notarized articles of incorporation with the RCS through the notary/MyGuichet.lu and obtain the RCS extract registering the corporate identity; (2) submit the AEDT electronic VAT registration application, yielding a VAT number in format LU + 8 digits, attaching the RCS extract, articles of association, EU/EEA seat certificate of the foreign parent, and identity documents for managers and ultimate beneficial owners; (3) direct tax registration with ACD is generated ex officio in the form of the corporate matricule/TIN; (4) file the ultimate beneficial owner (RBE) return within 30 days; (5) a business license (autorisation d'établissement) is not required for ISIC 6202 offshore software development activities — the ODC is an unregulated commercial activity

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; an LU SARL is an opaque, tax-resident legal entity (registered seat or effective management in Luxembourg) subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax on worldwide income; no passthrough eligibility; foreign PE income qualifies for treaty-based exemption with progression and the participation exemption shields qualifying dividends and capital gains; no sub-national tax distinction in Luxembourg (unitary national system)

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box regime: classification is statutory. Available elections: (1) VAT single-unit (assujetti unique) grouping for VAT; (2) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale under Art. 164bis LIR) for 95%+ owned Luxembourg subsidiaries; (3) treaty-based branch/PE exemption election (exemption-with-progression); (4) Nexus-compliant IP Box election (Art. 50ter LIR) granting an 80% exemption of net qualifying IP income, including copyrightable computer software and databases relevant to the ODC; (5) ATAD hybrid-mismatch notification/neutrality elections; the SCSp partnership transparency election is available only for alternative vehicle structures

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (Luxembourg-established small business annual exemption threshold, excluding VAT); non-resident suppliers have no threshold — any taxable supply triggers Luxembourg VAT registration unless the B2B reverse charge, EU OSS/IOSS regime, or the EUR 10,000 EU-wide cross-border distance-sale threshold applies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% export VAT under Art. 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Code where title passes to a non-EU purchaser and the goods are physically dispatched from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU, with transport evidence and an export declaration filed with customs. Services (ISIC 6202 ODC scope): software engineering, maintenance, hosting and digital services rendered by the SARL to customers established outside the EU fall outside Luxembourg VAT (place-of-supply B2B destination rule under Arts. 17(2) and 24 of the VAT Code), producing an effective 0% outcome; services to EU B2B customers are reverse-charged in the customer's member state. Zero-rating eligibility requires the recipient to be non-established in Luxembourg and, for digital services, effective use and enjoyment to occur outside Luxembourg

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document or electronic export declaration lodged via Luxembourg Customs e-Douane/ECS module)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 or correspondent credit confirmation evidencing export proceeds received by the LU SARL within the agreed payment term)
  • Signed contract/statement of work and itemized invoices for the offshore software development and maintenance services
  • Proof of the client's non-resident status (EU VIES validation certificate, foreign VAT registration certificate, or tax residence certificate)
  • Delivery evidence (acceptance reports, sign-off tickets, IT access logs, and remote-delivery records)

Luxembourg has no mandatory B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance as of 2025; no tax-authority clearance or digital stamping (fiscal cachet) exists; PEPPOL-BIS electronic invoicing is mandatory only for suppliers to central public authorities; the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, effective March 2025, will mandate structured e-invoicing and transaction reporting for LU VAT-registered entities in phases from 2030, but there is currently no continuous transaction control (CTC) system

The AEDT and ACD require complete export and cross-border service files during audit; insufficient substantiation triggers reassessment at 17% VAT plus 0.8% monthly late-payment interest; intentionally fraudulent zero-rating claims attract administrative penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax, criminal fines of up to EUR 50,000, and imprisonment of up to two years; the normal five-year limitation period is extended to ten years for fraud; retrospective disallowance of input VAT credit and joint and several liability of directors and the controlling foreign parent apply

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible, and Luxembourg maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account regime pursuant to Articles 63-66 TFEU and Council Directive 88/361/EEC. No prior exchange-control authorization is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, intercompany service payments, or profit repatriation by a Luxembourg SARL. External account and current account transactions are unrestricted, and no differentiated surrender or buyback regime applies to residents. The only material frictions to profit remittance are fiscal rather than FX-related: Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at 15%, reducible to 5% or 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable double tax treaties, together with ordinary corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax borne before distribution. Cross-border payments trigger BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting, but that obligation lies on the reporting credit institution, not on the company, and no permission or restriction is attached to such reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the euro area impose no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline for the repatriation of export proceeds. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely, converted at commercial rates, or netted internally subject to transfer-pricing arm's-length principles. For a low-risk cost-plus ODC whose functional currency is EUR, best treasury practice is to match invoicing and disbursement currencies, convert foreign receipts within the contractual payment cycle, and periodically revalue foreign-exchange balances under IFRS or LuxGAAP to recognize foreign-exchange gains or losses; non-compliance with such practice is a fiscal and audit issue, not a BCL violation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem under the authority of the European Central Bank (ECB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) serves as the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor of credit institutions in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for low-value operational disbursements; Low for statutory escrow and BCL-statistical-reporting duties. Under PSD2, an electronic money institution (EMI) licensed in an EEA member state may passport payment accounts, IBANs, and money-remittance services into Luxembourg, so offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) can technically provide EUR IBANs and SWIFT rails. The decisive legal constraints are: (1) a bank account at a BCL-reporting credit institution is required to evidence the SARL capital deposit and to facilitate the notarial deed; (2) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot issue escrow confirmations acceptable to a Luxembourg notary or the RCSL; (3) cross-border receipts routed exclusively through EMIs may remain outside the bank's balance-of-payments reporting loop and create a BCL statistical compliance gap; and (4) business-operations clauses of several tier-1 banks prohibit primary payroll and social-security flows through EMIs. The blueprint therefore authorizes EMI rails only as a secondary egress for sub-EUR 50,000 contractor payments, with the main treasury ledger and all statutory capital, payroll, and tax flows maintained at a BCL-reporting credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The capital injection from the parent or investor must be executed by SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfer (or MT202 COV for an interbank cover arrangement) to the SARL's escrow account at a BCL-reporting bank, settled in EUR via TARGET2. Field 50 must bear the funding shareholder's account; Field 70 (Remittance Information) must read 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / LU_SARL <RCSL registration number>' to enable purpose-of-code classification; the bank also requires the underlying notarial draft deed and a capital deposit instruction letter signed by the shareholder. Verification steps: (i) sanctions and name screening of the remitter; (ii) KYC check on the source of funds; (iii) BCL balance-of-payments coding as direct investment in equity; and (iv) notarization and filing of the deed with the RCSL before the escrow restriction is lifted. No BCL exchange-control approval is required. For non-EUR injections, the bank will convert at its corporate rate, and the inward transaction must be pre-declared for transfer-pricing consistency with the parent.SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) is the primary rail for EUR disbursements within the EEA, covering salaries, social security, suppliers, and intra-group fees, with SEPA Instant for sub-EUR 100,000 urgent payments and TARGET2 for high-value settlements. Non-EEA and non-EUR payments use SWIFT MT103 for credits to beneficiary accounts and MT202 COV for cover payments; each payment carries a purpose code (e.g., SUPP for supplier payment, SALA for salary, INTC for intercompany charge) in Field 70 while the bank runs EU, OFAC, and UN sanctions screens and BCL statistical classification. Commercial letters of credit (documentary credits under ICC UCP 600) are available through the trade-finance desks of BIL, BNP Paribas Luxembourg, and Société Générale Luxembourg for procurement of hardware or licensed infrastructure where the supplier requires an LC; standby LCs are not required for the ODC's ordinary software-engineering fee flows. The bank account may not be used as a pass-through of third-party funds, and no cross-border payment exceeding EUR 200,000 should be issued without a board-approved cash-flow forecast and, where relevant, the CRS/FATCA remittance report.Deliverable plain-vanilla FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement and the 1998 FX and Currency Options Definitions, as amended.FX swaps (swap-in/swap-out) for short-term liquidity conversion between EUR and the ODC's receivable currencies, with tenors not exceeding 12 months.Cross-currency swaps converting long-term intercompany debt (e.g., USD shareholder loans) into EUR exposure, matched by the SARL's assets and approved by the board.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs), permitted exclusively for hedging genuine commercial exposure in restricted non-convertible currencies (e.g., INR, KRW, RUB), with an EEA-licensed investment firm counterparty when a deliverable forward is unavailable.Cancellable FX option or zero-cost collar structures, allowed only with an investment-grade counterparty, a signed board hedging policy, and full integration into the ODC's transfer-pricing documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for low-value operational disbursements; Low for statutory escrow and BCL-statistical-reporting duties. Under PSD2, an electronic money institution (EMI) licensed in an EEA member state may passport payment accounts, IBANs, and money-remittance services into Luxembourg, so offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Revolut Business) can technically provide EUR IBANs and SWIFT rails. The decisive legal constraints are: (1) a bank account at a BCL-reporting credit institution is required to evidence the SARL capital deposit and to facilitate the notarial deed; (2) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot issue escrow confirmations acceptable to a Luxembourg notary or the RCSL; (3) cross-border receipts routed exclusively through EMIs may remain outside the bank's balance-of-payments reporting loop and create a BCL statistical compliance gap; and (4) business-operations clauses of several tier-1 banks prohibit primary payroll and social-security flows through EMIs. The blueprint therefore authorizes EMI rails only as a secondary egress for sub-EUR 50,000 contractor payments, with the main treasury ledger and all statutory capital, payroll, and tax flows maintained at a BCL-reporting credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (group consolidated turnover) and cross-border controlled transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 1,000,000 trigger the obligation to maintain a Local File and, where subject to Group reporting, a Master File; CbCR applies at EUR 750,000,000. Contemporaneous OECD-compliant TP documentation is recommended for any related-party transaction of the ODC.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an Economic Substance Regulation jurisdiction, but the tax administration enforces economic substance principles under the GAAR (Article 6 AStL? CTL), the Luxembourg participation exemption, and ATAD. The ODC must have real staff, physical office premises, and commercial control in Luxembourg. Absent such substance, treaty benefits and domestic exemptions can be denied and the income may be reallocated to the beneficial owner/source State.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of unpaid CIT plus 10% administrative surcharge and monthly late payment interest; criminal fine up to EUR 125,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 59 LIR).

Director personal liability: Director may be held jointly and severally for evaded corporate tax if personally involved; civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies; criminal imprisonment for fraudulent intent.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment under the OECD Guidelines; additional tax assessment up to 10% penalty on the understated profit; secondary dividend reclassification; interest for late payment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for reckless advice or false declarations; assets can be attached in execution of tax liabilities; a travel ban may be issued when substantial unpaid taxes are being dismantled.
Substance Deficiency / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and the participation exemption; reattribution of profits to the owner/beneficiary; court‑ordered striking‑off from the Luxembourg register; penalty up to 10% of the additional assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell SARL can be sued in civil restitution for damage caused to the State and third parties; prohibition from acting as director; travel ban imposed if non‑compliant with tax summons.
AML/Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004; enforcement by the Public Prosecutor; underlying tax fraud penalties may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors exposed to personal fines up to EUR 1,000,000; prohibition from exercising managerial positions; imprisonment for repeat debt non‑payment and money‑laundering offenders.

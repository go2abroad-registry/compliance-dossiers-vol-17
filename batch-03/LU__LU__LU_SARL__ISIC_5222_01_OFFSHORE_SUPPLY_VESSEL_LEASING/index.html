Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes; Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT; integrated e-government portal: myguichet.lu; Luxembourg Business Registers portal for RCS registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL is formed by notarial deed and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS); the RCS matricule number serves as the statutory business identification number. For direct taxation, the ACD opens a corporate tax file and issues a direct tax TIN upon registration/incorporation notification; for non-resident entities with a Luxembourg permanent establishment, a direct tax registration application and appointment of a local tax representative must be filed. For VAT, the taxable person must submit a VAT registration application (electronically via myguichet.lu or on prescribed form 640) to the AEDT, attaching constitutional documents, proof of economic substance, and, where required, appointment of a VAT representative; AEDT issues the VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. A foreign entity leasing offshore vessels may need a Luxembourg commercial licence (business permit) issued by the Ministry of the Economy if it maintains a fixed establishment or is deemed commercially active in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL is formed by notarial deed and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS); the RCS matricule number serves as the statutory business identification number. For direct taxation, the ACD opens a corporate tax file and issues a direct tax TIN upon registration/incorporation notification; for non-resident entities with a Luxembourg permanent establishment, a direct tax registration application and appointment of a local tax representative must be filed. For VAT, the taxable person must submit a VAT registration application (electronically via myguichet.lu or on prescribed form 640) to the AEDT, attaching constitutional documents, proof of economic substance, and, where required, appointment of a VAT representative; AEDT issues the VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. A foreign entity leasing offshore vessels may need a Luxembourg commercial licence (business permit) issued by the Ministry of the Economy if it maintains a fixed establishment or is deemed commercially active in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election; classification follows the legal form, and a SARL is a per se resident corporation subject to entity-level taxation. Available electivities of relevance include (i) VAT fiscal unity (single taxable person) for Luxembourg group members, (ii) small-business VAT exemption election where annual turnover is below EUR 30,000, (iii) tax consolidation under Article 164bis of the Income Tax Law (integration fiscale), and (iv) no elective hybrid-mismatch carve-outs; ATAD hybrid rules apply mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover (Luxembourg small-enterprise exemption); non-established taxable persons are not eligible for this exemption and must register from the first euro where taxable supplies are made in Luxembourg; a resident SARL carrying on offshore vessel chartering will generally exceed the threshold or be subject to B2B displacement/reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Luxembourg applies exemption with credit (0%) to the supply, chartering and operation of sea-going vessels used in commercial maritime navigation, including offshore support vessels serving non-EU installations, and to ancillary services for the direct needs of such vessels (VAT Directive art. 148). Zero-rating also applies to goods physically exported outside the EU evidenced by a customs export declaration. For B2B charter services to foreign taxable customers, the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction, so the supply is outside Luxembourg VAT if the customer holds a valid VAT registration in another EU member state or represents a non-EU taxable person; the invoice must state the applicable place-of-supply rule and the customer's VAT number, and the operator must retain charter party, vessel registry and seaworthiness documentation, and operational voyage records demonstrating non-EU or commercial oceanic use. If the recipient is a non-taxable person, use-and-enjoyment or transport-related special rules may re-ground the supply in Luxembourg; accordingly, the exporter must confirm each transaction's place of supply before applying zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document / EX-A form in Luxembourg customs system) evidencing exit of the vessel or goods from EU customs territory
  • Bill of lading or non-negotiable sea waybill evidencing the physical repositioning or offshore operational use of the vessel outside Luxembourg/EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 or Luxembourg bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of the charter hire from the non-resident counterparty within the 180-day repatriation window

No general B2B e-invoicing mandate and no real-time tax clearance, API stamping or digital SAF-T submission regime is in force as of 1 January 2025. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G supplies via the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 channel. VAT returns, recapitulative statements and annual listings are filed electronically through myguichet.lu; accounting and export substantiation files must be retained in a form enabling SAF-T XML audit extraction by AEDT.

AEDT verifies export and offshore charter claims strictly; absence of the customs and remittance bundle results in retrospective zero-rating reversal, assessment of 17% Luxembourg VAT, default interest, and denial of input VAT recovery. Administrative penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating range from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000, up to 20% of the evaded tax may be imposed, and criminal tax fraud proceedings may impose imprisonment and asset confiscation; the ordinary five-year VAT audit period is extended to ten years in fraud cases, and directors or tax representatives who knowingly signed false declarations may be held jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full liberalization of both current and capital account transactions in accordance with the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is freely convertible; no general foreign-exchange approval is required for inbound equity injections, loan disbursements, charter-hire payments, profit repatriation, or trade settlements. Profit remittance restrictions are not imposed by exchange-control law; the practical hurdles are corporate-law solvency tests, the arm's length principle, substance requirements, and the 15% Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends (reduced to 0% under the parent-subsidiary directive where conditions are met; reduced under applicable tax treaties). Payments to non-cooperative or high-risk jurisdictions may be refused by banks under AML/CFT obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation timelines, or central bank settlement requirements in Luxembourg. Export/charter receipts may be retained in any currency. The BCL nevertheless requires resident credit institutions and, where applicable, direct resident reporters to submit balance-of-payments statistical declarations for cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12,500 per operation, and transaction-level reporting applies under ECB Regulation (EU) No 1844/2006 and BCL Circular 201210/L. The SARL should instruct banks to use correct purpose codes and maintain supporting invoices and charterparty contracts to satisfy BCL statistical reporting without triggering AML reviews.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for bank licensing and AML/CFT supervision, and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance for monetary and exchange rate policy. Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State, so exchange rate policy is vested in the Eurosystem/ECB for EUR-denominated operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorised by the CSSF under PSD2 and supervised by the CSSF are permissible payment-service providers for operational treasury, charter-hire collection, and supplier disbursements; they may generate fast onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and API-driven clearing. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot hold deposits, extend credit, offer investment products, or provide FX derivative hedging; settlement balances must be safeguarded under national transposition of PSD2/EMD2. Offshore digital EMIs merely registered as 'electronic money agents' or 'payment facilitators' are not authorised to serve a Luxembourg SARL with actual settlement risk; the treasury lead should use only CSSF-authorised EMI/PSP or EEA passported EMIs and obtain a valid agent agreement, safeguarding account, and SCA-compliant interfaces.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Offshore holding ownership structure with multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: Offshore holding ownership structure with multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Notarised articles of association, RCS excerpt, up-to-date UBO and share register, group chart, organigramme, corporate accountability declarations, LEI codes, audited accounts or opening balance sheet, and explanation of the commercial purpose of intermediate holding vehicles.
Vessel chartering to entities in sanctioned or high-risk waters or ambiguous charterparty counterparties

Root cause: Vessel chartering to entities in sanctioned or high-risk waters or ambiguous charterparty counterparties

Defense evidence: Full charterparty contract, vessel AIS records and voyage history, sanctions screening reports (EU, UN, OFAC, UK), safety management certificates (ISM), International Ship Security Certificate (ISPS), P&I insurance cover, commercial invoices, and a written trade-control policy confirming no sanctioned Russian, Belarusian, Iranian, North Korean, Syrian, Cuban, or Crimea transactions.
High-value or high-frequency cross-border payments inconsistent with vessel leasing activity

Root cause: High-value or high-frequency cross-border payments inconsistent with vessel leasing activity

Defense evidence: Detailed invoice ledger, port call and delivery order documentation, MT103 transaction history, charter-hire schedules, vessel registration abstracts, flag state certificates, periodic BCL balance-of-payments reports, and narrative description of each payment class (bunkers, crew, port dues, repairs, insurance premiums).
Initial capital injection by non-resident shareholders or intercompany loans

Root cause: Initial capital injection by non-resident shareholders or intercompany loans

Defense evidence: Notarised share subscription deed, bank credit advice of subscription payment, shareholder loan agreement with commercial interest rate and maturity, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, sale contracts, dividend records), transfer pricing analysis, Luxembourg substance documentation (registered office, board minutes, management appointments), and clean tax residence/beneficial ownership declarations.
Expected receipts from third parties whose beneficial owners are undisclosed or opaque

Root cause: Expected receipts from third parties whose beneficial owners are undisclosed or opaque

Defense evidence: Enhanced due diligence file on charterers, ownership registry extract from the flag authority, commercial registry, source of charterer funds, invoice and payment schedules, sworn declarations, and legal opinion on ultimate beneficial owner identification under AML requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial share capital contribution in a Luxembourg SARL, use a wire transfer denominated in EUR to the bank account designated in the notarial deed, referencing the company name and 'Capital Subscription'. The bank will credit a temporary capital escrow/subscription account until registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). From EU payment accounts, a SEPA Credit Transfer using IBAN and BIC is sufficient; from outside the EEA, use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 containing purpose code CAPC (Capital Payment) or an equivalent ISO 20022 code, and field 72 include the RCS reservation and notary reference. The originator must be the shareholder's own account; third-party payments are generally rejected. Cash, bearer instruments, and cryptocurrency are not acceptable for capital formation. For shareholder loans, use purpose code CTAX/INVS and an executed loan agreement; the bank may require evidence of beneficial ownership, source of funds, and payment of any applicable financial transaction taxes, although Luxembourg imposes no stamp duty on ordinary loan advances. Inbound funds from non-EU jurisdictions are subject to Regulation (EU) 2015/847 information requirements; the payment must show full originator and beneficiary identification, and the bank must report cross-border flows to the BCL for statistical purposes.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers covering charter-hire remittances, insurance premiums, bunker settlements, port dues, crew payroll, and owner's disbursements. EUR payments inside the EEA clear via TARGET2 using MT103-equivalent ISO 20022 pain.001/pacs.008 with the beneficiary IBAN and purpose code TRAD or CHGS; USD payments clear through correspondent banks via SWIFT MT103 and may require MT202 COV for interbank settlement. For vessel acquisition or long-term lease capital expenditure, Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700/MT720) with payment at sight or deferred payment, backed by a conformity certificate, bill of lading, and marine insurance, is the preferred documentary clearing rail. The Luxembourg SARL should maintain segregated accounts for operational cash and escrowed/marine proceeds to preserve the integrity of the vessel leasing business and avoid mixing personal or non-trade flows. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 of aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers the Local File; preparation of a Master File is expected for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 50,000,000; CbC reporting applies for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but central management and control, ATAD GAAR, and OECD BEPS Action 5 require the offshore-supply-vessel leasing entity to demonstrate commercial substance in Luxembourg through board decisions, operating control, qualified personnel, and real charter-management functions; inadequate substance will trigger income reallocation, denial of treaty benefits, and forfeiture of the participation exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsified Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative penalty of 50% to 200% of the evaded tax plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, subject to travel bans and up to 5 years imprisonment
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate entity for tax purposes, reallocation of charter income to foreign shareholders, denial of treaty benefits, and a GAAR penalty of 10% of reclassified income (minimum EUR 5,000)

Director personal liability: Directors who permit the sham suffer piercing of the corporate veil, civil restitution for unpaid corporate taxes, judicial management prohibition, and travel bans
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ACD upward income adjustment to arm's-length levels, denied deductions, 0.6% monthly late-payment interest, and administrative fines up to 10% of the adjustment

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length intercompany terms without documentation become personally liable for the underpaid tax; criminal liability for tax evasion if the pricing is wilfully fraudulent
AML / UBO Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 5 million for the legal entity under Luxembourg AML law (Law of 25 March 2020), prohibition of activity, and civil fines for each aggravating deficiency

Director personal liability: Board members and the AML responsible officer are personally liable for fines up to EUR 250,000, may face travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years for money-laundering or financing-terrorism offenses

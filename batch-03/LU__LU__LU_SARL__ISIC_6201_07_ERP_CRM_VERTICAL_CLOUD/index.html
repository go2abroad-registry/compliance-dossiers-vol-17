Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum subscribed and issued share capital is EUR 12,000. At least 25% of the cash capital, i.e. EUR 3,000, must be paid up before RCS registration. Subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked company-in-formation account held by a Luxembourg credit institution, or an equivalent escrow arrangement, and released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy permit, fire safety certification, structural load inspection, ITM workplace safety clearance, electricity installation conformity, and any municipal civil-defense signposting must be obtained before continuous office operations commence.

Paid-in capital rule: The minimum subscribed and issued share capital is EUR 12,000. At least 25% of the cash capital, i.e. EUR 3,000, must be paid up before RCS registration. Subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked company-in-formation account held by a Luxembourg credit institution, or an equivalent escrow arrangement, and released only after RCS registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax; official portal is MyGuichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS).

E-government filing portal

TVA') and corporate tax file number. As a resident SARL, no separate foreign TIN is required. Timeframe: 3-5 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime in Luxembourg. A SARL is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICB) and net wealth tax (ISF). No fiscal-transparency election is available for a SARL; however, hybrid-mismatch alignment elections under ATAD 2 may apply to cross-border instruments, and certain partnerships may be treated as transparent for Luxembourg tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT) for the domestic small-business exemption. Mandatory VAT registration is nevertheless required for intra-Community acquisitions, distance sales exceeding EUR 10,000 (OSS), certain cross-border B2C digital services, or when exercising the option to apply normal VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the export of goods physically dispatched/transported to a non-EU destination. For exported services, including cloud CRM/ERP software B2B services, Luxembourg VAT is displaced by the place-of-supply rule: (i) EU B2B customer - reverse charge; (ii) non-EU B2B customer - no Luxembourg VAT if the customer's establishment is outside Luxembourg; (iii) for B2C digital services to non-EU customers, destination rules similarly remove Luxembourg VAT. Eligibility requires documentary proof of recipient identity, establishment and use; absence of abuse; and, for goods, a valid customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Déclaration en Douane) - required for physical goods; for pure digital services no customs declaration is applicable.
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / airway bill - transport evidence demonstrating export; for digital services, service contract and delivery evidence are the functional substitute.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation showing export proceeds collection, together with invoices and customer VAT number/economic operator status documentation.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API filing or digital stamping applies to general B2B invoicing in Luxembourg. Structured e-invoicing (Factur-X/UBL via PEPPOL) is mandatory only for B2G contracts. Paper and PDF invoices remain valid for VAT; VAT returns are filed electronically through MyGuichet. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package is expected to introduce future mandatory e-invoicing for intra-EU transactions.

The AEDT may reassess VAT for up to 3 years (5 years on omission, 10 years for fraudulent evasion). Unsubstantiated zero-rating or abusive arrangements trigger clawback of unpaid VAT, default interest (0.8% per month) and fines up to the amount of the evaded tax; criminal penalties for VAT fraud include imprisonment and publication of sanction. Mitigation requires preserving complete customer due diligence and export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible without exchange-control restrictions in Luxembourg. Under the EU Treaty and Luxembourg monetary law, both current account and capital account transactions are liberalised. Profit remittances from a Luxembourg SARL may be distributed freely via SEPA or SWIFT rails; the only substantive hurdles are standard anti-money-laundering (AML) identification of the ultimate beneficial owner and possible Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends, which does not require any central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency at the exporter's discretion. The only applicable regulatory duty is the statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligation to the BCL, which must be discharged through the reporting bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem; foreign exchange control and sanctions enforcement are coordinated with the Ministère des Finances and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for a Luxembourg-resident low-risk software SARL is high for EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs), which may provide online account opening, SEPA direct debit collection, and cross-border payment services under an EU passport. A wholly offshore digital EMI without a CSSF licence or an EEA passport is legally unacceptable as a primary treasury account. A validly authorised fintech EMI may be used as a non-core payment gateway, provided the principal transaction banking relationship remains with a fully regulated bank in Luxembourg.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline rate in Luxembourg City, comprising 17% corporate income tax on taxable income above EUR 175,000 (15% on the first EUR 175,000), a 7% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, and a 6.75% municipal business tax; the full combined rate fluctuates marginally by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax; full exemption applies where the beneficial owner is (i) a Luxembourg resident full-tax company holding directly at least 10% of the share capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period, (ii) an EU/EEA parent company satisfying the same 10%/12-month conditions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or (iii) a qualifying non-resident company protected by a bilateral DTA whose reduced rate is 0%, 5%, 10% or 15% depending on the treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due within three months of the end of the financial year; a calendar-year SARL must file by 31 March of the following year. Extension requests are routinely granted, commonly to 31 December of the year following the accounting year, only where the tax return and audited financial statements are prepared by a registered tax professional; corporate income tax prepayments remain due quarterly on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File under Article 56bis LIR apply to a Luxembourg SARL that is part of an MNE group whose consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 50 million and whose related-party transactions in the year equal or exceed EUR 1 million; CbC reporting applies at the consolidated group revenue threshold of EUR 750 million and is filed by the ultimate parent or a designated surrogate in Luxembourg.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council style Economic Substance Regulations; substance requirements are embedded in the ACD's beneficial ownership test, the Arsenal Diana and abuse-of-law doctrines, the Soparfi administrative practice, and the OECD BEPS. A vertical ERP/CRM cloud SARL must demonstrate genuine economic substance locally: physical premises in Luxembourg, at least one locally resident director with independent powers, employees performing core operational functions, board minutes taken in Luxembourg, local bank account, and real decision-making; absence of these elements will invalidate tax residence, treaty benefits, the participation exemption, and the IP regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Article 214 LIR: fine of EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years; mandatory publication of the judgment; 10% administrative increase of the evaded tax; default interest of 0.6% per month; exclusion from public procurement and from holding a professional banking/technology licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a false return are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and may be subject to a travel ban (interdiction de quitter le territoire) imposed by the criminal court, asset seizure orders, and imprisonment up to 5 years; the corporate veil is pierced where the SARL is used as a mere façade.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ACD applies the general anti-abuse rule and the beneficial ownership doctrine to recharacterise the arrangement, deny DTA relief and the parent-subsidiary exemption, and reassess the full corporate income tax, municipal business tax and 15% dividend withholding tax, plus 10% penalty and 0.6% monthly interest; in cases of sham incorporation, the commercial register may order judicial liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face joint and several civil liability for the company's unpaid taxes where the entity is held to be fictitious; criminal prosecution may attach under the fraud provisions, with travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years; courts may disallow the limited liability shield of the SARL.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ACD may reconstruct the pricing under the arm's length principle, imposing a primary adjustment plus a secondary adjustment on hidden dividends; penalties for missing documentation include a taxe d'attente up to EUR 25,000 and a 10% increase of the reassessed tax, with monthly default interest; cross-border unilateral adjustments may trigger mutual agreement procedure and potential 25% penalties for gross valuation misstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false TP documentation are personally exposed to criminal fines and imprisonment for tax evasion; in administrative liability actions, managing directors who fail to maintain the mandated documentation may be held personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency and the Luxembourg tax authority may seek enforcement against the directors' personal assets.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: RBE violations carry criminal fines of EUR 500 to EUR 25,000 for legal persons and EUR 500 to EUR 1,250 for natural persons per default, with repeated offences publishing the sanction and potentially doubling; AML compliance failures under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 attract administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 or 10% of annual turnover for the SARL, and revocation of the electronic payment institution authorisation where applicable; professional certificate holders face suspension.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated responsible person for AML are personally liable for failures to implement UBO and KYC procedures; they may receive criminal fines and, in serious or repeated cases, imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; the CSSF may impose individual fines on directors, and the commercial court may order travel bans pending investigation.
Dividend Withholding and Revenue Repatriation Mismatch

Statutory sanction: The payer SARL is held liable for the unwithheld 15% dividend withholding tax plus 50% penalty for failure to withhold where the ACD discovers the misuse; a late withholding is compounded by monthly default interest and can be transformed into a criminal charge of misappropriation where funds were distributed knowing the withholding obligation.

Director personal liability: The directors who authorised the distribution without ensuring compliance are personally liable for the withheld tax under Luxembourg company law, and the tax authority can pierce the corporate veil to recover the deficiency, impose a travel ban where flight risk is shown, and pursue criminal liability for directors who knowingly circumvented the obligation.

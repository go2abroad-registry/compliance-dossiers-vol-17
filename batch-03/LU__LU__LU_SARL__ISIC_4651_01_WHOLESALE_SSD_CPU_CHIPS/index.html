Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Following the Law of 10 August 2016, the SARL has no statutory minimum share capital (statutory floor of EUR 1.00); the capital fixed in the articles must be fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be credited to a blocked bank account opened in the company's name, with a bank attestation confirming irrevocable availability of funds; the funds are released only after RCS registration. No mandatory government escrow beyond this blocked-account mechanism.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for any office or logistics premises must be issued following inspection by the communal fire prevention service (Service Incendie et de Secours), including emergency evacuation plans and alarm testing. Warehousing of high-density storage racks requires a structural load inspection certificate from an approved engineer (shelving systems rated for their design load). Occupational clearance is obtained from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), covering workplace ergonomics, electrical installation verification, and thermal management for rooms holding servers or electronic equipment; a standing workplace register and annual safety audit are required.

Paid-in capital rule: Following the Law of 10 August 2016, the SARL has no statutory minimum share capital (statutory floor of EUR 1.00); the capital fixed in the articles must be fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be credited to a blocked bank account opened in the company's name, with a bank attestation confirming irrevocable availability of funds; the funds are released only after RCS registration. No mandatory government escrow beyond this blocked-account mechanism.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax matters and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT matters. Electronic access is consolidated via the national business and tax portal myGuichet.lu (guichet.public.lu), complemented by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) company file and the AED VAT e-filing systems.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first complete commercial registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and obtain a national identification number (matricule). For direct VAT registration without a permanent establishment, a non-resident taxable person must file Form 642 with the AED and submit the constitutive documents, powers of attorney, and evidence of the foreign VAT ID, where applicable. Corporate income tax registration is effected through the annual corporate tax return filing procedure with the ACD, using the matricule as the tax identification number. A Luxembourg VAT identification number is then issued by the AED for all taxable supplies effected in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first complete commercial registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and obtain a national identification number (matricule). For direct VAT registration without a permanent establishment, a non-resident taxable person must file Form 642 with the AED and submit the constitutive documents, powers of attorney, and evidence of the foreign VAT ID, where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box transparency election exists under Luxembourg domestic law; SARLs are opaque corporations taxed at entity level. The Luxembourg fiscal unity regime allows a 95%-owned subsidiary to consolidate corporate income tax with its resident parent where both entities are resident companies. A separate VAT group election is available for closely connected persons established in Luxembourg. Anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD apply, with no elective hybrid mismatch characterisation permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established taxable persons supplying goods in Luxembourg; any taxable supply triggers VAT registration. Domestic small enterprises with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000 may benefit from the Luxembourg small business exemption, but that exemption is inapplicable to cross-border wholesale semiconductor trading.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported from Luxembourg or from another EU member state by the vendor or on its behalf to a destination outside the EU. For a wholesale SSD and server processor trader, the vendor must hold evidence of dispatch or transport out of the EU, the customer must be established outside the EU or otherwise export the goods, and title in the goods must pass to the non-EU customer. No VAT is charged where the export documentation is complete, and the related input VAT is separately recoverable by the taxable person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the automated export system SAD/EX declaration with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipt evidencing FX repatriation to Luxembourg
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order references
  • Incoterms-based proof of delivery and dispatch documents

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies in Luxembourg as of 2025. E-invoicing is permitted and treated as equivalent to paper invoices where authenticity of origin, integrity, and readability are preserved. Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing applies to central government contracts through PEPPOL BIS 3.0. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through myGuichet.lu or the AED portal; SAP-based clearance and API stamping are not yet required for wholesale supplies.

The Luxembourg tax authorities and customs administration cross-match VAT export records with ECB and bank payment data, transport documents, and consignee registries. Where export zero-rating is inadequately substantiated or where goods fail to leave the EU within the statutory export period, the AED disallows the zero rate and assesses the VAT that should have been declared. Fraudulent claims expose the taxable person to retroactive VAT collection, late interest at 0.8% per month, administrative fines, and, in severe cases, criminal penalties under Luxembourg tax and customs law. For wholesale semiconductor transactions, export compliance documentation must be retained for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The euro is freely convertible; there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or repatriation of capital for non-resident shareholders. EU free-movement-of-capital rules apply. Only statistical Balance of Payments declarations are required for cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 12,500, a reporting function discharged by the resident banking institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for export-receipt repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or held in USD, CNY, TWD, KRW, or other freely convertible currencies at the SARL's disposal, subject only to standard AML/CFT bookkeeping and tax documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); banking prudential supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to strict legal constraints. The SARL may use only a Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution authorized by the CSSF under PSD2, or an EU-passported EMI/PI under the passporting regime. Offshore non-EU digital EMIs, including UK post-Brexit firms without a Luxembourg branch or EU passport, are not permissible as the primary treasury rail; they cannot provide capital-blocked or escrow accounts (compte séquestre), which require a regulated credit institution. An EU-licensed EMI may support ancillary collections, FX conversion, and cross-border payment initiation, but high-value SSD/CPU trade settlements and escrow releases must be executed through a Tier-1 credit institution to preserve counterparty safety and AML integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer to the SARL's blocked capital account (compte séquestre) opened at the Luxembourg bank before notarial execution. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SUBSCRIPTION OF SHARES — [NOTARIAL DEED DATE] / [SHAREHOLDER RESOLUTION REF]'. The beneficiary bank applies the BCL Balance of Payments purpose code for direct investment (equity capital) for EU statistical reporting. Verification package: authenticated shareholders' resolution, notarial deed of incorporation, subscribed share ledger, certificate of deposit, and RCS registration extract. No central-bank approval is required; available funds are released to the operational account immediately upon registration in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS).Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers in USD or EUR for supplier payments, with invoice numbers, INCO terms, and goods description (ISIC 4651 semiconductors) cited in field 70, and BCL purpose code 'Payment for goods'. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% standard corporate income tax (IRC) for taxable income above EUR 200,000; plus 7% unemployment solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax (standard Luxembourg City rate 6.75%), producing an aggregate effective Luxembourg City rate of approximately 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Luxembourg SARL; reduced to 0% for qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive shareholders (at least 10% holding for 12 months); double tax treaties generally reduce the rate to 0%, 5% or 15% depending on shareholding percentage and treaty partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local file and master file documentation required for controlled transactions where annual related-party transaction value exceeds EUR 1,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not enforce offshore Economic Substance Regulations as a separate statute, but the Luxembourg tax residence, effective management, GAAR, anti-abuse, BEPS and ATAD rules require real economic substance. For the SARL's wholesale SSD/CPU brokerage business, the company must maintain employees executing contracts, tax-resident board decision-making, physical storage or logistics arrangements, independent treasury control, contemporaneous transfer pricing documents, and accounting evidence in Luxembourg; otherwise the Luxembourg tax administration may disregard the entity, deny treaty application, reattribute profits, or assess a permanent establishment of the non-resident owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent corporate income tax return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income, 10% tax fraud penalty or criminal fine up to EUR 250,000, accrual of late interest, and imprisonment of up to five years for natural persons under the Luxembourg General Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent statements or approve false invoices are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, may be subject to civil restitution, travel bans, frozen bank accounts, disqualification from management, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may disregard the entity, re-assign profits to the effective-manager jurisdiction, deny participation exemption and treaty benefits, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and, under commercial law, apply corporate dissolution or strike-off procedures for companies that become mere shells.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable when the shell was used to commit tax fraud or mislead third-party creditors and tax authorities; civil liability, director disqualification, travel bans, asset seizures and money-laundering criminal liability are available.
AML-CFT non-compliance and trade-based money laundering

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML law empowers the Financial Intelligence Unit and professionals supervisory authority to impose regulatory fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 5% of annual turnover; criminal money-laundering sanctions include forced confiscation of assets, fines, freezing orders and imprisonment up to five years; underlying tax consequences include full reassessment and penalty stacking.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly perform or facilitate trade-based money laundering can be criminally prosecuted individually, subjected to asset freezing and travel bans, ordered to pay civil compensation, held personally liable for fines not covered by the entity, and permanently disqualified from managing Luxembourg companies.
Transfer pricing documentation failure / artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: A transfer pricing audit adjustment increases the Luxembourg taxable base; a documentation penalty may be imposed separately, late interest accumulates, and tax treaty benefits may be denied under the Limitation on Benefits clauses or the Principal Purpose Test; monetary penalties can reach EUR 250,000 in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for the transfer pricing policy may be personally liable for fraudulent misrepresentations made to the tax administration, may be summoned for tax criminal proceedings, and can face personal seizure of assets or travel restrictions where the profit shift was knowingly supported by false documents.

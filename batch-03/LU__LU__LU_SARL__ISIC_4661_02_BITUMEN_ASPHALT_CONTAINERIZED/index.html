Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect tax; Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency (Administration des douanes et accises) for import/export formalities. Central e-portal: myguichet.lu; tax portals: impotsdirects.public.lu and aedt.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

A non-established foreign entity must submit the Luxembourg VAT registration form (specifically the form for taxable persons not established in Luxembourg, referencing Form 102 or the equivalent EIC procedure) through the MyGuichet.lu electronic portal, together with certified copies of the constitutive documents, proof of the foreign VAT identification number, and appointment of a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) where the foreign entity has no permanent establishment and no EU VAT identity in another Member State. The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by 8 digits within approximately 10 to 15 business days. In parallel, the SARL obtains a Luxembourg national registration number (numéro de matricule) from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) and is registered ex officio with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a resident Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) is a corporate taxpayer fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax on worldwide income and assets, subject to tax treaties, the participation exemption for qualifying shareholdings, and the ATAD-compliant interest limitation rules. The SARL is not eligible for pass-through taxation.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections: (1) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (2) option to subject real property transactions to VAT rather than registration duties; (3) election for a commercial company to be tax-transparent only for the specific partnership forms (SCS, SCSp); (4) article 164 group taxation election for certain integrated domestic corporate groups; (5) hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply mandatorily with no elective opt-out, although entity classification disalignment with foreign jurisdictions can be mitigated through the Luxembourg entity choice (e.g., SCS/SCSp transparent vs. SARL corporate).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual net turnover (exclusive of VAT) for the Luxembourg small enterprise exemption applicable only to taxpayers established in Luxembourg; a non-established foreign supplier has no de minimis threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT before effecting any taxable supply of goods or services in Luxembourg; the EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance sales threshold applies to online/remote sales of goods under the OSS procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is available under Article 41 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for export supplies of goods (including containerized drums and bags of penetration grade bitumen) dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods physically leave the EU under a complete customs export declaration, the supplier retains credible transport and trade documentation, and, where the buyer is an EU-established counterparty, the intra-Community supply qualifies under Article 42 with the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and the supply is included in the recapitulative statement. Zero-rating for intra-EU business-to-business supplies requires the goods to be dispatched from Luxembourg to another EU Member State and the customer to communicate a valid VAT ID; such supply remains subject to proof of transport within the EU.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic declaration) lodged in the Export Control System (ECS/ICS-AES) and validated by Luxembourg Customs or the customs office of exportation in the Member State of departure
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent multimodal transport document (CMR for road, sea waybill, air waybill) evidencing dispatch and physical exit from the European Union
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List identifying the goods (ISIC 4661, bitumen/asphalt products), quantities, container numbers, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 payment evidence demonstrating receipt of export proceeds on the seller's Luxembourg bank account
  • Luxembourg VAT return (Form TVA) and recapitulative EU sales list (Form RE) showing the zero-rated supply and, where applicable, Intrastat declaration for intra-EU movements

Luxembourg has no general B2B electronic invoicing mandate and no real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control system. Invoices may be issued electronically (PDF/A-XML, EDI, or other formats) subject to authenticity of origin and integrity of content under the VAT invoicing rules. Mandatory e-invoicing applies exclusively to suppliers of Luxembourg public contracting authorities through the PEPPOL BIS format. VAT returns (monthly or quarterly), the recapitulative EU sales list (RE), and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via the Luxembourg tax authorities' e-filing platforms.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs conduct joint risk-based audits of export traders that systematically claim zero-rated supplies. Documentary deficiencies, mismatched Incoterms, warehouse intermediaries without physical movement, or failure to evidence receipt of export proceeds within the commercial payment term (typically indistinguishable from the 180-day documentary credit/telegraphic transfer benchmark) can trigger a retroactive reassessment of the supply at 17% VAT, denial of input VAT deduction on related purchasing costs, and the withdrawal of zero-rated refunds. Statutory consequences include default interest at the Luxembourg statutory rate, administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 10,000 per infringement under Article 71 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, and criminal fiscal fraud sanctions under the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), including imprisonment in aggravated cases, joint and several liability of directors, and publication of the corrective tax assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full eurozone member with unrestricted capital-account and current-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and the BCL Organic Law of 23 December 1998. The euro is freely convertible; there is no national foreign-exchange control, no prior authorization for inbound or outbound payments, and no barrier to profit remittance by an SARL. Practical constraints are fiscal and documentary only: dividend withholding tax (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU for qualifying corporate shareholders, 5% to 15% under double tax treaties, or 15% at the domestic rate to non-qualifying non-residents), net wealth tax good-standing, and the CSSF-mandated AML/KYC verification of each treasury movement. USD bitumen receipts may therefore flow freely into EUR accounts or remain in USD at the SARL treasury's discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law does not impose export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation timelines, or a statutory obligation to convert USD receipts to EUR. Banks must nevertheless provide the BCL with balance-of-payments statistical classification on each cross-border transfer and, as a matter of CSSF practice, request a purpose identification statement; no substantive compliance burden is placed on the trading SARL beyond accurate remittance reference fields.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank and euro-area implementing arm of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential banking supervision is co-administered by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Luxembourg Law of 5 April 1993; economic sanctions and capital-movement prohibitions are enforced via EU Regulations and the Luxembourg law implementing the AML/CFT framework of 27 May 2022.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with material reservations. Luxembourg recognizes CSSF-authorised and passported EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) that may lawfully execute account-to-account MT103-equivalent settlement and issue payment cards on an SARL treasury account. However, an EMI/PI is legally barred from granting credit, opening trade documentary credits, negotiating letters of credit, or holding banking deposits. For a containerized bitumen wholesale operation requiring commercial LCs, supplier guarantees, and USD correspondent clearing above EUR 500,000 per event, an EMI is suitable only as a supplementary ancillary-collection and contingency-payment wallet; it is not a compliant substitute for a Tier-1 bank. Legal constraints include safeguarding obligations, transaction limits, adverse counterparty rating perception, and exclusion from BCL TARGET2 direct participation. Feasibility is therefore rated Medium for fringe treasury uses and Low as the primary banking rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must comply with the Luxembourg SARL constitution rules: minimum issued capital of EUR 12,010 fully subscribed and, for treasury credibility in a trading business, fully paid in cash. The routing is a SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from the shareholder or corporate lender to the bank account of the LU SARL, whether a pre-incorporation blocker account or the post-incorporation operating account. Field 50K must show the registered legal name of the remitting shareholder; field 59 the full name, BCL code and IBAN of the LU SARL; field 70 must state precisely: Capital contribution pursuant to shareholders resolution dated DD/MM/YYYY, or alternatively Intercompany loan under Luxembourg treasury policy. The receiving Luxembourg bank will also require a balance-of-payments direct-investment classification (equity versus intercompany loan) and, under CSSF AML/CFT rules, the full corporate file: signed resolution, notarial deed of contribution, UBO declarations, sanctions and PEP screening, certified charter, and evidence of economic substance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, with material reservations. Luxembourg recognizes CSSF-authorised and passported EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) that may lawfully execute account-to-account MT103-equivalent settlement and issue payment cards on an SARL treasury account. However, an EMI/PI is legally barred from granting credit, opening trade documentary credits, negotiating letters of credit, or holding banking deposits. For a containerized bitumen wholesale operation requiring commercial LCs, supplier guarantees, and USD correspondent clearing above EUR 500,000 per event, an EMI is suitable only as a supplementary ancillary-collection and contingency-payment wallet; it is not a compliant substitute for a Tier-1 bank. Legal constraints include safeguarding obligations, transaction limits, adverse counterparty rating perception, and exclusion from BCL TARGET2 direct participation. Feasibility is therefore rated Medium for fringe treasury uses and Low as the primary banking rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg TP documentation law (Law of 28 December 2023): Master File/Local File mandatory where the MNE group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million AND the aggregate related-party transactions of the Luxembourg entity exceed EUR 1 million per fiscal year. The Local File must be delivered within 30 days after a request from the ACD; the Master File within 30 days. CbC Report threshold EUR 750 million group revenue. Penalty for deficient documentation: EUR 50,000 per document (Article 56 LIR), plus possible 10% revaluation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply ESR-type offshore legislation, but substance is mandatory: (1) Article 14 LIR — management and control (siège de direction effective) in Luxembourg; (2) General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 6 StAnpG) and ATAD GAAR deny treaty/participation benefits where the arrangement is non-genuine; (3) EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse clause (12-month holding and subject-to-tax test); (4) FATF/AML substance for the bitumen wholesale business: local office, staff, warehouse/incoterm evidence, transport contracts, and trade documentation to avoid a finding of a nominee 'letterbox' operation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years (Article 1733 AO and Article 496 et seq. Criminal Code); administrative 10% surcharge; late-payment interest at 0.6% monthly; denial of treaty benefits under the GAAR

Director personal liability: Criminal liability of signatories: imprisonment of the managing director; personal asset seizure; travel ban and international arrest warrant issued by the investigating magistrate; civil claim by the tax administration in the criminal proceedings (partie civile) for restitution of the evaded tax plus interest.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Luxembourg seat of management and control; corporate residence reattributed to the parent jurisdiction; reclassification of the entity as transparent for CIT/net wealth tax; imposition of the 18% (formerly 15%) filing fine where no return; OECD/ATAD GAAR denies the EUR 50 million group thresholds and participation exemptions; possible de-registration from the RCS

Director personal liability: Directors face 'faute de gestion' liability under Article 495-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 (action en comblement du passif) — joint civil liability for company debts including taxes where negligence caused insolvency; criminal charges for falsification of accounts; travel bans in proceedings for concealment of assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 56 LIR, the ACD re-determines the taxable base at the arm's-length price; the taxpayer bears the burden of proof; adjustment of hidden profit distribution triggers 15% dividend WHT and solidarity surcharge; TP documentation failure: EUR 50,000 fine per missing document plus a 10% penalty on the re-assessed tax; interest at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: The tax authority can name the director in the assessment order as a donee of the hidden distribution (transparent attribution), creating personal solidarity liability for the WHT shortfall; criminal referral for income tax evasion where the TP adjustment exceeds materiality thresholds; interdict (travel ban) on the director's private assets pending payment.

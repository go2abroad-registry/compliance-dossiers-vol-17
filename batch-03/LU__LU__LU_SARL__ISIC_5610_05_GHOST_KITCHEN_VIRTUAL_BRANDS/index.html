Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permissible for the statutory registered seat: a SARL may be registered at a private address, at the manager's residence, or at the office of a CSSF-approved domiciliation agent under the Law of 31 May 1999 on the domiciliation of companies. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000; the entire capital must be subscribed and fully paid up before incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a report by an independent auditor (réviseur d'entreprises).

Funding & unlock path: Before opening, the facility must obtain a fire safety clearance from the competent municipal fire protection service, including emergency exit signage, extinguisher location and kitchen exhaust fire-suppression testing; a structural load inspection certificate for cooking equipment, ovens and extraction systems; and occupational health and safety registration with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including affiliation with the accident insurance association and a workplace inspection report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT. Central portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg-resident SARL obtains its corporate income tax NIF automatically upon registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés. For VAT, the SARL must file Form 506 (VAT registration application) via MyGuichet with the Administration de la TVA, indicating estimated taxable turnover. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) and submit the registration application through that representative; no registration threshold applies to non-resident suppliers. The VAT number is issued in the format LU00000000 prior to commencing Luxembourg taxable activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg-resident SARL obtains its corporate income tax NIF automatically upon registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés. For VAT, the SARL must file Form 506 (VAT registration application) via MyGuichet with the Administration de la TVA, indicating estimated taxable turnover. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) and submit the registration application through that representative; no registration threshold applies to non-resident suppliers. The VAT number is issued in the format LU00000000 prior to commencing Luxembourg taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) tax consolidation (fiscal unity) under Article 164bis LIR; (ii) VAT grouping under Article 59 of the VAT Law; (iii) option to waive the small-business VAT exemption; (iv) participation exemption election under Article 166 LIR for qualifying shareholdings; (v) application of hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD as implemented by Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for resident small businesses (VAT exemption); non-resident entities have no threshold and must register before supplying goods/services in Luxembourg; intra-Community distance sales subject to OSS threshold of EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of 0% VAT applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or on behalf of the customer. The customer must establish non-EU status and the goods must leave the EU territory within the statutorily accepted timeframe (generally within 90 days or the transport document validity). For a ghost kitchen, export zero-rating is limited to physical meal/consumer goods sold to non-EU customers with adequate transport evidence; offshore services to non-EU recipients may be outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT if the recipient is a business and the service is effectively used outside the EU. The taxpayer must retain full documentary evidence of export movement and the non-EU business status of the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/payment evidence showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU customer
  • Commercial invoice / proforma invoice matching the export declaration
  • Proof of non-EU customer status (e.g., contract, order, country of establishment)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping for B2B/B2C VAT invoices in Luxembourg. VAT returns (Form 102) must be filed electronically via MyGuichet on a monthly or quarterly basis depending on turnover. E-invoicing is mandatory in the B2G sector through the PEPPOL network. All invoices must comply with EU VAT invoicing rules and be retained for 10 years.

The Luxembourg tax authorities may deny zero-rating where documentary evidence is incomplete or fraudulent, and issue additional VAT assessments at the standard 17% rate. Late-payment interest accrues at 0.6% per month on the underdeclared amount. Administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 or double the evaded tax may be imposed for deliberate VAT evasion, with criminal liability in cases of fraud. Input VAT deductions originally claimed against the zero-rated export may be retrospectively clawed back. The standard limitation period for VAT assessments is five years, extending to ten years in fraud situations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls on capital or current account transactions pursuant to Article 63 TFEU and applicable OECD/EU liberalisation codes. Profit repatriation, intercompany payments and liquidation proceeds are freely remittable, subject only to standard AML/KYC diligence, proper corporate authorisation and statutory withholding tax at 15% or treaty-reduced rates for non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, foreign-exchange conversion or holding-period rule for export proceeds in Luxembourg. EUR and non-EUR export receipts are freely creditable to the company's bank accounts; the bank may require the standard balance-of-payments statistical declaration to the BCL for material cross-border receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the European System of Central Banks and in coordination with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for prudential and AML matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. A Luxembourg or EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution can support multi-platform payouts, API-driven foreign-currency settlement and lower-cost cross-border transfers; however, an EMI cannot replace the physical bank needed for statutory capital escrow, notarised contributions, credit lines or documentary trade finance. Offshore digital EMIs without EEA passporting are Low viability and will be rejected by Luxembourg clearing banks because they lack correspondent bank sponsorship and regulatory equivalence.

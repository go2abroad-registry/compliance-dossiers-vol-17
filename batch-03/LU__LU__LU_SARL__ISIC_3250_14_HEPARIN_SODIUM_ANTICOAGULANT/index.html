Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) for corporate registration; official portal: guichet.public.lu and myguichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first obtain a Luxembourg national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule) by filing an application with the ACD through the Luxembourg Business Registers portal. For VAT purposes, registration is made with the AEDT using the relevant VAT registration form (Form 1 for domestic or Form 3 for non-established taxable persons). If the foreign entity has no Luxembourg establishment, it may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT compliance. Tax identification is generally issued within one to two weeks after successful RCS incorporation or its foreign equivalent filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a SARL is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). Luxembourg resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, with relief available under the participation exemption and double-tax treaties; non-resident entities are taxed only on Luxembourg-source income attributable to a permanent establishment or Luxembourg real estate.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg domestic law. Available statutory elections include: (i) optional tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for resident subsidiaries and their 95%+ owned sub-subsidiaries; (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) election allowing related parties to be treated as a single taxable person; (iii) optional VAT election (option d'assujettissement) for real-estate leases; and (iv) ATAD-related hybrid mismatch elections, limited to neutralising or re-characterising hybrid instruments/entities in accordance with Luxembourg implementation of EU Directive 2016/1164.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual turnover (VAT-exclusive) for the small-business exemption; no registration threshold for a foreign supplier making taxable supplies in Luxembourg; intra-Community distance sales threshold is €35,000 per calendar year; services subject to reverse-charge rules are exempt from local VAT and do not require Luxembourg registration except where validation of a Luxembourg VAT ID is needed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations may be zero-rated provided the goods physically leave the territory of the European Union within the legally prescribed period and the supplier retains documented proof of export. Services provided to non-EU businesses are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rules (B2B reverse-charge), but the supplier must obtain and retain reliable evidence of the customer's non-EU status. Domestic zero-rating is also available for certain intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered customers when a valid EU VAT number is provided.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD, or EX-1) or authorised exporter certification
  • Bill of Lading or electronic air waybill (e-AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export payment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport contract/carrier evidence (CMR consignment note, rail waybill, ship manifest, or freight forwarder confirmation)

B2G electronic invoicing is mandatory via PEPPOL standards in Luxembourg; domestic B2B e-invoicing is not a legal requirement; no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory tax-authority validation of e-invoice national codes. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the AEDT eTVA portal.

Luxembourg tax authorities can reassess zero-rated export transactions for up to ten years from the due date of the tax period. If zero-rating is incorrectly applied or documentary evidence is missing, the VAT is retroactively clawed back together with late-interest. Fraudulent claims may incur penalties ranging from 10% to 50% of the tax due depending on the degree of intent (good faith vs. wilful deception), and criminal prosecution is possible in cases of fictitious exports or forged customs documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg has full and free currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. As a euro-area Member State, the euro is legal tender and there are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for cross-border transfers, no quantitative limits on payments or repatriations, and no distinction between resident and non-resident for convertibility purposes. Article 63 TFEU prohibits all restrictions on payments and capital movements between Member States and between Member States and third countries, subject only to EU-wide prudential, AML and sanctions measures. Profit remittance is unrestricted in FX terms; the only frictions are fiscal: dividends paid by an SARL incur Luxembourg withholding tax of 15% (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU/EEA corporate parents; 5%/10%/15% tax-treaty rates for treaty-eligible foreign shareholders; 15% for non-treaty shareholders), interest and royalties generally paid to non-residents are free of Luxembourg withholding tax except in abusive/interest-coverage cases, and no branch-repatriation tax applies. All payments are routed through banks which, under the AML Law of 25 March 2020, must verify the underlying economic purpose. Sanctioned jurisdictions, especially Russia, Belarus, Iran, Syria and DPRK under EU Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) regulations as implemented by Luxembourg, are blocked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement in Luxembourg. The law imposes no timeline for repatriation of export receipts, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to sell foreign currency generated by exports into euro. A Luxembourg SARL may freely invoice in EUR, USD, CNY, CHF or any other currency; may maintain foreign-currency accounts at any licensed bank or, under PSD2 rules, at a licensed payment/e-money institution; and may hold export proceeds offshore or onshore indefinitely. Indirect constraints are limited to: (i) tax accounting in euro for the annual accounts and Luxembourg corporate income tax; (ii) exchange gains/losses being recognised as ordinary taxable income or deductible losses; and (iii) AML obligations requiring the bank to receive a plausible source-of-funds narrative and to perform sanctions screening on the paying party. The liberal treatment results from Luxembourg being bound by the free-movement-of-capital rules of the TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) / Central Bank of Luxembourg; the BCL is the euro-area national central bank and the operational arm of the European Central Bank (ECB) for the euro, TARGET2 (T2) settlement and balance-of-payments statistics (VADER reporting). The BCL does not conduct exchange-control administration because none exists in Luxembourg. Supervision of commercial banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), operating under the CSSF Law of 12 November 2004 and the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, together with the AML/CFT Law of 25 March 2020.

Sanctions/embargo exposure: US or EU sanctioned jurisdictions in the trade route, any vessel/shipowner or beneficial owner matching SDN/EU/UK/HMT lists.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Luxembourg SARL the statutory minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. The founders must open a blocked escrow account ('compte bloque') at a Luxembourg bank in the name of the company in formation; the bank issues an attestation de blocage (capital-release certificate) on which the notary relies when authenticating the articles of association. The compliant wire protocol is: (1) the founding shareholder instructs its non-resident bank to send a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA/T2 message where both parties are in EEA) in euro to the escrow account, using the IBAN starting with LU and the Luxembourg BIC; (2) the payment reference must include the words 'versement du capital social - constitution LU_SARL' (share capital subscription) and the shareholder's name; (3) if the sending bank requires a balance-of-payments/ECB purpose code, the correct classification is 'equity capital - inward direct investment' in the direct-investment enterprise; (4) the remittance must originate from the subscribing shareholder's own account, never from a third party; (5) the Luxembourg bank performs source-of-funds verification and may request a source-of-funds memorandum, certified shareholder resolution and audited financials; (6) at notarial deed signing the escrow account is released and becomes the operational account (or is transferred to it upon RCS registration); (7) capital increases follow the same MT103 rail with a notarial/shareholders resolution and updated ECB/BCL direct-investment reporting. Ecuadorian? Not applicable. Inbound non-EU capital requires no Luxembourg exchange-control permit; only the bank's ordinary AML/KYC analysis and BCL external-statistics reporting apply. ESG? E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM (restricted to non-core treasury flows). A Luxembourg SARL may legally open payment accounts with CSSF-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) or payment institutions (PIs), or with EEA-authorised EMIs/PIs passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and Directive 2009/110/EC. Providers such as Revolut Business, Wise, iBanFirst, Airwallex and Payhawk can execute currency conversion, SEPA, and some MT103-equivalent correspondents, with video-only KYC. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not grant credit, issue or confirm letters of credit; (ii) client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts consistent with EMIR/financial-services safeguarding rules; (iii) transaction/volume limits and refusal rights are common for pharmaceutical trade, especially with Chinese/US counterparties; (iv) no MT700 documentary credit capability; (v) digital-only onboarding is accepted, but on later EDD the EMI may require physical documents and bank references, and may freeze mid-size flows pending proof of trade. Conclusion: an EMI is viable only for marginal FX conversion and supplier payments below EUR 50,000, not for the SARL's core LC/SWIFT treasury. BGL BNP Paribas or BIL remain the anchor banks.

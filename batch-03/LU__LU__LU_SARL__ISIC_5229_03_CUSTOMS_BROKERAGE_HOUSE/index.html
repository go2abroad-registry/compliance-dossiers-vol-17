Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS), operated under the Ministry of Justice via the eJustice portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended; Articles 180-190 and associated transitional provisions governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Grand-Ducal Regulations implementing the RCS electronic filing system.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif) containing the articles of association (Statuts), filed electronically with the RCS.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 52 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum capital for a Luxembourg SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account in the company’s name at a Luxembourg credit institution, and a bank release certificate must be produced to the notary. In-kind contributions require an independent auditor’s valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties, and excise; central business registration portal: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / MyGuichet.lu; customs authority: Administration des douanes et accises (Customs and Excise Administration).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SARL must first execute incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). The RCS issues a registration number which triggers notification to the AEDT for VAT and registration duties. The ACD subsequently attributes a national direct-tax identification number (matricule). For non-resident entities with no fixed establishment, direct VAT registration with the AEDT is required, with appointment of a local VAT representative if even one taxable transaction is performed in Luxembourg. The EORI number must be obtained separately for customs activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an SARL must first execute incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). The RCS issues a registration number which triggers notification to the AEDT for VAT and registration duties. The ACD subsequently attributes a national direct-tax identification number (matricule). The EORI number must be obtained separately for customs activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. An SARL is a statutory capital company subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax at entity level. No pass-through election exists. Available elections include: (1) joint election for VAT group registration (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Code for closely linked entities; (2) election for fiscal unity under Article 164bis LIR for resident parent-subsidiary groups holding at least 75% shareholding; and (3) option for the 30,000 EUR small-undertaking VAT exemption (which is generally inadvisable for a B2B customs brokerage). Hybrid mismatch elections and CFC rules are governed by the ATAD implementation law of 21 December 2018 and do not provide elective reclassification of entity status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No universal turnover registration threshold applies: a taxable person whose annual turnover (excluding VAT) does not exceed EUR 30,000 may benefit from the small undertaking exemption, but VAT registration remains mandatory without threshold for non-established taxable persons, for intra-Community acquisitions, for receipt of services subject to reverse charge, and for any taxable person who waives the exemption. A customs brokerage SARL will generally be mandatorily registered because its B2B services fall within the general reverse-charge regime and cross-border activities require a VAT identification number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating may be claimed for (a) supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier, provided the goods leave the EU customs territory; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer with a valid EU VAT ID and submission of the EC Sales List; (c) auxiliary transport and ancillary services directly related to exports and intra-Community supplies, including customs brokerage, forwarding, warehousing, and transit services performed directly for the exporter or consignee; and (d) services performed by customs brokers as intermediaries where the underlying supply qualifies for zero-rating and all statutory proof requirements are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD/DAU) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch
  • Proof of departure from EU customs territory (customs office endorsement, exit summary declaration, T1 transit document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent banking evidence of receipt of payment from the foreign counterparty
  • Commercial invoice and VAT invoice complying with Articles 43-44 of the VAT Code
  • EORI number and, where applicable, customs brokerage power of attorney issued by the exporter
  • Proof of the counterparty's VAT registration for intra-Community supplies (valid VAT ID and EC Sales List)

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. E-invoicing is permitted under EU rules and B2B e-invoicing is not yet mandatory. VAT returns must be filed electronically via the FEDeRation (Déclaration TVA) XML gateway; EC Sales Lists (recapitulatif) must be filed electronically through eCDF. Customs declarations are lodged via the EU Customs Decision System / Import Control System 2 / AES (Automated Export System); certified customs brokers must connect through the Customs Information System with an EORI number, use authorized electronic message formats, and comply with the electronic filing mandatory for customs operations.

The AEDT may conduct an audit typically within a 6-year assessment window (extendable to 10 years in case of fraud or undeclared taxable transactions). Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger repayment of under-declared VAT plus default interest at the statutory rate of 3.0% per annum. Administrative penalties range from EUR 750 up to the amount of evaded tax; in cases of deliberate fraud, penalties may reach 100% of the evaded VAT. Criminal liability attaches under Article 89 of the VAT Code, with imprisonment up to 5 years for tax fraud. Customs authorities may impose separate penalties for false export declarations, including forfeiture of goods and suspension or revocation of the customs broker license.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates a fully liberalised euro-area currency regime with complete convertibility on both current and capital account. No exchange-control authorisation, BCL permit, or CSSF approval is required for cross-border transfers, dividend distributions, royalty or interest payments, repayment of shareholder advances, or repatriation of equity on liquidation. Eurozone membership and Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantee free movement of capital and payments; conversion is subject only to standard bank-side AML/KYC screening, EU-wide targeted financial sanctions, and statistical declaration thresholds. The only genuine profit-remittance tax hurdle is the domestic 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, which is reduced to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA parent companies under Directive 2011/96/EU (subject to a 12-month holding period, minimum participation and substance/anti-abuse conditions) and to 5% for eligible US corporate shareholders under the Luxembourg–US income tax treaty; direct dividend and service-fee payments are otherwise unlimited in amount and currency. A customs brokerage SARL may therefore invoice and receive in EUR, USD, CNY, GBP, CHF or any freely convertible currency without central-bank approval, provided each flow is properly documented to substantiate the underlying UCC customs activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export receivables may remain indefinitely in foreign currency in a properly declared resident account, may be converted into euro at the commercial interbank rate, or may be netted under genuine back-to-back offset structures. The BCL external statistics regime (pursuant to the BCL regulation on external statistics, applying BPM6/Eurostat codes) obliges the resident credit institution to classify each export or service-receipt flow and report it to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes; this is purely a statistical reporting obligation and not a conversion mandate. The practical compliance burden for the Luxembourg customs brokerage entity is documentary: recurring foreign-currency receipts should be matched to the corresponding commercial invoice, UCC customs declarations, DELTA/eCustoms records, CMR/airway bills, and the brokerage service contract so that the bank's monitoring system does not flag the flows as unexplained third-party payments or trade-based money laundering.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the Luxembourg central bank and national member of the Eurosystem — exercising prudential and macro-prudential oversight of resident credit institutions jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), the competent Luxembourg authority for AML/CFT supervision, licensing and supervision of banks, payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs), and enforcement of CSSF Regulation 12-02 and the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing. The BCL operates the TARGET2-Luxembourg settlement system, manages the euro-liquidity window for resident banks, and collects balance-of-payments and external-stats reporting from resident credit institutions under the BCL external statistics regulation (implementing the BPM6/Eurostat classification framework).

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined: 17% corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), 7% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax (effective 1.1875%), and 6.75% municipal business tax (Luxembourg City base rate).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% ownership, 12-month holding period) or to 5% under DTA provisions.

Box 44 – Permanently exempt income Dividends and capital gains qualifying for the 100% participation exemption, including associated foreign withholding tax (covered by treaty or EU directive). Attach a statement of the participation, holding period, and the effective taxation of the subsidiary in its residence country. Late filing: administrative fine €250–€2,500 (up to €5,000 in case of recurrence); late payment: 9% annual interest on the unpaid amount, plus 3/6/9/12% staggered surcharge after; fraud: 50% penalty on evaded tax on top of 9% interest and possible criminal fine of up to €250,000.
Box 69–71 – Net taxable income Taxable basis after adding back non-deductible expenses (taxes, fines, excessive directors' fees) and deducting available loss carryforwards. Apply a pro-rated loss compensation if the Luxembourg PE has a different fiscal history from the head office. Late filing: administrative fine €250–€2,500 (up to €5,000 in case of recurrence); late payment: 9% annual interest on the unpaid amount, plus 3/6/9/12% staggered surcharge after; fraud: 50% penalty on evaded tax on top of 9% interest and possible criminal fine of up to €250,000.
Box 73 – Municipal business tax base Assessment base for municipal business tax, usually equal to the taxable income plus 2% of interest on long-term debt and certain royalties. Compute separately for each Luxembourg municipality where the PE operates. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File when the Luxembourg-resident member is part of an MNE group whose consolidated turnover exceeds €750 million; preparation required upon demand if the local entity has revenue below €1.5 million and exceptional characteristics.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not enact a separate Economic Substance Regulations law, but tax residence, OECD BEPS, and EU anti-abuse doctrines apply (Art. 6 Abgabenordnung, EU directive ATAD, and CJEU jurisprudence). A customs brokerage SARL must demonstrate adequate premises, qualified staff, operational independence, and risk management commensurate with its receipts; a pure shell exercising no real control over customs brokerage contracts will be disregarded, and its payments may be recast as constructive dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud and Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment up to 10 years back; evasion penalty equal to 50% of the evaded tax (25% if spontaneous disclosure), 20% penalty for incomplete declarations, 9% annual interest; criminal fine up to €250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years (Art. 187 of the General Tax Law / Art. 405 of the Criminal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certified false financial statements are jointly and severally liable for the corporate liability, are subject to travel bans, prohibition of leaving Luxembourg while under investigation, and may be sentenced to 5 years’ imprisonment; the corporate veil may be disregarded under Art. 18 of the Tax Adaptation Law.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence, DTA benefits and EU directive exemptions; reclassification of all flows as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT; 100% surcharge on the under-declared tax and 9% interest; potential notification to the anti-abuse network of the DAC-6 regime.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for the resulting tax if the entity is deemed a test tube (société écran); personal assets may be seised and managers may face imprisonment up to 3 years for abusing signals of law (abus de droit) under Art. 8 of the General Tax Law.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment by the tax administration; secondary adjustment reclassifying the excess as a loan or dividend (15% WHT); fine of 25% of the adjustment, increasing to 50% for bad faith; country-to-country reporting penalty of up to €25,000 per unjustified failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve off-market pricing may be liable under general tort (Art. 1382 of the Civil Code); in the event of insolvency, liquidators or tax office may call on the directors' personal liability for the unpaid corporate tax plus penalties, and if fraud is proven, 5 years imprisonment.
AML/CTF Non-Compliance & Licence Violation in Customs Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Imposition of large VAT and customs penalties (up to twice the evaded duties), revocation of the customs licence, AML administrative fines up to €5 million or 10% of turnover (Law of 12 November 2004 as amended); criminal confiscation of goods and up to 5 years' imprisonment for money laundering, extended to 10 years for organised crime.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal conviction, disqualification from commercial activity, travel restriction, and confiscation of personal assets; Luxembourg courts allow the lifting of the corporate veil in case of abuse of the company's personality to commit illegality, resulting in joint liability.

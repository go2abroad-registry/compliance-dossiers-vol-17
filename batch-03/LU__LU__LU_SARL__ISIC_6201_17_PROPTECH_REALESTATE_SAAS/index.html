Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office address is not acceptable as the registered office of a Luxembourg SARL. The registered office must be a physical address in Luxembourg where the company's corporate records can be inspected, legal process can be received, and general administration can be carried out. A serviced office, business center or registered office rental from a regulated corporate service provider may be acceptable if supported by a genuine service agreement and the location is not merely a mail-forwarding facility. For a PropTech SaaS platform, a serviced office with substantive managed infrastructure is acceptable for incorporation, but the company should maintain local business substance appropriate to its activities.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000. All quotas must be fully subscribed and at least 25% of each quota must be paid up at incorporation. The unpaid balance must be paid within five years. Capital must be deposited on a blocked escrow bank account in Luxembourg and can be released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification must be obtained from the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Service (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours), covering evacuation routes, smoke detection, and alarm systems. Structural load clearance from the building owner is required for office occupancy. Occupational workplace clearance must be provided by the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines (ITM) covering electrical safety, ventilation, sanitary facilities, and general worker health and safety conditions.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000. All quotas must be fully subscribed and at least 25% of each quota must be paid up at incorporation. The unpaid balance must be paid within five years. Capital must be deposited on a blocked escrow bank account in Luxembourg and can be released only after RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes; Registration, Estates and VAT Administration (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDV) for VAT; principal portal: guichet.lu; online VAT filing portal: eTVA.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL, the company is registered ex officio upon incorporation. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must apply for a VAT identification number to the AEDV using Form 563 (available via MyGuichet.lu) and, unless exempt under special measures, must appoint a Luxembourg-established VAT representative. Direct tax registration for a permanent establishment is done through the ACD by filing the annual corporate tax return and registering on the eCDF platform; the company obtains a national business number (numéro de registre) from the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Luxembourg SARL, the company is registered ex officio upon incorporation. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment must apply for a VAT identification number to the AEDV using Form 563 (available via MyGuichet.lu) and, unless exempt under special measures, must appoint a Luxembourg-established VAT representative. Direct tax registration for a permanent establishment is done through the ACD by filing the annual corporate tax return and registering on the eCDF platform; the company obtains a national business number (numéro de registre) from the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; a SARL is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Available elections include the option to form a single taxable person for VAT purposes (VAT grouping under Article 60 Loi TVA), optional tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 164 bis LIR for qualifying resident companies, and certain hybrid mismatch alignment elections required by the European Union Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small business exemption under Article 57 Loi TVA); exemption does not apply to certain digital/electronic services; non-established taxable persons supplying digital services or goods from abroad are obliged to register from the first euro of Luxembourg taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods outside the European Union is zero-rated where the supplier retains commercial and customs evidence proving that the goods left Luxembourg/EU territory within the prescribed timeframe (generally within 90 days of dispatment). Qualifying intra-EU dispatches to a taxable person are likewise zero-rated when the customer's valid EU VAT number is stated. Offshore digital and SaaS services supplied to a foreign recipient outside the EU fall outside the Luxembourg VAT scope (no VAT), provided the place of supply rules are met; zero-rating also applies to certain international services relating to intellectual property or real estate located abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A/SAD customs declaration in the exporting member state)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document, e.g., CMR or air waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of payment in foreign currency received from the non-EU buyer)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping mandate applies for B2B invoicing. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies through Peppol when responding to public tenders. All VAT returns and recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) must be filed electronically via the eTVA portal. Invoices must comply with Directive 2006/112/EC authenticity, integrity, and content requirements.

Luxembourg tax authorities scrutinize documentary evidence for zero-rated exports; if substantiation is inadequate or false, a VAT assessment on the export transaction is issued for the standard rate, with default interest from the due date of the VAT return, administrative fines (up to 10% of the tax amount for non-fraudulent errors, and higher penalties for bad faith), and criminal sanctions for deliberate fraud. Retroactive clawbacks may reach all undeclared VAT plus interest, and liability can extend to the responsible directors and the appointed VAT representative.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as an EU Member State within the euro area, maintains unrestricted currency convertibility. Both current-account items (dividends, interest, royalties, service fees) and capital-account operations (equity injections, intercompany financing, M&A transactions) are free from exchange-control approvals, quantitative limits, or forced conversion. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally unrestricted; any tax leakage is limited to withholding taxes under domestic law and applicable tax treaties, not FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender obligations for export proceeds, receivables, or SaaS subscription revenues in Luxembourg. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency offshore or onshore without mandatory conversion into euros. The BCL imposes only statistical reporting obligations for balance-of-payments purposes, with no conversion ratios or repatriation deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which jointly administer Luxembourg's monetary, financial-stability, and banking-prudential compliance framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorized by the CSSF under PSD2, or EU/EEA EMIs passing into Luxembourg under the freedom-of-services passport, can be used for operational collection and disbursement rails. They are not viable substitutes for full credit institutions when the SARL must hold capital escrow balances, issue guarantees, or establish complex trade-finance structures. Offshore EMIs without EU/EEA licensing face legal prohibition from providing payment services in Luxembourg unless properly passported, and will present significant AML/KYC and operational risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Beneficial owner or ultimate shareholder located in a jurisdiction classified as high-risk or non-cooperative by FATF, EU, or CSSF.

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled corporate registry extracts, registered joint-venture or shareholder agreements, UBO registry transcript from the Luxembourg Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs (RBE), independent source-of-wealth report prepared by a regulated fiduciary, and sanction-screening clearance from a recognized screening provider.
PropTech SaaS platform processing rental payments on behalf of third-party landlords, creating perceived third-party payment flows or unlicensed payment-service risk.

Root cause: PropTech SaaS platform processing rental payments on behalf of third-party landlords, creating perceived third-party payment flows or unlicensed payment-service risk.

Defense evidence: Audited operational agreements with landlords and tenants, a detailed description of the platform flow ensuring no legal reception of client funds, an external legal opinion under PSD2 classifying the model as non-payment-service activity, a written AML/CFT risk assessment, and current CSSF registration confirmation if any exemptions apply.
Complex cross-shareholding or multi-tier corporate structure obscuring the economic substance of the SARL.

Root cause: Complex cross-shareholding or multi-tier corporate structure obscuring the economic substance of the SARL.

Defense evidence: A complete organizational chart, chain-of-title documents, constitutional documents of each intermediate holding entity, audited financial statements for the prior two years, evidence of substance (local registered office, Luxembourg resident directors, physical operational activity), and declaration confirming the absence of nominee-shareholder arrangements without proper disclosure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection of share capital, shareholder loans, or intercompany contributions is routed via SWIFT MT103 over TARGET2 for EUR-denominated wires. The transfer should reference the Luxembourg balance-of-payments statistical purpose code (e.g., Code 'FDI' / Capital-in-kind identifier) and the SARL's standing client reference. Verification protocols require matching the received amount to the shareholders' recorded commitment in the statutory accounts, the CSSF UBO declaration, and the capital call documentation. Amounts exceeding EUR 50,000 require standard AML identification and may prompt Article 4 law requests; always execute the capital injection as a single clear-correspondent EUR transfer without intermediary currency conversion.Outbound disbursements for SaaS procurement, cloud-hosting fees, technology royalties, and subcontracted services are settled via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for euro-area counterparts. For non-euro trade settlement, SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers are used. If the underlying commercial contract necessitates documentary instrument protection, the disbursement rail may be a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) with an advising and confirming Luxembourg bank, or a documentary collection (URC522) using a bill of exchange.

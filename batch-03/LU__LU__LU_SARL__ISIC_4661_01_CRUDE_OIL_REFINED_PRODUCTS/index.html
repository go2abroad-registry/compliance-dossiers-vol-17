Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 1.00. The entire subscribed capital must be fully subscribed and at least 25% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation. The paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account ('dépôt bloqué') at a Luxembourg credit institution until RCS registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Certificat de sécurité incendie) is required for the registered office if the premises exceed 200 persons' capacity or 500 m² usable floor area; this is issued by the Inspection Générale de la Protection Civile after inspection. Occupational clearance from the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines (ITM) is mandatory, including the declaration of any employees and compliance with the Luxembourg Labor Code (travaux réglementés, first-aid, and workplace risk assessment).

Paid-in capital rule: The statutory minimum share capital is EUR 1.00. The entire subscribed capital must be fully subscribed and at least 25% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation. The paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account ('dépôt bloqué') at a Luxembourg credit institution until RCS registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg; direct tax authority: Administration des contributions (ACD); official e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu / eTVA

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg must register for VAT via the AEDT before carrying out taxable supplies in the Grand Duchy. The application is submitted electronically through MyGuichet.lu or via AEDT Form 100 (TVA); non-EU/third-country entities must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative established in Luxembourg unless an exemption applies under a VAT mutual assistance agreement. The AEDT issues a 15-digit national VAT identification number (format LU + 8-digit). For corporate income tax, the entity must first be registered in the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and obtain a Luxembourg business number; corporate tax registration is then effected with the Administration des contributions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg must register for VAT via the AEDT before carrying out taxable supplies in the Grand Duchy. The application is submitted electronically through MyGuichet.lu or via AEDT Form 100 (TVA); non-EU/third-country entities must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative established in Luxembourg unless an exemption applies under a VAT mutual assistance agreement. The AEDT issues a 15-digit national VAT identification number (format LU + 8-digit). For corporate income tax, the entity must first be registered in the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and obtain a Luxembourg business number; corporate tax registration is then effected with the Administration des contributions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a check-the-box mechanism. Available elections include (i) group taxation (fiscal unity) for resident companies with 95% owned taxable persons under Article 164ter of the Income Tax Law (LIR); (ii) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains under Article 166 LIR; (iii) hybrid mismatch and subject-to-tax elections under the ATAD implementation law (loi du 18 décembre 2019); (iv) optional simplified VAT small-enterprise exemption regime under Article 57 of the VAT Law (loi TVA).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 of annual taxable turnover for resident small businesses; no threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales to Luxembourg consumers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier retains documentation proving the export within 30 days (or an extended period approved by the AEDT). Intra-EU dispatches of goods to a customer identified by a valid EU VAT number are zero-rated if the EC sales list is submitted. Additionally, supplies of marine fuel and lubricants to ships operated for international navigation (bunker fuels) are exempt with a right to deduction under Article 50 of the VAT Law, subject to proof of the ship's international voyage.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory electronic invoicing for business-to-government (B2G) transactions through the PEPPOL network (BIS 3.0); no statutory B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance; VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu (monthly or quarterly based on turnover); no digital stamping or continuous transaction controls.

The AEDT may retroactively recharacterise zero-rated exports as domestic taxable supplies if the required documentary evidence is not held at the time of dispatch. The resulting assessment is at 17% VAT, plus monthly late interest of 0.6% and administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 for documentation failures. Fraudulent export-zero-rating claims are subject to criminal sanctions under Article 92 of the VAT Law (imprisonment of up to three years and/or a fine of up to EUR 250,000), together with a retrospective clawback for five years and the loss of the right to automatic VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies no exchange control restrictions. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account, consistent with Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-65 and the euro-area freedoms. Profit repatriation, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, repayment of shareholder loans and liquidation proceeds are unrestricted, provided corporate law formalities are met (annual general meeting approval of distributions, statutory auditor statement when required under the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, and the net-asset test). Practical hurdles are limited to (i) banking due-diligence documentation, (ii) EU/OFAC and United Nations sanctions screening on counterparties and payment corridors, and (iii) the absence of a correspondent relationship for banks located in non-cooperative or high-risk jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements and no minimum mandatory conversion ratios in Luxembourg. A Luxembourg entity engaged in the physical wholesale brokerage of marine fuel oil and distillates may freely retain foreign currency receipts in a Luxembourg bank account, convert them at market rates, or apply them to offshore trade settlement. The only obligations are general AML/CFT record-keeping and accounting transparency under CSSF Regulation 12-02 of 14 December 2012, as amended, and the Luxembourg Commercial Code; no BCL declaration or license is required for foreign currency holdings.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential conduct supervision and AML/CTF oversight of banks are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector and the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary collection/disbursement floats, Low for primary trade settlement. A Luxembourg or EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution, operating under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), can provide IBAN accounts, FX conversion and payment initiation; however, wholesale marine-fuel brokerage transactions are high-value, high-volume and inherently risky, so major commodity counterparties, LC issuing banks and trade-finance providers will require settlement through a fully licensed credit institution with direct TARGET2 or SWIFT access. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs (e.g., unregulated or weakly regulated entities in the Caribbean, Middle East or Asia) are not viable because (i) they lack access to SEPA/T2 and CHIPS/Fedwire, (ii) Luxembourg banks will reject incoming credits from unregulated EMI accounts as a de-risking measure, and (iii) documentary LC and standby LC rails cannot be operated through an EMI.

Defense evidence: Certified business plan, pro-forma treasury forecast, share capital proof, list of strategic suppliers and buyers, due diligence files on counterparties, management CVs, internal AML/KYC and sanctions screening policies, and audited financial statements of the direct parent or guarantor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed through the Luxembourg treasury account via TARGET2/SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR (SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008) with reference to the purpose code 'EQUI' (equity or participation) and the legal text 'Capital contribution to LU_SARL'; for USD/GBP/CHF injections use SWIFT MT103 through correspondent banks in New York or London, with the same purpose coding and full supporting remittance information. The bank will require the executed shareholder subscription resolutions, up-to-date register of shareholders, notarised deed of contribution (for in-kind or mixed contributions) and, where required, an independent auditor's report under the Law of 10 August 1915. The SARL statutory minimum capital threshold (EUR 12,000) must be fully paid or covered by a bank certificate; surplus injections can be booked as share premium or subordinated shareholder loans. All inbound USD received on the foreign-currency account may be converted to EUR or retained freely, subject only to the bank's pre-transaction sanctions and source-of-funds screening.Physical commodity disbursements should be settled through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct freight, VAT, storage and demurrage payments; MT202 COV for third-party cover payments routed through correspondent banks; for price risk and documentary security, use MT700 (issued or confirmed commercial documentary LC), MT760 (standby LC) and, where appropriate, the SWIFT Trade Services Utility Bank Payment Obligation (BPO). USD settlement flows through CHIPS/Fedwire correspondents in New York; EUR flows clear in T2. Every payment instruction must be fully audit-trailed with the trade contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, full set of shipping documents, independent inspection certificates (e.g., SGS, Intertek), quantity/quality reports, certificate of origin and customs declarations; instruction narrative should include purpose code 'TRAD' (Trade) or 'SUPP' (Suppliers), invoice/LC reference, and no inconsistent free-text. Payment release should be subject to a two-person authorisation matrix and real-time sanctions screening (OFAC, EU, UN) on all parties and vessels.Deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, EUR/GBP) executed bilaterally with a Luxembourg-licensed credit institution or investment firm under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex, or under a 1998 FX Global Code-aligned master agreement, with physical delivery at maturity.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for hedging real trade receivables/payables denominated in non-convertible or restricted currencies of petroleum-importing jurisdictions; the NDF must be documented as a risk-reduction instrument tied to a specific underlying trade invoice.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/CNY) allowable for medium-term risk mitigation on trade-finance and working-capital facilities, subject to ISDA/CSA documentation, EMIR margin and reporting obligations (EU Regulation 648/2012), and internal hedge-policy approval.Vanilla FX options (call/put, European style) are permissible for hedging commodity margin exposure where the hedging intent is documented under IFRS 9 hedge accounting; exotic or leveraged options are discouraged and generally unavailable absent a strong credit rating and additional collateral.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (IRC) for taxable income above EUR 175,000; 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 175,000 (art. 174 LIR). After 7% solidarity surcharge on IRC and 6.75% municipal business tax for Luxembourg City, the top aggregate effective rate is 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (art. 90 LIR). Reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate shareholder holds >=10% for at least 12 months; many Luxembourg tax treaties reduce the rate to 5% or 10% for portfolio holdings and 0% for qualifying substantial holdings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is 30 June of the year following the fiscal year-end where the fiscal year equals the calendar year. Extension to 31 December is routinely granted upon request. Corporate income tax prepayments are due quarterly on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December; any residual balance is due upon final assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: required where the consolidated group revenue is >= EUR 50 million. Local File: required where the Luxembourg entity's annual net turnover is >= EUR 1 million and group consolidated revenue is >= EUR 50 million (Circular 2017/1); CbCR: required for groups with consolidated revenue >= EUR 750 million. All amounts in EUR.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE-style regimes; however, substantive minimum-threshold tests are mandatory through: (i) the General Anti-Abuse Rule (art. 6 LIR and paragraph 6 of the Luxembourg Tax Adaptation Act); (ii) TP functional and risk analysis under Circulars 2017/1-2017/4; (iii) the participation exemption conditions (subject-to-tax and, for capital gains, 12-month holding); (iv) beneficial-ownership analysis for treaty and EU Directive claims; and (v) for a marine fuel oil and distillates trading SARL: a physical trading desk in Luxembourg, local risk-management and decision-making staff, independent office premises, bank accounts and capitalisation sufficient for the trading volume, and documented trade transactions, failing which profits may be re-attributed to the real principal jurisdiction and treaty benefits denied under the MLI principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 500,000 under the Criminal Code and art. 396-1 LIR; reassessment of the full tax shortfall plus 0.6% monthly interest; civil surcharge of up to 100% of the evaded tax; publication of the conviction; in aggravated cases, asset freezing and confiscation of trading proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be held jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts where they knowingly signed false returns; exposure to criminal penalties, travel bans, personal asset seizure and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the Luxembourg entity for tax purposes, re-attribution of profits to the foreign principal, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, recharacterisation of contracts, retroactive reassessment with 100% surcharge and interest, mandatory DAC6 disclosure penalties up to EUR 250,000, and removal from the Luxembourg commercial register in extreme cases.

Director personal liability: Civil liability to the tax authority for the full corporate tax debt upon veil-piercing; administrative fines; disqualification from office; in cross-border fraud cases, extradition exposure and personal restitution orders.
AML / Sanctions Violation (Oil Trading)

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004: fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons and imprisonment up to 10 years for money laundering (Criminal Code art. 506-1); confiscation of assets and trading proceeds; revocation of the mandatory wholesale licence; CSSF administrative sanctions and delisting from trade finance facilities; freezing orders under EU sanctions regulations; exclusion from SBLC and SWIFT-based payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability of up to 10 years imprisonment, personal confiscation of funds, travel bans, professional debarment, and potential OFAC secondary sanctions exposure for USD clearing; corporate veil may be pierced to recover laundered proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Commodity Mis-pricing)

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment by the ACD plus secondary adjustment and 0.6% monthly interest; penalty of 25% of the additional tax for gross negligence; documentation penalty up to EUR 25,000 for failure to produce Local/Master File; extended 10-year assessment limitation period where the taxpayer has not declared cross-border related-party transactions; EU Arbitration Directive mutual agreement procedure where no relief is obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured or approved the mispricing may be personally liable for the additional tax where the arrangement lacks commercial substance; risk of criminal proceedings for tax fraud in intentional cases, travel restrictions, and claw-back claims by minority shareholders or liquidators.

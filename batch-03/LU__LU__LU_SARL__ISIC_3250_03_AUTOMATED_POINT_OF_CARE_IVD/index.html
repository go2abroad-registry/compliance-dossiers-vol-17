Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Luxembourg; applications are filed electronically via the MyGuichet.lu portal and the eJustices platform, with the notary acting as the statutory intermediary.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), together with the Law of 23 September 2020 modernising company law; SARL governed by Articles 710-1 to 710-25; supplemented by the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 19 December 2008 on the Trade and Companies Register (RCS/RESA) and Regulation (EU) 2017/746 for the IVD manufacturing scope.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif / statuts) certified by a Luxembourg notary, followed by mandatory publication of the incorporation particulars in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA) and registration on the official RCS application form (formulaire C); the RCS extract (extrait) constitutes the certificate of incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 8 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation; cash contributions must be paid into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened with a Luxembourg credit institution or with the Caisse de Consignation; the banker's blockage attestation must be remitted to the notary, and the funds are released only upon effective registration in the RCS.

E-government filing portal

SOP: (1) Execute a notarial deed of incorporation and register the SARL with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS); the State IT Centre (CTIE) automatically assigns the corporate national identification number (matricule), which serves as the direct-tax TIN. (2) Obtain the business licence (autorisation d'établissement) from the Ministry of the Economy for manufacturing activities; IVD producers additionally require regulatory licensing under the EU IVDR and Ministry of Health notification. (3) File electronic VAT registration with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu using the form Déclaration d'inscription à la TVA before commencing taxable supplies; the AEDT issues a VAT identification number in the format LU plus 8 digits and a VAT certificate. (4) Notify the competent ACD local tax office of the registered seat to open the corporate income tax file; annual CIT and municipal business tax filing is mandatory regardless of profitability. (5) Non-established foreign suppliers or zero-turnover structures may be required to appoint a Luxembourg VAT and customs representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Luxembourg SARL is a resident full taxpayer at entity level: subject to corporate income tax at 17%, municipal business tax at the communal rate (6.75% in Luxembourg City), solidarity surcharge at 7% of corporate income tax, and net wealth tax at 0.5% with applicable allowances. The territorial scope reaches worldwide income, relieved by Luxembourg's treaty network and the domestic participation exemption for qualifying dividends and capital gains. No pass-through or disregarded-entity status exists under Luxembourg domestic law.

Available election forms

Luxembourg domestic law provides no check-the-box or pass-through election; the SARL is mandatorily an entity-level CIT taxpayer. Available elections: (i) full fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under LIR Article 164bis for Luxembourg-resident groups holding at least 95% of eligible subsidiaries; (ii) the Notional Interest Deduction (NID) on qualifying new equity; (iii) the 80% IP-box income exemption under LIR Article 50bis on net qualifying IP income, subject to OECD nexus substantiation. For US federal tax purposes, the SARL is not a per se corporation, so a Form 8832 check-the-box election may classify it as a partnership or disregarded entity; hybrid mismatch consequences are neutralized or reported under Luxembourg ATAD transposition (LIR Articles 168ter/168quater) and DAC6 disclosure rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover is the domestic small-business exemption ceiling for Luxembourg-established businesses; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration. In practice a manufacturing SARL supplying IVD equipment to B2B customers, importing raw materials, or making intra-EU acquisitions is required to register irrespective of the threshold. Non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold: registration is due before the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Intra-EU distance sales of goods exceed the EUR 10,000 pan-EU annual threshold and become taxable in the destination member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies to (i) final exports of goods outside the European Union under Loi TVA Article 44(1)(1), provided the goods leave EU territory within 90 days of dispatch and the exporter retains complete transport and customs evidence; and (ii) intra-EU dispatches of IVD equipment to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number under Article 44(1)(2), supported by compliant invoicing and EC Sales List filing. Supplies of microfluidic cartridges, immunoassay analyzers, and directly related calibration or installation services are zero-rated where the goods physically exit the EU or the service is received by a non-EU recipient. Export refunds are claimed through periodic VAT returns; excess credit positions are repayable by the AEDT within statutory timeframes.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD with Movement Reference Number (MRN)) lodged with Luxembourg Customs and Excise
  • Commercial invoice (final) denominated in the contract currency, describing the goods, Incoterms, and the export supply
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical movement from Luxembourg or the EU
  • Customs exit confirmation generated by the EU electronic export system (departure message or export proof within the 90-day departure window)
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) and customer order referencing the recipient's valid EU VAT identification number for intra-EU zero-rated supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no digital stamping mandate, and no API-based continuous transaction controls for domestic VAT invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to business-to-government (B2G) invoicing via the national Peppol-compliant platform, phased in for central government transactions from 1 January 2024 and for broader public entities thereafter. Invoice content, sequential numbering, and 30-day issuance requirements follow Loi TVA Articles 57-58. VAT periodical returns (monthly for large taxpayers, quarterly for the small-business regime) and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through the AEDT e-filing portal. Luxembourg is transposing the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform, which will introduce mandatory e-invoicing and near-real-time digital reporting for cross-border B2B supplies by 1 July 2030.

The AEDT may claw back incorrectly zero-rated VAT within the standard five-year limitation period, extended to ten years for fraud or intentional concealment, reassessing the VAT at 17% plus default interest of 0.8% per month under Article 39 Loi TVA. Administrative fines under Articles 62 and 63 Loi TVA range from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 and may reach four times the evaded tax for fraudulent conduct. Criminal tax fraud (fraude fiscale) is punishable under the General Tax Law and the Criminal Code by imprisonment of one to five years and fines from EUR 12,500 to EUR 125,000. Customs offences attract penalties under the Union Customs Code (Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, Article 42) and Luxembourg customs law, including confiscation and per-declaration fines. The AEDT, ACD, and customs administration cross-verify export declarations, VIES counterparty status, and banking receipts, triggering joint audits and mutual assistance procedures. Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as a prudential substantiation and transfer-pricing convention, and failure to repatriate within that period may raise commercial substance and pricing audit questions rather than a standalone VAT penalty.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Luxembourg applies the EU capital-movement freedoms (Art. 63 TFEU) and Directive 88/361/EEC Annex I; there are no exchange-control permits, investment approvals, or profit-repatriation licences for a Luxembourg SARL. Remittance of dividends or liquidation proceeds is subject only to corporate-law gates: annual net-asset test (net assets must exceed subscribed capital plus non-distributable reserves, including the 5% legal reserve), notarial/board formalities, statutory auditor confirmation, and a 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (treaty-reduced if applicable). No central-bank or CSSF consent is required for shareholder distributions or capital reduction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in Luxembourg and the euro area. IVD export proceeds may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency, freely deployed, converted, or reinvested without mandatory surrender, conversion ratios, or holding-period constraints. Enterprises are only obliged to file periodic balance-of-payments statistical declarations with the BCL (e.g., forms for trade in goods/services), which have no effect on the disponibility of funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), a Eurosystem member, exercises national central-bank functions for TARGET2/T2 settlement, liquidity management, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting; the European Central Bank (ECB) sets euro-area monetary policy and administers foreign-exchange reserves. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent authority for bank licensing, ongoing prudential supervision, AML/CFT enforcement, and payment-services/EMI regulation.

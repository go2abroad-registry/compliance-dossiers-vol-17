Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,100), fully subscribed at incorporation. Each cash share must be paid up to at least 25% of nominal value at subscription, with the balance due within five years. The capital is deposited into a blocked escrow bank account ('compte bloqué') opened with a Luxembourg or EU credit institution and released only upon RCS registration; non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety attestation from the Inspection Générale de la Protection Civile (Ministry of Home Affairs), including fire-resistance ratings, extinguishing systems, and chemical-storage segregation; structural load and stability clearance by an approved technical control bureau (bureau de contrôle agréé, e.g., Secolux); workplace safety authorization from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Labor Code, with mandatory Safety Data Sheets (SDS), chemical hazard signage, personnel protective-equipment plans, and occupational medical surveillance records.

Paid-in capital rule: SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,100), fully subscribed at incorporation. Each cash share must be paid up to at least 25% of nominal value at subscription, with the balance due within five years. The capital is deposited into a blocked escrow bank account ('compte bloqué') opened with a Luxembourg or EU credit institution and released only upon RCS registration; non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT registration and indirect tax administration; Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) – corporate income tax administration; central government e-portal: MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A non-established foreign entity must file Form 31 with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number, providing evidence of identity, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, and expected turnover. The AEDT issues an 8-digit VAT ID prefixed 'LU'. A Luxembourg-incorporated SARL first obtains a national business registration number from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register and then registers for VAT and corporate tax through the competent offices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-established foreign entity must file Form 31 with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number, providing evidence of identity, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, and expected turnover. The AEDT issues an 8-digit VAT ID prefixed 'LU'. A Luxembourg-incorporated SARL first obtains a national business registration number from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register and then registers for VAT and corporate tax through the competent offices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a check-the-box or similar pass-through election for a SARL. Qualifying resident parent companies may elect fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for CIT purposes with their 95%-owned subsidiaries, simultaneously covering corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by ATAD-aligned provisions and generally not available to alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established taxable persons; non-established suppliers and distance sellers are subject to mandatory registration without a local turnover threshold, subject to EU One-Stop-Shop schemes and the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the physical export of goods to a destination outside the EU, provided the goods leave EU territory and are documented within 90 days of supply. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are also subject to the zero rate where the customer provides a valid EU VAT ID. Offshore B2B services are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT if the place of supply is deemed to be the customer's establishment outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from EU territory
  • CMR transport contract or equivalent proof of carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate for export proceeds received in Luxembourg

No general real-time fiscal clearance or API stamping mandate for B2B issuance. B2G invoicing is mandatory in EN 16931 PEPPOL electronic format. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted with recipient consent and must support complete audit trails. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and export declarations are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu and EU reporting systems.

The Luxembourg VAT authority may retrospectively audit export substantiation. Missing or unsatisfactory evidence triggers reversal of the zero rate, assessment of VAT at 17%, late interest of 0.8% per month, and administrative penalties ranging from 10% to 50% of the underpaid tax. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims may be prosecuted as criminal tax fraud, with fines up to EUR 240,000 and custodial penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg for current-account and capital-account transactions. Article 63 TFEU prohibits restrictions on capital movements and payments between EU Member States and between Member States and third countries, subject only to EU-wide sanctions, counter-terrorism financing measures and public-policy exceptions. No exchange-control permit, prior approval, registration, waiting period or repatriation fee applies to equity injections, shareholder loans, trade settlements, dividend distributions or capital repayment. Dividends remitted to a non-resident shareholder are subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at 15% in the absence of relief, reduced or abolished under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% after a 12-month minimum holding period, provided substance and beneficial-ownership conditions are met) or under applicable double-tax treaties. The only practical frictions are documentary: banks require a payment-purpose statement, source-of-funds evidence and sanctions screening to satisfy BCL balance-of-payments statistics and CSSF AML obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory repatriation timeline and no compulsory conversion ratio. As a euro-area member, euro-denominated export receipts require no conversion, and non-euro proceeds are freely retained or converted. The applicable obligations are documentary and AML-driven: export proceeds must be credited through a supervised Luxembourg or EU credit/payment institution; transaction-level documentary evidence, including sales contracts, commercial invoices, customs Single Administrative Documents (SAD), Intrastat declarations for EU dispatches, bills of lading and batch certificates of analysis under Directive 2011/62/EU, must be retained for at least five years; and payments above the reporting threshold (€12,500 per individual transaction) are captured by the servicing bank in the BCL balance-of-payments reporting. Commercial terms should require settlement within open-account or documentary-credit maturities consistent with bank working-capital facilities.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), established by the Law of 22 April 1998, is the national central bank and a member of the Eurosystem. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent prudential and AML/CFT supervisory authority, while the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises direct prudential supervision over significant Luxembourg credit institutions under the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM). The BCL operates the TARGET2 euro large-value payment and settlement system in Luxembourg, collects balance-of-payments statistics, and executes monetary policy; the CSSF enforces the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended, together with CSSF Regulation 12-02 and subsequent AML/CFT circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary operating and treasury account; MEDIUM only as a supplementary collection rail. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs), including PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. and Mangopay S.A., and passported EU EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex and Currencycloud, may provide EUR IBANs, SEPA and SWIFT execution and digital onboarding; however, the ISIC 4649 pharma-API wholesale profile is an EBA-recognized high-risk sector for counterfeit medicines, product diversion and trade-based money laundering, and EMIs generally reject or heavily restrict API wholesalers. Legal constraints under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended by PSD2, and the EU E-Money Directive prohibit EMIs from granting credit, issuing documentary letters of credit or holding pre-incorporation capital escrow. The treasury architecture must therefore place all material liquidity, escrow, trade-finance, LC and FX-hedging flows with a Tier 1 Luxembourg credit institution; EMIs should be limited to low-value collections with automated reconciliation and immediate repassaging to the primary bank.

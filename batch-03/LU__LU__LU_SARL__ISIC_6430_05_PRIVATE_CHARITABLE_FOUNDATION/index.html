Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), L-2914 Luxembourg, acting through the notary’s authenticated electronic filing portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, especially Title IV, Articles 101 et seq. on the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (S.à r.l.); Law of 16 May 2007 on the Organisation of the Luxembourg Business Registers; Law of 19 May 2020 introducing digital procedural reforms; and, for charitable foundation status, the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2001 on foundations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) deposited electronically with the RCS, followed by mandatory publication in the official electronic companies gazette, the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 36 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for an S.à r.l. is EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,000). The full amount must be subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be placed in a blocked bank account in the name of the company in formation; the bank’s blocking certificate is delivered to the notary. In-kind contributions require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d’entreprises agréé). The blocked funds are released only upon successful RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Corps Grand-Ducal d’Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) is required before occupancy, including emergency exits, extinguishers, and evacuation signage. The municipal building authority must issue structural load and occupancy clearance. The Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) must accept the workplace occupational risk assessment, first-aid provisions, and workstation ergonomics.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for an S.à r.l. is EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,000). The full amount must be subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be placed in a blocked bank account in the name of the company in formation; the bank’s blocking certificate is delivered to the notary. In-kind contributions require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d’entreprises agréé). The blocked funds are released only upon successful RCS registration.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Direct Tax Administration of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, with portal access via guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu and the eCDF filing platform; indirect taxes (VAT, registration duties) are administered by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); company registry functions are performed by the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL), with publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and complete the single RCSL registration through the one-stop 'Guichet unique' on MyGuichet.lu, which generates the national business registration number (matricule / numéro d'identification) used as the base tax identifier across all administrations. Step 2 — The ACD automatically opens the corporate income tax file (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax file (impôt commercial communal) and net wealth tax file (impôt sur la fortune) upon RCSL data transfer; formal tax registration is perfected when the matricule is referenced in the first eCDF filing. Step 3 — Apply separately to the AEDT for the Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA, format LU + 8 digits) via the MyGuichet business procedure; application must be submitted before commencing taxable activity (no notarial requirement). Step 4 — Register the charitable endowment business model with the Ministry of Justice/competent ministry if recognition of public utility status is sought; obtain the AEDT 'tax exemption file' where applicable. Step 5 — Register for municipal business tax with the relevant commune, which follows the RCSL data propagation. Non-resident owners require a local registered office, a professional director (chef d'entreprise agréé for certain regulated activities), and must appoint a designated tax representative for procedural compliance; the entire onboarding normally completes within 10-15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a statutorily opaque legal entity subject to corporate income tax (IRC) on worldwide income at the progressive rate of 15% up to EUR 175,000 and 17% above EUR 200,000, plus a solidarity surcharge of 7% on the IRC amount (18.19% top effective IRC); municipal business tax applies at approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City (combined top rate approximately 24.94%); net wealth tax applies at 0.5% with minimum tax of EUR 535 (or EUR 3,550 where financial assets exceed 90% of total assets and EUR 350,000). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a statutorily opaque legal entity subject to corporate income tax (IRC) on worldwide income at the progressive rate of 15% up to EUR 175,000 and 17% above EUR 200,000, plus a solidarity surcharge of 7% on the IRC amount (18.19% top effective IRC); municipal business tax applies at approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City (combined top rate approximately 24.94%); net wealth tax applies at 0.5% with minimum tax of EUR 535 (or EUR 3,550 where financial assets exceed 90% of total assets and EUR 350,000). The SARL is tax-resident by virtue of its registered office and effective management in Luxembourg; no territorial reduction applies. A SARL operated as a private charitable foundation does not automatically enjoy exemption: full CIT/NWT exemption is available only upon judicial or ministerial recognition of non-profit public utility status, or where the entity pursues exclusively non-commercial charitable mission per section 165 LIR, in which case non-business endowment income is exempt but commercial activities remain taxable.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under section 164bis LIR for groups holding at least 95% of a resident subsidiary, permitting offset of profits and losses; (2) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under articles 60-61 of the VAT law for closely linked resident entities; (3) option to waive the EUR 35,000 small-business VAT exemption and register voluntarily; (4) election for the non-profit public utility regime with resulting CIT/NWT exemption and tax-deductible donation treatment for donors; (5) election of the investment-management fund regime if the endowment vehicle is structured as a specialized investment fund (SIF) alongside the charitable purpose. Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; legal classification is determined by the statutory form, and hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by mandatory ATAD anti-hybrid rules (law of 23 December 2016 and subsequent amendments).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover exclusive of VAT (small-business exemption under article 57 of the VAT law); registration becomes mandatory as of the day the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. For non-resident suppliers, a separate EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold and the reverse-charge regime apply, but the resident SARL is not affected. Charitable grant income is disregarded for the threshold where it constitutes true donations rather than consideration for a taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under article 74 of the VAT law if the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch and the exporter holds full transport evidence. Ancillary services directly linked to exported goods (handling, transport, inspection, loading) are zero-rated under article 75. For the charitable endowment SARL (ISIC 6430), cross-border services — grant management, endowment administration, philanthropic advisory and grant-distribution services — supplied to non-EU foundations and donors are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules (customer-location principle with reverse charge in the recipient's jurisdiction); these are not treated as 'export services' requiring zero-rating documentation. Domestic services supplied for a fee to Luxembourg-resident clients are taxable at standard or reduced rates (17% standard; 14%, 8%, 3% for enumerated categories). Pure grant distributions and donations received are outside the scope of VAT in the absence of a direct consideration. The 180-day benchmark reflected in this dossier corresponds to the documentary retention and evidentiary window applied by Luxembourg customs and tax authorities for export completion files; no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline exists in Luxembourg due to full capital-account liberalization, but the 180-day period is used prudentially for customs liquidation and zero-rating proof.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD) export declaration accepted by Luxembourg Customs with valid Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Proof of physical exit from EU customs territory (customs office exit confirmation, ECS/ICS records, transit documents)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR/rail consignment note evidencing carriage to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice, pro forma invoice and sales contract containing delivery terms (Incoterms)
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign currency consideration
  • Insurance certificates, packing list, weight notes and any correspondence evidencing transport instructions and export intent

Luxembourg operates no real-time fiscal clearance (CTC) or digital stamping system. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only in the B2G segment (invoices to Luxembourg public-sector entities must be submitted via the PEPPOL network in EN 16931 format). B2B e-invoicing is voluntary subject to recipient acceptance; a mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime is under legislative preparation (draft tax package expected to phase in from 1 January 2026, aligned with the EU ViDA framework). Taxpayers must issue structured electronic invoices under the agreed standards when used, and all invoices must be archived in a tamper-evident electronic format for 10 years in accordance with article 56 of the VAT law; the AEDT may require access to the archived data during audits, but pre-clearance or real-time approval by the authority is not required.

The AEDT may reassess zero-rated transactions within a 5-year standard limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or deliberate misdeclaration. Where export evidence is incomplete, the 0% rate is retrospectively clawed back and the 17% standard VAT becomes due, together with default interest normally computed at 0.6% per month (7.2% annualized) on the underpaid amount. Administrative fines for invoicing and declaration irregularities reach EUR 12,500 per violation; fraudulent export claims and forged customs documentation attract criminal fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of directors, additional customs fraud penalties under the Union Customs Code, publication of sanctions, and personal civil liability of the SARL's managers under company law. The tax authorities apply enhanced scrutiny to recurring export patterns, rapid reimbursement requests, and transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions; clawback assessments are frequently combined with net wealth tax and corporate income tax adjustments in the same audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area state with full convertibility on both current account and capital account transactions. No exchange-control approval is required for inward or outward capital movements, profit repatriation, or charitable grant remittances. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantees unconditional free movement of capital within the EU; remittance and distribution routes are subject only to EU AML/CTF rules, sanctions, tax reporting, and the statutory governance of the philanthropic foundation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts and other foreign currency earnings may be freely retained in any currency. There is no conversion timeline; the only administrative obligation is statistical reporting of cross-border payments to the BCL for balance-of-payments purposes, undertaken by resident banks rather than the entity.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); banking and AML/KYC supervision is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

FEASIBILITY RATING: Medium-High for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions passporting into Luxembourg; Low for offshore non-EU/EEA EMIs without a Luxembourg branch or CSSF authorization. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot act as the primary custodian of large endowment capital, cannot issue credit or treasury products, and must comply with the Luxembourg AML/CFT framework, e-money safeguarding rules, and CSSF conduct-of-business requirements. EMIs are suitable primarily for low-value grant disbursements, operational cards, and ancillary payment flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment contributions must use SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the beneficiary IBAN (LU account), with full remitter information, transaction reference, and a clear purpose indicator such as 'CHARITABLE CONTRIBUTION', 'ENDOWMENT CAPITAL INJECTION', or 'DONATION'. The remitting and beneficiary banks verify the travel-rule dataset under EU Regulation 2015/847, screen against sanctions and AML/CTF lists, and confirm the foundation's RCS registration and tax status. Commercial letters of credit are not suited to pure philanthropic grant flows; an LC is used only if the foundation's treasury holds genuinely trade-related assets or performs collateralized transaction operations.

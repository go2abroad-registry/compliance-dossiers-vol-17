Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office consisting solely of a postal or correspondence address is generally not permissible for an SARL that will conduct physical parcel consolidation, because Luxembourg company law requires a genuine 'siege social' situated in Luxembourg and a physical location where the management can be contacted and records are kept. A co-working suite or serviced office with a physical address, a lease, and local management presence is acceptable if such presence is not merely sham or mailbox-only. Given that ISIC 5229 (freight transport agency and parcel consolidation) does not require a regulated license, the practical tolerance for a low-cost physical office is moderate; however, the tax authorities and commercial registry may challenge insufficient substance if the company cannot demonstrate a real, permanent establishment in Luxembourg.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCSL), operated under the Ministry of Justice via the eJustice portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated; Title V, Articles 710-1 to 710-20 concerning the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Luxembourg Business Registers and company and annual accounts filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte de constitution) together with the articles of association (statuts), filed electronically through eJustice/RCSL and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For a SARL the minimum issued and paid-up capital is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be fully paid up before incorporation and deposited into a temporary blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank must issue a blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) that is released only after RCSL registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps clearance for fire load, evacuation routes, extinguishing systems and hazardous material storage; structural engineer's load certificate for racking and mezzanine floors; workplace inspection by the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines covering occupational safety, forklift traffic plans, noise, manual handling, first-aid equipment and worker welfare facilities.

Paid-in capital rule: For a SARL the minimum issued and paid-up capital is EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be fully paid up before incorporation and deposited into a temporary blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank must issue a blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) that is released only after RCSL registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – direct tax authority; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT/registration; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) – customs; secure e-filing portal: MyGuichet (https://guichet.public.lu/en)

E-government filing portal

Register the SARL before the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through an authenticated notarial deed; the RCS automatically transmits the company's incorporation data to the AEDT for VAT number (numéro d'identification à la TVA) issuance. Simultaneously apply for a corporate tax reference number (numéro de référence) with the ACD. A foreign company that is not resident in Luxembourg but has a permanent establishment or supplies taxable operations in Luxembourg must apply for a VAT ID through form 640C/12 (TVA registration application) filed via MyGuichet, attaching the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, proof of place of business, identity of legal representatives and an EORI number where customs transactions are planned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' classification election exists under Luxembourg tax law. However, an election is possible under Article 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LITL) to form a tax consolidation group for resident companies. A VAT group election is available under Article 62 of the VAT Law, enabling qualifying entities established in Luxembourg to be treated as one taxable person. The Luxembourg IP Box constitutes an elective patent box regime in respect of qualifying IP income. The ATAD-consistent hybrid mismatch rules (Law of 18 December 2017) apply automatically without any elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 net annual turnover for small-business exemption; EUR 10,000 aggregate intra-Community distance sales threshold for non-resident suppliers; mandatory registration where a fixed establishment or Luxembourg place of business exists (no de minimis).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU may be zero-rated (0%) provided the exporter (1) retains evidence of export within 90 days of delivery, (2) holds a valid export declaration accepted by customs, and (3) maintains transport documentation proving departure from EU territory. Freight forwarding and ancillary services (sorting, consolidation, packing, handling, temporary storage) connected with internationally moved goods may benefit from a 0% rate, provided they are directly supplied to the international carrier/consignor or linked to a documented export operation. Intra-EU supplies require the counterpart's active VAT number and evidence of shipment to another Member State.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A / SAD) validated by the Administration des douanes et accises (ADA)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note showing physical movement from Luxembourg to non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement (SWIFT/SEPA) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in EUR within the statutory window

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime currently applies. B2B e-invoicing is valid when generated in a standard European format (EN16931) with the buyer's consent. VAT returns, recapitulative statements (Recap) and annual listings must be submitted electronically through the MyGuichet e-filing portal. Reporting thresholds and formats align with EU VAT Directive. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) project is expected to impose uniform digital e-invoicing from 2028, but not yet law.

The Luxembourg AEDT may challenge zero-rated export treatment if documentary evidence is incomplete or contradicted by shipping records. Standard audit limitation period is six years from the due date of the VAT return (ten years in cases of suspicion of fraud or tax evasion). Late assessments attract interest at the legal rate (currently 4.5% p.a.) and penalties for recalcitrant taxpayers can escalate to 100% of the tax due. Criminal penalties for false VAT declarations include imprisonment and/or financial sanctions where fraudulent export schemes are detected. Retroactive cancellation of zero-rating will result in VAT, interest and penalties being collected from the taxpayer, and possible liability of directors under corporate law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency within the Economic and Monetary Union. Luxembourg maintains no unilateral exchange-control restrictions on current-account or capital-account operations. Capital movements and current payments are liberalised under Articles 63–66 TFEU, subject only to EU sanctions, AML/CFT (notably Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers) and statistical reporting obligations to the BCL. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is not subject to an exchange-control permit or conversion approval; the practical hurdles are corporate-law distribution tests, economic-substance expectations, and Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (15% domestic rate, reduced under tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive). There are no restrictions on holding foreign currency accounts in Luxembourg or on repatriating export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit for converting or retaining export receipts. A Luxembourg SARL may lawfully keep foreign-currency receivables in its bank accounts, convert at negotiated commercial rates, or use them for trade settlements without BCL approval. Credit institutions may collect balance-of-payments and monetary statistics for the BCL/ECB, and all cross-border payments must satisfy the EU Travel Rule, but no percentage of export proceeds must be repatriated or converted.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) acting as the competent national authority for banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision, and compliance enforcement under Luxembourg law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions and payment institutions that are passported into Luxembourg under PSD2 and supervised by their home-state regulator, provided they maintain client-money safeguarding and full Transfer of Funds Regulation compliance. An example is an Airwallex-type EU-licensed EMI operating through virtual IBANs and SEPA clearing rails; this is feasible for operational FX, local collections, and high-frequency parcel-settlement payments. However, an EMI is not a Luxembourg credit institution and does not offer the same deposit-guarantee protection; the legal constraint is that it cannot take deposits or engage in commercial banking lending without a CSSF credit-institution licence. Non-EEA offshore digital banks are rated Low unless they obtain a Luxembourg/EU passport, because they lack direct access to SEPA/TARGET2 and may present AML substance concerns.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be made by bank transfer only, never by cash. For euro-denominated receipts from EEA shareholders, use SEPA Credit Transfer to the LU SARL IBAN. For non-EEA transfers, use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 with cover instructions for high-value transfers), including the recipient legal name, LU IBAN, BIC of the receiving bank, and a purpose wording such as 'Capital contribution to LU SARL' or 'Shareholder loan to LU_SARL'. The receiving Luxembourg bank must be provided with the notarial incorporation deed or capital-increase resolution, share register, UBO declarations, source-of-funds bank statements, and commercial justification before final credit. No BCL exchange-control approval is required, but the bank will verify that the sender and beneficiary are not on sanctions lists and that the transfer complies with the EU Travel Rule requiring full remitter and beneficiary data. Any in-kind contribution must be separately documented and reported in the corporate filings.For outbound trade settlements, the primary rails are SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated invoices within the EEA, SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA disbursements, and TARGET2 for irrevocable euro settlement of high-value logistics payments through the participating Luxembourg bank. For large parcel linehaul, freight, or customs-duty cascades, commercial documentary letters of credit under ICC UCP600 and standby letters of credit may be used with the bank’s trade-finance desk. For high-frequency low-value disbursements to consolidators, an XML pain.001 file may be submitted to an EMI or local clearing provider, but full SWIFT/SEPA traceability and transfer information compliance must be preserved. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined corporate income tax rate (17% IRC + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax for Luxembourg City)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax; reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (qualifying 10% holding for 12 months); treaty rates typically 5%–15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year; Master File required for MNE groups with consolidated turnover >= EUR 750,000,000; CbC Report above same threshold.

Economic substance scope: No Cayman/BVI-type ESR regime, but Luxembourg enforces economic substance through GAAR, EU ATAD Principal Purpose Test, and domestic substance-based anti-abuse rules. A SARL must have real local substance (registered office, physical presence, employees, meetings) to obtain tax residence, treaty benefits, and participation exemptions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of tax, monthly late interest (0.6%), fines ranging from 10% to 200% of underpaid tax; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if gross negligence or fraudulent misrepresentation is proven; imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to reflect arm's length profit, late payment interest, and penalty of up to 10% of adjustment; secondary adjustments may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who directly set non-arm's length prices may be personally liable for penalty assessments and interest; travel bans and asset seizures may be imposed during enforcement.
Shell Company / Denial of Substance Benefits

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, rejection of the participation exemption, re-qualification of dividends subject to 15% WHT, and potential assessment of additional taxes.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors may owe civil damages for facilitating sham structures and may be prohibited from acting as directors for up to 10 years.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: AML administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000; confiscation of assets; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability for laundering losses, travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification under Luxembourg company law.

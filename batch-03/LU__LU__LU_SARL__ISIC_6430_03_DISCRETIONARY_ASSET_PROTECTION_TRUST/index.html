Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital, fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution, evidenced by a deposit certificate; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the competent fire prevention authority (Service central d'incendie et de secours) confirming evacuation routes, local fire alarms, and extinguishers; structural load inspection by a certified engineer where office loads exceed initial building assumptions; workplace safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) for electrical and occupational-health compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum share capital, fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution, evidenced by a deposit certificate; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax (VAT); Business registration portal: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.lu)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing or operating through a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) incorporate and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a Luxembourg business number (Numéro d'identification), which also serves as the TIN for direct tax purposes; (2) file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 645) with AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, or through an appointed VAT representative if the entity has no establishment or fiscal agent in Luxembourg; (3) activate the registration for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax via the ACD; (4) provide notarized articles of association, proof of registered office address, appointment of managers, and AML/KYC documents for the ultimate beneficial owner. No separate tax registration is required beyond the consolidated business registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: Luxembourg-resident SARL is a fully taxable corporate entity, subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (Impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax. The SARL is not a pass-through vehicle; profits are taxed at entity level and distributions are subject to withholding tax. — Foreign entities establishing or operating through a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) incorporate and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) to obtain a Luxembourg business number (Numéro d'identification), which also serves as the TIN for direct tax purposes; (2) file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 645) with AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, or through an appointed VAT representative if the entity has no establishment or fiscal agent in Luxembourg; (3) activate the registration for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax via the ACD; (4) provide notarized articles of association, proof of registered office address, appointment of managers, and AML/KYC documents for the ultimate beneficial owner. No separate tax registration is required beyond the consolidated business registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Luxembourg-resident SARL is a fully taxable corporate entity, subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (Impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax. The SARL is not a pass-through vehicle; profits are taxed at entity level and distributions are subject to withholding tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available in Luxembourg; all SARLs are opaque and entity-level taxed. Statutory elections relevant to this structure include: (i) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law; (ii) fiscal unity tax consolidation under Article 164bis LIR for resident companies at least 95% owned; (iii) election for the notional interest deduction under Article 168bis LIR; (iv) alignment elections under the ATAD hybrid mismatch rules to neutralise double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes. These elections do not alter the entity's default non-transparent classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 per annum for taxpayers established in Luxembourg under the small-business exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); non-established foreign suppliers have a zero threshold and must register for VAT in Luxembourg as soon as they make any taxable supply (Article 48 VAT Law). An intra-EU distance-sale threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to cross-border online sales, but not to the trust administration services at issue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: a 0% VAT rate applies where the goods are physically exported from the EU to a non-EU destination, evidenced by customs export declaration, transport documents, and proof of departure before or on the 90th day following supply. For services: fiduciary trust administration services rendered by a Luxembourg SARL to a non-EU business customer fall under the place-of-supply rule (Article 58 VAT Directive), placing supply outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and resulting in an effective 0% charge without input VAT deduction. Where the customer is a private individual outside the EU, the services may still be zero-rated if they are ancillary to export/import of goods or if they fall under the B2C service list. In all cases, the exporter must hold contemporaneous commercial and accounting evidence demonstrating the non-EU status of the client and the actual cross-border performance, otherwise the standard 17% VAT will be assessed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Douane 2001) – required only for exports of physical goods; not applicable to pure service exports
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill – required only for goods exports as proof of exterior transit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT confirmation) – required to evidence receipt of foreign proceeds and movement of funds from outside the EU
  • Service contract and detailed invoice showing the non-EU beneficially entitled entity and the place of effective use and enjoyment
  • Tax residence certificate or VAT identification number of the non-EU client, plus evidence of the client's business activity

B2G invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL network (Law of 7 July 2019). B2B e-invoicing is not mandatory, but textual XML e-invoices are recognised if the recipient consents. There is no real-time fiscal clearance system, no requirement to obtain pre-approved digital stamps, and no continuous transaction control (CTC) upload obligation for standard commercial invoices in Luxembourg. Invoices must be archived in a tamper-evident inventory system to satisfy AEDT audit requirements.

Luxembourg's ACD and AEDT scrutinise zero-rating claims through mandatory document reviews and post-clearance audits. If a zero-rated treatment is disallowed, the tax authority will issue an assessment for the unpaid VAT plus late-payment interest at 0.8% per month (or part thereof) and a penalty of up to 50% of the evaded tax in cases of wilful misstatement. Erroneous filings may attract a 10%–20% administrative surcharge. In cases of fraudulent export zero-rating – including false customs declarations, forged bills of lading, or simulated service contracts – the Luxembourg Criminal Code and Tax Fraud Act (Loi du 23 mars 2016) impose criminal fines up to EUR 125,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, together with a retrospective clawback of any refund with no limitation period for civil restitution where the underlying intent is fraudulent. The standard limitation for recovery of VAT is 10 years from the end of the year in which the tax became chargeable; this period is suspended upon the opening of a judicial audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full free movement of capital under Articles 63-66 TFEU; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions with EU/EEA counterparts. No exchange control authorization is required for inbound or outbound payments, but transactions between residents and non-residents exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistics. Profit remittances to non-EU shareholders may be subject to Luxembourg withholding tax (generally 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate holders, or 5% under certain DTTs); funds may be repatriated freely in EUR or any other currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in Luxembourg. There is no obligation to repatriate receivables in EUR or to convert foreign currency balances; however, all cross-border receipts and payments relating to the SARL's fiduciary activity must be booked domestically in compliance with CSSF AML/CFT and accounting law, and must be declared to the BCL under the monthly/quarterly balance-of-payments reporting regime when the €12,500 threshold is exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) under the Eurosystem; CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier) supervises all credit institutions and licensed professionals of the financial sector, including professional trustees.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low for this high-risk special-license profile. Luxembourg-authorized EMIs (e.g., Mangopay S.A., PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie, S.C.A.) may provide payment initiation and e-money wallets, but most will reject a professional fiduciary trustee administering irrevocable offshore spendthrift trusts because such entities are classified as High_Risk_Special_License under CSSF Circular 20/744 and require a full FATF/AML framework, dedicated safeguarding accounts at a credit institution, and segregation of client funds. EMIs cannot substitute for the statutory segregated client-account structure and are not viable for escrow or settlement rails of the trust ledger.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unnamed discretionary beneficiaries; fallacy of 'beneficiary secrecy'

Root cause: Unnamed discretionary beneficiaries; fallacy of 'beneficiary secrecy'

Defense evidence: Complete trust deed including all schedules and the discretionary beneficiary class definition, a signed protector consent, a side letter memorialising the settlor's wishes (non-binding), a written statutory statement of the trustee's fiduciary duties under Luxembourg Law of 27 July 2003, and a beneficiary class register (or, where beneficiaries are unascertained, a formal trustee attestation and independent legal opinion confirming enforceability and compliance with the Law of 27 July 2003 and the 1985 Hague Trusts Convention).
Settlor-retained powers creating sham or USA-risk exposure

Root cause: Settlor-retained powers creating sham or USA-risk exposure

Defense evidence: Settlor power waiver/limitation deed, irrevocable and spendthrift clause extraction, independent trustee resolutions demonstrating the SARL's discretionary control, and a reputable U.S./international tax counsel opinion confirming non-sham status of the offshore irrevocable spendthrift trust; plus FATCA classification (W-8BEN-E / W-9 as applicable) and CRS registration certificates.
Source of wealth / source of funds opacity for trust corpus

Root cause: Source of wealth / source of funds opacity for trust corpus

Defense evidence: Audited bank statements for the last 24 months from the funding account, sale and conveyance documents proving the origin of wealth, tax returns or stamp duty receipts, signed wealth-origin questionnaire, and independent accountants' confirmation of the trust settlement route.
PEP status of settlor, protector, or beneficiaries

Root cause: PEP status of settlor, protector, or beneficiaries

Defense evidence: PEP self-certification forms for all relevant persons, mitigation justification from the bank's AML compliance, and source-of-funds evidence to the beyond-reasonable-doubt threshold; unacceptable if unexplained PEP-controlled political funds are the corpus.
Sanctions/Embargo exposure of the trust or its grantors

Root cause: Sanctions/Embargo exposure of the trust or its grantors

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening certificate from a professional screening database (World-Check or equivalent) covering UBOs, beneficiaries, protector, and all counterparties; legal entity ownership cascade; and compliance manual comprising a sanctions policy, refusal-of-unregulated-funds policy, and training records aligned with EU Regulations 765/2006 and 269/2014 and OFAC screening.
Regulatory licence verification failure (professional trustee)

Root cause: Regulatory licence verification failure (professional trustee)

Defense evidence: CSSF licence or passporting documentation issued under Article 29-1 of the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, proof of professional indemnity insurance, audited financial statements, and the latest CSSF return confirming compliance with prudential solvency and AML conditions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for share capital and trust corpus settle via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) in EUR using TARGET2/SEPA-instant clearing rails where possible. The routing protocol requires: (i) a dual-authority authenticated instruction from the SARL's manager and a co-signatory; (ii) a payment purpose code reflecting the settlement — 'CAPITAL INJECTION / SUBSCRIPTION OF SHARES' for the SARL's capital, and 'INITIAL TRUST SETTLEMENT / FIDUCIARY DEPOSIT' for the trust corpus; (iii) inclusion in Field 70 (Remittance Information) of the reference /BOP/ code and the CSSF transaction discrete-trust reference; and (iv) BCL cross-border balance-of-payments declaration within the statutory deadline for any inbound amount exceeding €12,500. Funds must be pre-cleared by the bank's sanctions engine; the bank may request a copy of the executed trust deed, the corporate authorisation, and the CSSF licence before credit is passed to the escrow ledger.Outbound settlements use the following clearing rails under the jurisdictional hierarchy: (1) SEPA EUR products (SCT, SCT Inst, SDD) for intra-EEA disbursements under €100,000; (2) SWIFT MT103 for global customer credit transfers, preferably with straight-through-processing and cover via MT202 for foreign-currency legs; (3) documentary trade finance rails — SWIFT MT700/MTA702 for standby/commercial letter of credit issuance against trustee disbursements, and collections via MT400/MT410; and (4) segregated high-value TARGET2 instructions for EUR payments above the RTGS threshold. Every outbound instruction must carry a specified disbursement purpose (e.g., 'BENEFICIARY DISTRIBUTION', 'INVESTMENT ASSET PURCHASE', 'ADMINISTRATION FEES'), the BCL balance-of-payments code, and an exact source-account designation referencing the segregated client money account rather than the firm's own house account, to preserve the trust's asset-partitioning under the Luxembourg Law of 27 July 2003 and CSSF Circular 20/744.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP forwards (vanilla forwards) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bilaterally confirmed FX deal ticket with a Luxembourg-authorized credit institutionFX swaps (foreign-exchange swap / roll-over) for same-day liquidity management between EUR and USD, GBP, CHF, or HKD, permitted without mandatory clearing but subject to BCL EMIR reporting when outstanding notional exceeds BCL/ESMA thresholdsCross-currency swaps (fixed-for-fixed or fixed-for-floating EUR/USD) for term-matched maturity of trust investment cash flows, cleared via LCH or CME where required under EMIR, or bilaterally with variation margin under the uncleared margin rules (BCBS-IOSCO/CSSF Circular)EUR/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) and non-deliverable options (NDOs) are permissible only for converting emerging-market currency exposures where the underlying currency is non-convertible or restricted; must be cash-settled in EUR/USD, be executed with an EU-authorized credit institution, and be reported to the BCL with full underlying trade evidenceFX options and swaptions on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP for asymmetric hedging of trustee obligations, stated at-the-money, with premiums paid in EUR and no speculative open position beyond the trust's committed distribution schedule

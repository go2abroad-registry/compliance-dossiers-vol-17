Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes; Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT; single digital portal: MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must execute a notarized deed of incorporation, file it with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and subsequently obtain a national tax identification number (Numéro d'identification) from the ACD. VAT registration is then required with the AEDT, supported by the RCS extract, statutes, identity documents of directors and shareholding declarations. Non-resident shareholders may be required to evidence their own tax identification and beneficial ownership; the SARL's registered office and local director appointments must be in place before TIN/VAT-ID issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include the election for VAT grouping (assujetti unique), the election for group taxation / fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for resident corporate taxpayers, and the application of participation exemption for qualifying shareholdings. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized under EU ATAD provisions transposed into Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for the small business exemption; however, non-established taxable persons supplying taxable goods or services in Luxembourg are required to register for VAT regardless of turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods supplied to non-EU (third-country) destinations qualify for 0% VAT where the exporter retains documentary evidence of dispatch or transport outside the EU, including a customs export declaration accepted by customs authorities. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated only if the customer is a VAT-registered business with a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another Member State. Services physically performed on goods destined for export and certain ancillary services may be zero-rated; franchise royalties and general management services to customers established abroad are subject to the place-of-supply rules and may be taxable where the customer resides.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ENS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Proof of Payment

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoices are legally recognized if authenticity, integrity and readability are ensured. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions through the PEPPOL network under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. National B2B e-invoicing obligations are not yet generally mandated, but Luxembourg is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, which will impose structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions in the coming years.

Fraudulent or insufficiently substantiated export zero-rating triggers a retrospective VAT assessment for the statutory limitation period (generally 5 years), plus late-payment interest. Administrative penalties can reach up to 100% of the underdeclared VAT, and serious fraud may result in criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment. The Luxembourg tax authorities may also recharacterize sham transactions and hold the VAT representative jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Both current and capital account payments are free under Article 63 TFEU; there are no exchange control permits or profit-remittance delays. Only statistical reporting (Balance of Payments) to the BCL and bank AML explanations are required, e.g., dividend, royalty, or loan-repayment remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion of export receivables or trade proceeds exists in Luxembourg. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained, transferred, or converted freely, subject only to standard bank compliance and BCL statistical classification.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the prudential and AML/CTF supervisor; BCL acts within the Eurosystem framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to Low as sole banking solution. CSSF-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) may provide EUR IBANs and cross-border payment services, but a Luxembourg SARL ordinarily requires a licensed commercial bank for notarised capital subscription blocking, corporate treasury, and tax-administration credibility. An EMI is viable as a payment-completion or settlement-support rail for point-of-sale or small royalty flows, not as the primary treasury bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity and shareholder-loan injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the Chinese parent bank to the SARL's blocked or operational EUR account in Luxembourg. The wire message must contain the BCL Balance-of-Payments purpose code 'S302' (direct investment – equity) or 'S320' (intercompany loan) in the remittance field /70 (or the equivalent BCL statistical reporting annex). For pre-registration capital, the beneficiary is the notary's Luxembourg blocked escrow account or the bank's blocked capital account; remittance details must state 'Capital subscription – SARL incorporation' and the bank certificate is used to obtain the RCS registration. The bank will verify source of funds and the UBO, and may request a copy of the draft articles of association and the notary's instructions before crediting the blocked account.Royalty, distribution, and operating payments are cleared via SEPA credit transfer for EUR-denominated outflows, or via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border, non-EUR payments (especially CNY/USD). For import sourcing of equipment and ingredients, commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are issued through acceptance at a Luxembourg bank, with MT700 for issuance and MT103/MT202 after document compliance. Payments to China must include the SAFE-mandated Chinese purpose code for royalty or trade and supporting contract registration documents.

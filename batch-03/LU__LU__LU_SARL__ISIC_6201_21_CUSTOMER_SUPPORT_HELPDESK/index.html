Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of Luxembourg, operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the supervision of the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated (Title V, Chapter 2, Articles 710-1 to 710-27, Société à Responsabilité Limitée; Article 710-2 on capital; Article 710-3 on the constitutive deed; Article 711-1 on the registered office); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Publicity of Annual Accounts; Law of 2 September 2011 on the establishment permit, Article 7-2 (exemption of information-technology services); Law of 21 March 2012 on waste management.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Articles of Association), signed before a Luxembourg notary or executed by private deed or electronic standardized instrument under the Law of 10 August 1915, accompanied by a declaration of conformity, filed on the RCS e-file platform and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 2 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 1 for a modernized SARL; capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash into a blocked escrow account opened in the future company's name with a Luxembourg credit institution; funds are released only upon RCS registration. If capital is below EUR 12,500, enhanced safeguards apply (in-kind contribution audit by a réviseur d'entreprises and a balance-sheet test before distributions and share buy-backs). Recommended standard capital: EUR 12,500.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for indirect taxes; central portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu; company register: Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SARL must file the incorporation deed through a Luxembourg notary, obtain the RCS registration number, and be assigned the national identification number (matricule) which serves as the general TIN for direct tax purposes. A Luxembourg VAT number (N° TVA) must then be applied for with the AED through MyGuichet.lu. Non-resident entities without a Luxembourg establishment generally do not register for Luxembourg VAT if B2B supplies are reverse-charged to the customer or if B2C e-services are declared under the EU OSS; direct VAT registration is required only when the supply is treated as taxable in Luxembourg and no other simplification applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an SARL must file the incorporation deed through a Luxembourg notary, obtain the RCS registration number, and be assigned the national identification number (matricule) which serves as the general TIN for direct tax purposes. A Luxembourg VAT number (N° TVA) must then be applied for with the AED through MyGuichet.lu. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; an SARL is automatically an opaque resident company subject to corporate income tax. Statutory elections include VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Luxembourg VAT law, fiscal integration for corporate income tax for qualified groups, and waiver of the small-undertaking VAT exemption. Hybrid mismatch treatment is not elective; EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-undertaking VAT exemption; no general VAT registration threshold applies to established taxable persons. Non-resident suppliers of B2C e-services may use the EU OSS instead of local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies where goods leave the EU under customs supervision and the supplier holds transport and export evidence; the export declaration and commercial documents must prove physical removal. For services, including helpdesk/SaaS supplies, B2B services to an overseas taxable person are outside Luxembourg VAT under place-of-supply rules and are generally reverse-charged; B2C e-services to non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT, while B2C e-services to EU consumers are taxable at destination and may be reported via OSS. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is used only as a commercial documentation benchmark.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic via LU-CUS / EU AES)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent payment evidence

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API-based reporting, or digital stamping. B2B e-invoicing is permitted when authenticity and integrity are ensured. E-invoicing is mandatory for invoices to Luxembourg central government entities using the PEPPOL-compatible EU standard.

The AED may claw back VAT if export proof is not substantiated within the assessment window, with interest running from the original VAT due date. Fraudulent zero-rating or false documentation is treated as VAT fraud, leading to reassessment, administrative fines, criminal penalties, and extended limitation periods of up to 10 years in bad-faith cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current-account (subscription and helpdesk service revenue) and capital-account (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend and profit remittance) transactions. No surrender, transfer, or notification requirements apply. Profit remittance hurdles are non-monetary: the SARL must satisfy the corporate-law balance-sheet and liquidity tests before distributions, and the domestic 15% dividend withholding tax (reduced to 0%-15% under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive) may apply. Foreign-currency (USD, GBP) accounts are freely permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation on export proceeds from SaaS/helpdesk services. USD receipts collected through Stripe Billing and PayPal may be retained in a multi-currency account or converted at market rate at the discretion of the SARL's treasury policy.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) within the Eurosystem (ECB); financial-sector supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg abolished exchange controls upon implementation of Directive 88/361/EEC and Article 63 TFEU; no ministry or central-bank prior authorization exists for cross-border payments or FX conversion.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. Luxembourg provides a modern PSD2 framework (CSSF-licensed payment and e-money institutions passported across the EEA), which suits payouts-only treasury functions. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot issue the capital-escrow attestation (attestation de blocage) required by Luxembourg notaries; they may not extend credit or hold idle commercial liquidity beyond payment-facilitation limits. Because the target rails are Stripe Billing (Stripe Payments Europe Ltd, Ireland) and PayPal (PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie, S.C.A., a Luxembourg-licensed credit institution), the SARL should maintain a credit-institution account and use EMIs solely as an add-on acquiring and settlement layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share subscriptions are wired by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to a blocked escrow (compte séquestre) at the Luxembourg bank using purpose code DE/EQTY (direct-investment equity) with reference 'capital subscription LU_SARL'; the bank issues the attestation de blocage to the notary before incorporation. Post-incorporation capital increases require a notarized shareholders' resolution and transfer via TARGET2/SEPA on a dedicated equity IBAN; USD injections are converted at the ECB reference rate plus margin or ring-fenced as a USD sub-account equity tranche. Verification: proof of source of funds (dividend minutes, disposal agreements), CSSF-preserved account statements, and publication of the capital increase with the RCS.EU/SEPA rails: SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for payroll, CCSS social-security contributions, and EU cloud/SaaS vendors (Microsoft, AWS-EU) via EBICS/API. Non-EU rails: SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP service invoices and subcontractor settlements (India, Philippines), transmitted with ISO 20022 pain.001 and structured remittance fields (INV./PO./tax-ref); daily Stripe/PayPal payouts are swept to the EUR operational account while USD payouts remain in a USD sub-account pending FX decision. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File are required where the Luxembourg SARL belongs to a multinational group with consolidated annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million and the SARL itself has net turnover above EUR 5 million or enters into cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per annum. In all other cases, a contemporaneous arm’s-length benchmarking study should nevertheless be prepared and made available to the ACD within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the Gulf Cooperation Council type. However, the ACD enforces substance-over-form through the national anti-abuse rule (Article 6 ATA), the EU ATAD interest limitation and CFC rules, and the OECD BEPS minimum standards. The benefit of tax treaties, the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, the Interest & Royalties Directive, and the participation exemption are denied where the Luxembourg SARL is a pure letterbox company without employees, premises, business activities, and effective management in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate underreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Luxembourg tax law (Article 396 LIR): imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 125,000, increased to five times that amount for a corporate entity; in addition, the tax administration reopens the fiscal year, imposes a 100% late-filing addition in aggravated cases, and levies monthly late-payment interest of 0.8% on the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and statutory managers are personally and jointly liable for the corporate income tax, withholding tax, and VAT debts if the evasion is attributable to their intentional fault. Courts can order travel bans, seizure of personal bank accounts, disqualification from board service, and imprisonment in connection with the underlying tax fraud.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance / incomplete documentation

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax authorities re-determine the taxable income under the arm’s-length principle, impose a corporate income tax increase and municipal business tax recalculated at 24.94%, add a penalty equal to the full tax reassessed when the failure is persistent, and apply 0.8% monthly interest. In egregious intra-group financing cases, thin-capitalisation and ATAD interest limitation rules deny a deduction and reclassify distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign an intercompany service agreement without a functional and economic analysis, or who ignore a documented tax-advice warning, may be held personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and penalties; under Luxembourg insolvency law, they may also face court-imposed personal contribution orders and a professional ban for mismanagement.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, denial of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, and recapture of 15% withholding tax on gross distributions; the tax administration re-assesses the entire profit under the substance-over-form doctrine, adds a 10% tax increase, and in cross-border situations may report the arrangement as a sham using the EU joint transfer-pricing forum and OECD anti-BEPS toolkits.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and effective managers incur personal liability under Luxembourg company law (law of 10 August 1915) for losses caused by maintaining a letterbox structure; civil restitution orders, personal fines up to EUR 125,000, and mandatory travel bans are available; in insolvency proceedings, the manager’s personal assets may be used to satisfy corporate tax liabilities.
AML / KYC and UBO disclosure failure

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of the annual turnover of the SARL; the tax administration can invalidate the tax return and re-open the statute of limitations under fraud time limits (10 years); proceedings under the Luxembourg anti-money laundering law can trigger fines equivalent to the amount laundered and imprisonment of up to five years for responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal punishment for AML breaches, including imprisonment of eight days to five years and criminal fines; they may also be prohibited indefinitely from serving as board members of any Luxembourg company, while seizure of personal assets and EU-wide travel bans can be issued in the context of a money-laundering investigation.

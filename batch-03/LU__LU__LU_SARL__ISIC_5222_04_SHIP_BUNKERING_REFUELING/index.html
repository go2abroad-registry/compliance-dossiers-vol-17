Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); notarial e-filing through the eRCS online portal.

Governing statute

Consolidated Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Part III on the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), Articles 100-1 et seq.; in particular the provisions on minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, paid-up contribution rules, registered office, and appointment of managers (gérants); together with the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and annual accounts.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarised deed of incorporation) together with the statuts coordonnés (coordinated articles of association), filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés (RES).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL requires a minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed at incorporation. At least 25% of the par value of each cash-funded share must be paid up at subscription, collected in the notary's blocked client account and released after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid at incorporation and supported by the statutory auditor's report; no separate escrow bank account is statutorily required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; primary digital portal: MyGuichet.lu and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first complete commercial registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). Following RCS clearance, the SARL must apply to the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for Luxembourg VAT identification, and to the Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate tax file opening and income tax identification. A local tax representative is ordinarily required for non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Luxembourg. The application requires the global ultimate beneficial owner details, RCS extraction, constitutive deed of the SARL, and evidence of business activity (including the statutory license for marine fuel bunkering issued under the Luxembourg maritime / logistics licensing regime). The VAT number and national TIN are issued in official communication from AEDT and ACD, respectively. Registration must be completed before commencing taxable supplies; a registration application can be made by mail, via a Luxembourg-based professional (titre professionnel or certified accountant), or via a notarial filing where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Luxembourg SARL is resident and fiscally opaque, subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on its worldwide income, subject to territoriality sourced-income modifications, tax treaties, and participation exemption. The SARL is not a pass-through entity; shareholders are taxed only upon distributions and deemed or actual capital gains in their own jurisdiction, subject to tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box classification regime. Available elective measures include (i) application for a Luxembourg VAT grouping regime (Groupe TVA) enabling affiliated taxable persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (ii) election for Luxembourg fiscal unity (consolidated corporate taxation) among directly or indirectly qualifying resident companies, including a SARL, subject to at least 95% joint control; (iii) the special alternative investment vehicle regimes and securitization regime elections; (iv) application for a bilateral APA and/or unilateral advance VAT clearances; and (v) anti-hybrid election mechanisms contained in Luxembourg's implementation of EU ATAD/ATAD II to reverse mismatches arising from hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities, double inclusion income, and deductive/non-deductive arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for taxable persons established in Luxembourg to benefit from the SME VAT exemption; non-established taxable persons and providers of strictly taxable supplies are required to register irrespective of turnover; distance sales of goods into Luxembourg exceed the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold to trigger Luxembourg VAT registration and compliance. For ISIC 5222 ship bunkering and refueling to international vessels, zero-rated supplies generally arise without reference to the domestic registration threshold, and the taxable person must hold a valid Luxembourg VAT number to issue compliant invoices and to support export and non-EU export claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods and services qualifying as exports outside the European Union and, specifically, supplies of marine fuel (bunkers), lubricants, and ship provisions for vessels used in international navigation on the high seas are zero-rated under Luxembourg VAT law transposing EU VAT Directive Article 148 and implementing Article 66 of the Luxembourg VAT law. Eligibility requires the SARL to prove both the physical export from the EU customs territory and the economic nexus of the supply to the operation of an oceangoing vessel engaged in international trade. For ISIC 5222 marine bunkering, qualifying fuels/greases placed onboard a vessel undertaking a voyage to a non-EU port, an activity in international waters, or otherwise performing international transport of goods or persons, and which are delivered under a verifiable bunker delivery receipt, will be invoiced at 0% VAT. Exports of goods to non-EU jurisdictions require the vendor to obtain and keep positive proof of export including customs export declarations and transport documents. Offshore services performed from Luxembourg to owners/operators of non-EU vessels can also be zero-rated where the direct connection to the qualifying vessel and its international activity is evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document and/or electronic ECS/E-export notification) naming the SARL as exporter located in Luxembourg unless a deemed seller / triangulation arrangement applies
  • Bill of Lading or Seaway Bill or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing export out of the EU customs territory
  • Bunker Delivery Receipt (BDN) and physical quantity surveyor report demonstrating marine-fuel quantity supplied to the identified ocean-going vessel
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation showing settlement in the agreed payment rail (Swift Wire or Standby LC) from the foreign counterparty
  • Vessel particulars, IMO number, flag state certificate, voyage plan, and port clearance demonstrating the international voyage to/from a non-EU destination and absence of cabotage-only activity
  • Commercial invoice and signed delivery note cross-referenced to the BDN and vessel name
  • Contract of affreightment, charter party, or purchase order stating ISIC 5222 bunkering scope and international route
  • Intrastat declaration waiver or statement proving the goods have physically left the EU if routed through another Member State

Luxembourg mandates the use of structured e-invoicing for all B2G transactions routed through the PEPPOL network under the EU Directive 2014/55/EU; the Public Sector Invoice Management platform (eGP) is the receiving system. There is currently no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for B2B invoices in Luxembourg. Taxpayers must preserve integrity of content, legibility, and audit trail; invoicing via EDI or EU-compliant structured PDF is permissible. For bunkering operations, the supply to a vessel must be evidenced by a bunker delivery note / delivraison de soutes report, which is not an e-invoice substitute but is a mandatory fiscal and customs substantiation document. Electronic reporting of the VAT return is performed through the AEDT MyGuichet portal; Intrastat and profiling data are filed separately where required.

The Luxembourg AEDT, together with the Customs and Excise Administration, may retrospectively deny zero-rating and disallow input VAT deductions where export documentation is incomplete, fictitious, or fails to unambiguously establish physical removal from the EU and settlement from the foreign buyer. In the absence of robust BDN, customs, and FX repatriation evidence, the SARL is retroactively assessed at the Luxembourg standard VAT rate of 17% together with statutory late-interest charges and administrative penalties. In cases of proven fraud, criminal sanctions under the amended Luxembourg General Tax Law and VAT law can include imprisonment, public criminal fines, confiscation of assets, and permanent VAT registration derecognition. The ordinary reassessment period is three years from the end of the relevant VAT period, but in cases of bad faith or fraudulent conduct the clawback period may extend to ten years. The same penalties apply to any artificial arrangement, sham entity, or triangular scheme that seeks to zero-rate bunkers supplied to a vessel engaged exclusively in domestic or cabotage traffic rather than in genuine international trade.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% (fiscal year 2024 onward); aggregate effective mainstream rate in Luxembourg City is approximately 24.94%, comprising corporate income tax (CIT), a 4% solidarity surcharge on CIT, a 6% unemployment fund contribution on CIT, and deductible municipal business tax (IBT) at 6.75% (3% base rate × 225% multiplier).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders, levied under Article 148 LIR; no solidarity surcharge is added to the 15% withholding. No WHT applies if the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption is satisfied (qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder, minimum 10% holding, minimum 12-month holding at payment date).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutorily anchored deadline: 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end (e.g., FY2024 return due 31 December 2025). Automatic extension to mid-subsequent year is granted for returns prepared and transmitted by a registered Luxembourg tax adviser; further prolongations de délai are available on reasoned request to the Administration des contributions directes (ACD).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A – Identification / Matricule SARL legal name, registered office, RCS number, Luxembourg matricule d'impôt, RBE beneficial-owner reference, and precise fiscal year start/end dates. Non-resident officers must be identified by name and country of residence; the SARL must disclose its permanent establishment in Luxembourg and the foreign tax residence of each beneficial owner for GAAR/directive screening. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Section B – Résultat comptable (Box 100–115) Accounting net profit/loss before tax per the audited annual accounts, including bunkering turnover, cost of fuel sales, vessel-supply logistics costs, and financial income. Must reconcile with the filed statutory financial statements of the Luxembourg SARL; foreign head-office allocations are inadmissible, and all revenue derived from Luxembourg operational decisions is attributable to the Luxembourg permanent establishment/entity. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Section C – Réintégrations (Box 120) Non-deductible expenses and tax add-backs: disallowed entertainment costs, excessive director remuneration, non-deductible interest (earnings stripping), and penalties. Include intra-group management fees paid to a foreign affiliate without substance or adequate documentation; such fees are typically rejected in a non-resident-owner structure. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Section D – Déductions (Box 130) Deductions from the accounting result: carry-forward tax losses, municipal business tax (IBT) paid, participation-exempt income (dividends/capital gains) and IP-Box deductions. Participation exemption requires a qualifying 10% shareholding (or €1.2 million cost) held for at least 12 months; a non-resident parent must evidence the holding period and economic substance of the Luxembourg entity to retain the deduction. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Box 140 – Revenu imposable (CIT/NWT base) Taxable income base after adjustments; represents the consolidated base for CIT, net wealth tax (NWT) and municipal business tax (IBT). Bunkering margins realized through the Luxembourg entity are fully taxable in Luxembourg; treaty-dependent profit allocation is irrelevant where the SARL is the resident principal. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
IBT / NNA Schedule (Supplementary boxes) Separate computation column for municipal business tax applying the communal multiplier (e.g., 225% for Luxembourg City) on IBT taxable base. A foreign-owned SARL remains subject to IBT solely by virtue of registered office in the commune; no relief is granted for non-resident shareholders. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Section E – Impôt sur le revenu des collectivités (Box 150–155) CIT computation at 17%, plus 4% solidarity surcharge and 6% unemployment contribution, after applicable tax credits. Unilateral foreign tax credits are rarely relevant; treaty credits apply if the foreign state taxed the same bunkering income inconsistently with Luxembourg residency rules. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. Non-filing can additionally be prosecuted as fiscal negligence with fines of €25 to €250 per missing declaration and the revocation of the tax-adviser-filed deadline.
Section F – Impôt net à payer (Box 170-180) Final CIT, IBT and NWT liability after preliminary payments, payable by bank transfer to the ACD's treasury account. Advance payments made by the non-resident parent on behalf of the SARL must be formally recharacterized as shareholder loans or equity injections; unpaid declarations trigger ACD garnishment of the SARL's bank accounts. Late filing exposes the SARL to taxation d'office on the inspector's own estimate, a late-payment interest of 0.6% per month on all unpaid CIT/IBT/NWT, and an administrative increase of up to 10% of the assessed tax under Article 176 LIR. A certificate of tax residence of the ultimate parent and proof of beneficial ownership must be attached; failure renders the combined filing materially incomplete. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 (cross-border related-party transactions exceeding this per fiscal year trigger the Local File obligation, provided the multinational group has consolidated turnover above EUR 50,000,000). The Master File must be maintained where the group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 regardless of the local transaction volume; documentation must be delivered to the ACD within 15 days of an official demand (30 days with justified cause). Panama-style minimal margin resellers and back-to-back bunker supply arrangements are specific audit targets.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf-Council sense, but substance is legally decisive through (i) the beneficial-ownership doctrine applied under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and DTAs, (ii) the EU ATAD GAAR (Article 6, in force since 1 January 2019), and (iii) settled Luxembourg case law denying treaty/directive benefits to letterbox entities. A bunkering SARL is expected to demonstrate real local direction: locally resident directors approving procurement/pricing, physical premises, qualified staff, local bank accounts, genuine risk-bearing, and commercial records evidencing actual bunker-supply decision-making. Absence of such substance invites reclassification of domicile, denial of the participation exemption, and re-source of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on the ACD's ex-officio basis; 10% administrative increase on the understated tax; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; criminal prosecution under Articles 227–229 LIR with fines up to ten times the defrauded tax and imprisonment of one month to five years for the responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors personally subject to criminal fines and imprisonment; civil liability for the full tax debt in case of fraudulent concealment; seizure of personal assets, professional disqualification, judicial travel bans, and in cross-border cases coordination with foreign tax authorities through administrative assistance.
Shell / Conduit Abuse (Letterbox SARL)

Statutory sanction: Denial of the participation exemption and DTA/directive benefits; retroactive reclassification of foreign-managed income as taxable Luxembourg profit; 15% withholding tax (plus 0.6% monthly late interest) on deemed distributions; pecuniary penalties up to 10% of the reassessed tax; potential inclusion of the entity on the Luxembourg public list of abusive tax arrangements under DAC.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners may be held jointly liable for unpaid corporate taxes under veil-piercing jurisprudence and the Luxembourg criminal rules on misuse of corporate assets; travel bans and freezing orders are routinely issued in fraud-linked offshore conduit cases.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 (AML/CFT), as amended: administrative fines for legal persons up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; imprisonment of up to five years for implicated individuals; closure of bank accounts and revocation of the economic operator licence; the SARL's maritime-fuel licence can be suspended by the competent ministry.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution, confiscation orders for laundered assets, travel bans, and exclusion from serving as managers of any regulated or licensed entity; civil liability extends to losses sustained by other creditors where the AML failure enabled dissipation of company assets.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ACD upward adjustment of taxable profit under Article 56 LIR; additional municipal business tax and CIT; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; 10% surcharge on the reassessed tax; in egregious cases, criminal prosecution for false bookkeeping with fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed inaccurate TP documentation or intercompany pricing undertakings can be personally assessed for the additional tax if the SARL is insolvent; reckless or intentional mispricing supports civil fraud claims, director disqualification, and travel bans during cross-border tax investigation.

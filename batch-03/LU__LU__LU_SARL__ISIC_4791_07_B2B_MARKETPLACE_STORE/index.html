Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 for an S.à r.l., fully subscribed and fully paid up upon incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account in the name of the company in formation (or notary escrow) and remain blocked until RCS registration evidence is provided. In-kind contributions require compliance with asset valuation rules.

Funding & unlock path: For the physical registered office, the company must obtain an occupancy or building-safety confirmation from the competent municipal commune, including compliance with public fire-safety and structural load standards. Workplace clearance requires registration with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) and adherence to the Law of 17 June 1994 on occupational safety and health.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is EUR 12,000 for an S.à r.l., fully subscribed and fully paid up upon incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account in the name of the company in formation (or notary escrow) and remain blocked until RCS registration evidence is provided. In-kind contributions require compliance with asset valuation rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. There is no foreign exchange control legislation, no prior central bank authorisation for foreign exchange transactions, and no restriction on the free movement of capital and payments between EU member states and third countries, subject only to EU sanctions, anti-money laundering rules, and statistical reporting obligations. Profit remittances are unrestricted from an exchange control perspective; however, any dividend distribution must satisfy Luxembourg corporate law solvency tests and may be subject to domestic dividend withholding tax unless exempted under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export proceeds surrender rules in Luxembourg. Exporters are not required to convert, repatriate, or sell foreign currency earnings. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, settled in EUR, or used for commercial hedging. The only obligation is the bank's and direct respondent's duty to report cross-border transactions above the applicable BCL threshold (generally EUR 12,500 per transaction) for balance of payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting together with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium-to-High only if the EMI is licensed in Luxembourg or validly passporting under PSD2 within the EEA. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA cannot provide payment services to a Luxembourg SARL without a local establishment or a licensed Luxembourg agent. Using an offshore or non-regulated EMI as the primary payment rail is legally incompatible with the CSSF framework and will create substantive AML/KYC blockers at any tier-1 Luxembourg bank. Recommended practice: route platform payouts through a CSSF-regulated payment institution or an EEA-passported EMI, while maintaining a direct SWIFT/Mainland account at a Luxembourg credit institution for core treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders' equity contributions must be credited in EUR to the SARL's dedicated capital contribution escrow account at a Luxembourg bank. The credit transfer shall be initiated by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account, with the reference text stating 'SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE / RESERVE / APIC' and identifying the contributing shareholder. For cross-border operations equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, the BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation arises and is generally discharged by the receiving bank on behalf of the client. The bank will verify the notarised share subscription document, the board resolution authorising the capital increase, the RCS filing confirmation and the investment/service agreement. After the funds are credited and compliance checks are completed, the capital increase is notarised and filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register.Clearing rails for outbound settlement rely primarily on SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to overseas trade suppliers, using TARGET2 for intra-EUR transactions and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for urgent low-value settlements. For larger cross-border purchases requiring documentary security, an irrevocable at-sight commercial letter of credit issued by a Luxembourg bank and advised through a correspondent bank is the appropriate instrument. Standby letters of credit may be used as performance guarantees. Non-EUR and EUR treasury payments will be executed through the bank's nostro accounts; every outgoing transaction must be backed by an underlying trade document in the bank's client file. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% corporate income tax (IRC) + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19% effective national rate; aggregate with Luxembourg City municipal business tax (6.75%) = 24.94% (commune-dependent)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory; fully eliminated (0%) for qualifying EU parent-subsidiary / participation-exemption distributions (≥10% or acquisition value ≥€1.2M held ≥12 months, substance and anti-abuse satisfied); treaty rates typically 5% for ≥10% investors and 10–15% for portfolio holdings

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue (mandatory CbCR); no fixed domestic statutory threshold for Master/Local File, but ACD may order full TP documentation for any intra-group flow within 15 days, and practical safe compliance starts at EUR 1,000,000 intercompany turnover

Economic substance scope: No standalone Luxembourg ESR law equivalent to the UAE's Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced indirectly through: Article 6 LIR abuse-of-law doctrine, ATAD GAAR, participation-exemption abuse clause, DTA PPT / beneficial-ownership rules, and ACD administrative practice requiring local registered office, local management decisions, employees, own accounting records and local bank accounts for treaty/WHT relief claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent tax filing

Statutory sanction: Restitution of evaded CIT plus 10% late-payment surcharge and 9% p.a. default interest; criminal fine between EUR 5,000 and EUR 50,000 (doubled on relapse within 10 years) and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years

Director personal liability: The gérant or de facto director is criminally liable as principal; civil recovery via 'action en comblement du passif' against insolvent mismanagement; courts may impose passport seizure, travel ban (interdiction de sortie du territoire) and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies
Shell company / substance abuse and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of participation exemption and treaty relief; recharacterization of distributions as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT plus interest; retroactive reassessment with 10% surcharge; publication of the decision and potential CFC/inclusion adjustment abroad

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations or beneficiary-owner forms face abuse-of-law reassessment exposures, civil liability to the shareholders, and criminal charges for false certification where intent is proven
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to the arm's-length profit plus correlated secondary adjustments, default interest at 9% p.a. and a 10% surcharge on the deficiency; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 for failure to produce documentation, with daily astreinte for continuing non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors can be ordered to complete the company's loss (comblement du passif) where TP adjustments render the company insolvent; continued non-cooperation subjects management to fines and, in flagrant cases, criminal tax evasion prosecution
AML / DAC7 platform reporting and payment-rail abuse

Statutory sanction: DAC7 non-reporting penalties up to EUR 20,000 per annual cycle; AML administrative fines for legal persons up to EUR 1,250,000 or three times the illicit benefit; criminal money-laundering exposure up to 5 years' imprisonment for the responsible officers

Director personal liability: Luxembourg AML law imposes direct duties on the gérant; failure to file suspicious-transaction reports or to maintain UBO registers exposes the director to personal fines, travel bans, and disqualification; civil compensation to third parties is available under Article 1382 of the Civil Code

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for a SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation; the funds must be held in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the name of the company 'en formation' and are released only upon issuance of the RCS registration certificate; no separate state escrow account is required. For the equity crowdfunding licence, the CSSF prudential own-funds requirement is at least EUR 25,000 or one tenth of fixed overheads (or 25% of forecast fixed overheads for the year following authorisation), whichever is higher.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety authorisation delivered by the communal fire prevention service (Service de prévention incendie) covering evacuation plans, smoke detection, fire extinguishers and emergency lighting; structural load verification by a certified engineer for the server room and archive areas; occupational safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering conformity of electrical installations, safe workstations, first-aid equipment and management of psychosocial risks; certificate of maximum occupation density and accessibility for the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for a SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash or in kind at incorporation; the funds must be held in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the name of the company 'en formation' and are released only upon issuance of the RCS registration certificate; no separate state escrow account is required. For the equity crowdfunding licence, the CSSF prudential own-funds requirement is at least EUR 25,000 or one tenth of fixed overheads (or 25% of forecast fixed overheads for the year following authorisation), whichever is higher.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Administration des douanes et accises for customs; unified taxpayer portal: MyGuichet.lu / espace securise (guichet.lu) operated with the Government IT Center (CTIE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The SARL is incorporated by a Luxembourg notary and filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), which issues the company's RCS registration number. Step 2: The ACD automatically allocates the corporate taxpayer identification number (matricule d'identification) upon extraction from the RCS file; non-resident entities without an RCS file must apply by filing the first corporate income tax and net wealth tax return. Step 3: VAT registration is submitted electronically on MyGuichet.lu using the matricule; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) within approximately two weeks. A non-resident crowdfunding portal supplying taxable services in Luxembourg must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative and file via the eCDF module. Step 4: The CSSF crowdfunding licence under the Law of 16 July 2019 and Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (ECSPR) must be granted before client onboarding; the regulatory licence creates an unconditional VAT registration obligation regardless of the EUR 30,000 small-business franchise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Luxembourg SARL (Societe a Responsabilite Limitee) is an opaque corporate person fully tax resident in Luxembourg where its registered office or place of effective management is in Luxembourg; it is subject to entity-level corporate income tax (IRC) at 17%, a 7% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 18.19%), municipal business tax (impot commercial communal, e.g., 6.75% in Luxembourg City, making an aggregate headline rate of approximately 24.94%), and net wealth tax (0.5% on taxable net wealth); no territorial scope limitation, no pass-through election exists; the entity is not situated in a tax-exempt zone; the equity-crowdfunding activity is a licensed financial-sector activity subject to CSSF supervision.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections include: (i) fiscal unity (integration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR, available to a resident parent holding at least 95% direct or indirect ownership in resident subsidiaries for a five-year commitment period; (ii) participation exemption election for qualifying shareholdings (minimum 10% capital participation or an acquisition cost of at least EUR 1.2 million) held for at least 12 months, exempting dividends and capital gains under Articles 147, 148 and 166 LIR; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections and counter-measures under ATAD rules implemented in Articles 168quater to 168septies LIR; (iv) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) election under Article 21 of the VAT Law for closely-linked financial entities; (v) no election permits pass-through treatment for the SARL because the CSSF licence requires a regulated, taxable legal vehicle; (vi) optional application of notional interest deduction under Article 168bis LIR in the ordinary CIT return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 on annual taxable turnover for the small-business exemption scheme (Article 177 of the VAT Law). The threshold is unavailable to a CSSF-licenced crowdfunding service provider because licensed financial activities are mandatorily registrable irrespective of turnover; a non-established foreign portal supplying Luxembourg B2B services must register under the non-established taxable person / EU One Stop Shop (OSS) procedure and cannot invoke the EUR 30,000 franchise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies when goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier to a non-EU destination, evidenced by EU customs export clearance; intra-EU B2B supplies are zero-rated when the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another Member State. Services: B2B platform, escrow, custody, administrative and due-diligence services supplied by the portal to a business customer established outside Luxembourg fall outside the Luxembourg place-of-supply under the general B2B rule and are reportable as zero-rated/outside scope; B2C remote digital intermediary services supplied to EU consumers are taxable via the OSS scheme in the consumer's Member State; services effectively rendered offshore and invoiced to non-EU business counterparties evidenced by a valid non-EU VAT/tax registration certificate qualify for 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document) electronically lodged through the Luxembourg AES/ECS export system
  • Bill of Lading (maritime), Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from Luxembourg/EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU counterparty
  • Commercial export invoice with Incoterms and declared customs value
  • Proof of export status: customs office exit stamp or electronic ARN/EX number from the AES/ECS export clearance record

No real-time national fiscal clearance or mandatory continuous transaction-control mandate (unlike the Italian SDI model). Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies to Luxembourg public-sector authorities through the PEPPOL network in PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / EN 16931 format; private B2B e-invoicing is permitted in any EU-standard XML or PDF format. All VAT returns (Model 1026 TVA), recapitulative EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via the AEDT's eCDF central filing module on MyGuichet.lu; invoices must display the LU VAT number of the supplier and the customer; the crowdfunding portal's escrow, ACH and bank-wire payment rails must preserve an immutable documentary audit trail for AEDT inspection for at least 10 years.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating is sanctioned under Articles 427-433 of the General Tax Law and Article 59 of the VAT Law. The AEDT may reassess uncollected VAT at 17% on the full consideration for up to 5 years (10 years in cases of wilful tax fraud), impose default interest at 0.6% per month, and levy administrative fines of 10%-25% of the evaded tax, rising to 100% for deliberate concealment. Criminal tax fraud is punishable with imprisonment of one to five years and fines up to EUR 150,000; the VAT representative and managing officers incur joint and several liability. For regulated crowdfunding portals, cross-border export or disbursement anomalies are flagged through the Eurofisc network, with retroactive clawback of refunded export VAT and suspension of zero-rating privileges for at least 24 months.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the euro as sole legal tender and operates a fully liberalised current and capital account regime under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no exchange-control statute, no central bank approval requirement for inward or outward capital movements, and no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility in domestic law. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the FX level; the only material hurdle is the Luxembourg withholding tax of 15% on dividends distributed by an SARL, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under Directive 2011/96/EU and further relieved under applicable double tax treaties. Distributions are legally blocked where they would cause the company's net assets to fall below the called-up share capital plus the non-distributable statutory reserve, which is set at 5% of annual net profits until reaching 5% of subscribed share capital. For the equity crowdfunding portal, investor funds are not corporate assets: the platform must hold all client monies in segregated escrow compartments at a credit institution, ring-fenced from the platform's insolvency estate, and such funds may only be released to the relevant issuer or refunded to investors in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 and the Luxembourg law of 18 July 2016 on crowdfunding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender obligation exists in Luxembourg or elsewhere in the euro area. The statutory conversion ratio for export receipts is zero percent and no mandatory timeline applies; a Luxembourg resident company may lawfully retain foreign currency proceeds in any currency and jurisdiction, subject only to general anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and transfer-pricing rules. The only administrative requirement is indirect: resident credit institutions and, where directly relevant, resident enterprises must report cross-border flows to the BCL under EU Regulation 184/2005 and BCL implementing instructions, using the applicable balance-of-payments purpose codes for direct investment, loans, and other services.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and responsible for Luxembourg's balance-of-payments statistics, TARGET2 participation, currency circulation, and oversight of payment systems. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent financial-sector supervisor for the licensed equity crowdfunding service provider, including AML/CFT supervision, licensing, and prudential controls over banking and payment operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High, subject to strict structural limits. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution may be onboarded under the PSD2 passport into Luxembourg as a technical payment gateway for collecting and disbursing investor funds, provided the EMI has completed the CSSF notification procedure and holds adequate safeguarding arrangements. Regulation (EU) 2020/1503, Article 10, however, requires the crowdfunding service provider itself to ensure that client funds are protected in the event of the platform's insolvency, which in practice compels holding the pooled investor monies at a supervised credit institution or at an EMI that holds such funds in a segregated account. Luxembourg banks will typically permit the EMI as an execution-only routing layer but will require the platform's own escrow ledger to remain with the credit institution opening the relationship. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or European passport are not viable for investor-facing operations, as receiving funds from Luxembourg residents without local licensing would constitute an unauthorised payment service under CSSF enforcement practice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscriptions into the Luxembourg SARL are executed by SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP and other non-euro currencies, and by SEPA Credit Transfer for euro amounts; high-value euro transfers settle in central bank money on TARGET2 (T2 RTGS) with the BCL as the national settlement agent. The beneficiary bank verifies the name-match against the RCSL, checks the wire for the correct balance-of-payments purpose code, and applies sanctions and AML screening to the remitter. For a share capital injection the remittance reference should identify that the payment is a subscription for shares; for shareholder loans the reference should identify the intercompany credit. Where the historical funding route is US ACH escrow, the US leg clears through an approved partner institution and the cross-border leg reaches Luxembourg as a SWIFT MT103 with an unambiguous beneficiary reference. For crowdfunding investor funds, the inbound escrow process requires every receipt to carry a unique investor reference, enabling automatic reconciliation against the platform ledger; the Luxembourg financial intelligence unit (Cellule de Renseignements Financiers) receives reports for suspicious operations exceeding EUR 10,000, and the platform must retain the source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership evidence for each investor.Euro-denominated outbound disbursements, including issuer payouts, refunds, and operating expenses, clear through SEPA Credit Transfer in ISO 20022 format or through SEPA Instant for near-real-time obligations. Cross-border payments in foreign currency are routed by SWIFT MT103 with strict reference matching; disbursements to US beneficiaries may settle on the domestic US leg via ACH or Fedwire through a partner bank before the euro leg is delivered to the Luxembourg treasury. Where the underlying investment involves documentary trade finance, the commercial bank's trade desk issues Letters of Credit under UCP 600 or handles Collections under URC 522, with euro settlement through the T2 correspondent network. The platform's escrow system must maintain a closed loop: no client money can leave the segregated compartment until the target closing conditions under ECSPR are met, and each outgoing instruction must carry the corresponding investor reservation ticket, the issuer closing record, and the final cap-table update.EUR/USD deliverable outright forwards aligned with the underlying issuance and disbursement cycleEUR/GBP deliverable outright forwardsFX swaps for rolling settlement liquidity without extending residual currency exposureMark-to-market cross-currency swaps for long-dated foreign-currency assets associated with acquired SPV positionsPurchased long-dated FX option strategies protecting committed capital subscription obligations; writing naked or uncovered FX options is not permitted under Luxembourg prudential practice for a non-bank treasury

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High, subject to strict structural limits. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution may be onboarded under the PSD2 passport into Luxembourg as a technical payment gateway for collecting and disbursing investor funds, provided the EMI has completed the CSSF notification procedure and holds adequate safeguarding arrangements. Regulation (EU) 2020/1503, Article 10, however, requires the crowdfunding service provider itself to ensure that client funds are protected in the event of the platform's insolvency, which in practice compels holding the pooled investor monies at a supervised credit institution or at an EMI that holds such funds in a segregated account. Luxembourg banks will typically permit the EMI as an execution-only routing layer but will require the platform's own escrow ledger to remain with the credit institution opening the relationship. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg or European passport are not viable for investor-facing operations, as receiving funds from Luxembourg residents without local licensing would constitute an unauthorised payment service under CSSF enforcement practice.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% (higher corporate income tax bracket above EUR 200,000), plus 7% solidarity surcharge levied on the CIT, plus municipal business tax at approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City; combined statutory headline rate ≈ 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions (LIR Art. 105 and 115); reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate shareholder holds a participation of at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period; applicable treaty rates range from 0% to 15%, with the 0% rate commonly available for EU/EEA parent companies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline: 31 March following the fiscal year-end; administratively extended to 31 December of the year following the fiscal year-end (e.g., FY 31 December 2024 return due 31 December 2025); quarterly advance CIT/ICC instalments are due in February, May, August and November of the current tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: EUR 100,000 in aggregate controlled transactions for any single related-party transaction category (loan, service, licensing, financing) under the Luxembourg transfer pricing documentation law of 28 December 2023, applicable to tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2023; Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Report (DAC4/CbCR): required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000, filed with the ACD and exchanged automatically.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as such, but substantive presence is indirectly mandated through: (i) the ATAD GAAR and CFC rules, (ii) the participation exemption substance test under Article 148/166 LIR, (iii) Administrative Circular LIR 14/4 requiring real local value-adding functions for tax residence and treaty access, and (iv) the beneficial ownership thresholds applied to WHT reductions; a licensed equity crowdfunding portal under ECSPR must retain licensed registered offices in Luxembourg, qualified AML/CFT responsible officers, locally employed transaction and compliance staff, board minutes evidencing Luxembourg decision-making, and operational bank/escrow accounts, failing which treaty benefits, the participation exemption, and favorable tax rulings can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to 5 years; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 under the law of 25 March 2020; mandatory 10% evasion surcharge on the evaded taxes; default interest of 0.8% per month; revocation of all loss carryforwards and tax credits; automatic re-assessment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are criminally liable with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000; personal civil exposure to the fiscal authority for the full evaded amount plus interest; asset tracing and seizure of personal bank accounts; travel bans pending criminal investigation.
Artificial Arrangement, Shell Company and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization and denial of treaty and directive benefits under ATAD GAAR; disallowance of the participation exemption with reassessment at 24.94% combined rate; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; potential unilateral penalty increase of 10% on the additional assessment; EU Unshell Directive (ATAD 3) pending thresholds impose minimum substance reporting with fines and deemed-shareholder consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid taxes where the arrangement constitutes wrongful management under Article 710-1 of the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915; courts pierce the veil upon proof of sham incorporation; managing directors face civil restitution to creditors and the tax administration; criminal conviction for use of fictitious corporate vehicles.
Unauthorized Crowdfunding Operation and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for compliance breaches; criminal AML penalties of imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and fines up to EUR 5,000,000 under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004, as amended; immediate license revocation and deletion from the ECSPR register; forfeiture of the crowdfunding business and freeze of all escrow accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally prosecutable for personal knowledge of money laundering conduits; imprisonment up to 10 years and personal fines up to EUR 5,000,000; civil liability to defrauded retail investors for losses; professional disqualification and travel bans; personal seizure of management assets.
Failure to File, Late Filing and Non-Payment of Tax

Statutory sanction: Late filing fines up to EUR 25,000; imposition d'office with burden of proof reversed against the taxpayer; default interest 0.8% per commenced month on the entire unpaid balance; additional 10% late-payment surcharge; forced collection through garnishment of the SARL's escrow and bank accounts; fines up to EUR 5,000 for annual accounts non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to cause voluntary liquidation or filing may be held personally liable for the company's tax debts to the extent of mismanagement; courts may order civil restitution of dissipated funds; exit bans (interdiction de séjour) and passport retention are commonly issued for tax debts above threshold amounts; imprisonment in aggravated fraud scenarios.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Penalty adjustments and primary TP adjustment with a 10% surcharge on undeclared profit; refusal of the corresponding deductions; default interest applied to the offset tax; denial of Master File/Local File acceptance leading to evidence-weight penalties; Luxembourg courts uphold ACD determinations when documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns without TP documentation expose themselves to fines up to EUR 25,000 for incomplete declarations; personal civil liability toward the state for the undervalued tax base where they knowingly approved the mispricing; criminal proceedings for false certificates in cross-border group documentation.

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as consolidated; SARL provisions at Articles 710-1 through 710-25, in particular Article 710-4 (minimum capital), Article 710-15 (manager appointment), and Article 710-24 (registered office).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif et statuts — notarially executed deed of incorporation and articles of association, filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés (RES).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 under Article 710-4; full subscription and full payment in cash required at incorporation; each share has a minimum nominal value of EUR 1; capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain a fire safety certificate from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for any occupied warehouse premises. Structural clearance: verify that the leased building complies with the municipal building permit (autorisation de construire) and the confirmed structural load inspection. Occupational clearance: register the workplace with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under Article L.610-1 of the Luxembourg Labor Code, notify all working staff before first work, and display the internal safety regulations; if staffing exceeds 25 employees, establish a joint works council representation.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 under Article 710-4; full subscription and full payment in cash required at incorporation; each share has a minimum nominal value of EUR 1; capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and released only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (EIS) and net wealth tax (IF); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT - Registration, Estates and VAT Administration) for value added tax (TVA); Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration) for export controls. Digital service portals: MyGuichet.lu, Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCSe), eCDF/eTax for direct tax filings, and the AEDT electronic VAT filing platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) execute notarized articles of association before a Luxembourg notary and file them with the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) through Luxembourg Business Registers; (2) obtain the national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule, 15-digit format) issued ex officio by the CTIE, which serves as the central business master file identifier; (3) submit VAT registration form 640 to the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) before carrying out any taxable supply; a foreign-owned SARL is registrable from the first euro, with no de minimis threshold; (4) register for IRC, EIS and IF with the ACD, which opens a corporate tax file (numéro d'affiliation); (5) appoint a Luxembourg-resident manager (gérant) and, where statutory thresholds are exceeded (balance sheet over EUR 4.4 million; net turnover over EUR 8.8 million; over 25 employees), an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé). No sectoral license is required for wholesale distribution of electronic components (ISIC 4652). All registrations are completed electronically via MyGuichet.lu, with the registered office (siège social) as the sole physical presence requirement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - a Luxembourg-resident SARL is fully taxable at entity level and is not a pass-through vehicle. It is subject to corporate income tax (IRC) at 17% headline rate, a 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC, municipal business tax (EIS) at rates between 6.75% and 10.5% depending on the commune (10.5% in Luxembourg City), producing an approximate combined effective corporate rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City; net wealth tax (IF) of 0.5% applies to taxable net assets exceeding EUR 500,000, with a minimum net wealth tax. Worldwide income is taxable, subject to territorial carve-outs for qualifying foreign permanent establishments and the participation exemption (article 166 LIR) for qualifying shareholdings (at least 10% or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost, held for 12 months). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - a Luxembourg-resident SARL is fully taxable at entity level and is not a pass-through vehicle. It is subject to corporate income tax (IRC) at 17% headline rate, a 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC, municipal business tax (EIS) at rates between 6.75% and 10.5% depending on the commune (10.5% in Luxembourg City), producing an approximate combined effective corporate rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City; net wealth tax (IF) of 0.5% applies to taxable net assets exceeding EUR 500,000, with a minimum net wealth tax. Worldwide income is taxable, subject to territorial carve-outs for qualifying foreign permanent establishments and the participation exemption (article 166 LIR) for qualifying shareholdings (at least 10% or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost, held for 12 months). Non-resident ownership of 100% of the SARL is permitted.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a US-style check-the-box regime; available elections include: (i) participation exemption election (article 166 LIR) on a per-shareholding basis; (ii) IP box election (article 50bis LIR) granting an 80% exemption on net qualifying IP income; (iii) group taxation (fiscal unity) election under articles 164bis-164ter LIR permitting 99% profit and loss transfer among Luxembourg-resident companies with a 75% or greater holding; (iv) VAT grouping election under article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law treating the SARL and its 90%-controlled Luxembourg affiliates as a single taxable person; (v) hybrid mismatch substantiation elections under ATAD implementation (articles 168ter-168septies LIR) for reverse hybrid structures; and (vi) Pillar Two top-up tax election for in-scope MNE groups under the law of 22 December 2023. For a passive electronic inventory trading SARL, the recommended elections are VAT grouping and participation exemption on any equity stakes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident suppliers: any taxable supply made in Luxembourg triggers mandatory VAT registration from the first euro. Luxembourg-established small enterprises may elect the franchise exemption under article 57 of the VAT Law for annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, but a foreign-owned trading SARL will not qualify in practice. Intra-EU distance sales of the electronic components are governed by the harmonized EUR 10,000 annual distance-selling threshold (OSS scheme), above which Luxembourg VAT is due in the destination Member State. No domestic reverse-charge exception applies to wholesale distribution of electronics; the SARL must charge Luxembourg VAT at 17% on local B2B and B2C sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under article 155 of the Luxembourg VAT Law to: (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting on behalf of either, provided proof of exit from EU territory is obtained within 90 days of the supply; (ii) intra-EU B2B supplies of goods transported to another Member State where the customer communicates a valid EU VAT identification number and both parties file recapitulative statements; and (iii) ancillary services directly linked to the export (freight, inspection, testing, handling). For the passive MLCC and resistor inventory model, the SARL must demonstrate physical removal of warehoused goods from Luxembourg or EU territory through warehouse release documentation, transport evidence, and customs declarations. Consignment and call-off stock movements may benefit from the optional simplification under article 17a of EU Implementing Regulation 282/2011, provided the supplier maintains a consignment stock register; failure to substantiate zero-rating results in recharacterization of the supply as taxable in Luxembourg at 17%.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD-DAU) or electronic export declaration accepted by Luxembourg Customs within the EU Export Control System (ECS)
  • Transport document evidencing EU exit: Bill of Lading (sea), CMR/CIM consignment note (road/rail), air waybill (air), or courier receipt with date of departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying HS codes, Incoterms, quantities, batch references, and VAT registration numbers of both parties
  • Proof of payment and FX repatriation: Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement evidencing receipt of payment within the contractual term
  • Warehouse inventory release documentation (picking list, delivery note, stock movement report) evidencing removal from the passive electronic inventory
  • Customer's valid EU VAT ID validation printout (VIES) and Intrastat DEB declaration for intra-EU supplies

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-authority API validation system. Mandatory structured e-invoicing requirements are: (i) since 1 January 2021, all B2G invoices to Luxembourg public-sector entities must be transmitted via the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network; (ii) pursuant to the Law of 18 July 2023, with effect from 1 January 2025, every Luxembourg-established taxable person must issue and receive structured electronic invoices in Factur-X / EN 16931-compatible format for B2B transactions, and must retain invoices for 10 years (article 88 of the VAT Law). Exchange is performed through certified PEPPOL access points; there is no government clearance gateway. Taxpayers must maintain a VAT purchase/sales journal and file recapitulative statements (Intrastat / DEB) for intra-EU movements of goods.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs apply the 'no proof, no zero-rating' doctrine: where export substantiation is not produced within 90 days from the supply, VAT is reassessed at 17% with late-payment interest of 10% per annum and a 10% late-filing surcharge. In cases of fraud or deliberate falsification under articles 66-68 of the VAT Law, criminal fines range from EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years, together with a civil penalty (droits en sus) of up to 100% of the VAT evaded. The reassessment limitation period is 5 years in good faith, extended to 10 years in fraud. Luxembourg Customs conducts post-clearance audits cross-linking export declarations, Intrastat listings, warehouse movement records, and payment flows; refund claims for input VAT on export costs are closely scrutinized, with joint and several liability extending to the SARL managers and the foreign parent company.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), guaranteeing free movement of capital and payments. Luxembourg maintains no foreign-exchange control regime and imposes no prior central-bank authorization for current account transactions, capital account movements, or profit remittances. Dividend distributions by a Luxembourg SARL are subject to a 15% withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying shareholdings or to 5%–10% under applicable tax treaties; no exchange-control hurdle applies to the underlying transfer. The composite corporate income tax burden in Luxembourg City is approximately 24.94% (CIT 17% plus solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax). Foreign-currency accounts are freely permitted within and outside Luxembourg, and no BCL approval is required for repatriation or reinvestment of earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation-timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Luxembourg. As the euro is a fully convertible reserve currency, the BCL does not require exporters to convert foreign-currency receipts into EUR and does not impose any holding-period restriction; USD, CNY, or other currency balances may be retained indefinitely. The only compliance obligations are (i) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL under ECB Regulation (EU) 2021/379 and BCL implementing instructions for external transactions above the applicable de minimis thresholds (EUR 12,500 or EUR 50,000 depending on the reporting tail), and (ii) anti-money-laundering reporting to the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit for cash or cash-like payments exceeding EUR 10,000 under the amended Law of 28 November 2018, as modified by the Law of 26 March 2023. No surrender certificate or BCL clearing approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and exercising monetary policy, foreign-exchange statistics, and payment-system oversight functions jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential supervision of credit institutions and financial intermediaries is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which also issues licensing and AML/CFT regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. An EU-passported electronic money institution (EMI) licensed by the CSSF or by another EEA regulator under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may legally open a commercial account for a Luxembourg SARL without a local branch. For the target business (passive electronic-component distribution), an EMI is operationally acceptable as an ancillary sub-account for vendor payments and low-value receivable collection. It is not viable as a primary treasury rail because (i) most EMIs lack documentary letter-of-credit issuance, supply-chain finance, and committed credit-line capabilities; (ii) established tier-1 Luxembourg banks generally decline to aggregate funds flowing only from an offshore EMI owing to end-to-end traceability concerns; and (iii) high-frequency settlement of excess inventory trades requires robust SWIFT, TARGET2, and correspondent-banking corridors that EMIs often route through partner banks with degraded service windows. A CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI can be used as a complement, but the anchor banking relationship should remain a supervised commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial share capital and subsequent capital increases must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (for standard commercial amounts) or MT202 COV (for interbank cover transfers) to the SARL's EUR account at the Luxembourg bank. The wire must carry the ECB balance-of-payments purpose code corresponding to the BPM6 category, transactions affecting the reporting enterprise in the form of additions to equity capital, or, for shareholder debt, the intercompany lending code. The beneficiary bank requires the notarised subscription deed, shareholder resolution, proof of the remitter's debit instruction, and final-blocking certificate prior to disbursement. Cash contributions above EUR 50,000 must be consistent with the RBE update and the bank's automatic reporting to the Luxembourg FIU and BCL statistics. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility. An EU-passported electronic money institution (EMI) licensed by the CSSF or by another EEA regulator under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) may legally open a commercial account for a Luxembourg SARL without a local branch. For the target business (passive electronic-component distribution), an EMI is operationally acceptable as an ancillary sub-account for vendor payments and low-value receivable collection. It is not viable as a primary treasury rail because (i) most EMIs lack documentary letter-of-credit issuance, supply-chain finance, and committed credit-line capabilities; (ii) established tier-1 Luxembourg banks generally decline to aggregate funds flowing only from an offshore EMI owing to end-to-end traceability concerns; and (iii) high-frequency settlement of excess inventory trades requires robust SWIFT, TARGET2, and correspondent-banking corridors that EMIs often route through partner banks with degraded service windows. A CSSF-licensed Luxembourg EMI can be used as a complement, but the anchor banking relationship should remain a supervised commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,500,000 for transactions with related parties in Luxembourg (OECD TP documentation, including Master File and Local File) where the turnover threshold exceeds EUR 3,000,000; Luxembourg law requires a contemporaneous TP documentation file to be maintained if the group’s consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and intra-group transaction thresholds exceed EUR 1,500,000 (financial) or EUR 300,000 (non-financial) per year; the ACD circular LIR 56/1 - 56/2 prescribes the content of the Master File and Local File.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies a substance-over-form doctrine under Allgemeines Steuerrecht and OECD standards; since the 2019 Finance Law and EU ATAD, the ACD can disregard a Luxembourg entity lacking minimum economic substance (board meetings in Luxembourg, local strategic decisions, employees, office premises, operational risk, bank accounts) and re-characterise it as a shell; the EU DAC6 and ATAD1/ATAD2 contain a general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) that denies treaty benefits where a transaction lacks commercial rationale; the Luxembourg relationship (Section 166bis LIR) denies participation exemption to vehicles without taxable substance or where the income is not subject to adequate taxation in the source state.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraud (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment up to 200% of the evaded tax (Luxembourg GAAR, abuse of law doctrine); criminal fine under Loi fiscale of EUR 1,500 to EUR 150,000 (increased where systematic); administrative surcharges of 10% plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; stripping of treaty benefits and denial of participation exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal civil liability for payment of taxes owed if they co-signed or knowingly received assets of the company in fraud of tax; travel bans may be imposed during criminal tax proceedings; imprisonment of 5 - 10 years in cases of aggravated tax fraud, with cumulative damages and interest.
Failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation

Statutory sanction: Formal tax assessment based on deemed taxable margins estimated by the ACD; administrative fine up to EUR 250,000 for non-compliance with TP documentation obligations; penalty interest 5% per annum applied to adjustments; potential 10% late-filing penalty.

Director personal liability: The managing director (gérant) of an SARL may be jointly liable with the company for TP documentation penalties if the director failed to exercise ordinary prudence; directors are exposed to criminal liability in a civil fraud suit by tax authorities seeking to pierce the veil where assets were dissipated intentionally.
Shell company absence of substance (economic substance doctrine violation)

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg participation exemption; retroactive requalification of income as profit distribution subject to withholding tax; refusal of a Luxembourg tax residence certificate (Form 720) or withdrawal if already issued; anti-abuse directive on shell entities (EU ATAD/shell proposal framework) requiring reclassification of the tax residence to the jurisdiction of the parent, losses and penalties assessed accordingly.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax claimed if they certified the substance declarations in tax returns; under Luxembourg law, directors can be held liable for the company’s tax debts in cases of manifest serious negligence; imprisonment possible if participation in a criminal shell-company arrangement is proven.
AML and criminal enforcement (money laundering of tax proceeds)

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of funds equal to the laundered values; fine of up to EUR 10,000,000 for legal entities under Article 506-2 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code; statutory mandates under the fiscal criminal law apply; exclusion from public procurement and potential order of dissolution of the SARL.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in laundering face imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and a fine of up to EUR 50,000 under the Criminal Code; AML compliance officers and managing directors are subject to CSSF sanctions including personal fines up to EUR 500,000 and suspension; civil reparation and worldwide asset-tracing orders follow.

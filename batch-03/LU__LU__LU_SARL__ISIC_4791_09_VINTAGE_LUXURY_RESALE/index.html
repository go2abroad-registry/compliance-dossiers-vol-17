Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 13c, rue de Bitbourg, L-1273 Luxembourg-Hamm; online portal: e-justice.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 for the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Grand-Ducal Regulation of 13 July 2017 on the Register of Commerce and Companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association (Statuts) constituting the SARL, filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL requires a minimum issued share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account in the name of the company-in-formation with a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be evaluated by a statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) with a full valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for any office, showroom, or warehouse must be obtained from the municipal fire prevention authority (Inspection du service d'incendie et de secours) and the Commune's building inspectorate, including a structural load certification for storage areas. Occupational clearance from the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (Inspection du travail et des mines, ITM) is required to confirm ergonomic workstations, safe access, emergency exits, and compliance with the Labour Code for employees. For a purely online consignment business without physical handling of goods, only office-related occupational safety certificates and a fire evacuation plan are required.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL requires a minimum issued share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up before incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account in the name of the company-in-formation with a Luxembourg credit institution and may be released only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be evaluated by a statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) with a full valuation report.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Luxembourg VAT and Registration Administration; online portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities established outside Luxembourg must register for VAT directly via MyGuichet or, if established outside the EU, appoint a tax representative domiciled in Luxembourg. The application requires submission of the RCSL extract (for EU entities), power of attorney, and proof of economic activity. The Luxembourg VAT identification number is assigned in the format 'LU' followed by 8 digits, typically within 10 to 15 business days after submission.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities established outside Luxembourg must register for VAT directly via MyGuichet or, if established outside the EU, appoint a tax representative domiciled in Luxembourg. The application requires submission of the RCSL extract (for EU entities), power of attorney, and proof of economic activity. The Luxembourg VAT identification number is assigned in the format 'LU' followed by 8 digits, typically within 10 to 15 business days after submission.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg. A SARL is inherently opaque and subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax. Optional VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') is available to related taxable persons. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply and may disallow deductions or reclassify payments; no elective hybrid regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption threshold: EUR 35,000 annual turnover. Intra-EU distance sales threshold: EUR 10,000 (Union-wide). Non-established foreign sellers have mandatory registration with no turnover threshold for Luxembourg VAT purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, the buyer, or a third party acting on behalf of either qualify for the 0% export VAT rate if the export is completed within 3 months of supply. The seller must hold documentary proof of export. Offshore B2B services and services directly related to export goods may also be zero-rated. For vintage luxury resale, the second-hand goods margin scheme cannot be combined with zero-rating on the same transaction; if margin scheme is applied, the supply remains taxable in the EU unless an explicit export waiver is exercised.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic customs declaration through EU CTAS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Bank Transfer Proof
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Transport Evidence (CMR waybill, carrier tracking report, or courier proof of delivery)

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control system. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to central government entities using PEPPOL BIS / EN 16931 format. B2B e-invoicing requires customer consent. No digital stamping or government pre-clearance is required; invoices must preserve authenticity, integrity, and legibility.

The Luxembourg VAT authority may audit export evidence for up to 5 years. Missing or fraudulent export substantiation triggers retrospective VAT assessment at 17%, late interest, and administrative fines up to 100% of the tax evaded. Criminal prosecution for serious VAT fraud and export zero-rating abuse can impose fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of directors. Reclaimed input VAT associated with disallowed zero-rated exports is clawed back with penalty surcharges; due diligence on transport documents and payment traceability is mandatory.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full and unrestricted capital and current account convertibility for euro-based and foreign-currency transactions under EU Treaty provisions (Art. 63 TFEU) and the Luxembourg law of 19 December 2002 on the payments system. There are no exchange-control barriers to inbound equity injections, profit distributions, dividend remittances, or loan repayments, provided the underlying documentation satisfies standard corporate law, tax compliance, and AML/CFT due diligence. The SARL is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax, but foreign exchange conversion is not taxed. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is permitted assimilated to dividend distribution, subject to withholding tax (15% standard, reduced to 0% under a properly applied EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable DTT if 10% participation holding is maintained for 12 months). No Central Bank pre-approval is required for transfers exceeding any threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligations or mandatory conversion ratios in Luxembourg or within the Eurozone. Export receipts from cross-border vintage luxury resale, whether denominated in EUR, USD, GBP, or other currencies, may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at Luxembourg credit institutions or in EU-licensed payment accounts. No BCL declaration is required for trade-related current account transactions, though amounts above EUR 15,000 must be reported by the credit institution to the CSSF/BCL as part of Anti-Money Laundering (AML) transaction-monitoring protocols. For statistical balance-of-payments reporting, the BCL requires banks to use standardized purpose codes (e.g., service codes for e-commerce intermediation), but no surrender ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and exchange-control oversight; foreign exchange policy is harmonized at EU level through Economic and Monetary Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for operational collections and settlement through EU passporting under PSD2, e.g., Stripe Treasury, Adyen, or a Luxembourg-licensed EMI such as Licks or ZEN, for daily e-commerce flows in EUR, USD, and GBP. These platforms accelerate merchant onboarding for resale marketplaces and offer API-based payment orchestration. However, EMIs cannot serve as a substitute for a regulated credit institution for capital escrow, inward equity injections, commercial letters of credit, or FX hedging line establishment, because the SARL must maintain segregated client funds in a commercial bank for fiduciary and audit defensibility. Legal constraint: EMIs do not have CDD authority in all third-country jurisdictions; they cannot issue documentary credits (LC) and they are not counterparties under ISDA/EMIR for non-deliverable forwards. The SARL should therefore maintain one primary tier-1 bank account for capital and treasury operations and one EMI account as a collection conduit subject to daily zero-balance sweeps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound capital contributions from the ultimate shareholder through the primary tier-1 Luxembourg bank using SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in EUR if the contributor is within an EEA jurisdiction, or SWIFT MT103 (CBPR+) with essential fields charged to the beneficiary's cash account. The wire instruction must include purpose code 'PCAP' (Paid-in Capital) or 'EQUI' (Equity) in the SWIFT purpose field and reference the notarized capital subscription deed and the RCS registration number of the SARL. Beneficiary account must be identified as the capital escrow/operational account in the name of LU_SARL, and the bank requires the submission of the notarized deed, an updated RCS extract, and certification of no capital laundering. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% base corporate income tax (plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax; maximum combined headline incidence in Luxembourg City is 24.94%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on distributions to non-resident shareholders; domestic inter-corporate dividends qualifying for participation exemption are exempt when the recipient holds at least 10% of the payer or an acquisition cost of at least EUR 1,200,000 and satisfies the 12-month holding condition; reduced rates or 0% apply under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due no later than 31 December of the following calendar year; for non-calendar year-ends, the return is due within twelve months of the fiscal year-end. Extensions are available on reasoned request before the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg does not impose a fixed local-file threshold in domestic legislation; the ACD requires transfer-pricing documentation to be supplied when related-party transactions are material, commonly where the aggregate value exceeds EUR 1,000,000. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNC groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000, in which case the Luxembourg entity must file its CbCR declaration through the ACD portal.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations similar to other financial-centre statutes, but substance is enforced through the Luxembourg GAAR (abuse of law doctrine), ATAD provisions, and LIR anti-deferral rules. A SARL must demonstrate commercial substance through a registered office, local management, employee/decision-maker capacity, bank accounts, accounting and corporate records maintained in Luxembourg, and annual board or shareholder meetings. Absent substance, tax benefits, treaty access, and participation-exemption treatment can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and aggressive under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income plus default interest of 0.6% per month; late-payment penalty of 10%; civil penalties of 20% to 80% of the evaded tax for intentional fault; criminal prosecution can lead to fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years; surrender of unlawfully obtained tax advantages.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes not withheld or deliberately evaded if they sign false returns or tolerate shadow accounting; personal assets can be seized, travel bans imposed, and directors can be imprisoned where their involvement is proven.
Shell / letterbox company and abusive structure

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for tax purposes under the Luxembourg anti-abuse doctrine; denial of tax residence certificate, participation exemption, treaty relief, and VAT registration; imposition of tax at the level of the actual beneficial owner; fines and civil penalties for failure to demonstrate economic substance in audit proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham entity can be held personally liable to the tax administration for taxes that should have been borne by the entity, repay artificial distributions, suffer disqualification as directors, be subject to travel restrictions, and face criminal sanctions where the sham was used to facilitate fraud.
AML, unlicensed escrow, and payment-rail misconduct

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 under Luxembourg AML/CFT laws; withdrawal of the payment service provider's licence or termination of bank/PSP relationships; freezing of escrow wallets and Stripe reserves; criminal money-laundering penalties up to ten years' imprisonment; prohibition from conducting the resale activity; potential seizure of online business property.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution for client funds lost or misused, joint liability for fines if they knowingly allowed the unlicensed activity, personal criminal liability for AML breaches, asset freezing orders, foreign travel bans, and imprisonment where escrow funds were knowingly used to conceal illicit wealth.

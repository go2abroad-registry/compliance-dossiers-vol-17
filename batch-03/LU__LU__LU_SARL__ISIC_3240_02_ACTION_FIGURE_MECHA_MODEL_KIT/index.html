Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, Articles 710-1 to 710-23 (SARL regime); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and Annual Accounts (RCS Law); Law of 2 September 2011 on the Freedom of Establishment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif / statuts) executed before a Luxembourg notary, filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid-up in cash and/or in kind before incorporation; cash capital must be deposited on a blocked account of a Luxembourg credit institution and evidenced by a bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage de fonds) produced to the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report where applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification must be obtained from the municipal fire authority and the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS); structural load inspection certificates for injection-moulding machinery must be issued by an approved engineering bureau; workplace occupational clearance and machinery conformity verification are performed by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including the mandatory risk assessment and safety measures prescribed by the amended Law of 17 June 1994 on Occupational Health and Safety.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid-up in cash and/or in kind before incorporation; cash capital must be deposited on a blocked account of a Luxembourg credit institution and evidenced by a bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage de fonds) produced to the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report where applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; official portals: myguichet.lu, guichet.lu, and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first file for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through myguichet.lu via a Luxembourg professional appointed as authorized signatory or incorporation agent. After RCS registration, the entity automatically receives a business identification number (matricule d'identification). The entity must then submit a separate VAT registration application to the AEDT (form 640 or via myguichet.lu) indicating its intended taxable activities in Luxembourg. For a SARL, the RCS extract, articles of association, proof of registered office, identity documents of managers, and a signed declaration confirming the commencement of taxable activities are required. The TIN (matricule) is used for corporate income tax; the VAT number (numéro de TVA) is issued separately by the AEDT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide a check-the-box election comparable to the US. A SARL is, by default, a separately assessable corporate taxpayer. Optional regimes available include the election for joint filing under the Luxembourg integrated fiscal regime (intégration fiscale), VAT grouping election, and the shipping or IP box regimes where eligibility is satisfied. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD and Luxembourg domestic anti-hybrid provisions may restrict elective outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic registration threshold applies to a Luxembourg-established taxable person; any person carrying out taxable transactions is required to register for VAT irrespective of turnover level. For cross-border distance sales of goods into Luxembourg by non-established suppliers, the EU distance selling threshold is currently EUR 10,000 per year, measured against overall distance sales within the EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf, provided the goods leave the EU. Services that are directly linked to the export of goods or supplied to non-EU customers where the place of supply is outside the EU also qualify for zero-rating. The supplier must hold documentary evidence establishing that the goods have been physically exported, the export transaction is commercial in substance, and the recipient is established abroad. For an action figure manufacturing scope, typical qualifying exports include goods sold to non-EU distributors or customers with import customs documentation proving entry into a third country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation / Single Administrative Document SAD)
  • Proof of leaving the EU (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR road consignment note, or railway consignment note)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Luxembourg SARL
  • Purchase order and sales contract
  • Payment proof (SWIFT wire or PayPal transaction record)
  • For certain countries, a certificate of arrival or customs import documentation issued by the destination customs office
  • Internal transport records and proof of dispatch from Luxembourg warehouse

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control for all B2B/B2C invoicing. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for public sector supplies (B2G) using the PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format, and e-invoicing is accepted generally with certain authentication requirements. No digital stamping or API-based real-time reporting to the tax administration is imposed as of the current reporting date, though invoice storage and access obligations align with EU VAT invoicing rules.

Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims may trigger recharacterization as taxable domestic supplies, resulting in VAT assessment at 16%, plus default interest and late-payment penalties up to 10% per annum. Intentional or repeated irregularity attracts administrative fines ranging from EUR 750 to EUR 12,500, or up to double the evaded tax, and criminal sanctions for tax fraud may be imposed. The AEDT may also claw back input VAT correspondingly, deny refunds for up to five years, and impose a 10% penalty for gross negligence or intentionally documented fraud. Directors and managing officers of the SARL may incur joint and several liability for unjustified VAT benefits when they knew or should have known of the abuse.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg franc was permanently replaced by the euro (EUR) on 1 January 1999; the SARL operates under a fully liberalised regime. Both current-account and capital-account operations are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Directive 88/361/EEC. Profit repatriation and dividend distributions require only ordinary corporate formalities under the law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies (annual shareholders' meeting, distributable-reserve sufficiency, and certain minimum statutory reserve requirements). Dividend withholding tax of 15% applies at a standard domestic rate, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 months, or to treaty-reduced rates for third-country investors. No prior exchange-control approval, central-bank notification, or convertibility permit is required for any inward or outward payment; the only residual obligation is periodic balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export receipts. A Luxembourg SARL may freely retain foreign currency (USD, JPY, CNY) in multi-currency accounts, convert into EUR at market rates, or use the proceeds to settle external trade payables. Non-financial corporations are subject exclusively to BCL statistical reporting rules: direct investment flows and positions vis-à-vis non-residents must be reported on a quarterly basis when the accumulated transaction threshold exceeds EUR 12.5 million per reporting period; below this threshold, the intermediating credit institution reports on the company's behalf.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem, with prudential regulation and AML/CFT supervision delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); no separate foreign exchange administration exists in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary operational flows; Low as a primary treasury rail. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Qonto, or N26 Business) may lawfully provide the SARL with a working-capital account, EUR/GBP/USD receivables collection, and expedited supplier payout via IBAN and SWIFT connectivity under PSD2 passporting. However, such EMIs do not hold the legal status of a credit institution in Luxembourg, are not CSSF-supervised as banks, cannot open a broad documentary-credit line or issuance of standby LCs, and are generally unsuitable for large inbound capital escrow or trade-finance disbursements. The SARL must retain at least one Tier-1 CSSF-regulated bank as the core treasury counterparty; EMIs are permitted only as a payment-rail supplement, with a maximum balance per EMI capped at operational float requirements (recommended EUR 50,000 - 100,000). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for equity subscription: (1) the shareholder wires funds from its home bank via SWIFT MT103 to the SARL's EUR account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank; (2) the payment must be tagged with the BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for direct-investment equity ('Equity capital excluding reinvestment of earnings') and must include the SARL's registered legal name and RCS number (e.g., LU-XXXXXX) in the beneficiary field; (3) the originator must provide complete originator information (legal name, address, account number) pursuant to EU Regulation 2015/847, otherwise the receiving bank must reject or hold the funds; (4) the receiving bank will require a board/shareholder resolution, the updated coordinate list of authorised signatories, a completed source-of-funds questionnaire for amounts exceeding EUR 100,000, and the notarised deed of contribution if the capital increase is registered with the RCS; (5) upon confirmation of receipt, the SARL must update the RBE register and file an annual accounts note in the Luxembourg trade registry; (6) if the cumulative direct-investment flow exceeds EUR 12.5 million in a single quarter, the SARL must file a direct BCL balance-of-payments report online via the BCL's e-reporting portal; below the threshold the bank reports on its behalf. For convertible notes, the routing is identical but requires a tax-law validation that the note qualifies as a genuine debt instrument and not as re-characterised equity for Luxembourg corporate income and net-wealth tax purposes.Clearing rails used: (a) Packaging/moulding materials sourced from EU suppliers (plastic resin pellets, pigments, packaging) - settle via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant within the EU, typically clearing through TARGET2 within the same business day; (b) high-precision steel injection moulds and tooling from non-EU toolmakers (China, Taiwan, Japan) - settle via irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit at sight (MT700/MT710 commercial LC) advised by the Luxembourg bank through the beneficiary's confirming bank, or via SWIFT MT103 direct wire with a signed commercial invoice, packing list, and bills of lading as underlying evidence; (c) royalty and licensing payments to anime/IP licensors in Japan or the US - SWIFT MT103 quoting the relevant tax-treaty withholding position (Luxembourg-Japan income tax treaty or Luxembourg-US treaty) and accompanied by the licensor's W-8BEN-E/Tax board form and the royalty invoice; (d) freight, customs brokerage, and third-party logistics - SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT, with supplier invoices matched to the shipping manifest; (e) PayPal-based DTC micro-trades in the sub-EUR 10,000 tranche - PayPal mass-payout or instant-transfer bridge to the SARL's associated operational bank account; PayPal proceeds are swept daily to the primary bank to minimise whale-high fees and FX margin. For all outbound non-EUR disbursements, the bank's instructions should include the SWIFT BIC, IBAN/CLABE/ABA equivalent, and the correspondent-bank routing confirmed in advance by the receiving bank to avoid MT202 cover rejection.EUR/USD plain-vanilla outright forward contracts (tenor 1-24 months) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX Master Confirmation Agreement; collateral is generally not required below a EUR 2 million notional.EUR/JPY outright forwards for royalty and tooling payments to Japanese counterparties, locked in conjunction with daily LIBOR/EURIBOR (BOFR) conversion conventions.EUR/CNY deliverable forwards (where the Luxembourg bank has CNY clearing capability via Hong Kong) or non-deliverable forwards (CNH NDF) for Chinese tooling suppliers holding offshore CNY accounts.FX swaps (swap-out/swap-in) for temporary EUR liquidity mobilisation against USD trade receivables.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCS) only for large cap-ex financing linked to an underlying EC-laundered loan; must be cleared or exchanged via a registered EU trade repository (e.g., REGIS-TR or DTCC Data Repository) according to EMIR Refit reporting obligations.Vanilla European-style FX options on EUR/USD for downside protection on major export orders; premium payment must be considered as a taxable treasury expense.Non-deliverable swaps on exotic currencies (e.g., EUR/BRL, EUR/TRY) are permissible if the bank maintains an approved NDF settlement policy and the notional is supported by an underlying overdue trade payable; the CRD V/EMIR risk-floor applies.PayPal currency conversion is contractually available but is classified as a non-bank treasury hedging mechanism; it is permissible only for immaterial DTC transaction balances and does not satisfy prudent FX-hedging policy requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary operational flows; Low as a primary treasury rail. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Qonto, or N26 Business) may lawfully provide the SARL with a working-capital account, EUR/GBP/USD receivables collection, and expedited supplier payout via IBAN and SWIFT connectivity under PSD2 passporting. However, such EMIs do not hold the legal status of a credit institution in Luxembourg, are not CSSF-supervised as banks, cannot open a broad documentary-credit line or issuance of standby LCs, and are generally unsuitable for large inbound capital escrow or trade-finance disbursements. The SARL must retain at least one Tier-1 CSSF-regulated bank as the core treasury counterparty; EMIs are permitted only as a payment-rail supplement, with a maximum balance per EMI capped at operational float requirements (recommended EUR 50,000 - 100,000). PayPal serves the low-risk DTC/anime-hobbyist direct-sales channel under the target payment rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File mandatory when group net turnover exceeds EUR 50 million and intra-group transactions exceed EUR 100,000; simplified Local File for lower-value flows; Country-by-Country Report required when consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750 million; documentation must be produced within 60 days of an ACD request

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg enforces substance requirements through the domestic general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit), the ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 implementations, beneficial-ownership precedent for treaty and Directive relief, and ACD circular practice; the SARL must maintain in Luxembourg its registered office, administrative employees, board decision-making, operational bank accounts, and transfer pricing records; the participation exemption and IP box are denied where the entity lacks genuine economic activity and is used as a conduit for the mecha model kit IP or royalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (Art. 396 AO): imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and/or a fine up to EUR 25,000; aggravated fraud (Art. 397 AO): fine up to EUR 250,000; plus full tax reassessment, 0.6% monthly late interest, and an official-assessment surcharge up to 10%

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are prosecuted personally; courts may order civil restitution of evaded taxes, freeze personal and corporate assets, impose travel bans, and disqualify directors from managing commercial companies
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization of the structure; denial of participation-exemption dividend treatment and treaty withholding tax relief; administrative fine up to EUR 250,000 for hybrid-mismatch notification failures (Art. 168quater LIR); reclassification of the Luxembourg entity as a foreign principal's permanent establishment with retroactive assessment

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for the abusive arrangement, claw-back claims by creditors and tax authority in insolvency, and criminal prosecution in cases of deliberate sham; travel bans and management prohibitions may be imposed in criminal proceedings
AML / Money Laundering Abuse

Statutory sanction: AML Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF Regulation 12-02: supervisory fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; Penal Code Art. 506-1 money laundering: imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and fine from EUR 12,500 to EUR 1,250,000, doubled to EUR 5,000,000 in organized-crime circumstances

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable as AML gatekeepers; sanctions include preventive asset freezing, international travel bans, professional bans, personal fines, and imprisonment upon conviction
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax audit adjustment of the Luxembourg taxable base with 0.6% monthly late interest; administrative fine up to EUR 250,000 for documentation and disclosure failures; double taxation exposure without mutual agreement procedure relief in severe cases

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct mispricing or destroy evidence are jointly liable for the tax undercharge; aggravated conduct may be prosecuted as tax fraud under Art. 396 AO, triggering imprisonment and personal fines

Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) as the statutory national business registry under the Ministry of Justice; electronic filing via the LBR e-file portal (lbr.lu).

Governing statute

Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, consolidated, as amended by the Law of 23 June 2016 modernizing the SARL), Articles 710-1 to 710-24 governing SARL formation, minimum capital, shares, management and governance; supplemented by the Loi du 10 août 1991 sur la profession d'avocat (Law of 10 August 1991 on the legal profession) and the Loi modifiée du 12 novembre 2004 (AML/CFT obligations for law firms).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution / Statuts (Articles of Incorporation) executed as a notarial deed (acte notarié) or private deed (acte sous seing privé), filed at the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000 (≈ USD 13,080), fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the 'SARL en formation' with a Luxembourg credit institution (or an EU/EEA credit institution under Article 710-6 of the Law of 1915); the bank issues a blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) that is released only upon presentation of the RCS registration extract; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report and immediate full delivery.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax; unified electronic access via MyGuichet.lu and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / RCS portal.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL, the company is first registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers portal, which automatically issues the national business registration number (matricule). Following incorporation, the SARL must file the AEDT VAT registration request (formulaire 640) via MyGuichet.lu, obtaining a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA); the application is processed within 5–10 business days. A foreign non-resident entity with no Luxembourg fixed establishment that performs taxable domestic supplies must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative (représentant responsable solidaire) and register immediately, with no turnover threshold; the engagement of legal counsel in Luxembourg is typically undertaken directly by the SARL, which registers for VAT in its own name.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a Luxembourg SARL is a resident corporate entity subject to full Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; it is not eligible for pass-through/check-the-box treatment. The SARL is taxed on its worldwide income unless relief applies via treaty or the territorial provisions for foreign permanent establishments; legal services rendered abroad are not automatically territorial and require a foreign PE analysis under applicable double tax treaties. — For a foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL, the company is first registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers portal, which automatically issues the national business registration number (matricule). Following incorporation, the SARL must file the AEDT VAT registration request (formulaire 640) via MyGuichet.lu, obtaining a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA); the application is processed within 5–10 business days. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; a Luxembourg SARL is a resident corporate entity subject to full Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; it is not eligible for pass-through/check-the-box treatment. The SARL is taxed on its worldwide income unless relief applies via treaty or the territorial provisions for foreign permanent establishments; legal services rendered abroad are not automatically territorial and require a foreign PE analysis under applicable double tax treaties.

        
Available election forms
Available elections: (i) group taxation regime (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for direct or indirect 95% ownership held for at least 24 months, covering corporate income tax and municipal business tax; (ii) the participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) for qualifying dividends and capital gains; (iii) the optional transfer-pricing documentation summary (CbC local file elections). Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD) restrict deduction/non-inclusion outcomes for hybrid instruments, hybrid entities, and reverse hybrid structures, which are relevant for cross-border litigation fee payments and IP cost-sharing arrangements.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
17%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Zero for non-resident taxable persons — any foreign supply of services or goods subject to Luxembourg VAT triggers immediate registration or reverse-charge compliance; the domestic small-business exemption of EUR 35,000 annual turnover applies solely to Luxembourg-established taxable persons and does not shelter a foreign SARL branch or a foreign law firm. For cross-border commercial litigation services supplied to a taxable business customer established abroad, the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction and no Luxembourg VAT registration is required for that specific supply.

      

      
      
No universal B2B e-invoicing clearance mandate is currently in force; mandatory B2G (public procurement) e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network applies, phased in under Luxembourg law. VAT-registered businesses must file periodic recapitulative statements (VIES) for intra-Community services and goods, transmit Intrastat declarations where applicable, and retain invoices in a structured audit-valid format (PDF/A-3 or XML/UBL). Luxembourg is transposing the EU ViDA framework, which will introduce real-time digital reporting and e-invoicing clearance for B2B supplies in the near term; cross-border litigation service providers should prepare for transactional digital stamping and API-based reporting.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full convertibility. Luxembourg is a Eurozone OECD member with unrestricted current-account and capital-account flows under Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. No exchange-control permit or central-bank investment authorization exists for a SARL. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than currency-related: distributions incur the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax unless reduced by treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, and the SARL remains subject to standard corporate income tax and municipal business tax. Foreign-currency accounts may be held and converted freely; exchange spreads and transfer costs are the only practical constraints.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds or service income, including cross-border legal fees. The BCL only imposes statistical balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border flows exceeding published thresholds; legal-services revenue and intra-EU group flows fall under the monthly BCL reporting framework but are subject to no currency conversion mandate.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in cooperation with the European Central Bank (ECB); banking and financial-market supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High

Recommended architecture: an EMI as a liquidity and expense side-rail, with Tier-1 bank accounts as the exclusive clearing and client-funds spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Capital injections, whether share-capital subscription or shareholder loans, must arrive by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR legs with unambiguous beneficiary naming and a clear remittance purpose. For equity subscription, use the BCL balance-of-payments direct-investment category E (equity capital) and annotate the notarial deed and RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) filing reference in SWIFT field 70; for shareholder loans, use the intercompany financial-loan category referencing the dated loan agreement and shareholders' resolution. The receiving bank will require the notarized subscription deed, an RCS excerpt not older than three months, and board minutes authorizing the facility. EUR legs settle across TARGET2; USD and GBP legs clear via nostro and correspondent networks.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for all non-SEPA cross-border disbursements including foreign counsel fees, court and arbitration costs, expert-witness charges, and asset-tracing vendor payments; SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) covers EUR payments inside the SEPA zone with next-day value; Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 are permissible where litigation security bonds or collateralized settlements require documentary collection mechanics; securities-linked collateral operations use Clearstream Banking Luxembourg and TARGET2-Securities interoperability. Standard practice: instruct wires with detailed purpose codes, such as the BCL balance-of-payments code for legal and other professional services, and pre-agree beneficiary KYC documentation to avoid clearing delays.Vanilla FX forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CHF under ISDA 2002 or a local Luxembourg master agreement, permitted without BCL authorizationFX swaps for treasury liquidity conversion between EUR, USD and GBP, permitted under CSSF MiFID II rules where the SARL qualifies as an eligible professional counterpartyNon-deliverable forwards in non-convertible or restricted currencies solely to hedge litigation and settlement exposures in jurisdictions where physical currency delivery is legally impededPurchased premium-bearing vanilla FX options to hedge fee income and settlement receivables, permitted; writing naked options is not recommended and may constitute dealing activity requiring CSSF licensingCross-currency interest rate swaps applicable to term-structured litigation funding receivables, permitted under EU EMIR with clearing obligation and trade-reporting assessment, typically only where notional exposure exceeds EUR 10 million
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium: feasible and legally permissible for the SARL's own treasury balances, FX conversion and daily operating disbursements through Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions or EU-passported EMIs; however, EMIs are NOT viable for client money or litigation settlement funds because Luxembourg fiduciary and escrow rules require client funds to be held with a credit institution in a segregated account. Recommended architecture: an EMI as a liquidity and expense side-rail, with Tier-1 bank accounts as the exclusive clearing and client-funds spine.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
17% corporate income tax (IRC) plus 7% employment fund surcharge plus municipal business tax (Luxembourg City headline 6.75%), producing a combined headline rate of 24.94% on taxable income; first EUR 175,000 of taxable income is taxed at 15% IRC.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
15% domestic dividend withholding tax; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying parent holding at least 10% for at least 12 months; treaty rates typically range from 0% to 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue triggers Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations; Local File is required when annual related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services, based on the Luxembourg transfer pricing administrative guidance; the tax authorities may request documentation at lower thresholds on a case-by-case basis.

      
Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substances Regulations comparable to OECD low-tax jurisdictions, but economic substance is independently required under the general anti-abuse rule (Section 6 LIR), the ATAD GAAR, the OECD principal purposes test, and Luxembourg administrative circulars on holding and financing companies. A SARL engaged in cross-border litigation services must maintain local premises, qualified and decision-making personnel, and genuine commercial risk in Luxembourg to secure treaty benefits, participation exemption, and reduced dividend withholding treatment.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fine from EUR 5,000 to EUR 150,000, or an amount up to six times the evaded tax, plus imprisonment ranging from eight days to five years; administrative tax adjustments, 10% late-filing surcharge, and monthly interest of 0.6% on underpaid tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and the gérant of the SARL may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties when the evasion is attributable to deliberate mismanagement; criminal conviction may trigger travel bans, asset freezing, confiscation orders, and civil restitution via the Luxembourg action en comblement du passif.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration may re-determine profits under the arm's-length standard, deny deductibility of unsubstantiated charges, impose late-payment interest and negligence penalties, and deny entitlement to tax treaty or EU directive benefits.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for restitution where false or misleading transfer pricing documentation caused a balance-sheet deficit; repeated non-compliance may result in administrative travel bans and disqualification for serious mismanagement.

      

      
      

        Abusive Dividend Repatriation and Treaty Shopping
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of the 0% or treaty-reduced rate; retroactive application of the 15% domestic dividend withholding tax; assessment of withholding under-withheld penalties and monthly interest; possible determination of the Luxembourg entity as a fully taxable shell with sanctions under the GAAR.

        
Director personal liability: The responsible director is personally liable for any withholding tax the company failed to account for, plus related fines; in fraud circumstances, the director may face imprisonment and travel bans under mutual assistance in criminal tax matters.

      

      
      

        Money Laundering and Shell Company Facade
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg anti-money laundering law of 12 November 2004 and the 2019/2023 reforms, penalties include imprisonment of one to ten years and fines up to EUR 2,500,000 for natural persons and up to 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; the SARL may be dissolved, client assets confiscated, and beneficiary accounts frozen.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, asset confiscation, travel bans, and director disqualification; they can also be ordered to restore trust funds under civil recovery proceedings where their conduct enabled the laundering of third-party funds.

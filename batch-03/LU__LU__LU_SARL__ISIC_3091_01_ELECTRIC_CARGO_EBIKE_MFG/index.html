A Luxembourg-registered office is mandatory (Law of 31 May 1999 on company domiciliation). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD – Direct Tax Administration), 33 rue de la Gasperich, L-1615 Luxembourg, official portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu; VAT competence rests with the Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT – Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Administration), with electronic filing through the eTVA module of the single official portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the SARL before the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS Luxembourg) via the e-business platform (LBR.LU); upon incorporation the company is entered in the Luxembourg Central Register of Companies and automatically obtains the national Tax Identification Number (matricule national). Step 2 – The AEDT automatically issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (n° TVA; format 'LU' followed by 8 digits) for the newly incorporated resident company; no separate registration application is required for resident entities. Step 3 – A non-resident entity that carries out taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a local establishment must actively register for VAT before the AEDT registration department through the eTVA/MyGuichet.lu portal; the application requires certified articles of association, proof of the foreign VAT/TIN registration, description of the Luxembourg taxable activity and, for non-EU applicants without an EU establishment, appointment of a Luxembourg tax representative. Step 4 – Complete the beneficial ownership declaration (RBE register) and, if the SARL operates a commercial establishment in Luxembourg, obtain the autorisation d'établissement from the Ministry of the Economy. Filing obligations start as of the registration date: VAT returns (monthly or quarterly depending on turnover), VIES/EC Sales Lists, and SAF-T accounting data filed through the electronic channels; no tax registration fee applies for standard VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a 'check-the-box' or S-corporation-style pass-through election; classification follows the legal form of the SARL (a capital company) mandatorily subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax. Elections available: (1) Fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR – 95%-or-more owned Luxembourg-resident companies and certain EEA-company permanent establishments may elect to file on a consolidated taxable-result basis for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; the election must be supported by a written application filed with the ACD. (2) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for Luxembourg-established and financially integrated persons. (3) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) elections for intra-Community distance sales and import consignments, replacing local Luxembourg registration for those flows. (4) No hybrid-mismatch elections: rules implementing EU ATAD (Law of 21 December 2017) neutralize hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities through mandatory multilateral adjustments rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (exclusive of VAT) for the small-enterprise exemption threshold (Article 25b of the Luxembourg VAT Law), above which VAT registration becomes mandatory for locally established persons; EUR 10,000 Union-wide turnover for intra-Community distance-selling/OSS registration; non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis threshold – any taxable supply of goods or services deemed made in Luxembourg triggers immediate VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply qualifies for the 0% VAT rate when goods are exported by the seller or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union (Article 41 Luxembourg VAT Law; Article 146 Directive 2006/112/EC). Conditions: (i) physical departure of goods from EU territory, normally evidenced within 90 days of the supply; (ii) the export declaration is made in the name of the exporter/seller; (iii) the supplier receives concurrent documentary proof of the customs export procedure (MRN/ECS data) and international transport contract; (iv) for goods sold on ex-works, FOB, CIF or DDP terms, title and risk transfer must be verifiable; (v) export-related ancillary services (freight, handling, insurance, inspection) attached to exported goods may be zero-rated under Article 146(1)(c) of the VAT Directive, and offshore B2B services supplied to non-EU taxable customers are outside Luxembourg VAT scope (place-of-supply rules) and therefore effectively 0% if the recipient has no Luxembourg establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) accepted by the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (Administration des douanes et accises) through the ECS/ECS2 electronic export system, including the MRN and release-for-export date
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR/CIM waybill (road/rail) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical dispatch of goods to a non-EU destination, with consignee name and port or airport of unloading
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and payment terms evidencing the economic ownership transfer, including Incoterms and export contract documentation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 / bank payment confirmation evidencing the actual receipt and ultimate repatriation of export proceeds in convertible currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory crypto-stamping and no per-invoice API transmission to the tax administration (model differs from Italy's SdI). Mandatory PEPPOL-BIS electronic invoicing applies to B2G supplies to the Luxembourg central public administration since 1 March 2021. B2B and B2C e-invoicing remain voluntary, provided authenticity and readability requirements are met. Reporting obligations are periodic: VAT returns and EC Sales Lists (VIES) filed electronically through eTVA/MyGuichet.lu; annual SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) in XML format; and forward-looking implementation of EU ViDA/European Single Reporting (ESR) for transaction-level disclosure of intra-EU cross-border supplies, effective from calendar-year 2024/2025 reporting windows.

Luxembourg Customs and the AEDT may audit zero-rating claims at any time within the ordinary 5-year reassessment period (extended to 10 years in cases of fraud). If the documentary chain (customs MRN, transport title, remittance evidence) is incomplete or inconsistent, export zero-rating is withdrawn, the supply is recharacterized as domestic, output VAT is clawed back at 17% and statutory late-payment interest accrues from the original due date. Fraudulent export zero-rating – including forged transport documents, circular export/import transactions or carousel VAT fraud – additionally triggers administrative fines of up to double the tax evaded (capped at EUR 250,000), criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg tax fraud law (Law of 23 March 2016) with custodial sentences up to 5 years (up to 10 years for organized fraud), retroactive denial of input VAT deductions, joint and several liability of directors and tax representatives, and publication of the sanction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. Luxembourg is a Eurozone member state and imposes no exchange-control regime; Article 63 TFEU guarantees the free movement of capital and payments, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalized. Profit remittance is subject to the standard 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, but an EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption (10% shareholding, minimum 12-month holding period) removes withholding for qualifying EU corporate shareholders, and treaty relief applies for non-EU shareholders. There are no administrative approvals, currency allocation queues or repatriation ceilings; the SARL may freely invoice in EUR, USD, CNH or any convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory bank-account concentration requirement, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts in any currency; however, the BCL balance-of-payments statistical regime requires the resident credit institution to report each transaction above the reporting threshold (set at or near EUR 12,500) and to identify the underlying economic-purpose code, so the SARL must tag all cross-border wires with accurate purpose labels to avoid BCL data-quality queries.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem; monetary policy and foreign-exchange policy competence are vested in the European Central Bank (ECB), while the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises banking conduct, AML compliance and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. A CSSF-authorised or EEA-passported electronic-money institution (EMI) under PSD2 may hold minor operational float, but it cannot issue the statutory blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) required for SARL share-capital deposit, and major Luxembourg banks will not accept an offshore digital EMI as maintainer of the capital escrow or as primary settlement bank. For trade disbursements above EUR 50,000 and documentary LC workflows, the EMI rail is legally constrained by its absence of a full credit-institution licence and by correspondent-bank de-risking policies; therefore, an offline regulated credit institution remains mandatory for the SARL's anchor treasury relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL's statutory share capital must be wired by SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the Luxembourg bank's blocked account (compte de blocage) before notarial execution; the bank issues the blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) required by the Luxembourg notary, with funds released to the operational account only upon RCS registration. The remittance instruction should state the SARL's corporate name, the notarial deed reference and the BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment in equity capital; no prior administrative or central-bank approval is required, and no FX conversion is needed because the investment is made in EUR from the shareholder's own account.Three primary clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for EU suppliers, freight forwarders, customs and payroll, with day-ahead value dating; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for high-value TT wires in USD, EUR or CNH to Asian component suppliers, supported by invoice-backed payment instructions carrying full beneficiary-bank and SWIFT/BIC details, and automatically reported by the Luxembourg bank to BCL balance-of-payments statistics above the EUR 12,500 threshold; and (iii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit governed by UCP600 and ISBP745 for containerised component batches, issued by the Luxembourg issuing bank and advised or confirmed by a beneficiary-side bank in the supplier's jurisdiction. The ERP payment file must embed the ISIC 3091 activity reference and order numbers in the structured remittance field to accelerate customs clearance, reconciliation and BCL statistical classification.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY and EUR/CNH) traded bilaterally with a Luxembourg credit institution under ISDA documentation, limited to underlying import and export trade-flow hedging; the SARL, as an EMIR non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold (NFC-), is exempt from central clearing for FX forwards and only reports to a trade repository.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing USD/CNH for offshore renminbi exposure conversion into EUR, executed to hedge supplier payments invoiced in CNY where domestic deliverability is unavailable; subject to the same NFC- EMIR reporting-only treatment.FX swaps (spot-forward swap combinations between EUR, USD and CNH) for short-term liquidity and disbursement-date alignment, with physically settled leg obligations and no mandatory variation-margin posting under EMIR exemptions for physically settled FX forwards.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/CNY) used to swap the currency basis of ancillary term-loan liabilities against forecast USD and CNH revenue flows from North American and Asian distributors, structured under a Luxembourg-law-governed ISDA/CSA and executed bilaterally as an NFC-.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium feasibility. A CSSF-authorised or EEA-passported electronic-money institution (EMI) under PSD2 may hold minor operational float, but it cannot issue the statutory blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) required for SARL share-capital deposit, and major Luxembourg banks will not accept an offshore digital EMI as maintainer of the capital escrow or as primary settlement bank. For trade disbursements above EUR 50,000 and documentary LC workflows, the EMI rail is legally constrained by its absence of a full credit-institution licence and by correspondent-bank de-risking policies; therefore, an offline regulated credit institution remains mandatory for the SARL's anchor treasury relationship.

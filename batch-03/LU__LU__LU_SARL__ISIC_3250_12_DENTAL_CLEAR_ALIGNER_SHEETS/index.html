Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; online portals: myGuichet.lu, eTVA, eCDF

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL must first be registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) to obtain a business registration number. Tax identification numbers are then automatically issued: a national tax number (NIF) from the ACD and a separate VAT identification number from the AEDT for taxable persons. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment must apply directly to the ACD (NIF) and the AEDT (VAT) using the VAT registration form (Formulaire 1), typically requiring a local approved tax representative for non-EU entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - SARL is a fully taxable resident company subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (communal trade tax), and the solidarity surcharge on worldwide income. Tax residence is based on place of effective management or statutory seat in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Luxembourg allows certain elections under specific regimes: VAT grouping (assujetti unique), fiscal unity for corporate income tax and municipal business tax (at least 95% ownership), and the 80% exemption for intellectual property income (IP box) are available. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply, and no elective avoidance of those rules is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover for small business exemption, excluding certain cross-border supplies); no statutory threshold for mandatory registration of established businesses or non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) supply of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or on behalf of the customer; (2) intra-Community supply of goods where the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another Member State; (3) direct export of dental clear aligner sheets when the documentary evidence demonstrates physical departure from the EU within the prescribed time limits. Services physically performed on goods that are subsequently exported (e.g., processing) may be zero-rated under specific conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are NOT required. E-invoicing is permitted subject to B2B customer consent. Mandatory e-invoicing in PEPPOL format for B2G supplies exceeding EUR 10,000 (for eligible public contracts). Periodic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and annual recapitulative statements (listings) of intra-Community supplies are filed via eTVA. SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) must be electronically submitted in XML format upon request by the tax authorities.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary scrutiny by the Luxembourg VAT authorities. If documentation is insufficient or the export is not proven, VAT is retrospectively assessed at the standard rate, plus interest (default interest at 0.45% per month). Fraudulent claims (including false or forged export documentation) trigger penalties ranging from 10% to 200% of the evaded tax, monetary fines, criminal prosecution (imprisonment up to 5 years), and debarment from VAT valid registration. Directors may be held personally liable in cases of intentional misconduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; Luxembourg applies no exchange controls, no surrender obligations, no prior authorization, and no quantitative limits for current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, shareholder loan repayments, royalties, and trade settlements are free, subject only to (i) domestic withholding tax rules on dividends (15% standard, fully reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where a qualifying parent holds at least 10% of the capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period, or under Luxembourg tax treaties), (ii) EU/international sanctions and asset-freeze screening, and (iii) mandatory statistical reporting to the BCL for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500; no regulatory authorization is required for inbound equity transfer into a Luxembourg SARL, but the receiving bank must verify the capital increase notarial deed and the RCS update.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, endorsement, or retention rules for export proceeds; a Luxembourg SARL receiving dental aligner revenues in USD, CHF, or other currencies may retain the funds in a multicurrency account, convert at market rate, or hedge them freely. The only compulsory requirement is that every resident enterprise reports cross-border receipts to the BCL for balance-of-payments statistical purposes through its bank, applying the BPM6/ECB Regulation (EU) No 184/2005 coding grid; there is no minimum conversion ratio and no BCL purchase obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); foreign exchange and capital movement policy is delegated to the Ministry of Finance (Trésor) and the European Central Bank within the Eurosystem; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the competent AML/CFT supervisor for credit institutions and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary payments channel only, but legally insufficient as the sole treasury banking rail: a Luxembourg SARL may open a business account with a licenced e-money institution (e.g., a Luxembourg PFS or a passporting EU EMI under Directive 2009/110/EC) for pay-in/pay-out of volume card transactions and international supplier payments, provided that the EMI is authorized in the EEA and registered with the CSSF; however, an EMI cannot grant credit, cannot hold client deposits in the legal sense, and its safeguarding arrangement (segregated pool at a credit institution) creates third-party risk that fails the corporate treasury's liquidity-safety test. The finance ministry and BCL reporting obligations are not automatically discharged by the EMI; all cross-border reporting must be executed through the settlement bank, which makes the tier-1 credit institution account mandatory. For recurring B2C/Stripe card settlements, a payment institution acquiring partner is acceptable if settlement is directly into the SARL's BIL or BCEE account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from the US/EU parent is executed through a standard SWIFT MT103 SEPA or MT202/202COV wire into the SARL's euro account at a Luxembourg tier-1 bank. The banking reference must contain the legal entity name (LU SARL), the notarial deed reference of the capital increase, and the BCL balance-of-payments purpose code 'S301' (Direct investment in a reporting enterprise - equity) or 'S331' for a shareholder loan, as applicable. The settlement bank will request, before crediting, an RCS excerpt showing the capital increase and the deposit certificate issued by the bank itself on day one; the account cannot be used for operational expenses until the notary certifies the capital subscription. For payment platforms (Stripe), the parent may also fund the SARL's Stripe balance via a linked payout bank account using MT103 with reference 'Stripe funding - aligned with BCL code S301/S331'; nevertheless, for FATF compliance, the accounting entry must match the purpose code and be supported by the equity transfer agreement.Trade disbursements for thermoplastic pellets, PETG/TPU sheets, packaging materials, and laboratory consumables are routed via: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank supplier payments, with BOP purpose code 'S810' for goods imports and 'S891' for services, cross-referenced by the supplier's commercial invoice and bill of lading; (2) commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 when the supplier requires an unconditional payment undertaking; (3) SWIFT MT760 standby LCs for security obligations; (4) SEPA Credit Transfer for EU suppliers; (5) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for urgent small disbursements, e.g., testing laboratory fees or customs duties; (6) card acquiring rails for B2C/B2B2C sales: Stripe settlement into the SARL's EUR settlement account via a Luxembourg or Belgian acquiring PSP. All outbound payments require online confirmation of the invoice, proof of goods receipt, and verification of the beneficiary against EU sanctions lists. For intra-group management fees and royalty payments to the parent, the SARL must maintain an intercompany services agreement and a transfer pricing file in accordance with Luxembourg arm's-length legislation (Article 56bis LIR).EUR/USD vanilla outright forwards (spot-deferred and fixed-date, up to 12 months) under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Luxembourg-authorized credit institution or an EEA investment firm, subject to EMIR clearing and trade repository reporting obligations; designated as hedge accounting under IFRS 9 if the SARL qualifies.EUR/USD cross-currency swaps (plain vanilla, fully collateralized under an ISDA CSA) to convert USD surplus from US-oriented dental aligner sales into EUR without transfer restrictions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD against non-convertible or restricted-delivery currencies of the target export markets (e.g., CNH, INR, BRL) where physical delivery is legally unavailable; NDFs are permitted only with an EU-authorized FX counterparty and fully documented against underlying trade or intercompany exposure; the notional must not exceed 110% of forecast aligned-practice cash flows in the relevant currency per the Luxembourg CSSF market-conduct expectations.FX options (vanilla European call/put and zero-cost collars) for protecting payroll and dividend cash flows, allowed for hedging, not for speculative or leveraging treasury positions; regulated under the same EMIR/CSDR framework, requiring daily valuation and margining.Foreign exchange spot contracts and banked automated dealing lines for monthly profit remittance and VAT payments; all hedging is executed within the documented treasury policy of the SARL, authorized by a board resolution, and reported to the BCL as part of the bank's balance-of-payments filing.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Identification RCSL registration number, Luxembourg tax identification number (NIN), legal name, legal form (SARL), and registered office address in Luxembourg. A non-resident company with a Luxembourg permanent establishment (PE) files Form 750 using the PE's Luxembourg address and the foreign corporate identification data; a company without a Luxembourg PE is not subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax and must not file. Late filing: administrative fine of up to EUR 250 per return; formal notice followed by a surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax; late payment: default interest of 0.6% per commenced month (7.2% per annum) plus enforcement costs; fraudulent non-filing is prosecuted as tax fraud with fines up to EUR 66,500 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Non-resident filers select the limited tax liability category; the foreign tax residence certificate must be attached where treaty relief is claimed. Late filing: administrative fine of up to EUR 250 per return; formal notice followed by a surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax; late payment: default interest of 0.6% per commenced month (7.2% per annum) plus enforcement costs; fraudulent non-filing is prosecuted as tax fraud with fines up to EUR 66,500 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Line 3 – Net commercial result The accounting profit or loss before tax per the statutory annual accounts approved by the shareholders' meeting; financial statements must be filed with the RCSL within one month of approval. Non-resident PE: report the PE's separate profit and loss statement prepared under Luxembourg GAAP or a reconcilable standard (IAS/IFRS with adjustments). Same add-backs apply to the Luxembourg PE; recharge of head-office costs is deductible only when supported by arm's length allocation records. Line 5 – Deduction adjustments Tax-exempt income: qualifying dividends and capital gains under the participation exemption; 80% of qualifying net IP Box income; and municipal business tax actually incurred. Deductions are available to the PE only where the PE holds the participation or exploits the qualifying intellectual property through its Luxembourg operations. Late filing: administrative fine of up to EUR 250 per return; formal notice followed by a surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax; late payment: default interest of 0.6% per commenced month (7.2% per annum) plus enforcement costs; fraudulent non-filing is prosecuted as tax fraud with fines up to EUR 66,500 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Box 6 – Loss carryforwards Unrelieved tax losses brought forward under the 17-year rule; the set-off is denied where the company has ceased its actual activity and the operation is deemed artificial or the shareholding continuity test is abused. Late filing: administrative fine of up to EUR 250 per return; formal notice followed by a surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax; late payment: default interest of 0.6% per commenced month (7.2% per annum) plus enforcement costs; fraudulent non-filing is prosecuted as tax fraud with fines up to EUR 66,500 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 — aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions above this threshold trigger the mandatory Local File obligation; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000 under EU DAC4. Below the threshold, a simplified Annex 6 declaration is filed with Form 750.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply sectoral Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE/Barbados style; nevertheless, Article 6 of the Luxembourg Tax Adaptation Law (ATAD GAAR), the judicial doctrine of fraus legis, and the Principal Purpose Test under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI Article 29(9)) require genuine economic substance. A dental clear aligner manufacturing SARL must maintain real production facilities, qualified operational and quality-control staff, active local management decisions, physical bank accounts, and substance proportionate to its contractual functions (manufacturing, sourcing, IP exploitation, and financing). Without such substance, the ACD may deny treaty benefits, the 80% IP Box exemption, loss carryforwards, or recharacterize intra-group payments as constructive dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and filing fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under the Luxembourg General Tax Act: fine up to EUR 66,500 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years; civil recovery of evaded tax with monthly default interest of 0.6% and a surcharge of up to 10%; the company's tax clearance certificate may be revoked, rendering it ineligible for public tenders and regulated licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent returns or divert corporate assets to evade tax incur joint and several civil liability for the company's tax shortfall under the Luxembourg Companies Law (action en responsabilité), personal criminal fines and imprisonment, travel bans and asset freezes imposed by the investigating judge, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg entities.
Shell company / artificial structure

Statutory sanction: Disapplication of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test and the GAAR; full taxation of the diverted income; reversal of the 80% IP Box exemption; denial of loss carryforwards; administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 under DAC6 abusive arrangement disclosure rules; possible strike-off from the Luxembourg trade register.

Director personal liability: Nominee and executive directors who knowingly enable the artificial interposition are liable jointly and severally for the restored taxes and interest; they face criminal prosecution for aiding tax fraud, suspension of management functions, travel bans during investigation, and personal guarantee of tax debts where assets have been stripped from the company.
Transfer pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment under Article 56a LIR; recharacterization of the excess as constructive dividend subject to 15% withholding tax; default interest of 0.6% per month; additional assessment surcharge of up to 10%; if documentation is not produced, the ACD may use estimated assessments and shift the burden of proof; separate fine of up to EUR 250 for missing annexes.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally set non-arm's length terms to strip the Luxembourg manufacturing margin breach their fiduciary duty and may be held personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall; aggravated conduct triggers criminal tax evasion charges, travel bans, and court-ordered capital reinstatement from the directors' personal assets.
AML and payment-chain compliance failure

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg Criminal Code money laundering offences: imprisonment from 1 to 10 years and fines from EUR 12,500 up to EUR 1,250,000 depending on habitualness; RBE register violations carry fines up to EUR 1,250,000 and the court may suspend distributions; payment processors may terminate the SARL's merchant agreement and the entity may be barred from holding regulated authorizations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process or conceal illicit proceeds face personal criminal liability with custodial sentences, travel bans and asset freezes by the Luxembourg Public Prosecutor, civil liability for compensation to affected third parties, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Luxembourg company for up to 10 years.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), electronically administered by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the supervision of the Ministry of Justice; e-filing portal: rcs.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 180–190 (SARL regime), Articles 7, 9 and 26–28 (formation, subscription and legal personality), together with the Law of 19 December 2020 on digitalisation of company law and the Grand-Ducal Regulation on the electronic transmission of RCS filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif / Articles of Association (Statuts) executed as a notarial deed or as an electronic private deed bearing qualified electronic signatures, filed via the e-RCS registration form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must issue share capital of at least EUR 12,500; the whole capital must be fully subscribed before formation; each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value at subscription. Cash contributions must be transferred to a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the S.à r.l. en formation, and the deposit must be evidenced by a banker's certificate (attestation bancaire) attached to the RCS filing. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety conformity certificate from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGIDS) for the registered office and any warehouse space; structural load inspection by an approved engineer confirming floor and rack capacity for bagged urea/potash; and occupational health-and-safety clearance by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM), comprising the statutory workplace risk assessment, first-aid equipment check, and verification that no ammonium-nitrate-based explosive products are within the permitted trade scope.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must issue share capital of at least EUR 12,500; the whole capital must be fully subscribed before formation; each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value at subscription. Cash contributions must be transferred to a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the S.à r.l. en formation, and the deposit must be evidenced by a banker's certificate (attestation bancaire) attached to the RCS filing. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration; VAT: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); Portal: MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned SARL is incorporated through a Luxembourg notary and registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the entity is automatically assigned a Luxembourg national registration number and a corporate tax identification number (NIF) by the ACD. For VAT, the SARL must file Form 105 with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu or the eCDF platform to obtain a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number. The SARL must maintain a registered office in Luxembourg and appoint a VAT representative only if it is a non-established taxable person. Tax registration must be completed prior to commencing economic activity, and employer registration is required for any staff hiring.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The foreign-owned SARL is incorporated through a Luxembourg notary and registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the entity is automatically assigned a Luxembourg national registration number and a corporate tax identification number (NIF) by the ACD. For VAT, the SARL must file Form 105 with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu or the eCDF platform to obtain a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number. The SARL must maintain a registered office in Luxembourg and appoint a VAT representative only if it is a non-established taxable person. Tax registration must be completed prior to commencing economic activity, and employer registration is required for any staff hiring.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. A SARL is mandatorily classified as a fully taxable capital company subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available elections include the Luxembourg group fiscal consolidation regime (intégration fiscale) for 95%-owned subsidiaries, the VAT group election for closely related persons, and the option to apply the micro-company VAT special scheme under certain conditions. Hybrid mismatch rules follow EU ATAD and are mandatory rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general annual turnover exemption for established taxable persons; registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable economic activity in Luxembourg. Non-established suppliers are subject to the EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance selling and digital services threshold, above which Luxembourg VAT becomes due unless the supplier already has a Luxembourg establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated where the goods are physically shipped or transported by the supplier, or on its behalf, within the statutory period (within 90 days of supply usually evidenced). The SARL must obtain and retain transport documents proving the goods left EU territory. For intra-EU supplies to a VAT-registered customer, zero-rating applies if the customer's VAT number is valid and the supplier files an EU Sales List. Services directly related to export of goods may also be zero-rated under Luxembourg practice.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

B2G invoices must be submitted electronically via Peppol. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted and must ensure authenticity and integrity; real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is not currently mandatory for B2B transactions. VAT invoices must be issued within 15 days of the supply and retained for 10 years. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing will be phased in under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, with Luxembourg aligning to the EU-wide timeline (by 2028-2030).

The AEDT and customs authorities can retrospectively deny zero-rating if lack of evidence of export is established. Penalties include immediate VAT assessment, interest at 8% per month or pro-rata, fines up to EUR 20,000 per invoice, and criminal sanctions including up to 8 years imprisonment for fraudulent schemes. Proof of refund may require cash repatriation within statutory periods (typically the underlying sale conditions); failure to repatriate can be treated as an indicator of sham export, triggering anti-abuse restatement and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender and domestic settlement currency. Luxembourg maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions, with no exchange-control licensing or prior-authorisation regime for resident SARLs. Inbound equity subscription, shareholder loans, trade payments, dividend distributions, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are freely permitted unless specifically blocked by EU sanctions or prudential anti-money-laundering measures. Profit remittance is operationally unrestricted; the only friction is tax-based, namely Luxembourg withholding tax of 15% on dividends, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parent companies, 5% or 15% under selected double-tax treaties, and 0% on qualifying interest and royalty payments under the EU Interest and Royalties Directive. No exchange-control approval is required for the pass-through of commodity-trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg maintains no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency into EUR, and no fixed repatriation timeline for receivables arising from international wholesale trade. There is no obligation to hold export earnings in a designated domestic account. The only applicable requirements are (i) complete and accurate identification of the underlying commercial transaction for balance-of-payments statistical reporting, performed by the corporate bank on behalf of the BCL under BCL Regulation 2003/2, and (ii) full retention of transport, contract, and invoice documentation for five years for AML/CFT and tax audit purposes. Where trade receivables are denominated in USD, the SARL may freely convert or retain such funds in a foreign-currency account with a Luxembourg credit institution.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem; banking and financial-sector prudential supervision is administered by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Because Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State, monetary policy and euro foreign-exchange operations are set by the European Central Bank (ECB), and the BCL implements the ECB General Documentation, regulates balance-of-payments statistics, and enforces EU capital-movement rules under Articles 63-66 TFEU.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Under PSD2, a European Economic Area (EEA)-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may passport payment services into Luxembourg and hold corporate accounts for an LU SARL without a local branch. However, for the stated banking AML risk tier of 'Medium_Risk_Enhanced' for wholesale agrochemical commodities, most offshore digital EMIs decline the onboarding because they (i) do not support documentary instruments such as SWIFT MT700 letters of credit or MT760 standby L/Cs, (ii) impose strict sectoral restrictions on fertiliser/commodity merchants due to trade-based money-laundering exposure, and (iii) lack capacity for the BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting. EMIs are viable only as a supplementary collection/operation account for small EU-counterparty SEPA receipts, never as the primary clearing rail for bulk urea and potash trade settlements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscription must be wired before notarisation into a dedicated blocked (escrow) euro account in the name of the SARL 'en formation' or the notary's client ledger, with remittance field stating 'Subscription of share capital — LU SARL [name]'; the credit institution issues a blockage certificate (blocage de fonds) confirming the minimum EUR 12,000, which the notary files with the RCS. For shareholder loans or additional paid-in capital, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code BCL-COMP 'T02 — Shares and other equity (capital contribution)' for equity and 'T11 — Loans received' for shareholder debt; the proceeds must originate from a bank account held in the shareholder's name with source-of-funds documents attached. If the shareholder remits in USD, the receiving bank converts at the ECB reference rate and may require a copy of the FX-forward confirmations for hedging; SEPA Credit Transfer (pacs.008) is acceptable for EU shareholders. No BCL approval is required, but the bank must identify the nature and counterparty country for monthly balance-of-payments reporting.Primary clearing rail for high-value bulk fertiliser charter payments is SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlements and documentary collection (MT400 series), with SWIFT MT700 for import letters of credit and MT760 for standby L/Cs used as performance guarantees on charter parties. EUR-denominated counterparties within the EEA settle via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (pacs.008) up to EUR 100,000 per transaction and via TARGET2 (pacs.009 FIN) above that limit; USD-denominated settlements are cleared through the correspondent networks of the Luxembourg credit institution over CHIPS/Fedwire, with value-date controls aligned to vessel nomination and demurrage deadlines. All outbound disbursements must carry the BCL balance-of-payments purpose code (e.g., 'T04 — Goods (wholesale trade)' for urea and potash purchases, 'T12 — Freight and other transport services' for charter-party hire), and must match invoice, transport, and inspection documentation retained for five years. Commercial letters of credit must be issued in irrevocable, confirmed (if possible) form under UCP 600 with Incoterms 2020 (CFR, CIF, or FOB as contracted) and a maximum tenor of 90 days from B/L date.Deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/GBP spot and outright forward contracts (tenors typically 1, 3, 6, and 12 months) with Luxembourg credit institutions or EU-domiciled dealers, exempt from mandatory margining below the EMIR clearing threshold.FX swaps (swap of principal and interest between EUR and USD) for managing settlement-date mismatches on trade flows, subject to EMIR trade reporting and, above clearing thresholds, mandatory clearing through a CCP.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) between EUR and USD for hedging long-term shareholder loans or trade-finance rollover risk, permitted as OTC derivatives under EMIR and MiFIR with confirmation under the ISDA Master Agreement.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD European-style FX options (puts and calls) purchased for downside protection on fertiliser receivables and freight costs; writing of uncovered options is restricted under Luxembourg prudential guidance and generally not permitted for non-financial corporate end-users.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing currencies of non-convertible or restricted emerging markets (e.g., certain African or Central Asian settlement currencies for potash exports) are permissible only for actual commercial underlying exposure, documented with a signed underlying sale/purchase contract, and subject to EMIR risk-mitigation requirements, ECB reporting, and CSSF prudential review; deliverable forwards are preferred and mandatory for all convertibile currencies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File obligations are triggered where the Luxembourg entity is part of a multinational group with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750,000,000 and has controlled transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. A Country-by-Country Report is required where the ultimate parent's consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to OFC standards, but the ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 6 LIR), the Luxembourg abuse-of-law doctrine, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test require genuine economic substance. A Luxembourg SARL engaged in wholesale fertilizer commodity trading must maintain physical presence, local management, administrative staff, and substantive decision-making functions. Without such substance, the tax administration may deny treaty benefits, disallow the participation exemption, recharacterise the entity as a shell, and treat it as managed and controlled elsewhere.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fiscal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, late-payment interest, late-filing fines, and an additional penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax. Criminal liability under Articles 396-398 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law carries a fine up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; aggravated cases can increase imprisonment to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or procure a fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, may be subjected to criminal imprisonment, asset freezes, travel bans, and may be disqualified from future directorships in Luxembourg.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the structure, denial of treaty benefits, retrospective application of 15% dividend withholding tax, clawback of the participation exemption or IP Box benefit, and GAAR penalties of up to 100% of the tax advantage.

Director personal liability: Directors who shelter behind a sham entity may be subject to civil restitution of the tax shortfall, personal liability if the SARL is insolvent, travel restrictions and, in criminal abuse cases, imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Ex officio upward adjustment of taxable profit to an arm's-length amount, discretionary assessment, a withholding-type penalty on the increased tax liability, and an administrative fine up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer-pricing documentation may be held civilly liable for the additional tax, exposed to prosecution for tax fraud, and subject to travel bans and disqualification from board seats in Luxembourg.
AML / Cross-Border Payment Integrity

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Luxembourg AML framework (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) is punishable by criminal fines up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment of up to ten years for money laundering; regulatory fines for suspicious-transaction reporting failures are substantially elevated.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally manage the payment rails or knowingly ignore red flags may be criminally indicted for money-laundering, subjected to travel bans, asset confiscation, and permanent exclusion from financial or corporate management roles.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS).

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, in particular Articles 710-1 to 710-23 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée/S.à r.l.); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies, as amended.

Formation / constitutional filings

Demande d'inscription au Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés together with the signed and dated articles of association (statuts) and capital payment certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The registered capital of a Luxembourg S.à r.l. must be at least EUR 12,000 and must be fully subscribed at incorporation. At least 25% of each cash share must be paid up before registration; the balance remains callable under the articles. The paid-in portion must be evidenced by a Luxembourg bank certificate. No separate statutory escrow account is mandatory, but if the articles require immediate payment beyond 25%, the notary or bank must hold such amounts in a blocked account until the RCS registration extract is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: CGDIS (Corps grand-ducal et d'incendie et de secours) fire safety certification for the production area; structural load inspection certificate for the building floors by a certified engineer; ITM (Inspection du travail et des mines) workplace clearance covering machine safety, CE conformity of equipment, occupational health, and employee working conditions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; common filing portal: guichet.lu (MyGuichet).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Luxembourg SARL in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) to obtain a 16-digit business identification number and a Luxembourg TIN. Step 2: Register with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Step 3: Register for VAT with the AEDT using the national single registration form, providing constitutional documents, RCS extract, proof of registered office, and identity of authorised signatories. Step 4: Receive a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits) from the AEDT used for domestic supplies and intra-Community transactions. Non-resident foreign entities carrying on taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly through the AEDT's simplified non-established VAT registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is an opaque, fully taxable resident company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no pass-through is available; worldwide income is taxable with treaty and unilateral relief for foreign source income; no tax-exempt zone applies to a standard SARL.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists under Luxembourg law. A SARL may elect to join a Luxembourg fiscal unity (group taxation) with resident subsidiaries under Article 164bis LIR. Under Article 175bis LIR, a joint venture company may opt for transparency if owned at least 95% by two resident parent companies from different EU member states or treaty partners, subject to conditions. Anti-hybrid elections are not discretionary; EU ATAD rules override inconsistent treatments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover exemption threshold ('franchise') for established small businesses; non-resident suppliers have no de minimis threshold for VAT registration in Luxembourg when making taxable supplies in Luxembourg. Intra-Community distance sales to Luxembourg consumers exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar year triggers Luxembourg VAT registration; B2B supplies may trigger registration if the customer is required to self-assess under reverse charge mechanisms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the seller or purchaser outside the European Union, and to intra-Community supplies to a purchaser holding a valid EU VAT identification number, provided the goods physically leave the territory and evidence is kept in the prescribed export file. Services supplied to businesses outside the EU are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule; certain ancillary services are zero-rated as exports of services where the recipient is established outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export summary declaration lodged via Luxembourg Customs / Single Administrative Document or e-Export system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note (showing dispatch from Luxembourg and arrival in third country)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing payment by the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice matching the customs declaration and transport evidence
  • Purchase order or contract proving ownership transfer and export sale
  • Proof of the purchaser's VAT identification number and status for intra-Community zero-rated supplies

Luxembourg mandates B2G electronic invoicing through a PEPPOL-compliant platform for public procurement; no general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice stamping applies. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via MyGuichet; invoices must be issued within the statutory timeframe, retained for ten years, and contain all VAT invoice requirements under Articles 5 to 9 of the Luxembourg VAT law.

Zero-rated export claims are audited on the basis of complete, coherent and consistent documentary evidence; missing or inconsistent proof results in VAT reassessment at the standard rate, late payment interest, and administrative fines up to the amount of the tax evaded. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims are treated as tax fraud under Article 68 of the Luxembourg VAT law, triggering penalties of up to ten times the evaded tax and criminal fines plus imprisonment. The AEDT may claw back unjustified zero-rating for up to five years after the chargeable event and ten years in cases of fraud, with succession of penalties and publication of assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current account and capital account. Formal exchange controls were abolished by the Law of 23 December 1988, and Luxembourg is bound by the free movement of capital under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is fully convertible; there is no prior-authorisation regime, no foreign-investment screening, and no BCL licensing gate for remittances. Profit and dividend repatriation is subject only to corporate-law formalities: distribution requires an ordinary shareholders' resolution and the SARL's net assets must not fall below subscribed capital plus non-distributable reserves (Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, Article 710-1 for SARLs, as amended). No tax-clearance certificate is required for dividend remittance, although a 15% Luxembourg withholding tax applies unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to repatriate export proceeds into euro. An exporter may freely hold, hedge, or deploy foreign-currency receipts. However, the SARL must maintain its statutory books and prepare annual accounts in EUR under Luxembourg GAAP (Lux GAAP); realised and unrealised FX differences are recognised in profit or loss. Luxembourg banks report anonymised balance-of-payments statistics (BPM6 classification) to the BCL for Eurosystem purposes, which is a statistical reporting obligation of the bank, not a regulatory approval or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem framework alongside the European Central Bank (ECB). The BCL, established by the Law of 23 December 1998, is the competent monetary, payment-systems and balance-of-payments statistical authority. Anti-money-laundering and prudential conduct supervision of credit institutions and payment/electronic-money institutions is vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended, and CSSF Circular 18/698. Significant Luxembourg banks are subject to joint prudential supervision by the ECB through the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM – supplementary treasury rail only. A CSSF-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institution (EMI) operating in Luxembourg under PSD2 may onboard the SARL quickly and is operationally capable of pooling Platform Pay (Amazon marketplace) payouts, providing multi-currency IBANs and making SEPA/MT103 domestic and cross-border payments. Legal constraints: an EMI may not extend credit, may not issue commercial letters of credit or bank guarantees, and is not a prudentially acceptable venue for equity-capital escrow or significant trade disbursements. Third-country (non-EU) offshore EMIs without an EU passport or a Luxembourg branch are legally barred from opening accounts for a Luxembourg corporate. The SARL must therefore anchor its treasury in a fully licensed credit institution and may use an EMI only as a secondary collections/wallet conduit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the SARL's minimum share capital of EUR 12,394.68 is deposited before incorporation into a blocked Capital Escrow Account (compte séquestre) opened by the founders at the chosen Luxembourg bank; the bank releases the funds to the Operational Account upon registration in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS). Subsequent injections arrive as SWIFT MT103 wires denominated in EUR from the shareholder's home bank to the SARL's LU-format IBAN. The Remittance Information (field 70) must state: 'Share capital contribution – SARL [legal name] – deed dated [date]' so that the bank classifies the flow for BCL/ECB balance-of-payments statistics as direct-investment equity capital (BPM6 instrument E1). Before crediting, the bank verifies the funder's identity and source of funds, and requires the notarised contribution deed and updated RBE excerpt. No BCL foreign-exchange approval is required; credits are typically effected within one to three business days.Three-tier clearing design: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments to suppliers of silicone polymer, injection/compression moulds, pigments, and packaging – each wire is referenced to the underlying purchase order and invoice with Incoterms discipline and archived in the SARL's AML file; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Luxembourg bank in favour of overseas suppliers for large raw-material consignments – typically 100% at-sight or deferred 60–120 days, payable against compliant shipping documents (bill of lading, packing list, inspection certificate); (iii) Platform Pay (Amazon Marketplace) receipts settle as biweekly SWIFT MT103/SEPA batches into the EUR operational IBAN; the SARL may either accept Amazon's proprietary Currency Converter (which embeds an FX margin) or request payout in USD and execute a matching EUR forward with the Luxembourg bank, which is materially cheaper for sustained Platform Pay volumes.Deliverable FX Forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) – tenors up to 24 months under ISDA/CSA documentation; available from Luxembourg credit institutions without BCL authorisation; EMIR clearing and reporting obligations limited to the non-financial counterparty (NFC-) hedging exemption.FX Swaps / Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) – principal and interest exchange structures aligned to trade-receivables financing and working-capital funding.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) (EUR/CNH, EUR/INR, EUR/TRY) – permitted hedging vehicles for payables in onshore-inconvertible currencies, settled in EUR or USD on a net basis.Plain Vanilla FX Options (EUR call / USD put and inverse pairs) – premium-protected budget-rate certainty for large Platform Pay receipts and material capex purchase contracts.

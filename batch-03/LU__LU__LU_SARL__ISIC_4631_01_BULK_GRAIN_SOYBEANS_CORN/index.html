Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), under the authority of the Luxembourg Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended), Articles 710-1 to 710-26 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), including notarial incorporation, minimum capital, contributions, and management; read together with the Loi du 19 décembre 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and annual accounts.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) containing the articles of association (statuts), filed electronically by the notary with the RCS, followed by mandatory publication in the RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must have a minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited with a Luxembourg or EU credit institution and evidenced by a bank attestation to the notary. The deposit is customarily blocked (compte bloqué) until RCS registration; after registration the funds may be used for corporate purposes. No separate statutory escrow account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Office occupancy only – fire safety is governed by the Grand-Ducal Regulation on workplace safety under the Luxembourg Labour Code; the rented or domiciliated office must be covered by the building's valid occupancy certificate. No civil-defense structural inspection is required for standard low-rise commercial office premises. The domiciliation or lease agreement must confirm the fire safety compliance of the hosting building.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must have a minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited with a Luxembourg or EU credit institution and evidenced by a bank attestation to the notary. The deposit is customarily blocked (compte bloqué) until RCS registration; after registration the funds may be used for corporate purposes. No separate statutory escrow account is mandated.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the subscribers hereby adopt the Articles of Association, irrevocably subscribe for, and accept the issued shares of the Company as set out in the incorporation deed, all shares to be fully paid up and entered in the shareholders' register. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the registered office of the Company is established at [address], Luxembourg, Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, and is designated as the statutory seat for legal and tax purposes. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the initial Manager (Gérant) shall act as legal representative, with the power to sign alone, subject to the articles. RESOLVED THAT the Manager is instructed to complete all filings, publications, and registrations before the Luxembourg Business Registers and to open the corporate bank account.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – the commercial model is agency and wholesale brokerage of agricultural commodities (ISIC 4631) without owned storage, silos, processing, or physical handling; no general industrial license is required in Luxembourg for this trading business.

Category: No operating license required for the pure brokerage or wholesale intermediation activity. If the company later adds owned grain silos or transshipment facilities, an operating authorization (autorisation d'exploitation) under the Luxembourg Classified Establishments Law of 31 January 1996 (commodo/incommodo) would become mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Direct Tax Administration (portal: Luxembourg eCDF); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT and Registration Duties Authority (portal: eTVA/MyGuichet.lu); Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for the entity matricule; single state portal: myGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SARL incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and deposit it with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); obtain the 15-digit business registration number (matricule), which serves as the primary national corporate tax identification. Step 2: Within 15 days of commencing taxable activity, file VAT registration form 640 (or 645 for simplified procedure) with the AEDT, thereby obtaining a separate VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. Step 3: File the direct tax census declaration (registre fiscal) with the ACD, including intended commercial activities (ISIC 4631 wholesale of agricultural raw materials); the ACD assigns the corporate income tax file reference. Step 4: Complete the beneficial ownership registration (RBE) and the AEDT/ACD confirmation for customs (EORI) if exporting or importing physical grain consignments. Step 5: For non-EU-resident intermediaries, a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative is required, with joint and several liability for VAT.

A Luxembourg-resident SARL is a separate legal and taxable person subject to corporate income tax on worldwide profits (after PE carve-outs and participation exemption), municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and VAT. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg-resident SARL is a separate legal and taxable person subject to corporate income tax on worldwide profits (after PE carve-outs and participation exemption), municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and VAT. Pass-through treatment is unavailable; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions, and the SARL remains the sole debtor of indirect and direct taxes.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. Available elections include: (1) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law of 12 February 1979; (2) corporate income tax fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for entities in a 95% ownership chain; (3) small-enterprise VAT franchise exemption under Article 57 for turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000; (4) option for standard VAT taxation when falling under the franchise; (5) election to apply the participation exemption regime for qualifying shareholdings under Article 166 LIR; and (6) compliance with EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules with mandatory notification of mismatches affecting cross-border grain trading or financing structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 in annual taxable turnover (Article 57 small-enterprise VAT exemption); registration is compulsory without any threshold for importers of goods into Luxembourg, for traders whose customers supply the VAT number, and for intra-Community acquisitions of goods exceeding EUR 10,000 per calendar year. A bulk grain and soybean wholesale SARL undertaking active cross-border trading is expected to exceed the threshold in the first month of activity and must register before any taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 54 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, sales of bulk soybeans, corn, and other agricultural commodities qualify for the 0% (exempt with right of deduction) rate only when: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller, the buyer, or a third party acting on their behalf within 90 days from the transfer of the power of disposal; (ii) the export is evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration (SAD) and transport documents; (iii) intra-Community supplies are made to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number included in the VIES system, with goods physically leaving Luxembourg into another Member State; (iv) ancillary services (vessel charter brokerage, warehousing, fumigation, inspection, and freight) are directly linked to the exported goods and are supplied to the non-resident principal; and (v) Incoterms (e.g., FOB, CIF, EXW) clearly allocate the outbound transport to a destination outside the EU. Mere paper transfers of grain while goods remain in a Luxembourg or EU warehouse do not qualify as exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) containing the MRN number
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/air waybill) evidencing the destination port outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103 with LC conformity for the physical grain sale
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract with full buyer details and Incoterms
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) and Intrastat declaration for EU intra-Community deliveries
  • Certificate of origin (EUR.1/COO) and quality/weight certificates where contractually required

Electronic invoicing via PEPPOL BIS 3.0 is mandatory for all invoices addressed to Luxembourg public-sector bodies since 1 January 2024. B2B electronic invoicing remains voluntary (Factur-X/UBL formats allowed), with mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing expected after the EU ViDA framework enters into force (targeted from 2028 onward). No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control engine, and no digital stamping are currently activated in Luxembourg; VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically via the eTVA portal.

The AEDT may audit zero-rating claims for five years from the end of the relevant tax year, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or tax evasion; unsupported zero-rating is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply at 17% VAT, triggering a clawback of the deducted input VAT, late-payment interest at 0.8% per month, and administrative fines. Fraudulent export claims are criminal offences under Article 65 of the VAT Law, punishable by imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 250,000, with the manager and VAT representative jointly and severally liable; OLAF and EU anti-fraud proceedings may lead to additional penalties, exclusion from export simplification schemes, and enhanced TP/CIT audits where the grain trade is booked as a low-risk commodity pass-through.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member. The euro is fully convertible; both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under the EU Treaty’s free movement of capital provisions and the OECD Code of Liberalisation. No central bank exchange-control approval is required for profit distribution, dividend remittances, or capital repatriation, provided beneficial-ownership and substance requirements under Luxembourg tax rules are satisfied. Trade-related payments (imports/exports) are settled freely in any convertible currency through SEPA/TARGET2/SWIFT. The only formalities are statistical reporting to BCL for balance-of-payments purposes when transactions exceed certain thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement applies in Luxembourg. Export proceeds in foreign currency may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at a Luxembourg or EU credit institution and may be credited to the SARL’s operational account without conversion. For EUR-denominated bulk grain contracts, settlement occurs via TARGET2-compatible payment systems. Statutory balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL is triggered above EUR 12,500 per transaction, but no authorisation or surrender obligation is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); the Luxembourg Financial Sector Supervisory Commission (CSSF) exercises shared prudential and market-conduct oversight of credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate as a payment-facilitation rail but Low as a full treasury substitute. Luxembourg offers an authorised Payment Institution / Electronic Money Institution (EMI) regime under EU PSD2/EMD2, supervised by the CSSF. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, Lithuania, Singapore) are not permitted to provide operational IBANs or clearing services to a Luxembourg SARL unless they hold a passport into Luxembourg or a Luxembourg establishment. For bulk grain commodity trading requiring documentary letters of credit, an EMI generally cannot issue LCs, act as confirming bank, or provide structured trade finance. Thus, EMIs are suitable only for ancillary SEPA collections or receivables segregation, not as primary clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocols: Shareholder capital contributions must be paid in EUR via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the SARL’s Luxembourg operational account, with purpose code “SUBS” (share capital subscription) or “CAPIT” (capital contribution). The BCL balance-of-payments report must identify the counterparty and nature of inflow; no authorisation is required. For intragroup loans, use purpose code “LOAN” and maintain a fully executed facility agreement specifying principal, interest, repayment schedule, events of default, and tax treatment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Moderate as a payment-facilitation rail but Low as a full treasury substitute. Luxembourg offers an authorised Payment Institution / Electronic Money Institution (EMI) regime under EU PSD2/EMD2, supervised by the CSSF. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, Lithuania, Singapore) are not permitted to provide operational IBANs or clearing services to a Luxembourg SARL unless they hold a passport into Luxembourg or a Luxembourg establishment. For bulk grain commodity trading requiring documentary letters of credit, an EMI generally cannot issue LCs, act as confirming bank, or provide structured trade finance. Thus, EMIs are suitable only for ancillary SEPA collections or receivables segregation, not as primary clearing rails.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate for Luxembourg City (17.00% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge at 7% of CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax; rate varies by commune)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory for Luxembourg-resident 'large enterprises' (more than 250 employees and either annual net turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000 or balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43,000,000) belonging to a group with consolidated annual net turnover of at least EUR 750,000,000. For smaller taxpayers, the annual Form 700 TP annex is compulsory where cross-border related-party transactions in goods exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year or where services, royalty, and interest transactions exceed EUR 250,000 per year.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no codified Economic Substance Regulations statute as in UAE offshore regimes; however, substance-over-form is vigorously enforced through LIR Article 6 (abus de droit/Steuerumgehung), the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test (applicable from 1 January 2019), ATAD I and II general anti-abuse rules, and the substance self-assessment framework introduced by the Law of 30 July 2021 and the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 17 September 2021. A bulk-grain SARL must maintain a local office, adequate employees, real decision-making in Luxembourg, and substantive commercial activity commensurate with its booking of trading income; otherwise tax residence, treaty access, and EU Directive benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale) and Falsification of Books

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years under the General Tax Law and Criminal Code; civil penalty of 50% of the concealed tax; automatic 10% late-filing increase; default interest at 0.9% per month; closure of the business establishment; publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false declaration are personally and jointly liable for the understated tax, with piercing of the corporate veil; foreign travel bans and extradition under the European Arrest Warrant; disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies for up to 10 years; seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence; recharacterization of the SARL as tax-transparent; 15% dividend withholding tax on deemed distributions; denial of treaty and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits under the MLI PPT; imposition of net wealth tax on the full net assets without deductible surplus; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000 for non-filing of the substance self-assessment declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes re-assessed on the foreign parent; the corporate veil is pierced under the doctrine of fictitious companies (théorie de la fraude à la loi); criminal conviction for complicity in tax evasion; travel restrictions; personal liability for corporate debts under Article 210 of the Law of 10 August 1915 when the company is deemed fictitious or mismanaged.
AML/CFT Failure in Commodity Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines by the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit and CSSF up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal money-laundering penalties under the Law of 12 November 2004, as amended: imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 1,250,000; freezing of the SARL's bank accounts; dissolution of the entity in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors individually prosecuted for failure to report suspicious transactions; freezing of personal assets; prohibition from exercising banking, trading, or management functions; international travel ban; extradition risk under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to a deemed arm's-length profit; secondary adjustment and recharacterization; 10% tax increase for late filing; default interest of 0.9% per month on the underpaid corporate income tax; fine up to EUR 50,000 for failure to submit TP documentation; risk of economic double taxation if a mutual agreement procedure is not invoked.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax where pricing was knowingly manipulated; criminal exposure for false certification of the return; seizure of board minutes and professional emails; debarment from serving on Luxembourg boards; travel restrictions pending resolution of criminal proceedings.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the authority of the Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016; complemented by the Law of 2 September 2011 on access to professions and the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif) containing the Articles of Association (Statuts), electronically filed with the RCS by the drafting notary; publication of a legal extract in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,000), fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash at incorporation; capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution or an EEA-based bank and is released to the company only upon RCS registration; the notary must certify the deposit and the bank's blocking attestation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Corps (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours – CGDIS): obtain the fire department's approval of the building plans and a post-completion fire safety inspection certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) before operating the warehouse and dispatch areas; structural load clearance via a certified engineering inspection of racking systems (EN 15512 compliance) and for the building's load-bearing capacity; occupational safety clearance from the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (Inspection du Travail et des Mines – ITM) including registration of the workplace, preliminary safety audit, first-aid equipment, and ergonomic assessment of packing stations; issuance of the municipal building occupancy permit (certificat de conformité au permis de construire) by the competent Commune for the COD warehouse and office premises.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,000), fully subscribed and fully paid up in cash at incorporation; capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution or an EEA-based bank and is released to the company only upon RCS registration; the notary must certify the deposit and the bank's blocking attestation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct tax: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD); VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT); Customs: Administration des Douanes et Accises; unified e-portal: MyGuichet.lu; corporate registry: Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS).

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL is formed by notarial deed registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which assigns the statutory national identification number (matricule). VAT registration is filed with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu, attaching the RCS extract, articles of association, beneficial-ownership declarations, head-office proof and director identification; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (prefix LU + 8 digits). For customs operations the taxpayer must obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration. No local agent is required for a resident SARL, even if wholly foreign-owned.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL is formed by notarial deed registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which assigns the statutory national identification number (matricule). VAT registration is filed with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu, attaching the RCS extract, articles of association, beneficial-ownership declarations, head-office proof and director identification; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT number (prefix LU + 8 digits). For customs operations the taxpayer must obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration. No local agent is required for a resident SARL, even if wholly foreign-owned.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime; a SARL is an opaque corporate CIT taxpayer resident in Luxembourg, subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income with participation exemption available. Available elections: (i) VAT group registration (assujetti unique, Art. 60 VAT law); (ii) corporate fiscal unity (intégration fiscale, Art. 164bis LIR); (iii) participation exemption claim (Art. 166 LIR); (iv) IP box election (Art. 50ter LIR); (v) ATAD hybrid-mismatch alignment choices (Arts. 168septies et seq. LIR).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic registration threshold applies: every taxable person carrying on an independent taxable activity must register irrespective of revenue. The optional small-business franchise (Art. 6 VAT law) exempts turnover up to EUR 35,000 for goods and EUR 25,000 for services; export-oriented SARLs commonly waive the exemption to recover input VAT. For Union B2C distance sales (e-commerce), registration via the Union One-Stop-Shop is triggered once EU-wide distance-sales turnover exceeds EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods consigned to non-EU emerging-market buyers qualify at 0% under Art. 62 VAT law (implementing Art. 146(1) of Directive 2006/112/EC) where the goods leave EU customs territory within three months of supply; cash-on-delivery e-commerce consignments satisfy this condition when carrier and customs records prove physical exit. Intra-Community supplies (Art. 43 VAT law) are also zero-rated subject to the customer's valid VIES-registered VAT ID. International freight, logistics and ancillary services directly connected with exported goods are eligible for the corresponding export-services exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A) lodged with Luxembourg Customs (AES/ECS-confirmed)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment proof evidencing export proceeds receipt
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Customer purchase order and COD delivery confirmation issued by the foreign carrier or logistics partner
  • VIES validation printout of the customer VAT ID for intra-Community zero-rating
  • EORI and transport booking records retained in the VAT file

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is currently mandated in Luxembourg. B2B/B2C invoices are issued, archived and audited ex post under Arts. 88/90 VAT law and retained for 10 years; B2G e-invoicing uses PEPPOL under Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and EU recapitulative statements are filed electronically through the AEDT portal; the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age package is expected to introduce mandatory e-reporting/clearance from approximately 2030.

The AEDT audits export zero-rating strictly on contemporaneous export evidence; missing or internally contradictory documentation causes immediate reassessment of 17% VAT on the sales price plus statutory default interest. Fictitious export declarations trigger the 10-year fraud limitation period, loss of input-VAT deduction, administrative fines under the General Tax Law of 22 May 1931, publication of the decision, and criminal sanctions (fines and imprisonment up to 5 years) for deliberate evasion. Clawbacks are retrospective and are not relieved by the foreign customer's subsequent insolvency.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible and Luxembourg implements the EU free movement of capital under Articles 63–66 TFEU. Both current and capital account operations are liberalized; there are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or central-bank permits for profit remittance. Dividend repatriation and distribution of profits are subject to standard withholding tax procedures and ordinary corporate tax filings, but not to any convertibility or liquidity restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg mandates no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no minimum conversion ratio, and no regulatory timeline for repatriation of trade receipts. Cash-on-delivery revenues from emerging-market jurisdictions may be collected and held in foreign currency, or converted freely at market-based rates. For financial reporting and tax purposes, all non-euro transactions must be translated into euro using the ECB or a reliable institutional reference rate at the relevant valuation date.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg; banking and AML/CFT supervision is also exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate foreign-exchange administration exists in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for use as a non-bank ancillary payment channel. The legal constraint is that the Luxembourg SARL must maintain its principal settlement account at a Luxembourg- or EU-authorised credit institution; a non-EU electronic money institution cannot passport into Luxembourg without a licensed branch. EU-licensed EMIs/PIs may be used for multi-currency collection, FX conversion, and local payout rails in emerging markets, but the entity must independently satisfy AML/CFT due-diligence and cannot rely exclusively on a third-country EMI as its treasury backbone.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City aggregate of 17% CIT plus 7% unemployment surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax; standalone CIT effective rate is 18.19%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders; may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% holding, 12-month ownership) or eliminated under a relevant DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required when the Luxembourg group has consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750 million; Local File required when related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million for goods or EUR 500,000 for services, royalties, financing, or intangibles.

Economic substance scope: Although Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulation in the Cayman-style sense, EU ATAD, the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule, and beneficial ownership jurisprudence require genuine economic substance, local decision-making, and business purpose for treaty and directive benefits to apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax prosecution: amende fiscale equal to 100% of the evaded tax plus tax penalties, interest, and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment of up to 5 years under Luxembourg tax fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations expose themselves to civil restitution of evaded taxes, travel restrictions, and criminal custodial sentences.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, antidirector and anti-abuse penalties, reclassification of income within Luxembourg, retroactive reassessment, and withdrawal of tax compliance certifications.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil: directors become personally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties, and may be prohibited from managing or leaving Luxembourg.
AML and CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 1 million for entities and EUR 250,000 for responsible individuals; confiscation of instruments and proceeds; withdrawal of commercial functions and revocation of any relevant licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution, disqualification orders, asset freezing, travel bans pending investigation, and imprisonment where the breach forms part of organized fraud or money laundering.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to EUR 100,000 for non-production of documentation; the Luxembourg tax authorities may estimate taxable income and impose a 20% accuracy-related penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax adjustment and penalties where they knowingly caused the absence of contemporaneous documentation or signed inconsistent TP policies.

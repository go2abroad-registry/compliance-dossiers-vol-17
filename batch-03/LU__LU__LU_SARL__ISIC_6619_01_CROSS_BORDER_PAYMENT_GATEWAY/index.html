Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue – Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; VAT and registration taxes – Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED); Customs – Administration des douanes et accises. Electronic access: MyGuichet.lu single e-government portal and eCDF electronic VAT filing platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity: (1) register the legal entity with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) or register a Luxembourg branch; (2) RCS filing automatically creates a national business identification number (NRC) used as the tax file number with the ACD; (3) apply to AED for a VAT number using Form 1600, attaching articles of association, RCS extract, and proof of foreign tax residency; a tax representative may be required for non-established providers; (4) complete social security registration with the Common Centre of Social Security (CCSS) if carrying on Luxembourg activity. Foreign entities with no Luxembourg establishment supplying B2B services from abroad generally do not need Luxembourg VAT registration for input VAT recovery unless Luxembourg is the place of supply under the reverse-charge mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not offer a check-the-box or partnership-style transparency election for a SARL. A SARL is a capital company taxable at entity level. Available elective mechanisms include: (i) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) pursuant to Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for related established taxable persons; (ii) corporate income tax fiscal unity for 95%-owned Luxembourg tax-resident subsidiaries under Section 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law; (iii) application for an advance tax ruling from the Luxembourg Tax Authorities to confirm financing structures, hybrid characterization, or participation exemption treatment; (iv) ATAD hybrid-mismatch neutralisation rules may be triggered by elective transparent/investment entity classifications recognised by another Member State, but Luxembourg itself does not provide a unilateral check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for supplies of goods and services in Luxembourg; the threshold does not apply to non-established taxable persons when the place of supply is Luxembourg or when the reverse-charge mechanism applies; intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000 and distance sales or electronically supplied services under EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) regimes may trigger registration obligations outside Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export of goods to a non-EU state qualifies for 0% VAT if the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU and the supplier or its agent retains authoritative proof of export; intra-Community supplies to a taxable person in another EU Member State are zero-rated subject to the customer's valid VAT ID, EC Sales List, and transport evidence. Services: B2B payment gateway, merchant acquiring, and digital intermediary services supplied to a customer in another jurisdiction generally fall outside Luxembourg VAT through the reverse-charge mechanism in the customer's jurisdiction; financial services, including payment intermediation and clearing by an authorised PSP, are VAT-exempt under Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law implementing Article 135(1)(d) of the EU VAT Directive. Services physically rendered to non-EU recipients and directly connected with exported goods may also be zero-rated. No PSP service is automatically zero-rated unless the specific supply falls within an expressly exempt or outside-scope rule.

  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document with proof of physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Luxembourg exporter
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT proof of foreign exchange receipt

No real-time VAT clearance, fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is currently in force in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is mandatory exclusively for B2G transactions and must use the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 interoperable format through the Luxembourg e-Submission platform. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists are submitted electronically through eCDF (Electronic Cash Desk and Filing) on MyGuichet.lu; there is no public mandatory API clearance mandate beyond authenticated XML/e-Submission for government contracts.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may withdraw zero-rating or the applicable exemption where transport or customs documentation is missing or inconsistent, issuing assessments for unpaid VAT plus default interest. Administrative fines for documentary non-compliance can reach EUR 4,000; in cases of deliberate fraud, tax evasion, or use of false export declarations, criminal penalties include imprisonment up to five years and fines proportional to the evaded tax, with retrospective reassessment under the anti-abuse doctrine and the standard ten-year statute of limitations for indirect tax offences.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg provides full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63 and 64 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange licence, approval mechanism, or discriminatory currency rationing exists for payment services, direct investment, or profit remittances. Cross-border transfers in EUR or any other currency are unrestricted except where applicable EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML/CFT) controls, or customs/counter-terrorist financing rules impose screening obligations. Dividend remittances from a Luxembourg SARL are subject to the 15% domestic withholding tax on distributions, but the rate can be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to treaty rates under most Luxembourg double tax treaties where beneficiary conditions are satisfied. Capital repatriation on liquidation is generally not subject to withholding tax beyond the taxation of accumulated profits. The practical hurdles are statistical rather than regulatory: resident companies must report cross-border payments and external financial assets/liabilities to BCL for the compilation of the Luxembourg balance of payments, typically through the current account, financial account, and direct investment reporting forms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligations on export proceeds in Luxembourg. Resident companies may retain, borrow against, transfer, or freely convert any foreign currency earnings into EUR or other currencies without a timeline or a conversion ratio. For balance-of-payments statistical completeness, BCL requires that enterprises directly report each cross-border transaction above the prevailing reporting threshold (individually or annually, depending on the nature and counterparty country) and that foreign receivables and payables outstanding be reported through BCL's external asset/liability survey schemas. Failure to file accurate BCL returns may expose the company to administrative fines, but this is a reporting duty, not an exchange control limit.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign-exchange policy and monetary sovereignty are exercised through the ECB for euro-area matters and the BCL for national statistical, supervisory, and account-holding functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) that passport into Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2/EU 2015/2366). Such entities may hold a Luxembourg license from the CSSF or a comparable license in another EU/EEA state and may open settlement accounts with local banks and directly access SEPA, SWIFT, and card clearing systems. Feasibility rating: Low for offshore digital EMIs headquartered outside the European Economic Area; they cannot passport under PSD2 and would require either a Luxembourg-authorised subsidiary, a branch licence, or a partnership with a licensed EU PI/EMI. The legal constraints include the prohibition on deposit-taking, the requirement to safeguard client funds through segregated accounts, the mandatory capital and own-funds requirements (initial capital of EUR 350,000 for an EMI, EUR 125,000 for a PI), and CSSF regulatory notification before using agents or outsourcing critical operational functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from non-resident shareholders must be executed through SWIFT wire transfers (MT103) to the SARL's Luxembourg capital-escrow or operational account. The sender must use the company’s exact FX-efficient EUR IBAN with no intermediate account and provide an explicit wire reference in field 70 such as 'Share Capital Contribution under Resolution dated [date]' or 'Capital increase SARL [name]'. The receiving bank will require a copy of the notarised board/shareholder resolution and an updated RCS extract after registration. Where the shareholder is a holding company in a tax treaty jurisdiction, the bank also requests the corporate tax registration and proof of real economic activity to validate treaty-based withholding rules. The BCL balance-of-payments report must classify the incoming amount under direct investment equity capital, with counterparty country and BIC/IBAN details, by the 10th business day after the month of receipt at the corporate's reporting portal.The SARL uses multi-rail clearing for trade and merchant settlements: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EEA transactions, using the debtor’s and creditor’s IBAN with standard SEPA purpose codes; (2) SWIFT MT103/M202 for payments outside the EEA or non-EUR currency, routed via a Luxembourg bank or its correspondents; (3) commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) opened by the Luxembourg bank in favour of overseas sellers, especially for high-value or documentary trade flows; (4) payment card clearing through Visa/Mastercard settlement reports, with funding to acquiring banks, generally via SWIFT MT202 COV or the Fedwire system for USD balances; and (5) local instant clearing (LuxSEA) where high daily frequency requires batch settlement. For treasury control, outbound disbursements must be pre-authorised under a four-eyes principle, matched against the procurement or merchant agreement, and reported to BCL under the monthly balance-of-payments survey with the corresponding counterparty sector and country.Deliverable OTC FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY) executed with a Luxembourg bank or EU-authorised broker, provided the notional does not exceed the SARL’s measured currency exposure and the instrument is booked for cash-flow hedging.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies subject to convertibility restrictions or price controls, settling in EUR on a spot reference date, and permissible under Luxembourg law when the counterparty is licensed under EU MiFID II/CASS custody rules.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) matching the tenor and coupon of underlying trade finance or capital structure, fully hedged on a natural cash-flow basis.FX swaps (swap transactions involving simultaneous spot sale/purchase and forward re-purchase) for intraday or 48-hour funding relief, with the economic substance being purely liquidity management.European-style FX options purchased or sold in tandem with an independent risk committee mandate, strictly hedge-only, and prohibited for speculative trading unless separately authorised by a future CSSF licence for portfolio management.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for EU-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) that passport into Luxembourg under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2/EU 2015/2366). Such entities may hold a Luxembourg license from the CSSF or a comparable license in another EU/EEA state and may open settlement accounts with local banks and directly access SEPA, SWIFT, and card clearing systems. Feasibility rating: Low for offshore digital EMIs headquartered outside the European Economic Area; they cannot passport under PSD2 and would require either a Luxembourg-authorised subsidiary, a branch licence, or a partnership with a licensed EU PI/EMI. The legal constraints include the prohibition on deposit-taking, the requirement to safeguard client funds through segregated accounts, the mandatory capital and own-funds requirements (initial capital of EUR 350,000 for an EMI, EUR 125,000 for a PI), and CSSF regulatory notification before using agents or outsourcing critical operational functions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective headline rate for Luxembourg City, FY2024: 17% corporate income tax (IRC) + 7% solidarity surcharge on IRC + 6.75% municipal business tax (TASOC); FY2025 combined rate reduced to 23.94% following IRC decrease to 16%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (WHT) on distributions by a SARL; reduced to 0% where the parent qualifies under the Luxembourg participation exemption or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% capital, 12-month holding period); treaty rates for non-resident corporate shareholders generally 0% or 5%, portfolio shareholders 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Luxembourg SARL's annual gross consideration for related-party transactions equals or exceeds EUR 1,000,000; Master File is mandatory where the worldwide consolidated group net turnover reaches EUR 750,000,000; below these thresholds the OECD contemporaneous documentation standard still applies on request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but economic substance is enforced through the ATAD Article 6 General Anti-Abuse Rule, the administrative substance doctrine applied by the Luxembourg Ruling Committee, the requirement to satisfy treaty-beneficial-ownership tests, and the CSSF prudential licensing regime for payment institutions under PSD2; a SARL that is a letter-box PSP with no local decision-making, risk management, full-time staff, or office infrastructure will be disregarded and profits reattributed to the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealed Gateway Revenues

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment of undeclared IRC, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; 10% late-filing surcharge; default interest at 0.6% per commenced month; criminal fine of up to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to five years before the Tribunal d'arrondissement.

Director personal liability: Gérants and directors face personal criminal liability for tax fraud, civil restitution of evaded tax, joint and several liability for corporate tax debts where the veil is pierced, asset seizures, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to five years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR reassessment with denial of the participation exemption and treaty benefits; reclassification of profit repatriation as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT; 10% penalty surcharge; fines for prohibited shell arrangements under Luxembourg and EU anti-avoidance frameworks.

Director personal liability: Luxembourg courts will pierce the corporate veil where separate legal personality is a pure façade; directors and shadow directors are civilly liable for the shortfall, may be subject to travel bans, freezing orders, and up to five years imprisonment for abusive fraud or false attestation.

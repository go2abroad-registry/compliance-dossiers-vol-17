Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) (Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; national electronic portals: myGuichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu, pvat.public.lu, and the AEDT e-filing/e-TVA platform; VIES database maintained by the Luxembourg VAT administration for intra-Community verifications.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg-resident SARL is registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) on incorporation and receives a national identification number (matricule), which serves as the corporate tax identification number (NIF/TIN). For VAT, the SARL must file the official VAT registration application (Formulaire 645 / Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) with the AEDT before commencing taxable supplies; the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix (LU + 8 digits + 2 check digits). A non-resident foreign entity supplying taxable fintech or lending services in Luxembourg and not covered by the reverse charge must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative (representant responsable solidaire) and submit the same registration form through myGuichet.lu, attaching certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, articles of association (statuts), home-country trade register extract, identity documents of directors/managers, and a description of the intended activities. Registration must be perfected before the first taxable supply; late registration exposes the entity to penalties and interest but does not invalidate the underlying transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A Luxembourg-resident SARL with registered office (siège statutaire) and central administration in Luxembourg is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) on worldwide profits, plus the 7% solidarity surcharge (contribution au fonds de chômage) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City (17% IRC + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Luxembourg-resident SARL with registered office (siège statutaire) and central administration in Luxembourg is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) on worldwide profits, plus the 7% solidarity surcharge (contribution au fonds de chômage) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City (17% IRC + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax). No look-through or transparent taxation applies to a commercial P2P micro-lending SARL; taxable profits include interest margins, origination fees, underwriting commissions, platform fees and ancillary lending charges, reduced by arm's length operating, financing, AML-compliance, technology and bad-debt costs under article 109 LIR.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections for a commercial SARL micro-lending platform: (i) application of the Luxembourg participation exemption (régime mère-fille) evidenced through the corporate tax return and holding documentation; (ii) option to waive the article 57 small-business VAT franchise and apply normal taxation; (iii) election to form a VAT group (assujettissement unique) on application to the AEDT for closely linked taxable persons (available since 1 January 2019); (iv) article 110 LIR election for roll-over deferral on reinvested capital gains; and (v) mandatory ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch provisions (no elective mechanism), although symmetrical treatment of hybrid instruments may be achieved through contractual design of subordination, profit participation and conversion rights in the loan agreements, subject to general anti-abuse review.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 (annual taxable turnover, exclusive of VAT) - small-business franchise under article 57 LVAT; registration is compulsory irrespective of turnover for taxable supplies subject to reverse charge, for intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000, for distance sales, or where the entity opts for full VAT taxation. A non-resident supplier without a Luxembourg fixed establishment must nevertheless register for Luxembourg VAT (or appoint a tax representative) when making taxable supplies in Luxembourg falling outside the general reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies to (a) goods physically exported outside the EU by the supplier or a non-established purchaser within three months of supply (article 42 LVAT), supported by a validated customs export declaration and transport documents evidencing exit from the EU customs territory; (b) B2B services supplied to a customer established outside the EU, which are taxable at the customer's place of establishment (article 56 LVAT), including algorithm underwriting, loan-origination, platform-access, credit-scoring and data-processing services provided from Luxembourg to non-EU borrowers and platform users; and (c) in the P2P micro-lending context, interest income, loan-origination fees and credit intermediation services are VAT-exempt financial services (article 135(1)(b)-(d) of Directive 2006/112/EC) with no input VAT recovery, while platform fees effectively consumed outside the EU fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope. The platform must contemporaneously document the non-EU establishment of each counterparty through a valid foreign tax identification number, registered-office address, AML/KYC file and, where applicable, a tax residency certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) validated electronically through the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency (PLDA/ECS system) with proof of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or CMR international consignment note evidencing physical dispatch of goods outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank transfer confirmation, SWIFT MT103, or lender-funded disbursement record) evidencing receipt of export proceeds or non-EU customer payments
  • Commercial invoice and underlying loan agreement for P2P lending fees, underwriting commissions and platform charges
  • Packing list and weighing/quantity certificate for any goods-related transactions
  • Proof of customer's non-EU establishment: foreign trade register extract, VAT/tax identification certificate, or tax residency certificate (certificat de résidence fiscale)
  • Transport manifests, CIM rail consignment note, or EUR.1/certificate of origin where preferential customs origin is claimed

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API-based government clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Luxembourg for B2B/B2C transactions. Electronic invoicing is permitted on mutual consent and is mandatory only for B2G supplies through the PEPPOL network (law on electronic invoicing in public markets, phased in since 1 January 2022). VAT returns (form TVA), EC sales lists (VIES) and Intrastat declarations must be e-filed through the AEDT/myGuichet portal using structured XML attachments. The P2P lending platform must preserve all electronic invoices and transaction-level records in original format for 10 years and make them accessible for AEDT/ACD audits.

Luxembourg law does not impose an express statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is applied as the commercial and arm's length benchmark for collection of export receivables and platform fees, consistent with Luxembourg transfer-pricing practice (article 56bis LIR). Where export zero-rating is claimed abusively (goods never exported, fictitious transport documents, services effectively consumed in Luxembourg), the AEDT issues a post-clearance assessment (redressement) recharging VAT at the standard 17% rate, with monthly late interest of 0.6% (7.2% per annum) and a non-payment majoration of up to 10% of the tax due. Deliberate tax fraud is criminally punishable under the Luxembourg law of 17 March 2016 with imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and/or a fine of up to EUR 250,000, plus confiscation of the tax advantage and possible publication of the judgment. The ordinary VAT assessment limitation period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of established fraud, allowing retrospective clawback of older zero-rated transactions. Directors, statutory managers and the appointed tax representative may be held jointly and severally liable (article 53 AO) for unremitted VAT and penalty amounts of the SARL.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully liberalised jurisdiction within the EU Single Market; the euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances are unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/KYC validation and corporate formalities (board approval, audited accounts, and withholding tax compliance on dividends, where applicable). No exchange control approval is needed for inward or outward transfers, but all cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 require balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL via the domestic banking system; the P2P lending platform must file periodic BOP statements for non-EEA investors and borrowers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists under Luxembourg law. Entities may freely retain, convert, or remit export receipts and service fees. The only obligation is bookkeeping and tax declaration accuracy under the Luxembourg Commercial Code and CIT law; for an ISIC 6499 financial services platform, loan repayments and interest income are classified as services/financial income and are not subject to any mandatory conversion or retention timeline.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for financial-sector FX compliance; the BCL compiles external statistics and applies EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) liberalisation, with no autonomous Luxembourg foreign-exchange control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. A Luxembourg-licensed Payment Institution (PI) or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorised by the CSSF under the Loi du 10 novembre 2009 is viable for domestic SEPA processing; however, offshore digital EMIs lacking a Luxembourg branch or passporting under PSD2 face legal barriers to clearing inbound loan repayments and outbound disbursements in EUR. Offshore EMIs may provide ancillary multi-currency virtual IBANs, but cannot serve as the primary regulated clearing rail for retail lending because the SARL must isolate client funds in a CSSF-approved segregation scheme and maintain supervisory compliance. Use of an unauthorised non-EEA EMI is prohibited.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity or shareholder loan injections: use SWIFT MT103 via the BCL TARGET2-SECURITIES infrastructure or SEPA wire if the sender is EEA-based; the EU Common Reporting Standard requires clear reference to the Payment Purpose Code – for Luxembourg BOP purposes, use purpose code 'ND0' for capital investment (direct investment) or 'F.6' for loans. For investor subscriptions into the P2P platform: each inbound transfer must be tagged with the loan/investment reference and include a statement of the beneficial owner; banks will require proof of the underlying investment agreement. Non-EEA inbound transfers in EUR or USD should be routed through a correspondent bank with SWIFT MT103 covering an intermediary bank; strict use of ISO 20022 remittance information is required, and any proceeds funding the platform must not exceed the authorised payment-to-payment timeline defined in the CSSF licence.Loan disbursements to retail borrowers must be executed via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for euro-area disbursements (max EUR 100,000 per transaction) or via SEPA Credit Transfer for sums above instant limits. For disbursements to non-EEA beneficiaries, use SWIFT MT103 with instruction 'OUR' (charges borne by sender) to ensure full settlement. Commercial letters of credit (UCP600) are applicable only for genuine trade flows, not consumer microloans; an ISIC 6499 P2P platform must use standard payment initiation services through its licensed bank or a regulated PI. Each disbursement must embed a structured creditor reference (e.g., loan number + repayment schedule) to permit automated reconciliation and satisfy the CSSF's obligation to trace funds. Offshore EMIs may provide ancillary multi-currency virtual IBANs, but cannot serve as the primary regulated clearing rail for retail lending because the SARL must isolate client funds in a CSSF-approved segregation scheme and maintain supervisory compliance. Use of an unauthorised non-EEA EMI is prohibited.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is required when the group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Local File is required when the Luxembourg entity is a member of a multinational group with intra-group transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 annually, per Luxembourg ACD Circular LIR 170/2. For this P2P micro-lending platform, any intercompany fee for origination, credit scoring, or funding exceeding EUR 500,000 must be supported by a contemporaneous arm's length benchmark.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, substance is assessed under OECD/ATAD principles, Article 56 LIR, and the GAAR. The micro-lending SARL must maintain real premises, qualified staff, board decision-making on loan policies, origination algorithms, risk underwriting, and servicing functions in Luxembourg. A letterbox company may be disregarded, with profits re-attributed to the actual control seat.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full evaded tax plus a penalty of 50% of that tax; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Articles 396-399 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code and the General Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fraudulent returns are personally liable for the corporate tax shortfall on the basis of 'faute caractérisée' (Article 7 Civil Code, Article 180 Abgabenordnung). Courts may apply veil piercing, asset forfeiture, director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for directors found to have organised the fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Tax administration disregards the entity under GAAR and Article 56 LIR; taxable income is re-attributed to the shareholder jurisdiction; a hidden distribution is recharacterised and taxed at 15% withholding plus late interest; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 for failure to disclose substance indicators.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting Luxembourg tax arrears if they knowingly used the entity as a shield. Luxembourg courts may lift limited liability due to abuse of law, impose travel bans, block bank accounts, and order directors to pay unpaid taxes and fines personally.
AML and Unlicensed Micro-Lending Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanction up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover imposed by the CSSF; criminal money-laundering penalties under Article 506-1 of the Criminal Code include imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and fines up to EUR 5,000,000; the court may also order public publication of the sanction and dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the absence of AML controls. They face criminal imprisonment, personal fines, asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification from management functions, and extradition proceedings in cross-border cases. The corporate veil is pierced where the SARL was used as a conduit for illicit lending funds.

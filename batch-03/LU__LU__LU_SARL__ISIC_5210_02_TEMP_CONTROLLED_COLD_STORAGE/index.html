Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority (https://impotsdirects.public.lu) for corporate income tax and withholding tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) (https://aed.public.lu) for VAT, registration duties and excise; Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (ADA) for export clearance; unified e-administration and tax portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and PFI e-filing (https://pfiweb.etat.lu).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL by deed before a Luxembourg notary and file the constitutive documents with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) (https://www.lbr.lu); the RCS issues the national business registration number (matricule), which is the master identifier for all subsequent tax files. Step 2 — Upon publication of the incorporation in the RCS, the company is automatically registered with the ACD for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; the ACD allocates the corporate tax file number; no separate application is required for a Luxembourg-resident SARL. Step 3 — Apply for Luxembourg VAT identification with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu using the form 'Demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA'; the AEDT issues a 7- or 8-digit Luxembourg VAT number prefixed 'LU' (format LU00000000). The application must be submitted before the first taxable supply. Step 4 — A non-resident foreign entity without a Luxembourg fixed establishment that carries out Luxembourg-taxable transactions must apply for direct VAT registration through the AEDT electronic portal before commencing activity; no de minimis turnover relief is available to non-established suppliers. Step 5 — Register as employer with the Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale (CCSS) where the SARL hires staff, and obtain the employer matricule for wage withholding and social security filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable corporate entity (personne collective) resident in Luxembourg, subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune); no domestic pass-through election exists for a SARL (unlike SCS/SCSp partnerships, which may elect fiscal transparency). Available statutory mechanisms: (i) Fiscal Unity ('intégration fiscale') under Article 164bis LIR, permitting a Luxembourg-resident parent and Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at a minimum 95% interest to elect aggregation of taxable results and intra-group loss offset; (ii) ATAD-compliant hybrid and imported hybrid mismatch counter-measures under Articles 168quater and 168quinquies LIR, which deny deductions where payments are tax-deductible in Luxembourg and non-includible, or doubly deductible, abroad; (iii) Participation Exemption under Article 166 LIR — 100% exemption of qualifying dividends and capital gains where the SARL holds at least 10% of the subsidiary's capital or an acquisition price of at least EUR 1.2 million and the subsidiary is subject to a tax equivalent to Luxembourg corporate income tax; (iv) VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') for consolidation of intra-group VAT positions. No US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises (Article 55 Loi TVA); a Luxembourg SARL engaged in cold-storage warehousing will almost always exceed this threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable turnover once exceeded. Non-established foreign suppliers enjoy no de minimis threshold and must register for VAT from their first taxable transaction in Luxembourg. Intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers are subject to the EUR 10,000 annual cross-border threshold (Article 58 Loi TVA), after which VAT applies in the customer's Member State via the OSS regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods under Article 41(3) Loi TVA applies where: (i) the SARL is a taxable person identified for VAT in Luxembourg; (ii) the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union; (iii) the goods leave the EU within 90 days of the supply or within the normal contractual dispatch timeline; and (iv) the export declaration (SAD/DAU) is lodged electronically with the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (ADA) and the corresponding Movement Reference Number (MRN) and transport documents are retained. Where the SARL supplies cold-chain storage, handling and ancillary logistics services to a taxable recipient established outside the EU, the supply falls outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Articles 14 and 41 Loi TVA), or is zero-rated where directly connected to exported goods; the SARL must obtain the customer's foreign VAT identification number and retain contractual and delivery evidence. Intra-EU cold-storage services supplied to EU taxable persons are taxable in the customer's Member State under the reverse-charge mechanism, with the SARL required to file recapitulative statements and monitor the EUR 10,000 OSS threshold for any B2C supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / DAU) endorsed by the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration (ADA), including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and entry of the transport and destination details
  • Transport document proving physical departure from the EU: Bill of Lading (maritime), CMR waybill (road), Air Waybill (air), or multimodal transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank transfer evidence demonstrating settlement of the export proceeds by the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the exported goods and the VAT zero-rating or outside-scope basis
  • Cold-chain custody and temperature-monitoring records (time-stamped logger data) evidencing continuous temperature-controlled storage from intake through dispatch
  • Warehouse stock ledgers, inventory movement registers, and customs warehouse or authorised storage authorisation documents applicable to the refrigerated logistics facility
  • Purchase invoices and import documentation substantiating input VAT allocation to the exported goods

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority pre-clearance (SDI-style), or digital stamping mandate applies to B2B VAT invoices in Luxembourg. Mandatory electronic invoicing exists exclusively in the B2G segment: under the Law of 6 October 2021, suppliers invoicing Luxembourg central government entities must issue e-invoices through the PEPPOL network using Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 via a certified access point, with phased mandatory compliance completed for central contracting authorities from 1 January 2025. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and may be implemented by bilateral EDI or PEPPOL XML agreement. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (VIDA) proposals, which would introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU B2B transactions, are expected to apply from 1 July 2030 but are not yet in force. The SARL must submit periodic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) electronically via the PFI portal and file the Intrastat and recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) where intra-EU supplies occur.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to ex-post documentary audits by the AEDT (VAT) and customs post-clearance verification by the ADA; the taxpayer must produce the complete documentary bundle within the statutory response period (generally 30 days). Deficiencies or inconsistencies in documentation lead to retrospective clawback of the zero-rated VAT, plus statutory late-payment interest. Fraudulent or recklessly unsupported claims trigger: (i) administrative penalties under Article 81 Loi TVA and the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung — AO LUX) of up to 100% of the evaded differential tax; (ii) criminal sanctions for tax fraud and customs fraud under Articles 395-396 AO LUX and Article 82 Loi TVA, with fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of the responsible directors and managers; (iii) joint and several civil liability of the SARL's directors and liquidators under the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies for tax deficiencies arising from non-compliance; (iv) withdrawal of the customs warehouse or AEO status, cancellation of export licences, exclusion from the OSS and IOSS schemes, and publication on the AEDT list of delinquent taxable persons; (v) a five-year retroactive assessment window under Article 225 AO LUX, extended where fraud or concealment is established. The SARL should therefore maintain a formal export compliance file ('preuve d'exportation') assembled at the time of each zero-rated supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalized regime: the euro is fully convertible, and Luxembourg applies no exchange-control approvals, no capital-export restrictions, and no prior authorization for profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or liquidation proceeds. Both current-account and capital-account transactions benefit from the free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63-65, subject only to EU-wide prudential safeguards, national security screening, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the BCL. For a Luxembourg SARL invoicing in EUR within the eurozone, no convertibility exposure exists; cross-currency USD/CHF/GBP receipts are convertible at market rates without reference to the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation-surrender requirement for export proceeds, including warehouse logistics service receipts from third countries. The SARL may freely retain, convert, hedge, or reinvest export earnings in EUR or any other convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank; banking prudential and AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a supplemental payment-collection channel, but not viable as the primary treasury and clearing bank. Luxembourg-licensed E-Money Institutions under the CSSF E-Money Law 2010 and PSD2 can conduct SEPA credit transfers, FX conversion, and virtual multi-currency accounts, but they cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit, provide UCP600 documentary trade services, hold BCL settlement reserves, or grant overdraft/working-capital facilities. For a cold-storage logistics SARL servicing pharma clients, the recommended architecture is a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution for all account-balance, escrow, trade, and liquidity functions, with a supervised EMI (e.g., licensed Luxembourg branch of Airwallex, Currencies Direct, or similar) restricted to cross-border receivable acceleration and automated reconciliation, subject to daily sweeping of balances into the Tier-1 bank and contractual client-fund safeguarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital formation and subsequent capital increases must be routed through a Luxembourg credit institution holding a capital-blocking certificate (attestation bancaire de blocage) for notarization; for a SARL the statutory minimum capital is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid in cash. The compliant routing is: shareholder instructs its foreign bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-resident shareholders) to the SARL's account at BIL/BGL/BCEE using the SARL's IBAN LUxx, with field 70 remittance information: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION LU_SARL — FORMATION/INCREASE, NOTARY DEED [date], RCS LUXEMBOURG [number]'; the bank will freeze the funds until the notary confirms completion of incorporation and issuance of court-approved RCS extract. For balance-of-payments statistics, classify the flow as direct-investment equity capital under BCL/Eurosystem reporting; the bank includes the capital-blocking certificate in the notarial file. No prior BCL approval is required for inbound euro transfers, but the source of funds must be documented for AML purposes.Eurozone suppliers are paid by SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant using ISO 20022 pain.001 messages; cross-border non-EU trade disbursements use SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers with full beneficiary identifiers and reference to the underlying warehouse-services or logistics invoice. For larger multi-country equipment purchases, the SARL may instruct Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) or standby letters of credit (ISP98) through BGL BNP Paribas or BIL, but standby/LC usage is expected to be rare for a low-risk storage business; open-account MT103 rails are preferred. Outbound dividends and shareholder-loan repayments require a separate MT103 with reference to the general meeting decision, the audited financial statements, and the applicable double-tax-treaty article; Luxembourg domestic dividend withholding of 15% applies unless reduced under treaty, and the paying bank may ask for a tax-residence certificate of the shareholder. All outbound transfers are subject only to transaction-based BCL statistical reporting thresholds and CSSF/EU funds-tracing rules; there is no exchange-control approval.EUR/USD outright deliverable FX forwards with maturity up to 12 months under an ISDA 2002 or bank standard master agreement; permitted only against documented underlying USD-denominated warehousing receivables or payables.EUR/CHF and EUR/GBP deliverable FX forwards for Swiss-pound and sterling-invoiced pharma clients; tenor matched to the commercial invoice cycle and limited to 100% of notional exposure.EUR/USD and EUR/CHF cross-currency swaps for capital-expenditure or shareholder-loan liabilities exceeding EUR 5 million notional; subject to EMIR margining, clearing, and trade-reporting obligations applicable to non-financial counterparties above the clearing threshold.FX options and zero-cost collar structures (EUR call / USD put) on forecast USD receipts, with an internal policy capping delta exposure at 25% of the next 12-month forecast and requiring senior-management approval.FX spot outright transactions (T+2) for routine treasury conversion via the bank's electronic dealing desk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for a Luxembourg-resident SARL as a primary hedge because the euro is fully convertible; NDFs are limited to hedges of subsidiary-entity cash flows located in currencies with capital controls, and even then only through a licensed Luxembourg credit institution with IFRS 9 fair-value accounting and full EMIR documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as a supplemental payment-collection channel, but not viable as the primary treasury and clearing bank. Luxembourg-licensed E-Money Institutions under the CSSF E-Money Law 2010 and PSD2 can conduct SEPA credit transfers, FX conversion, and virtual multi-currency accounts, but they cannot issue or confirm Commercial Letters of Credit, provide UCP600 documentary trade services, hold BCL settlement reserves, or grant overdraft/working-capital facilities. For a cold-storage logistics SARL servicing pharma clients, the recommended architecture is a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution for all account-balance, escrow, trade, and liquidity functions, with a supervised EMI (e.g., licensed Luxembourg branch of Airwallex, Currencies Direct, or similar) restricted to cross-border receivable acceleration and automated reconciliation, subject to daily sweeping of balances into the Tier-1 bank and contractual client-fund safeguarding.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate standard rate for Luxembourg City (CIT 17% + unemployment surcharge 7% of CIT + municipal business tax 6.75%); municipal business tax varies by commune of the exploitation centre.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Luxembourg SARL; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders (>=10% interest or >= EUR 1.2 million participation held >=12 months); DTA reductions typically 5% for direct holdings >=10% and 15% for portfolio holdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party controlled transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 trigger mandatory contemporaneous local documentation. Master File: required where the group's consolidated annual net turnover reaches EUR 750 million. Thresholds are in EUR.

Economic substance scope: No freestanding Economic Substance Regulations (UAE-style) exist in Luxembourg; substance is enforced through (i) place of effective management/central administration for tax residence, (ii) beneficial ownership requirements for DTA and EU Directive relief, (iii) the Luxembourg GAAR / abuse-of-law doctrine, (iv) ATAD interest limitation (30% EBITDA) and anti-hybrid rules, and (v) the anticipated EU Unshell Directive tests. A cold-storage SARL must maintain physical premises, employees, board meetings and logistics decision-making in Luxembourg; an offshored management fee or booking-shell structure will be recharacterised and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: fine of EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of 2 to 10 years (aggravated); administrative reassessment at 100% of evaded CIT/WHT; default interest at 0.6% per month; possible publication of sanction.

Director personal liability: Signing directors (gérants) face criminal liability, personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans and asset freezes; civil courts may order restitution of evaded taxes and disqualification from board positions.
Late / non-filing of form 700

Statutory sanction: Late-filing fine of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500; summary assessment (taxation d'office) with 10% surcharge; 0.6% monthly default interest on sums due; recovery costs charged to the company.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives remain personally exposed to fines; the tax authority can seize the director's personal estate where corporate assets are insufficient; flagrant recidivism leads to criminal proceedings.
Shell company / lack of substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the entity as fiscally transparent; denial of Luxembourg tax residence; refusal of participation exemption; re-allocation of profits to the foreign beneficial owner; 15% deemed-dividend WHT assessments; denial of DTA benefits under beneficial-ownership/GAAR analysis.

Director personal liability: Nominee and resident directors may be held personally liable for complicity in abuse of law; civil restitution orders for unpaid WHT/CIT; travel bans or entry restrictions during tax-criminal investigations; disqualification as Luxembourg statutory directors.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 2,500 for missing documentation; re-assessment of taxable income to the arm's-length profit; 0.6% monthly default interest; burden of proof shifts to taxpayer; risk of double taxation and additional penalties for gross negligence.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's-length intercompany fees without a transfer-pricing study can be sued for corporate loss; the tax authority may pierce the veil to enforce the TP adjustment against directors' personal assets in cases of gross negligence.
AML / beneficial ownership (RBE) non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious AML breaches (Law of 22 January 2023); imprisonment up to 3 years for individuals; RBE register fines between EUR 500 and EUR 25,000 per violation.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for AML fines; personal criminal conviction; travel bans and seizure of personal assets; civil liability to third parties for damages caused by missing UBO filings.
Dividend withholding tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of 15% WHT on the gross distribution plus 0.6% monthly default interest; penalty of up to 100% of evaded WHT in bad-faith cases; criminal fine of EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment for fraudulent schemes.

Director personal liability: The gérant acts as state collection agent; personal joint and several liability for the unwithheld WHT; seizure of director assets; travel bans pending recovery; disqualification from management of Luxembourg companies.

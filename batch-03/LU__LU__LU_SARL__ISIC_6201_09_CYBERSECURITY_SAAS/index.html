Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office without a physical Luxembourg situs is not acceptable as the statutory registered office under Articles 161 and 186 of the consolidated Law of 10 August 1915. The SARL must maintain a real, physical address in Luxembourg at which the statutory books, share register, accounting records and corporate documents are accessible and at which legal process and administrative service can be effected. Professional domiciliation under the Law of 31 May 1999 by a Ministry-of-Justice-approved domiciliation agent is expressly permissible and is the accepted market practice for a remote-first cybersecurity SaaS, provided the agent operates a physical Luxembourg office, provides registered-agent and mail-handling services, retains the statutory records and supports UBO-compliance duties; the domiciliation agreement and the identity of the authorized registered agent must be notified to the RCS.

Minimum subscribed and paid-up capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL, fully paid at subscription; funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or escrow and released only after RCS registration; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT. Digital services: MyGuichet.lu, eTVA filing portal, RCS/BRC business register

E-government filing portal

The SARL is registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through a notary and appointed approved statutory auditor where required. Upon RCS registration the ACD issues a Luxembourg corporate tax identification number (N° d'identification national). For VAT, the SARL must file Form 510a with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number before invoicing, intra-EU acquisitions, imports, or e-service supplies. Non-resident foreign entities supplying taxable services into Luxembourg with a fixed establishment or economic nexus must similarly register via a Luxembourg authorised representative and provide proof of non-resident status and place-of-supply documentation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The SARL is registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through a notary and appointed approved statutory auditor where required. Upon RCS registration the ACD issues a Luxembourg corporate tax identification number (N° d'identification national). For VAT, the SARL must file Form 510a with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number before invoicing, intra-EU acquisitions, imports, or e-service supplies. Non-resident foreign entities supplying taxable services into Luxembourg with a fixed establishment or economic nexus must similarly register via a Luxembourg authorised representative and provide proof of non-resident status and place-of-supply documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The SARL is per se opaque and taxable at entity level. Available elections: Luxembourg tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for a 95%+ owned Luxembourg subsidiary group, VAT grouping election as a single taxable person under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT law, and parent-subsidiary participation exemption election. ATAD hybrid mismatch rules may preclude or neutralise elective payment/classification outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for the domestic small-business exemption; no threshold for foreign businesses with a Luxembourg fixed establishment or deemed establishment. Non-Union OSS optional for EU B2C e-services; Union OSS for intra-EU B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT with proof of dispatch/transport. Electronically supplied services, cybersecurity SaaS, cloud vulnerability scanning, and penetration testing qualify as 'electronically supplied services' under Article 58 of the EU VAT Directive. B2B supplies to a non-Luxembourg EU VAT-registered customer fall under the reverse-charge procedure; B2B or B2C supplies addressed to a customer outside the EU/territorial scope may be zero-rated if the supplier proves the customer's establishment and effective receipt of the service at that overseas location. Pure domestic supplies are taxable at 17% standard rate.

  • Customs/Intrastat Export Declaration (SAD/DE-507) where goods are simultaneously exported
  • Transport documents: Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice with explicit zero-rating / reverse-charge annotation
  • Executed service contract or order form evidencing the customer's non-Luxembourg/non-EU establishment
  • Proof of customer's foreign VAT ID or TIN where available
  • Payment and inward remittance evidence: bank transfer, SWIFT/SEPA records, card settlement statements
  • Technical records: IP logs, server location metadata, access logs substantiating the place of effective use and enjoyment outside Luxembourg

No national real-time fiscal clearance/API/stamping mandate exists. Electronic invoicing is accepted and encouraged. Mandatory PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing (UBL or Factur-X via PEPPOL network) applies for B2G invoices and public procurement contracts. Standard EU VAT e-invoicing rules apply; invoices must include VAT details and reverse-charge or zero-rating references.

The Luxembourg tax authorities may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and reassess standard-rate VAT plus default interest and penalties for a five-year limitation period (ten years for tax fraud). Penalties range from administrative fines under the Luxembourg tax code (Abo) and VAT law up to EUR 250,000, and criminal penalties including imprisonment for wilful evasion or fraudulent false export declarations. Clawback may include the full under-collected VAT, interest, and disqualification from future zero-rating treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without exchange controls. The Euro (EUR) is freely floated and fungible; Luxembourg maintains no capital or current account convertibility restrictions, benefiting from complete capital-movement liberalization under TFEU Article 63. Profit remittance is frictionless: dividends, interest, and fees may be transferred abroad without prior central-bank approval, subject only to the corporate-law net-asset distribution test (Luxembourg Companies Act Art. 725-1) and the receiving bank's AML clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts (USD, GBP). The SARL may freely retain, convert, or hold proceeds in any EU-authorized credit institution or passported payment institution.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential and AML conduct oversight is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as an ancillary collection rail, but Low as a primary settlement bank. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) holding a valid PSD2 passport lodged with the CSSF may lawfully open EUR payment accounts and process SaaS payouts; however, an offshore digital EMI without a European passport cannot legally hold segregated EUR accounts nor settle funds for the SARL. The core treasury account structure must remain at a CSSF-licensed credit institution to preserve creditworthiness, evidence treasury centralization for Luxembourg tax purposes, and avoid concentration risk on unregulated custodians.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six (6) months after the end of the fiscal year. For calendar-year taxpayers, the statutory deadline is 30 June of the following year; the ACD routinely grants an administratively extendable deadline to 31 December on standard request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File – mandatory for groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding EUR 50,000,000. Local File – mandatory for Luxembourg entities where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 500,000 (or EUR 1,000,000 for financial transactions), as set out in administrative guidance. Country-by-Country Report – required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies economic substance standards through the OECD/BEPS framework, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) transposition, and the domestic General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). The ACD may disregard artificial arrangements lacking real business purpose and economic substance (Articles 6 AStG and 106 LIR). For a cybersecurity SaaS SARL, evidence of substance includes local office premises, qualified technical staff exercising cybersecurity product development and client oversight, hosting infrastructure decisions, management of SaaS contracts and risk, and local bank accounts. IP-holding and financing entities are particularly scrutinized. A shell company without these substance indicators is exposed to treaty denial, reclassification of income, additional assessments, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment for one to five years (Articles 39 and 40 LIR). Civil re-assessment of evaded tax plus an administrative fine of 10% to 100% of the evaded tax; late interest at 0.6% per month; court conviction may be published; professional licence restrictions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and de facto officers face criminal liability and can have the corporate veil pierced for fraudulent conduct. Personal assets may be seized to settle tax restitution; travel bans may be imposed; imprisonment is available for aggravated fraud; civil restitution orders against natural persons.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend WHT

Statutory sanction: The unpaid WHT becomes a corporate debt; ACD may impose administrative penalties up to 10% of the withheld amount, late interest of 0.6% per month, and joint and several assessment against the company and its officers.

Director personal liability: Under Luxembourg enforcement rules, the responsible manager or director is personally liable for withheld taxes not remitted to the treasury. The benefit of inventory does not apply, enabling direct seizure of directors' personal bank accounts and assets; misuse of corporate assets may also trigger criminal prosecution.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ACD adjusts taxable income upward under Article 56bis LIR and OECD TP rules. A 10% penalty on the additional assessment may be imposed, plus monthly late interest. Corresponding adjustments may be denied where documentation is insufficient.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns without reviewing TP documentation, or who approve fabricated TP analyses, may be civilly liable for the additional tax and penalties. Intentional mispricing is tax fraud and can result in criminal fines, director imprisonment, and travel restrictions during investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ACD may apply the GAAR to disregard the entity, re-attribute income to the beneficial owners, deny treaty benefits, and reclassify transactions. The minimum net wealth tax may be increased to EUR 3,210 where substance tests are not met; VAT deregistration and reputational sanctions may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for trading debts where the entity is used heterogeneously or as a façade; criminal complicity in tax evasion may lead to personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced for deliberate abuse, allowing attachment of personal estates.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the amended Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF supervisory practice, administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for laundering include imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and fines up to EUR 1 million; asset freezing and public sanction registers apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners can be personally prosecuted for AML failures. Regulatory prohibition on holding management functions, travel bans pending proceedings, and restitution orders against personal assets are possible. The corporate veil is pierced for severe compliance failures.

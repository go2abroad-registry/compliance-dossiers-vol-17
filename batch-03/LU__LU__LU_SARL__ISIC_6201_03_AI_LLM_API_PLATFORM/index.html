Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated; SARL provisions under Articles 180 to 185-1 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS); Law of 2 September 2011 on the Business Establishment Permit

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for a standard SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation; the capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution and confirmed by a bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) delivered to the notary prior to execution of the deed; the alternative EUR 1 capital regime is available only for micro-enterprises and subject to statutory payment restrictions

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the Luxembourg premises issued by the CGDIS (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours); structural load inspection for the server and IT equipment room; workplace safety inspection and risk assessment under the ITM (Inspection du travail et des mines); electrical installation conformity certificate for the registered office

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum share capital for a standard SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation; the capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution and confirmed by a bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) delivered to the notary prior to execution of the deed; the alternative EUR 1 capital regime is available only for micro-enterprises and subject to statutory payment restrictions

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg (www.impotsdirects.public.lu). VAT: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg (www.aedt.public.lu). Customs: Administration des douanes et accises (ADA). Unified taxpayer portal and e-filing: www.myguichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

SOP: (1) file the notarised deed of incorporation with Luxembourg Business Registers (www.lbr.lu) to obtain the 11-digit national identification number (matricule d'identification); (2) after RCS registration, file VAT registration form (form 176/F/3 or myguichet.lu eDesk application) with the AEDT within 15 days of commencing taxable activity, receiving a LU + 8-digit VAT identification number; (3) ACD corporate income tax file is opened automatically upon RCS registration, and notices are issued to the registered office; (4) a non-resident entity supplying B2B/B2C digital services into Luxembourg without an establishment may register directly under the OSS/IOSS or standard VAT registration, and a tax representative is required only where no EU establishment exists and special regimes so prescribe.

Participation exemption fully exempts qualifying dividends (100%) and capital gains (100%) on qualifying shareholdings; foreign PE profits are exempt under treaty/PE exemption. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Luxembourg-resident SARL is fully taxable on worldwide profits: corporate income tax 17% + 7% solidarity surcharge (effective 18.19%) + municipal business tax of approx. 6.75% (Luxembourg City) = aggregate headline rate approx. 24.94%. Participation exemption fully exempts qualifying dividends (100%) and capital gains (100%) on qualifying shareholdings; foreign PE profits are exempt under treaty/PE exemption. SARL is not a pass-through entity; no fiscal transparency applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Mandatory entity-level taxation unless an entity opts into, or satisfies the conditions of: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for CIT and municipal business tax requiring a direct or indirect holding of at least 95% for a continuous 5-year commitment; (2) VAT group election (assujetti unique) for persons established in Luxembourg; (3) option to treat certain transparent partnerships as CIT taxpayers under Art. 172bis LIR (not applicable to an SARL); (4) EU ATAD hybrid mismatch neutralisation rules apply (Directives 2016/1164 and 2017/952) without elective repatriation, and the IP box election (80% exemption) is available for qualifying IP income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-resident suppliers: EUR 0 (no threshold; any taxable supply of digital services in Luxembourg triggers VAT registration unless covered by OSS/IOSS). Domestic SME exemption: EUR 35,000 annual turnover (Art. 57 VAT Law). Intra-EU distance sales of goods: EUR 100,000 EU-wide annual threshold; imports of goods in consignments not exceeding EUR 150: IOSS. Mandatory registration applies for B2C telecommunication, broadcasting and e-services irrespective of turnover when the customer is established in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to (a) export of goods to destinations outside the EU, evidenced by transport documents leaving the customs territory of the Union; (b) intra-EU B2B supplies of digital services subject to reverse charge; (c) B2B services to non-EU business customers where place of supply is outside Luxembourg; (d) B2C digital services to consumers outside the EU and outside the Union VAT area, which fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope. For AI/LLM API platform services supplied B2B to EU customers, the reverse charge applies (place of supply at customer location); for B2C EU customers, VAT applies at 17% via OSS. No statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline is codified in Luxembourg law; the 180-day convention reflects prudent OECD transfer-pricing practice and standard commercial invoice terms.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Single Administrative Document) accepted and cleared by Luxembourg Customs (ADA) or another EU customs office of export
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export accompanying document (EAD) and transport contract
  • Proof of payment and, where applicable, Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation of receipt in EUR/USDC

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing through the PEPPOL network applies from 1 January 2023 for public sector supplies; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, with EDI and structured e-invoice formats accepted. VAT returns are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu on a monthly or quarterly basis; intra-EU recapitulative statements (ESL) are filed monthly; OSS and IOSS returns are filed quarterly through the national portal.

Luxembourg tax and customs authorities may claw back unjustified zero rating within a 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud, wilful default or material irregularity (Art. 114 VAT Law). Deliberate VAT fraud is punishable by fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years (Art. 248 VAT Law; Art. 396 LIR); negligent or incorrect filing triggers surcharges from 10% to 200% of the tax due. Customs export fraud penalties reach EUR 250,000 per material false declaration. Supporting customs, transport and invoicing records must be retained for at least 5 years, or 10 years where fraud is suspected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully integrated member of the Economic and Monetary Union (EMU). The euro is freely convertible on both current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls or restrictions on capital movements under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the Luxembourg Law of 11 January 2008 implementing EU free movement of capital provisions. Profit remittance is generally unrestricted; dividends may be freely distributed subject to the Luxembourg SARL's articles of association, the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies as amended, and compliant corporate formalities (e.g., approval of annual accounts by the shareholders' meeting and clearance of the corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax arrears). There are no convertibility hurdles for resident or non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg and the eurozone have no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts, and no requirement to repatriate proceeds to a specific local bank account. All receipts may be held in EUR or any freely convertible currency in accordance with standard treasury policy.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); in the context of the Eurosystem, the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises supranational monetary authority for the eurozone, including Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to medium viability. Luxembourg-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are supervised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and may offer local IBANs, but most established EMIs focus on payment services rather than complex corporate treasury cash management. Offshore digital EMIs (non-EU/EEA licensed, e.g., from the UK or the British Virgin Islands) cannot legally provide payment services to Luxembourg entities without an EU/EEA passport. Such EMIs may be used only in limited, non-prudential areas (e.g., receivable collection) but are not recommended as primary clearing rails for a regulated SARL due to EU passporting constraints and CSSF compliance expectations.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (IRC); combined Luxembourg City statutory burden 22.80% including 7% solidarity surcharge and 6.75% municipal business tax; effective rate approx. 21.72% after deduction of municipal tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for a 12-month period; treaty rates of 0%, 5% or 10% may apply to non-EU shareholders depending on shareholding and beneficial ownership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 100 must be filed within twelve months after the fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the deadline is 31 December of the following year; later extensions may be granted on request to the Luxembourg tax administration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CbCR/Master File obligation arises when the multinational group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750 million; Luxembourg tax circulars additionally require Local File and TP documentation when annual controlled transactions exceed EUR 250,000; arm's-length benchmarking is mandatory for all related-party transactions in AI/LLM API licensing and token monetisation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have a distinct ESR statute comparable to BVI/UAE, but ATAD, GAAR, and Administrative Circular LIR n° 56/1 require real staff, office, business decision-making, risk-taking capability, and economic substance to retain Luxembourg tax residence, treaty benefits, and transfer-pricing recognition. A letterbox SARL controlling AI/LLM token revenue without actual activity is exposed to treaty denial and recharacterisation of its seat.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fraudulent tax evasion and false declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years per count; additional tax assessment of evaded tax plus interest at 2.6%; civil penalties between 20% and 200% of the evaded amount; loss of loss-carryforwards and all credits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; criminal travel bans, asset freezes, and confiscation orders may be imposed; corporate veil may be pierced where the SARL is a mere façade.
Shell company / letterbox company without economic substance

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the Luxembourg seat, reallocate profits to the jurisdiction of effective management, deny DTA benefits and treaty withholding relief, and assess 10% to 200% penalties, plus 15% dividend withholding on deemed distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers can be made personally liable for re-assessed tax, subject to personal bankruptcy, travel bans, and imprisonment up to three years in cases of intentional abuse.
Transfer pricing and arm's-length abuse on AI APIs / USDC revenue

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustments restore the profit to arm's-length level; additional tax bears 5% monthly surcharge up to 20% plus 2.6% late interest; abusive adjustment is recharacterised as a constructive dividend subject to 15% withholding tax; criminal prosecution applies if false documentation is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer-pricing documentation or approve non-arm's-length intercompany agreements are liable in civil damages for the tax deficit; in agreement with fraud, personal criminal liability, travel bans and disqualification orders apply.
Disguised profit repatriation through loans, royalties or cryptocurrency transfers

Statutory sanction: Payment is treated as a constructive dividend taxed at 15% Luxembourg withholding tax; non-payment triggers automatic penalties of 5% on the unpaid withholding tax per month, a fine up to EUR 1 million for serious failures, and possible criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Company officers who fail to discharge withholding obligations become personally liable for the withheld amount; criminal imprisonment up to two years, travel bans, and civil restitution orders where intent and personal benefit are demonstrated.

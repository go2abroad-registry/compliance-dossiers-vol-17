Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Inland Revenue (direct taxes); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – VAT Authority; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) hosted by the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) – Customs and Excise; unified e-government portal MyGuichet.lu operated by the State Information Technology Centre (CTIE) and requiring LuxTrust electronic authentication.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the SARL deed and file with the RCS via the Luxembourg Business Registers platform to obtain the commercial registration number and the 13-digit national identification number (matricule). Step 2: File the corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC), and net wealth tax (IFF) registration with ACD using the matricule; the ACD issues the direct tax file number. Step 3: Apply to AEDT for VAT registration on Form 640/641, obtaining the Luxembourg VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix. Step 4: Apply to ADA for an EORI number to clear exports. Step 5: Foreign entities not established in Luxembourg that make taxable supplies of goods or services subject to VAT in Luxembourg must register directly with AEDT from the first taxable transaction, without any local turnover threshold; non-established suppliers may also use the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales and electronically supplied services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Luxembourg SARL is fully subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide profits, with no US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections include: (i) fiscal integration/group taxation (intégration fiscale) for qualifying groups with direct or indirect 95% ownership linking Luxembourg-resident companies and Luxembourg permanent establishments of EU/EEA companies; (ii) the participation exemption election for qualifying dividend income and capital gains on qualifying shareholdings (minimum 10% participation or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost for dividends, and 10% participation held 12 months for capital gains, subject to anti-abuse and substance requirements); (iii) the small-enterprise VAT franchise option allowing exemption for turnover below EUR 35,000, with the elective right to apply normal VAT; (iv) reverse-charge self-assessment for intra-community acquisitions and imported services; and (v) mandatory hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD that override elective outcomes for cross-border hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for established Luxembourg taxable persons; registration is compulsory from the first euro of taxable turnover. Small-business franchise exemption may apply below EUR 35,000 annual turnover excluding VAT, but the taxpayer can elect to waive the exemption. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg, except where the customer is liable for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism. Intra-EU distance sales of goods and certain digitally supplied services to non-taxable Luxembourg consumers trigger Luxembourg VAT registration only when the EUR 10,000 Union-wide annual threshold is exceeded, unless the supplier elects Luxembourg taxation through the OSS regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf, where prima facie proof of export is obtained within 90 days from the time of supply; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid VAT identification number in another EU Member State, with the supply reported in the VIES recapitulative statement; and (iii) certain offshore services, including installation, commissioning, calibration, and post-sale maintenance of the color Doppler ultrasound consoles performed outside the EU for a recipient established outside the EU, provided the services are directly linked to exported goods. The exporter must hold complete documentary evidence demonstrating that the goods left the EU customs territory and reached the foreign customer; the customs export declaration must be lodged electronically by the exporting SARL or its customs agent.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (SAD/EX-A) endorsed by Luxembourg Customs with a validated Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list identifying the ultrasound console by model, serial number, quantity, and value
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical carriage to a destination outside the EU
  • Transport contract and delivery note or proof of receipt signed by the foreign distributor/hospital evidencing arrival at the overseas destination
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice demonstrating repatriation of the foreign-currency export proceeds to the Luxembourg SARL
  • VIES listing confirmation of the customer and recapitulative statement for intra-community supplies (where applicable)
  • Export licence, CE/medical-device clearance certificate, and importing-country regulatory approval, as required by the destination market

Luxembourg currently has no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory digital stamping, and no transactional API reporting obligation for B2B invoicing. E-invoicing is accepted provided that the invoice guarantees authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility, typically through EDI, structured XML, or digital signature. A PEPPOL-based e-invoicing channel is available for B2G supplies, but not yet mandatory. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework, Luxembourg is expected to adopt mandatory electronic invoicing and real-time digital transaction reporting from approximately 2030. AEDT requires retention of all VAT books and e-invoices for 10 years in a legible electronic format, and access must be available for the tax authority on demand.

Luxembourg AEDT applies strict scrutiny to export zero-rating claims, cross-checking the customs MRN, VIES records, transport documents, and bank receipts. A wrongly zero-rated export is reassessed at the standard 17% VAT, with statutory late-payment interest accruing monthly from the original due date, plus recovery of any input VAT improperly credited. In cases of fraudulent export claims, forged documentation, or artificial circular export arrangements, Luxembourg imposes administrative fines, retrospective clawback of all contested VAT refunds, and criminal penalties for tax fraud of up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years for the responsible directors. The limitation period for the tax administration to reassess and recover is prolonged to 10 years where fraud is established, with the burden of proving the physical export falling on the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-65 TFEU. No exchange-control approval, licence, or repatriation permit is required to convert EUR or foreign currency, inject capital, pay suppliers, or remit dividends/profits abroad. The euro is fully convertible. Profit distribution from the SARL is constrained only by the solvency rules of the Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (net-asset test and interim-accounts test for interim dividends) and by domestic dividend withholding tax, not by FX regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and EU law impose no surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or holding-period restriction on export proceeds. The SARL may retain 100% of EUR and foreign-currency export receipts onshore or offshore, subject only to BCL balance-of-payments/external-statistics declarations when triggering reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), a member of the Eurosystem. BCL performs monetary, payment-systems, and external-statistics functions. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises banking conduct and AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury only. EU-authorised fintech/EMI providers (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, MultiPass) may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2, but they cannot hold the capital-escrow account, cannot issue documentary commercial letters of credit, and often rely on third-party correspondent banks for high-value MT103 flows required for medical-device trade. They are adequate for sub-€50,000 collection/disbursement flows and payroll; the principal clearing account and all USD/CNH trade settlements should remain with a Tier-1 domestic bank. Legal constraints: a non-EEA EMI must hold an EEA/Luxembourg authorisation to provide services locally, and all transfers must satisfy the Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU) 2015/847 with complete originator and beneficiary data.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the non-resident shareholder to the SARL: single SWIFT MT103 in EUR with an unbroken reference chain — shareholder's name, the company's RCS/LEI, the notarial deed or board resolution, and the BCL external-statistics purpose code for 'direct investment – equity capital'. For EEA-based shareholders, a SEPA Credit Transfer may be used only below the bank's reporting threshold; otherwise MT103 is mandatory. Funds must be credited and blocked on the capital escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution before the notary obtains the 'blocage de fonds' certificate and RCS registration is completed. Applicable SARL capital requirement: minimum €12,000, at least half (€6,000) paid in cash at incorporation. Do not split payments or mix trade invoices with equity transfers.(1) Open-account or advance-payment settlements to suppliers of components, display systems, and transducers: SWIFT MT103 with commercial-invoice reference and full Transfer of Funds Regulation data — TARGET2 for EUR, correspondent clearing via CHIPS/Fedwire for USD. (3) EU supplier payments below €500,000 can be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), with e-filing available via www.lbr.lu and the Guichet.lu portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales); Title I and Articles 100-112 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée; Law of 2 September 2011 on the access to regulated professions, where applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif – notarial deed of incorporation containing the articles of association (statuts), filed with the RCS for registration and publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash. The capital must be deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before notarial execution, and a blocked-funds certificate must be obtained. The funds are released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance of the registered office and any administrative workspace is required from the local commune upon SDIS inspection; occupational health and safety declaration to ITM is required for any employee workstations; no structural load inspection applies to normal office occupancy.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash. The capital must be deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before notarial execution, and a blocked-funds certificate must be obtained. The funds are released only after RCS registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED FIRST MEETING OF FOUNDERS: 1) The subscription of the entire share capital of EUR 12,000, represented by 12,000 shares of EUR 1 each, is confirmed and accepted as fully paid. 2) The registered office is established at [Address], Luxembourg, and the manager is authorised to execute the lease and notify the RCS. 3) [Name], born [date], resident at [Address], is appointed sole gérant of the company, with power to represent the company, open bank accounts, and execute all registration and tax formalities. 4) All pre-incorporation acts and expenses are ratified. Passed unanimously by all founders.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – no industrial or manufacturing licensing authority applies to ISIC 7020 overseas market-entry consulting. For general business formalities, the competent authorities are the Ministry of Economy, the local commune, and the RCS.

Category: No operating, manufacturing, or commercial permit is required for a Luxembourg SARL engaged exclusively in management consultancy services under ISIC 7020.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg: Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for registration duties and VAT; e-government portal Guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu, with dedicated e-TVA electronic filing and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / RCS) company registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a Luxembourg SARL must (i) execute the bylaws before a notary and file with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), which automatically issues the 13-digit national business registration number (numéro matricule) used uniformly for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and social security references; (ii) file the VAT registration application (form 010) with the AEDT through MyGuichet.lu, after which the authority issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by eight digits; (iii) where the foreign entity has no permanent establishment in Luxembourg and makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg, VAT registration from the first euro is mandatory unless the reverse charge shifts liability to the customer or an OSS/IOSS scheme applies; (iv) opening a corporate bank account at a Luxembourg or EU credit institution is a practical precondition for obtaining inward remittance evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Luxembourg SARL is a resident company taxable at entity level on worldwide income, subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax, while qualifying dividends and capital gains benefit from the participation exemption regime; the SARL is not a pass-through entity and has no US-style check-the-box capacity.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide a check-the-box election. Statutory elections available to a SARL include: (a) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for corporate income tax, available where a Luxembourg parent directly holds at least 95% of the SARL and each consolidated Luxembourg subsidiary; (b) VAT group registration under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, allowing jointly liable VAT consolidation of Luxembourg establishments; (c) waiver of the small-enterprise VAT exemption under Article 57 of the VAT Law to enter the normal VAT system and deduct input VAT; (d) EU OSS/IOSS options for cross-border distance sales and B2C electronic services; (e) ATAD hybrid-mismatch alignments, with mandatory recharacterisation of deductible payments producing double-non-taxation outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000: Luxembourg domestic small-enterprise exemption threshold under Article 57 of the VAT Law for resident taxable persons with annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000; registration becomes mandatory when the threshold is exceeded, when intra-EU acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000, or for non-established foreign suppliers from the first euro of taxable Luxembourg supply unless the reverse charge or OSS/IOSS applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two distinct pathways apply to a Luxembourg SARL providing overseas market-entry consultancy: (1) B2B services: consultancy services are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and thus effectively zero-rated where the customer is a taxable person established in a third country and the general B2B place-of-supply rule under Article 58 of the VAT Directive (transposed at Articles 34-35 of the Luxembourg VAT Law) places the supply in the customer's jurisdiction; the Luxembourg 'use and enjoyment' carve-out may nonetheless re-characterise the supply as taxable in Luxembourg when the services are effectively used and enjoyed in Luxembourg. (2) Goods: physical exports of goods (e.g., printed market reports or regulatory deliverables) qualify for the 0% rate under Article 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law only where the goods are consigned to a destination outside the European Union and the exporter retains full documentary proof of physical export. Zero-rating remains conditional on proper books and records, and input VAT attributable to zero-rated or outside-scope supplies remains creditable in full or pro rata under the normal deduction rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Single Administrative Document (DAU) or electronic declaration lodged with the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency through the EU Automated Export System, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export acceptance record evidencing EU exit.
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (or CMR consignment note for road freight) showing the Luxembourg exporter as consignor and the named third-country destination, with carrier date stamp or electronic event log.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or a confirmation issued by a Luxembourg or EU paying bank evidencing receipt of export consideration in convertible currency.
  • Commercial invoice containing explicit zero-rating wording, Incoterms rule, country of destination, and a detailed breakdown of consultancy fees and deliverables.
  • Executed consultancy agreement or engagement letter together with feasibility reports, progress correspondence, and time records substantiating the overseas market-entry services actually rendered.
  • Foreign customer taxable-status evidence, such as an overseas business registry certificate or a foreign VAT/GST identification number, to support the B2B zero-rated treatment.

Luxembourg currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, e-invoicing API mandate, or digital stamping for domestic VAT. Electronic invoicing is accepted provided the authenticity and integrity of the invoice are guaranteed; Peppol and Factur-X are permitted voluntarily. Mandatory digitalisation applies to (i) electronic VAT return filing and payment through the e-TVA portal by companies subject to corporate income tax, and (ii) electronic lodging of customs/export declarations with the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency through the EU Automated Export System (AES) and the national single window (myguichet.lu / LU-CIS), with an MRN evidenced on each exported consignment.

The AEDT rigorously audits zero-rated and export-exempt supplies; where the documentary evidence bundle is missing, incomplete, or false, the supply is re-characterised as taxable in Luxembourg and 17% VAT is assessed on the gross consideration, plus late-payment interest under the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) at 0.6% per month and an evasion supplement of up to the full amount of the evaded tax. Intentional or materially false zero-rating declarations constitute VAT fraud under Articles 55-64 of the Luxembourg VAT Law and Articles 25-28 of the General Tax Law, exposing the SARL and its directors to criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 in aggravated cases, confiscation orders, and custodial sentences of up to five years. The standard reassessment limitation is five years, extended to ten years for fraudulent or concealed facts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for residents and non-residents, and Luxembourg applies the EU free-movement-of-capital rules (Article 63 TFEU). Capital-account and current-account operations are unrestricted; no exchange-control authorization, currency-transaction permit, or profit-repatriation license is required. Profit remittance is constrained only by ordinary company-law distribution tests (balance-sheet test, legal reserve, and going-concern test) and by any applicable withholding tax; once the SARL has fulfilled those tests, dividends can be paid freely by wire. All transfers remain subject to EU asset-freeze/sanctions regulations and to BCL balance-of-payments reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds repatriation, surrender, or mandatory conversion rules for the euro area/Luxembourg. A Luxembourg SARL may retain export receivables in a foreign-currency account or convert them into euro at the bank rate. The only routine regulatory requirement is statistical reporting to the BCL: payments received from non-residents, or sent to non-residents, must be declared by the SARL or by its bank through the national balance-of-payments reporting system when the transaction exceeds the applicable de minimis threshold. No conversion percentage is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL is the monetary, payments and statistical authority; the CSSF is the licensing, prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for banks. Luxembourg has no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary settlement rail; Low as the sole treasury rail. EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment-service providers are permitted in Luxembourg through the European passport under PSD2/EMD2 and may be used for low-value fee collections, multi-currency current accounts and the 'Wise_B2B' rails available to the client. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, enter into derivative contracts, or hold client funds in a deposit-guarantee-protected account; client funds must be safeguarded, not lent. Therefore the SARL must maintain a principal relationship with a Luxembourg or EEA licensed credit institution for trade settlements, FX hedging and subscription-capital custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial share capital, route a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for euro if from an EU account) to the SARL's formation or blocked account at the chosen Luxembourg bank. The beneficiary account name must exactly match the notarial deed and bank reference; the MT103 narrative should state 'direct investment – subscription of share capital in LU_SARL'. The bank will classify the inbound remittance for BCL balance-of-payments purposes under direct investment, inward equity capital, not as a trade or service payment. Required verification package: notarized articles of association, RCS or registration reference, shareholder identification and source-of-funds evidence, proof of full payment of the subscribed capital, and the completed bank KYC file. No CSSF consent is required solely for the capital route; the funds remain blocked until the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) files the incorporation deed. For subsequent intra-group loans or additional paid-in capital, the purpose code should reflect intercompany financing and, if material, a cross-border intra-group loan agreement and pricing benchmark must be supplied.The SARL should use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border client-facing settlements in EUR or USD and for paying foreign contractors; documentary trade settlements are executed through Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) opened by the bank and governed by UCP600 and ISBP745; import collections and documentary collections may also be used. Within the EEA, ordinary operational disbursements clear via SEPA Credit Transfer; high-value USD cross-border clearing uses SWIFT, CHIPS or the bank's correspondent network. Treasury disbursements must include BCL balance-of-payments reporting and sanction screening of every beneficiary, and the bank must append an accurate purpose code (services, direct investment or financial investment) to avoid regulatory misstatement.Outright forward foreign-exchange contracts (EUR/USD and EUR/non-EUR) with delivery on the contracted settlement date, documented as hedge-accounting instruments.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted emerging-market currencies that have no liquid onshore forward market, cash-settled at an agreed fixing source and approved by the bank's risk desk.Foreign-exchange swaps combining a spot and reverse forward transaction for managing funding mismatches and intercompany loan rollovers.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-term foreign-currency assets or loans, executed under an ISDA and CSA framework.European-style plain vanilla FX options purchased for downside-rate protection, with no barrier, binary or speculative credit enhancers.

Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), with mandatory prior execution of the constitutive deed before a Luxembourg notary (Chambre des Notaires du Grand-Duché de Luxembourg).

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Accounting Records and Annual Accounts of Undertakings; Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to the professions of craftsman, merchant, industrialist and certain liberal professions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation and articles of association (acte constitutif / statuts) executed before a Luxembourg notary, electronically filed with the RCS, and subsequently published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,500, fully subscribed at incorporation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 25% of each share's nominal value at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years, although full payment is market practice for an SFO; the capital must be blocked on a Luxembourg bank account and evidenced by a certificate of capital blocking (attestation de blocage de fonds) delivered to the notary; contributions in kind require a valuation report from an independent statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,500, fully subscribed at incorporation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 25% of each share's nominal value at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years, although full payment is market practice for an SFO; the capital must be blocked on a Luxembourg bank account and evidenced by a certificate of capital blocking (attestation de blocage de fonds) delivered to the notary; contributions in kind require a valuation report from an independent statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified e-services via MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor must first incorporate a Luxembourg entity (SARL) before a notary and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS). The RCS registration triggers automatic attribution of a national taxpayer number (numéro de matricule). If the entity engages in taxable supplies of goods/services in Luxembourg, separate VAT registration is filed with the AEDT through Form 640 or via MyGuichet.lu; non-resident entities with no place of business in Luxembourg must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative for VAT registration. The VAT ID (TVA number) is issued after evidence of taxable activity and, where applicable, posting of security.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign investor must first incorporate a Luxembourg entity (SARL) before a notary and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS). The RCS registration triggers automatic attribution of a national taxpayer number (numéro de matricule). If the entity engages in taxable supplies of goods/services in Luxembourg, separate VAT registration is filed with the AEDT through Form 640 or via MyGuichet.lu; non-resident entities with no place of business in Luxembourg must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative for VAT registration. The VAT ID (TVA number) is issued after evidence of taxable activity and, where applicable, posting of security.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a Luxembourg SARL. However, a SARL may apply to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) with Luxembourg subsidiaries, and may elect Luxembourg fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax if the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by anti-hybrid rules implementing ATAD (EU Directive 2016/1164), and no elective hybrid treatment is available for a standard single-family office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for mandatory VAT registration of established Luxembourg businesses; EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance-sale threshold for EU suppliers; no threshold for non-established suppliers of B2B services (reverse charge).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside Luxembourg and outside the EU are zero-rated (0%) if the supplier retains proof of export. Supplies of services to non-EU businesses are generally zero-rated where the recipient is a taxable person and effective use/exploitation is outside the EU; financial services and asset management supplied to an SFO remain exempt without credit rather than zero-rated. For goods, the supplier must obtain export confirmation from customs. For VAT-reclaim by non-established businesses, the 13th Directive/8th Directive refund procedures apply.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) / electronic ECS confirmation
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate / proof of payment
  • Evidence of transport from Luxembourg to destination outside the EU

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies (via PEPPOL/UBL); B2B invoices may be issued electronically by mutual consent. VAT-registered persons must file periodic recapitulative statements (EC sales lists) and VAT returns electronically through the AEDT portal. There is no requirement for real-time API validation or digital stamping from a fiscal device.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may reassess zero-rating within the normal limitation period (5 years, extended to 10 years in case of fraud). Fraudulent export claims are subject to administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded (usually 10%-50% depending on good faith), and criminal sanctions for tax fraud (imprisonment and fines) under the General Tax Law and LJF. Retroactive clawback of unjustified zero-rating plus default interest at the statutory rate is routinely applied upon insufficient documentation or missing intra-EU/export proof.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates a fully open capital and current account regime under EU law (Article 63 TFEU). The euro is freely convertible for all transactions, including capital injections, trade settlements, dividend remittances, and liquidation proceeds. There are no exchange-control permits, no prior central-bank approvals, and no currency quotas; the only practical frictions are (i) documentary proof of source of funds under AML/KYC rules and (ii) withholding tax compliance for outbound dividends (15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax, reduced under tax treaties or eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export receipts. A resident SARL may lawfully retain export proceeds in any currency; the only administrative obligation is the BCL/ECB balance-of-payments statistical reporting for cross-border transactions above the applicable reporting threshold (generally EUR 12,500 per transaction or monthly cumulative amount), which the servicing bank files on the client's behalf.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank of Luxembourg within the Eurosystem; the BCL is the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistical reporting, foreign-exchange reserve management, and compliance with European Central Bank regulations. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and enforces anti-money laundering and customer due diligence requirements under the Luxembourg AML Law of 25 March 2020 and CSSF Regulation 12-02.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low to Medium. EU-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions established in Luxembourg (Law of 14 April 2011 on e-money institutions; Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services) can offer payment accounts and SEPA/SWIFT rail access, but they cannot provide regulatory securities custody, credit lines, letters of credit, or FX hedging, and client funds are safeguarded outside the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL). An offshore non-EU EMI has no EU passporting rights and cannot lawfully serve as the primary treasury bank for a Luxembourg SARL without a Luxembourg/EU branch; EMIs should be used only as a temporary operational collection bridge, never for capital escrow or custody of family investment assets.

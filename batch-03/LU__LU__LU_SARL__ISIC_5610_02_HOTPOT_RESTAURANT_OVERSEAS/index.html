Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) – Luxembourg Inland Revenue for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; central electronic portal: guichet.lu (business registration and tax identification).

        
E-government filing portal
For non-resident taxable persons: submit the VAT registration form to the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number. For establishing a SARL: execute incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary, file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via the RCS e-Desk; the RCS process automatically issues the national business and tax identification number (n° d'identification), which is also used for VAT; no separate TIN application is required for direct taxes.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For non-resident taxable persons: submit the VAT registration form to the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number. For establishing a SARL: execute incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary, file with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) via the RCS e-Desk; the RCS process automatically issues the national business and tax identification number (n° d'identification), which is also used for VAT; no separate TIN application is required for direct taxes.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box election exists; statutory elections available include VAT groups (assujetti unique), fiscal unity for corporate income tax (intégration fiscale), and election to apply Luxembourg’s participation exemption regime for qualifying dividends and capital gains; ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply without elective deployment.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
17%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for established small businesses; non-established foreign suppliers must register from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg, subject to reverse-charge rules if applicable.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods outside the EU are subject to 0% VAT where the goods are removed from the EU within statutory time limits and the exporter maintains full evidence of export. Services to non-EU business customers qualify for 0% VAT when the place of supply is outside Luxembourg under place-of-supply rules; hotpot dining services physically performed overseas fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Luxembourg maintains full euro convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions under Article 63 TFEU. No exchange-control authorization is required for dividend remittance, franchise royalty payments, condiment-import settlements, intra-group loans, or equity injections. Dividend distributions by an SARL remain subject to the solvency and net-asset test under the Luxembourg Companies Act of 10 August 1915, as amended, shareholder approval at a general meeting, and a 15% domestic withholding tax, which is commonly reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) or to a lower treaty rate where the parent qualifies. Profit repatriation impediments are limited to standard corporate documentation, statutory audit thresholds, and statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the BCL for aggregated annual cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12.5 million.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion timeline, or compulsory repatriation threshold exists under Luxembourg law. Foreign-currency export receipts may be retained indefinitely in accounts held with CSSF-regulated credit institutions. The only binding requirements are: (i) statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for aggregate annual flows exceeding EUR 12.5 million; (ii) physical cash-courier declaration under EU Regulation 2018/1672 for cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more; and (iii) standard customs, VAT, and trade-invoice documentation for hotpot-condiment exports and imports. Treasury policy recommends internal conversion of material foreign-currency balances into EUR within 30 business days to dampen CNH/USD exposure, but this is an internal governance rule, not a legal surrender mechanism.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); banking prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Rated Medium as a supplementary treasury rail and Low as a primary banking layer. Only CSSF-licensed electronic money institutions, or EU-EEA EMIs validly passporting under PSD2, may lawfully hold payment funds for a Luxembourg SARL. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in the United Kingdom, Hong Kong, or Singapore, without an EU passport, are legally incapable of issuing the bankers' blocking attestation required by the Luxembourg notary for the statutory capital-blocage, and expose treasury funds to safeguarding limitations and redemption mechanics under Directive 2009/110/EC. Fintech EMIs are therefore usable only for ancillary, low-value, and capped disbursements (e.g., condiment-sample purchases, small vendor outpayments), with mandatory daily sweeping to a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution and absolute exclusion from the equity-injection and capital-escrow workflow.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Ultimate beneficial owner resident in the People's Republic of China or Hong Kong SAR holding more than 25% of the SARL through a multi-tier holding chain, generating source-of-wealth and layered-structure scrutiny.
        
Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner resident in the People's Republic of China or Hong Kong SAR holding more than 25% of the SARL through a multi-tier holding chain, generating source-of-wealth and layered-structure scrutiny.

        
Defense evidence: RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) filing extract, notarized and apostilled corporate ownership chart from the ultimate shareholder down to the Luxembourg SARL, certified passports of all UBOs with a >25% interest, a written source-of-wealth letter covering the share-capital subscription funds, 12-month bank statements of the subscribing parent entity, group audited financial statements, and a documented commercial rationale for the Luxembourg holding and direct-operated hotpot chain.

      

      
      

        Negative-news or sanctions screening flags attributed to Asia-Pacific condiment supply-chain counterparties, freight forwarders, procurement agents, or franchise partners.
        
Root cause: Negative-news or sanctions screening flags attributed to Asia-Pacific condiment supply-chain counterparties, freight forwarders, procurement agents, or franchise partners.

        
Defense evidence: Automated World-Check and consolidated EU/OFAC/UN sanctions screening snapshots for each counterparty, a physical-trade evidence trail comprising import customs declarations, bills of lading, commercial invoices, logistics agreements, and tax-identification verification, plus a negative-news remediation memorandum prepared by the appointed Luxembourg AML compliance officer documenting absence of sanctioned individuals and the closure of red flags.

      

      
      

        FATCA and CRS classification ambiguity where the SARL is owned by non-EU shareholders through a Hong Kong or Chinese parent, raising a risk of misclassification, misreporting, or opaque-structure determinations.
        
Root cause: FATCA and CRS classification ambiguity where the SARL is owned by non-EU shareholders through a Hong Kong or Chinese parent, raising a risk of misclassification, misreporting, or opaque-structure determinations.

        
Defense evidence: Executed CRS self-certifications on the Luxembourg standard form (Form 5900) for all account holders, Form W-8BEN-E or W-9 for FATCA indicia, tax-residence certificates issued by the relevant Chinese and Luxembourg tax authorities, and a documented CRS/FATCA data-flow matrix confirming the SARL's Luxembourg tax residence and entity-level taxable classification.

      

      
      

        Pre-incorporation or in-formation account opening: the SARL is not yet registered, and the bank is asked to open a blocked capital account without a final RCSL extract, creating signature-capacity and formation-structure risk.
        
Root cause: Pre-incorporation or in-formation account opening: the SARL is not yet registered, and the bank is asked to open a blocked capital account without a final RCSL extract, creating signature-capacity and formation-structure risk.

        
Defense evidence: Notarized draft articles of association with the subscribed-capital schedule, the notary's written instruction letter to the bank, a draft RCSL incorporation excerpt, certified identification documents of all founding directors, and a signed undertaking freezing withdrawals from the bloqueur until the RCSL registration certificate is delivered.

      

      
      

        Cash-intensive restaurant operations: daily POS cash takings, multi-store float movements, and condiment-supply receivables perceived as a money-laundering layering or commingling vector.
        
Root cause: Cash-intensive restaurant operations: daily POS cash takings, multi-store float movements, and condiment-supply receivables perceived as a money-laundering layering or commingling vector.

        
Defense evidence: Bank-integrated POS acquiring contract with automated end-of-day settlement to the operational bank account, a treasury cash-management policy requiring cash-in-transit collections with tamper-evident seals, monthly reconciliation of cash deposits against POS terminal reports, an operational cash-handling procedure, appointment of an internal AML compliance officer, and a documented transaction-monitoring protocol covering the Low_Risk_Standard banking-AML risk tier of ISIC 5610 full-service dining.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The statutory SARL minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (minimum paid-up portion EUR 3,000, being 25% of the subscribed capital) must be injected by the non-resident parent via SWIFT MT103 into the SARL's dedicated blocked capital-escrow account at the Luxembourg relationship bank. The SWIFT remittance information must state 'Subscription of SARL share capital - incorporation of LU_SARL' together with the relevant notary's file reference, and where the sending jurisdiction applies a purpose-code regime (e.g., SAFE code 621011 for outward direct investment from China), that code must be quoted in the payment header. The bank issues the statutory blocking attestation to the Luxembourg notary, the notary files incorporation with the RCSL, and the funds are released to the operational account only upon presentation of the final RCSL extract. For inbound transfers of EUR 10,000 or more, the bank applies origin-of-funds verification under Luxembourg AML law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended); no prior CSSF or BCL authorization is required for inbound equity in a dining-chain SARL, and the group's Low_Risk_Standard tier supports streamlined documentary processing.Trade settlements for hotpot-ingredient procurement, condiment supply-chain logistics, franchise-vendor payouts, and intra-EU food-service disbursements are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV wires in EUR, USD, or CNH for direct supplier payments; (ii) UCP 600-compliant commercial letters of credit for bulk condiment and restaurant-equipment imports from Asian suppliers, with the Luxembourg bank issuing or confirming the LC; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for recurring mid-value consignments; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for intra-EU payroll, rent, utility, and vendor obligations; and (v) bank-integrated POS merchant acquiring with T+1 EUR settlement for dining revenues. A multi-currency settlement account may be held at the relationship bank, but must be swept same-day into the EUR master operating account to enforce centralized liquidity control and to mitigate mismatches between CNH/USD import payables and EUR-denominated merchant receipts.EUR/USD outright forwards and FX swaps executed with the Luxembourg relationship bank under an ISDA master agreement or FX Global Code-aligned frameworkCNH/USD and CNH/EUR non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) transacted through a CSSF-regulated credit institution, supported by documented underlying hotpot-condiment import and supply-chain exposuresVanilla EUR/CNH and EUR/USD FX options (premium-paid) under the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions for revenue and gross-margin protectionEUR/USD and EUR/CNH cross-currency swaps for the management of medium-term intra-group loan exposures, subject to EMIR trade-reporting to an authorized trade repository and BCL statistical reportingCollateralized OTC forward contracts compliant with EMIR regulatory technical standards, including counterparty due-diligence documentation and margin arrangements acceptable to the Luxembourg treasury

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
24.94% aggregate combined Luxembourg City headline rate (17% corporate income tax / IRC + 7% solidarity surcharge on IRC + 6.75% municipal business tax); standalone IRC statutory rate is 17%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions by a resident SARL, unless reduced or exempted. Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption applies at 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10% for 12 continuous months; DTA rates may reduce the 15% statutory rate depending on the treaty and beneficial-ownership conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue threshold triggers mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting under EU DAC4; Luxembourg does not impose a separate fixed statutory EUR threshold for Local File/Master File, but OECD-compliant contemporaneous documentation must be available upon tax-audit request. Form 34 must be filed for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding the applicable Luxembourg administrative filing threshold.

      
Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary SARLs. Substance requirements are nonetheless embedded in OECD transfer-pricing rules, CJEU case-law principles (Cadbury Schweppes), Luxembourg CFC rules, DAC6 disclosure and beneficial-ownership/treaty-shopping jurisprudence. If adopted, the proposed EU Unshell Directive / ATAD 3 would require minimum indicators of substance for shell entities.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Offshore Profit Diversion
        
Statutory sanction: Additional corporate income tax and municipal business tax assessment on the full concealed worldwide income, 10% late-filing/omission surcharge after formal notice, monthly late-payment interest of 0.6%, and penal tax-interest accumulation. Wilful tax fraud is a criminal offense carrying fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment for responsible persons.

        
Director personal liability: A gérant who signs false tax returns or directs concealment can be held jointly and severally liable for restitution of the tax deficiency, civil damages to creditors and minority shareholders, and in criminal proceedings may face disqualification from managing companies, travel restrictions and imprisonment.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing and Form 34 Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may recompute the taxable base at the arm's-length normal value, deny deductibility of excessive royalty/interest or management fees, impose a surcharge up to 10% of the additional tax after formal notice and charge 0.6% monthly late-interest. Serious false-record offenses trigger criminal penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and the gérant may be personally liable for civil restitution where gross negligence in TP documentation caused the company's tax loss; criminal liability and travel bans may arise where false documentation was knowingly used as instruments of tax fraud.

      

      
      

        Shell-Company and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative recharacterization of the structure, denial of a Luxembourg tax-residence certificate, denial of treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits, CFC inclusion of shifted profits at the shareholder level, and in the event of the EU Unshell Directive, additional penalties for shell entities, including deemed income adjustments and exchange-of-information enforcement.

        
Director personal liability: Directors facilitating sham-board structures can be prosecuted for forgery, abuse of corporate form and tax fraud; civil liability includes disgorgement and restitution; criminal proceedings may impose travel bans, confiscation and imprisonment.

      

      
      

        AML, Cash-Handling and Authorised Economic Operator Risks
        
Statutory sanction: Fines under Luxembourg AML Law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) against legal persons can reach up to €5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; additional VAT assessments, customs penalties and disqualification of firms from European payment institutions may be imposed.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and executive officers are civilly and criminally liable for AML violations, including direct orders for restitution and personal fines; in money-laundering cases, responsible gérants face deprivation of liberty, travel bans and professional disqualification from management functions.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), e-filing via MyGuichet.lu and lbr.lu

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, LSC), as amended notably by the Law of 10 August 2016; SARL provisions under Articles 100-1 et seq.; general provisions under Articles 1-99; complemented by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the RESA (RCS/LBR Law).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif / statuts), filed as a certified RCS extract, followed by mandatory publication of the abstract in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum fully subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000 (LSC Art. 100-5, ex-Art. 102). Each cash share must be paid up to at least 25% of nominal value at subscription, but aggregate cash paid-up may not fall below EUR 12,000; in-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation. Cash is deposited in a blocked account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL en formation at a CSSF-licensed Luxembourg credit institution and is released only upon RCS registration, as evidenced by the bank's release certificate filed with the notary and the RCS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and registration duties; official portal: MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident SARL or its Luxembourg branch must submit VAT registration via the AED e-TVA portal or MyGuichet.lu using the Standard Audit File for Tax and VAT registration form; the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LV number) is issued once the taxable status is recognised. A corporate direct-tax ID is the national registration number assigned upon registration with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). A fiscal representative may be required where the foreign entity is not established in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A Luxembourg SARL is fully subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax at entity level. Resident entities are taxed on worldwide income, with the participation exemption applicable to qualifying shareholdings. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed at the SARL level on distributed profits unless treaty rules otherwise provide. — A non-resident SARL or its Luxembourg branch must submit VAT registration via the AED e-TVA portal or MyGuichet.lu using the Standard Audit File for Tax and VAT registration form; the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LV number) is issued once the taxable status is recognised. A corporate direct-tax ID is the national registration number assigned upon registration with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). A fiscal representative may be required where the foreign entity is not established in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Luxembourg SARL is fully subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax at entity level. Resident entities are taxed on worldwide income, with the participation exemption applicable to qualifying shareholdings. Non-resident shareholders are not taxed at the SARL level on distributed profits unless treaty rules otherwise provide.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms include: VAT grouping as a single taxable person, tax consolidation for resident direct 95%-owned subsidiaries, joint filing for certain transparent entities, and a formal election to apply the participation exemption. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are applied in accordance with ATAD as implemented in Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general zero-turnover registration threshold; any taxable person engaged in an independent economic activity must register for Luxembourg VAT. The small-business exemption applies only where annual taxable turnover does not exceed EUR 30,000; cross-border distance sales to Luxembourg consumers trigger registration at EUR 10,000 per calendar year under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, either by the supplier or by a customer not established in Luxembourg, and to services directly related to such exported goods, including GDP pharma freight and logistics services. Services performed for non-EU taxable customers, or effectively used and enjoyed outside the EU, may also qualify. Full documentary proof of physical exit, customs clearance, and commercial terms must be retained.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic export entry through the Luxembourg customs system)
  • Bill of lading / CMR / air waybill or other transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement proving receipt of the export consideration on a Luxembourg bank account

Luxembourg does not currently impose mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or an API-based clearance regime. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via PEPPOL in accordance with EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns are filed periodically in SIF/XML format through the e-TVA portal; there is no statutory continuous transaction control requirement.

The AED and customs authorities may audit the export movement, remittance trail, and documentary consistency. Where export substantiation fails, the supply is re-characterised as a taxable local supply; the VAT is retroactively due with late-interest. Administrative fines range from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax, and deliberate export fraud may lead to criminal penalties including imprisonment and publication of the conviction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Luxembourg imposes no exchange control authorization requirement, no capital export moratorium, and no restriction on profit remittance. A Luxembourg SARL may freely repatriate dividends, repay shareholder loans, and disburse trade payments in any currency. Dividend distributions are subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at the standard rate of 15%, with a complete exemption (0%) available under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period, and under applicable tax treaties for non-EU shareholders. The only statutory constraint is the fiduciary duty of the gérant to maintain share capital intact, preserving a mandatory legal reserve equal to 5% of annual net profits until reaching 10% of the registered share capital

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg has no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion ratio for export or trade receivables. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in freely convertible foreign-currency accounts held with Luxembourg banks. The only compliance obligation is statistical in nature: resident businesses must report their external assets, liabilities, and cross-border payments to the BCL under the Luxembourg balance-of-payments reporting regime, using the BCL coding framework, on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis depending on transaction volume

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as central bank; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as prudential and AML supervisor for credit institutions; European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy and TARGET2 oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for a Luxembourg-domiciled electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution licensed by the CSSF under the Law of 21 December 2022 on payment services and electronic money, which may provide payment accounts and card issuance through EU passporting; Low for offshore EMIs not established in the EU/EEA, which are disqualified as the primary treasury rail. Offshore EMIs cannot hold commercial bank money in the corporate's own name, do not have direct TARGET2 settlement access for euro clearing, and are ineligible for the statutory capital escrow function requiring a regulated Luxembourg credit institution. Such offshore entities may only serve as ancillary disbursement channels if passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2 from another EU Member State and remain subject to enhanced negative-news diligence by tier-1 domestic banks

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital and shareholder contributions must be received by SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the SARL's temporary capital escrow account (compte de blocus) at a Luxembourg credit institution, using the BCL balance-of-payments statistical purpose code for 'Direct investment - equity capital' in the payment narrative and referencing the notarised incorporation deed. Funds transferred from EEA banks settle same-day via TARGET2 through the BCL. The receiving bank must issue the capital release certificate (attestation de blocage) permitting the notary to authenticate incorporation. Shareholder loans are routed by MT103 with a corresponding debt-instrument purpose code and a fully documented loan agreement to prevent equity recharacterisation; commercial revenue from GDP freight activity enters via ordinary SWIFT wire or SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with clear invoice referencesTrade settlements to freight forwarders, GDP-qualified storage providers, and EU pharmaceutical consignees are executed via SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 COV for interbank cover where applicable) or, within the euro area, via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for same-day settlement through TARGET2; recurring warehousing and logistics charges may be collected through SEPA Direct Debit (SDD), which aligns with the Direct ACH rail profile. For non-EU logistics commitments, commercial documentary credits (SWIFT MT700) and documentary collections (MT756) are permissible where counterparty credit circumstances require; all outbound payments must be accompanied by the underlying freight invoice, transport document, and GDP compliance record in the compliance file to satisfy CSSF transaction monitoring expectationsOnshore deliverable EUR/USD forwards and FX swaps executed with Luxembourg-licensed credit institutions under ISDA or French-Danish Master Agreement documentationEUR/CHF and EUR/GBP rolling spot forwards (tom-next rollover contracts) for freight and warehousing invoice hedging in the EU/CHF/UK corridorCross-currency swap (EUR versus USD principal and interest exchange) permitted strictly in conjunction with an authorised euro-term loan facilityNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited solely to counterparties in non-convertible or restricted-currency jurisdictions where a deliverable forward market is absent; NDFs are unnecessary and unsuitable for EUR-based clearing because the euro is fully deliverable and TARGET2-liquid

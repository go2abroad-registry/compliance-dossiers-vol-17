Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg does not operate an exchange control system. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account by virtue of Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the 1999 Law on the Monetary Status of the Euro, and the CSSF/BCL regulatory framework. Dividends, distributions, loan repayments, and capital repatriation are payable without prior FX authorization. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate law formalities and withholding tax administration rather than currency convertibility constraints.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed repatriation deadlines for export receipts in Luxembourg. A Luxembourg SARL may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts or convert them freely. Periodic balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations apply to resident entities under ECB Regulation ECB/2013/31, but they do not restrict timing, conversion, or disposition.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); FX control and treasury-related compliance oversight is also exercised by the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for anti-money laundering, sanctions, and payment services supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium for EU-licensed fintech EMI solutions and Low for offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA. Luxembourg permits the use of CSSF-licensed electronic money institutions and EU/EEA EMIs passporting under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). An offshore EMI without an EU passport cannot lawfully maintain an operational payment account for a Luxembourg SARL. Even with a compliant EMI, the SARL may still require a credit institution account to meet capital escrow and substance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Luxembourg entity's net annual turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50,000,000 and cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 in the case of financing transactions or EUR 1,000,000 for other transactions; Master File is mandatory where the consolidated worldwide group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; below these thresholds the SARL must still keep simplified contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation proportionate to the transactions, including app-store commission arrangements, intercompany service agreements, and IP licensing contracts

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the economic substance doctrine through the general anti-abuse clause (abus de droit) in §6 AO, the SOPARFI circular LIR 14/4, the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, and the OECD 'substance over form' standards; a fitness-app SARL must demonstrate, where treaty or EU tax advantages are claimed, a local decision-making seat, actual employees and payroll, an operational fixed establishment, real administrative management in Luxembourg, and economic ownership of the developed software; the mere centralisation of sales revenue through a Luxembourg mailbox without local development, management or risk absorption enables recharacterisation of the Luxembourg entity as a conduit, denial of tax residence benefits, and reallocation of the entire commercial profit to the foreign beneficial owner's jurisdiction

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of one to five years under §§396-397 AO; automatic addition of the evaded tax and interest at 0.8% per month; extended 10-year assessment period for tax fraud

Director personal liability: The managing director or any signatory of the false return is jointly and severally liable for the evaded taxes, penalties and interest; criminal conviction may trigger a travel ban, asset freeze, publication of the sanction, and disqualification from managing any Luxembourg commercial company
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment by Luxembourg tax administration, recharacterisation of the transactions under the arm's length principle, additional CIT assessed at 24.94%, late-payment interest, and an increase of up to 10% on the tax deficit; DAC6 disclosure violations carry separate fines of EUR 5,000 to EUR 50,000

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements without economic justification incur civil liability for restitution of the corporate loss; in bankruptcy, the 'action en comblement du passif' allows the liquidator to recover the unreported commercial value from the directors' personal assets; aggravating circumstances lead to criminal sanctions for fraudulent impoverishment of the company
Shell Company Absence of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the claimed Luxembourg residence for tax treaty purposes, reallocation of all business profits to the beneficial owner's state, loss of the participation exemption and IP-box regime, additional tax assessments, penalties, and potential application of the EU anti-tax-avoidance directives; the Luxembourg tax administration may impose fines under the general tax law for failure to keep local books and records

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly maintaining the shell structure are personally liable for the additional corporate taxes and penalties; a travel ban and seizure of personal assets can be ordered during the tax fraud investigation; European investment and pension fund victims of the conduit structure may bring civil claims against the nominee directors and managers
Unlawful Dividend Distribution and Shareholder Loan Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the purported interest or royalty payments as dividends subject to 15% WHT, or outright denial of the deduction; penalty interest of 0.8% per month; company law invalidation of the unlawful distribution; criminal fines for fraudulent distribution under the Law of 10 August 1915

Director personal liability: Under Article 710-18 of the Luxembourg Companies Law, directors must restore to the company any sums improperly distributed; in insolvency, creditors may initiate an 'action en comblement du passif' against the managers; fraudulent distribution in the face of insolvency is punishable by imprisonment up to five years and personal asset seizure
AML/UBO Register and Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 5,000 per violation and criminal fines of up to EUR 1,250,000 for legal persons in aggravated cases; imprisonment of up to six months for individuals who fail to disclose UBO information after formal notice

Director personal liability: The appointed manager or the statutory officer responsible for the UBO filing is personally prosecuted; where the hidden beneficial owner is using the SARL for money laundering of app-generated revenue, directors without an adequate AML governance framework face confiscation orders and civil damages for third-party losses
Abusive Use of Treaty and EU Directives (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Application of the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule and the EU anti-abuse jurisprudence (Danish Cases C-116/16 and C-117/16); denial of the WHT exemption and imposition of 15% WHT on the full dividend flow; additional tax interest, fines and publicity of the abusive arrangement; DAC6 mandatory exchange of information on the scheme with penalties for non-disclosure

Director personal liability: The directors and the Luxembourg tax representative who signed the treaty relief forms may be jointly liable for the unpaid WHT if they knew or should have known of the artificiality; professional intermediaries (banks, auditors, tax advisers) are also subject to fines up to EUR 250,000 and potential imprisonment for complicity in tax evasion

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box or hybrid pass-through election exists; a SARL is a statutorily opaque corporate taxpayer. Applicable elections: VAT grouping (assujetti unique) for related Luxembourg persons; fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for 99%-owned qualifying subsidiaries; participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains; and advance tax ruling applications (art. 29a LIR) to the ACD for cross-border structuring certainty.

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU where the supplier has transferred the power of disposal to the customer and proves the goods' EU exit within the statutory time limit (art. 51 VAT Law); 0% also applies to identified offshore supplies of drone assembly, repair, calibration, and technical services under art. 58 VAT Law where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside the EU.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full de jure and de facto convertibility. As a euro-area Member State, Luxembourg applies the EU Treaty framework of free movement of capital and payments under Article 63 TFEU, and there are no exchange-control authorisations, licensing requirements or surrender obligations for current-account or capital-account flows. Profit remittance is unrestricted at FX level, but a distribution requires ordinary shareholders' approval, availability of distributable reserves verified against Luxembourg statutory accounts, and compliance with withholding taxes: the domestic rate on dividends is 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and the domestic participation-exemption conditions (qualifying holding of at least 10% held for at least 12 months, effective-substance and anti-abuse tests under article 166 LIR), and further reduced under applicable double tax treaties. The entity must also satisfy BCL BOP statistical reporting, CSSF AML/KYC documentation, and Luxembourg FDI screening under Regulation (EU) 2023/1734 for non-EU direct investors where applicable. No central-bank clearance for incoming or outgoing payments is required in normal euro payments.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, and no timing deadline for repatriation of export receivables (statutory conversion ratio is 0% and the retention period is unrestricted). Export receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, CNH, EUR or other foreign currency in a segregated corporate FX account. The only compliance overlays are: (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for cross-border flows and positions above the BCL survey materiality thresholds under BPM6 methodology; (ii) CSSF AML/CFT obligations to maintain underlying trade documents, invoices, airway bills and shipping evidence supporting each FX conversion or international settlement; and (iii) Luxembourg VAT rules for intra-EU and export supplies of drones, including periodic recapitulative statements.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential supervision of Luxembourg credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL is responsible for monetary policy operations, TARGET2 participation, payment-system oversight, foreign-exchange statistics and balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting, while the CSSF applies EU banking, AML/CFT and markets rules to all licensed banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Shareholders wire the subscribed SARL share capital (minimum EUR 12,000; at least 25% of each cash share paid up at constitution; in-kind contributions fully transferred) into a blocked attestation account in the name of the société en formation at BCEE, BGL BNP Paribas or BIL. The bank runs sanctions, UBO and source-of-funds KYC, then issues an attestation de blocage, which the notary files with the RCS on incorporation. After registration the blocked funds are released to the operating account. For inbound shareholder loans and group cash, use SWIFT MT103/TARGET2 in EUR or the relevant nostro chain for USD/CNH, quoting ISO 20022 purpose code CAPG for capital injection or INTC for intra-company treasury; the BCL BOP declaration must classify equity and loan injections separately, and arm's-length interest documentation is required to avoid recharacterisation. All customer wires must quote the purpose, the underlying contract, and the beneficiary LEI to satisfy CSSF AML and BCL statistical rules. PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie, S.C.A. holds a CSSF e-money licence and is the natural primary PSP for the EU and cross-border FPV drone retail rails; Stripe Technology Europe DAC (Ireland) passports payment services into Luxembourg and settles via SEPA payouts to a tier-1 Luxembourg bank account. Medium for United Kingdom-based APMs and fintechs: post-Brexit EU passporting has ceased, so a UK EMI requires either a Luxembourg branch authorisation, an EU-licensed partner bank, or reliance on the FCA temporary-permissions regime for inbound cross-border services, which banks only partially accept. Low and generally Prohibited for non-EEA offshore digital EMIs: offering payment accounts, acquiring, or virtual-IBAN 'rent-a-rail' services to Luxembourg residents without CSSF or ECB authorisation is a criminal offence under the Luxembourg law of 10 August 2020 on payment services (transposing PSD2); commercial banks will refuse to credit an unwrapped offshore EMI float.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
24.94% (Luxembourg City combined rate for 2024: 17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge + 6.86% municipal business tax)

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
15% on distributions to corporate and individual shareholders; reduced to 0% for a qualifying parent company under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (SAARL acting as a direct parent, ≥10% shareholding and holding period of at least 12 months), and for Luxembourg resident parent companies meeting the 10%/12-month participation exemption; treaty-provisioned rates frequently 5% or 10% (e.g., US: 5% with ≥10% holding; UK: 0% with ≥10% holding).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 for intercompany service transactions; EUR 500,000 for inventory, goods, royalties and financial transactions; master file/CbCR obligatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000. The Luxembourg tax administration (AGD) may require lower thresholds upon request under Circular LIR 56/1-56/20.

      
Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to UAE/Caribbean jurisdictions; however, the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR), the EU ATAD, beneficial ownership tests, and the jurisprudence on 'artificial arrangements' impose an effective substance requirement. A SARL manufacturing and distributing FPV racing drones must demonstrate management presence in Luxembourg, physical premises (or leased facilities), personnel (engineering, production, sales staff) and actual risk control for supply-chain functions. Without such substance, the tax administration may deny treaty benefits, participation exemption, parent-subsidiary WHT exemption and may recharacterize the structure as a shell company.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Fraud
        
Statutory sanction: Fractional reassessment preserving a 10-year statute of limitations; tax increase of 50% of the evaded tax; late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; criminal fine of EUR 25,000; imprisonment of up to 5 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, managers and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes if the evasion was committed with their knowledge or negligence. Civil recovery may attach to personal assets; criminal proceedings may trigger travel bans, provisional detention and, on conviction, a prison sentence.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 6 LIR (abuse of law), the tax authorities may ignore the corporate veil for tax purposes, deny treaty relief, reject the participation exemption, reclassify the income as derived by the ultimate shareholder, and re-open the assessment period up to 10 years. Additional penalties equal to 100% of the tax avoided may be imposed.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are personally liable for the amounts of tax, interest, and penalties not collected from the corporate vehicle. A travel ban and freezing of personal assets may be imposed during investigation; wrongful trading constitutes a criminal offense.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misstatement
        
Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to the Luxembourg taxable income; secondary adjustment (recharacterization of hidden distribution); late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; administrative penalty of up to 20% of the resulting under-assessment; in severe cases, a penalty equal to the excess income-shifting amount plus interest. The unlawful deduction is disallowed.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or approve non-arm's-length transfer prices are jointly liable for the additional tax and interest. If the misstatement involves false evidence or tax fraud, criminal liability of the director may lead to imprisonment and personal seizure of assets.

      

      
      

        AML/CFT and Corporate Registry Breach
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg Law of 13 January 2019 (AML register), administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 or 1% of annual turnover; separate criminal fine up to EUR 1 million for serious breaches. The director may be banned from serving as manager or director for up to 10 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for AML failures if they acted with intent or gross negligence; personal asset freezing, travel bans, and civil restitution to investors or creditors (including tax creditors) are applicable.

      

      
      

        Late or Non-filing of Corporation Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: Automatic assessment based on the AGD's estimation; late-filing surcharge of 10% of the assessed tax (capped at EUR 12,500); late-payment interest at 0.6% per month; additional fine of EUR 50 per day of continued non-compliance, not exceeding EUR 5,000; cancellation of the tax clearance certificate necessary for public markets and borrowing.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who remain non-compliant can be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, subject to a 5-year ban on managing any Luxembourg entity. Persistent non-filing may escalate to criminal prosecution for fraud.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) operating the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 5, rue Plaetis, L-2338 Luxembourg; electronic filing via www.lbr.lu and e-justice portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016 modernizing company law; SARL regime at Articles 710-1 to 710-26; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS); Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif) containing the articles of association (Statuts), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital (Article 710-1, 1915 Law as amended). Capital must be fully subscribed; each cash share must be paid to at least 25% of nominal value at subscription (EUR 3,000 minimum), while in-kind shares must be fully paid at subscription; the paid-up amount is blocked in a Luxembourg bank account (compte bloqué) or with the Caisse de Consignation until RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) preliminary inspection and clearance for warehouse/office premises, including extinguishers, emergency egress, and signage; structural load verification of any cement silo or elevated storage by a certified engineering bureau; occupational safety: Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) workplace clearance with exposure controls for cement dust (respiratory protection, ventilation), plus accident-prevention attestation; building occupancy clearance issued by the communal authority under the amended Law of 10 June 1999 and municipal building regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital (Article 710-1, 1915 Law as amended). Capital must be fully subscribed; each cash share must be paid to at least 25% of nominal value at subscription (EUR 3,000 minimum), while in-kind shares must be fully paid at subscription; the paid-up amount is blocked in a Luxembourg bank account (compte bloqué) or with the Caisse de Consignation until RCS registration.

If the company operates a warehouse, silo, or transshipment point, it must obtain the general business establishment permit (autorisation d'établissement) under the Law of 2 September 2011 and, where applicable, the municipal permit for a classified establishment (autorisation d'exploitation d'un établissement classé) under the amended Law of 10 June 1999.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — the Luxembourg Registration, Estates and VAT Administration — is the competent authority for VAT registration, audit and refund matters. Direct corporate income tax is administered separately by the Administration des contributions directes (ACD); customs export formalities are administered by the Administration des douanes et accises (ADA). Official e-government portals: myguichet.lu, guichet.lu, pfi.public.lu (interactive tax platform), lbr.lu (Luxembourg Business Registers).

E-government filing portal

STEP 1 — For the resident LU SARL, execute the notarial deed of incorporation and register the company with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), obtaining the alphanumeric RCS corporate number (format B#######), which serves as the corporate identifier for direct-tax purposes at the ACD. STEP 2 — Submit the electronic VAT registration application to the AEDT through a myguichet.lu business account prior to commencing taxable supplies, attaching the articles of incorporation, RCS excerpt, manager identification and proof of effective management in Luxembourg; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by 8 digits. STEP 3 — Apply to the ADA for an EORI number to lodge customs export declarations. STEP 4 — Register for employer obligations with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) only if payroll is maintained in Luxembourg. STEP 5 — For foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment, the same AEDT route applies without RCS registration; non-EU suppliers must appoint an approved Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative before the first taxable supply. All registrations must be completed before the first taxable event.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box or elective pass-through system. The SARL is by law an opaque resident corporation subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax. Available mechanisms: (i) group tax consolidation (fiscal unity) election under Article 164bis LIR for 95%-owned resident companies, with a 5-year irrevocable commitment; (ii) the participation exemption regime (Sections 115(5) and 166(6) LIR) for qualifying dividends and capital gains, subject to holding-period and substance conditions; (iii) the intellectual property income election under Article 50bis LIR granting an 80% income exemption for qualifying IP; (iv) hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD I/II are mandatory and not elective. The wholesale trading SARL cannot elect transparent treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 — first-euro registration for an EU-established taxable person. Luxembourg law imposes no tax-free turnover threshold on a resident SARL engaged in wholesale supplies; VAT registration and charging obligations commence from the first taxable transaction. The domestic small-enterprise franchise under Article 57 LTVL is reserved exclusively to resident micro-traders, is subject to strict turnover ceilings and cannot be applied by a bulk export wholesaler. Non-established foreign suppliers must likewise register from the first euro unless the reverse-charge, distance-sale or warehousing carve-outs apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bulk supplies of Portland cement clinker qualify for the export zero-rating of Article 54 LTVL (implementing Article 146(1)(a) VAT Directive) where the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union to a third-country destination by the supplier, the non-EU purchaser or a third party on their behalf, and leave the EU within the statutory 90-day dispatch window from the date of supply. The SARL bears the burden of proof and must hold an accepted customs export declaration together with complete transport and banking evidence before the zero rate may be applied. The exemption extends to exports leaving via another EU port of exit (e.g., Rotterdam, Antwerp or Dunkirk) provided the Luxembourg seller is named in the export documentation and the welfare transport to the non-EU destination is continuous and substantiated.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EXS) lodged in the EU Customs Decisions System and cleared by the customs office of exit
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (shipped-on-board) evidencing carriage of the clinker cargo to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing collection of the export consideration in alignment with the invoice value
  • Commercial invoice with full seller/buyer identification, Incoterms (FOB/CIF/FCA basis), bulk quantity and the export zero-rating reference
  • Packing list and weight certificate / draft survey report for the vessel-loaded clinker cargo
  • Transport contract, charter party or booking confirmation together with vessel call certificate at the EU port of exit
  • Freight and cargo insurance certificates where CIF or CIP terms of sale have been agreed

No real-time fiscal clearance regime, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no state-operated e-invoicing API and no digital fiscal stamping ('cachet fiscal') currently applies to domestic or cross-border B2B trade in Luxembourg. Structured e-invoicing is mandatory only for B2G transactions, where invoices must comply with EN 16931/Peppol BIS when supplied to Luxembourg public entities. Export invoices for bulk clinker may be issued in paper or electronic form, provided that authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are preserved, and the invoice must show the statutory export zero-rating reference. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing will apply in Luxembourg only after transposition, with no obligation before the 2030s.

The AEDT subjects export zero-rating claims to three-way document matching among the customs exit record, the ocean Bill of Lading and the banking receipt. Where the export movement, the consignee identity or the inward foreign-exchange repatriation cannot be substantiated, the AEDT retroactively recharacterises the supply as taxable, assesses Luxembourg VAT at 17%, denies or claws back input VAT on the goods and freight, and adds statutory late-payment interest. Administrative penalties are graduated from 10% up to 100% of the evaded tax, and intent-based concealment doubles the maximum. Deliberate submission of false export declarations, forged bills of lading or fabricated remittance certificates exposes the SARL and its managers to criminal tax and customs fraud prosecution — fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment — and extends the reassessment window from the ordinary five years to ten years. AEO status moderates inspection frequency but does not attenuate clawback liability.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully convertible on both current and capital account. No Luxembourg registration, licensing, or approval is required for cross-border capital movement or profit remittance. Dividends and retained profits can be converted into any market currency and remitted, subject only to Luxembourg withholding taxes and the standard Anti-Money-Laundering/CRS information obligations of the paying institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, compulsory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export earnings. A Luxembourg wholesale trader may lawfully hold exported proceeds in foreign-currency accounts with an authorised Luxembourg/European Economic Area bank, fund onward working-capital disbursements, or hedge; there is no deadline or percentage requirement. Only standard transaction reporting for AML/CTF and CSSF statistical purposes is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating inside the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank and the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance set monetary and external-payments policy. Luxembourg does not have a separate exchange-control authority; capital movement rules derive from EU Treaty Articles 63 to 65.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate. An EEA-authorised EMI/Payment Institution exercising a Luxembourg passport can open payment accounts and execute SEPA/SWIFT transfers, and may provide a useful high-velocity disbursement overlay for freight payments and minor operational expenses. It is not suitable for large equity injections, trade-finance instruments, or documentary letters of credit. Non-EEA offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to a Luxembourg client without a Luxembourg/EEA branch or agent under PSD2 and the Luxembourg AML/CTF law; their practical viability is Low unless licensed in Luxembourg and subject to CSSF prudential supervision.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital/equity funding must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer where the remitter is in SEPA) to the SARL's Luxembourg bank account in the corporate legal name. Payment reference must state 'Shareholder equity contribution / capital increase' and, where required by the receiving bank, include the Luxembourg balance-of-payments category for direct-investment equity. The bank posts the credit after standard KYC/CDD and sanctions verification; back-up file includes board resolution, notarised capital increase deed, subscribed shares, and proof of source of funds. There is no central-bank filing or approval for convertible capital, but anti-money-laundering declarations apply above statutory thresholds.Use SWIFT MT103 for open-account telegraphic transfers to suppliers and freight providers; include full beneficiary name/IBAN/BIC, invoice/contract number, and Incoterms. For large vessel/portland clinker deliveries, use an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit opened under UCP600; the issuing bank sends an MT700, confirming bank negotiates the draft against shipping warranty/documents, and payment is executed by MT202 cover/MT103 as usual. All disbursements should pass only through cleared CRS/CSSF-regulated banking counterparties; crypto assets or unlicensed payment aggregators are excluded for treasury liquidity.Outright deliverable FX forwards for EUR/USD, EUR/TRY and EUR/CNH, with ISDA 1998 FX Definitions and bilateral CSA/credit support.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted/inaccessible currencies where physical settlement is impractical; lawful as OTC derivatives subject to EMIR trade reporting and margin rules.FX swaps for near-term liquidity management.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) aligning USD trade receivables/payables with EUR funding; permitted under MiFID II if the SARL obtains professional-investor treatment or receives investment advice from the clearing bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% in Luxembourg City (17.00% corporate income tax + 7.00% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax + 6.75% municipal business tax; municipal business tax rate varies by commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends distributed by the SARL to non-resident shareholders. EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive provides a 0% source rate when a qualifying EU parent holds at least 10% for at least 12 uninterrupted months; many comprehensive double tax treaties reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, 10%, or 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the return is due by 31 May of the following calendar year. For non-calendar fiscal years, the return is due by the last day of the fifth month following the fiscal year-end. The ACD routinely extends the deadline to 31 December for taxpayers filing electronically and providing the required cooperation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: annual related-party transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 for any category of goods, services, royalties, financing, or freight-related intercompany charges. Master File/CbCR threshold: worldwide consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more. The ACD may request transfer pricing documentation even below these thresholds when the intra-group price or margin appears abnormal.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations in the BVI/Cayman sense, but domestic jurisprudence, Art. 56 LIR, and ACD Circular 56/1 require functional, asset, and risk substance aligned with reported intercompany compensation. For an ocean-vessel freight and wholesale cement clinker SARL, the company must demonstrate local commercial negotiation, physical movement monitoring, invoice management, and business risk assumption through employees, offices, board minutes, and payment records. Letterbox companies that merely pass invoices and arrange LC/TT funds without active decision-making are vulnerable to tax recharacterization, treaty abuse denial, and criminal audit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud penalties: imprisonment up to five years and fine up to EUR 250,000; civil reassessment of full tax, 10-100% administrative penalties, monthly late-payment interest, and disclosure of the debt in public registers.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize false returns are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. Luxembourg courts may impose travel bans, freezing orders, disqualification from directorship, and personal bankruptcy where assets were moved abroad.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ACD recharacterization of income under Art. 56 LIR, upward adjustment of SARL taxable income, denial of expenses, imposition of a 10% filing penalty and up to 100% understatement penalty, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to ACD recovery actions when the transfer pricing file is absent or fraudulent; repeated violations trigger director disqualification and criminal liability for aiding tax fraud.
Shell Company / Pure Letterbox Activity

Statutory sanction: Refusal of tax residence and treaty benefits under Circular 56/1; reallocation of profits to the foreign parent; administrative fines for false declarations; potential judicial liquidation and strike-off from the Luxembourg trade register.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be liable for unpaid taxes, prohibited from managing Luxembourg entities, and subject to extradition assistance in EU cross-border tax investigations.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Accessory money laundering penalties under the Luxembourg AML regime: fines up to EUR 1.25-5 million, asset freezing, confiscation of cargo proceeds, professional bans, and criminal prosecution of the Luxembourg responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal conviction, imprisonment, personal asset seizure, and travel restrictions when they deliberately process fraudulent trade documents through the Luxembourg entity.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Repatriation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as constructive dividend, recapture of 15% withholding tax, late payment interest, up to 10% late-filing penalty, denial of expense deduction, and liability for solidarity surcharge and municipal tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: The SARL is the withholding agent; directors are jointly liable for the uncollected withholding tax and may be personally pursued where the entity is insolvent. Serious concealment can lead to criminal tax fraud charges.

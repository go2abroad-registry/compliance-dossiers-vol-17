Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. The full capital must be subscribed and paid up in cash before notarization. The cash contribution is blocked in an escrow account held by a Luxembourg credit institution or notary, released upon execution of the notarial deed; the notary certifies receipt and the RCS verifies the bank certificate.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety clearance is mandatory for a virtual publishing operation. If a dedicated office is leased, the building owner must hold a valid fire-safety conformity certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie). Employer obligations are limited to affiliation with an approved occupational health service and completion of a workplace risk assessment in accordance with the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 28 December 2016.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000. The full capital must be subscribed and paid up in cash before notarization. The cash contribution is blocked in an escrow account held by a Luxembourg credit institution or notary, released upon execution of the notarial deed; the notary certifies receipt and the RCS verifies the bank certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax), acting through the official portals impotsdirects.public.lu and myguichet.lu; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT (TVA) registration and indirect tax compliance, via aed.public.lu and myguichet.lu; Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS / Luxembourg Business Registers, lbr.lu) for company incorporation and business registration; Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency for export-formalities and PLDA electronic customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL is incorporated before a notary, registered with the RCS and the Registre National des Sociétés et Associations (RNSA) to obtain the national business registration number (matricule, i.e., the RCS number and fiscal identification number), and is simultaneously registered with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax. VAT registration is filed separately with the AED on the standard registration form, which leads to issuance of the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LV format). Non-resident entities establishing a Luxembourg branch must appoint a local authorized representative, register the branch with the RCS, and file the corresponding VAT and direct-tax registrations with the AED and the ACD; taxpayer identification is evidenced by the RCS matricule. EU-resident suppliers of cross-border B2C digital services may alternatively discharge Luxembourg VAT obligations through the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) without a full local VAT registration, while non-EU suppliers must either register directly in Luxembourg or identify themselves under the OSS regime through a selected EU member state and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identifier where supplies are made.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is an autonomous corporate tax resident (société de capitaux) subject to corporate income tax (headline rate 17% plus a 7% solidarity surcharge), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, aggregate standard combined rate 24.94% in Luxembourg City), and annual net wealth tax (0.5% on taxable net wealth exceeding EUR 500,000). Taxable scope is unlimited (worldwide income) for Luxembourg-resident companies, mitigated through double-tax treaties, the participation-exemption regime, and foreign-tax-credit relief; specific Territorial_Scope features apply to qualifying intra-group, outbound-dividend, capital-gains, and foreign-branch situations. — A Luxembourg SARL is incorporated before a notary, registered with the RCS and the Registre National des Sociétés et Associations (RNSA) to obtain the national business registration number (matricule, i.e., the RCS number and fiscal identification number), and is simultaneously registered with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is an autonomous corporate tax resident (société de capitaux) subject to corporate income tax (headline rate 17% plus a 7% solidarity surcharge), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, aggregate standard combined rate 24.94% in Luxembourg City), and annual net wealth tax (0.5% on taxable net wealth exceeding EUR 500,000). Taxable scope is unlimited (worldwide income) for Luxembourg-resident companies, mitigated through double-tax treaties, the participation-exemption regime, and foreign-tax-credit relief; specific Territorial_Scope features apply to qualifying intra-group, outbound-dividend, capital-gains, and foreign-branch situations.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; entity classification is fixed by corporate and tax statutes. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (i) fiscal unity election (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for Luxembourg-resident group companies held at least 95% by the elected parent; (ii) the opt-in participation-exemption regime under Article 166 LIR for qualifying shareholdings (≥10% or ≥EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost, held for an unbroken 12-month period and satisfying substance/subject-to-tax conditions); (iii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT law; (iv) advance tax rulings (confirmatory rulings) from the ACD for financing arrangements, transfer pricing and hybrid-issue clearance; (v) the status of société de participations financières (SOPARFI) as a factual holding-company classification. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory and do not provide elective carve-outs; hybrid arbitrage structures are statutorily disallowed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 — Luxembourg imposes no general de minimis turnover threshold for VAT registration; any person carrying out taxable economic activities must register for VAT in Luxembourg. A small-business exemption applies under Article 57 of the VAT law only to resident taxable persons with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, and entails forfeiture of input-VAT recovery. Non-resident digital suppliers of B2C electronic services to Luxembourg consumers must register in Luxembourg unless they use the EU OSS regime; the EUR 10,000 annual Union-wide distance-sales and cross-border B2C digital-services threshold governs application of the supplier's home-state rules, with automatic registration in Luxembourg once exceeded. Intra-Community acquisition thresholds do not apply to a fully taxable Luxembourg SARL.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including printed magazines, books, and physical publications) to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 43 of the VAT law, provided the goods physically leave the EU within the statutory/administrative timeframe and the exit is proven by customs and transport documentation. Intra-EU supplies of goods to EU VAT-registered business customers are zero-rated where both parties hold valid VAT identification numbers, the customer's VAT number is confirmed in VIES, and EC Sales List / Intrastat reporting is rendered. Digital magazine subscriptions and electronically supplied advertising/media placements do not benefit from the classic customs export zero-rating: B2B e-services to non-EU customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rules (Articles 44 and 45 of the VAT law) if the customer's business location and non-taxable status are evidenced; B2C digital services to EU consumers are taxed in the destination member state and reported through the OSS at the applicable foreign VAT rate; B2C digital services to non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT subject to two non-contradictory pieces of evidence of customer location (e.g., IP address, bank BIC country code, fixed-line country code). Zero-rating of advertising-media export services additionally requires a written contract demonstrating the economic link between the Luxembourg supplier, the non-EU customer, and the actual service provision outside Luxembourg.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EU Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration filed in the Luxembourg PLDA customs clearance system)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR International Consignment Note / Air Waybill or equivalent proof of physical exit from the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent payment-credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the SARL's bank account)
  • Commercial invoice and executed subscription, advertising, or digital-content contract
  • VAT registration numbers of both parties and EC Sales List records for intra-EU supplies
  • Customer-location evidence for digital services (IP address, bank country code, fixed-line country code, or two non-contradictory business-evidence items)
  • Franchise/platform payout records from Stripe and AdSense-payout rails evidencing the gross consideration, platform fees, and settlement currency

No real-time fiscal clearance, software certification, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Luxembourg for standard B2B or B2C invoices; the AED does not operate a continuous transaction-control (CTC) clearance system. Invoices must satisfy Articles 34-36 of the VAT law (essential content, issuance, archiving for at least 6 years); e-invoicing is freely permitted, and the PEPPOL/EN 16931 format is mandatory for public-procurement (B2G) electronic invoicing. DAC7 digital-platform reporting applies to the operator of the digital magazine portal for reportable sellers. The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework is expected to introduce mandatory structured e-invoicing and transaction-based digital reporting during the 2028-2030 implementation horizon; until then, the EC Sales List, Intrastat declarations, and OSS quarterly returns remain the operative compliance instruments.

The AED may audit export zero-rating and OSS claims at any time during the standard five-year limitation period, requiring full documentary substantiation before the 0% rate is retained; where proof of physical export, customer location, or FX repatriation is deficient, the AED re-invoices the supply at the standard 17% VAT rate and levies late-payment interest at the statutory monthly accrual plus an administrative fine of up to EUR 250,000. Knowingly false zero-rating or fraudulent OSS declarations constitute VAT fraud, punishable by criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to five years, with joint and several liability of corporate directors and officers; the limitation period for reopening assessments is extended to ten years in cases of intentional evasion. Any input VAT provisionally refunded on the strength of an invalid export claim is clawed back with statutory interest from the date of refund. Although Luxembourg imposes no dedicated statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, persistent non-repatriation of export receivables beyond the 180-day safe-harbor convention is treated by the ACD as a transfer-pricing red flag requiring arm's-length interest accruals, submission of transactional documentation, and potential double-counting of income in the corporate tax base; late repatriation must be supported by the underlying receivable ledger and FX controls in the SARL's transfer-pricing file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a Eurozone member state, Luxembourg applies full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. The free movement of capital and payments is enshrined in Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted and not subject to prior administrative authorization. However, financial counterparties must conduct transaction-level sanctions screening and verify the legitimate source of funds to mitigate money-laundering and terrorist-financing risks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or forced conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Luxembourg. Corporate entities may maintain foreign-currency accounts (e.g., USD, GBP) without any obligation to surrender or convert export earnings into EUR. All FX operations are executed freely in the interbank market at market-determined rates, provided the underlying transaction is declared for statistical purposes to the BCL when required under the EU balance of payments reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting under the supervisory coordination of the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct and AML/CTF compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for integrating EU/EEA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Stripe, Adyen, or Luxembourg-licensed EMIs) as payment service providers for merchant collection and disbursement. Legal constraints: (i) any EMI must be licensed under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and supervised by a European Economic Area (EEA) national authority; (ii) the EMI must not be used as a substitute for a corporate bank account for statutory capital deposits or balance sheet treasury; (iii) contracts must specify data residency and consumer-protection compliance for digital magazine subscriptions.

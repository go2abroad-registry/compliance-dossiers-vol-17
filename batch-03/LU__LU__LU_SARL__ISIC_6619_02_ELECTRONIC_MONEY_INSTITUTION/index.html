Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) for customs and excise; electronic filing and registration portal: myguichet.lu with the eCDF dedicated VAT clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL through the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) (one-stop-shop notary/portal) and obtain the 17-digit national business number (matricule) and RCS identification. Step 2: The RCS matricule triggers automatic tax registration with the ACD and automatic issuance of the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) by the AEDT for resident entities. Step 3: For a non-resident EMI establishing a branch or engaging in taxable Luxembourg supplies, file VAT Form 163 with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative if the EMI has no EU seat or permanent establishment, and obtain an LU-prefixed EORI number through the ADA where any goods flows exist. Step 4: Activate the eCDF/MyGuichet taxpayer account for electronic submission of VAT returns (LTV 500/501), the EC Sales List (Form 600), annual tax declarations for CIT (form 400), and any VAT refund application under the 13th Directive (2003/11/EC) or Directive 2008/9/EC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections. Available statutory elections and regimes: (1) fiscal unity ('intégration fiscale') under Article 164bis LIR for resident Luxembourg subsidiaries held at least 95%, irrevocable for 5 years; (2) VAT grouping allowing a single taxable person ('assujetti unique') under Articles 58-59 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, extending to cross-border permanent establishments; (3) territorial allocation option for Luxembourg permanent establishments of foreign head offices under Article 183bis LIR; (4) participation exemption regime (Articles 147-148 LIR) for dividend and capital gains relief, which is statutory and not elective; (5) advanced binding tax rulings pursuant to Article 29 LIR and circulars (including the Intra-Group Financing, IP, and VAT ruling guidelines) permitting embodiment of the EMI's payment-rail pricing and exempt financial margin treatment. Hybrid mismatch elections are an accepted offensive/mitigative mechanism for Luxembourg hybrid entities only to the extent permitted by the Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (EU) 2016/1164 and the Luxembourg Law of 21 December 2018, primarily through court-tracked misinterpretation avoidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident small-business exemption threshold: EUR 35,000 taxable turnover per calendar year (Article 57(2) of the Luxembourg VAT Law). Non-EU-established taxable persons benefit from zero de minimis and must telematically register unless their supplies are subject to reverse charge; intra-EU distance sales and TBE-supplied digital services are assessed against the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold under the On-Shore/OSS scheme. Financial and payment services (virtual IBAN, wallet custody, SEPA clearing) are VAT-exempt and, when exclusively performed, do not count toward the registration/taxable turnover threshold; however, any taxable agency, e-identification or consultancy revenue will aggregate for threshold purposes and topple the small-business profit remuneration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The EMI's principal services—virtual IBAN issuance, digital wallet reserve management, SEPA credit processing and payment transactions—are VAT-exempt without credit under Article 44 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (implementing Article 135(1)(d) VAT Directive); such exempt financial intermediation is not zero-rated. Zero-rating (0%) is available for: (1) exports of tangible goods from Luxembourg to third-country destinations under Article 56 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, subject to proof of physical departure within 90 days; (2) intra-Community supplies of goods under Article 51 of the Luxembourg VAT Law where the acquirer holds a valid EU VAT number (validated via VIES); and (3) business-to-business services whose place of supply is fixed in a non-EU jurisdiction pursuant to Articles 17 and 20 of the Luxembourg VAT Law—for those offshore services the supply falls outside Luxembourg VAT territorial scope and is effectively zero-rated; the customer's status as a taxable person and its non-EU establishment must be documented contractually and evidenced by its foreign TIN/registration, served through reverse-charge or no-VAT invoicing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) lodged via Luxembourg LU-Export / AES with the ADA for any physical goods movement
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or CMR for road transport) evidencing physical departure from Luxembourg to a third country
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the EMI's LU VAT number, the customer's/originator's VAT/TIN, and the exact export reason
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/SEPA payment voucher showing forex repatriation and bank settlement traceability
  • For offshore services: executed service agreement, IT-systems documentation, customer KYC/AML file, proof of non-EU business establishment (residency certificate or foreign VAT/TIN), and the VIES evidence of the EU customer's VAT status where applicable

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance mechanism, digital stamping regime, or mandatory SAF-T for VAT. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies under the Law of 27 June 2018 as implemented through PEPPOL BIS 3.0 (central public procurement portal) effective 1 March 2021. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary on PEPPOL/EN 16931 standards, with mandatory B2B e-invoicing and structured e-reporting expected to be phased in from 2026 under draft Bill 7947. VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically via eCDF/MyGuichet; for the licensed EMI, VAT filing is generally monthly (standard regime) because its output is dominated by exempt financial services and input VAT deduction is unavailable except for the segregated taxable sleeve, mandating careful proportional deduction and affiliate services invoicing.

The AEDT and ADA perform automated Big Data cross-checks between SAD export declarations, VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and bank payment flows; a false export zero-rating claim or service-supply mischaracterisation triggers full retrospective reversal of the VAT exemption plus the underlying input VAT clawback, monthly statutory late interest, and administrative fines of up to three times the evaded VAT. Where abuse of law (abus de droit) or fraudulent intent is shown under Articles 66 et seq. of the Luxembourg VAT Law and the General Anti-Abuse Rule operating through Article 6 of the Tax Adaptation Law (Steueranpassungsgesetz), the limitation period extends to 10 years, criminal fines of up to EUR 20,000 may be imposed on responsible management, and the directors become joint and severally liable for the reassessed VAT; foreign-exchange manipulation or circumvention of the 180-day repatriation benchmark further exposes the EMI to CSSF supervisory sanctions and revocation of its electronic-money licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates inside the Economic and Monetary Union with the euro as legal tender; full convertibility applies to both current account and capital account transactions under Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no exchange-control authorizations, surrender requirements, or conversion quotas for cross-border capital movements, profit distributions, dividend remittances, or loan repayments. Any restriction would only arise from EU restrictive measures, UN/EU sanctions, or AML/KYC-related payment blocking, not from foreign exchange regulations. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate law solvency tests, Luxembourg GAAP/IFRS distributable reserves, and withholding tax compliance for outbound dividends, none of which constitute capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. An entity may hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts with Luxembourg credit institutions or convert them freely into euro through the interbank market. The only obligations are business-accounting congruence (the export receipt must correspond to a declared commercial invoice), transfer pricing compliance where related parties are involved, and CSSF/BCL AML transaction-reporting duties for cash or high-risk cross-border flows. For an Electronic Money Institution, the safeguarding obligation under the E-Money Directive and Luxembourg law must be maintained in low-risk liquid assets, which in practice requires euro or major foreign currency deposits with authorized credit institutions rather than uncontrolled export receivables.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is High for a Luxembourg-domiciled SARL holding a CSSF-authorized EMI license, because such an entity may join SEPA, hold settlement accounts with the BCL, and access TARGET2 via a sponsoring credit institution. Feasibility is Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs (for example, UK, Lithuania, or Netherlands license holders) seeking direct Luxembourg clearing access: they may open virtual IBANs and nostro accounts with Luxembourg partner banks, but they cannot obtain BCL settlement membership without a Luxembourg establishment, a passport under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and full compliance with CSSF outsourcing, safeguarding, and fit-and-proper requirements. Most domestic tier-1 banks limit onboarding of offshore EMIs to 'partner-acceptance' or 'bank-as-a-service' arrangements with restricted transaction velocity and mandatory daily reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR or third-country injections and SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR transfers originating within the EEA. For equity capital, use economic-purpose code such as 'Equity injection / subscription of shares' with the creditor UMR (Unique Mandate Reference) and a structured creditor reference; for loans from shareholders, use a shareholder loan agreement with transfer pricing documentation and a clear repayment schedule to avoid reclassification as equity. The receiving Luxembourg bank will require the underlying corporate resolution, board authorization, and AML source-of-funds declaration before crediting the escrow account; funds must match the constitutional minimum capital (at least EUR 12,000 for an SARL, and EUR 350,000 for an EMI) and must be disclosed to the CSSF under the e-money authorization file.For EUR-denominated trade settlements within the EEA, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) through the TARGET2 settlement system via the Luxembourg clearing infrastructure; for non-EEA or non-EUR payments, use SWIFT MT103 with full remittance information and correct ISO 20022 purpose codes. For documentary trade settlements, use Commercial Letters of Credit opened through Luxembourg credit institutions with SWIFT MT700/MT710 issuance and UCP 600 terms. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is High for a Luxembourg-domiciled SARL holding a CSSF-authorized EMI license, because such an entity may join SEPA, hold settlement accounts with the BCL, and access TARGET2 via a sponsoring credit institution. Feasibility is Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs (for example, UK, Lithuania, or Netherlands license holders) seeking direct Luxembourg clearing access: they may open virtual IBANs and nostro accounts with Luxembourg partner banks, but they cannot obtain BCL settlement membership without a Luxembourg establishment, a passport under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and full compliance with CSSF outsourcing, safeguarding, and fit-and-proper requirements. Most domestic tier-1 banks limit onboarding of offshore EMIs to 'partner-acceptance' or 'bank-as-a-service' arrangements with restricted transaction velocity and mandatory daily reconciliation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% Luxembourg corporate income tax (impot sur le revenu des collectivites) plus 7% solidarity surcharge plus municipal business tax (impot commercial communal); aggregate effective statutory burden in Luxembourg City approximately 24.94%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Country-by-Country Report: MNE groups with consolidated revenue at least EUR 750,000,000. No fixed annual Master File/Local File threshold is yet codified in Luxembourg statute for FY 2024/2025; the ACD may compel OECD-aligned documentation under LIR Art. 56 and Circular LIR 56/1 (2016) regardless of size. From FY 2026 (EU Directive 2024/2810 transposition): Local File required where taxpayer net turnover is at least EUR 50,000,000 and intra-group transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File required for groups with revenue at least EUR 750,000,000; CbCR notification threshold EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: No separate UAE-style ESR statute exists, but economic substance is imposed via LIR Art. 6/56 (abuse of law), LIR Art. 56bis (GAAR), the principal purpose test under DTAs/MLI and CSSF licensing requirements. An EMI must keep its central administration, board meetings, senior employees, AML/compliance function, e-money float treasury management, IT access and service decision-making in Luxembourg; minimal substance triggers reintegration of profits and loss of treaty protection

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent misdeclaration on Form 100

Statutory sanction: Default assessment and 10% surcharge; 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; administrative fiscal fines under AO Arts. 396-401; on criminal conviction, fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax fraud penalties and damages; courts may impose travel bans, freezing orders and up to 6 months imprisonment on the responsible manager
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and disregard of artificial transactions under LIR Art. 56/56bis; upward adjustment of taxable base plus 10% surcharge; denial of dividend WHT relief and treaty benefits under the PPT; EU anti-shell presumption leads to reallocation of income to the ultimate parent state

Director personal liability: The entity's managers/beneficial owners may be assessed for restitution equal to the tax shortfall, prohibited from managing Luxembourg companies, and subjected to exit travel bans during collection proceedings
AML/CFT breach by licence holder (High-Risk EMI)

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines of up to EUR 2,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover per breach; licence suspension or withdrawal; criminal money-laundering sanctions under Luxembourg Criminal Code Articles 506-1 et seq. (imprisonment 3 months to 5 years, fine EUR 12,500 to EUR 250,000) in addition to confiscation of criminal proceeds

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, executive directors and de facto shadow managers face personal criminal conviction, disqualification from banking/fintech management, pecuniary sanctions up to EUR 250,000, travel bans and asset seizure for serious AML failures
Unlicensed e-money payment activity (running without CSSF licence)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 (payment services regime); CSSF injunctions to cease activity; restitution of all reserve balances; contracts concluded in breach are voidable

Director personal liability: All directors who directed or tolerated the unlicensed activity are personally liable for losses of customers and creditors, subject to imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, freezing of personal assets and exclusion from acting as company officers in Luxembourg

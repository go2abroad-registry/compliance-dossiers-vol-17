Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), 1216 Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), particularly Articles 109-125 regarding Sociétés à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the annual accounts of undertakings; Law of 2 September 2011 on the access to activities and market regulation and quality of services.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised Articles of Association and constitutive instrument (acte constitutif et statuts), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 63 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For a SARL, the minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,000. Each share must have a minimum nominal value of EUR 1. At incorporation, the entire subscribed capital must be subscribed and at least 25% of the nominal value of each cash-contributed share must be paid up. The paid-up amount must be deposited by the founders in a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the company at a Luxembourg credit institution. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report and full payment at incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Service d'Incendie et de Secours (SIS) following review of fire-resistance plans, emergency exits, extinguisher placement and evacuation measures; structural load inspection certificate issued by a licensed inspection bureau confirming floor capacity for heavy telemetry equipment and assembly benches; workplace clearance from the Administration de l'Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering electrical safety, ergonomic work-stations, lighting, ventilation, noise, first-aid facilities and dated occupational risk assessment.

Paid-in capital rule: For a SARL, the minimum subscribed share capital is EUR 12,000. Each share must have a minimum nominal value of EUR 1. At incorporation, the entire subscribed capital must be subscribed and at least 25% of the nominal value of each cash-contributed share must be paid up. The paid-up amount must be deposited by the founders in a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the company at a Luxembourg credit institution. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report and full payment at incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) - Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration; Registration Portal: MyGuichet.lu (official e-government portal); VAT registration is administered by Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must complete incorporation before a notary, register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés), obtain a national identification number (numéro d'identification national) assigned by the Luxembourg tax administration, and file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 1 - Déclaration d'inscription à la TVA) with the AEDT. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment but making taxable supplies may register directly via the AEDT or appoint a fiscal representative as required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must complete incorporation before a notary, register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) / RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés), obtain a national identification number (numéro d'identification national) assigned by the Luxembourg tax administration, and file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 1 - Déclaration d'inscription à la TVA) with the AEDT. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment but making taxable supplies may register directly via the AEDT or appoint a fiscal representative as required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide a check-the-box regime analogous to the U.S. SARL may opt for the status of a regulated 1929 holding company, a family wealth management company (SPF), or a SOPARFI subject to the parent-subsidiary directive; for VAT, an entity may join a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (Law of 21 December 2018) impose restrictions on hybrid instrument/entity outcomes for Luxembourg tax residents.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for Luxembourg-established entities: any person making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register from the first euro; distance sales of goods into Luxembourg trigger registration when annual turnover exceeds EUR 100,000 (domestic threshold), except where the Union OSS regime is applied and the EU-wide distance selling threshold of EUR 10,000 is exceeded for intra-EU distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by a Luxembourg VAT-registered supplier to a destination outside the EU qualify for zero-rating under Article 41(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the supplier retains evidence of physical dispatch/transport outside the EU within 90 days and holds the required export documentation; services linked to exported goods and certain cross-border services supplied to non-EU recipients may also qualify as zero-rated where the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU; supplies to other EU Member States are zero-rated as intra-Community supplies if the customer's VAT ID is valid and an EC Sales List is filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic declaration via Luxembourg Customs LU-Customs / EU AES)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/airway bill as transport evidence)
  • Packing List
  • Evidence of dispatch/transport out of the EU (including Cargo Movement Reference or equivalent)
  • Proof of Payment / Bank Transfer Evidence
  • Customer Purchase Order or Contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Proof as required by banking and FX rules

Luxembourg does not currently require mandatory continuous transaction controls or real-time clearance; e-invoicing is permitted subject to authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility. As of 2024-2025, B2B e-invoicing is not universally mandatory in Luxembourg, but the Luxembourg government has announced future alignment with EU ViDA proposals requiring e-invoicing for cross-border B2B transactions; digital fiscal clearance / real-time stamping (e.g., Italy's Sistema di Interscambio or France's Factur-X) is not yet imposed. For export transactions, customs electronic declarations (ECS) and the AES/Export Control System are mandatory; SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) may be required for VAT audits in electronic format.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may deny zero-rating and reassess VAT where export documentation is incomplete, untimely, or inconsistent; penalties range from VAT adjustments plus default interest (interest = 0.6% per month of delay) to fines up to EUR 250,000 for serious fraud; criminal fines and imprisonment can apply for deliberate falsification of export evidence; the AEDT conducts post-customs-release audits and may claw back zero-rated VAT within the general five-year assessment period, extended to ten years in cases of proven fraud or material concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Luxembourg is a euro-area member state and applies the free movement of capital guarantee under Article 63 TFEU. There is no exchange-control licence, no approval gate for profit repatriation, no dividend-investment obligation and no restriction on foreign-currency accounts held domestically or abroad. The EUR is the reporting functional currency of the SARL; invoices, receivables and payables may be denominated freely in any convertible currency. Dividend outflows to an EU/EEA corporate parent holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital for 12 months qualify for a 0% Luxembourg withholding rate under the transposed EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; non-qualifying shareholders face the standard 15% rate, reducible by applicable income tax treaties. The only procedural obligation is the BCL's external-statistics reporting of cross-border payments above de minimis thresholds, which is statistical in nature and never a convertibility or remittance obstacle. For an ISIC 3250 ECG telemetry assembly operation, no sector-specific FX permit is required to import electronic components or to export finished patient-monitoring devices.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no repatriation deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. The SARL may retain USD, CNY, JPY or other foreign-currency receivables in multi-currency accounts at its Luxembourg bank or at any EU CRD V-licensed institution. The bank merely reports aggregated cross-border flows to the BCL on a monthly basis per the BCL external-statistics instruction. As prudent treasury policy, the company should convert significant net export balances back into EUR within a 30-day rolling cycle and hedge anticipated residual exposure to avoid unmanaged balance-sheet volatility.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB). Banking licensing, prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement for credit institutions and electronic money institutions are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the BCL is responsible for monetary operations, payment-systems oversight, cash distribution and external statistics/balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for non-core payment flows; Low legality for trade finance products. Luxembourg-licensed EMIs and EU passporting electronic-money institutions under PSD2/EMD2 can provide SEPA IBANs, multi-currency wallets, FX conversion and card disbursement rails, but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, issue MT760 guarantees, grant credit or overdraft facilities, or serve as the reference settlement account for CSSF-monitored financial statements. Offshore digital EMIs may be used only as a marginal cash-collection periphery (e.g., small distributor receipts), with full downstream reconciliation into the primary bank account to avoid circular-flow alarms. The primary operating and trade-finance accounts must rest with a CRD V-licensed credit institution; any EMI used must hold a valid EEA licence and be registered on the Luxembourg AML/CFT register.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are fully liberalized and require no BCL or CSSF approval; only corporate formalities and statistical classification apply. Equity route: the shareholder wires EUR via SWIFT MT103 with field 70 reading 'Souscription de capital social' and the company name; the bank credits the labelled capital sub-account, verifies the amount against the EUR 12,500 statutory minimum and against the notarial deed of incorporation or the capital-increase deed; after the updated RCS filing is produced, the bank releases funds to the operational account. The inflow is classified for BCL purposes under 'direct investment - equity capital'. Shareholder loan route: an executed compte courant d'associé agreement with arm's-length interest must exist before transfer; the MT103 field 70 references 'Shareholder Advance - intercompany financing'; the BCL statistical category is 'other investment - loans to affiliated enterprises'. The remitting bank must use the beneficiary's LU IBAN and include the corporate reference; the receiving bank runs EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening and a source-of-funds check. Contributions in kind (e.g., assembly and calibration equipment) are permitted but require a statutory auditor's report where the value exceeds materiality thresholds set by the notary. No exchange-control receipt or import certificate is required for any inbound route.Two principal rails support the ISIC 3250 supply chain. (1) Trade payables by wire: for EU/EEA suppliers, ordinary SEPA Credit Transfer is used (same-day, low cost) against commercial invoice and goods-reception note; for non-EEA component suppliers (sensors, microprocessors, PCBs, batteries, plastic enclosures from Asia, Japan or the US), SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary account, intermediary bank, invoice reference and the corresponding customs import declaration; settlement is executed in EUR or in the traded currency (USD, CNH, JPY) with an FX forward booked the same day. (2) Documentary credits: the Luxembourg bank opens an irrevocable, optionally confirmed, sight or usance letter of credit (MT700) for procurement above EUR 200,000, with document sets comprising commercial invoice, full-set bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, certificate of origin and inspection certificate. On the export side, the SARL may receive payment by TT/MT103 before shipment or through an advised or confirmed export LC (MT710/MT720 for transferable credits), especially for Middle East and Asian public-hospital tenders; such tenders frequently require standby LC or on-demand guarantees MT760, which the bank issues under the SARL's security. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold for Local/Master File under Luxembourg law; all controlled transactions must be documented. Country-by-Country Reporting threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of zero-tax jurisdictions are not applicable in Luxembourg. However, Luxembourg enforces ATAD's general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) and its domestic substance doctrine. Real presence (office, employees, local decision-making, operational expenditure) is required to access treaty benefits, participation exemption, IP box, and to avoid recharacterisation. Substance deficiencies trigger tax benefit denial and potential director liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, late-payment interest of 0.8% per month, fine up to 50% of evaded tax, criminal imprisonment under Art. 407 of the Luxembourg General Tax Code, and ancillary administrative penalties

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns face criminal liability, imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and civil restitution to the Luxembourg state
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, 20% material-misstatement penalty, monthly late-payment interest of 0.8%, and potential net wealth tax reassessment

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for tax deficiencies if documentation failures result from intent or gross negligence; exposure includes disqualification and administrative fines
Shell Company without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, participation exemption, IP box 80% exemption; recharacterisation or tax transparency treatment; net wealth tax at 0.5%; additional 10% administrative fine

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid corporate taxes, subject to travel bans, commercial court disqualification, and prohibition from operating in Luxembourg
AML/CTF Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 or double the economic benefit derived from the transaction; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation of assets under Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004

Director personal liability: Directors can incur criminal liability for money laundering if they knowingly or negligently permit such payments; penalties include personal fines, travel bans, asset freezing, and imprisonment

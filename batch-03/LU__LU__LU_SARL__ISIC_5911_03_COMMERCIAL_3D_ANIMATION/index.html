Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; central e-portal MyGuichet.lu / Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation of the SARL, the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) assigns the RCS registration number and the business/tax identification number (numéro d'immatriculation), which is used by ACD for direct tax filings. The SARL must then apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + numeric ID) with AEDT through MyGuichet.lu/eDesk using the standard VAT registration procedure. A non-resident foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with AEDT; where the entity is established outside the EU, a Luxembourg tax representative is generally required. The application must state the business activity, shareholders and beneficial owners, place of establishment, and expected turnover. VAT registration is required before taxable supplies begin or as soon as the annual turnover threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident SARL is treated as a fully taxable separate person subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and VAT; no fiscal transparency or pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box and no transparent entity election. A Luxembourg SARL may elect fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR for corporate income tax and municipal business tax purposes, and VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Art. 60 LTV for Luxembourg VAT consolidation. It may also elect the optional 80% IP-box deduction for qualifying IP income under Art. 50bis LIR. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply mandatorily and override bilateral treatment; no elective hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for the Luxembourg small-business VAT franchise; no threshold for a resident SARL making taxable supplies, intra-Community acquisitions or imports; registration is required before taxable supplies begin and is obligatory when the threshold is exceeded. Non-resident suppliers with taxable supplies in Luxembourg may be required to register regardless of turnover, subject to OSS/reverse-charge exceptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported physically outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT if the SARL retains proof of removal from EU territory, including customs export declaration, transport and payment evidence. Intra-EU B2B supplies and B2B services supplied to customers established outside Luxembourg are subject to place-of-supply rules and do not attract Luxembourg VAT where the customer is a taxable person; exports of tangible animation assets, such as master files on storage media, qualify for zero-rating on physical export. Services to non-EU B2B customers and cross-border B2B services to EU customers are generally not subject to Luxembourg VAT, provided the taxable status and establishment evidence is contemporaneous.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EBL) for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and export contract (sales order / service agreement)
  • Proof of foreign customer establishment and taxable status, including the customer's VAT registration number or foreign tax identification
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank wire confirmation for the export consideration
  • Production/service delivery records and technical documentation evidencing the 3D animation services supplied

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping for B2B invoicing in Luxembourg. Invoices issued for VAT purposes must satisfy EU invoicing rules under Art. 5 LTV; B2G e-invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities must be transmitted via compliant PEPPOL formats where required. VAT returns are filed periodically (usually monthly or quarterly) through the AEDT e-space; no API-based real-time clearance is currently imposed on commercial animation/VFX service providers.

AEDT may claw back zero-rated VAT if the documentary bundle is incomplete or the supplier cannot substantiate physical export or the foreign business destination of the services. The disputed VAT becomes payable with default interest, and monetary fines may be imposed; intentional fraud may lead to criminal fines and imprisonment. Luxembourg's VAT assessment period is normally five years, extended to ten years in fraud cases, so export zero-rating files should be retained for at least that full period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member with EUR as sole legal tender. There is no exchange control law and no restriction on inward or outward transfer of funds for current or capital account transactions, as guaranteed by Articles 63 and 64 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittance is unblocked: interest and royalties incur zero Luxembourg withholding tax; dividend withholding tax is 15% domestically, 0% for EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (participation of at least 10% held for 12 months), and reduced treaty rates apply otherwise. Capital contribution, invoiced service payments, loan repayments and dividend distributions may all be effected without prior administrative authorization. Remaining formalities are limited to EU financial sanctions screening (e.g., EU Regulation 833/2014 as amended), suspicious transaction reporting, CRS/FATCA reporting, and BCL BOP statistical declarations for resident transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 per transaction or aggregated thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Non-applicable in the euro area. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation or conversion requirement on export proceeds; the SARL may retain foreign currency receivables (e.g., USD, GBP) indefinitely in any bank or non-bank payment account. Only statistical obligations apply: the resident company must report inward and outward service trade settlements to the BCL under the Luxembourg BOP reporting framework, and the intermediary bank generally files the transaction-level declaration. The 3D animation studio should maintain a VAT-compliant invoice trail for each export service to satisfy both BOP classification (EBOPS service category) and Luxembourg VAT rules governing B2B services place of supply.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); monetary policy competence is vested exclusively in the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem. Prudential and AML supervision of banks and non-bank payment institutions is exercised by the CSSF (Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier), with the BCL performing oversight of payment, clearing and settlement systems and compiling mandatory balance-of-payments (BOP) statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility, only as an operational disbursement augmentation layer; not viable for statutory capital. Wise B2B (Wise Europe S.A./Wise Payments Ltd) is an EEA/UK e-money institution passported into Luxembourg, not a Luxembourg credit institution; it can open current-account style multi-currency accounts and issue IBANs, and is legally permitted to provide payment services to an LU SARL under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive. Legal constraints: (1) the SARL's société en formation cannot use an EMI for the capital subscription because the notarized deed must be supported by a bank attestation bancaire issued by a credit institution, not an EMI; (2) EMI balances are e-money claims, not deposits, and are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL — Fonds de Garantie Dépôts Luxembourg); (3) EMIs may not grant credit or overdrafts; (4) treasury policy should cap EMI balances at operational day-to-day levels (e.g., below EUR 50,000) and route client receipts above that threshold to a Tier-1 prudential bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for share capital injection into the LU SARL: (1) the founding UBO or parent company transfers EUR into the blocked capital subscription account opened at an approved Luxembourg credit institution under the SARL's corporate name 'LU SARL(société en formation)', referencing field 70 of the SWIFT MT103, e.g., 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / SARL IN FORMATION / NOMINATIVE LIST OF SUBSCRIBERS' or, for SEPA payments, structured remittance information; (2) the notary requests the bank attestation certifying that the minimum statutory capital of EUR 12,000 (fully paid) is held and blocked until notarization; (3) after notarization, the bank statement of the blocked account must reflect release of funds to the free operational account; (4) for statutory formalities, include the RCS reservation number and the identity of each subscriber; (5) foreign-currency capital contributions (e.g., USD) must be converted to EUR at the ECB reference rate and any conversion loss must be topped up to preserve the minimum capital; (6) the bank and BCL treat the injection as a direct investment inflow to Luxembourg and require BOP statistical reporting under the Luxembourg investor-in-liabilities category; (7) sanctions screening applies to the ordering customer, intermediary banks and the UBO, so the origin of funds documentation must be provided before credit is given.Primary clearing rail for trade disbursements of on-going 3D animation projects is the SWIFT MT103 / MT202 COV message via the bank's corporate e-banking platform, used for all non-EUR and extra-EEA settlements (e.g., paying US studio partners and UK freelancers), with mandatory field 70 structured remittance referencing the qualified invoice, P.O. number and service description. For EUR payments inside the SEPA zone, the rail is the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with a maximum execution time of one business day and no practical value cap, processed through T2 (RTGS) or EURO1/STEP2 batch systems for same-day high-value credits. The bank should be instructed to use the ISO 20022 pain.001 message with the purpose code TXND/SUPP for service fees, so that the beneficiary's bank applies the correct EBOPS classification. For high-value disbursements above EUR 1 million, T2 direct participation (via the bank as member) provides finality; for ordinary VAT and payroll runs, SEPA Direct Debit (SDD) is available. Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) are not a standard instrument for CGI/VFX service trade; they should be avoided and replaced by MT103 documentary transfers with warranties against non-delivery, because services are intangible and bank-confirmed L/Cs create unnecessary discrepancies and eligibility risk. The SARL should maintain at least two beneficiary banks and one Wise B2B backup to ensure treasury resilience when intra-EU correspondent lines fail; cross-border outgoing wires should be batched before 11:00 CET to meet Luxembourg cut-off for same-day value.Plain vanilla deliverable FX forwards traded with an EEA-licensed credit institution under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement (or Luxembourg local form) — primarily EUR/USD and EUR/GBP to lock future client and international distribution revenues.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without full convertibility, permitted only where the underlying exposure is a confirmed production contract (e.g., remote visualization services for customers in restricted countries), documented so that the NDF settles against the reference rate without a deliverable leg.FX swaps for short-term liquidity rolling of USD receivables into EUR operational cash, executed under the same ISDA/CSA framework and subject to the SARL treasury policy prohibiting speculative positions.Vanilla FX options and zero-premium collars to protect annual USD revenue budgets without sacrificing upside, allowable as hedge-only instruments under the Luxembourg company law and IFRS 9 hedge accounting rules if the hedge relationship is formally documented.Cross-currency swaps for long-term financing or intercompany loans denominated in USD or GBP with mirror accounting, permitted under EU EMIR rules with CCP clearing threshold monitoring and confirmation/trade reporting obligations if volume thresholds are exceeded.

Legal constraints: (1) the SARL's société en formation cannot use an EMI for the capital subscription because the notarized deed must be supported by a bank attestation bancaire issued by a credit institution, not an EMI; (2) EMI balances are e-money claims, not deposits, and are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme (FGDL — Fonds de Garantie Dépôts Luxembourg); (3) EMIs may not grant credit or overdrafts; (4) treasury policy should cap EMI balances at operational day-to-day levels (e.g., below EUR 50,000) and route client receipts above that threshold to a Tier-1 prudential bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 consolidated group turnover to obligatory preparation of Local File and Master File; CbCR threshold EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not legislate a discrete ESR code but applies the EU ATAD substance-over-form, the OECD nexus approach, and the SOPARFI substance expectations; companies must demonstrate real personnel, decision-making, invoicing, and risk-management capacity commensurate with their IP, finance, and animation-production functions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 25,000 or the amount of evaded tax (whichever is greater) and/or custodial sentence up to five years; immediate assessment of evaded corporate income tax with 100% penalty and interest

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or orchestrate the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the underlying taxes; can face the same criminal penalties, travel bans, and debarment from managing Luxembourg companies
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the participation exemption and treaty benefits; recharacterization of transactions under Article 56ter LIR; retroactive tax assessment plus 5% monthly late-payment interest and administrative fines

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally charged under the 'abuse of law' doctrine for the additional tax, have decisions voided, and be subject to professional debarment if they knowingly facilitated the artificial arrangement
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for the SARL; suspension of operations; reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal imprisonment up to five years, personal fines, and prohibition orders where money-laundering facilitation is established; the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution to victims

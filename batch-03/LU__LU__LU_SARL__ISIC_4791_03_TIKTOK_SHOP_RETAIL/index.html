Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), maintained under the authority of the District Court of Luxembourg (Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg), electronic portal www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Amended Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (SARL regime), as modernized by the Law of 10 August 2016; access to the retail profession governed by the amended Law of 2 September 2011 on the establishment of commercial, artisanal, industrial and certain liberal activities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif / statuts) executed before a Luxembourg civil-law notary, filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed at incorporation; at least 25% of each cash quota must be paid up (aggregate minimum EUR 3,000) and deposited into a blocked escrow account ('compte bloqué') opened in the name of the company in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; funds are released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's (réviseur d'entreprises) valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) covering evacuation routes, fire-extinguishing equipment and alarm systems; structural load and stability inspection of storage racks and mezzanines; electrical installation conformity certificate from an approved inspector; workplace safety and occupational health clearance (visite) by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including ergonomic and manual-handling risk assessment for order-picking staff.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed at incorporation; at least 25% of each cash quota must be paid up (aggregate minimum EUR 3,000) and deposited into a blocked escrow account ('compte bloqué') opened in the name of the company in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; funds are released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's (réviseur d'entreprises) valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Digital portals: MyGuichet.lu (business registration), eTVA VAT filing desk, and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SARL with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RBCS/LBR) through MyGuichet.lu and obtain the national 13-digit identification number (matricule). 2) File VAT registration Form 640 with the AEDT to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits). 3) Non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a domestic establishment must register for VAT through the AEDT (non-resident VAT registration) and, if established outside the EU/EEA without a fixed establishment, appoint an approved VAT representative in Luxembourg (Article 65 of the Luxembourg VAT Law). 4) Register any beneficial owner in the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE) within one month of incorporation. 5) Submit supporting documents electronically: articles of association, proof of registered address, lease or service agreement, financial standing documents and powers of attorney; VAT ID is effective from the date the taxable activity commences or from the date of first taxable transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a Luxembourg-resident SARL is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IRC), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune, IF). Territorial scope: resident entities are taxed on worldwide income with unilateral and treaty-based relief; qualified dividends and capital gains benefit from the participation exemption (10% or EUR 1.2 million participation thresholds; 12-month holding requirement). The SARL's shareholders are additionally taxable on distributed dividends.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box classification regime; tax status follows legal form (SARL = corporation). Available statutory elections: (i) VAT group option (assujetti unique, Article 61 VAT Law) allowing a single VAT registration for financially integrated resident entities; (ii) corporate income tax fiscal unity (intégration fiscale, Article 164bis LIR) for resident companies directly or indirectly holding 95% of a Luxembourg subsidiary; (iii) registration under the Union OSS or Import OSS schemes for cross-border distance sales and low-value consignments; (iv) binding advance tax ruling requests (Article 29a General Tax Law). Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD 1 and 2 are fully transposed (Article 168quater LIR; Articles 61-61c of the tax adaptation law); no elective hybrid-mismatch carve-out is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual VAT-exclusive Luxembourg turnover; small-business exemption threshold under Article 57 VAT Law as of 1 January 2025; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold). For EU distance sales of goods covered by the One Stop Shop regime, the EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000; non-established non-EU suppliers are registrable regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to (i) goods physically exported outside the European Union and evidenced by a customs export declaration, and (ii) services directly connected with such exports (transport, freight-handling, ancillary services) under Articles 70-74 of the Luxembourg VAT Law. For cross-border TikTok Shop retail consignments, 0% treatment requires proof of physical dispatch from Luxembourg, transport out of the EU, and the exporter's status as legal owner of the goods; consignments of intrinsic value not exceeding EUR 150 sold through an Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) scheme are VAT-relieved at the point of import. Intra-EU distance sales are subject to the Union OSS regime at the destination country's VAT rate. The 180-day export-proceeds benchmark serves as the audit-standard documentation window for the export file; Luxembourg VAT law does not separately impose a civil FX repatriation deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EAD/DRN) issued through the Luxembourg PLDA/ECS customs system, including MRN
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR international transport consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching TikTok Shop order data
  • IOSS VAT identification number and IOSS sales list (for consignments with intrinsic value ≤ EUR 150)
  • Inward remittance evidence of export proceeds (SWIFT MT103, banking statement, or Wise B2B payout confirmation)
  • TikTok Shop marketplace payout report / platform sales certificate reconciled to the export documents
  • Customs broker representation authorization or indirect representation mandate, where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory API-based transaction clearance applies to Luxembourg VAT. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 network and the national bill.lu e-invoicing platform; B2B and B2C invoicing remains voluntary (PDF, EDI or equivalent accepted), subject to recipient consent, authenticity and integrity control, and retention rights. A future mandatory structured e-invoicing (EN 16931) regime for cross-border B2B transactions is expected under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) directive, effective no earlier than 2030.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT reassessment at the standard 17% rate, with the limitation period extended from 5 to 10 years in cases of fraud, plus monthly default interest of 0.8% on unpaid VAT and clawback of all input VAT previously recovered in reliance on the invalid zero-rating. Administrative fines apply for documentation failures, and criminal prosecution under Article 93 of the VAT Law and the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) may result in fines up to EUR 250,000 (or double the evaded tax) and imprisonment of up to 5 years, with additional publication sanctions and professional disqualification of responsible managers. LuxLeaks-style whistleblower and administrative cooperation channels further increase audit scrutiny for cross-border marketplace sellers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under EU free movement of capital and payments (TFEU Articles 63-66). Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no prior authorisation for cross-border payments, and no restriction on repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, or profits. An SARL may freely convert EUR to any currency for business purposes. Dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at 15% (as reduced by double tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive), but there is no currency convertibility or central-bank approval hurdle. Injected share capital may be returned to shareholders without material FX restrictions, subject to notarial/registry and corporate law compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg has no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters may retain all EUR or foreign-currency proceeds and are not required to transfer them to the BCL or any state entity. Documentary retention obligations under customs, VAT, and AML regulations remain the primary compliance requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting in concert with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and financial stability.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex are legally viable for cross-border payment settlement and operating expenditures under the EEA passport framework. They are not licensed as full credit institutions and therefore may not serve as the principal treasury or capital-escrow bank for a Luxembourg SARL. Their utility is strongest for fast TikTok Shop merchant payouts, FX conversions, and low-value cross-border supplier disbursements. Legal constraints include the absence of Luxembourg Credit Institution status, reduced depositor protection coverage, and bank-specific KYC onboarding limitations on high-volume e-commerce flows. A hybrid model — primary institutional bank account plus an EMI sub-ledger for high-speed clearing — is prudent and widely accepted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be routed through SWIFT MT103 messages using purpose code CAPT (Capital Injection to a Subsidiary) or GFC (Funding of a Branch/Subsidiary). The remitter must include the Luxembourg SARL's full corporate name, commercial registry number, and the specific bank account IBAN. The bank may require the initial capital confirmation from the Luxembourg Trade Register (LBR) or a notary's certificate for the souscription and libération of shares. The European wire-transfer regulation (EU) No 2023/1113 and AML provisions impose mandatory originator/beneficiary information. No BCL approval or pre-approval for FX conversion is required; any EUR/USD or EUR/CNY conversion may be completed at the receiving bank's applicable spot rate.Outbound trade disbursements to suppliers and logistics providers are cleared through SWIFT MT103 or SEPA instant/regular credit transfers for EU counterparties. For high-value inventory or supply-chain purchases, commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the Luxembourg bank and confirmed through a correspondent bank provide the preferred clearing rail. Each disbursement must be supported by an invoice, packing list, transport document (B/L or airway bill), and, when applicable, proof of intra-Community VAT-exempt supply or export VAT zero-rating. The bank will normally apply market-standard FX margins and may require a standing execution order for recurring TikTok Shop inventory payments in CNY or USD.Outright FX Forwards – physically settled EUR/USD and EUR/CNY forwards with maturities up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA or local FX master agreement with a licensed Luxembourg bank.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permitted for currencies with delivery restrictions (e.g., CNY, KRW, INR) through the Luxembourg bank's international branch, with settlement in EUR or USD on fix dates referencing the offshore currency rate.FX Swaps / Rollovers – simultaneous spot and forward transactions to manage interim liquidity mismatches between TikTok Shop payout timing and supplier payment deadlines.FX Options – vanilla call and put options on EUR pairs providing asymmetric hedge protection, subject to bank OTC derivative eligibility and mandatory margining rules under EMIR.

A hybrid model — primary institutional bank account plus an EMI sub-ledger for high-speed clearing — is prudent and widely accepted.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined, comprising 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax (1.19% aggregate), and Luxembourg City municipal business tax of 6.75%; the municipal business tax rate varies by commune between approximately 6.75% and 10.10%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders, unless a Domestic Law exemption or Double Tax Treaty provides a lower rate.

The corporate income tax return for a fiscal year must be filed electronically no later than 31 December of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; extensions are discretionary and subject to ACD approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No single universal transaction threshold is published in the Luxembourg Income Tax Law. The ACD expects a full OECD-aligned Transfer Pricing Master File and Local File for Luxembourg entities forming part of a multinational group with global consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or greater (CoCR threshold). Smaller groups should maintain contemporaneous local TP documentation when cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 500,000 in aggregate or when the Luxembourg entity's annual turnover surpasses EUR 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg is not an Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction, but the ACD applies the OECD-style economic substance and substance-over-form doctrine through the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR), ATAD anti-hybrid legislation, Circular LIR 14/6, and Pillar Two global minimum tax rules. A SARL relying on treaty benefits or holding-company exemptions must demonstrate local decision-making, personnel, premises, and real economic activity commensurate with its digital retail operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment, 0.8% monthly late-payment interest, administrative fines up to EUR 500,000, confiscation of the improperly shifted profit, and criminal fines of up to EUR 500,000 on conviction for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts when they have signed false returns, abusively drained corporate assets, or knowingly permitted fictitious invoicing; criminal conviction may carry imprisonment of up to five years, travel bans, and civil restitution orders.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Rejection of treaty benefits, denial of participation exemption, retroactive reclassification of income under GAAR, application of Pillar Two substance rules, net wealth tax recalculations, and supplementary tax assessments with penalties.

Director personal liability: De facto directors and shadow managers may be treated as taxable persons or managers of the sham entity, incurring personal income tax exposure, civil liability for the tax shortfall, and potential criminal sanction for fraudulent use of a corporate vehicle.
AML / UBO Registration Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the RBE framework up to EUR 250,000 and criminal fines for money laundering of up to EUR 1,250,000, together with asset freezes and closure of the SARL's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be criminally prosecuted for money laundering, incurring imprisonment, restrictive travel measures, personal asset forfeiture, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
VAT and Cross-Border E-Commerce Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessments, penalty interest, fines of up to EUR 250,000, and potential suspension of the marketplace account where the Luxembourg tax authorities issue a joint liability claim against the platform.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally directed the false VAT position may be jointly liable for the unpaid VAT, may be subject to travel bans during criminal proceedings, and may face imprisonment in aggravated fraud cases.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct and corporate income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) as the national company and RCS registry; official business and tax portals include guichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu and lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LU SARL first obtains a national business identification number (matricule) through RCS/LBR after notarial or e-formation registration; the RCS/LBR transmits the registration data to the tax administrations. Before commencing taxable activity, the company must apply for VAT registration through the guichet.lu professional portal or by written application to AEDT, generally within 15 days of the start of activity, attaching the RCS excerpt, constitutional documents, manager and shareholder identification, place of business evidence, ownership details and projected turnover. AEDT then issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (TVA ID in LU format). If the company will make customs-export declarations, an EORI number is also required. A non-resident SaaS vendor without an EU establishment may be required to register directly for Luxembourg VAT and may be obliged to appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative where the relevant simplified registration conditions are not available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; entity classification follows the civil-law legal form, and an SARL is a taxable opaque entity resident in Luxembourg, not eligible for pass-through treatment. The principal statutory tax elections available include the fiscal unity regime (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR, whereby a 95%-owned Luxembourg subgroup may elect consolidated treatment for corporate income tax and municipal business tax, with the election being binding for five years; the IP box election for qualifying intellectual property income; notional interest deduction notification; and the VAT option to tax real estate. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply to cross-border structures and do not create elective pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover, being the current statutory small-enterprise VAT franchise threshold for Luxembourg-established businesses; a non-established foreign EMR SaaS provider supplying into Luxembourg has no turnover threshold and must register from its first taxable supply in Luxembourg, and the EU EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold may apply separately to distance sales and electronically supplied services supplied to EU consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union may be zero-rated at 0% VAT if the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside EU territory by or on behalf of the Luxembourg seller, and the seller obtains and retains proof of export within the period required by AEDT practice, generally within 90 days from the supply. For medical EMR SaaS and other digital services, the place-of-supply rules generally remove the supply from Luxembourg VAT rather than applying an export zero rate: B2B supplies to a non-EU taxable business are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT; B2B supplies to an EU taxable business are reverse-charged to the customer; B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside Luxembourg VAT jurisdiction; and B2C supplies to EU consumers may be taxed through the OSS mechanism. To support 0% or outside-scope treatment, the EMR SaaS contract must identify the customer's business establishment, the service recipient's VAT/tax status, and the place of use or enjoyment, and the invoice must contain all statutory minimum particulars.

  • Validated customs export declaration (IE-529 or equivalent electronic export file released by Luxembourg/EU customs), evidencing departure to a non-EU destination and the Luxembourg seller's EORI status
  • Transport document, being the signed Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note, showing the Luxembourg entity as exporter/shipper and the non-EU customer as consignee or destination receiver
  • Inward remittance certificate, comprising bank credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or equivalent foreign-exchange receipt document evidencing payment from the foreign buyer in convertible currency

Luxembourg currently does not impose mandatory real-time B2B fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction controls for VAT purposes; invoices, including electronic invoices, must preserve authenticity, integrity and legibility under the Luxembourg VAT Law. B2G electronic invoicing to Luxembourg public bodies is mandatory under EU Directive 2014/55/EU and is exchanged through the Luxembourg PEPPOL BIS/eFIA environment. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, Intrastat filings and OSS returns must be submitted electronically, and complete invoice audit files must be available to AEDT upon request.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may reassess a zero-rated export where proof of export, transport or inward remittance is deficient, fraudulent or inconsistent. The standard limitation period for VAT assessment is five years, extended to ten years where fraud, omission or false declarations are established. Reassessment reverses the 0% rate and applies Luxembourg VAT at the standard 17% rate together with late-payment interest; administrative fines may reach up to twice the tax evaded, and criminal sanctions, including fines and imprisonment, apply for knowingly false documentation or forged export evidence. Any input VAT previously attributed to the failed zero-rating claim is retrospectively clawed back in the same assessment. The complete customs and remittance file must be preserved in the AEDT audit archive for a minimum of ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg treats the euro as fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the EU Capital Requirements Regulation protect unrestricted cross-border payments, trade financing, and repatriation of profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from an LU SARL is subject only to ordinary corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax (15% standard, reduced under tax treaties), not to convertibility or prior authorization hurdles. The BCL does not impose exchange controls, surrender requirements, or open-position limits on resident non-financial corporations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or regulatory repatriation timelines in Luxembourg. Export receipts denominated in foreign currency may be retained in commercial accounts, sold freely, or used to settle foreign trade obligations. The BCL only requires statistical reporting under the Balance of Payments (BoP) framework for cross-border payments exceeding de minimis thresholds; such reporting is satisfied through the resident bank's automatic transmission of transaction data and does not affect timing or use of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank and competes with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking oversight; however, Luxembourg is a euro-area member and foreign exchange policy is primarily harmonized under the Eurosystem framework of the European Central Bank (ECB). No separate Luxembourg foreign exchange administration or capital-control licensing authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium to Low for a Luxembourg SARL's primary treasury relationship. A UI (user-interface) digital EMI chartered outside the EEA is not viable for statutory share capital blocking or high-value operational settlements because Luxembourg CSSF-authorized banks must hold the legal capital account until registration. EEA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions may be used for low-value client collections and SaaS subscription disbursements under the EU payment services passport, but they are not substitutes for a full-service Luxembourg banking relationship covering trade finance, FX hedging, and shareholder distributions. Offshore EMIs pose compliance risk because they generally lack ISO 20022 SEPA-native clearing, corporate KYC infrastructure, and CSSF regulatory standing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital wires must be routed through SWIFT MT103 to the Luxembourg bank's euro clearing account. The payment should reference the shareholder loan or capital subscription documentation and use the ISO 20022 remittance information for transparent classification. Purpose codes in the SWIFT field 72 or 77T must align with EU BoP statistical categories: for subscribed share capital use code 'D.992' or 'ECU' depending on bank preference; for shareholder loans use 'D.161' with supporting board resolution. Verification includes confirmation of the originator's bank and country, reconciliation of the amount to the share-capital blocking certificate, and generation of a BCL external-assets/liabilities report. To prevent misclassification, instruct the remitting bank to state 'INCREASE OF SHARE CAPITAL' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' in the payment details, attach the RCS excerpt and notarized signatures, and record the funds as an escrow deposit capable of release only after notarization and RCS registration.For vendor payments and SaaS infrastructure costs, the primary clearing rails are SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for euro-zone beneficiaries and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA trade settlements. Payment under the LC must be routed through the issuing bank's SWIFT MT700/MT400 messaging. Same-day euro clearing is available via TARGET2, while intra-SEPA payments are automatic. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% headline corporate income tax (IRC) for fiscal year 2024; adding the 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC (effective 18.19%) and the municipal business tax (impot commercial communal, 6.75% in Luxembourg City) produces an aggregate statutory burden of approximately 24.94% for a Luxembourg City SARL.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends distributed by a SARL to non-resident shareholders. A 0% rate applies to qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds a direct shareholding of at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12 months. Double tax treaty rates for portfolio shareholders typically range from 5% to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed no later than 31 December of the calendar year following the close of the fiscal year (e.g., the return for fiscal year ending 31 December 2024 is due by 31 December 2025). The ACD may grant an extension, commonly until 30 June of the following calendar year, upon written request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory local file or master file threshold exists in Luxembourg. A mandatory Country-by-Country Report applies to Luxembourg ultimate parents or surrogate filing entities of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000, due within 12 months of fiscal year-end. For all cross-border controlled transactions, including EMR SaaS licensing fees and IT development services, contemporaneous Article 56 LIR transfer pricing documentation must nevertheless be available upon request; failure to produce it exposes the SARL to an arm's length adjustment, denial of deduction, and penalty surcharges.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code. Substance is instead enforced through Article 56 LIR (arm's length principle), the anti-abuse doctrine under section 6 of the Abgabenordnung, ATAD CFC and anti-hybrid rules, OECD Nexus requirements for the IP Box, and beneficial-ownership conditions under tax treaties and EU directives. The EMR SaaS SARL must evidence genuine local presence, including board decisions taken in Luxembourg, qualified staff managing the SaaS contract book and IP exploitation, physical premises, and local bank accounts. A letterbox structure would be denied IP Box, treaty, and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits and recharacterised as an abusive conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: ACD reassessment with a penalty surcharge of 50% of the evaded tax for deliberate omission, rising to 100% for fraudulent manoeuvres, plus default interest of 0.6% per month. Criminal prosecution for fiscal fraud carries fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, together with publication of the conviction and exclusion from public tenders.

Director personal liability: The managing director (gerant) and beneficial owners may face veil-piercing for simulation and fraud, becoming jointly and severally liable for the underpaid corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and penalties. Criminal conviction may trigger a ban on leaving Luxembourg pending payment of the tax debt and disqualification from holding directorship positions in Luxembourg entities.
Shell Company and Letterbox Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of reported IP royalty and SaaS income as constructive distributions to the foreign shareholder, triggering a 15% withholding tax recapture and loss of the 80% IP Box exemption; penalty of 25% to 50% of the reassessed tax; deregistration from the Luxembourg RCS and refusal to issue tax residence certificates.

Director personal liability: The gerant is personally liable for the withholding tax that should have been collected on recharacterised distributions. Civil clawback actions may be brought in insolvency proceedings, and criminal sanctions under section 6 Abgabenordnung may include travel restrictions, asset freezes, and confiscation of the diverted EMR revenues.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Synthetic Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Article 56 LIR primary transfer pricing adjustment with default interest at 0.6% per month; denial of deduction for non-substantiated intercompany fees; penalty of 10% to 50% of the reassessed tax, increased to 100% for gross negligence; notification of the adjustment to treaty partners under mutual agreement procedures.

Director personal liability: Signing directors face personal fines for knowingly participating in false transfer pricing documentation; bank account garnishment and seizure of personal assets may follow a TP-driven insolvency, and the commercial court may impose personal bankruptcy prohibition (faillite personnelle) for negligent management.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Escalating administrative fines imposed by the RBE registrar and the CSSF, including coercive daily penalties (astreinte) up to EUR 250 per day until the declaration is made; criminal fines for knowingly false UBO declarations; suspension of shareholder rights and distribution payments in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: De jure directors and the designated compliance officer are personally fined for obstruction of the registrar; imprisonment of up to 5 years may apply where the EMR subscription cash flows are used to disguise proceeds of crime, and travel bans may be imposed during the criminal investigation.
VAT, Payroll, and Social Security Leakage

Statutory sanction: Immediate enforced recovery of unpaid VAT and payroll taxes, default interest of 0.6% per month, late-filing fines up to EUR 1,500, and VAT reassessment penalties up to 10% per quarter; criminal exposure for fraudulent misappropriation of client-withheld amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes withheld from employees or collected from clients where funds are diverted to the parent company; the insolvency administrator may pursue civil restitution, and criminal courts may impose travel bans and custodial sentences for misappropriation of public monies.
Health Data Protection and Statutory Licensing Breach

Statutory sanction: CNPD (Luxembourg data protection authority) administrative fines up to EUR 20,000,000 or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher; complementary criminal sanctions and suspension of the medical software service pending compliance.

Director personal liability: Chairpersons and data protection officers may face personal fines and criminal liability for unlawful processing of health data; travel bans and prohibition from exercising commercial activity may accompany criminal convictions for serious GDPR breaches affecting patient safety.

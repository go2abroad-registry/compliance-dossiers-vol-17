Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permissible. Under the Law of 10 August 1915, a SARL must have a registered office ('siege social') situated in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, being a real, identified place capable of receiving service of process and where statutory corporate records are accessible. A commercial domiciliation company providing a full administrative address satisfies the bare registration requirement. However, for a high-risk cross-border nutraceuticals e-commerce SARL (ISIC 4791), a pure virtual-postbox model is not commercially viable: a physical operating substance is expected for the mandatory establishment authorisation under the Law of 2 September 2011, the Ministry of Health food-supplement market-access notification, VAT registration, and the required high-risk merchant account. A hybrid model (domiciliation address plus local substance arrangement with staff, records, and management oversight) is strongly recommended.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company-in-formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; funds are released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a registered auditor's valuation report (rapport du réviseur d'entreprises).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) following inspection of the registered office and warehouse premises; structural load inspection certificate delivered by the municipal building authority for racking and storage installations; workplace clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) confirming compliance with occupational safety, electrical safety, first-aid, evacuation planning, and hygiene requirements for employees handling dietary supplements.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company-in-formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; funds are released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a registered auditor's valuation report (rapport du réviseur d'entreprises).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

VAT and excise: Administration des douanes et accises (ADA), Luxembourg City, via adu.public.lu and the e-TVA/MyGuichet.lu portals. Corporate income tax and withholding: Administration des contributions directes (ACD), via impotsdirects.public.lu. Commercial registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS).

E-government filing portal

For a resident LU_SARL: (1) incorporate with the LBR/RCS and obtain the 13-digit national identification number; (2) file Form 14 (VAT registration) with the ADA through MyGuichet.lu / e-TVA, receiving an LU + 8-digit VAT number; (3) register beneficial owners and managing directors in the RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs). A non-resident vendor without a Luxembourg establishment applies directly to the ADA for a LU VAT number; if intra-EU B2C distance sales remain below the €10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold, the vendor may elect OSS/IOSS filing and avoid full local registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: A Luxembourg-resident SARL has unlimited tax liability (worldwide income) and is subject to corporate income tax (CIT 17%), solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT) and municipal business tax (Luxembourg City rate 6.75%, combined ~24.94%). A foreign-owned SARL is fully eligible for the 100% participation exemption on qualifying dividends and the 50% exemption on capital gains, subject to holding conditions. A branch of a foreign company is taxed only on Luxembourg-source commercial profit. — For a resident LU_SARL: (1) incorporate with the LBR/RCS and obtain the 13-digit national identification number; (2) file Form 14 (VAT registration) with the ADA through MyGuichet.lu / e-TVA, receiving an LU + 8-digit VAT number; (3) register beneficial owners and managing directors in the RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: A Luxembourg-resident SARL has unlimited tax liability (worldwide income) and is subject to corporate income tax (CIT 17%), solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT) and municipal business tax (Luxembourg City rate 6.75%, combined ~24.94%). A foreign-owned SARL is fully eligible for the 100% participation exemption on qualifying dividends and the 50% exemption on capital gains, subject to holding conditions. A branch of a foreign company is taxed only on Luxembourg-source commercial profit.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; the SARL is a statutory entity-level taxpayer. Available elections: (i) Article 164bis LIR fiscal unity (95%+ direct/indirect Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries, with profit/loss attribution), (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) per Art. 21(3) VAT Law, (iii) ATAD 2 hybrid/mismatch self-notification, and (iv) quarterly/advance VAT return filing for eligible small taxpayers. Foreign entity classification choices (e.g., US check-the-box) must be reconciled with Luxembourg ATAD hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€35,000 annual taxable turnover for the resident small-business exemption (Art. 57 VAT Law); non-resident suppliers have no threshold — any taxable supply in Luxembourg triggers registration unless B2B reverse charge or OSS/IOSS applies; intra-EU B2C distance-selling threshold: €10,000 EU-wide (OSS regime).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) exports of goods to non-EU destinations (Art. 56 VAT Law) and (ii) intra-EU B2B supplies where the customer provides a valid VAT number confirmed via VIES and the supply is recorded in the EC Sales List (Art. 55 VAT Law). For the nutraceuticals e-commerce model, goods physically dispatched from Luxembourg to non-EU consumers qualify for 0% only with documentary proof of actual departure from the EU within 90 days. Ancillary freight, transport, and handling services can also be zero-rated where directly linked to the exported goods. Non-export related online B2C digital supplies follow place-of-supply rules subject to Luxembourg VAT at 17%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS confirmation with MRN) evidencing export
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR / CIM showing physical exit from the EU
  • Proof of payment and/or Inward Remittance Certificate
  • For intra-EU B2B: customer VAT number validated via VIES and corresponding EC Sales List entry

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control in Luxembourg. Mandatory PEPPOL e-invoicing for B2G since 1 Jan 2024; B2B e-invoicing mandatory from 1 Jan 2026 for financial/insurance sectors and for all B2B from 1 Jan 2028 (Law of 7 July 2023). E-invoices must comply with EN 16931 (UBL/CII) and be exchanged via a certified PEPPOL access point. Periodic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly), EC Sales Lists, and OSS returns remain the primary reporting skeleton.

Export evidence must be secured within 90 days of supply; failure converts the supply to taxable at 17% with late-payment interest at 0.8% per commenced month (Art. 61 VAT Law). Incorrect zero-rating claims attract clawback of VAT plus administrative fines up to €25,000 per exposure; intentional fraud under Art. 60 VAT Law carries criminal fines (€10,000–€50,000) and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years. The reassessment period extends to 10 years in cases of concealment. Luxembourg has no express statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark serves as the arm's-length intercompany settlement standard for transfer-pricing integrity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for current-account and capital-account operations under Luxembourg and EU law (TFEU Article 63). The euro is legal tender; there are no exchange-control licences, quotas, approval requirements or import/export licensing obstacles for current payments, dividends, interest, royalties or capital repatriation. Profit remittance is unrestricted after statutory reserves are maintained and annual accounts are approved; the practical hurdles are AML/KYC documentation, the Luxembourg CIES ultimate-beneficial-owner register filing, and substance/board resolution approvals, not currency regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory retention period for export receipts. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained in FX accounts, converted freely to EUR, or used directly for cross-border trade payments; only general Luxembourg company-law, transfer-pricing and substance documentation requirements apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the central bank of Luxembourg and member of the Eurosystem; prudential banking supervision is shared with the European Central Bank under the Single Supervisory Mechanism, while the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) acts as the national licensing, conduct-of-business and AML/CFT supervisor. No separate foreign-exchange control institution exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High as a supplementary payment-collection and disbursement rail, but Low as the primary treasury or escrow bank. A non-Luxembourg fintech EMI cannot hold the SARL's issued capital or give the same legal certainty as a CSSF-regulated credit institution. EU passporting of an EMI is available under PSD2 after CSSF notification, while non-EU providers require Luxembourg e-money or payment-institution authorisation. They remain acceptable only as merchant acquiring or commercial payment modules and must be backed by a sub-ledger sweep to a Luxembourg bank account; core treasury and statutory capital must be maintained with a licensed EU credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline rate in Luxembourg City (17% national corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge, i.e. 1.19%, + 6.75% municipal business tax); municipal component varies by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (Article 164 LIR); 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parents holding at least 10% and fulfilling a 12-month holding period; DTA rates may reduce the statutory rate to 5%, 10% or 0% depending on ownership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return for a fiscal year closing on 31 December of year N must be filed by 31 December of year N+1; for non-calendar fiscal years, the deadline is generally 31 December immediately following the close of the fiscal year. Extension requests must be submitted before the original deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting; Luxembourg does not impose a fixed Local File or Master File filing threshold, but the ACD can demand documentation for any material controlled cross-border transaction, usually within 30 days. For the SARL, all nutraceutical intercompany royalties, e-commerce service fees and procurement flows should be contemporaneously documented even below the CbCR threshold.

Economic substance scope: No separate ESR statute comparable to Caribbean or Gulf regimes; however, Luxembourg applies the OECD BEPS general anti-abuse principles and Article 56a LIR, requiring real activity, staff, premises, functions, assets and risks. The SARL must be more than a conduit to access treaty benefits and EU directives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud / False Return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 10% late payment or late filing surcharge, 0.6% monthly default interest, administrative fine up to EUR 25,000, criminal fine and up to five years imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons signing the return are jointly liable for unpaid taxes in case of intent; the corporate veil may be pierced, with civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans and disqualification orders in criminal tax proceedings.

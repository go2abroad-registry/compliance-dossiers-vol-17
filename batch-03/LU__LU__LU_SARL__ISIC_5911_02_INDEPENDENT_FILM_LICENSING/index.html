Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation; capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and the blockage certificate (attestation de blocage) delivered to the notary before execution of the deed; each share must have a minimum nominal value of EUR 1.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGIDS) and the competent municipal authority is required for office premises with a surface exceeding 500 m2 or an occupant load of over 100 persons, including evacuation plans, fire-extinguishing equipment, and electrical installation verification; the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) must receive the prior written opening declaration and the workplace must satisfy the health, lighting, ventilation, ergonomic, and first-aid requirements of the Luxembourg Labor Code; structural load clearance applies where open-shelf storage of physical film prints is maintained.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at formation; capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a Luxembourg credit institution and the blockage certificate (attestation de blocage) delivered to the notary before execution of the deed; each share must have a minimum nominal value of EUR 1.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The unified electronic portal is MyGuichet.lu (www.guichet.lu); company registration is managed by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

E-government filing portal

[1] Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary with a minimum share capital of EUR 12,000; register within one month in the RCS via the e-Registration platform 'LBR.lu'. The RCS issues the company's 13-digit national identification number ('matricule'), which serves as the corporate tax identification number for all direct tax purposes; no separate ACD registration is required. [2] For VAT, file the official application Form 640 electronically via MyGuichet.lu, or for non-established foreign entities Form 644 (simplified registration of non-established taxable persons), attaching the amended articles of association, RCS extract, proof of the manager's identity and power of attorney if a VAT representative is appointed. The AEDT issues the VAT identification number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits, normally within 2-4 weeks. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque taxpayer). The SARL is a Luxembourg tax-resident company taxed on worldwide income: corporate income tax (IRC) at a flat 17% headline rate, an unemployment/solidarity surcharge of 7% on IRC, and the municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at a communal rate of 6.75% in Luxembourg City, leading to an aggregate corporate tax burden of approximately 24.94% for the 2024/2025 fiscal year. Net wealth tax of 0.5% applies (0.05% on the portion of taxable net wealth exceeding EUR 500 million). The participation exemption regime (Art. 166 LIR) exempts qualifying dividends and capital gains from IRC and net wealth tax. The tax scope is worldwide but a territorial approach applies only to non-resident taxpayers taxed on Luxembourg-source income; there is no tax-exempt zone regime.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; the SARL is irrebuttably opaque under the Luxembourg corporate income tax code. Available elections and statutory regimes of relevance: (i) VAT group election ('assujetti unique', Art. 60 Luxembourg VAT law) allowing the SARL and its close economic, financial, and organisational links to file as a single taxable person; (ii) corporate fiscal unity election (Art. 164bis LIR) for a resident parent holding at least 95% of a resident subsidiary, enabling profit/loss offset; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) election on increases in qualifying equity; (iv) IP box election granting an 80% exemption of qualifying intellectual property income (must be declared with the annual tax return and substantiated with qualifying IP assets and development evidence); (v) participation exemption is mandatory once the 10% or EUR 1.2 million holding threshold and substance conditions are met, with no separate election; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD II apply automatically and cannot be elected out; (vii) SCSp-type fiscally transparent vehicles are available at constitution but are not relevant to a classic SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 of annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the domestic micro-business exemption under Art. 57a VAT law; revenue above EUR 35,000 triggers mandatory VAT registration in Luxembourg. For non-established foreign suppliers there is no turnover threshold - registration is mandatory before carrying out any taxable transaction in Luxembourg, subject to the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) simplification for distance sales of goods up to EUR 10,000 and for electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons, in which case the supplier can declare VAT in its home Member State instead of registering in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical exports of goods (e.g., film masters, hard drives, marketing materials) dispatched to a destination outside the EU, a 0% VAT rate applies provided the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days and are evidenced by a customs export declaration. For the independent film licensing business model of the LU_SARL, the predominant cross-border supplies are licensing services / royalties: intra-EU B2B licensing of copyrights and film rights is outside Luxembourg VAT, with the customer self-assessing VAT under reverse charge; B2B licensing to third-country licensees is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction); B2C electronically supplied content to foreign consumers is taxable where the consumer is established (OSS may be used for EU consumers). Thus, classic 'export zero-rating' on goods is the exception, while the zero/no-VAT outcome on licensing services is achieved through place-of-supply and reverse-charge mechanisms, requiring commercial and payment documentation rather than a customs export declaration.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration d'exportation, lodged electronically in the EU ICS/ECAX customs system) for physical goods; not applicable to pure intellectual property licensing flows in the absence of tangible goods.
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, air waybill, or certified postal receipt evidencing physical dispatch of film materials outside the EU, where applicable.
  • Executed licence agreement containing the IP description, territory, duration, royalty formula, and clear identification of the foreign licensee (relevant in all licensing scenarios).
  • Commercial tax invoice issued by the LU_SARL referencing the place-of-supply rule, the customer's VAT registration number validated through VIES, and the reverse-charge mention for intra-EU B2B services.
  • Bank credit evidence (SWIFT / MT103, bank statement, or letter of credit settlement) showing the inward remittance of the royalty consideration to the SARL's Luxembourg bank account.
  • Proof of the licensee's establishment: foreign trade register extract, OECD/EU VAT certificate, or a certificate of tax residence when the licensor claims no-Luxembourg-VAT treatment.

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time tax clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API-based revenue stamping, or fiscal digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg as of 2025. E-invoices are accepted only with the recipient's consent; paper and PDF invoices are still valid. In line with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' directive adopted on 11 March 2025, Luxembourg is required to transpose mandatory structured e-invoicing obligations for intra-EU B2B transactions by 30 June 2028, with the actual mandatory issuance of e-invoices from 1 July 2030; until then, invoice storage must guarantee authenticity, integrity, and legibility for the statutory 10-year retention period, and there is no requirement to pre-clear invoices electronically with the AEDT.

Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure serves as the practical documentation benchmark used by the AEDT and ACD for verifying that the underlying export/licensing transaction is genuine and economically performed. Under the VAT law (Art. 82-89), failure to produce the required export or place-of-supply evidence results in: (i) reassessment of VAT at the full 17% standard rate on the gross royalty amount, with no deduction for foreign input VAT; (ii) late-payment interest of 0.8% per month (9.6% p.a.); (iii) administrative fines from EUR 250 to EUR 6,000 per infraction, and up to EUR 12,500 for fraudulent concealment; (iv) a standard limitation period of 5 years for reassessment, extended to 10 years in the case of fraud, simulated operation, or abuse of law (abus fiscal under Art. 6 AStG / Art. 89 LDIR). Criminal prosecution may be initiated under the general criminal law and the Luxembourg tax fraud statute with imprisonment up to 5 years where the licensing documentation is fabricated or where the structure lacks genuine economic substance; the AEDT's audit division specifically examines cross-border royalty chains for artificial conduit arrangements, mandatory substance requirements, and the economic reality of the film licensing rights.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible across both current and capital account transactions in Luxembourg. EU Treaty Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EMU legal framework guarantee complete freedom of capital movements, with no residual exchange-control restrictions. Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividend distributions attract a 15% domestic withholding tax unless reduced under the Luxembourg participation exemption, a bilateral tax treaty, or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Cross-border royalty payments are not subject to Luxembourg withholding tax, which materially facilitates treasury flows for an independent film licensing SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Luxembourg. Export and royalty receipts may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts without BCL licensing. The only macro-relevant obligation is statistical reporting to the BCL under the balance-of-payments framework, requiring the settlement data and classification codes for transactions above specified de minimis thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as national central bank within the Eurosystem, jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and AML supervision, and the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro monetary policy and payments oversight.

They are not viable for capital escrow, standby letters of credit, documentary collections, or BCL statistical reporting and cannot replace a Luxembourg-authorised credit institution for high-value IP transactions. Any EMI must be restricted to sub-posting accounts with a twice-monthly sweep to the tier-1 bank to maintain audit integrity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax (IRC) on taxable income above EUR 175,000, plus a 7% employment-fund surcharge, giving a headline national rate of 18.19%; municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) adds approximately 6.75% in Luxembourg City, producing a blended statutory burden of about 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory Luxembourg dividend withholding tax is 15% on gross distributions to shareholders, including de facto profit-sharing payments and similar income; the rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive shareholders holding at least 10% of the SARL share capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period; under applicable double tax treaties, rates may further be reduced to 0%, 5%, or 10% depending on the shareholding and LOB provisions.

The return is due by 30 June of the year following the fiscal year-end; a three-month automatic extension is available to 30 September, and further administrative extensions are granted when the return is prepared by a licensed tax adviser (commonly to 31 December).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Luxembourg Local File is mandatory when the resident entity's cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 500,000 for goods or tangible assets, or EUR 150,000 for services, financing, royalties, and intangibles; a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover reaches EUR 750 million; the documentation must be contemporaneous and filed within 30 days of the tax authority's request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations statute like UAE/offshore regimes, but the ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 6 LIR), BEPS treaty substance requirements, and the IP box's 'business activity' condition all require a real economic presence; a film-licensing SARL must demonstrate local staff with decision-making authority, an own office, board minutes approving licensing terms, active monitoring of licensees, and a substantive royalty account; otherwise the IP box, treaty benefits, and deduction for intercompany royalties will be denied under the principle that the conduit entity lacks economic reality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment of the hidden profit, late-interest charge of 7.2% per annum, administrative penalties of 10% to 50% of the evaded tax, and criminal conviction punishable by fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; the court may also order restitution of fiscal losses.

Director personal liability: Directors that participate in or knowingly approve evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, subject to civil restitution, personal asset seizure, travel bans restricting movement from EU territory, and imprisonment where criminal fraud is proven.
Shell Company / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits, withdrawal of the IP box exemption, recharacterization of accumulated royalties as constructive dividends subject to 15% withholding tax, penal reassessment, and possible fine under the General Tax Law; under ATAD, the arrangement may be disregarded and taxed as in the substantive owner's hands.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the SARL is used as a pure sham; directors become jointly and severally liable for the full tax deficiency, repatriation of illicitly shifted profits, personal travel restrictions pending criminal investigation, and imprisonment for complicity in tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may upwardly adjust the Luxembourg taxable profit to an arm's-length margin, deny the deduction of excess royalty payments, and impose late-payment interest; administrative penalties for non-production of documentation can reach up to 10% of the resulting adjustment, and if negligence is evidenced, an additional fine of up to EUR 100,000 may apply.

Director personal liability: Chief officers who signed or caused the flawed transfer-pricing policy to be adopted are jointly liable for the resulting recovery, may face administrative fines, be disqualified as directors for up to 5 years, and in cases of deliberate mispricing be subject to criminal sanctions including travel bans and imprisonment.
AML / Economic Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Luxembourg AML Law (Law of 12 November 2004) of up to EUR 5 million; the SARL's corporate registration may be struck from the RCS, its bank accounts frozen, and the tax administration may impose an additional 15% gross-up on non-compliant payments; asset freezes apply in economic-sanctions cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for money-laundering and sanctions breaches, including criminal fines, seizure of personal assets, travel bans (EDTFA measures), and imprisonment up to 5 years; the corporate veil may be pierced under the AML law to reach the controlling persons, and professional sector bans are commonly imposed.

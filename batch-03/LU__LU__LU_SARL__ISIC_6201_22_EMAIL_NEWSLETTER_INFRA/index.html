Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000; the entire capital must be subscribed and at least 25% of each cash share's nominal value (min. EUR 3,000) must be paid up before incorporation; contributions in kind must be fully paid; the paid-up cash must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation, and the bank capital-deposit attestation is delivered to the notary.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office lease must be compliant with communal fire safety and building codes; a fire safety certificate from the municipal fire brigade or an approved inspection office is required for any premises open to employees or visitors; an occupational clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) is obtained for office workplaces, including first-aid and evacuation plan documentation.

Paid-in capital rule: SARL minimum share capital is EUR 12,000; the entire capital must be subscribed and at least 25% of each cash share's nominal value (min. EUR 3,000) must be paid up before incorporation; contributions in kind must be fully paid; the paid-up cash must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation, and the bank capital-deposit attestation is delivered to the notary.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), Luxembourg (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu). Indirect taxes: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), VAT division (portal: aedt.public.lu). Integrated business and tax registration portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.public.lu). Corporate legal registration: Luxembourg Business Registers / Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); registration with the RCS auto-triggers statutory tax registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign shareholder establishing a Luxembourg SARL: (1) execute and notarize the articles of association; (2) file the incorporation with the RCS (via Luxembourg Business Registers) and publish in RESA; (3) the ACD automatically issues the national tax number (TIN/N° d'identification) and communal number; (4) register d'office for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax (IFS); (5) apply separately to the AEDT for VAT identification using the VAT registration form (Form 570) through MyGuichet or the AEDT; non-EU-established non-resident companies must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative; an EU-established non-resident company may register directly without a representative; a non-resident company with no Luxembourg permanent establishment and no VATable activity in Luxembourg is not required to obtain a Luxembourg direct-tax TIN but may be required to VAT-register for specific supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign shareholder establishing a Luxembourg SARL: (1) execute and notarize the articles of association; (2) file the incorporation with the RCS (via Luxembourg Business Registers) and publish in RESA; (3) the ACD automatically issues the national tax number (TIN/N° d'identification) and communal number; (4) register d'office for corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax (IFS); (5) apply separately to the AEDT for VAT identification using the VAT registration form (Form 570) through MyGuichet or the AEDT; non-EU-established non-resident companies must appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative; an EU-established non-resident company may register directly without a representative; a non-resident company with no Luxembourg permanent establishment and no VATable activity in Luxembourg is not required to obtain a Luxembourg direct-tax TIN but may be required to VAT-register for specific supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The SARL is an opaque, entity-level taxpayer subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) Luxembourg tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under LIR art. 164bis for a 95%+ Luxembourg parent holding resident subsidiaries; (ii) VAT grouping election under Loi TVA art. 60 allowing multiple legal persons to be treated as one taxable person; (iii) election to apply the participation exemption regime under LIR art. 166 if the shareholder meets the 10% participation, holding-period and subject-to-tax conditions; (iv) specific debt/equity hybrid instrument elections under LIR art. 168. Luxembourg's hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD leave no elective mismatch treatment; adjustments are mandatory to the extent the mismatch persists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic Luxembourg 'small enterprise' VAT exemption: EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover (Loi TVA art. 57), but this exemption does not apply to foreign-established suppliers. Cross-border email API and newsletter infrastructure supplied to Luxembourg B2B customers is normally subject to reverse charge with no Luxembourg VAT due, hence no Luxembourg registration is triggered by that B2B supply alone. Non-established suppliers supplying B2C e-services to Luxembourg consumers have mandatory registration from the first euro (no threshold) unless they use the EU OSS non-Union/Union scheme; intra-EU cross-border distance sales trigger destination-country VAT once the supplier's annual EU distance sales exceed EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods: export outside the EU qualifies for 0% VAT under Loi TVA art. 40(3) when the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and export evidence is obtained within 90 days; intra-Community supplies are exempt under art. 41 if the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID, an EC sales list is submitted, and the consignment movement is evidenced. For email newsletter infrastructure and SaaS services, the place of supply follows the general B2B rule: the supply is taxable at the customer's establishment. Supplies to a non-EU business customer fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope and therefore effectively carry 0% Luxembourg VAT without requiring a formal zero-rating claim. B2C digital services supplied to customers resident outside the EU are also outside Luxembourg VAT scope if the customer's residence/normal place of use is outside the EU; in contrast, B2C e-services to EU consumers are taxable at the consumer's location via the OSS scheme.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD) via the national DECOD system for physical goods; not applicable to pure electronic email infrastructure services
  • Commercial invoice stating the supplier and recipient VAT/TIN numbers, full description of services, pricing, place-of-supply indicators, and the applicable reverse-charge annotation where relevant
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU where any hardware, equipment, or promotional physical media is bundled with the digital service
  • Written service contract, purchase order, and IP address/log analytics or platform campaign delivery reports demonstrating that the recipient is a non-resident and that the email newsletter infrastructure is actually used outside Luxembourg
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/FX settlement documentation showing proceeds repatriation to the Luxembourg entity; retained for VAT audit and transfer pricing purposes

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping is currently enforced in Luxembourg for general B2B supplies. Electronic invoices are accepted provided the authenticity of origin and integrity of content are guaranteed (via EDI, advanced electronic signature, or auditable internal controls). For B2G transactions, e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL network using a structured EN 16931-compliant format such as UBL or PEPPOL BIS 3.0; no Luxembourg-specific digital stamp or prior clearance duty applies. VAT invoices must contain all legal data fields under Loi TVA art. 43; the business remains obliged to preserve structured accounting export files and to produce them during audit.

Luxembourg tax authorities (ACD for direct taxes and AEDT for VAT) may launch documentary, transactional and accounting audits. A zero-rating or export exemption claim that fails legally required evidence is disallowed and the VAT is clawed back together with statutory late interest. Administrative penalties range from a non-fraud adjustment of 5% of the deficiency up to 50% for negligence or reckless understatement; cases of fraudulent export zero-rating claims can trigger recovery of double the evaded tax, fines up to EUR 25,000, loss of business permits, and criminal prosecution of the entity and its directors (including imprisonment under Luxembourg tax-criminal provisions). VAT records, especially export evidence, must be retained for at least 10 years; failure to produce them automatically reverses the zero-rating benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully and freely convertible, with zero restrictions on capital account transactions between Luxembourg and non-EU jurisdictions for a Luxembourg SARL. Luxembourg applies no foreign exchange controls or approval requirements for either current account transactions (e.g., SaaS subscription fees, newsletter infrastructure service charges) or capital account transactions (e.g., equity injections, dividend distributions, intercompany loans). Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to generally applicable corporate law, commercial substance documentation, anti-money laundering (AML) screening, and, where applicable, withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% in most qualifying cases) or Luxembourg domestic law (15% unless treaty relief applies). No currency rationing, repatriation queues, or prior written authorisation from the BCL is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion period, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts arising from cross-border SaaS, email infrastructure, or newsletter hosting services. Export/service proceeds may be retained in EUR or any other freely convertible currency, credited directly to a Luxembourg-resident bank account or a foreign account, with no obligation to sell forex to the central bank. The only requirement is to maintain proper accounting and tax documentation in line with Luxembourg GAAP and transfer pricing rules, and to report suspicious transactions under AML legislation (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL, Luxembourg Central Bank) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem; monetary policy and EUR currency issuance are the exclusive competence of the European Central Bank (ECB) in Frankfurt.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for regulated EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting into Luxembourg under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) or the E-Money Directive, subject to CSSF notification and AML compliance. Fully digital EMIs without a Luxembourg local branch or agent may open remote accounts on a cross-border basis; however, bank-grade settlement rails and corporate transactional needs (e.g., escrow for capital, large USD/EUR clearing) are best served by tier-1 Luxembourg credit institutions or regulated EMIs with strong banking partnerships. Unregulated offshore digital EMIs should be avoided. Luxembourg requires that any EMI holding client funds must safeguard those funds with an authorised credit institution or qualifying insurance, and the account must be denominated in EUR where the service is rendered in Luxembourg. For the target entity (low-risk subscription infrastructure), an EMI account is feasible for receivables collection, but the primary corporate treasury account should remain with a prudentially supervised Luxembourg bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders (e.g., subscription via parent or VC) must be routed through the SWIFT international payment network using a EUR nostro account at the Luxembourg bank. The payment should reference purpose code 'S301' (Direct Investment in an enterprise) or 'SRG' (Investment Capital) under the BOP/CPM classification set by the ECB; the receiving bank must verify that the legal owner of the funds is the transferring shareholder as registered in the RBE to satisfy banking AML expectations. Funds may be sent as a plain SWIFT MT103, and the receiving bank will confirm the account currency (EUR) and apply no capital control pre-approval; nevertheless, it is best practice to notify the bank in advance through a narrative field describing the transaction as 'Allotment of SARL share capital' or 'Shareholder loan for email infrastructure working capital.' For larger injections exceeding bank thresholds, the entity should prepare a certificate from the notary and updated shareholder registry to present to the bank to avoid the blocking of the receipt or a request for further KYC evidence. All inbound amounts must be reconciled in the corporate ledger and must match the registered capital or the loan agreement to ensure no accidental reclassification as taxable revenue.The primary clearing rails for outbound disbursements from Luxembourg for a SaaS/email newsletter infrastructure company are: 1) SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border wire transfers to vendors, contractors, and partner platforms (optimised by issuing the payment in EUR for EU counterparts and in USD/GBP via the same SWIFT MT103 with corresponding-bank routing); 2) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) and regular SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for all EUR-denominated EU payments; 3) commercial letters of credit are rarely standard for SaaS licensing (reserved for high-value infrastructure hardware purchases) but can be issued on request by tier-1 banks under the UCP 600 rules if a vendor requires a documentary LC; and 4) worldwide further clearing may involve usage of virtual IBANs issued by regulated payment institutions for split payments to multiple vendors. The treasury must enforce an approve-pay workflow with dual-approval signatories, IP-address-restricted banking tokens, and a segregated 'escrow-for-disbursement' signing profile within the bank portal.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is permissible in Luxembourg solely for the statutory registered seat (siège statutaire), administrative correspondence, and records filing, provided the address provider is a supervised entity such as a licensed fiduciary, trust company, or registered company service provider. The virtual seat cannot, however, satisfy the real commercial substance requirements applicable to an active manufacturer and distributor of Class IIb/III medical devices. Under Luxembourg tax administration Circular L.I.R. n. 14/4 on profit attribution and substance, a mere letterbox or virtual-office seat, without effective management, staff, premises, and decision-making, creates a rebuttable presumption of non-residence for corporate tax purposes. For the present ISIC 3250 endoscope tower manufacturing and distribution activity, the Luxembourg operational functions — including CE marking vigilance obligations under Regulation (EU) 2017/745, post-market surveillance, quality management system records, import/export documentation, and authorized representative duties — require a physical establishment in the Grand Duchy with dedicated personnel and servers. The virtual office is therefore conditionally permissible for the statutory seat during the pre-incorporation and licensing phase, but a fully equipped physical office must be established before the first commercial invoice is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and withholding taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT, registration duties, stamp duties, and excise tax administration; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for commercial registration and the national business identity number; all filings are supported by the Guichet.lu and e-Space digital portals.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Luxembourg SARL must be incorporated by notarial deed, registered with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés. The RCS registration automatically generates the 13-digit Luxembourg business identification number ('matricule'). For VAT purposes, the entity must then file Form 810 (identification for VAT) with the AED, submitting the articles of association, notarised proof of incorporation, RCS excerpt, Luxembourg registered office evidence, identity documents of all managers and shareholders, certified copy of the corporate bank account statement, and, where applicable, valid audited financial statements or parent company details for group coordination. Non-resident suppliers established outside Luxembourg but making taxable transactions in Luxembourg must register directly with the AED using Form 810-A, appoint a tax representative when prescribed, and receive a LU VAT number under Article 15 of the Simplified VAT Collection Act; registration is mandatory prior to first taxable supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident SARL is a corporate tax subject with full legal personality, taxed on its worldwide income unless treaty or EU law relief applies; non-resident entities are taxable only on Luxembourg-source income and assets. There is no transparent pass-through assessment for an SARL, and the entity is separately subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax, and VAT.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; tax classification follows the statutory commercial legal form. Available elections include: (i) VAT fiscal unity ('assujetti unique') under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for closely linked established taxable persons; (ii) non-Union OSS / Union OSS registration for cross-border B2C services and distance sales; (iii) optional application of the Luxembourg intellectual property box regime under Article 50ter of the Income Tax Law; (iv) an election for the small business VAT exemption to be waived; and (v) elections related to tax consolidation for VAT only. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective; they are mandatorily adjusted under the Luxembourg provisions transposing EU ATAD, with no subjective check-the-box relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg, the registration threshold is 0 EUR; a VAT registration is mandatory before the first taxable transaction, regardless of turnover. For resident small enterprises, Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law grants an exemption where annual turnover, excluding VAT, does not exceed 25,000 EUR; the threshold applies to the preceding calendar year, and the enterprise may elect to register and charge VAT. Distance sales of goods by businesses established in another EU Member State to Luxembourg private consumers become taxable in Luxembourg only when the annual pan-EU distance-sales turnover exceeds the general EU threshold of 10,000 EUR, unless an OSS election is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT export rate applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported to a third country or third-territory destination, provided the goods physically leave the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party instructed by the supplier, within the statutory time frame (generally three months from the supply date, subject to extension on justifiable grounds). The supplier must hold documentary proof of the export, including a customs export declaration accepted by the competent customs office and evidence of the goods' actual exit from the EU. Export-related ancillary supplies, such as freight, packing, insurance, and inspection services directly related to the exported goods, are also zero-rated when included in the same export chain. Services rendered to non-EU customers are generally zero-rated where the customer is a taxable person established outside the EU, or where the effective place of use and enjoyment lies outside the EU for private consumers. Intra-EU supplies of medical devices to EU taxable persons are not 'exports' but are treated as zero-rated intra-Community supplies contingent on the customer's valid UK? — in Luxembourg context, a valid EU VAT identification number and submission of an EC sales list.

Luxembourg does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or pre-clearance API for B2B invoices, and there is no digital stamping requirement. Taxpayers must submit periodic VAT returns, recapitulative statements, Intrastat declarations, and SAF-T/TLOG files through the AED's e-Space portal. All invoices issued for taxable supplies must comply with the VAT invoice content requirements under Articles 106-109 of the Luxembourg VAT Law. Traders issuing cash receipts for retail activities must use approved, tax-monitored electronic cash registers (eCDF/TLOG-compliant devices) that maintain a verifiable audit trail. For public-sector contracting, suppliers must transmit electronic invoices in the European standard EN 16931 through the Luxembourg BISON/PEPPOL interoperability network; B2B clearance is governed by EU ViDA transitional rules and is not yet active.

Luxembourg's AED applies strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; if the export evidence is absent or defective within the statutory retention period, the VAT initially zero-rated is clawed back at 17% with statutory late-payment interest and late-filing surcharges. Administrative penalties may reach 100% of the tax evaded where the exporter knowingly issued a false export declaration, presented counterfeit transport documents, or artificially arranged an export transaction without an actual physical removal of goods. Criminal sanctions for fraud include fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment for up to five years for responsible officers, with possible disqualification of directors. Retrospective assessments are permitted for five years normally, extending to ten years in the presence of fraud or wilful concealment. The managing directors, fiscal representative, and, in limited cases, the general commercial agent are jointly and severally liable for the unrecovered VAT, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted Euro convertibility as a Eurozone member state. Luxembourg applies no exchange controls to either current or capital account transactions, consistent with Articles 63–66 TFEU on the free movement of capital. Profit remittance is freely executable via TARGET2/SEPA without FX licensing, but a standard statutory 15% Luxembourg withholding tax applies to dividends unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders). The practical remittance hurdle is purely documentary: banks require evidence of dividend approval, UBO disclosure, and corporate capacity, not FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory repatriation deadline and no mandatory EUR conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receivables in domiciled or foreign currency accounts indefinitely. Only CSSF AML/CFT reporting thresholds apply to large cross-border movements; medtech exporters invoicing in USD or CNY therefore enjoy complete treasury flexibility.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem under the ultimate monetary authority of the European Central Bank (ECB). Luxembourg maintains no separate national foreign exchange administration; exchange control and monetary policy competences are pooled at the ECB level, with the BCL executing operations and supervising Luxembourg-licensed credit institutions within the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payment flows, Low for statutory capital escrow. EU-licensed cross-border electronic money institutions may passport payment services into Luxembourg, but they cannot hold the SARL's registered capital in a blocked deposit account because deposit-taking and escrow of share capital are reserved to credit institutions under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended. Non-EU offshore EMIs without CSSF/EU authorization are not viable for corporate treasury. Practical recommendation: maintain the primary and escrow accounts at a Tier-1 bank and use fintech rails only for auxiliary disbursements, supplier payouts, or card programs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational payment flows, Low for statutory capital escrow. EU-licensed cross-border electronic money institutions may passport payment services into Luxembourg, but they cannot hold the SARL's registered capital in a blocked deposit account because deposit-taking and escrow of share capital are reserved to credit institutions under the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended. Non-EU offshore EMIs without CSSF/EU authorization are not viable for corporate treasury. Practical recommendation: maintain the primary and escrow accounts at a Tier-1 bank and use fintech rails only for auxiliary disbursements, supplier payouts, or card programs.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate and individual income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; single public digital portal: MyGuichet.lu (Guichet.public.lu) and LBR (Luxembourg Business Registers) for RCS company filing.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which issues a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) serving as the central company/tax identifier. The SARL must then file for corporate tax registration with ACD and, if taxable supplies are made, for VAT registration with AEDT. Non-resident companies acquiring a Luxembourg VAT number receive a 'LU + 8 digits' identifier. All filings, including online VAT registration, are submitted through MyGuichet.lu with authenticated electronic signature or a Luxembourg-registered authorised representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which issues a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) serving as the central company/tax identifier. The SARL must then file for corporate tax registration with ACD and, if taxable supplies are made, for VAT registration with AEDT. Non-resident companies acquiring a Luxembourg VAT number receive a 'LU + 8 digits' identifier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include group taxation (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for wholly-owned resident subsidiaries (95% or more shareholding) allowing profit/loss offset across the group; VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) for closely connected resident persons; and ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch adjustments requiring documentation/election to avoid double non-taxation. No separate fiscal transparency election exists for SARLs; they are always corporate taxpayers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for locally established small enterprises; non-established foreign suppliers have no general registration threshold and must register if they make taxable supplies in Luxembourg without reverse charge protection; distance sales and electronically supplied services have separate EU-wide EUR 10,000 P&O threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported to a destination outside the EU are eligible for 0% VAT under Article 37 of the Luxembourg VAT law, provided the exporter holds documentary proof of removal from the EU. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered counterparty are zero-rated with a valid EU VAT number and recapitulative statement. Services supplied to a business customer established outside the EU are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (equivalent to 0% under B2B place-of-supply rules) when the effective use and enjoyment is not in Luxembourg. Algorithmic high-frequency trading services to non-EU counterparties must be classified as taxable B2B services and may benefit from this offshoring/export treatment if the customer is a non-EU taxable person and no Luxembourg fixed establishment is involved.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Payment / Bank Transfer Instruction

No general real-time B2B fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital clearance mandate is currently in force in Luxembourg. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via PEPPOL-compliant formats (UBL/Factur-X). B2B electronic invoicing is permitted if the recipient accepts it; invoices must be retained in original form for 10 years for VAT audit purposes.

Luxembourg tax authorities may reopen VAT files for 5 years (10 years in proven fraud). Improper or unsupported zero-rating claims are reversed retroactively, with monthly interest and penalties up to 50% of the tax evaded; serious VAT fraud can trigger criminal fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment. Missing Trader Intra-Community (MTIC) fraud and abusive interposition of HFT trading entities may lead to joint and several liability, loss of VAT deduction rights, and clawback of input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a full-member eurozone jurisdiction with unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under EU law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63–66) and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital return, and trade settlement may be executed without prior governmental approval. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, or restrictions on non-resident ownership; the only practical hurdles are corporate law solvency tests and AML/KYC documentary obligations imposed by credit institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds. Luxembourg does not impose export receipt surrender requirements. All foreign currency earnings may be retained, freely converted, or applied offshore provided the underlying transaction is economically justified and properly documented for AML, CRS, and CB(e) CbCR or statistical reporting purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting in conjunction with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for financial sector supervision and the Ministry of Finance for exchange control statistics and compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-to-Moderate for primary treasury operations; Moderate as a secondary collection/payment rail. Cross-border electronic money institutions (EMIs) lawfully passporting into Luxembourg under the PSD2 framework may open client money accounts and process payment transactions, but they do not provide credit, direct TARGET2 access, or custodial clearing capacity. A proprietary ultra-low-latency market-making SARL requires direct correspondent banking relationships with Tier-1 institutions. CSSF imposes authorization, safeguarding, and consolidated supervision constraints; any FinTech EMI arrangement must be limited to float segregation, never as the sole clearing bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (combined Luxembourg City headline rate for a resident SARL: 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT, and 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Luxembourg SARL; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the share capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period, and to 0% or 5% under many Luxembourg tax treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is 31 May following the tax year for paper filing; electronic filing via MyGuichet.lu is administratively extended to 31 December of the same calendar year. For a non-calendar accounting year, the filing deadline is the end of the relevant statutory extension period computed from the accounting period close.

Late filing triggers an administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 depending on delay and repeated non-compliance; the tax office may assess tax ex officio. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers Master File, CbC Report and mandatory TP documentation under EU Directive 2016/881. For smaller groups, the Luxembourg tax administration may demand a comprehensive Transfer Pricing Report if the total value of controlled intra-group transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000 in the relevant fiscal year, or if specific high-risk transactions are identified.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the SARL must show real presence and decision-making in Luxembourg. A shell HFT company with no local premises, personnel, or substance may be challenged under anti-abuse doctrine, ATAD, the OECD Principal Purpose Test, and the Luxembourg abuse doctrine; such a challenge can lead to denial of treaty benefits and participation exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all evaded tax plus a fraudulent-return penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax; criminal fines and extended 10-year limitation period for tax fraud; judicial seizure of bank accounts and HFT clearing assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally liable for the unpaid tax and civil damages; the corporate veil is pierced; travel bans and arrest warrants may issue; imprisonment up to five years is possible.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, denial of the participation exemption, reclassification of financing income as taxable dividends, disallowance of intra-group deduction, and retroactive tax assessments with late interest.

Director personal liability: Where the entity is treated as a sham, directors may be personally assessed for tax liabilities and ordered to make civil restitution; personal travel bans and asset freezes are available in cross-border investigations.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; withdrawal or suspension of any required licence; confiscation of proceeds in criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or tolerate laundering are personally subject to criminal sanctions, disqualification from managing commercial companies, travel bans, and imprisonment up to five years; limited liability is unavailable for intentional misconduct.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment, add-back of excess deductions, reassessment of CIT and municipal business tax, interest surcharge, and a mandatory penalty where documentation is absent or knowingly incorrect.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or approve sham transfer pricing contracts can be held personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall if they acted in bad faith; they may face civil damages, travel bans, and extradition risk in treaty investigations.

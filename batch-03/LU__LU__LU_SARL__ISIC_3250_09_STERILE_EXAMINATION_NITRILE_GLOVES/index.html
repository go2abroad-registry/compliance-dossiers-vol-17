Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended by the Law of 10 August 2016; Articles 100-1 to 100-23 (SARL), Article 100-2 (minimum capital), and Article 110-1 (registered office).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised deed of incorporation (acte constitutif), RCS e-registration form, and RESA (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations) publication.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation; the capital must be deposited in a blocked account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution and released by the notary only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS); structural-load inspection certificate issued by an accredited Luxembourg bureau de contrôle; occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering risk assessment, dipping-line guarding, ventilation, chemical exposure limits, and personal protective equipment protocols.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital for a SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation; the capital must be deposited in a blocked account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution and released by the notary only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for indirect taxation; consolidated e-portal: MyGuichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / RCS)

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a Luxembourg Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) must execute a notarised deed of incorporation and submit it to the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés). Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically attributes a 13-digit matricule (national tax identification number). For VAT purposes, the SARL must file a VAT registration form (Formulaire 1A) with the AEDT and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). The SARL must also register for social security as an employer if it hires staff. The entire procedure can be initiated online via MyGuichet.lu or through a notary.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign investor establishing a Luxembourg Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) must execute a notarised deed of incorporation and submit it to the RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés). Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically attributes a 13-digit matricule (national tax identification number). The SARL must also register for social security as an employer if it hires staff. The entire procedure can be initiated online via MyGuichet.lu or through a notary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. However, a SARL may elect for VAT grouping (assujetti unique) with eligible related entities, may elect for fiscal integration (tax consolidation regime) of resident companies subject to 95% ownership and holding-period requirements, may opt into the EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules and may elect to apply the Luxembourg patent box (IP tax regime) for qualifying intellectual property. Additionally, certain optional provisions exist for notional interest deduction taking, VAT recovery for holding companies, and intra-group financing arrangements under accepted transfer pricing documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual turnover exemption threshold for small businesses; above this threshold, VAT registration is generally mandatory. A foreign-established SARL with any taxable activity in Luxembourg must register before the first taxable transaction unless the small business turnover exemption is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union are eligible for VAT zero-rating under the Luxembourg VAT law, provided the exporter retains the required evidence of export. Intra-EU dispatches of goods to a taxable person registered for VAT in another EU Member State are likewise zero-rated. Sterile examination nitrile gloves sold for export therefore qualify provided they are physically shipped out of the Union/intra-EU in accordance with commercial documentation and the buyer's valid VAT registration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / Export made via ICS/DUA)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms and export VAT zero-rating reference
  • Packing List
  • Invoice addressed to non-EU customer or EU customer with valid VAT ID
  • Intra-EU Sales List declaration and Recapitulative statement (for intra-EU dispatches)
  • Proof of transport and receipt by customer (signed CMR, customs stamp, or postal/courier receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming payment receipt

Luxembourg does not currently impose mandatory B2B e-invoicing or a real-time fiscal clearance stamping regime. Electronic invoicing is permitted subject to buyer consent and authenticity guarantees (EDI or PEPPOL formats accepted). For B2G transactions, e-invoicing via PEPPOL is mandated under European Directive 2014/55/EU. Standard audit requirements include 10-year invoice retention and submission to AEDT in case of investigation.

The AEDT conducts risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims. If a taxpayer fails to produce sufficient documentary evidence of export within the prescribed deadline, the AEDT may reassess VAT at the standard rate of 17% plus default interest and administrative penalties. Fraudulent claims or deliberately falsified export documentation are subject to criminal fines up to EUR 10,000 or imprisonment under Luxembourg tax law; additional tax penalties of up to 10% of the tax evaded may apply. Retroactive clawback is limited to the statutory assessment period of ten years for VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Article 63 TFEU) and the Luxembourg Monetary Law of 1998. There are no exchange controls, surrender obligations, or prior-authorization licenses for profit remittance. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds after the applicable Luxembourg withholding tax (15% domestic; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a Luxembourg tax treaty for qualifying corporate shareholders), subject only to standard bank-level KYC and BCL statistical reporting thresholds. Any restrictions derive solely from EU/UN sanctions or anti-money-laundering legal requirements, not from currency-account rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and does not require mandatory conversion into euros. Exporters are free to hold export receipts in EUR or foreign currency accounts at licensed credit institutions or in overseas accounts provided the accounts are declared to the Luxembourg tax administration and the balance-of-payments statistics obligations (BCL/CSSF Circular 2011) are satisfied.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) – monetary authority with sole competence for monetary statistics and balance-of-payments reporting; prudential banking supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Ministry of Finance umbrella. There is no separate foreign-exchange-control administration in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Payoneer Fit: High

Medium – Luxembourg-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Payoneer, Currencycloud, or local EMIs under PSD2) can provide euro-denominated e-money wallets, payment initiation, and cross-border settlement services for operational liquidity. However, EMIs are not licensed to take deposits or grant credit and are not appropriate for capital-escrow, large trade finance, commercial LC advising/confirming, or complex treasury hedging. Non-EU/offshore digital EMIs without a Luxembourg passport are not viable as principal banking rails due to the CSSF's requirement that payment services for Luxembourg-domiciled entities be rendered through EU-licensed or passporting institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for inbound shareholder capital: (i) transfer is sent via SWIFT MT103 from the parent company's corporate account, clearly referencing the beneficiary SARL's name, IBAN, and BIC in Luxembourg; (ii) the payment purpose must be notified to the receiving bank as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' using the BCL balance-of-payments harmonised purpose code D.1.1 (capital participation); (iii) the subscription deed, board resolution, and any shareholders' agreement must be pre-submitted to the Luxembourg bank's KYC unit; (iv) funds are credited to a capital-escrow or temporary blocked account, then released to the operational account upon the CSSF AML release and BCL statistical reporting (monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments reporting for transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500); (v) if the contribution takes the form of a shareholder loan, the MT103 narrative must reference loan agreement and purpose code D.1.2/D.1.3, with the funds subject to the same verification.Primary clearing rails: SEPA/SEPA Instant for EUR payments under EUR 100,000; SWIFT MT103 for TT wires in EUR, USD, or EUR-cross-border denominations; and commercial documentary Letters of Credit (LC) with SWIFT MT700/MT701 issuance, MT710/MT720 negotiation, and MT103 reimbursement for settlement of raw-material purchases of nitrile latex, packaging, and sterilization services. Luxembourg commercial banks provide LC advicing/confirmation through their international correspondent networks. Where the counterparty requires guaranteed settlement, a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) on the Swift Trade Services Utility may be used as a complementary rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File required if group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies substance requirements under domestic law and EU anti-abuse rules; for manufacturing entities, substance is evidenced by physical presence, employees, and operational activities; ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) as per OECD standards are not directly applicable but must be demonstrated to avoid treaty abuse

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional tax penalties up to 100% of evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, retroactive tax assessments, and penalties up to 50% of additional tax

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes due if they fail to ensure substance; potential criminal charges for fraud
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; revocation of business license

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 50% of additional tax, and interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; civil restitution and potential criminal charges

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Since the Law of 10 August 2016, a SARL has no statutory minimum capital; the Articles freely fix the capital (here EUR 12,000 divided into 120 shares of EUR 100). At incorporation, at least 10% of cash contributions must be paid up, with a minimum paid-in amount of EUR 1; in-kind contributions must be fully paid in on formation. The paid-up funds must be deposited into a blocked account of the company-in-formation at a Luxembourg credit institution, evidenced by a bank certificate produced to the notary, and are released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the registered office/operational premises must be obtained from the local commune and the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) in conformity with the Law of 17 June 1994 on fire and rescue services, as amended; structural load inspection and building fit-out authorization (autorisation de bâtir) are required from the local commune prior to occupancy; occupational health and safety clearance of the premises and any electrical installations must be verified by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including compliance with the Labor Code, first-aid equipment requirements, and workstation ergonomics for operational staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD – Luxembourg Direct Tax Administration) for income and corporate taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED – Registration, Estates and VAT Authority) for indirect taxes; unified public portal: guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

New Luxembourg SARLs obtain a national tax identification number automatically upon notarial incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). The VAT identification number is applied for through AED Form 640 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA), submitted personally or by counsel. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment that becomes VAT-liable must appoint a VAT representative approved by the Minister of Finance. Non-resident entities deriving Luxembourg-source income obtain a direct tax number via Form 782 submitted to the ACD.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — New Luxembourg SARLs obtain a national tax identification number automatically upon notarial incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS). The VAT identification number is applied for through AED Form 640 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA), submitted personally or by counsel. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment that becomes VAT-liable must appoint a VAT representative approved by the Minister of Finance. Non-resident entities deriving Luxembourg-source income obtain a direct tax number via Form 782 submitted to the ACD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists; a Luxembourg SARL is mandatorily tax-resident and subject to entity-level corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available elections: VAT grouping for qualifying affiliated entities (Art. 60 VAT Law), optional fiscal consolidation for groups satisfying the 95% ownership threshold (Art. 164bis ITL), and advanced tax ruling for intra-group financing or IP-box structures. Hybrid mismatch and ATAD rules apply mandatorily; no separate hybrid entity election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

35,000 EUR annual turnover (excl. VAT) for small-business exemption; no higher mandatory registration threshold — any taxable person making taxable supplies must register unless the exemption is validly claimed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 55 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the exporter retains evidence of departure within a statutory period. International services—including certain electronically supplied services—are treated as outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT where the place-of-supply rules transfer taxation to the recipient's jurisdiction (B2B reverse charge or B2C OSS mechanism). For B2C digital services to EU consumers, Luxembourg VAT is not chargeable when the OSS regime applies; to non-EU consumers, the supply is entirely outside Luxembourg taxing jurisdiction.

  • Customs export declaration (single administrative document – SAD/DAU) duly lodged with Luxembourg customs
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export declaration matching description of goods
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent banking proof of payment in respect of the export transaction

No real-time fiscal electronic clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg as of the current date. Electronic invoicing is acceptable on a voluntary basis and must comply with the European standard EN 16931. Luxembourg is preparing to implement the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, which will eventually introduce e-invoicing and digital reporting requirements, but no concrete mandate has been enacted.

The tax administration will disallow zero-rating if documentary evidence is incomplete or contradictory. Reassessed output VAT accrues default interest at 0.8% per month and the assessment may be increased by up to 100% in cases of gross negligence. Willful fraud or materially false statements are criminally punishable with fines from EUR 250 up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years. Refunds improperly obtained are clawed back together with a 20% statutory penalty in accordance with Article 4(2) of the VAT enforcement law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. There are no exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions, as Luxembourg fully transposes the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. Profit remittances and dividends can be freely distributed, subject only to standard corporate law solvency tests and documentation requirements. For a Luxembourg SARL, a mandatory annual audit threshold may apply, but no ministerial authorization is needed for outbound remittances. The bank will require proper proof of beneficial ownership and economic substance to distinguish legitimate cross-border treasury flows from tax evasion or money laundering.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any statutory requirement to surrender or convert export proceeds into euro. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation and no minimum conversion ratio. Invoicing in foreign currency is permitted. However, for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, Luxembourg banks are required to report individual cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Banque centrale du Luxembourg; the SARL must provide a plausible business purpose for any large incoming or outgoing transfer.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and AML/CTF supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-high viability only for EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg under PSD2. A Luxembourg SARL may hold funds at a licensed EMI (e.g., an e-money institution from another EEA member state) as a digital wallet, but the primary corporate settlement account must be at a Luxembourg credit institution or a branch of an EEA credit institution to ensure regime-consistent insolvency protection and access to Target2. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU e-money licence are not viable; they cannot provide payment services to a Luxembourg entity on a cross-border basis and would expose the SARL to regulatory, tax and reputational risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound equity contributions and shareholder loans must be executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the investor's bank to the SARL's blocked capital account at the Luxembourg bank. The payment reference must clearly state 'Share capital contribution - [SARL name]' or 'Shareholder loan - [SARL name]' to satisfy bank KYC and BCL balance-of-payments statistics. A notarised subscription certificate, a board resolution approving the issuance, and a declaration of the UBO must be filed with the bank before the transfer settles. For loans, a written loan agreement with published arm's-length interest rate and repayment schedule is mandatory; the bank will verify the lending institution's licence and source of funds. The Luxembourg Register of Commerce requires beneficial ownership filings within one month of any change.For prize-money payouts to contestants in the European Union, use SEPA Credit Transfer (XML ISO 20022) with the SARL's international bank account; for payouts in USD or other non-EUR currencies to the United States, Asia or other third states, use SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) with full beneficiary details and a purpose description such as 'Tournament prize under platform T&Cs'. For high-value platform development contracts or event supply agreements, an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit opened by the Luxembourg bank via SWIFT MT700 is the preferred clearing rail, with payment against presentation of commercial invoices, customs/Sanctions screening, and proof of service delivery. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file must be prepared when the aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 700,000 in a fiscal year. Master file must be prepared when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million. Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to offshore regimes; however, substance is enforced through the Luxembourg general anti-abuse rule (GAAR, Article 6 AATL), EU ATAD, beneficial ownership tests, and ACD administrative circulars. A Luxembourg SARL must demonstrate employees, premises, autonomy, economic ownership of its esports platform assets, and effective decision-making. Lack of substance can lead to denial of treaty relief, WHT exemptions, and tax rulings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Luxembourg General Tax Law Art. 42: fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from one to five years; additional administrative penalty of up to 200% of evaded tax plus 0.6% monthly late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly signed false returns are joint and several liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; criminal conviction may trigger a travel ban, personal attachment, and confiscation of assets.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and WHT exemptions; retroactive 15% dividend withholding tax; GAAR recharacterisation with a surcharge of up to 25% of the tax advantage; deregistration or forced liquidation of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the artificial arrangement may be held personally liable for the uncollected withholding taxes and penalties; the Luxembourg courts may impose a commercial activity ban for up to ten years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Defaults

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income; monetary penalty up to EUR 100,000 for missing documentation; additional penalty of 10% to 50% of the primary adjustment; 0.6% monthly default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved inaccurate intercompany agreements or failed to respond to documentation requests can be exposed to personal fines and civil damages in a subsequent liability claim by the tax authority.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: AML Law of 12 November 2004: administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000; criminal money-laundering sanctions of imprisonment up to five years and fine up to EUR 1,250,000; freezing and confiscation of funds.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors may incur criminal liability, personal fines, imprisonment, and prohibition from serving as managing directors for up to ten years.
Dividend WHT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid 15% withholding tax from the company; late payment interest at 0.6% per month; a 10% surcharge; criminal penalties if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the distribution without proof of exemption are jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld tax, regardless of whether the company retains assets; personal bank accounts may be garnished.

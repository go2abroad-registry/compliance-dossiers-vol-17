Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) de Luxembourg, operating under the authority of the Ministry of Justice; electronic filings via the LBR and Guichet.lu portals.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Title VII, Articles 710-1 to 710-26 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and Business Accounting (RCS Law); Law of 2 September 2011 on Access to the Professions of Craftsman, Trader, and Industrialist.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (acte constitutif) embodying the Articles of Association (statuts), executed before a Luxembourg notary, followed by mandatory RCS registration and publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 3 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Standard SARL minimum share capital: EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation; alternatively, under Article 710-17 of the 1915 Law, capital may be set below EUR 12,000 but not below EUR 1, cash contributions only, subject to statutory reserve and profit-distribution restrictions for the first five years. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a credit institution in Luxembourg or another EU/EEA state and the bank's blocking certificate delivered to the notary before execution of the deed; in-kind contributions require a valuation report by an approved réviseur d'entreprises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (portal: aedt.public.lu); unified state e-services portal: guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate SARL before a notary and file with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); the RCS company number serves as the national identification number for the legal entity. Step 2: ACD automatically opens the corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and municipal business tax file and issues the e-file access (eCDF). Step 3: receive the AEDT VAT file and Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) by filing the VAT registration form (formulaire TVA) via the guichet.public.lu e-space before the first taxable transaction. Non-resident foreign entities establishing a branch follow the same RCS then VAT registration sequence; there is no de minimis threshold for non-residents unless they use the EU OSS or reverse charge mechanism. Standard processing time is 2-4 weeks after submission of the certified statutes, certificate of residence of managers, and power of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the resident SARL is independently taxable on worldwide income with tax transparency disallowed; profits are taxed at the corporate level and distributions are subject to a 15% withholding tax (reduced to 0% under the participation exemption); branch-level territorial sourcing applies only to non-resident taxpayers. The entity is outside any tax-exempt zone regime.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory mechanisms include: (i) fiscal unity/group taxation election for corporate income tax and municipal business tax effective from tax year 2025 (95% ownership threshold), (ii) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, (iii) automatic application of the participation exemption (no election required if the 10% holding / EUR 1.2 million cost and 12-month holding conditions are met), and (iv) mandatory, non-elective hybrid mismatch adjustments under the ATAD implementation law of 21 December 2018.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover (excl. VAT) for the resident small-business exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold for B2C e-services/distance sales below which Luxembourg VAT applies locally and above which the OSS returns are used; no registration threshold whatsoever for non-resident suppliers of taxable goods or services in Luxembourg outside OSS/reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate applies to (i) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number and (ii) exports of goods outside the EU evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration. For the target activity (simulated social casino and virtual slots software distributed via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing), supplies are electronically supplied services: B2B supplies are reverse-charged to the business customer and B2C supplies are declared in the customer's EU member state via OSS; consequently no Luxembourg output VAT is triggered and no export zero-rating claim is needed unless software is exported on physical media, in which case the export documentation bundle below applies. Input VAT on domestic costs is creditable or refundable by the AEDT within the ordinary rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A) with electronic acceptance reference IE-529 evidencing dispatch outside the EU
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note bearing the consignee's signature as transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment statement showing receipt of export consideration within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order/contract, and customer proof of EU-VAT-ID where intra-Community supply is claimed

Luxembourg imposes no general real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory continuous transaction control (CTC) API clearance, and no digital stamping for B2B/B2C invoices. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL is phased in: central state entities from 1 January 2024 and all public contracting authorities from 1 January 2025. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary and consensual (PDF, EDI, PEPPOL or XML accepted). VAT returns and the EuC3 / recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the eCDF portal; OSS returns are filed quarterly on the national OSS portal.

The AEDT may reassess zero-rated exports within the standard 5-year limitation period, extended to 10 years in case of fraud or omission. Unsubstantiated export zero-rating is clawed back at 17% VAT with late-payment interest accruing at 0.6% per month; administrative fines are imposed under Article 671 et seq. of the Luxembourg General Tax Code (A.O.), and intentional or reckless claims constitute VAT fraud punishable by imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 25,000 (or twice the evaded tax). OSS misdeclarations trigger analogous reassessment through the OSS portal with automatic information exchange to the member state of consumption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current and capital accounts. As a Eurozone member applying TFEU Article 63, Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls, no pre-authorisation for capital movements, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty or capital repatriation. EUR is the domestic legal tender, but foreign-currency accounts are freely admitted for corporate treasuries. Remittance frictions are fiscal rather than monetary: 15% dividend withholding tax (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when a 10% participation is held for at least 12 months; reduced treaty rates apply) and 10% withholding on certain royalty outflows unless treaty-relieved. BCL statistical reporting applies to cross-border transactions above EUR 12,500 per item or EUR 50,000 per counterparty per year, submitted monthly via the BP6 web-portal; no central-bank approval is ever required to open a bank account or to move gaming-software revenues into Luxembourg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligations, mandatory conversion ratios or holding-period restrictions apply in Luxembourg. Receipts from Apple/Google storefronts, sub-publishers and international licensees may be retained indefinitely in USD or any convertible currency without repatriation schedules. Statutory duties are limited to: (i) BCL BP6 monthly balance-of-payments declarations for cross-border credits/debits above the EUR 12,500/50,000 thresholds, capturing ISIC 5820 software royalties and in-app-purchase settlements; (ii) CSSF AML/CFT suspicious-transaction reporting; (iii) standard banking provenance checks requiring the company to hold the underlying merchant settlement statements and export invoices on file. There is no surrendering institution, and no conversion into EUR is mandated for any trade flow.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), as the Eurosystem national central bank, holds monetary policy execution, foreign exchange reserves and balance-of-payments statistics. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, e-money institutions and enforces AML/CFT under the Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing and CSSF Regulation 12-02. Significant banks fall under the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism. Payment services are regulated under the Law of 10 November 2009 transposing PSD2; e-money issuers are regulated under the Law of 20 December 2019. The Ministry of Finance has no direct casino-gaming licensing competence because the SARL's activity is non-gambling software publishing (ISIC 5820) outside the scope of the Law of 19 December 2022 on betting and gaming.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Adyen Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a complementary multi-currency payment overlay; Low as the sole treasury-banking solution. Luxembourg-regulated EMIs, notably MANGOPAY S.A. and PayTech Luxembourg S.A., plus EU-passported institutions such as Adyen N.V., can lawfully open payment accounts for the SARL, collect App-Store flows and automate virtual-card payouts to sub-licensees. Legal constraints under PSD2/EMD2 include: no credit intermediation or overdraft, no proprietary FX derivative execution, mandatory segregation of client funds into a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution, and prohibition on issuing capital-escrow confirmations for notarial SARL capitalisation. EMIs domiciled outside the EEA without an EU passport are not viable because they cannot offer EUR-denominated payment accounts inside Luxembourg without breaching the Law of 10 November 2009. Recommended operating model: maintain the primary operating and statutory capital accounts at a Tier-1 bank, and deploy one CSSF-authorised EMI solely for high-frequency payment-account automation, treasury data aggregation and commercial-card distributions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections must arrive as SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT in EUR) to the SARL's named IBAN at the Tier-1 bank. For the statutory minimum capital of EUR 12,000 required by Article 710-13 of the Law of 10 August 1915, the bank's account officer must issue a blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) on the capital-escrow account before notarial execution of the articles of incorporation. Ongoing shareholder contributions must use balance-of-payments purpose code S0301 (equity capital other than investment fund shares) in MT103 field 70 or 72; intercompany shareholder loans use S0202 (other loans). Apple/Google in-app-purchase proceeds arrive from the platform merchant-of-record through USD wire settlements referencing the operating agreement; the Treasury should instruct the bank to automatically match the ACH/SWIFT credit against the platform settlement reference and convert to EUR at the ECB reference rate when the hedging program mandates it. All inbound flows must reconcile to the monthly BCL BP6 declaration and to the statutory annual accounts.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR disbursements, using purpose code ROYL for royalty/licence payments to foreign sub-publishers and INTC for related-party software-development fees, with mandatory field-70 invoice-reconciliation references; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and TARGET2 instant payments for EUR disbursements to EU vendors; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit are generally unsuitable for software publishing because delivery is electronic and no transport documents exist, except as a standby LC where a large milestone-staged development contract is in place and the counterparty demands bank security. Vendor payments for user-acquisition (Meta, TikTok, Google Ads) execute as MT103 in USD after a documented FX spot transaction; intra-group profit distributions follow the statutory dividend procedure and may be paid via SEPA or MT103 with reference to the tax annex. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a complementary multi-currency payment overlay; Low as the sole treasury-banking solution. Luxembourg-regulated EMIs, notably MANGOPAY S.A. and PayTech Luxembourg S.A., plus EU-passported institutions such as Adyen N.V., can lawfully open payment accounts for the SARL, collect App-Store flows and automate virtual-card payouts to sub-licensees. Legal constraints under PSD2/EMD2 include: no credit intermediation or overdraft, no proprietary FX derivative execution, mandatory segregation of client funds into a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution, and prohibition on issuing capital-escrow confirmations for notarial SARL capitalisation. EMIs domiciled outside the EEA without an EU passport are not viable because they cannot offer EUR-denominated payment accounts inside Luxembourg without breaching the Law of 10 November 2009. Recommended operating model: maintain the primary operating and statutory capital accounts at a Tier-1 bank, and deploy one CSSF-authorised EMI solely for high-frequency payment-account automation, treasury data aggregation and commercial-card distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500 million group turnover for Master File; Local File required if related party transactions exceed EUR 1 million (goods) or EUR 100,000 (services).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) for entities engaged in IP holding, licensing, or leasing activities. For software publishing, substance is assessed by actual business activities, employees, and premises. A SARL must demonstrate real economic presence to avoid classification as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalties of 10% to 200% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in fraud; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax benefits, recharacterization of income, and potential liquidation; fines up to EUR 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if the company is deemed a shell; potential criminal liability for fraud.
AML

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; suspension of operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, imprisonment up to 5 years, and personal fines.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties of 10% to 50% of the underpaid tax, and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for negligence if they failed to ensure proper TP documentation.

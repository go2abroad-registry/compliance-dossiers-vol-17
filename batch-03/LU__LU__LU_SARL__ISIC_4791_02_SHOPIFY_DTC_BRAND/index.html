Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales, as amended, particularly Articles 710-1 to 710-24 (SARL); Luxembourg law on commercial companies and the law of 19 May 2023 on the digitalization of the RCS.

Formation / constitutional filings

Authentic deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) and articles of association (statuts), filed electronically via LBR e-filing portal

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or held by the notary, with a blocked-funds certificate (blocage de fonds) issued to the notary.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate is required for a pure online store without physical premises. If an office or warehouse is used, a fire safety conformity certificate from the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Service (SIS) and a building occupancy certificate are required; if employees are present, a workplace clearance under Labor and Mines Inspectorate (ITM) rules must be obtained.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or held by the notary, with a blocked-funds certificate (blocage de fonds) issued to the notary.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg: Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT registration and compliance; Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs) for export/customs procedures. Electronic portals: eCDF (ecdf.etat.lu) for VAT and customs filings, impotsdirects.public.lu for direct tax formalities, and MyGuichet.lu / Guichet.lu for corporate administrative procedures.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the notarized incorporation deed for the SARL and file it with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS); the RCS/Luxembourg Business Registers issue the company registration number and automatically attribute the national identification number (matricule / n° d'identification national). Step 2: Submit the VAT registration application (Demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) to the AEDT via the eCDF online portal or the approved form within 15 days of the first taxable transaction; the AEDT allocates the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format: LU followed by 8 digits). Step 3: Direct tax registration (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax) is effected automatically by the ACD based on the RCS file, using the national matricule as the taxpayer reference. Step 4: For cross-border e-commerce distance sales to non-taxable persons in other EU member states, optionally register for the Luxembourg One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme; update PSP accounts (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) with the allocated VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL is an opaque, non-transparent legal entity subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). A resident SARL is taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available; profits are not taxable at shareholder level until distributed, subject to dividend withholding tax and the participation exemption.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Available elections and options: (i) opt into normal VAT taxation instead of the EUR 35,000 small-business exemption (Article 57 of the VAT Law); (ii) elect VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law; (iii) elect Luxembourg fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax where a resident parent holds 95% or more directly or indirectly (Article 164bis LIR); (iv) elect the IP box regime (Article 50bis LIR) for qualifying IP income (80% exemption); (v) elect the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales of goods to consumers in other EU member states; (vi) rely on the participation exemption for qualifying dividends and capital gains. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules are statutorily transposed and are non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Luxembourg-established taxable person (SARL), VAT registration is required when carrying out taxable transactions, unless the statutory small-business exemption applies; the exemption is available where annual turnover (exclusive of VAT) does not exceed EUR 35,000 (Article 57 of the VAT Law). Once turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or the entity opts to charge VAT, standard registration is mandatory. Separately, for distance sales of goods to non-taxable consumers in other EU member states, the EUR 10,000 Union-wide annual threshold governs the place of supply: below EUR 10,000, Luxembourg VAT applies; above EUR 10,000, the destination member state's VAT applies and must be reported through the OSS scheme unless a local VAT registration is held.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate under the provisions of the Luxembourg VAT Law transposing Article 146 of EU Directive 2006/112/EC, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier, by the customer or by a third party acting on behalf of either, and the supplier obtains and retains genuine documentary proof of export. For DTC e-commerce (Shopify webstore), every parcel to a non-EU delivery address must be linked to a valid electronic export declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN), transport documents and payment evidence; shipments to consumers in other EU member states are not zero-rated exports but distance sales taxable at destination via OSS. International services directly linked to exported goods (including transport and ancillary handling services) may also be zero-rated under the same evidentiary conditions. Export evidence must be completed within the period prescribed by the Luxembourg VAT administration, generally 90 days from the supply, and retained for audit.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration lodged via eCDF / Luxembourg Customs, bearing the MRN confirming physical exit from the EU customs territory)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document (or integrated courier parcel manifest for DHL/FedEx/UPS) evidencing delivery to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103 statement / PSP settlement report) evidencing receipt of the export price and FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (goods description, quantity, unit value, Incoterms and destination country)
  • Proof of customer identity and non-EU establishment (delivery address, purchase order, payment source and IP address evidence where available)
  • Export Summary Declaration / Exit Summary Declaration data and, where applicable, customs transit documentation for goods moving through other EU member states before exit
  • For intra-Community zero-rated supplies: the customer's valid EU VAT number and a timely recapitulative statement (Recap)

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, no government e-invoicing API clearance and no digital stamping regime for domestic invoices. Invoices must satisfy Articles 24-33 of the VAT Law, ensuring authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility. Electronic invoicing in accepted structured formats (PEPPOL/UBL, Factur-X, EDI, PDF-XML) is permitted. VAT returns and recapitulative statements of intra-Community supplies must be filed electronically through the eCDF portal. Luxembourg Customs and the AEDT apply risk-based data analysis for export controls. Mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting obligations are expected to follow the EU ViDA package; until then, structured e-invoicing is recommended to substantiate export zero-rating and to future-proof DTC e-commerce records.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs conduct risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims, cross-referencing eCDF declarations, carrier manifests, PSP settlement records and bank remittance certificates. Where the prescribed export evidence is missing, inconsistent or fabricated, the AEDT reassesses the supply at the applicable Luxembourg VAT rate and issues a deficiency assessment for the VAT not collected; default interest runs from the date the tax should have been paid. Administrative penalties include fines of up to EUR 250,000, and for willful tax evasion up to 200% of the evaded tax; the taxpayer may lose the right to deduct input VAT, be required to repay any export VAT refund previously granted, and may face joint and several liability for persons preparing or using fraudulent documentation. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud (fraude fiscale) may result in imprisonment of up to five years. The statutory reassessment period is generally three years from the end of the year in which the chargeable event arose; it may be extended in cases of fraud or material irregularities, and retroactive clawbacks may be accompanied by interest, penalties and publication of sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EUR is a fully convertible reserve currency. Luxembourg has abolished exchange controls: current-account operations (e-commerce goods/services export proceeds) and capital-account operations (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, loan repayments) are unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU and the 1988 financial-relations law. No prior authorization or licence applies to a SARL operating an internet retail business. Profit remittance: dividends to an EU/EEA parent are exempt from Luxembourg withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; dividends to third-country shareholders attract a 15% statutory withholding tax, reduced to 0–5% under applicable double-tax treaties. There is no foreign-exchange repatriation hurdle. The only practical compliance burden is balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the BCL, carried out by the resident credit institution on behalf of the SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline applies to export proceeds or internet-sale receipts. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; the SARL may retain foreign currency in EUR-denominated or USD/GBP/CAD/AUD multi-currency operating sub-accounts at the resident bank. The only legal duty is statistical: cross-border receipts and payments are reported by the resident credit institution to the BCL under BCL Regulation 2002/111 (balance-of-payments statistics, as amended) pursuant to ECB methodology. The SARL must identify the commercial purpose ('e-commerce sale of goods') in each payment instruction and provide settlement documentation to its bank for classification. A direct-reporting obligation to the BCL arises only if the company elects self-reporting or exceeds the materiality thresholds set by the BCL for direct statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank framework for monetary policy; exchange control liberalization is governed by the Loi du 24 décembre 1988 concernant les relations financières avec l'étranger and the accompanying Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 November 1989. The Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and enforces AML/CFT obligations (Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF Regulation 12-02).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a primary treasury rail, High as an auxiliary disbursement rail. An EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or a Luxembourg-licensed EMI such as Monavate) may passport its licence into Luxembourg under PSD2 / Directive 2009/110/EC and provide multi-currency IBANs, fast FX, and virtual accounts without obtaining a Luxembourg banking licence. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit or accept deposits repayable on demand, so surplus cash is not protected by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (DGDL/DGS up to EUR 100,000 applies only to credit institutions); (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded rather than deposit-insured; (iii) certain tier-1 banks and the Shopify payout regime require settlement into a regulated credit-institution account; (iv) the SARL remains responsible for the provenance-of-funds evidence accepted by the EMI as well as by its main bank. Recommended structure: keep the escrow and operating accounts at a CSSF-licensed bank and use EMIs only for low-value multi-currency supplier disbursements and FX conversion, with daily sweeping controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For incorporation: open a blocked 'compte bloqué' at a Luxembourg credit institution before notarization and transfer at least EUR 12,000 (the classic statutory minimum under Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915; if the company elects the reduced-capital regime introduced by the SARL modernization Law of 3 May 2024, the minimum may be as low as EUR 1 subject to the statutory balance-sheet restrictions) via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank. Remittance field 70 must state 'CAPITAL INJECTION – EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION – LU SARL'; where the bank's e-banking portal requests a purpose code, select the BCL/ECB balance-of-payments direct-investment equity category ('creation of enterprise'). The bank issues the attestation de blocage to the notary; funds are released to the operational account only after notarization and RCS registration. For post-incorporation working capital: inject through a documented shareholder loan (advance d'actionnaire) via SEPA Credit Transfer or MT103 with narrative 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN – LU SARL', supported by a signed loan agreement containing subordination language. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a primary treasury rail, High as an auxiliary disbursement rail. An EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or a Luxembourg-licensed EMI such as Monavate) may passport its licence into Luxembourg under PSD2 / Directive 2009/110/EC and provide multi-currency IBANs, fast FX, and virtual accounts without obtaining a Luxembourg banking licence. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit or accept deposits repayable on demand, so surplus cash is not protected by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (DGDL/DGS up to EUR 100,000 applies only to credit institutions); (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded rather than deposit-insured; (iii) certain tier-1 banks and the Shopify payout regime require settlement into a regulated credit-institution account; (iv) the SARL remains responsible for the provenance-of-funds evidence accepted by the EMI as well as by its main bank. Recommended structure: keep the escrow and operating accounts at a CSSF-licensed bank and use EMIs only for low-value multi-currency supplier disbursements and FX conversion, with daily sweeping controls.

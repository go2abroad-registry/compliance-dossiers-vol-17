Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, particularly Articles 180-206 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée, SARL); supplemented by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and the electronic gazette (RESA)

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) filed with the RCS and published in the electronic gazette (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations – RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash or in kind at incorporation. The capital must be deposited into a blocked bank account opened in the name of the company 'in formation' and released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: No physical manufacturing facility is presupposed. If a commercial office or storage site is used, the SARL must obtain a fire safety certification in accordance with the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 27 February 1986 on fire safety in buildings; a structural load inspection and an occupancy permit (autorisation d'exploitation) issued by the competent municipal authority; and an occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM).

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL, fully subscribed and fully paid in cash or in kind at incorporation. The capital must be deposited into a blocked bank account opened in the name of the company 'in formation' and released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), 45 boulevard Roosevelt, L-2983 Luxembourg (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu and MyGuichet.lu). VAT and registration duties: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDTVA), 1-3 avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg (portal: guichet.lu and MyGuichet.lu). Customs and excise: Administration des douanes et accises (Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL via a notary and file with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the RCS registration number (matricule). Step 2: File VAT registration form (Formulaire 640 'Déclaration de début d'activité') with the AEDTVA to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) required for all taxable supplies. Step 3: Register with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; the ACD establishes the national corporate tax file (NIF/reference number). Step 4: Apply for an EORI number with the Customs and Excise Administration before the first export or import transaction. Step 5: Foreign entities without a Luxembourg seat may register directly for VAT via the AEDTVA using the non-established taxable person procedure (Form 640), with no physical establishment required; a Luxembourg tax representative may be mandated in specific cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (non-transparent, resident corporation). A Luxembourg SARL is a body corporate taxable on its worldwide income; it is subject to corporate income tax (IRC) at 17% (plus 7% solidarity surcharge, effective 18.19%), municipal business tax (ICC) at the applicable communal rate (6.75%–13.85%; Luxembourg City base 6.75%), and net wealth tax (IF). The SARL is neither a pass-through entity nor eligible for territorial profit exemption absent treaty or statutory regime application.

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for fully consolidated Luxembourg subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at 95%; (ii) optional VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) for eligible related parties; (iii) participation exemption election (50% exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains) subject to holding-period and substance conditions; (iv) option for the partial-inclusion tax regime on qualifying foreign permanent-establishment income; hybrid mismatch adjustments follow mandatory ATAD transposition (LIR articles 168sexies et seq.) with no elective carve-outs. No sub-national tax distinctions apply (unitary national regime).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the domestic small business franchise exemption (Article 57 of the 1979 VAT law). Non-resident suppliers and distance sellers have no right to the exemption and must register from the first euro of Luxembourg supplies; intra-EU distance sales of goods up to EUR 10,000 per calendar year remain taxable in Luxembourg unless the supplier elects destination-state taxation via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to territories outside the EU qualify for the 0% rate under Article 54 of the amended Luxembourg VAT law of 12 February 1979, provided the POD apparel is physically dispatched or transported out of the EU within three months of supply and the supplier retains conclusive documentary evidence. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered purchaser in another Member State are zero-rated under Article 55 of the same law. B2B print-on-demand services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (subject to reversal where applicable). The invoice must state the applicable zero-rating reference and be matched to the export customs declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/Single Administrative Document) with electronic filing through the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and exit confirmation from the EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing consignee, destination and date of departure
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and transport documents (CMR/CTN) supporting the export transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank FX inflow statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration from the overseas buyer
  • EORI registration number and, for intra-EU supplies, the recapitulative EC Sales List (ESL) filing reference

No continuous transaction controls (CTC), fiscal API clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg as of 2025. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary; B2G e-invoicing is mandatory through the PEPPOL network under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT compliance is discharged via periodic VAT returns (Form 640, filed monthly, quarterly or annually through MyGuichet.lu), recapitulative EC Sales Lists, and Intrastat declarations where applicable; EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) real-time digital reporting for intra-EU supplies is expected to apply from 2030.

Unsupported or fraudulent export zero-rating claims (including fictitious exports, false consignee declarations, or non-repatriation of foreign currency proceeds) are subject to combined audits by the ACD, AEDTVA and the Customs and Excise Administration. Retrospective reassessments can claw back the zero-rated treatment, deny input VAT recovery, and impose statutory late-interest. Administrative fines for VAT evasion may reach up to 100% of the evaded tax under Article 59 of the 1979 VAT law, with criminal exposure for intentional fraud including fines and imprisonment. The standard five-year reassessment limitation is extended to ten years in cases of established fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts for residents and non-residents. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Directive 88/361/EEC prohibit any exchange controls on capital movements and payments between Luxembourg and the rest of the world. There is no prior authorization regime, no currency-conversion permit, no repatriation quota, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, shareholder return or trade settlement. The only remittance friction is tax-based: dividends paid to an EU parent satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) conditions (qualifying holding of at least 10% or EUR 1.2 million acquisition cost, 12-month holding period) are exempt from Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; distributions to non-EU shareholders are subject to the standard 15% dividend withholding tax under Article 147 LIR, mitigated or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty. Anti-abuse and transfer-pricing controls (Article 56 LIR, ATAD 1/2, thin-capitalization and interest limitation rules) discipline pricing of intragroup funding but do not restrict exchange convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg law imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into EUR, and no surrender ratio. A Luxembourg SARL may lawfully retain USD, GBP, CNY or any other currency balances onshore or offshore. The sole administrative requirement is statistical: resident credit institutions execute BCL reference reporting (collecte de données de référence) for balance-of-payments purposes, requiring the company to provide the economic nature of each transaction (e.g., 'export proceeds of goods', 'trade payables') for wire transfers typically at or above EUR 12,500 or equivalent; this reporting does not create an authorization, conversion or repatriation obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; prudential and conduct supervision of credit institutions and payment/electronic-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; exchange-control authority is fully subordinated to the ECB framework and to the Luxembourg law of 27 November 1986 on balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary operational settlement and FX rail; Low as the sole primary banking relationship for a Luxembourg SARL. A non-Luxembourg EU/EEA electronic-money institution (EMI) licensed under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe authorised by the National Bank of Belgium, Revolut Bank UAB, or an equivalent passporting EMI) may bank the SARL on a cross-border services basis without a CSSF licence. A Luxembourg-domiciled EMI would require CSSF authorization under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended. Material legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot grant credit, take deposits, or settle directly on central-bank money (TARGET2/T2); (ii) balances are protected only through safeguarding arrangements with a partner credit institution, not by the EUR 100,000 deposit-guarantee scheme; (iii) Luxembourg notaries, statutory auditors and tax administration substance reviews expect a relationship with a Luxembourg-regulated credit institution, particularly for the capital blocking certificate and for audit confirmations; (iv) Stripe and PayPal are payment acquirers/processing PSPs rather than banking substitutes and should be integrated solely as merchant settlement rails into the treasury structure.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Aggregated PSP settlement flows: Stripe/PayPal payouts arrive as pooled, anonymous lump sums without customer names, which can be misread as third-party or unusual fund movements inconsistent with the stated POD retail business.

Root cause: Aggregated PSP settlement flows: Stripe/PayPal payouts arrive as pooled, anonymous lump sums without customer names, which can be misread as third-party or unusual fund movements inconsistent with the stated POD retail business.

Defense evidence: Merchant service agreements with Stripe and PayPal; 6 to 12 months of PSP payout statements and settlement reports (gross/net); platform transaction-level exports in CSV with order ID, SKU, customer country and payment reference; PCI-DSS Attestation of Compliance; published refund and chargeback policy; and a written financial-flow diagram showing customer -> acquirer -> PSP -> SARL settlement account mechanics.
Geographic supply-chain friction: trade payables to third-country POD producers (e.g., China, Vietnam, United States) from a Luxembourg retail SARL with no physical warehouse or local inventory.

Root cause: Geographic supply-chain friction: trade payables to third-country POD producers (e.g., China, Vietnam, United States) from a Luxembourg retail SARL with no physical warehouse or local inventory.

Defense evidence: Signed POD production and fulfilment agreements; supplier KYB extracts including business registration and beneficial owner confirmation; sample commercial invoices matching production volumes; HS classification codes (e.g., HS 6109, 6114, 6211) and Incoterms (DAP/DDP); logistics waybills from USPS, DHL, DPD or similar carriers; and a board resolution approving the supplier list and payment terms.
UBO layering through non-resident holding structures: ultimate natural persons holding indirectly via a chain of non-EU SPVs, triggering enhanced due diligence and RBE cross-checks.

Root cause: UBO layering through non-resident holding structures: ultimate natural persons holding indirectly via a chain of non-EU SPVs, triggering enhanced due diligence and RBE cross-checks.

Defense evidence: Notarized articles of association of the SARL; RBE (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) filing extract identifying all natural persons with more than 25% interest; signed corporate organigram; passport copies of each ultimate natural person; AML source-of-wealth questionnaire; and a bank reference letter from the parent group's existing regulated financial institution.
Refund and reversal opacity: frequent e-commerce refunds, chargebacks and PSP adjustments creating circular or reversed flows that may resemble layering or unauthorized payment activity.

Root cause: Refund and reversal opacity: frequent e-commerce refunds, chargebacks and PSP adjustments creating circular or reversed flows that may resemble layering or unauthorized payment activity.

Defense evidence: Publicly available refund policy; PSP refund and chargeback dispute logs; reconciliation ledger mapping every refund to the original transaction; VAT/e-commerce return excerpts; and an explanatory note from the statutory auditor confirming that refund liabilities are operating obligations and not client consignments or fiduciary balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial subscription of SARL share capital, funds are deposited in a EUR capital-blocking account (compte de dépôt de capital) at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution; the bank issues a blocking certificate (attestation de blocage de fonds) to the notary; upon signature of the notarial deed, funds are released to the operational account. Subsequent capital increases are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for foreign currency using ISO 20022 Purpose Code CAPC ('Capital Payment') or INTC ('Intra-Company Payment') where the contributing shareholder is a group company; the bank requires the shareholders' resolution or notarial deed excerpt for its file. E-commerce operating inflows settle through Stripe/PayPal payouts to the SARL's designated EUR and USD accounts; verification by the receiving bank matches the PSP settlement reference to the registered merchant account ID and the PSP master agreement. For BCL balance-of-payments reference reporting, the resident bank must record a purpose/motif économique for transactions with non-residents at or above EUR 12,500 equivalent; supporting documentation includes the underlying commercial invoice, share capital resolution, or loan agreement.(i) EU/EEA POD suppliers and fulfilment partners: SEPA Credit Transfer cleared via TARGET Services (T2) or SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) with EPC purpose code TRAD or SUPP, settlement in EUR within the same business day. (ii) Non-EEA suppliers (US, UK, CN, HK): SWIFT MT103 through the correspondent network in USD, GBP or CNY, accompanied by the commercial invoice and BCL motif économique reference for trade payables. (iii) Bulk or bespoke procurement orders above approximately EUR 50,000 (e.g., regional batch production runs): irrevocable documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600, issued via SWIFT MT700 against presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list and certificate of origin; for order-by-order POD micro-flows, LCs are economically unsuitable due to issuance and acceptance charges of 0.5 to 1.5 percent per annum plus USD 50 to 150 per amendment, so open-account telegraphic transfer with 30 to 60 day terms is the prevailing market standard. (iv) Ancillary marketing and platform payments (Meta, Google, Shopify apps) use commercial card rails or SEPA Direct Debit (SDD B2B). The SARL should maintain segregated EUR and USD accounts to avoid repeated forced conversion at bank spreads; FX conversion should be executed only on consolidated monthly positions through a dedicated FX-forward line.EUR/USD deliverable FX Forward contracts with tenors up to 12 months documented under an ISDA Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex or an FX-specific ACDA governed by Luxembourg or English law, covering USD settlement proceeds retained from US POD salesEUR/USD FX Swaps (near leg and far leg combination) for rolling management of USD balances across quarterly VAT and payroll windowsFX Spot contracts for immediate conversion of USD, GBP or CNH proceeds into EUR for operational disbursement, executed on consolidated PSP treasury balances to minimize spread costsCNH/CNY deliverable FX forwards for RMB payables to Chinese POD suppliers via offshore CNH clearing through Luxembourg-licensed credit institutions and their Hong Kong or Mainland correspondent branchesCurrency options (EUR call / USD put) permitted under MiFID II professional-client rules where a genuine hedging exposure is evidenced, limited to premium-payment structures and prohibited for naked option-selling without an underlying commercial exposureNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) permissible but not applicable to EUR/USD because EUR/USD is a fully convertible deliverable pair; NDFs are reserved exclusively for residual exposures to non-convertible counterpart currencies (e.g., VND, IDR, KRW) should the supplier base expand into those jurisdictions

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High as a supplementary operational settlement and FX rail; Low as the sole primary banking relationship for a Luxembourg SARL. A non-Luxembourg EU/EEA electronic-money institution (EMI) licensed under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe authorised by the National Bank of Belgium, Revolut Bank UAB, or an equivalent passporting EMI) may bank the SARL on a cross-border services basis without a CSSF licence. A Luxembourg-domiciled EMI would require CSSF authorization under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, as amended. Material legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot grant credit, take deposits, or settle directly on central-bank money (TARGET2/T2); (ii) balances are protected only through safeguarding arrangements with a partner credit institution, not by the EUR 100,000 deposit-guarantee scheme; (iii) Luxembourg notaries, statutory auditors and tax administration substance reviews expect a relationship with a Luxembourg-regulated credit institution, particularly for the capital blocking certificate and for audit confirmations; (iv) Stripe and PayPal are payment acquirers/processing PSPs rather than banking substitutes and should be integrated solely as merchant settlement rails into the treasury structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold expressly prescribed for Local File or Master File in Luxembourg domestic law; however, EU Country-by-Country reporting applies to ultimate parent groups with consolidated annual revenue ≥ EUR 750 million. All taxpayers must maintain transfer pricing documentation sufficient to support related-party transactions upon ACD request; documentation should be available before filing for fiscal-year 2024.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not maintain type-based Economic Substance Regulations akin to offshore jurisdictions, but the OECD substance requirement and the EU General Anti-Tax Avoidance Rule (ATAD GAAR) apply. For a Print-on-Demand retail entity, sufficient commercial substance includes real staff in Luxembourg, substantive management decisions, actual e-commerce contracts, and a meaningful operational presence. Failure to demonstrate substance allows the ACD to recharacterize income, deny treaty benefits, and treat the SARL as a transparent shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to Declare Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of responsible persons up to 5 years under Luxembourg Criminal Tax Law; reassessment of evaded income plus late payment interest at 0.75% per month; administrative penalty of 100% of the evaded tax for bad faith.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to personal criminal imprisonment and travel bans; civil liability of officers for restitution of corporate tax debts and fines; piercing the corporate veil is permissible where the SARL is a mere alter ego.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits and domestic participation exemption; profit recharacterization to the controlling shareholder; levy of 15% dividend withholding tax on redistributed sums plus penalty of 25% of the additional tax under GAAR legislation; forced deregistration by the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners risk asset freeze orders, disqualification from any directorship for up to 10 years, and joint and several liability for the company's tax and penalty amounts.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines ranging from EUR 100 to EUR 5,000 per violation; additional daily enforcement penalties of EUR 250 per day for non-compliance; criminal fines for deliberate false statements.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally liable for fines; directors may receive criminal record entry and be temporarily barred from conducting commercial activities in Luxembourg.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to realign profits; additional tax assessments plus monthly interest; penalty up to 100% of the additional assessed tax for deliberate mispricing; potential referral to the Mutual Agreement Procedure or EU arbitration.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false contemporaneous documentation can face criminal prosecution for aiding tax fraud; they may be held personally liable for the penalty component and subject to a European Arrest Warrant in cross-border investigations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, divided into shares of at least EUR 1 par value. At incorporation, at least 25% of each share must be paid up in cash (minimum EUR 3,000) and deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in favor of the company; the remaining 75% must be fully paid within five years of incorporation. Contributions in kind require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé). A single corporate or individual shareholder is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: obtain the fire-safety attestation (attestation de sécurité incendie) from the competent communal fire authority for the registered office under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 13 February 1989 on fire safety in buildings; standard leased office floors do not require structural load inspections as the landlord's building permit covers structural integrity. Occupational clearance: declare the workplace and employees, if any, with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), implement first-aid equipment and emergency protocols under Articles L.311-15 and L.312-8 of the Luxembourg Labour Code, maintain electrical conformity statements for office equipment, and ensure workstation ergonomics compliance; a building permit (permis de construire) is required only if substantial interior alterations are made.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital is EUR 12,000, divided into shares of at least EUR 1 par value. At incorporation, at least 25% of each share must be paid up in cash (minimum EUR 3,000) and deposited into a blocked Luxembourg bank account in favor of the company; the remaining 75% must be fully paid within five years of incorporation. Contributions in kind require a valuation report by an approved statutory auditor (réviseur d'entreprises agréé). A single corporate or individual shareholder is permitted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg has a split VAT/income tax administration: the Administration des contributions directes (ACD) is the competent authority for corporate income and municipal business tax, while the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) is the national VAT authority. Central filing and information portal: https://guichet.lu; direct tax portal: https://impotsdirects.public.lu; VAT portal: https://pfi.tax.lu (PFI electronic filing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SARL must first complete notarial incorporation and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which issues a 13-digit national registration number. After RCS registration, the entity must apply for VAT identification with the AEDT using the standard registration form (Form 640), supplying the RCS excerpt, statutes, proof of registered office, identity of managers, and description of activities. For an SARL, no separate tax ID is needed for corporate income tax; the RCS number serves as the administrative identifier, while the AEDT issues a distinct VAT number in the format LU + 8 digits. Non-resident entities not establishing a permanent establishment must register directly with the AEDT for VAT from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg, without a turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply US-style check-the-box rules. A resident SARL is primarily taxable at entity level on worldwide income under the LIR. Available statutory elections include: (i) joint taxation of a resident parent and qualifying subsidiaries under Article 164bis LIR (tax consolidation) if the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary shares directly or indirectly; (ii) VAT grouping towards AEDT under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT law, allowing closely linked persons to be treated as a single taxable person; and (iii) the EU OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme election for intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C electronic services, subject to Luxembourg procedural requirements. Hybrid mismatch elections are restricted to the ATAD-aligned rules, and any elective structure must be commercially, legally, and economically substantive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover threshold for local small-business exemption for Luxembourg-established suppliers; no threshold for non-resident suppliers—registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg; EU distance sales threshold of EUR 100,000 applies for EU suppliers unless OSS is used)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for the supply of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller or a third party acting on the seller's behalf, provided the future buyer is a non-EU person, the goods leave EU territory within the legally required time limits, and the supplier holds bona fide documentary evidence of export. For services, zero-rating applies under B2B place-of-supply rules where the customer is a taxable person established outside the EU, and for services directly connected to exported movable goods, provided the customer identification and service description are properly documented. For sales of the mobile utility app through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing to a non-EU consumer, the supply is an electronically supplied service taxed in the customer's residence country; to the extent the customer is EU-established, Luxembourg VAT at 17% applies (unless an OSS election covers the cross-border B2C supply), and the app store platform arrangements determine the operative tax treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved exit summary declaration showing the date and customs office of departure from the EU, retained in the company's VAT file)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or equivalent transit document) evidencing physical dispatch of the goods outside EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer/payment settlement proof linking the foreign purchaser to the exported goods and demonstrating receipt of consideration in foreign currency or EUR

Luxembourg does not operate a general real-time fiscal clearance system and has no API-based pre-clearance mandate for B2B invoices. B2G invoices to central government and public-sector bodies must be transmitted electronically in standardized formats (currently PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices). For B2B transactions, paper and PDF invoices remain valid provided they meet Luxembourg VAT invoice content requirements and are stored in a reliable, legible format. A mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing regime has been announced with intended effect from 1 January 2026 (transitional period under discussion), but as of 2025 continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or mandatory reporting to the tax authority is not yet a legal requirement for private-sector invoices.

Luxembourg VAT and customs authorities actively audit zero-export claims. Where the required proof of export is incomplete, absent, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied and VAT at 17% is assessed on the initial supply value. The standard reassessment period is five years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud, falsification of documents, or deliberate concealment. Delinquent VAT charges attract late-payment interest at the statutory monthly rate of 0.8%, and administrative penalties can reach EUR 25,000; participation in a VAT carousel or knowing submission of false export declarations exposes the company and its managers to criminal tax-fraud sanctions, civil fines, imprisonment in severe cases, and EU-wide cross-border information-sharing through the Eurofisc network.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro. Capital movements are fully liberalized under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU); no exchange-control licensing applies to equity injections, shareholder loans, trade settlements, or profit remittances. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate tax formalities: no withholding tax on dividends paid to qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU, otherwise 15% domestic dividend withholding, reducible under Luxembourg's extensive tax treaty network. No convertibility restrictions, foreign-investment approvals, or documentary FX clearances are imposed by the BCL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export proceeds. EUR invoicing is the default; USD and other-currency receipts from Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, or direct B2B licenses may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted freely. The only relevant requirement is the BCL statistical balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting obligation triggered when an individual cross-border transaction or aggregate monthly flows exceed the applicable de minimis threshold (typically EUR 12,500), with no punitive or administrative consent requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises credit institutions and payment/electronic-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility as an ancillary collection and payout vehicle; Medium as a primary treasury bank. A CSSF-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under the Luxembourg law of 21 December 2007 (as amended) and PSD2 may lawfully issue e-money wallets, execute payment transactions, hold safeguarding accounts, and provide FX conversion. However, EMIs cannot grant credit or lending facilities, and safeguarding requirements restrict idle client funds. For this SARL's digitally sourced revenue (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, subscription), the optimal configuration is a hybrid treasury: a regulated Luxembourg bank as the operating/escrow account for capital injections, payroll, supplier disbursements, and a CSSF-regulated EMI or Payment Institution (PI) as an optional collection/settlement account for distributing storefront payout proceeds. Use of an offshore (non-EU) EMI is discouraged due to CSSF supervision gaps and corresponding correspondent-bank de-risking risk.

Use of an offshore (non-EU) EMI is discouraged due to CSSF supervision gaps and corresponding correspondent-bank de-risking risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de-minimis statutory threshold is codified in the LIR. In practice, the Luxembourg tax authorities expect an OECD-compliant Master File and Local File when the MNE group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 50 million; for a Luxembourg SARL with cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 per fiscal year, a Local File and benchmarking study should be maintained. Country-by-Country notification applies where the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies an overriding economic-substance doctrine grounded in the statutory general anti-abuse rule (Abuse of Laws) and OECD BEPS principles. A SARL distributing mobile-app revenue must demonstrate real local presence, payroll, office space, local board decision-making, bank accounts, and substantive control over the software IP. If the SARL is a shell with assets and risks formally owned but functionally controlled from abroad, the ACD may disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, disallow IP-box relief, and reassess income at the full Luxembourg CIT, solidarity surcharge, and municipal business tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Wilful tax evasion and fraudulent misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud proceedings may lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 250,000, in addition to restitution of all underpaid CIT, solidarity surcharge, municipal business tax, and late interest at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors signing fraudulent returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded corporate taxes and may be disqualified from managing Luxembourg companies for up to 10 years; travel bans and attachment of personal assets may be ordered during penal proceedings.
Shell company and absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The ACD may apply the anti-abuse doctrine and disregard the SARL as a fiscally transparent vehicle; income is reassessed at the Luxembourg resident shareholder level, treaty benefits are denied, and all previously exempt IP-box income becomes taxable at the aggregate 24.94% rate plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be summoned personally to restore distributions made without substance, may be held liable for unpaid withholding taxes, and, in egregious cases, face criminal prosecution for abusive use of a legal entity.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden royalty leakage

Statutory sanction: Under Article 56 LIR, the ACD may make a transfer-pricing adjustment increasing the taxable base, deny the deduction of the excessive payment, and impose a late-payment surcharge plus delay interest; absence of contemporaneous documentation attracts additional penalties and a 10% surcharge on the adjustment in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implemented an artificial pricing scheme are personally liable for the company’s tax shortfall to the extent of their benefit, can be barred from board memberships, and may be subject to extradition proceedings if they leave Luxembourg while a tax criminal inquiry is active.
AML non-compliance and misdirected payment flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML framework, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 5 million; aggravated tax fraud may raise the penalty to 7 years’ imprisonment. The ACD and Financial Intelligence Unit may also freeze corporate and personal accounts and seize the mobile-app revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors found to have participated knowingly in the laundering structure face personal criminal conviction, travel bans, professional disqualification, and civil restitution of all laundered funds, with no statutory cap on damages.
Failure to file, obstructing the tax audit

Statutory sanction: The ACD can issue an estimated taxation d'office on a high deemed basis, with a formal penalty of up to EUR 10,000 for non-filing and additional monthly delay interest. Obstructing an audit is a separate administrative offence that may be escalated to a criminal fine of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment if obstruction is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to cooperate are personally liable for penalties, may have their passports suspended while an obstructed tax audit is pending, and can be prosecuted for fraud if the obstruction conceals taxable app revenue.

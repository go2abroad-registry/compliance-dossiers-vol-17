Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum subscribed share capital for a Luxembourg SARL is EUR 12,000 (approximately USD 13,000). The capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank or escrow account in the name of the proposed SARL before notarial deed signing, and funds are released only after RCS registration gives the company legal personality.

Funding & unlock path: For the office premises used as registered and operational seat, the company must obtain a municipal occupancy certificate, fire-safety verification by the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) where required, and workplace-compliant structural and occupational-safety clearance under the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) before employees begin work.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxation (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax) and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; official portals: guichet.lu, myguichet.lu, rcs.lu (Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register), and the secured eCDF platform for electronic VAT and income tax filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder establishing a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) execute incorporation deeds before a Luxembourg notary and file them with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via myguichet.lu; (2) obtain the 13-digit national business number (matricule) and the RCS registration certificate; (3) file the VAT registration application (Formulaire 640/1) with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, obtaining a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) within approximately 3 to 5 business days; (4) register simultaneously with the ACD for corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT) and net wealth tax (NWT); (5) appoint a designated local tax representative only where the foreign shareholder performs direct taxable activities without a local establishment, noting that the incorporated SARL is ipso facto a Luxembourg resident taxpayer by reason of its registered office and central administration; and (6) open a Luxembourg professional bank account to support subsequent VAT refund routing and export proceeds reconciliation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign shareholder establishing a Luxembourg SARL must: (1) execute incorporation deeds before a Luxembourg notary and file them with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via myguichet.lu; (2) obtain the 13-digit national business number (matricule) and the RCS registration certificate; (3) file the VAT registration application (Formulaire 640/1) with the AEDT via myguichet.lu, obtaining a Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits) within approximately 3 to 5 business days; (4) register simultaneously with the ACD for corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT) and net wealth tax (NWT); (5) appoint a designated local tax representative only where the foreign shareholder performs direct taxable activities without a local establishment, noting that the incorporated SARL is ipso facto a Luxembourg resident taxpayer by reason of its registered office and central administration; and (6) open a Luxembourg professional bank account to support subsequent VAT refund routing and export proceeds reconciliation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid entity classification election exists under Luxembourg law; the SARL is per se a taxable corporation. Available elections: (a) option to waive the VAT small business franchise exemption and register for normal VAT (Article 57 VAT Law); (b) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) for closely linked resident entities; (c) joint taxation election under the fiscal unity regime (intégration fiscale, Article 164bis LIR) requiring a 95% participation, enabling offset of profits and losses within the group; (d) application for advance tax rulings (tax rulings) from the ACD/AEDT to confirm hybrid mismatch treatment, permanent establishment attribution and arm's length conditions; (e) election under ATAD (Law of 21 December 2018) to align interest limitation and exit taxation treatments; (f) for eligible EU parent companies, election to apply the Parent-Subsidiary Directive withholding tax exemption and the Interest and Royalties Directive regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover below which Luxembourg-established small businesses may claim the VAT franchise exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); registration is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded. Non-established foreign suppliers engaging in taxable supplies in Luxembourg are registrable from the first euro, except (i) intra-EU distance sales of goods and (ii) B2C electronic, telecommunications and broadcasting services (including SaaS payroll platforms), which benefit from the Union-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold administered via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction) is available under Articles 42 and 43 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for: (a) exports of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or a third party, evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by an EU customs office; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number, provided the statutory recapitulative statement is filed on time; (c) B2B supply of services (including the payroll automation SaaS platform) to a customer established outside Luxembourg, governed by the main place-of-supply rule (Article 58 of Directive 2006/112/EC), so that Luxembourg VAT is not chargeable and the customer accounts for the tax under reverse charge or local rules where applicable; (d) B2C digital services supplied to non-EU consumers when effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU. The exporter must retain evidence of actual dispatch within 3 months of supply, and there is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under Luxembourg VAT law; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the administratively accepted intercompany settlement period under OECD transfer-pricing practice and Article 56 LIR.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DES) accepted by the EU customs office of export
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR consignment note, or postal/courier receipt evidencing physical departure from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice signed by the supplier, customer purchase order and transport contract or insurance certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/SEPA credit confirmation or bank statement proving receipt of consideration in the seller's Luxembourg bank account
  • Intra-Community Sales List (recapitulative statement) and evidence of the customer's valid EU VAT registration number for intra-EU supplies
  • Export follow-up certificate (attestation de circulation) where requested by the AEDT, and freight forwarder's export certificate in air/sea consignments

No general real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous transaction controls or digital stamping is currently in force for B2B transactions. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies through the government PEPPOL network (Factur-X / PEPPOL BIS 3.0) for invoices to Luxembourg central public authorities and is being phased in for local municipalities. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary in 2025, with mandatory nationwide structured e-invoicing expected by 2027 pursuant to the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposals. VAT returns (Formulaire 1022/1023) and recapitulative statements (EC sales lists) must be transmitted electronically via eCDF; a monthly or quarterly return frequency applies, and the AEDT uses risk-based cross-matching with customs export declarations (DES) and Intrastat filings for audit selection.

The AEDT and ACD jointly scrutinize zero-rating claims by cross-referencing DES declarations, Intrastat returns, and OSS data with the exporter's accounting records. Where export documentation is incomplete, the supply is recharacterized as domestic, and the tax authorities claw back the VAT at 17% plus default interest of 0.8% per month (8% per annum). Administrative fines under Article 46 of the VAT Law range from EUR 25 to EUR 25,000 per violation; negligent under-declaration attracts a surcharge of 10% of the tax due, while deliberate fraud – including forged export certificates, fictitious customers or unrepatriated export proceeds – triggers the criminal sanctions of Articles 63-66 VAT Law (fines of EUR 50,000 to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years) and a 100% tax surcharge. The reassessment statute of limitations is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or concealment, permitting retrospective clawback of all previously refunded VAT; company directors and the Luxembourg tax representative may also be held jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro within the Luxembourg legal order. Luxembourg is bound by Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and by the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC), guaranteeing free movement of capital and payments between Member States and, to a substantial extent, with third countries. No exchange-control authorization applies to current-account transactions, and profit remittances (dividends, royalties, interest, software subscription fees) may be distributed freely by an SARL, subject solely to the company-law distribution tests under the Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (as amended), standard withholding tax obligations, economic-substance documentation, and intra-group transfer-pricing rules. Non-resident shareholders holding capital in a Luxembourg SARL may convert and repatriate liquidation proceeds, sale proceeds, and dividends without any central-bank permit or conversion license.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Luxembourg. As a Eurozone jurisdiction with fully liberalized capital movements, the BCL imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to convert foreign-currency export, licensing, or software-as-a-service subscription receipts into euro. An SARL engaged in cross-border payroll automation may retain export proceeds in foreign currency, hold them in multi-currency accounts, or convert them at freely negotiated market rates. Cross-border flows are subject only to statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the reporting bank to the BCL for macroeconomic data purposes, which carries no controlling or restrictive effect on treasury operations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), an integral member of the Eurosystem, operating in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy execution and with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium feasibility, legally permissible only as a secondary collection and disbursement rail and never as the primary treasury anchor for the Luxembourg SARL. An EU-authorized Electronic Money Institution under PSD2 and Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Mangopay, PayPal, or a Luxembourg payment institution licensed by the CSSF) may legally hold client float and execute SEPA direct debits and credit transfers; however, the SARL must treat such balances as counterparty credit exposure under CSSF Circular 19/732 on outsourcing and the Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT. The corporate treasury policy should cap aggregate EMI balances below EUR 250,000 per institution and require a monthly reconciliation and independent audit trail. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are structurally discouraged: they fail CSSF fit-and-proper expectations, complicate audit-trail integrity and GDPR data-processing chains, trigger enhanced due diligence under Circular CSSF 12/552, and materially delay tier-1 bank onboarding when the bank perceives residual reputational or sanctions risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent equity contributions should be executed via SWIFT MT103 in euro using the transaction-purpose codes of the ECB Balance-of-Payments and International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) taxonomy, in particular Category S001 (equity capital of direct investment) for share capital or Category S002 (other capital of direct investment) for shareholder loans. The receiving Luxembourg bank requires for equity injections: a notarized capital-increase deed or cash-contribution certificate, a board resolution approving the share issue, and subsequent filing with the RCSL; for shareholder debt: a signed shareholder-loan agreement with arm's-length interest, a board resolution approving the funding, and intercompany documentation satisfying transfer-pricing standards. EU-resident parent payments may be routed via TARGET2 or SEPA Credit Transfer; third-country wires must include complete remitter information (legal name, address, account number, and purpose code) to satisfy SWIFT ISO 20022 enhanced-data requirements under EBA and CSSF regulation. BCL statistical reporting is performed by the bank rather than the SARL, but the SARL must preserve all underlying agreements, board resolutions, and capital-movement evidence for at least ten years in line with professional record-keeping standards.For client refunds, vendor payments, payroll salary settlements, and dividend distributions, the SARL should employ: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro-denominated EU and EEA payments; SEPA Direct Debit (SDD Core and B2B) for recurring subscription collection where valid mandates and pre-notification procedures are in place; SWIFT MT103 for non-euro and third-country disbursements; and SWIFT MT202 COV for institutional cover payments where the beneficiary bank requires a covering institution. Commercial letters of credit governed by UCP 600 and documentary collections are not commercially suited to payroll SaaS flows but remain available as standby instruments for high-value implementation contracts or government procurement clients. A multi-currency account structure (EUR, USD, GBP) with zero-balance pooling and notional cash concentration at the tier-1 relationship bank minimizes cross-currency conversion leakage on global payroll processing fees and enables automated liquidity sweeping for capital calls and regulatory reserve coverage.Plain-vanilla FX outright forwards with tenors of 1 to 12 months on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, and other freely deliverable pairs, executed under an ISDA Master Agreement or bank FX Master Agreement with elective professional-client classificationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or inconvertible emerging-market currencies where physical settlement is legally unavailable to the SARL; permissible exclusively for hedging treasury exposures on payroll collections and settlements in those currencies, with final settlement in euroFX swaps and cross-currency swaps for balance-sheet liquidity transformation on shareholder loans, intercompany balances, or acquisition financing; require bank credit approval and comprehensive documentation under ISDAPlain-vanilla FX options (call and put) on transactional currencies when purchased as protective hedging instruments through the relationship bank under MiFID II professional-client categorization; speculative naked option writing or structured derivative strategies are not permitted without a CSSF investment-firm license

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility, legally permissible only as a secondary collection and disbursement rail and never as the primary treasury anchor for the Luxembourg SARL. An EU-authorized Electronic Money Institution under PSD2 and Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Mangopay, PayPal, or a Luxembourg payment institution licensed by the CSSF) may legally hold client float and execute SEPA direct debits and credit transfers; however, the SARL must treat such balances as counterparty credit exposure under CSSF Circular 19/732 on outsourcing and the Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT. The corporate treasury policy should cap aggregate EMI balances below EUR 250,000 per institution and require a monthly reconciliation and independent audit trail. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are structurally discouraged: they fail CSSF fit-and-proper expectations, complicate audit-trail integrity and GDPR data-processing chains, trigger enhanced due diligence under Circular CSSF 12/552, and materially delay tier-1 bank onboarding when the bank perceives residual reputational or sanctions risk.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate Luxembourg City rate for taxable income exceeding EUR 200,000 (17.00% corporate income tax + 7.00% solidarity employment fund surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax on a harmonised tax base, plus municipal surcharge multiplier)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg withholding tax on distributions; 0% when the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies (shareholder resident in an EU/EEA state holding 10%+ for 12 months) or under the domestic participation-exemption protocol (EUR 1.2 million / 10% holding continuous 12 months); reduced treaty rates as low as 5% for portfolio and 0% for substantial shareholdings

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg law imposes no statutory Master File / Local File triggering threshold; a full OECD-aligned TP study should nevertheless be available on demand once intercompany transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per annum, and Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory at consolidated group turnover exceeding EUR 750,000,000 (EU Directive 2016/881)

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but substance is enforced indirectly: the GAAR in Art. 6 AO and the abuse-of-law doctrine, 'siège de direction effective' residency test under LIR Art. 6, the AOA letterbox reporting framework, and ATAD/DFT application require the SARL to maintain premises, qualified payroll-processing staff, local board decisions, and real activity; inadequate substance leads to denial of DTA benefits, recharacterization of the Luxembourg entity as tax-transparent, and nullification of financing structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminally Misstating Revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of evaded CIT and municipal business tax, monthly interest 0.6% (7.2% p.a.), penalty up to 100% of evaded tax, criminal fine up to EUR 250,000, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Executive directors and the actual payroll-platform beneficiary may receive imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years, personal restitution orders, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans, and joint civil liability for corporate tax and withheld employee payroll tax.
Artificial Letterbox / Sham Substance Company

Statutory sanction: Determination that the executive seat is abroad; reclassification of the entity's domicile; re-taxation of Luxembourg-market revenue in Luxembourg; denial of Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption; penalties up to EUR 7,500 for failure to notify the register of actual business premises.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: trustees and statutory directors jointly liable for restitution of unpaid dividends, net wealth tax, and CIT; civil bankruptcy disqualification from serving as directors of any Luxembourg commercial company; travel prohibition under asset-preservation proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income with monthly 0.6% late interest, secondary adjustment as deemed distribution/dividend subject to 15% WHT classification, documentary penalty applied when the taxpayer cannot demonstrate arm's length compliance, and valuation penalties up to 10% of the adjusted transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transfer pricing policy in bad faith are jointly and severally liable for the marginal CIT, the WHT non-withheld, and related surcharges; the civil courts may freeze director assets under attachment while the criminal tax fraud proceedings remain pending.
AML / Unlicensed Payments Handling

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended); cessation and divestiture orders; money-laundering criminal classification when tax evasion proceeds are mixed with payroll funds; seizure of customer payroll balances.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners and authorising compliance officers may be imprisoned from 6 months to 5 years for unlawful payment-facilitation, receive personal fines twice the laundered amount, be banned from initiating or operating a financial business, and be subject to immediate preventive travel bans in cross-border investigations.
Payroll Withholding and Social Security Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery in full of unwithheld employee taxes with 0.6% monthly interest, administrative surcharges up to 10% of the sums evaded, ordered wage garnishment on the company banking circuits, and suspension of the payroll licence registration with the Joint Social Security Centre (CCSS).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for tax withheld in fact but not remitted, and for employee social contributions; in aggravated cases directors face customs-style imprisonment of up to 5 years, restoration orders to employees, and exclusion from managing any Luxembourg payroll or HR entity.
Systematic Non-Filing / Obstruction of Assessment

Statutory sanction: Ex officio assessment on estimated profits, late-filing penalties from EUR 75 to EUR 7,500 per return, a 10% add-on to assessed CIT, monthly 0.6% interest, and an aggravated 'absence de déclaration' criminal penalty under Art. 241 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law with fines up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Statutory directors who knowingly allow persistent non-filing are jointly liable for resulting taxes, interest and penalties up to the lesser of six months' gross salary or the total deficiency; Luxembourg courts may issue a travel ban against directors in foreign-flow schemes and order restitution from the parent's group accounts.

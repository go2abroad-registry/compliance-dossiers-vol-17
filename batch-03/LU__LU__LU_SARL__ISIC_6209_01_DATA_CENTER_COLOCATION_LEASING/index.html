Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT/TVA registration; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax. Primary digital portal: MyGuichet.lu and impotsdirects.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities are not automatically registered for Luxembourg tax. For VAT/TVA, a non-established taxable person must file VAT registration form (Formulaire 5100 / registration request) with the AEDT, either directly when established in the EU or through an appointed Luxembourg-based tax representative when established outside the EU. Independent professionals with Luxembourg e-ID may file via MyGuichet.lu. The Luxembourg VAT ID is issued in the format LU followed by eight digits. A Luxembourg SARL receives a national identification number (matricule/NIF) at company incorporation from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register and is automatically registered with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. A foreign branch or permanent establishment must obtain its own NIF via the RCS and register with the ACD separately.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities are not automatically registered for Luxembourg tax. For VAT/TVA, a non-established taxable person must file VAT registration form (Formulaire 5100 / registration request) with the AEDT, either directly when established in the EU or through an appointed Luxembourg-based tax representative when established outside the EU. Independent professionals with Luxembourg e-ID may file via MyGuichet.lu. The Luxembourg VAT ID is issued in the format LU followed by eight digits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Luxembourg law provides limited elections: (i) group taxation (intégration fiscale) for resident companies that are at least 95% held by another Luxembourg resident company; (ii) VAT grouping for closely connected Luxembourg entities with financial, economic, and organisational ties; (iii) optional hybrid mismatch treatment for foreign hybrids under ATAD, including alignment of country-of-origin treatment; (iv) election for the Luxembourg permanent establishment to compute taxable profits under Luxembourg accounting rules; (v) standard EU VAT reverse-charge election for cross-border B2B supplies by non-established suppliers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover threshold for the Luxembourg small business VAT exemption (resident established persons); no registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers supplying taxable goods/services in Luxembourg; intra-Community distance-selling threshold of EUR 35,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available only to taxable persons established in Luxembourg or acting through a Luxembourg registered branch, for three categories: (i) export of goods physically leaving the EU within a maximum period of three months after dispatch, with transport verified by appropriate documentation; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State, subject to a valid EU VAT number and EC Sales List requirements; (iii) supplies of services directly connected to exported goods, or B2B services where the place of supply is located in a non-EU jurisdiction. For data-center colocation, the characterization must be evaluated: if the supply is classified as leasing of immovable property situated in Luxembourg, it remains taxable at standard rate; if the supply is classified as data-center hosting/electronically supplied services to a non-EU established customer, zero-rating may apply provided the supplier holds sufficient commercial and documentary evidence that the customer belongs outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD, ENS declaration, or the official electronic export filing accepted by Luxembourg Customs and Excise)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate - in Luxembourg practice, bank or payment-confirmation documentation (SWIFT, bank transfer, or foreign exchange record) showing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate for domestic B2B/B2C VAT in Luxembourg. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions via the PEPPOL network or a Luxembourg-compliant e-invoicing platform. Tax invoices must include all EU VAT Directive details and must be submitted on the VAT return in aggregate. VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu or the AEDT e-space; no prior digital clearance of individual invoices by the tax authority is required.

Luxembourg tax and customs authorities may require the exporter to demonstrate, within audit, that the correct documentation was obtained at the time of supply and that the goods/services physically left the EU. Failure to substantiate a zero-rated export triggers immediate VAT assessment at the standard rate (17%) plus default interest at the statutory rate, late-payment surcharges, and administrative fines up to EUR 4,000 per irregularity. In cases of knowingly false zero-rating declarations or artificial export arrangements, Luxembourg law provides criminal penalties for tax fraud, including fines up to EUR 25,000 and/or imprisonment, plus a clawback period extended to ten years. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark is applied in Luxembourg administration as a banking and audit indicator; continued non-repatriation beyond 180 days without valid justification may be treated as evidence that an export transaction was not genuinely performed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Both the current account and the capital account are liberalised under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Luxembourg law of 19 December 2003 on the free movement of capital. No BCL approval, exchange licence, or prior authorisation is required for cross-border payments, foreign direct investment, dividend repatriation, or loan repayments. The sole substantive restrictions are EU restrictive measures (financial sanctions and asset freezes) and Luxembourg implementing regulations, which typically do not apply to a compliant data centre colocation SARL. Profit remittance from the SARL is not subject to any FX permit or convertibility hurdle; the only statutory limitation is the corporate-law distributability test (the SARL must satisfy the liquidity and net-asset tests under the Law of 10 August 1915), and outbound dividends are subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where a qualifying 10% participation is held, or further reduced under applicable bilateral tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable - Luxembourg imposes no obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert export or service proceeds into euros. Receipts from colocation, rack space leasing, bare-metal server hosting, and related services may be held in EUR or any freely chosen foreign currency. The only compliance requirement is statistical: Luxembourg resident banks and the SARL's treasury operations must record cross-border payments in the BCL balance-of-payments statistical framework, with classification under the services / telecommunication, computer, and information services category (EBOPS computer services) and, where applicable, direct investment financial flows; reporting frequency is typically monthly or quarterly through the resident clearing bank, with no approval or conversion mandate attached.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) - Luxembourg's central bank and member of the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); the BCL administers the national balance-of-payments statistics gateway and collects direct investment declarations through resident credit institutions; banking and payment-institution conduct, AML/CFT supervision, and sanctions-compliance enforcement are overseen by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Luxembourg framework contains no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control agency, as monetary policy and euro liquidity are managed by the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary banking rail; MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement rail. Luxembourg FinTech or payment institutions holding a CSSF EMI licence (under the Law of 1 August 2018) and e-money institutions authorised in another EEA Member State may operate in Luxembourg under passporting, but these entities do not hold a banking licence and cannot provide BCL statistical reporting, capital-escrow custody, commercial-letter-of-credit confirmation, or full-scope FX derivatives in the way a credit institution can. A data centre colocation SARL should not rely on a non-Luxembourg EMI (e.g., UK Finance, Revolut Business, or Airwallex) as the principal operating bank because of correspondent-bank restrictions and because the CSSF and the RBE framework require a clear, regulated local banking nexus for customer security deposits and lease-invoice collections. Digital EMIs can, however, serve as an execution-only rail for small vendor payments overseas once the main current account with a tier-1 bank is opened.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the LU_SARL must execute in the following compliant sequence: (1) the SARL convenes and passes a shareholders' resolution approving the capital increase and, if applicable, a non-monetary contribution; (2) a Luxembourg notary authenticates the capital-subscription deed (acte authentique d'apport en capital) and completes the RCS filing; (3) the parent or investor remits the funds by SWIFT wire (MT103) or, for EUR within the SEPA zone, by SEPA Credit Transfer from a corporate account; the wire's purpose text must state 'Subscription of Share Capital / Capital Injection' and the SARL's RCS number; (4) the Luxembourg bank records the BCL direct-investment statistical declaration using the inward direct investment category and the underlying enterprise code; (5) upon receipt, the bank credits the escrowed capital account or designated operational account, and the SARL simultaneously updates the RBE and maintains an audit trail from the notarial deed to the bank confirmation. The funds must be verifiable as originating from the known UBO's or the documented institutional investor's own account; third-party payer financing or anonymous shareholder loans should be avoided because they create a high likelihood of enhanced due diligence requests.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary banking rail; MEDIUM as a supplementary disbursement rail. Luxembourg FinTech or payment institutions holding a CSSF EMI licence (under the Law of 1 August 2018) and e-money institutions authorised in another EEA Member State may operate in Luxembourg under passporting, but these entities do not hold a banking licence and cannot provide BCL statistical reporting, capital-escrow custody, commercial-letter-of-credit confirmation, or full-scope FX derivatives in the way a credit institution can. A data centre colocation SARL should not rely on a non-Luxembourg EMI (e.g., UK Finance, Revolut Business, or Airwallex) as the principal operating bank because of correspondent-bank restrictions and because the CSSF and the RBE framework require a clear, regulated local banking nexus for customer security deposits and lease-invoice collections. Digital EMIs can, however, serve as an execution-only rail for small vendor payments overseas once the main current account with a tier-1 bank is opened.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if group consolidated turnover ≥ €50 million and entity turnover ≥ €1 million; CbC report required if group consolidated revenue ≥ €750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in geographically mobile activities; data center colocation is considered a high-risk activity requiring adequate premises, staff, and expenditure in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional 10% penalty on evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes and face travel bans if leaving the country with unpaid liabilities.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, retroactive tax assessment, and penalty of €10,000 to €100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate tax debts if they knowingly operated a shell entity; potential criminal charges for fraud.
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €1 million for legal entities; suspension of business license.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering facilitation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 10% of the adjustment, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they approved non-compliant pricing; potential criminal liability for tax evasion.

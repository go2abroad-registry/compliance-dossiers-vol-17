Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the MyGuichet.lu electronic portal.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as consolidated, particularly Articles 182-1 to 182-90 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and annual accounts; Law of 13 January 2019 on the Register of Beneficial Owners; Law of 12 November 2004 on anti-money laundering, as amended.

Formation / constitutional filings

Authentic incorporation deed executed by a Luxembourg notary, containing the Articles of Association (Acte de Société / Statuts), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 37 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum issued and fully subscribed capital is EUR 12,000 (Article 182-1). At incorporation each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value; the statutory capital clearance is evidenced by a certificate ('attestation de capital') issued by a Luxembourg credit institution. For SPAC structures, the offer proceeds are separately segregated under a blocked/escrow trust account with a regulated depositary bank, pursuant to the listing prospectus approved by the CSSF.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Corps Grand-Ducal d'Incendie et de Secours (CGDIS) confirming adequate alarms, extinguishers, and evacuation routes; structural load inspection and workplace clearance carried out by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering electrical safety, floor-load capacity, and first-aid provisions. The registered office may not be occupied until the ITM visual clearance and the fire brigade certification are filed, and must remain compliant with the Law of 17 December 2021 on occupational safety at work.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum issued and fully subscribed capital is EUR 12,000 (Article 182-1). At incorporation each share must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value; the statutory capital clearance is evidenced by a certificate ('attestation de capital') issued by a Luxembourg credit institution. For SPAC structures, the offer proceeds are separately segregated under a blocked/escrow trust account with a regulated depositary bank, pursuant to the listing prospectus approved by the CSSF.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes (corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; official portals: MyGuichet.lu and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS registration.

E-government filing portal

Incorporation is effected by notarial deed filed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS); the RCS number and the national identification number (matricule) serve as the master corporate IDs for all tax filings. For VAT, the company files Form 3409 with the AEDT to obtain a VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits); non-EU-established foreign suppliers with Luxembourg taxable transactions must register through a Luxembourg VAT representative. The ACD assigns the direct-tax reference automatically upon first corporate income tax return filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A Societe a Responsabilite Limitee (SARL) is a Luxembourg tax-resident company with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income and no pass-through eligibility. It is subject to corporate income tax (national rate 17% plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax, combined effective rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City), net wealth tax (0.5% on net assets exceeding EUR 500,000) and registration duties. The participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) applies to qualifying shareholdings. No special SPAC tax regime exists; a SPAC SARL is taxed as an ordinary company, while issuance of shares and escrow/trust functions are VAT-exempt financial transactions. — Incorporation is effected by notarial deed filed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS); the RCS number and the national identification number (matricule) serve as the master corporate IDs for all tax filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Societe a Responsabilite Limitee (SARL) is a Luxembourg tax-resident company with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income and no pass-through eligibility. It is subject to corporate income tax (national rate 17% plus 7% solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax, combined effective rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City), net wealth tax (0.5% on net assets exceeding EUR 500,000) and registration duties. The participation exemption (Article 166 LIR) applies to qualifying shareholdings. No special SPAC tax regime exists; a SPAC SARL is taxed as an ordinary company, while issuance of shares and escrow/trust functions are VAT-exempt financial transactions.

Available election forms

No Check-the-Box elections. Luxembourg elections: (i) fiscal unity (integration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for groups holding at least 95% of the subsidiary, filed with the ACD (subject to ongoing tax reform); (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) for Luxembourg-established persons closely bound by financial, economic and organisational links; (iii) application of the IP box 80% exemption on qualifying IP income (Article 50bis LIR), supported by nexus documentation; (iv) ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch filing and symmetrical-characterisation documentation for hybrid instruments and hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-established foreign suppliers: any taxable supply in Luxembourg triggers VAT registration. Established Luxembourg small enterprises may claim the Article 57 exemption for annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 40 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, provided the supplier obtains proof of export (customs export declaration and transport evidence) within 90 days of supply. Transport and ancillary services connected to the exported goods are likewise zero-rated under Article 146(1)(c) of Directive 2006/112/EC. B2B services supplied to customers established outside the EU fall outside Luxembourg VAT scope under the place-of-supply rule in Article 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC, which is economically equivalent to zero-rating. For a SPAC SARL, issuance of shares and escrow, custody and trust-management services are VAT-exempt financial transactions under Article 135 of Directive 2006/112/EC (no input VAT recovery) and remain outside the scope where supplied to non-EU counterparties. The burden of proof rests on the taxable person: the exporter must substantiate physical export, the non-EU establishment of the recipient and the direct link between the service and the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) electronically submitted and discharged via PLDA, including the EU exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or bank statement) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Transport contract, freight and cargo insurance certificate
  • Evidence of the non-EU establishment of the customer (foreign VAT ID or commercial register extract)

No general B2B real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital stamping regime as of 2024. Since 1 January 2022, invoices issued to the Luxembourg State administration are mandatorily transmitted through the PEPPOL network (Grand-Ducal Regulation of 14 December 2020). VAT returns are filed electronically via the AEDT e-filing portal; customs declarations are filed through the PLDA customs platform. EU-wide ViDA digital reporting and e-invoicing will apply once transposed, with expected application from 2030.

The AEDT may claw back export zero-rating and recharacterize the supply as domestic taxable if proof of export is missing, assessing 17% VAT, denying input VAT deduction and recovering refunds with late-payment interest at 0.6% per month. The reassessment period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or absence of returns. Administrative fines and criminal sanctions (including fines up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment) may be imposed on the company, its directors or, for non-EU suppliers, the Luxembourg VAT representative, who is jointly and severally liable. Although Luxembourg law does not impose a statutory FX-repatriation deadline, proceeds retained outside Luxembourg beyond the 180-day benchmark without adequate justification attract transfer-pricing scrutiny and possible income recharacterization.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area Member State; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account by virtue of the free movement of capital enshrined in Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No exchange control approval, licence, or administrative authorization is required for inbound equity injections, SPAC IPO proceeds, PIPE investments, profit distributions, or outbound acquisition consideration. The only practical restrictions arise from EU/UN sanctions regimes, the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive (FATF-aligned), and CSSF AML/CFT obligations. Distributions (dividends or redemption proceeds) from the Luxembourg SARL remain subject to the annual statutory audit, the net-assets balance-sheet test under the Luxembourg law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies (as amended), and the applicable withholding tax framework; none of these create FX convertibility or capital repatriation hurdles beyond standard corporate law formality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline. As a euro-area jurisdiction, the BCL does not operate a surrender or allocation regime for trade receipts. The only related compliance duty is statistical reporting: resident non-financial corporations must report direct investment positions and cross-border payments to the BCL for euro-area balance-of-payments (BoP) statistics purposes, with periodic filing thresholds; failure to file carries administrative fines but does not constrain the free disposition of FX proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — Central Bank of Luxembourg, operating under the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), in regulatory coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for statutory escrow functions, Medium for ancillary operational expense accounts. E-money institutions (EMIs) authorised under PSD2 — including licensed EU fintechs such as Revolut, Wise, or Qonto — may lawfully provide payment accounts, FX conversion, and mass-payment rails, but they cannot provide fiduciary trust, interest-bearing segregated capital escrow, or securities-custody services required for SPAC proceeds. Luxembourg SPAC practice and the CSSF's interpretation of the banking monopoly require the offering proceeds to be held by a credit institution (authorised under Part I of the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector) or by a regulated escrow agent. Offshore, non-EU digital EMIs without an EU passport are effectively unusable for any corporate account in Luxembourg and present a settlement and nexus risk; a foreign fintech can only be used for low-value pre-IPO operating disbursements, never as the principal liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For SPAC IPO proceeds: the public offering trust account (typically at a New York clearing bank or a US custodian) is released via Fedwire to the Luxembourg SARL's operational account at a Luxembourg credit institution, delivered as SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for the interbank leg) with the European BoP purpose code 'SC' (capital participation / equity securities), referencing the CSSF-approved prospectus and the escrow agreement in field 70. For PIPE placements and sponsor equity injections: standard MT103 credit transfers from the investor's bank with purpose code 'SD' or 'SC', the relevant economic classification, and the share-purchase agreement as reference. The receiving Luxembourg bank verifies the remitter's identity against the UBO list, matches fields 50 and 70 to the board-approved capital schedule, and for amounts above the CSSF threshold completes the BCL statistical reporting and the enhanced due-diligence file before crediting; all flows must be economically coherent with the disclosed SPAC offering memorandum.Business-combination consideration is settled via SWIFT MT103 (individual customer wires) or MT202 COV (bank-to-bank cover) over TARGET2 for EUR leg and over the USD correspondent banking network (Fedwire/CHIPS) for USD; where the target acquisition is structured as a share or asset purchase with deferred payment, payment is routed against delivery of transfer documents under a funded escrow instruction. For working-capital and trade-related disbursements, the SPAC vehicle may draw on a commercial letter of credit (documentary LC subject to ICC UCP600) negotiated by the target; the settling bank debits the Luxembourg operational account or the escrow sub-account after presentment of compliant documents. Invested escrow proceeds are also returned via MT103 to redeeming shareholders with the code 'P01' (pension/redemption — distributions), alongside the statutory notice and market-disclosure filings; all outbound wires are automatically matched to the approved board minutes and the escrow release condition certificate.Outright EUR/USD FX forwards (plain vanilla deliverable forwards), with tenors matched to expected acquisition-consideration and redemption-exposure datesFX options on EUR/USD (call or put structures), with option-premium funding from the operational cash balanceCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD CCS) for long-dated matching of USD-denominated target debt or deferred-purchase obligationsFX swaps (swap of principal and interest) for short-term liquidity management between the EUR operational account and USD escrow reinvestment positionsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to settlement currencies not freely convertible (e.g., exotic emerging-market currencies of certain targets), where a deliverable forward is legally or practically unavailablePermitted only with the two-way dealing counterparties of the Tier-1 Luxembourg bank or its parent group; prohibited: speculative options without an underlying exposure, leveraged derivatives, and any instrument causing the SPAC to deviate from its disclosed investment policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for statutory escrow functions, Medium for ancillary operational expense accounts. E-money institutions (EMIs) authorised under PSD2 — including licensed EU fintechs such as Revolut, Wise, or Qonto — may lawfully provide payment accounts, FX conversion, and mass-payment rails, but they cannot provide fiduciary trust, interest-bearing segregated capital escrow, or securities-custody services required for SPAC proceeds. Luxembourg SPAC practice and the CSSF's interpretation of the banking monopoly require the offering proceeds to be held by a credit institution (authorised under Part I of the Luxembourg law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector) or by a regulated escrow agent. Offshore, non-EU digital EMIs without an EU passport are effectively unusable for any corporate account in Luxembourg and present a settlement and nexus risk; a foreign fintech can only be used for low-value pre-IPO operating disbursements, never as the principal liquidity rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% national corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) since 1 January 2023; aggregate rate in Luxembourg City = 22.80% (15% IRC + 1.05% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% communal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends (précompte mobilier); 0% for EU/EEA parent companies satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding, 12-month holding, no anti-abuse disqualification); treaty rates typically 0-15% depending on the residence and holding structure

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File (plus CbC report) apply where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (EU Directive 2016/881, Luxembourg law of 30 July 2021); below the threshold no automatic filing is prescribed, but the taxpayer must produce a contemporaneous TP file within 30 days of an ACD request and bear the burden of proving arm's length pricing (Art. 56 LIR)

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies an OECD-aligned substance-over-form doctrine via the fraus legis jurisprudence, the statutory GAAR (Art. 6 LIR transposing ATAD), and the anti-abuse clauses of the participation exemption; a SPAC that behaves as a passive shell — no office, no employees, no local decision-making, no substance commensurate with its activity — risks denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of its income, and treatment as an abusive conduit under EU law

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — deliberately false or incomplete tax filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud: imprisonment of up to 5 years, fine of up to EUR 50,000, mandatory publication of the judgment, and reassessment of all evaded taxes plus 0.8% monthly late interest and a 10% assessment surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt in case of gross negligence or deliberate fraud; travel bans and surrender of passports may be ordered; imprisonment is possible for the directing mind
Shell Company / Conduit — economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the entity as fiscally transparent or as an abusive conduit; denial of the participation exemption and treaty withholding relief; retroactive taxation of the full capital gain at 22.80%–24.94%; interest and penalties

Director personal liability: Luxembourg courts may pierce the corporate veil (Art. 585-7 law of 10 August 1915) where the entity is fictitious or assets are confused; directors may be ordered to personally restore the company's assets and may be barred from managing companies for up to ten years
Anti-Money Laundering — unexplained third-party funds in escrow

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 2,500,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Law of 10 August 2018 on AML/CFT; freezing of the escrow account; criminal fines and up to 5 years imprisonment for laundering

Director personal liability: The 'director and compliance officer' is personally liable for AML failures; Luxembourg courts may issue travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification orders; civil damages are available to investors and creditors
Transfer Pricing / Loss Trafficking — abusive intragroup arrangements

Statutory sanction: Art. 56 LIR upward adjustment of income, disallowance of the deductions, 0.8% monthly late interest, 10% assessment surcharge, and criminal fraud penalties where concealment is proven

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable in insolvency proceedings (action en comblement de passif) for reckless management that caused the tax claim; travel bans may be ordered in cases of cross-border concealment

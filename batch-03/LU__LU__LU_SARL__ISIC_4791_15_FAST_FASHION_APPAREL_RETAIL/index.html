Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), 15 Boulevard Joseph II, L-1840 Luxembourg

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Articles 180-192 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée 'SARL'); Law of 23 December 2016 on minimum share capital and SME amendments; Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; Law of 13 January 2019 establishing the Luxembourg Business Registers and the RESA electronic gazette.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Deed of Incorporation (Acte de Constitution) containing the coordinated Articles of Association (Statuts Coordonnés), executed by a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically with the RCS.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 33 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The minimum share capital of a SARL is EUR 12,000 (Law of 10 August 1915 as amended in 2016). At least 25% of each cash share (minimum EUR 3,000 in aggregate) must be paid up at incorporation, while in-kind contributions must be fully paid up; the subscribed cash capital must be blocked on a dedicated account with a Luxembourg credit institution pending RCS registration, evidenced by a capital blocking certificate. Alternatively, under the Law of 23 December 2016, a SARL may be formed with fully paid-up cash capital between EUR 1 and EUR 12,000, subject to a statutory auditor's report and certain restrictions on share transfers and distributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued by the competent municipal authority is required for staffed premises such as offices and warehouses; the Groupement d'Intervention du Service National de la Protection Civile (CGDIS) conducts technical inspections for higher-risk sites. Occupational clearance: comply with the Luxembourg Labour Code and the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) standards covering work equipment, electrical installations, ergonomics, emergency exits and first-aid provisions. A 'déclaration de mise en exploitation' must be filed with the municipal authority before occupying any warehousing or office space.

Paid-in capital rule: The minimum share capital of a SARL is EUR 12,000 (Law of 10 August 1915 as amended in 2016). At least 25% of each cash share (minimum EUR 3,000 in aggregate) must be paid up at incorporation, while in-kind contributions must be fully paid up; the subscribed cash capital must be blocked on a dedicated account with a Luxembourg credit institution pending RCS registration, evidenced by a capital blocking certificate. Alternatively, under the Law of 23 December 2016, a SARL may be formed with fully paid-up cash capital between EUR 1 and EUR 12,000, subject to a statutory auditor's report and certain restrictions on share transfers and distributions.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - under the Law of 2 September 2011 on access to occupations, the electronic retailing of fast-fashion apparel and footwear (ISIC 4791) does not appear on the list of regulated trades requiring an establishment authorization from the Direction Générale des PME of the Ministry of the Economy. A physical retail store, if subsequently opened, would be subject to municipal commercial occupation rules.

Category: Not applicable - no industrial operating permit (autorisation d'établissement) is required for a pure e-commerce apparel retailer operating solely through a warehouse/fulfillment arrangement and online storefront.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; unified e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the AEDT business portal (https://pbl.etat.lu).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCSL), the RCSL automatically notifies the ACD for corporate income tax and municipal business tax registration. For VAT, the SARL (or a foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg) must file Form 640 'Déclaration d'identification à la TVA' with the AEDT, either via MyGuichet.lu electronic filing or in writing, before commencing taxable activity. The application requires: a copy of the articles of association (statuts) and RCSL extract, proof of the managing director's identity, a signed power of attorney, and a description of taxable activities. Non-established foreign e-commerce sellers without a Luxembourg fixed establishment may alternatively register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-EU distance sales rather than obtaining a full Luxembourg VAT ID; however, sales from local stock (fulfilment warehouse in Luxembourg) always require direct VAT registration. Standard processing time is 10-15 business days. VAT number format: LU + 8 digits for legal entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies (RCSL), the RCSL automatically notifies the ACD for corporate income tax and municipal business tax registration. For VAT, the SARL (or a foreign entity making taxable supplies in Luxembourg) must file Form 640 'Déclaration d'identification à la TVA' with the AEDT, either via MyGuichet.lu electronic filing or in writing, before commencing taxable activity. The application requires: a copy of the articles of association (statuts) and RCSL extract, proof of the managing director's identity, a signed power of attorney, and a description of taxable activities. Non-established foreign e-commerce sellers without a Luxembourg fixed establishment may alternatively register under the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-EU distance sales rather than obtaining a full Luxembourg VAT ID; however, sales from local stock (fulfilment warehouse in Luxembourg) always require direct VAT registration. Standard processing time is 10-15 business days. VAT number format: LU + 8 digits for legal entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; SARLs are inherently opaque and subject to full corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT). Available statutory elections: (i) Luxembourg VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 60 of the VAT Law allowing financially, economically, and organisationally linked resident entities to file VAT as a single taxpayer; (ii) Luxembourg tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) for direct taxation of 95%-owned resident or EU subsidiaries; (iii) EU OSS non-Union scheme election for intra-EU distance sales of fast-fashion goods; (iv) option to apply Luxembourg VAT to certain real estate transactions; (v) election for the small-business franchise exemption (franchise en base) for turnover below EUR 35,000, subject to a waiver of input VAT deduction. Hybrid mismatch elections follow ATAD I and II implementation in the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR Articles 168bis and 168ter).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small businesses (franchise en base); EUR 10,000 pan-EU threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers (below the threshold Luxembourg VAT at 17% applies; above the threshold the destination country's VAT applies via the OSS); no registration threshold for foreign suppliers operating from a Luxembourg warehouse or performing supplies in Luxembourg - they must register from the first euro of taxable sales in the absence of an applicable exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to: (i) exports of fast-fashion apparel and footwear dispatched or transported by the seller or on his behalf to a destination outside the European Union, evidenced by a customs export declaration (SAD) and proof that the goods left the EU within 90 days of supply; (ii) directly connected services such as international transport, loading, warehousing, and freight forwarding ancillary to those exports; (iii) supplies to non-EU customers of goods exported under the 'exporter's possession' rule provided the exporter is established at the EU, holds title, and retains full documentation per the Luxembourg VAT Circular N°703. Qualification requires that the supplier retain the original export invoice, the CMR/air waybill/Bill of Lading, the customs entry certificate, and a bank transfer or payment receipt demonstrating payment from the non-EU principal. For distance sales to EU consumers, standard destination-country VAT via OSS (or 17% Luxembourg VAT below the threshold) applies; goods imported into third countries (e.g., UK, USA) require VAT/customs duties of that jurisdiction and are not zero-rated under Luxembourg rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document (SAD) or automated export e-entry filed in the Luxembourg customs system (LuxTrade/ICS-ECS)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport out of the EU within 90 days (Circular TVA N°703/704)
  • Commercial invoice and proforma invoice matching quantities, values, HS codes, and consignee details
  • Certificate of Export (certificat d'exportation) issued/proof of customs exit from the competent customs office
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT and bank statement evidencing payment from the non-EU buyer; foreign exchange conversion records when payment is in a non-EUR currency

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists for B2B/B2C e-commerce invoicing. Luxembourg mandates B2G e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS 3.0) for invoices to central government entities since 1 January 2021, with extension to municipal and wider public sector bodies under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. There are no API-based tax authority clearance requirements, no SAF-T periodic filing mandate (SAF-T only on request), and no mandatory digital invoice stamping in Luxembourg. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (Intrastat/EC Sales Lists) are filed electronically via the AEDT e-TVA/MDG portal. XML/UBL e-invoicing for B2B remains voluntary, but the VIDA (VAT in the Digital Age) proposal may introduce structured e-invoicing by 2028.

Luxembourg AEDT and customs conduct targeted audits of cross-border fast-fashion e-commerce models. Zero-rating is invalidated retroactively if documentary proof is incomplete or if the goods are diverted to EU consumption; the VAT due at 17% is clawed back with default interest at 0.6% per month. Non-fraudulent documentary errors attract administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 and a 10% late-payment penalty. Fraudulently claimed export exemptions (false invoicing, merchandise circulation schemes) are subject to criminal sanctions: VAT fraud fines of up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, publication of judgments, exclusion from public contracts, and personal joint liability of directors; the statutory reassessment period is 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of tax fraud or wilful misdeclaration. Repatriation delay beyond 180 days without commercial justification is treated by tax audit as a permanent establishment indicator and may trigger transfer pricing adjustments under the Luxembourg TP Guidelines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full and unrestricted euro convertibility on both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the free movement of capital and payments regime, transposed into Luxembourg law without exchange-control authorizations. Retail e-commerce receipts, trade settlements, shareholder loans, and capital injections may be freely converted and remitted. No profit-repatriation licensing or administrative approval is required; the only prudential hurdles are the bank's transaction documentation standards under CSSF AML regulations and, for dividend distributions, the applicable dividend withholding tax (standard 15%, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of share capital for an uninterrupted 12-month period).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or timing requirement applies to export proceeds in Luxembourg. Receivables from cross-border fast-fashion sales may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency in an account at a Luxembourg or EEA credit institution, subject to the bank's balance-of-payment statistical declarations and applicable EU sanctions and asset-freeze screening obligations under Council Regulation (EU) No 765/2006 and Luxembourg implementing measures.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem, and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent authority for banking licensing, payment institutions, electronic money institutions, and AML/CTF supervision. The BCL compiles national balance-of-payments statistics and transmits monetary and financial data to the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for payment execution, multi-currency settlement, and API-driven FX interfaces, but Low for core treasury liquidity because balances at EMIs are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee scheme. An electronic money institution or payment institution authorized in an EEA state and passported into Luxembourg under the Luxembourg Law of 10 July 2018 implementing PSD2 may lawfully provide IBANs, cross-border collections, and FX conversion; however, the SARL should maintain the primary operating treasury and capital escrow at a Tier-1 commercial bank, using EMI rails only for subordinated disbursement workstreams.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders must wire the SARL's share capital, being at least EUR 12,000 for a standard SARL, by SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the dedicated capital escrow account at the onboarding bank, citing SWIFT purpose code EQUI (equity) or INTR (intra-group transfer). The bank will evidence receipt through a confirmation statement used by the notary in the incorporation file. For shareholder loans exceeding the equity injection, a written shareholder loan agreement and a board resolution authorizing the debt are required; the receiving bank will classify the transaction as a direct investment or intercompany loan for BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting, ensuring full compliance with EU capital movement free-flow provisions.For EEA and Eurozone suppliers, the clearing rail is SEPA Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 XML (pain.001.001.09) with the SARL's IBAN and the supplier's IBAN, avoiding FX conversion and SWIFT fees. For non-EEA apparel and logistics providers in Asia or the Americas, the rail is SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank telegraphic transfer in EUR, USD, GBP, or CNY, quoting the commercial invoice and order number in the payment narrative. For high-value garment purchase contracts, an irrevocable, confirmed documentary letter of credit (MT700) is recommended, issued by the SARL's Luxembourg bank in favor of the supplier's confirming bank, with the L/C, invoices, booking notes, and certificates of origin aligned under Incoterms 2020.Vanilla deliverable FX forward (EUR against USD, GBP, CNY, or CHF) executed bilaterally with a Luxembourg-licensed credit institution or a CSSF-registered investment firm under ISDA and EMIR clearing protocols.Non-deliverable FX forward (NDF) for hedging purchases denominated in restricted or non-convertible currencies such as INR, BDT, PKR, or VND, where the underlying exposure is a documented firm purchase order and the tenor matches the forecast payable.Cross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) for medium-term shareholder loans or asset-based financing, converting floating-rate foreign currency debt into EUR fixed-rate exposure.FX swap / spot-forward rollover for operational liquidity bridging between the Schengen treasury account and the offshore or foreign-currency settlement accounts, subject to the bank's conduct controls and without speculative open positions.

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg; electronic filing via lbr.lu and guichet.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, SARL provisions under Title V (Articles 179 to 210); Grand-Ducal Regulations on the RCS and electronic publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif de société) containing the Articles of Association (Statuts), accompanied by the RCS incorporation application form (Form C1).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

€12,000 minimum share capital for a standard SARL; cash subscriptions must be at least 25% paid up at incorporation (€3,000 minimum), fully subscribed in the articles; all cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the société en formation and released only upon RCS registration; any unpaid balance must be payable on demand of the management.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (attestation incendie) issued by the competent fire brigade for any building open to the public or used as storage; compliance with ITM (Inspection du Travail et des Mines) workplace safety rules; structural load inspection by a licensed engineer if heavy audio equipment racks are installed; an employer's occupational health risk assessment (POEL) must be completed for all employees and the workplace should maintain a registre de sécurité as required by law.

Paid-in capital rule: €12,000 minimum share capital for a standard SARL; cash subscriptions must be at least 25% paid up at incorporation (€3,000 minimum), fully subscribed in the articles; all cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the société en formation and released only upon RCS registration; any unpaid balance must be payable on demand of the management.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — direct corporate income tax and national tax number; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — VAT registration and administration. Primary portals: myGuichet.lu / MyTVA; official information portals: impotsdirects.public.lu and aedt.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL, incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) produces an RCS registration number, and the ACD allocates a direct-tax identification number (matricule fiscale). For VAT, the entity must file a separate Luxembourg VAT registration application with the AEDT through myGuichet.lu, attaching the RCS extract, articles of association, proof of Luxembourg business address, identification of managers, and a description of intended taxable activities. A foreign non-EU entity without Luxembourg establishment must appoint an AEDT-approved Luxembourg tax representative; an EU-established taxable person may register directly. The AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number bearing the 'LU' prefix, which must be obtained before invoicing for taxable Luxembourg supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (the resident SARL is treated as a separate Luxembourg corporate tax subject; it is taxable at entity level on worldwide income, with treaty-based and statutory territorial exemptions for eligible foreign permanent establishments and qualifying participation income under the Luxembourg participation exemption regime; no pass-through or check-the-box treatment is available). — For a Luxembourg SARL, incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) produces an RCS registration number, and the ACD allocates a direct-tax identification number (matricule fiscale). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the resident SARL is treated as a separate Luxembourg corporate tax subject; it is taxable at entity level on worldwide income, with treaty-based and statutory territorial exemptions for eligible foreign permanent establishments and qualifying participation income under the Luxembourg participation exemption regime; no pass-through or check-the-box treatment is available).

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election regime. The principal statutory elections are: (1) fiscal unity ('intégration fiscale') for corporate income tax and municipal business tax grouping among Luxembourg-resident companies satisfying the 95% capital/control thresholds; (2) VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') election for closely linked taxable persons; and (3) participation exemption election for qualifying EU and non-EU shareholdings subject to conditions. Hybrid mismatch reclassification elections are prohibited or constrained by ATAD transposition and Luxembourg domestic anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (Luxembourg small-enterprise exemption threshold on annual taxable turnover excluding VAT; registration is mandatory when turnover exceeds this ceiling. For intra-EU distance sales to consumers, the EU-wide OSS threshold is EUR 10,000, above which VAT must be accounted for in the destination Member State through the OSS scheme, independent of Luxembourg domestic turnover).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of musical instruments and studio audio gear qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are supplied to a non-EU destination and the SARL, a customer, or a third party acting on the SARL's behalf removes the goods physically outside the European Union within the statutory export period. Eligible ancillary services (e.g., transport handling, loading, freight, and intermediary services directly linked to the export) may also be zero-rated. Intra-EU distance sales to final consumers do not qualify for export zero-rating; they are taxable in the destination Member State under OSS/IOSS. The SARL must retain full customs and transport evidence proving physical export before applying the 0% rate.

  • Customs export declaration (EX/A) accepted by Luxembourg customs authorities
  • Bill of lading / airway bill / CMR consignment note
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank/payment evidence showing receipt of consideration from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Customs clearance and transport documentation demonstrating physical removal from the EU
  • Export ledger / VAT export register reconciling each invoice to the corresponding customs declaration

Luxembourg currently has no general real-time fiscal clearance regime, continuous transaction control API obligation, or mandatory digital stamping for standard B2B VAT invoices. Luxembourg has announced a phased transition towards EU-compliant B2B e-invoicing and electronic reporting, expected to become mandatory in alignment with EU legislative efforts (ViDA/SIMPL) likely from or after 2026. Until such mandate is in force, VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through MyTVA, SAF-T-compatible records must be maintained for tax audit purposes, and cross-border payment data must be reported to CESOP by payment service providers.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs scrutinize export zero-rating claims strictly. If export or proof-of-receipt conditions are not substantiated, AEDT may reassess VAT at 17%, impose late-payment interest and administrative fines, and claw back any input VAT recovered in respect of the export. In cases of fraudulent zero-rating or wilful misrepresentation, criminal proceedings may result in significant fines and imprisonment, and retroactive tax reassessments may extend to the full limitation period. All export documentation, payment evidence, and FX repatriation records must be retained for at least six years, or longer if an investigation or litigation is pending.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg for both current and capital account transactions. The TFEU free movement of capital (Article 63) and the Law of 5 April 1993 impose no exchange controls, no prior licensing, and no mandatory conversion for trade settlements, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or capital injections. Profit remittance from a Luxembourg SARL is treated as a dividend subject to 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) when the corporate beneficiary holds, for an uninterrupted 12-month period, at least 10% of the capital, or reduced under applicable double-tax treaties (commonly 5–15%). The only tax-law hurdle is the balance-sheet test for distributions: the SARL may distribute only if net assets exceed the statutory capital plus non-distributable reserves. The only administrative friction is the paying bank's KYC documentation and the BCL's balance-of-payments statistical reporting for direct investment and treasury flows; these are declarative, not consent-based controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — Luxembourg maintains full freedom of payment and no surrender rule. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export sales proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio (0% compulsory conversion; 100% may be freely retained). Receivables from the online sale of musical instruments and studio audio gear may be held in EUR, USD, CNY, GBP, or any convertible currency in Luxembourg or foreign accounts, provided that intra-group arrangements respect the arm's-length transfer-pricing standard, monetary items are translated at closing rates for statutory accounts (unrealised FX gains/losses are recognised in the profit and loss account), and cross-border flows are declared to the BCL for statistical purposes where thresholds apply. No exporter FX card and no Central Bank approval is required for retention or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential supervision and licensing of credit institutions and e-money institutions in Luxembourg are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) under the Luxembourg Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overall feasibility rating: High as an ancillary collection/payment rail; Low-Medium as the sole primary banking layer. PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie S.C.A. is a Luxembourg-regulated credit institution under CSSF supervision and is therefore fully acceptable for the SARL's merchant settlement. Stripe is established as an Irish EMI passporting into the EEA under the E-Money Directive and PSD2, and is lawful where the settlement liquidity is swept into the SARL's own bank account. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident UBO with no EU tax residency or thin economic substance in Luxembourg

Root cause: Non-resident UBO with no EU tax residency or thin economic substance in Luxembourg

Defense evidence: Apostilled passport copies of all UBOs and directors; tax residency certificates from the home tax authority; sworn declaration of source of wealth and source of funds supported by bank statements, asset-sale agreements and salary records; complete corporate ownership chart identifying every intermediary; notarised board resolutions of the SARL; professional reference letter from the UBO's home bank; commitment to maintain Luxembourg substance (registered office, local gérant, VAT and CIT filings, annual general meeting).
High-volume, low-ticket online retail flow through Stripe/PayPal without documentary trail

Root cause: High-volume, low-ticket online retail flow through Stripe/PayPal without documentary trail

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard compliant sequence: (1) the shareholder/gérant executes the notarial draft deed; (2) the bank opens a blocked account in the name of the future SARL; (3) the remitter transmits a SWIFT MT103 with reference 'Souscription au capital — SARL [name] — RCS [future registration no.]' and completes the bank's source-of-funds questionnaire; the remitter must be the UBO or an identifiable shareholder, never an anonymous third party; (4) the bank issues an attestation de blocage confirming receipt of the minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000 (statutorily at least 25% paid-up; market practice blocks the full amount); (5) the notary files the incorporation at the RCS Luxembourg; (6) after publication in the RESA, the block is lifted and the funds are swept into the operational EUR account. For shareholder loans, the wire reference must state 'Advance / shareholder loan — board approved' with a signed loan agreement and an arm's-length interest rate to prevent reclassification as hidden equity.Treasury clearing rails by payment class: (i) EU/SEPA supplier invoices — SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT or SEPA Instant) in EUR with the invoice number and reverse-charge reference in the structured creditor identifier; (ii) non-EUR or third-country suppliers — SWIFT MT103 (single customer payment) or MT202 COV (cover payment) with purpose code 'Trade payment for musical instruments and audio gear', Incoterms and customs entry numbers; (iii) high-value or documentary-risk procurement (e.g., Asian OEM components) — irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 with confirmation by a top-rated correspondent bank and an ICC-compliant document set (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin); (iv) receivables settlement — Stripe and PayPal payout wires (SCT or SWIFT) are credited daily to the operational account and swept via zero-balance account (ZBA) into a treasury term deposit; (v) intra-group supplier clearing — Luxembourg cash pooling and netting with signed intercompany agreements and transfer-pricing documentation. Legal constraints include EMI safeguarding and client-asset segregation, prohibition of lending and investment services, CSSF notification/registration for payment services provided in Luxembourg, and the absence of overdraft or credit facilities; the SARL's statutory capital escrow and primary treasury must therefore remain with a full credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: EUR 350,000,000 consolidated group revenue; Local File: cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per category of transactions (goods, services, IP).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies ATAD-aligned anti-abuse rules and the OECD BEPS Action 13 substance expectations. A SARL active in online retail (ISIC 4791) must demonstrate adequate business substance: physical premises, employees actively managing daily sales, bank accounts, books and records, board decision-making, and operational assets. Entities exploiting preferential regains such as the IP box must satisfy the nexus approach based on qualifying R&D expenditure. Failure to meet substance may result in treaty-shopping challenges, GAAR recharacterisation, and disregard of the legal entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; administrative penalties include a 20% surcharge on the evaded tax plus monthly 0.8% interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes evaded when fraudulent conduct is proven; civil restitution of wrongly distributed amounts, travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment are possible.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments increasing the Luxembourg taxable base; late-payment interest at 0.8% per month; a default penalty of up to 20% of the additional tax; and imposition of higher prevailing rates in the absence of compliant documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL may be held civilly liable for the resulting underpayment if gross negligence is established; disqualification from management may follow in egregious cases.
Hidden Profit Distribution / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of disguised amounts as constructive dividends; levy of 15% withholding tax plus interest; 20% administrative surcharge; denial of deductible expenses for the hidden distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; personal fines apply; repayments of improperly distributed monies may be ordered.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax residency may be challenged; the General Anti-Abuse Rule may recharacterise the structure, deny treaty protection, impose full Luxembourg taxation on global profits, and trigger penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing is available on proof of abuse of law; directors face civil restitution for distributions received with knowledge, travel bans in fraud proceedings, and personal criminal liability for tax sham.
Failure to File / Record-Keeping Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 125 for the return; mandatory surcharge of up to 20% of the assessed tax; 0.8% monthly arrears interest; inability to claim loss carryforwards or treaty credits until compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for the fines and interest if the omission is intentional; repeated violations result in criminal liability and possible imprisonment.

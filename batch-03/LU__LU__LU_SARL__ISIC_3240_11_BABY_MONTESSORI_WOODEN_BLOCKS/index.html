Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT/TVA; centralised digital access via MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment must submit VAT registration Form 563 via MyGuichet.lu or the AED e-filing portal, attaching constitutional documents, proof of activity, and the home-country VAT number where applicable; the AED issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number with LU prefix and 8 digits within approximately 15 business days. In parallel, the locally incorporated SARL must be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the business registration number (RC L / B number) and the national 13-digit identification matricule, after which the ACD assigns the corporate income tax file and processes the annual CIT return (form 500/510).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment must submit VAT registration Form 563 via MyGuichet.lu or the AED e-filing portal, attaching constitutional documents, proof of activity, and the home-country VAT number where applicable; the AED issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number with LU prefix and 8 digits within approximately 15 business days. In parallel, the locally incorporated SARL must be registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the business registration number (RC L / B number) and the national 13-digit identification matricule, after which the ACD assigns the corporate income tax file and processes the annual CIT return (form 500/510).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box election; entity classification is conclusive under the 1915 Companies Law and the LIR. Available elections include the VAT group registration option under Article 57 LTVT, the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for distance sales below EUR 10,000, the joint VAT filing for Integrated companies, and the group taxation relief (régime fiscal des sociétés intégrées) for CIT and municipal business tax. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through the ATAD implementing law of 21 June 2019 and DAC6 mandatory disclosure rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for small businesses established in Luxembourg; non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT from the first taxable supply; the intra-Community distance sales / OSS threshold is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to goods physically transported or dispatched outside the European Union by a taxable person acting as exporter, provided ownership has transferred, the export declaration has been lodged, and the exporter holds conclusive proof of export issued or obtained within 90 days from the time of supply. Qualifying offshore services directly connected to exported goods (transport, loading, handling) may also be zero-rated under Article 16(f) LTVT and AED guidance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD in ECS/ICS electronic format)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR consignment note or IATA Air Waybill
  • International Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Export per AED administrative practice (e.g., EX1 electronic export document or third-country import documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no continuous transaction control API, and no digital stamping regime are in force in Luxembourg as of 2025. B2B electronic invoicing is permitted under EU Directive 2014/55/EU, while B2G e-invoicing via the PEPPOL network is mandatory for Luxembourg public procurement contracts.

False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are reassessed within the five-year statutory limitation period, triggering payment of the omitted VAT, default interest of 5% per annum, and late-payment penalties of 10% of the tax. Deliberate VAT fraud entails administrative fines from EUR 250 up to EUR 125,000 or up to four times the evaded tax, possible seizure of goods, and criminal sanctions for customs and tax offences with fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment for one to five years, alongside joint and several co-liability of the exporter and any accomplices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under Articles 63–65 TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control licensing or prior administrative approval for capital injections, profit remittances, dividends, trade payments, or financial loans. Remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) standard AML/KYC and beneficial-ownership verification under the amended 1919 Law and the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE), (ii) BCL external balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the settlement bank for cross-border transactions exceeding the EUR 12,500 threshold, and (iii) arm's-length substance and transfer-pricing documentation for surplus repatriation; none of these constitutes a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation window applies to Luxembourg export receipts. The SARL may freely hold export proceeds in EUR or in the original settlement currency (notably USD and GBP arising from Amazon and Stripe marketplace payouts) and may convert into EUR at market rates. Administrative obligations are confined to: (a) proper booking under Lux GAAP/IFRS, (b) BCL balance-of-payments reporting above EUR 12,500 per cross-border transaction, (c) mirroring of export sales invoices and marketplace settlement statements, and (d) alignment with the transfer-pricing margin documentation for the ISIC 3240 beechwood-toy manufacturing operation.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg) – BCL; acting within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), with foreign-exchange and monetary-policy competence shared with the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium: only PSD2-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized by the CSSF, or passported via the EEA single market, may legally provide payment services in Luxembourg. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EAA licence cannot hold client funds directly and may operate solely through a licensed Luxembourg sponsor bank via virtual-IBAN or managed-account arrangements. Legal constraints include: FGDL deposit-guarantee cover applies only to credit institutions, not to EMI e-money float; CSSF Circular 17/650 imposes outsourcing, chain-of-KYC and IT-security requirements on EMI arrangements; and Amazon_Pay/Stripe settlement flows require the EMI to evidence a complete originator-to-beneficiary audit trail with full EU Regulation 2015/847 payer/payee data.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL's initial capital is wire-transferred to a blocked subscription account (compte de blocage) opened in Luxembourg before notarial signature, using SWIFT MT103 or SEPA/TARGET2 rails with ISO 20022 purpose code CAPH (Capital Increase) and explicit reference to the draft notarial deed. The sending bank must apply EU Regulation (EU) 2015/847 full payer-and-payee transparency; the receiving bank verifies the origin of funds against the share-subscription schedule and the initial certificate of deposit. After the notary files the deed and RCS registration is effective, the bank releases the blocked funds to the operational account. USD/GBP-denominated injections are converted at the bank's reference rate with a documented ECB fixing calculation, and the BCL external-statistics reporting obligation is discharged for any transaction above the EUR 12,500 threshold.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct supplier wires with invoice narrative and ISO 20022 purpose code TRAD; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV for the correspondent/cover leg of large USD and GBP settlements; (iii) commercial documentary Letters of Credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700 issuance, MT707 amendment, MT754 advice) for bulk beechwood procurement and strategic inventory builds; (iv) documentary collections (MT400/MT450) for low-risk delivered-duty-paid consignments; and (v) SEPA Credit Transfers via TARGET2 for intra-EU euro disbursements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective headline rate for Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, 6.75% municipal business tax; municipal rates vary by commune)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic withholding tax rate on dividend distributions to non-residents absent treaty relief; reduced to 5% under many relevant DTAs for portfolio corporate shareholders, and 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying shareholdings of at least 10% held for at least 12 months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 15,000,000 annual Luxembourg entity turnover triggers a mandatory Local File; a consolidated group turnover of EUR 50,000,000 requires a Master File; contemporaneous documentation must be produced within 30 days of a formal Luxembourg tax administration request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but requires real economic substance through Article 56(1) LIR anti-abuse rules, the EU ATAD GAAR, and OECD treaty access principles. A SARL with passive base-eroding flows must evidence premises, employees, board governance, and entrepreneurial risk-taking in Luxembourg; otherwise the structure will be disregarded as a shell for double-taxation agreement and intra-EU directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful Omission

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg criminal tax law, the taxpayer is liable to a fine of up to EUR 100,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil administration adds a 10% default surcharge, monthly late interest, and a 100% penalty on the evaded tax in aggravated fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who sign false declarations lose limited liability protection; the Luxembourg courts may order joint and several restoration of the evaded tax, travel bans, and imprisonment of directors found to have orchestrated the fraud.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Artificial Shell

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the pricing arrangement, upward profit adjustment, refusal of DTA relief, a 10% to 20% administrative tax increase, and accrual of monthly interest on the additional assessment; EU ATAD GAAR can deny the entire benefit of the abusing arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors approving artificial intercompany structures may be jointly liable for the tax deficiency under civil actions; severe cases result in disqualification from management, travel restrictions pending tax collection, and criminal liability if the pricing distortion is fraudulent.
AML and Cash Trade Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (consolidated), failure to declare cash transaction status and perform due diligence attracts administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 and criminal imprisonment of executives; the regulator can suspend business operations during investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face confiscation orders, personal fine enforcement, travel bans, and disqualification as company officers for gross AML compliance failure.
Persistent Non-Filing and Bankruptcy Negligence

Statutory sanction: Default assessments, EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 penalty per omitted return, 10% surcharge on assessed amounts, and criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to produce statutory books.

Director personal liability: Under Luxembourg company law, directors who cause the company to operate insolvently and do not timely initiate winding-up can be ordered by court to personally absorb the outstanding corporate tax debts; travel bans may accompany insolvency proceedings.

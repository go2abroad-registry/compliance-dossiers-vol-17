Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 1 is permitted under the reformed 2016/2017 SARL regime; capital must be fully subscribed at incorporation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) / Registration portal: MyGuichet.lu (business registration via RCS and tax number application through the Luxembourg direct tax administration)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first file incorporation documents with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through a notary; post-incorporation, the RCS automatically transmits corporate data to the Administration des contributions directes and the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), which issues the national tax number (numéro de registre national / matricule) and separate VAT number after submission of the VAT registration form (Formulaire 645) either via MyGuichet.lu or by post. Non-resident directors and shareholders may be appointed; no local residency requirement applies to initial registration, but a Luxembourg registered office and designated local agent (when no local director) are required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg SARL is subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax at entity level; no US-style check-the-box election exists. However, a fully taxable Luxembourg entity may form part of a corporate tax consolidation regime under Article 164bis LIR allowing integrated tax grouping among 95%-owned Luxembourg resident companies. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD implementation (Law of 27 December 2019) apply and may restrict deductions where payments are treated asymmetrically. Election for the Luxembourg VAT group regime is available per Article 59bis of the Luxembourg VAT Law for VAT purposes only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for Luxembourg-established businesses: any taxable person making taxable supplies is required to register. For EU distance sales of goods (non-established suppliers), the VAT registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year (Union-wide distance sales threshold); below this, VAT may be due in the customer's member state. For non-EU established suppliers, mandatory registration arises with immediate effect upon any taxable supply unless the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) is used for distance sales of imported consignments not exceeding EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Luxembourg supplier may apply 0% VAT to the export supply of goods if title passes within Luxembourg and goods are dispatched/transported outside the EU, or if the goods are shipped to another EU member state and the onward export is made by the customer or a third party under the VAT-exempt export provisions (Articles 155 and 156 of Luxembourg VAT Law). Documentation proving that the goods left Luxembourg/EU territory must be retained. For online retail of oversized furniture, zero-rating applies only to actual exports outside the EU; EU-distance sales remain taxable at the destination country's VAT rate, either via OSS registration or local VAT registration. Sales of offshore services (e.g., in-transit logistics, online marketplace facilitation) are zero-rated under Article 155(b) where the recipient is established outside the EU and direct effective use/exploitation occurs outside Luxembourg.

  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR, Air Waybill, seaway bill)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export contract and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange or bank settlement evidence

Luxembourg currently imposes no mandatory electronic invoicing in B2B domestic supplies nor a real-time fiscal clearance/stamping mandate. E-invoicing is permitted, subject to authenticity and integrity requirements (suitable business controls or EDI under Directive 2006/112/EC). Luxembourg has implemented EU e-reporting optionality and anticipates the EU ViDA framework; from 1 July 2026, IOSS and OSS taxpayers must maintain e-invoicing-compliant record availability, but no national clearance API or mandatory clearance stamp applies to domestic e-commerce transactions at present.

The Luxembourg tax administration and customs authorities may audit export zero-rating claims. If the exporter fails to provide valid dispatch/export proof or the goods are diverted back into the EU, VAT may be clawed back with interest at the statutory late-interest rate; the penalty regime includes fines up to 100% of evaded VAT for intentional fraud, administrative fines for material omissions, and potential criminal prosecution for forging customs declarations or false export documentation. Failure to repatriate foreign receipts within 180 days may trigger reclassification of the export as taxable domestic supply, VAT reassessment, and transfer-pricing adjustments where the exporter is a related entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; Luxembourg law and applicable EU treaty provisions impose no conversion restrictions, no prior-authorisation mechanisms, no surrender requirements and no economic-benefit or investment-screening tests for foreign ownership of a SARL. Profit remittance by a SARL is unrestricted under the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, subject only to (i) an annual general meeting approval, (ii) the statutory balance-sheet solvency test requiring net assets to remain at or above the issued share capital plus non-distributable reserves after distribution, and (iii) a standard 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, which may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the domestic participation-exemption regime where the parent holds at least 10% and satisfies the holding-period condition; there are no capital controls, no reinvestment obligations and no profit-repatriation quotas. The only administrative hurdle in practice is the requirement that annual accounts be filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) and that any shareholder loan be documented at arm's length for tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion rules apply. Luxembourg and the Eurosystem do not impose any statutory timeline or required conversion ratio for export proceeds, trade settlement receipts or inbound revenue from e-commerce payment aggregators; EUR and foreign-currency balances may be held indefinitely both onshore and offshore without declaration to the BCL. The sole relevant obligation is statistical: under the ECB external-sector statistics framework implemented by the BCL, resident enterprises are required to provide their bank with transaction and counterparty details for direct-investment and goods-trade flows equal to or exceeding EUR 12.5 million per counterparty per calendar year, and the bank then files the report on the company's behalf; this is a statistical-reporting duty, not an exchange-control, surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem framework alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), with the Ministry of Finance holding residual authority over exchange-control policy. As a euro-area member, Luxembourg applies the full free movement of capital under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and maintains no separate exchange-control administration or licensing regime for current or capital account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility as an auxiliary settlement layer, not as the primary treasury home. CSSF-licensed Luxembourg electronic money institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, and EEA EMIs passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2 (e.g., Stripe Payments Europe Limited, an Irish-authorised EMI, or Wise Europe / Revolut Payments UAB), can provide EUR/USD IBANs, faster remote onboarding and API-driven multi-currency accounts suited to receiving Stripe and Affirm BNPL payouts. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a credit institution, cannot grant credit, open documentary credits or issue import guarantees, and is prudentially subject to own-funds requirements equal to the greater of EUR 350,000 or 2% of outstanding e-money float, creating settlement-concentration risk for large trade disbursements; additionally, Tier-1 banks may treat major incoming flows from EMI pooled settlement accounts as heightened AML risk and may refuse to accept such funds as evidence of commercial substance. Recommended architecture: one Tier-1 bank account as the master clearing account and a single EMI used only for payout-optimisation sweeps, never the reverse.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for share capital and shareholder funding: (1) the non-resident shareholder executes a SWIFT MT103 credit in EUR to the SARL's blocked capital-escrow account at a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank, using ISO 20022 External Purpose Code CAPM (capital payment) or INTC (intra-company payment) and a structured field-70 reference reading 'Capital subscription - [SARL name] - notarial deed dated [date]'; (2) the bank issues the attestation de blocage de fonds to the notary, evidencing that EUR 12,394.68 (or higher stated capital) is blocked until registration; (3) after signature of the deed and RCS registration, the bank releases the escrow to the SARL's free operational IBAN; (4) any shareholder loan above EUR 15,000 must be supported by an arm's-length interest-bearing loan agreement, reported in the annual corporate tax return, and may require the bank to record the transaction for BCL external-statistics purposes if aggregate direct-investment flows reach EUR 12.5 million per counterparty per year; (5) repayment of principal and interest is unrestricted and requires no authorisation. The bank will verify that the CAPM/INTC purpose code matches the RCS capital figure and that the credit settles through TARGET2.Clearing rails for the oversized home-furniture trade cycle: (a) EU suppliers and warehouses: SEPA Credit Transfer (including instant SCT) in EUR, or SEPA Direct Debit for recurring freight and storage charges; (b) non-EU suppliers (chiefly China and Vietnam invoiced in USD or CNY): SWIFT MT103/MT202 for invoice settlement with ISO 20022 purpose code TRAD or GOODS, accompanied by the commercial invoice, packing list and transport documents; (c) large single consignments exceeding EUR 100,000: irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 issued by the Tier-1 Luxembourg bank, payable at sight against presentation of Bill of Lading, invoice and insurance certificate; (d) import duties and freight: postponed VAT accounting under the Luxembourg import-VAT deferment so cash is not advanced for customs VAT, with freight forwarders paid via SEPA or MT103; (e) inbound e-commerce receivables: Stripe settles via its Irish EMI to the SARL's EUR IBAN through SEPA or in USD via SWIFT, with daily automated sweeps from any EMI sub-account to the master Tier-1 account to minimise counterparty float exposure. All outbound instructions must carry valid ISO 20022 purpose codes, and the bank may require the underlying contracts on file for any USD settlement above its AML threshold.Outright vanilla EUR/USD delivery forwards with tenors up to 12 months, executed with a Luxembourg credit institution under ISDA 2002 or a bilateral FX master agreement; the SARL qualifies as a non-financial counterparty (NFC) under EMIR and is not subject to mandatory clearing for such forwards.FX swaps (swap de change) combining a spot and a forward EUR/USD or EUR/CNH leg to manage the timing mismatch between USD-denominated Stripe/Affirm settlements and EUR supplier payments.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (EUR/USD) limited to matched asset-liability hedging of any USD-denominated shareholder loan, subject to confirmation of NFC- status and the absence of any EMIR clearing-threshold breach (EUR 3 billion gross notional).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to non-convertible or offshore-regulated currencies (e.g., USD/CNH) where onshore CNY delivery is unavailable; NDFs are not applicable to EUR/USD within the euro area.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as an auxiliary settlement layer, not as the primary treasury home. CSSF-licensed Luxembourg electronic money institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, and EEA EMIs passporting into Luxembourg under PSD2 (e.g., Stripe Payments Europe Limited, an Irish-authorised EMI, or Wise Europe / Revolut Payments UAB), can provide EUR/USD IBANs, faster remote onboarding and API-driven multi-currency accounts suited to receiving Stripe and Affirm BNPL payouts. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a credit institution, cannot grant credit, open documentary credits or issue import guarantees, and is prudentially subject to own-funds requirements equal to the greater of EUR 350,000 or 2% of outstanding e-money float, creating settlement-concentration risk for large trade disbursements; additionally, Tier-1 banks may treat major incoming flows from EMI pooled settlement accounts as heightened AML risk and may refuse to accept such funds as evidence of commercial substance. Recommended architecture: one Tier-1 bank account as the master clearing account and a single EMI used only for payout-optimisation sweeps, never the reverse.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Group consolidated turnover > €50 million AND local entity intercompany transactions > €1 million (services), > €250,000 (other transactions), or > €100,000 (financial transactions)

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) for entities benefiting from certain regimes (e.g., IP box). The company must maintain adequate personnel, premises, and decision-making functions in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties up to 200% of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 15% withholding tax on deemed distributions; fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if the company is deemed a sham; potential criminal charges for abuse of law.
AML Violation

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment if they knowingly facilitate money laundering; regulatory bans from holding directorial positions.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the lbr.lu electronic portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), SARL regime at Articles 710-1 et seq.; amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS Law) and rules governing publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte de constitution notarié (notarised deed of incorporation), filed by the notary with the RCS, followed by electronic publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. For cash subscriptions, at least 20% (EUR 2,400 for a statutory-minimum SARL) must be paid up before notarisation and held in a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank releases the deposit only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up and supported by a statutory auditor's special report.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to a passive equity-holding SPV. The registered office must be a legally occupiable commercial address; the landlord or domiciliation agent must hold their own fire-safety, building-occupancy and structural-load certificates. No employee-related occupational health clearance is required for a manager-only passive holding company; if employees are added later, the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) requirements will apply.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed. For cash subscriptions, at least 20% (EUR 2,400 for a statutory-minimum SARL) must be paid up before notarisation and held in a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank releases the deposit only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up and supported by a statutory auditor's special report.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that: (1) the subscription of the issued share capital of the Société à Responsabilité Limitée described in the notarial deed of incorporation is ratified and adopted; (2) the registered office is fixed at [Address], Luxembourg; (3) [Name] is appointed manager (gérant) with full powers of ordinary administration and sole signature; (4) the manager is authorized to complete registration with the Luxembourg Register of Commerce and Companies and to arrange publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations; and (5) all prior acts performed by the promoters for the formation of the company are ratified and confirmed.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable; the Ministry of Economy's industrial licensing directorate would become competent only if the company's object changes to active commercial, manufacturing or other regulated activity.

Category: Not applicable — exempt pure equity-holding SPV under ISIC 6420; no autorisation d'établissement required under the amended Law of 2 September 2011 on access to the professions.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD, Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for corporate income and municipal business taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT (TVA). Unified e-government portal: MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated SARL: (1) execute notarial deed of incorporation; (2) register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) through MyGuichet.lu; (3) obtain the statutory national identification number (numéro d'identification national / matricule) and RCS registration number; (4) ACD automatically opens the corporate tax file (numéro de dossier fiscal); (5) apply to AEDT for the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format 'LU' + 8 digits) using form 640/641, generally issued within 2–4 weeks. No separate TIN is required beyond these identifiers; the matricule is used for social, statistical and filing purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Participation exemption (Art. 166 L.I.R.) — 100% exemption for qualifying dividend income and capital gains on qualifying shareholdings (≥10% of share capital or ≥EUR 1.2M acquisition cost; 12-month holding period; anti-abuse substance and look-through conditions).; VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Art. 60 of the VAT Law, allowing a single VAT registration for closely-linked Luxembourg entities.; Foreign permanent establishment exemption election (treaty-based or unilateral credit method), with corresponding taxable-base adjustment for exempt PE income.; No US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; entity classification is statutory, and a SARL is an opaque, CIT-liable legal person.; Hybrid mismatch alignments under ATAD I/II (interest limitation, hybrid instrument and hybrid entity rules) are reported on the Luxembourg corporate income tax return; no elective hybrid characterization is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (small-business exemption, Art. 57(b) VAT Law; threshold does not apply to a holding SPV that recharges management, finance, IT or similar services to affiliates, in which case VAT registration is mandatory regardless of turnover)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) exports of goods physically removed from Luxembourg to a non-EU destination, (ii) intra-Community supplies to an EU VAT-registered customer holding a valid VAT number, and (iii) specified international/re-export services (Art. 43–46 and Art. 58 VAT Law) including transport and services directly linked to exports. A pure equity holding SPV (ISIC 6420) generally supplies no goods; zero-rating is relevant only where the SPV recharges cross-border group services (e.g., financing, management, shareholder services) under B2B place-of-supply rules, or for share sales, which are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EXA) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical removal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer proof evidencing export proceeds (where required under AML/FX rules)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Customer's valid EU VAT identification number (for intra-Community supplies)
  • Delivery/acceptance certificates or warehouse release documents

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing through the PEPPOL network (Law of 2021); no general B2B real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping obligation; B2B e-invoicing (Factur-X, UBL) is voluntary; taxpayers must retain complete invoice archives, electronic fiscal records and customs documentation for the statutory retention period and must make them available to ACD/AEDT upon request during risk-based audits.

AEDT/ACD conduct risk-based audits verifying transport documents, counterparty VAT status and economic substance; absence of transport proof disqualifies zero-rating and triggers VAT reassessment at 17%, default interest, fines of EUR 250 to EUR 4,000 per infringement (Art. 67–70 VAT Law), criminal prosecution and imprisonment for fraudulent schemes, joint and several liability of directors, and retrospective clawback under the general anti-abuse rule (Art. 6 Tax Adaptation Law) and EU mutual-assistance / HERCULES fraud prevention procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The official currency is the euro (EUR), which is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions. Luxembourg law and EU law (Articles 63–66 TFEU) prohibit restrictions on capital movements and payments between member states and third countries, subject only to EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering measures. There are no exchange licences, surrender requirements, approval gates or repatriation taxes for equity injection, dividend remission, or disposal proceeds. Withholding tax on outbound dividends to EU/EEA qualifying parents can be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; non-EU shareholders may rely on Luxembourg tax treaties or the domestic 15% withholding tax regime where applicable. In practice, banks independently verify FX transactions for sanctions screening, source of funds, and tax transparency, but no administrative convertibility authorisation is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg has no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds. For a pure equity holding SARL (ISIC 6420), the concept of merchandise export proceeds is not relevant; any incidental service income may be retained in EUR or another currency at the entity's choice. All non-resident transactions above Eurostat/BCL reporting thresholds are captured via resident banks for national balance-of-payments statistics; the reporting obligation falls on the bank, not the SPV, and does not impose any conversion ratio or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) – the Luxembourg branch of the Eurosystem and the national monetary authority. Luxembourg does not maintain a separate foreign-exchange administration; foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy are governed by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and regulated at national level by BCL, the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), and the Ministry of Finance. BCL is responsible for balance-of-payments reporting statistics collected through resident banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium. Luxembourg recognises authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under PSD2 and the CSSF regulatory framework. An EEA-incorporated EMI passporting into Luxembourg may provide payment accounts, SEPA/wire issuance, and corporate cards, but it is not a substitute for a fully licensed credit institution for a pure holding SPV that receives equity capital, holds investor proceeds, or executes intercompany settlements. Non-EEA offshore digital EMI wallets are not viable: they are generally unregulated, cannot access SWIFT MT103 interbank clearing, do not provide deposit protection, and trigger AML/KYC and central-bank reporting gaps. Use a Luxembourg/EEA-licensed EMI only as a secondary disbursement tool for low-value operational payments and never as the principal custody or clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 (ACH/CHIPS in USD, TARGET2/SEPA for EUR) for inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, or subordinated debt. The remittance message should specify: (i) beneficiary IBAN in Luxembourg (format LUxx + domestic account number), (ii) beneficiary bank BIC, (iii) payment reference 'share capital increase' / 'shareholder loan' / 'equity contribution', and (iv) the shareholder's name and full bank details. Luxembourg resident banks may apply balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL for external transactions, but no prior exchange approval is required. To avoid onboarding delays, the SPV must provide board approval, source-of-funds evidence, CRS/FATCA forms, and signed loan/subscription agreements before the transfer is credited.For a pure equity holding entity, outbound payments are predominantly dividends, capital distributions, intercompany loan repayments, professional fees, and acquisition payments. Recommended clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all non-SEPA and non-EUR payments, (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EEA counterparties, (iii) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for bank-to-bank or correspondent clearing of high-value settlement instructions, (iv) commercial letters of credit under UCP600 where the SPV is financing or guaranteeing trade transactions of subsidiaries, and (v) direct debit/SEPA DD for recurring EU invoices. No restriction applies on using commercial LC or documentary collections, but those instruments are normally relevant only if the holdco intermediates goods trade.

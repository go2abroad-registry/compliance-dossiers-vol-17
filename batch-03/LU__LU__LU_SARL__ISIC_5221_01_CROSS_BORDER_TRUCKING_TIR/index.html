Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) - Luxembourg direct tax administration (https://impotsdirects.public.lu); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) - VAT, registration duties and excise administration (https://pfi.public.lu); Administration des Douanes et Accises - customs, TIR carnet control and export procedures (https://douanes.public.lu). Central e-administration portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) for VAT returns, RCS filings, customs declarations and e-invoicing.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute the SARL notarial deed of incorporation and file it with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers portal; obtain the RCS registration number, which is the primary legal entity identifier. Step 2: Obtain the Luxembourg corporate tax identification number (matricule) from the ACD; this number is allocated automatically upon RCS registration and upon the first corporate income tax declaration (Formulaire 600). Step 3: Register for VAT with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu using the VAT registration notification (notification d'assujettissement) to obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU followed by eight digits. Step 4: Apply to the Administration des Douanes et Accises for an EORI number (format LU plus eight digits) and, where applicable, for TIR carnet holder authorization and an Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) certificate to obtain guarantees and bond waivers under the TIR transit regime. The entire procedure is performed electronically; non-EU-established applicants must appoint a Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT purposes, whereas EU-established applicants may register directly without a local representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is a Luxembourg-resident capital company taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under Article 159 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR); profits are subject to corporate income tax, the solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax, and distributions are taxable at the level of the shareholder. No US-style check-the-box or partnership pass-through classification is available for a SARL. Luxembourg applies a modified territoriality rule: income of a foreign permanent establishment and foreign real estate income is exempt with progression provided it is subject to a comparable tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is a Luxembourg-resident capital company taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under Article 159 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR); profits are subject to corporate income tax, the solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax, and distributions are taxable at the level of the shareholder. No US-style check-the-box or partnership pass-through classification is available for a SARL. Luxembourg applies a modified territoriality rule: income of a foreign permanent establishment and foreign real estate income is exempt with progression provided it is subject to a comparable tax. No tax-exempt zone status applies to a road transport SARL established in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections for a SARL include: (i) election to form a tax consolidation group (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR to offset current-year profits and losses of Luxembourg-resident group companies; (ii) election to apply the participation exemption for qualifying dividends and capital gains under Articles 147 and 166 LIR (Soparfi regime); (iii) election under Article 50bis LIR for the intellectual property box regime; (iv) election under Article 60 of the Loi TVA to form a single VAT taxable person (VAT grouping) with Luxembourg-established affiliates; and (v) under the ATAD-compliant anti-hybrid rules introduced by the Law of 21 June 2019, a taxpayer may make a compliance election in its Luxembourg tax return to align the tax treatment of hybrid financial instruments with the counterparty jurisdiction, thereby preventing the mandatory disallowance of interest or dividend deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A Luxembourg-established SARL engaged in an economic activity is required to register for VAT from its first taxable supply without any monetary threshold. The general small-enterprise exemption (Régime de la franchise, Article 170bis Loi TVA) of EUR 35,000 annual turnover is available only to small undertakings and is not available to the SARL because cross-border TIR trucking constitutes an economic activity with input VAT recovery and intra-EU and third-country transport elements. Non-resident carriers are subject to mandatory Luxembourg VAT registration without threshold when they make taxable supplies in Luxembourg, unless the reverse-charge mechanism, the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or the TIR transit arrangements remove the local VAT liability.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with credit) is available under Article 148 Loi TVA for supplies of goods physically exported to a destination outside the European Union and for transport and ancillary services directly linked to those exports, provided the supplier retains documentary evidence of export. For a Luxembourg TIR trucking SARL, the cumulative criteria are: (i) the goods are transported under a valid TIR carnet and discharged at the customs office of the third country within the TIR operational period; (ii) an electronic export customs declaration is lodged with the Administration des Douanes et Accises under the Union Customs Code; (iii) the supply is made to a customer established outside Luxembourg; for B2B transport services the general place-of-supply rule under Article 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC applies, so services to a non-EU taxable person fall outside Luxembourg VAT and intra-EU B2B transport is reverse-charged in the customer's member state; and (iv) the foreign-currency freight proceeds are repatriated to Luxembourg and traceable to the specific export operation within the prescribed deadline.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration lodged with the Administration des Douanes et Accises under the Automated Export System (AES) of the Union Customs Code
  • TIR Carnet issued under the IRU guarantee chain, bearing the dated stamps and discharge certificates of the customs offices of departure, transit and destination
  • CMR international consignment note (lettre de voiture CMR) evidencing the haulage contract, cargo identification and delivery to the third-country consignee
  • Commercial invoice and freight invoice issued by the SARL for the transport service
  • Bill of Lading, truck manifest, or equivalent transport document where the trailer is subsequently carried by sea or air
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT MT103, or fuel-card clearing statement demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Proof of TIR guarantee cover issued by the national guaranteeing association (Chambre de Commerce du Luxembourg) and the carrier's TIR carnet holder authorization or AEO certificate

Luxembourg does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing, or digital stamping for B2B supplies as of the current law. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only for B2G supplies to Luxembourg public authorities, which must be submitted through the PEPPOL network and the MyGuichet.lu e-invoicing channel since 1 January 2022. VAT returns are filed electronically via MyGuichet.lu on a monthly, quarterly or annual basis depending on turnover, using the VAT 400 series forms. There is no real-time API clearance requirement; however, taxpayers must maintain a Standard Audit File for Tax (SAF-T) in an approved electronic format for submission upon request, and accounting records must comply with the Luxembourg Plan Comptable Normalisé (PCN) chart of accounts.

Export zero-rating positions are subject to joint audit by the AEDT and the Administration des Douanes et Accises. Where the TIR carnet is not properly discharged, the CMR is incomplete, the goods are not shown physically to leave the EU, or the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, the AEDT will retroactively claw back the 0% rate and reassess output VAT at 17%, together with statutory default interest and late-payment penalties. Administrative fines of up to EUR 125,000 may be imposed under Articles 62 and 63 Loi TVA. Deliberate fraudulent export schemes, including false declarations, counterfeit TIR carnets and fictitious exports, give rise to criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) and the Criminal Code, with imprisonment of up to five years and criminal fines of up to EUR 250,000. The assessment and clawback limitation period is five years, extended to ten years where fraudulent intent is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg maintains no exchange-control restrictions between residents and non-residents. Free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and by the Luxembourg legal framework on payments and securities settlement. There is no distinction between capital and current account for currency convertibility purposes; TIR trucking revenue may be received and held in any currency without prior authorization from the BCL. Profit remittance is unrestricted from an FX perspective: dividends approved by the SARL general meeting may be transferred abroad via SEPA or SWIFT without exchange-control clearance. The only material restriction is tax-related: a 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax applies at source unless reduced by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for qualifying EU parent shareholdings) or by an applicable double tax treaty. Polish, German, French, Belgian, Dutch and other EU counterparty payments settle freely in EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory surrender, repatriation timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Freight receipts from cross-border trucking under TIR carnets may be retained in EUR or in foreign currencies, converted at the company's discretion and held in Luxembourg or EEA bank accounts. No BCL reporting is required for the underlying freight transaction; the only possible reporting is statistical (BCL balance-of-payments data collection) and EU Intrastat customs statistics, none of which create surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), national central bank of Luxembourg and member of the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank (ECB) as the supranational monetary and foreign-exchange reserve authority; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent supervisory authority for banks, electronic money institutions and payment institutions in respect of AML/KYC and prudential supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Under PSD2 and the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport into Luxembourg and provide multi-currency payment accounts, FX conversion, mass disbursements, fuel-card clearing and payment initiation for a Luxembourg SARL without a full banking licence. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs cannot accept deposits and must safeguard client funds in a segregated account at a credit institution; (b) the statutory capital escrow of a Luxembourg SARL must be held with a Luxembourg notary or with a Luxembourg credit institution, and an EMI cannot act as custodian of the constitution capital; (c) TIR carnet guarantee providers (the Automobile Club du Luxembourg acting under the IRU TIR guarantee chain) require guarantees or sureties issued by an authorized bank, not by an EMI; (d) an EMI account cannot support documentary letters of credit or bank guarantees required by commercial counterparties. Recommended operating model: hold the operational, payroll and fuel-card accounts at a Tier 1 Luxembourg bank for statutory capital, TIR guarantees and SWIFT MT103 clearing, while using an EEA-passported EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer) for selective fast FX conversion and small-value international disbursements, subject to the bank's treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial share capital of the SARL, the compliant route is to transfer funds to the Luxembourg notary's escrow account (dépôt de fonds) or to a blocked capital passbook at a Luxembourg credit institution. The remitting bank must execute a SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR transfers or a SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (capital contribution) or 'EQUI' (equity) and a remittance reference containing the RCS number and the wording 'Share capital contribution - Société à Responsabilité Limitée'. The notary or receiving bank verifies that the payee matches the subscriber, that the source of funds is consistent with the incorporation file, and that no sanctions filters are triggered. No BCL authorisation is required. For subsequent capital increases, the same MT103/SEPA routing applies, with the notarial deed of increase referenced in field 70 or the remittance information. Supporting documents include the notarial incorporation deed, the RBE and RCS extracts, AML/KYC questionnaires, source-of-funds statements, and the notary's attestation of receipt, after which the capital is released for operating purposes.The Luxembourg clearing infrastructure offers the following outbound rails for the TIR trucking treasury: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated payments within the EEA, cleared via TARGET2 at the BCL and instant payments via TIPS/SCT Inst for urgent fuel and driver disbursements; (2) SWIFT MT103 for high-value or non-EUR payments requiring correspondent-bank clearing, with structured remittance information mapping each wire to the invoice, CMR reference or fuel-card settlement statement; (3) SWIFT MT101 for batch and mass disbursements, suitable for payroll and periodic supplier runs; (4) documentary trade instruments, including irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 and standby LCs or bank guarantees, used to back TIR customs guarantees (ACL/IRU chain) and large freight contracts; (5) fuel-card clearing via DKV, UTA or Eurowag, whereby cards are accepted at European fuel stations and settled centrally through the Luxembourg treasury via direct debit or periodic wires from the operational account. For auditability, all outbound wires should be matched to the underlying commercial contract, CMR document and customs declaration; the storage of SWIFT MT103 confirmation plus the commercial invoice in the treasury file satisfies banking AML/CFT and Luxembourg tax evidence requirements.Outright FX forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP, PLN/EUR, HUF/EUR, CZK/EUR and other trade-related currencies, executed OTC with a Luxembourg credit institution under an ISDA or FX master agreement; fully permissible for a non-financial corporate treasury without BCL authorisation.FX swaps (simultaneous spot/forward or matched opposite forwards) used for temporary liquidity bridging and rolling of maturing obligations.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic currencies where physical settlement is restricted, provided the counterparty is a licensed credit institution and the hedge has genuine commercial underlying exposure.Plain-vanilla FX options and collar strategies (EUR call/put combinations) from the options desk of a Luxembourg bank, subject to a board-approved treasury policy and an ISDA/CSA documentation framework.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) permissible for hedging long-term EUR-denominated fleet financing with a foreign-currency component; must be documented under ISDA and treated as cash-flow hedges in the financial statements.There are no mandatory hedging requirements and no BCL/ECB pre-approval for any of the above; the only limits are the company's corporate purpose, the board-approved treasury policy, and the prudential credit and onboarding requirements of the counterparty bank. All derivative instruments must be backed by an ISDA/CSA or a local FX master agreement and recorded in the annual accounts in accordance with IFRS or LUX GAAP derivative disclosure rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Under PSD2 and the Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services, EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport into Luxembourg and provide multi-currency payment accounts, FX conversion, mass disbursements, fuel-card clearing and payment initiation for a Luxembourg SARL without a full banking licence. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs cannot accept deposits and must safeguard client funds in a segregated account at a credit institution; (b) the statutory capital escrow of a Luxembourg SARL must be held with a Luxembourg notary or with a Luxembourg credit institution, and an EMI cannot act as custodian of the constitution capital; (c) TIR carnet guarantee providers (the Automobile Club du Luxembourg acting under the IRU TIR guarantee chain) require guarantees or sureties issued by an authorized bank, not by an EMI; (d) an EMI account cannot support documentary letters of credit or bank guarantees required by commercial counterparties. Recommended operating model: hold the operational, payroll and fuel-card accounts at a Tier 1 Luxembourg bank for statutory capital, TIR guarantees and SWIFT MT103 clearing, while using an EEA-passported EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer) for selective fast FX conversion and small-value international disbursements, subject to the bank's treasury policy.

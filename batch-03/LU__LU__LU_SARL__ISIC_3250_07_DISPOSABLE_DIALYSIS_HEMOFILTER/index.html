Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum issued capital EUR 12,000, fully subscribed at incorporation; each cash-funded quota must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value and the funds must be deposited in a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution prior to signing of the notarial deed; the unpaid balance may be called up by management within five years.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and register with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national matricule (RCS registration number), which serves as the company's statutory tax reference. Step 2 - File the electronic declaration of commencement of activity through MyGuichet.lu within 15 days of starting operations; the single filing simultaneously notifies the AEDT for VAT purposes and the Customs and Excise Agency for EORI purposes. Step 3 - The AEDT issues the VAT identification number in the format LU plus 8 digits (e.g., LU12345678); Customs issues the LU-prefixed EORI number; both are mandatory prerequisites for issuing valid invoices and lodging export declarations. Step 4 - For a non-established foreign enterprise without a Luxembourg fixed establishment, direct VAT registration is made under Article 57 of the Loi TVA; an applicant established outside the EU must appoint an accredited VAT fiscal representative with joint and several liability. Step 5 - Maintain the RCS extract, VAT certificate, and EORI confirmation in the permanent corporate and customs files; update the registration within 15 days of any change in activity, seat, or fixed establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Luxembourg SARL is an opaque resident company with no U.S.-style check-the-box election; it is mandatorily subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT), communal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) the Luxembourg fiscal unity tax consolidation election (integration of 95% plus Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries for CIT and municipal business tax, subject to prior AEDT approval); (ii) the VAT grouping election (assujetti unique under Article 60 Loi TVA) allowing closely linked Luxembourg persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (iii) the participation exemption election for qualifying dividend and capital gains sheltering; (iv) the IP box election (80% exemption of qualifying IP income) applicable to patented dialysis hemofilter technology; and (v) ATAD/ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch elections and mandatory documentary notifications to the AEDT. Any election requiring discretionary relief is documented in the electronic tax file and, where applicable, confirmed through the Luxembourg individual tax ruling procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 37,500 (annual taxable turnover ceiling for the small-business exemption under Article 57 Loi TVA); EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and digital services under the OSS scheme; no threshold applies to B2B supplies, intra-Community acquisitions, or non-established suppliers, so the SARL is mandatorily VAT-registered from its first taxable supply. Disposable dialysis hemofilters (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112) are standard-rated medical consumables at 17%; the reduced rates of 3%, 8%, and 14% do not apply to devices absent an express national listing, subject to confirmation through a binding VAT advance ruling.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of disposable dialysis hemofilters to non-EU destinations qualify for the VAT-exempt-with-credit (effective 0%) regime under Article 349(I) of the Loi TVA where: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU within 90 days of supply; (ii) the dispatch is carried out by the seller or for the seller's account and is evidenced by contemporaneous transport and customs documentation; (iii) the exporter retains the customs export declaration, transport documents, and the commercial sales file (contract with Incoterms, commercial invoice, and SWIFT LC/TT wire payment evidence); and (iv) the goods do not subsequently re-enter the EU. Intra-Community dispatches to EU VAT-registered customers are also exempt with credit, subject to the customer's valid EU VAT number and inclusion in the EC sales list. The exporter must maintain the export register and preserve all substantiation for the statutory 10-year retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (AES/ECS Export Accompanying Document with Movement Reference Number, lodged with the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR consignment note / Air Waybill) evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/MT700 payment evidence under Swift_LC or TT_Wire payment rails)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of B2B invoices exists in Luxembourg. B2B e-invoicing is optional and requires buyer consent; all invoices must contain the mandatory Article 72 Loi TVA content elements and be issued no later than the 15th day of the month following the supply. B2G e-invoicing to the Luxembourg State has been mandatory since 1 January 2023 via the PEPPOL network. VAT returns and EC sales listings are filed electronically through eTVA, and export declarations are lodged through the AES/ECS customs systems. An export ledger (journal des exportations) must be maintained, and all supporting documents must be retained for at least 10 years.

Post-clearance verification by the AEDT can reopen export zero-rating for 5 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud. If export proof is missing or the goods are found to have re-entered the EU, the VAT is clawed back together with statutory monthly late-payment interest and administrative fines; knowingly false export declarations trigger criminal liability (fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years), joint and several liability of directors and the fiscal representative, and loss of deduction rights for the customer. Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark aligns with standard ECA and bank documentary credit terms and must be consistent with arm's-length credit terms under Luxembourg transfer-pricing rules to withstand documentary audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full and unconditional capital-account and current-account convertibility as a Eurozone member; EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Article 63 guarantees free movement of capital and payments. No prior authorisation, central bank approval, or destination/beneficiary screening is imposed on profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty outflow, or intercompany loan repayment for a Luxembourg SARL. The only administrative obligations are statistical: resident companies must file balance-of-payments (ITRS-style) monthly declarations to the BCL for cross-border external assets and liabilities above EUR 250k equivalent, and large euro/foreign-currency payments are subject to ECB macro-prudential statistical reporting. Profit remittance via dividend distributions is subject to domestic tax form 700/notional withholding formalities but no FX conversion restraint. Currency inconvertibility risk is therefore negligible for EUR-denominated treasury operations and for EUR/USD trade settlements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeframe, mandatory conversion ratio, or central bank escrow requirement applies to export proceeds. A Luxembourg exporter may hold, convert, or transfer its dialysis hemofilter sales proceeds freely. Receipts denominated in third-country currencies (e.g., USD, CNY) may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency accounts without mandatory repatriation or forced sale into EUR. For practical treasury optimization, export invoices may designate EUR or USD settlement at the seller's option; proceeds received in USD should be promptly hedged or swept under the corporate EUR/USD forward program to eliminate balance-sheet translation exposure. BCL external statistics require the outward/inward claim to be reported on the monthly external balance sheet form, but this is purely informational and imposes no surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with prudential and conduct supervision reserved to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); the Luxembourg nexus of the Eurosystem applies to all euro-denominated clearing and reserve management.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM with strict legal boundary conditions. An EMI (Electronic Money Institution) holding a CSSF license (or an EEA passport under Directive 2009/110/EC) can provide multi-currency payment accounts, virtual IBANs, and SEPA Instant transfers for the SARL's working capital, but EMIs are not authorized to execute documentary credit operations, LC confirmations, or vendor direct financing for import/export of dialysis consumables; such transaction types require a tier-1 credit institution. Non-EEA digital EMI or Banking-as-a-Service middlemen must not be used as the sole treasury rail, because the SARL's AML/KYC credibility is impaired if primary operating funds flow through an unregulated third country; a non-EEA EMI without a Luxembourg or EU passport may hold e-money and execute payments but lacks the statutory deposit-take privilege and raises bank-account-verification concerns. The viable design is a two-rail structure: (i) a CSSF-licensed EMI or fully banked PSP for fast, low-value disbursements and employee/payroll payments; (ii) a tier-1 licensed bank holding the operational account, LC facilities, and FX execution. The EMI must be onboarded only if it provides a full complaint-handling and sanctions-typing engine supporting ISO 20022 purpose codes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the shareholder/equity injection into the Luxembourg SARL capital escrow account: a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA TARGET2 payment in EUR must be dispatched from the investor's bank with MT103 tags ensuring clean identification: Field 50K (ordering customer), 52A (ordering institution), 57A (beneficiary agent = Luxembourg bank), 59A (beneficiary SARL account), and purpose code as follows — for equity injection use ISO 20022 Category Purpose 'CAPM' (capital injection) or TFEU-code 'E520' in the SWIFT Remittance Information field; for intercompany shareholder loans use 'SUPP' or 'TRAD' only when invoice-backed. The BCL monthly external-assets form must be completed within the first 10 working days of the following month for any external asset above EUR 250k. It is mandatory that the incoming wire is a true SWIFT-correspondent payment with the Luxembourg bank acting as beneficiary agent; do not use an intermediate EMI wallet in an unregulated jurisdiction, as this triggers the correspondent-bank AML 'correspondent relationship' review. The capital escrow account must be released only after the notary's certificate of registration and receipt of the company's registration number from the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS).Trade settlements for procurement of raw materials (hollow-fiber PES membranes, potting compounds, packaging) and outbound sale of dialysis hemofilter cartridges leverage the following clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR-denominated payments to EU/EEA suppliers, with same-day or D+1 value; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for USD, GBP, and CNY disbursements to non-EEA counterparties, using the ordering bank's correspondent network; (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600-compliant) for high-value raw-material purchases and for sales contracts where the buyer's attorney requires documentary assurance — issuance by BGL BNP Paribas or Société Générale Luxembourg confirmed by the beneficiary's bank; (iv) Direct Debit (SDD B2B) for recurring supplier utilities; and (v) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) up to EUR 100k for urgent low-value disbursements. Outbound dispatching must reference the commercial invoice and the purchase-order number in SWIFT Field 70, and the bank's sanctions filter must be applied to the beneficiary, the country of origin of the FOB point, and the final delivery address of the dialysis units. For sale of medical devices, include the device's EU MDR registration number and the hospital/tender reference in the remittance info to avoid 'commodity-sanitization' flags. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (aggregate intercompany transactions) for the Local File; EUR 15,000,000 aggregate intercompany revenue for the Master File, aligning with Luxembourg’s transposition of EU TP documentation requirements. The Luxembourg Tax Administration may request contemporaneous documentation for any related-party transaction regardless of size if the arm’s length principle appears compromised.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies codified economic substance requirements in the law of 29 March 2013 (as amended) and Article 168bis L.I.R. Specifically, the substance doctrine requires a Luxembourg entity claiming treaty benefits, EU directive benefits, or the participation exemption to maintain sufficient local substance: (i) local management and decision-making; (ii) premises adequate to the activity; (iii) qualified full-time employees proportionate to the functions performed; (iv) relevant documentation of operational decisions; and (v) an adequate level of capitalization and operating expenditures. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) doctrine is also applicable if the entity is within the scope of the EU ATAD rules (harmful tax practices), including the requirement to file an annual economic substance return (Formulaire 705) to the Luxembourg tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years; administrative penalty of 20% to 200% of evaded tax; interest at 0.8% per month; loss of tax loss carryforward positions; exclusion from licenses and public tenders; publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers of the SARL may be subject to criminal imprisonment (up to 2 years), joint and several civil liability for the unpaid corporate tax, clawback of distributed profits, travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, and asset seizure under Article 96 of the Criminal Code and the Luxembourg Tax Fraud Law of 2016.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, denial of EU Interest & Royalty Directive relief, 15% withholding tax levied on the entire gross outflow, reclassification of intercompany financing as hidden equity distribution, net wealth tax reassessment, ATAD-grounded substance penalty under Luxembourg law of 29 March 2013, and damages for wrongful certification.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes, interest, and penalties if the entity is struck off for lack of substance; the director’s good faith is rebuttably presumed if they failed to monitor the entity’s substance reporting; personal directors’ liability insurance may be void for intentional breach; travel bans available for non-compliance in criminal tax proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the taxable base, late payment interest at 0.8% per month, a fine of up to EUR 250,000 for non-production of documentation, additional assessment of up to 10 years for fraudulent concealment, and in cross-border cases, mutual agreement procedure and potential double taxation rectification.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved falsified transfer pricing documentation or signed materially inaccurate certificates can be prosecuted for fraud, with personal liability for the resulting tax deficiency, fines up to EUR 125,000, and imprisonment up to 1 year for complicity in tax fraud.
Abusive Withholding Tax Avoidance / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Waiver of the treaty/directive reduction retroactively, 15% withholding tax plus 10% late filing surcharge, accrual of interest at 0.8% per month, denial of the participation exemption for the underlying dividend, and potential penalty of EUR 750 per false certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the dividend while knowing the treaty conditions were not met can be personally assessed for the unpaid withholding tax under the domestic liability for corporate officers; the Luxembourg criminal courts may also order restitution to the company for the loss caused by the unlawful distribution, ban the director from managing companies for up to 5 years, and impose imprisonment in cases of criminal concealment.
AML / Economic Sanctions / False Certification

Statutory sanction: AML fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; freezing orders; prohibition on the distribution of dividends; revocation of the medical device manufacturing authorization (ISIC 3250 license) and the ISO 13485 compliance certificate; disgorgement of the transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable with imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; disqualification from professional director positions; seizure of personal assets; extradition risks for cross-border financial crime; the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit can issue a travel ban and bank freezing order for imminent asset dissipation.

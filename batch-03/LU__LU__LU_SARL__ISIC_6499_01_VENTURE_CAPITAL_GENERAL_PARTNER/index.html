Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual, letterbox or shared-registered-agent address without genuine physical presence in Luxembourg is not permissible for a venture-capital general partner: the SARL must maintain its statutory seat ('siège statutaire') at a real physical address in Luxembourg, and because the entity will carry on licensed activity as an AIFM or as manager of a RAIF or SIF under CSSF supervision, it must demonstrate substantive local presence — dedicated premises, Luxembourg-based management, board meeting capacity and available staff. A virtual or registered-office arrangement may at most be used for a short transitional period pending installation of the permanent registered office; the CSSF will treat a letterbox establishment as a critical deficiency in the licensing and fit-and-proper assessment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Luxembourg direct tax authority; portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://myguichet.lu). Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Luxembourg indirect tax and duties authority; VAT registration, filings and rulings administered through MyGuichet.lu and the dedicated eTVA / EDES electronic applications.

E-government filing portal

On incorporation, every Luxembourg SARL is registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and the Luxembourg direct tax administration issues a single business reference number (matricule) used for corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT) purposes. The VAT identification number is applied for separately from the AEDT via form 640 (electronic filing on MyGuichet.lu). For a foreign entity without a Luxembourg seat, the AEDT requires a direct VAT registration application, accompanied by proof of existence, a certificate of fiscal domicile from the home jurisdiction, details of intended Luxembourg taxable activities, and, where applicable, evidence of a local fiscal representative. No hard turnover threshold applies; registration is due before the first taxable transaction. Upon approval, a LU-prefixed VAT number is assigned; processing normally completes within two weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a check-the-box election. An SARL is irrebuttably opaque and fully taxable in its own name. The principal statutory elections available are: (a) fiscal unity for income and municipal business tax under Article 164bis LIR, elective for a direct or indirect 95%-owned vertical group; (b) VAT grouping under Article 60 of the VAT Law, creating a single taxable person for Luxembourg VAT purposes; (c) the participation exemption under Article 166 LIR, applied where the holding reaches the statutory threshold (10% shareholding or EUR 1,200,000 acquisition cost) and the shares are held for at least 12 months, exempting qualifying dividends and capital gains; and (d) foreign entity classification mismatches are managed under the ATAD anti-hybrid rules, which do not permit taxpayer-driven filing elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 (annual turnover small-business exemption for Luxembourg-established businesses); EUR 10,000 (intra-Community distance-sales registration threshold for foreign EU suppliers); otherwise mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To zero-rate exports of goods, the supplier (whether established in Luxembourg or not) must prove: (i) the goods were sold to a purchaser outside the EU; (ii) the goods physically left the EU, generally within 90 days of dispatch (or within a reasonable period, with customs endorsement); (iii) possession of a definitive customs export declaration and transport documents evidencing exit from the EU; (iv) the supply was taxable in Luxembourg but for the exemption (i.e., place-of-supply rules point to Luxembourg); and (v) commercial records linking the invoice, payment, and delivery. Services ancillary to an export (transport, loading/unloading, freight forwarding) qualify for zero rating when directly linked to the export. B2B services to non-EU taxable persons are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the EU place-of-supply rules, so no Luxembourg VAT is due even though they are not technically zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or export declaration in the Luxembourg customs IT system)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, CIM rail consignment note, or Air Waybill as applicable
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing currency receipt
  • Packing list and delivery / loading note
  • Transport insurance and forwarding instructions (if available)
  • Certificate of origin and/or EUR.1 / EUSALTA statement where applicable
  • Evidence of non-EU purchaser status (e.g., certificate of incorporation, VAT registration certificate or comparable documentation)

Luxembourg neither operates real-time fiscal clearance nor requires B2B e-invoicing or digital stamping. E-invoicing is legally valid; all invoices must be preserved for at least ten years. B2G e-invoices to the Luxembourg central administration must be submitted via the PEPPOL network in compliance with European standard EN 16931 (using CIUS EDI/UBL/UBL invoice profile). VAT returns and EC sales lists must be e-filed through MyGuichet.lu; no transactional API reporting is currently mandated.

Luxembourg applies a strict evidentiary burden on the vendor in all export zero-rating claims. The AEDT may demand the full documentation bundle before accepting the 0% rate; if export is not evidenced within the statutory deadlines, VAT is assessed at the standard 17% rate with late-payment interest. The VAT Law imposes administrative penalties for non-compliance, including recurrent daily fines for failure to file and penalties for intentional under-declaration; criminal liability for VAT fraud can reach EUR 125,000 in fines and imprisonment. Ordinary assessment limitation is three years, extended to five years for omitted returns and to ten years for fraudulent conduct. Where fraudulent zero-rating is established, the AEDT retrospectively recharacterizes the supply as taxable and may claw back the VAT for all transactions in the fraudulent scheme, with joint liability applied to any party who knew or should have known of the fraud.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. Luxembourg is a Eurozone member with no exchange control legislation; capital movements and payments are liberalised under Articles 63–66 TFEU. Capital account inflows (share capital, capital calls, shareholder loans) and outflows (profit distributions, redemption of fund units, loan repayments) require no BCL or CSSF prior authorisation. The only applicable hurdles are (i) the 15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on SARL distributions to non-resident corporate shareholders, reducible under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or double tax treaties, and (ii) mandatory balance of payments statistical reporting by resident credit institutions to the BCL for cross-border transactions exceeding de minimis thresholds. No prior registration or negative clearance regime applies to FDI in the SARL or to onward investment by the GP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline for commercial or financial receipts. The SARL may hold and dispose of foreign currency freely. Cross-border receipts and payments are subject exclusively to (i) BCL balance of payments statistical capture by the resident bank, (ii) EU, UN and OFAC sanctions screening, and (iii) funds origin verification under Luxembourg AML/CFT law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended by the Law of 25 March 2020).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting as the Eurosystem national central bank; prudential supervision of Luxembourg credit institutions and authorised payment/e-money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The CSSF applies the EU Single Rulebook (CRR/CRD IV, AMLD4/5, PSD2) and issues binding circulars on money laundering (CSSF Circular 17/650 as amended) and on balance of payments reporting (BCL/CSSF joint framework).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium feasibility, subject to strict legal constraints. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 (implementing PSD2) and supervised by the CSSF may provide operational IBAN accounts, multi-currency receiving capacity and SWIFT-based outbound wires at lower cost and faster onboarding than tier-1 banks. However, for a licensed Venture Capital GP, EMIs are not a viable primary treasury rail because (i) they cannot grant credit, overdrafts or intraday liquidity; (ii) they cannot hold client money within the meaning of the AIFM Law of 12 July 2013 for fund-level capital calls; (iii) institutional LPs commonly reject EMIs in the Management Agreement's bank account covenant; and (iv) CSSF Circular 17/650 AML expectations require the GP to maintain fund flows through a fully prudentially supervised credit institution. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-authorised or Lithuanian-licensed) are further restricted by the EU 'passporting' rules and by Luxembourg banks' correspondent due diligence on foreign EMIs. Recommended architecture: tier-1 bank as the sole capital call and disbursement account; a CSSF-authorised EMI as a secondary operational bulk-payment account for small expense reimbursements, capped at EUR 50,000 per transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound funding must enter through the dedicated capital call and escrow account at the chosen tier-1 Luxembourg bank. For EUR transfers originating within SEPA, route via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) or via SWIFT MT103 if the remitting LP is outside SEPA or requires faster settlement. For non-EUR transfers (primarily USD, GBP, CHF), use SWIFT MT103 only, with the fund name, LPA date, clause reference, and 'Capital Call Drawdown' specified in field 70 (Remittance Information). The resident bank automatically captures the transaction for BCL balance of payments statistics under the ECB BOP6 classification, using the primary purpose code 'Direct investment – Equity capital' for injections into the SARL's own capital and 'Financial intermediation – Investment fund shares/units' for LP drawdowns into the regulated VC fund vehicle. The GP must instruct the bank to attach purpose code D.04 (or the bank-specific equivalent) and must provide (i) an executed capital commitment schedule per LP, (ii) the fund's banking authorisation resolution, and (iii) a source-of-funds certificate for any single injection above EUR 1,000,000. No prior BCL approval is required, but the bank must receive the beneficiary's full name and IBAN (LU00 BCCL...), and the GP must confirm that neither the remitter nor the underlying investor is a sanctioned or restricted person.Portfolio investments, follow-on financings, bridge loans, and management fee payments are cleared through the same operational account. Standard clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for all high-value cross-border wires to portfolio companies and to legal/ advisory counterparties, with the investment SPA/shareholder agreement reference in field 70; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV when the payment is directed through an intermediary bank for onward credit to the beneficiary's account, ensuring the ordering customer's information is transmitted in compliance with the FATF Recommendation 16 wire transfer rules; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfers for all EUR disbursements within the SEPA zone, including to portfolio companies in Germany, France, Belgium, and the Netherlands; (iv) TARGET2 immediate settlement for intra-day, critical closings above EUR 1,000,000; and (v) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) or Standby LCs (ISP98) only where the transaction is structured as a deferred payment in an acquisition or bridge financing; these are typically issued on the GP bank's own credit line and are not available against escrow balances. All outbound transfers require the bank to capture the balance of payments purpose code corresponding to the transaction (portfolio investment in non-resident equity for share purchases; management fees under 'management services'; and interest/principal under 'loan and credit operations'). The SARL must retain supporting board-approved investment minutes and the executed transaction documents at the registered office for a minimum of 5 years to satisfy the bank's post-transaction documentation audit.EUR/USD deliverable FX forward contracts, tenor up to 12 months, documented under the 1998 ISDA FX and Currency Option Definitions or the 2021 ISDA Master Agreement, available only against a signed FX ISDA or the bank's standard FX terms and credit support annex.EUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX swaps (swap points executed with one-week to three-month tenors), permissible for treasury working-capital smoothing and for periodic management fee payments, without margining if trade size is below the bank's FX credit line, otherwise subject to two-way collateral.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for medium-term acquisition financing purposes, permissible only where the SARL has an underlying debt facility in EUR and the portfolio asset generates cash flows in USD, with settlement netting performed via the clearing system at the target bank.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to portfolio exposures in emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) where the SARL holds unhedged equity stakes in unlisted technology companies; NDF tenors limited to 6 months and strictly linked to a documented underlying portfolio valuation event, because the Bank cannot settle NDFs without the beneficiary portfolio company's audited valuation data.Plain vanilla FX options (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF) as permitted premium instruments solely for protecting an executed investment agreement against adverse currency movement between signing and closing, not for speculative use; options require a specific board resolution and prior approval of the bank's foreign exchange desk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium feasibility, subject to strict legal constraints. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the Law of 10 November 2009 (implementing PSD2) and supervised by the CSSF may provide operational IBAN accounts, multi-currency receiving capacity and SWIFT-based outbound wires at lower cost and faster onboarding than tier-1 banks. However, for a licensed Venture Capital GP, EMIs are not a viable primary treasury rail because (i) they cannot grant credit, overdrafts or intraday liquidity; (ii) they cannot hold client money within the meaning of the AIFM Law of 12 July 2013 for fund-level capital calls; (iii) institutional LPs commonly reject EMIs in the Management Agreement's bank account covenant; and (iv) CSSF Circular 17/650 AML expectations require the GP to maintain fund flows through a fully prudentially supervised credit institution. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-authorised or Lithuanian-licensed) are further restricted by the EU 'passporting' rules and by Luxembourg banks' correspondent due diligence on foreign EMIs. Recommended architecture: tier-1 bank as the sole capital call and disbursement account; a CSSF-authorised EMI as a secondary operational bulk-payment account for small expense reimbursements, capped at EUR 50,000 per transaction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% top-band CIT (15% on first EUR 175,000 taxable income) + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax (standard Luxembourg City rate) = aggregate headline rate of approximately 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic dividend withholding tax; reduced to 0% under the participation exemption (10% shareholding or EUR 6,000,000 acquisition price held for 12 months) and 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate recipients.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is 31 May of the year following the calendar tax year; the ACD routinely grants an automatic extension to 31 December for returns filed through eCDF. No carryback of tax losses is permitted.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Notification Form 510: no later than 31 December of the reporting fiscal year. CbC report: within 12 months of the fiscal year end (surrogate filing may extend under the CbC Competent Authority Agreement).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 450,000 - Local File obligation for the Luxembourg entity when aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 450,000 in a fiscal year; Master File and CbC Report obligation when the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a Gulf-style ESR code, but economic substance is enforced through: (1) ATAD GAAR (law of 21 December 2017) recharacterizing artificial arrangements lacking commercial reality; (2) LIR Article 166 anti-abuse exception denying the participation exemption where the GP is a 'letterbox'; (3) AIFMD substance requirements for an authorized VC GP (two Luxembourg-based directors, physical registered office, investment decisions made in Luxembourg, delegated functions restricted to regulated delegates); (4) ACD Circular LIR No. 56/1 minimum substance guidance. The GP must demonstrate payroll, premises, board meetings, and compliance/KYC functions in Luxembourg to support treaty access and 0% dividend WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100% of the evaded CIT; mandatory late-payment interest at 0.8% per month; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for natural persons; corporate dissolution is possible in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and civil restitution of diverted assets; travel bans (restriction on leaving the Schengen area); criminal imprisonment for the masterminding director.
Shell Company / Letterbox and GAAR (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: ACD recharacterization under the ATAD GAAR; denial of participation exemption and treaty benefits; retroactive CIT at a combined 24.94% rate plus re-grossed 15% dividend WHT; negligence penalty of 10%; CSSF revocation of the AIFM/RAIF license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for unpaid corporate taxes when the entity is deemed a sham; criminal exposure for 'gestion frauduleuse'; professional disqualification and refusal of future CSSF fit-and-proper approvals.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ACD adjustment to the arm's-length price; negligence penalty up to 10% (up to 100% for deliberate mispricing); interest at 0.8% per month; documentation fines up to EUR 25,000; risk of economic double taxation and secondary adjustments.

Director personal liability: Director sign-off liability for inaccurate statutory accounts; characterization as 'fraude a la loi' piercing the corporate veil; civil exposure to LPs for inflated tax leakage and audit costs.
AML/CFT and KYC Violations (VC GP Specific)

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 (as amended): money-laundering imprisoned 5 to 10 years; fines up to EUR 250,000 for natural persons or EUR 1,250,000 for legal persons; CSSF administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; license revocation and public censure.

Director personal liability: The AML compliance officer and managing director face personal criminal liability; travel bans and asset freezes; confiscation orders against personal assets; prohibition from holding management positions in regulated entities.
Unauthorized Fund Management / License Infringement

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under the AIFM Law: imprisonment and fines; all management agreements rendered void; CSSF injunctions and winding-up orders; retroactive reassessment of the GP's income with 15% dividend WHT exposure.

Director personal liability: Each participating director is personally liable for damages to investors; prohibition from company management for up to 10 years; extradition alerts triggered in cross-border criminal proceedings.

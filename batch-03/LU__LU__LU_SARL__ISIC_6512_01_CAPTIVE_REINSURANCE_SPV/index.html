Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for a Luxembourg S.à r.l. is EUR 12,000; the capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked account held with a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank's blockage certificate is delivered to the notary. Contributions in kind require an approved auditor's valuation report. The blocked funds are released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Administration des services de secours (CGDIS) and the municipal warrant service; structural load inspection and building occupancy certificate obtained from the competent commune building authority for the registered office; occupational safety and health clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) upon first employee hire.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Luxembourg SARL is an opaque corporation per se: subject to corporate income tax (IRC) at 17% central rate, municipal business tax (ICC) at the commune rate (Luxembourg City approximately 6.75%, aggregate top rate approximately 24.94%), and the 7% solidarity surcharge on IRC; net wealth tax applies at 0.5% with the EUR 500,000 exemption band and the 15% minimum net wealth tax for regulated financial entities, though the global minimum top-up tax under Pillar Two applies to groups with EUR 750M+ revenue. Captive reinsurance SPVs cannot elect pass-through treatment and have no territorial scope exemption; profits distributed as dividends incur 15% withholding tax, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and full Luxembourg participation exemption conditions.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory and hybrid-originated mismatches are neutralized automatically under ATAD 1/2. Available elections: (1) Corporate income tax group (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR - a 95%-owned resident subsidiary may be consolidated into the parent IRC/ICC base where the SARL is held to 95% for the full fiscal year; (2) VAT group (assujetti unique) under Art. 59bis VAT Law - closely linked resident taxpayer entities may elect joint registration and pooling, of limited use because reinsurance is exempt; (3) VAT election on real estate leases under Art. 43 VAT Law to recover input VAT on the captive office lease; (4) SOPARFI participation exemption (Art. 166 LIR) applies automatically to qualifying participations (10% or EUR 1.2M cost, taxable subsidiary, retained for 12 months) and eliminates dividend and capital gain taxation; (5) IP box election under Art. 50ter LIR granting an 80% exemption on net IP income for qualifying assets; (6) SME VAT franchise under Art. 57 VAT Law is not viable for a licensed captive reinsurer. No elective regime for hybrid branch mismatches; the captive's CAA solvency classification under Solvency II is independent of VAT and income tax elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business VAT franchise under Art. 57 VAT Law. This threshold does not benefit a licensed captive reinsurer: VAT registration is mandatory for any taxable person established in Luxembourg, regardless of turnover, once it carries out exempt reinsurance activities or incurs input VAT on overheads; intra-EU distance sales of goods in excess of EUR 10,000 B2C trigger registration through the EU One-Stop-Shop; non-resident re/insurers writing Luxembourg risks must register without threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports from Luxembourg to third-country territories are zero-rated under Art. 56 VAT Law if the goods leave the EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply and the exporter has lodged an accepted customs export declaration. Intra-EU transfers require dispatch of the goods and a valid customer VAT number. International services supplied to a taxable person in another state or to a non-EU business are outside the Luxembourg VAT scope by reverse charge. Premiums ceded to a Luxembourg captive reinsurer are exempt without credit under Art. 44(1)(e) VAT Law; exemption (not zero-rating) is the applicable indirect tax treatment, and the captive may not offset input VAT unless the costs directly support taxable activities.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD EX) filed via DELTA-Declaration with EORI reference
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note demonstrating physical departure
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate evidencing the non-EU customer's payment
  • Reinsurance treaty, cession slips, bordereaux, and cedant confirmation letters where the supply is an exempt reinsurance service

Luxembourg does not operate a continuous transaction controls (CTC) clearance regime: no real-time fiscal pre-approval, no unique invoice stamping, and no mandatory SAF-T filing. Under the Law of 3 June 2019, structured electronic invoicing (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 XML) is mandatory for all business-to-government (B2G) invoices issued pursuant to public procurement contracts since 1 January 2022, submitted via the national e-Facture platform (https://efacture.public.lu). B2B e-invoicing remains optional; invoicing must occur within 15 days of the taxable event and invoices must be retained in original format for 10 years. Taxpayers with turnover above EUR 33,000,000 must e-file monthly VAT returns in the LED (Liasse électronique déclarative) system; AEDT may request the Fichier des Écritures Comptables (FEC) through the eDesk audit module. Reverse charge applies to B2B cross-border services and is reported in the EC sales listings; OSS procedures are electronic.

The AEDT and customs division may re-audit export and exemption files within 5 years (10 years for fraud). Failure to substantiate zero-rating leads to reclassification at 17% VAT, assessed interest, and penalties; proven fraud attracts criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, loss of input VAT, and retroactive clawback. For captive reinsurance, inconsistent treaty documentation or circular premium flows are recharacterized as taxable management fees at 17%, voiding the VAT exemption and exposing the SPV to criminal referral and CAA licence withdrawal.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty provisions on the free movement of capital. There are no exchange-control quotas, licensing regimes, or ad hoc approval requirements for profit repatriation. Capital account transactions such as share capital increases, shareholder loans, and distributions are freely permitted, subject to corporate law solvency restrictions, anti-money-laundering verification, tax obligations, and EU targeted financial sanctions. Current account transactions, including cross-border reinsurance premium receipts and claim payments, are unrestricted. Practical hurdles are limited to balance-of-payments statistics reporting to the BCL and enhanced customer due diligence by banks when the captive reinsurance SPV has complex ownership or cross-border underlying risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion window, and no minimum conversion ratio for export or reinsurance premium receipts denominated in foreign currency. A Luxembourg captive reinsurance SPV may retain, convert, or remit foreign-currency proceeds at its discretion. The only relevant compliance duty is the BCL’s external statistics and balance-of-payments reporting framework for cross-border transactions by resident undertakings; this is a statistical reporting obligation, not an exchange-control surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and the legal framework of the European System of Central Banks; the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commissariat aux Assurances retain regulatory authority over reinsurance authorization and insurance-related financial reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility for a licensed captive reinsurance SPV. Luxembourg recognizes electronic money institutions (EMIs) exploiting the EU passport under the E-Money Directive and PSD2, but the Commissariat aux Assurances and commercial banks generally expect the captive to maintain its primary underwriting reserves and claims ledger through a regulated credit institution. An EU-licensed EMI or non-bank payment institution may be used only for ancillary payment operations, not as the sole treasury rail. Legal constraints include safeguarding obligations, transaction monitoring, and the inability of an EMI to provide credit, structured FX hedging, or regulated reinsurance reserve custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EUR capital injections from the parent or group treasury should be routed through TARGET2 via the originating EU bank to the Luxembourg beneficiary account, with the payment field containing the SPV legal name, SARL registration number or bank account reference, and the purpose 'Capital contribution / share subscription'. Non-EUR capital injections should use SWIFT MT103 with clear beneficiary bank identifiers and the same capital-contribution purpose. The receiving bank will verify corporate authority, UBO documentation, and source of funds before crediting the account. For share capital at incorporation, the funds must be credited to a blocked account in the name of the SARL in formation and confirmed by the notary before public filing with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register.For reinsurance claims, ceding commissions, and retrocession settlements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 direct credit to the beneficiary cedant or reinsurer, using field 70 to reference the treaty number, claim reference, and confirmation of liability. For high-value commercial transactions in Europe, TARGET2 and SEPA provide compressed euro clearing; for non-euro trade settlements, commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600 and advised by SWIFT MT700/MT710, with reimbursement through MT103/MT202 and SWIFT MT760 guarantees, are permissible where the captive supports group commodity or supply-chain risk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg prescribes no statutory de minimis threshold; however, in administrative practice a Local File with a contemporaneous benchmark study and transfer pricing documentation is expected where related-party transactions (goods, services, financial, royalty) exceed EUR 1,000,000 per fiscal year. A Master File becomes relevant where the multinational group has consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 50,000,000. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the 'substance-over-form' doctrine and the anti-abuse provisions of the General Tax Law enable the Direct Tax Administration (ACD) to disregard an artificial structure. For a captive reinsurance SPV, the ACD expects genuine operational substance to be demonstrable: physical office premises, senior actuarial and underwriting staff in Luxembourg, regular board meetings with real decision-making, and actual risk-taking through the captive. Failure to satisfy these substance facts can invalidate treaty benefits, trigger a re-characterisation of the captive profit, and lead to deemed dividend taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Concealment of Income

Statutory sanction: Under Article 109(2) of the Luxembourg General Tax Law (AO) and Article 397 of the Criminal Code, a fine of up to EUR 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 3 years, plus payment of the evaded tax, default interest and a monetary penalty of up to 120% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally held under the doctrine of 'piercing the corporate veil' if they gave the unlawful instructions. This may lead to civil restitution, a travel ban and imprisonment. In cross-border cases, mutual legal assistance shall locate assets abroad.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ACD may annul the corporate structure as a sham and re-attribute all income to the controlling owner. This triggers a 15% dividend WHT on deemed profit repatriation, plus default interest of 0.8% per month and an assessment to net wealth tax. Criminal penalties apply if the sham was intentional.

Director personal liability: A director can be criminally convicted for falsifying the annual accounts or aiding an abuse of law; in addition, civil liability under Article 442 of the Commercial Code requires repayment of unlawful dividends or benefits, and a travel ban may be issued to prevent non-disclosure.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: The Financial Supervisory Authority (CSSF/ACD) can impose a warning and an administrative penalty of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover, under the AML Law of 12 November 2004. Criminal courts may impose a fine of up to EUR 1,250,000 for legal persons and imprisonment of up to 5 years for natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors, as officers of a non-bank financial institution subject to AML obligations, can be held personally criminally liable for failure to prevent money laundering, and are subject to disqualification from serving as directors, potential travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax law empowers the ACD to make a primary transfer pricing adjustment, re-determining the taxable profit at arm's length. This results in a tax deficiency plus default interest of 0.8% per month and a 25% negligence penalty (industrial interest). Misleading documentation can attract a penalty of up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: If a director approved transfer pricing documentation that knowingly mispriced a transaction, they can be jointly liable for the resulting tax deficit through civil proceedings by the Tax Administration; criminal liability for negligent misrepresentation arises under Article 397 of the Criminal Code, with travel bans possible for corporate officers facing extradition.
Unreported Profit Repatriation / Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The tax authority collects the WHT of 15% as a statutory debt, plus default interest of 0.8% per month. Evasion penalties are taxed at 10% of the sum; if fraud is proven, an additional criminal fine of up to EUR 75,000 per payment is imposed and the arrears must be immediately paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the unobserved distribution are personally liable to the fiscal administrator for the WHT amount (Art. 127 LIR, deemed officer liability), and can be sued in civil courts for restitution. For international structures, an arrest warrant and travel ban can be issued.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Ministry of Justice; exclusive e-filing channel: MyGuichet.lu.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 179 to 200 (SARL provisions), as amended by the Law of 23 September 2016; supplemented by the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés).

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif) with Articles of Association (Statuts), filed at the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital under Article 179 of the Law of 10 August 1915. The capital must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of each cash share (minimum EUR 3,000) must be paid into a blocked escrow account (compte de souscription bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution before RCS registration. In-kind contributions are fully paid up at incorporation and require a réviseur d'entreprises statutory auditor's report. The remaining 75% is payable within five years.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (certificat de sécurité incendie) from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) verifying means of escape, extinguishers, and safe storage of flammable resins; structural load inspection certificate for the workshop floor; and workplace safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), based on the risk assessment (document unique), ventilation control, PPE provision and first-aid organization.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum share capital under Article 179 of the Law of 10 August 1915. The capital must be fully subscribed; at least 25% of each cash share (minimum EUR 3,000) must be paid into a blocked escrow account (compte de souscription bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution before RCS registration. In-kind contributions are fully paid up at incorporation and require a réviseur d'entreprises statutory auditor's report. The remaining 75% is payable within five years.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; official filing and registration portals: impotsdirects.public.lu, myguichet.lu, and the Tax-on-Web e-filing platform for Luxembourg VAT and subscription tax declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign owner of a Luxembourg SARL must (1) enrol the company at the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) through an appointed local manager by filing the articles of association and the registered office declaration, thereby obtaining the business registration number that serves as the master corporate identifier; (2) obtain the 13-digit national identification number (NIF) from the ACD once the registration is published; (3) file the VAT registration form (Form 640) with the AEDT to secure the LU+8-digit VAT identification number before making any taxable supply in Luxembourg; and (4) complete ancillary registrations (CCSS social security for staff, beneficial ownership declaration at the LBR, and EORI application with the Luxembourg customs administration for export operations). VAT registration is mandatory without turnover threshold where a taxable transaction occurs, but small undertakings may claim the EUR 35,000 annual turnover exemption for supplies to non-taxable persons; the EUR 10,000 threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales and OSS services. A non-resident manager or day-to-day director requires an EU/EEA/Swiss residence address to preserve the Luxembourg management seat.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (worldwide taxation of a resident SARL; territorial exemptions available under the participation-exemption regime); a SARL is a fully taxable resident company if its registered office or central administration is in Luxembourg, and is subject to corporate income tax (statutory rate 17% for income above the EUR 175,000 band; approximate aggregate effective rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City including the solidarity/unemployment surcharge and communal business tax), net wealth tax (0.5% annual), and full VAT liability. There is no pass-through or check-the-box regime for SARLs, no tax-exempt zone regime for manufacturing, and non-resident ownership of capital is fully permitted. — A foreign owner of a Luxembourg SARL must (1) enrol the company at the Luxembourg Business Registers (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés) through an appointed local manager by filing the articles of association and the registered office declaration, thereby obtaining the business registration number that serves as the master corporate identifier; (2) obtain the 13-digit national identification number (NIF) from the ACD once the registration is published; (3) file the VAT registration form (Form 640) with the AEDT to secure the LU+8-digit VAT identification number before making any taxable supply in Luxembourg; and (4) complete ancillary registrations (CCSS social security for staff, beneficial ownership declaration at the LBR, and EORI application with the Luxembourg customs administration for export operations). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (worldwide taxation of a resident SARL; territorial exemptions available under the participation-exemption regime); a SARL is a fully taxable resident company if its registered office or central administration is in Luxembourg, and is subject to corporate income tax (statutory rate 17% for income above the EUR 175,000 band; approximate aggregate effective rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City including the solidarity/unemployment surcharge and communal business tax), net wealth tax (0.5% annual), and full VAT liability. There is no pass-through or check-the-box regime for SARLs, no tax-exempt zone regime for manufacturing, and non-resident ownership of capital is fully permitted.

Available election forms

There is no U.S.-style check-the-box election in Luxembourg, but the SARL may elect: (i) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for intra-group resident losses and profit offsets; (ii) participation exemption (Schachtelprivileg) election to exempt qualifying dividends and capital gains; (iii) notional interest deduction (NID) on qualifying new equity; (iv) IP Box deduction for qualifying intellectual property income; (v) group VAT unit as a single taxable person for financial/insurance groups under Article 60(3) of the VAT law; and (vi) express election to leave the small-undertaking scheme by registering for VAT even below the threshold. ATAD hybrid mismatch provisions (Article 168bis LIR) operate as mandatory disallowances and cannot be contracted around by election or hybrid instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for supplies to final consumers (small-undertaking exemption, electable); EUR 10,000 annual turnover for intra-Community distance sales and electronic/telecom services (OSS threshold); no threshold for physical B2B supplies in Luxembourg subject to the obligation of self-identification and reverse charge by the customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies when the SARL dispatches polyresin statues and garage kits outside the EU for a consideration and can prove that the goods physically left EU customs territory within 90 days from the supply (rebuttable presumption per EU Implementing Regulation 2018/1912 and AEDT practice). Eligibility requires a valid electronic export declaration (SAD) accepted by the customs office of actual export, reliable transport evidence (CMR/airway bill/bill of lading), the commercial invoice, and a corresponding EORI-linked shipment record. Plastic statuettes and composite resin articles under HS 3926 (e.g., 3926.40) require correct CN classification; artistic/replica statues may fall under HS 9705/4420 where applicable. Directly related export services may also be zero-rated. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable through monthly/quarterly Tax-on-Web claims; non-established EU suppliers use the EU VAT refund procedure (Directive 2008/9/EC) and non-EU suppliers use the Thirteenth Directive regime, with proof of export held in the refund file.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD with validated EORI number in Luxembourg customs IT system)
  • Transport documentation proving cross-border exit: CMR waybill for road transport, bill of lading for sea freight, or air waybill for air cargo
  • Commercial invoice issued by LU_SARL with reference to the export declaration
  • Customs exit confirmation (electronic 'exitus' message in the EU NCTS) or customs stamp for non-NCTS consignments
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank statement evidencing settlement by the non-EU buyer
  • Warehouse exit register or sworn carrier attestation for consignment and hub-export flows

No real-time clearance, tax-authority stamping, or continuous transaction-control API applies to B2B/B2C invoicing in Luxembourg. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to B2G contracts with central public entities since 1 January 2024 and will be extended to all public-sector purchasers through 2026; invoices must comply with EN 16931 and be transmitted through the national Peppol network (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 with UBL or Factur-X); suppliers must expose a Peppol access point (API connectivity) but no fiscal pre-clearance exists. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary in PDF/XML; EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) will introduce transaction-based e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-Community supplies on a projected 2030/2035 timeline.

Luxembourg VAT law does not impose a statutory FX-repatriation condition inside the zero-rating rule; the 180-day figure is the compliance pipeline standard for receipt of export funds. The AEDT applies heightened audit scrutiny to export zero-rating when goods are declared through the SARL rather than by the customer and when documentation is digital-only; absence of 90-day export proof causes retrospective re-characterisation as a domestic taxable supply, generating VAT at 17%, late-payment interest at 0.8% per commenced month, and administrative fines (statutory fine bands, multiplied up to 3 times the evaded VAT in proven fraud cases). Deliberate sham-export schemes attract criminal prosecution before the Luxembourg criminal courts, extension of the statute of limitation from 3 to 10 years, revocation of EORI and customs simplifications, denial of input VAT deduction, publication of sanction decisions, and, for group structures, joint and several liability of the SARL's legal representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Resident currency is the euro (EUR); full convertibility on both current and capital account under Articles 63–66 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC). No Luxembourg exchange control law, no prior authorization, no forex declaration, and no mandatory EUR conversion applies to inbound equity injections, export receivables, or profit remittances. Dividend distributions to a foreign parent are unrestricted under the Law of 10 August 1915 solvency tests and incur 15% withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% participation held ≥12 months); no exchange-control surcharge applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline, surrender ratio, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency. Export revenues from resin-statue e-commerce (Stripe/PayPal USD/EUR settlements) may be retained freely, converted at market EUR/USD rates, or used directly to pay foreign suppliers; only statistical BCL balance-of-payments reporting via bank purpose codes (e.g., S001/S109) is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — monetary authority, balance-of-payments statistics, and FX-market supervision; Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) — competent authority for credit institutions, payment institutions, and AML/CFT supervision; Ministère des Finances — EU/UN financial sanctions enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary collection and FX conversion rail, but not as the primary corporate bank. EU-licensed EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and are CSSF-supervised, and they may lawfully safeguard e-commerce proceeds in segregated accounts at EU credit institutions; however, an EMI cannot hold the SARL statutory capital blocked account (Art. 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 requires a Luxembourg credit institution), cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and is not eligible for the RCS/notarial capital certificate. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity is wired in EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the SARL's blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution before notarization (min. €12,000 under Art. 710-1); BCL balance-of-payments purpose code S001 (Direct investment — equity capital) must be entered in the wire's remittance information; the remitter name must match the shareholder disclosed in the incorporation file to avoid AML rejection; no BCL prior approval is required. After the notarial deed, RESA publication and RCS registration, the bank releases the block and funds move by internal transfer to the operational account. If the foreign shareholder wires USD, conversion to EUR occurs at the remitting bank or via the Luxembourg receiving bank's EUR/USD spot; the blocked-account certificate is then issued by the credit institution for the notary.Primary rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for intra-EEA suppliers (T+1 settlement, no correspondent fees); (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA raw-material purchases with purpose code and documentary backup (invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading); (3) Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP 600 for bulk resin/pigment procurement requiring bank payment undertakings — available through BGL BNP Paribas/BIL trade desks; (4) Stripe/PayPal settlements routed from their EU acquiring banks into the Luxembourg operational account via SEPA or MT103; (5) corporate purchasing card for sub-threshold consumables and freight; USD outflows are converted at spot via the bank or an EMI sweep account; all disbursements must be reconciled against pre-order deposits to maintain audit trail.EUR/USD deliverable vanilla FX forward (OTC) — tenors 1–12 months under ISDA 2002 or a Luxembourg FX Master Agreement, with BGL BNP Paribas or BIL; aligned to pre-order deposit cycle.EUR/USD FX swap (tom-next/spot-next) — intra-month liquidity rebalancing between USD e-commerce collections and EUR supplier disbursements.EUR/USD European-style FX option (call/put) — premium-bounded protection for concentrated one-off collections (large limited-edition launches).EUR/USD zero-cost collar — for a confirmed annual order book; removes premium cost while capping downside/upside.Cross-currency swap (EUR/USD) — permissible but disfavored for the SARL absent long-term debt; only if a trade-finance facility is structured with the bank.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — not applicable and not offered by Luxembourg resident banks: EUR is fully convertible and a deep onshore deliverable EUR/USD market exists; NDFs would carry prohibitive pricing and regulatory documentation burden.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a supplementary collection and FX conversion rail, but not as the primary corporate bank. EU-licensed EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) can passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and are CSSF-supervised, and they may lawfully safeguard e-commerce proceeds in segregated accounts at EU credit institutions; however, an EMI cannot hold the SARL statutory capital blocked account (Art. 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 requires a Luxembourg credit institution), cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and is not eligible for the RCS/notarial capital certificate. Recommended structure: Tier-1 bank for capital/escrow, LC and settlement; a CSSF-registered EMI for high-frequency Stripe/PayPal USD collection sweeps and sub-€50k supplier disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined headline rate for Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); other communes apply municipal business tax rates between approximately 6.75% and 11.2%

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends; 0% for qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% shareholding held at least 12 months); 0% for repayments of subscribed share capital

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 group consolidated turnover triggers mandatory Master File and Local File; per-transaction Local File thresholds are EUR 1,000,000 for goods, EUR 500,000 for other services, EUR 250,000 for financial transactions (interest) and intangible royalty flows; Country-by-Country reporting required for groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate formal Economic Substance Regulations; substance is nevertheless mandated through the abuse-of-law clause (article 6 Tax Adaptation Law), ATAD GAAR and the administrative concept of real activity. A resin-statue SARL must maintain physical premises, local employees, active board decisions in Luxembourg, and genuine commercial activity; a 'letterbox' profile risks refusal of tax residency certificates, denial of DTA benefits, re-qualification of payments as constructive dividends, and ATAD Unshell scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all underpaid taxes plus 0.5% monthly late-payment interest; criminal fines of EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 per tax year per tax; aggravated fraud using organized or cross-border arrangements increases fines and imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for reassessed corporate taxes and unpaid withholding amounts; criminal conviction triggers imprisonment between five and ten years, travel bans, prohibition from managing commercial companies, and civil restitution of evaded sums
Letterbox Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Abuse-of-law reassessment with a 50% increase of the evaded tax; refusal of the ACD tax residence certificate; cancellation of VAT registration; compulsory de-registration from the Luxembourg trade register; administrative bookkeeping fines up to EUR 25,000

Director personal liability: Directors' personal assets attachable for reassessed taxes; deemed constructive dividends taxed at 15% withholding; travel restrictions and disqualification from board positions in Luxembourg until full settlement
Anti-Money-Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML Law of 12 November 2004 as amended: up to EUR 1,000,000 for the legal entity and up to EUR 250,000 for individuals; persistent breach triggers RBE de-registration, freezing of bank accounts, and compulsory winding-up

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability for money laundering and facilitation of undeclared flows (imprisonment up to five years); civil restitution, international travel bans, asset seizure, and permanent disqualification from commercial activity
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment plus 0.5% monthly interest; administrative fines up to EUR 250,000; disallowance of deductible intra-group expenses; secondary adjustment treated as constructive dividend exposing a 15% withholding tax obligation

Director personal liability: Directors jointly liable for missing TP documentation and unpaid withholding taxes; participation in artificial pricing triggers criminal aiding of tax evasion; professional ban and travel restrictions imposed until penalties and interest are discharged

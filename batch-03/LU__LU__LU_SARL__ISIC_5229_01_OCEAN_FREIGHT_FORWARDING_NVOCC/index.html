Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended by the Law of 23 June 2016 and the Law of 19 December 2020; SARL-specific regime under Articles 180-190; incorporation formalities and third-party effect under Articles 11-12; beneficial owner disclosure under the Law of 13 January 2019 (RBE Register).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised Deed of Incorporation (acte constitutif) filed with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 60 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum issued and fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 under Articles 180-181 of the 1915 Act. Cash contributions must be deposited into a dedicated blocked corporate bank account in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, and the bank's blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) must be delivered to the notary for attachment to the deed; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS), including an on-site inspection and issuance of the safety attestation (attestation de sécurité incendie); structural load assessment of the registered office for document archives and container-file storage racks; workplace health and safety clearance by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) under the Labour Code, comprising the mandatory general risk assessment (évaluation des risques), fire-exit planning, and first-aid organisation.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum issued and fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 under Articles 180-181 of the 1915 Act. Cash contributions must be deposited into a dedicated blocked corporate bank account in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, and the bank's blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) must be delivered to the notary for attachment to the deed; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report (réviseur d'entreprises agréé).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct Taxation: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), Luxembourg (portal: impotsdirects.public.lu). Indirect Taxation and VAT: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AED), Luxembourg (portal: aed.public.lu). Customs: Administration des Douanes et Accises (ADT), Luxembourg (portal: douanes.public.lu). Unified digital front office: MyGuichet.lu (corporate registration) and MyTax.lu (electronic filing and assessment).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL through the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) at the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); a 13-digit national identification number (n° d'identification) is automatically issued and functions as the corporate TIN. Step 2 — File VAT registration (form 640-F) with the AED via MyGuichet.lu; a non-established foreign entity must register before any taxable supply in Luxembourg (zero threshold) and appoint a Luxembourg-resident fiscal agent (représentant responsable solidaire) when no local establishment exists; the AED grants a VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits within approximately 15 business days. Step 3 — Obtain an EORI number from the ADT for customs declarations and register for excise or bonded-storage procedures if used. Step 4 — Register with the ACD for employer withholding (taxe sur les salaires) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) once staff or premises are located in Luxembourg. Step 5 — Activate MyTax.lu e-filing with a LuxTrust certificate and appoint a tax representative where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The SARL is a Luxembourg tax-resident capital company taxed on worldwide profits: corporate income tax (IRC) at 17%, solidarity surcharge at 7% of IRC, municipal business tax (IBT) at 6.75% in Luxembourg City, yielding an aggregate headline rate of 24.94%; net wealth tax at 0.5% of net assets (EUR 500,000 allowance; SME growth allowance up to EUR 4.5 million). Pass-through status is unavailable; NVOCC freight-forwarding profits are fully allocated to the entity and are not within the Luxembourg tonnage-tax regime, which is reserved for qualifying vessel owners and operators.

Available election forms

Fiscal unity election (intégration fiscale, art. 164bis LIR) for groups holding at least 99% of Luxembourg subsidiaries, consolidating CIT and IBT after prior ACD approval with a minimum five-year commitment. No check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralised under the ATAD implementing Law of 21 December 2018. VAT group election (art. 62bis Loi TVA) permits closely linked Luxembourg entities to be treated as a single taxable person. Small business exemption election (art. 57 Loi TVA) applies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 35,000. Participation exemption election (art. 166 LIR) is available for qualifying shareholdings held through a Soparfi. Tax-neutral merger, demerger, and asset-transfer rollover elections are available under arts. 170–176 LIR.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-resident foreign entities: obligatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg, with no turnover threshold. Resident small businesses: optional exemption under art. 57 Loi TVA up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover; mandatory registration above that amount. The reverse charge applies without threshold to freight and ancillary transport services purchased from non-resident carriers where the B2B place of supply is Luxembourg; the NVOCC must self-assess VAT under reverse charge and may recover the corresponding input VAT in the same return.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate (exemption with right to deduct, art. 44 Loi TVA) applies to: (1) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the EU [art. 44(1)(a)]; (2) international transport of goods to a destination outside the EU [art. 44(1)(c)]; (3) freight-forwarding, brokerage, cargo handling, customs clearance, and other services directly connected to the export of goods [art. 44(3)–(6)]; and (4) ancillary services for goods in transit to non-EU destinations. An NVOCC zero-rates its ocean consolidation, booking, and handling fees where the consolidated cargo leaves the EU under a non-EU bill of lading; for international transport, the zero-rating is pro-rated by distance travelled outside the EU under the art. 17 place-of-supply rule for B2B services. Substantiation requires full export evidence, correct 0% VAT coding on invoices with the reference 'Exonération art. 44' and the export document number, and inward settlement of proceeds within the 180-day repatriation window. The reverse-charged VAT on vessel-operator freight purchases remains fully deductible against the zero-rated output.

  • Customs Export Declaration — LU-CERT/ECS electronic export message with MRN, customs office of export, and date of exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading — master or NVOCC house bill evidencing a non-EU consignee and a non-EU port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 or Luxembourg bank statement evidencing receipt of freight proceeds in EUR within 180 days of the payment request
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — HS codes, Incoterms, declared customs value, weight, and container references
  • Continuous Proof of Export — CMR road consignment notes for the pre-carriage leg to the EU port, T1 transit documents, exit summary message (EXS), and terminal loading lists

Luxembourg does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, API telemetry, or digital stamping. Business-to-government e-invoicing via Peppol BIS 3.0 (Factur-X/UBL) has been mandatory since 1 January 2022 for invoices issued to Luxembourg public-sector entities. B2B electronic invoicing is lawful upon recipient consent (art. 85(1) Loi TVA), including EDI or EN16931-compliant XML; paper and PDF equivalents remain acceptable. VAT returns (form 1074) are e-filed monthly for turnover above EUR 2 million, quarterly for turnover between EUR 112,000 and EUR 2 million, and annually for lower turnover, exclusively through MyTax.lu; recapitulative statements for intra-Community services are filed via the CSL system. All invoices and supporting records must be retained for 10 years.

The AED audits export zero-rating under the standard 5-year VAT limitation period, extended to 10 years where fraud is established. Any export voucher bundle that is incomplete — including absence of the inward remittance certificate within the 180-day window — is rejected; the VAT is retroactively clawed back and re-assessed at 17% with default interest at 5% per annum. Civil fines under art. 61 Loi TVA range from 10% of the VAT for formal negligence to 100% for deliberate concealment; invoicing, e-invoicing, and archiving breaches attract fines of EUR 750–25,000. The AED may impose joint and several liability on an NVOCC acting as fiscal agent for the VAT of non-registered foreign principals under art. 64 Loi TVA. Criminal sanctions under §397 of the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) provide for imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to EUR 250,000 for fraudulent zero-rating claims by de jure or de facto officers. Refund claims are suspended during audit, and fraudulently obtained refunds are subject to immediate clawback with accrual of penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is an integral member of the euro area, and euro (EUR) is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). There is no foreign exchange control regime, no approval requirement for cross-border capital movements, and no restriction on dividend or profit remittance beyond standard corporate law solvency tests and the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (reducible under tax treaties). Resident banks report cross-border transactions to the BCL for Balance of Payments (BoP) statistical purposes, but reportability is not a permissioning hurdle. The SARL may maintain multi-currency accounts (EUR, USD, CNY, GBP) and freely repatriate operating profits after audit and tax clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed period for repatriation of receivables. Export and freight-forwarding income generated by the Luxembourg SARL may be retained in original invoice currency, converted at freely negotiated bank rates, or credited directly to a foreign-currency sub-account. The only statutory obligations are accurate BoP reporting to the BCL through the resident account-holding bank and full traceability of trade-related payments under the CSSF AML/KYC framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and the competent monetary authority for Luxembourg; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the licensing and AML/CFT supervisory authority for credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions; the European Central Bank (ECB) retains supranational competence for monetary policy and the Single Supervisory Mechanism for significant banks in the Eurozone.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (ancillary treasury use only). Luxembourg recognizes EU-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2); their cross-border passporting into Luxembourg permits the holding of operational float, fee collection and outward disbursement execution. However, a digital EMI cannot lawfully serve as the primary treasury rail for a Luxembourg SARL because it cannot report BoP statistics to the BCL with the same statutory completeness as a licensed credit institution, and most EMIs decline capital subscription inflows, escrow mandates and documentary trade settlements. Non-EU offshore EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport are not viable and would expose the SARL to unlicensed activity risk under CSSF regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injection from the EU parent or third-country shareholder must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (outside SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (within SEPA) to the SARL's designated EUR operational account at the Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. The beneficiary bank will require the payment reference 'Share capital subscription — SARL [name], capital increase resolution [date]', supported by a notarized notarial deed of incorporation or capital increase minutes, a bank statement of the remitter, and a completed BoP purpose declaration (ECB/BCL statistical code, typically D.1.3 for direct investment equity capital). There is no exchange control authorization; the bank opens the account upon completion of its KYC and RBE verification. The funds can be retained in EUR or converted to USD at the SARL's discretion, and no statutory surrender rule applies.Outbound trade disbursements are settled through (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-EU and non-SEPA counterparties, principally payments to ocean carriers, overseas NVOCC partners, port authorities and foreign customs brokers; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer for EU payment recipients, using ISO 20022 XML (pain.001) formats harmonized under the ECB's November 2025 single EURO payment area message standard; and (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Luxembourg relationship bank for large-volume, documentary freight and charter commitments where the carrier requires a bank undertaking. All disbursements must be matched against underlying invoices, bills of lading, and shipping contracts to satisfy both CSSF AML controls and the SARL's own treasury verification matrix.Deliverable EUR/USD FX forwards — fully permissible for hedging the SARL's ocean freight receivables and carrier disbursement mismatch, with execution under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or local confirmations.FX swaps and spot-forward combinations for short-term rolling of USD, EUR, GBP and CNY positions during voyage settlement cycles.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CSS) on EUR/USD or EUR/GBP for converting long-term vessel-contract liabilities into functional currency.Vanilla FX options (puts/calls) on EUR/USD permitted for purchased freight margin protection, subject to CSSF conduct-of-business and IFRS hedge accounting documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to receivables or payables denominated in non-deliverable or restricted currencies (e.g., onshore CNY or INR) where a corresponding ordinary deliverable forward is structurally unavailable; such NDFs must be entered into only with the licensed Luxembourg bank or an EU-authorised investment firm and reported under EMIR with appropriate underlying trade documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (ancillary treasury use only). Luxembourg recognizes EU-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2); their cross-border passporting into Luxembourg permits the holding of operational float, fee collection and outward disbursement execution. However, a digital EMI cannot lawfully serve as the primary treasury rail for a Luxembourg SARL because it cannot report BoP statistics to the BCL with the same statutory completeness as a licensed credit institution, and most EMIs decline capital subscription inflows, escrow mandates and documentary trade settlements. Non-EU offshore EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU passport are not viable and would expose the SARL to unlicensed activity risk under CSSF regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: National Master File/Local File expectation for Luxembourg taxpayers with more than 150 employees and annual turnover exceeding EUR 50,000,000 or balance-sheet assets exceeding EUR 43,000,000; mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000; smaller NVOCC SARLs must still maintain contemporaneous documentation for intercompany freight, management and financing transactions

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separate Economic Substances Regulation comparable to UAE law, but Article 6 of the Luxembourg Tax Adjustment Act (Steueranpassungsgesetz), the Luxembourg GAAR and BEPS treaty-abuse rules require real economic substance; the NVOCC SARL must maintain an office, employees, contracts, client-facing freight operations and management decisions in Luxembourg to access tax treaties, the participation exemption and 0% withholding tax benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment of up to 50% of the evaded tax, late-payment interest at 0.6% per month, administrative fines up to EUR 25,000; criminal conviction for tax fraud may result in fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Managing directors who sign or direct false returns are personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax and may face sequestration, travel bans and imprisonment; corporate veil may be pierced on evidence of fraudulent abuse
Artificial shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax authorities may disregard the SARL's separate existence, recharacterise transactions, deny treaty benefits, disallow deduction of intercompany costs, treat distributions as hidden profit-sharing and reassess CIT plus 15% withholding tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for the reassessed taxes if they knowingly operated a sham entity; travel bans and director disqualification can be imposed in serious cases
Transfer pricing mispricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length taxable profit, additional CIT and municipal business tax, 0.6% monthly late-payment interest and accuracy-related penalties; intentional mispricing is treated as fraudulent tax evasion with fines and imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors who approve tax-motivated intercompany pricing without economic substance or documentation expose themselves to personal liability for resulting tax deficiencies and criminal penalties for intentionally false TP documents
Withholding tax evasion and dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid 15% dividend withholding tax plus interest; anti-abuse recharacterisation of the distribution; penalty of up to 50% of the evaded withholding tax and potential criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for withheld taxes that they intentionally fail to remit; they may be subject to restitution orders, penalties and imprisonment for breach of fiduciary duty
AML / sanctions / payment integrity

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; freezing of assets; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; revocation of NVOCC licences and bank access

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and AML responsible persons may be personally liable for failures in customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting; personal fines, professional disqualification and travel bans may be imposed

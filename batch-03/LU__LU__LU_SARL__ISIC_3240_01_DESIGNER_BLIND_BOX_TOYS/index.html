Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Administration des contributions directes (ACD, Direct Tax Administration) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT, VAT Authority); central e-portal MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for entity registration.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL automatically receives a national registration number (matricule) and an ACD tax number upon incorporation and commercial register filing. For VAT, the SARL, including foreign-owned entities, must apply to the AEDT for a Luxembourg TVA identification number (TVA number) before commencing taxable activities; non-EU resident persons must appoint a tax representative in Luxembourg; EU persons may apply directly if they perform taxable supplies in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL automatically receives a national registration number (matricule) and an ACD tax number upon incorporation and commercial register filing. For VAT, the SARL, including foreign-owned entities, must apply to the AEDT for a Luxembourg TVA identification number (TVA number) before commencing taxable activities; non-EU resident persons must appoint a tax representative in Luxembourg; EU persons may apply directly if they perform taxable supplies in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg law does not provide for check-the-box pass-through elections; a SARL is inherently subject to Entity_Level_Taxation. Taxpayers may elect fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and VAT where 95% or 75% (VAT fiscal unity) thresholds are met; hybrid mismatch re-characterisation elections are limited to choices explicitly provided under the ATAD implementing law for exempt permanent establishments and transparent entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for taxable supplies in Luxembourg; a small enterprise relief exists for annual turnover below EUR 35,000 (excluding VAT), but non-established suppliers must register without threshold if they make taxable supplies; intra-EU distance sales of goods are subject to the EU One-Stop-Shop registration threshold of EUR 10,000 cross-border annual turnover into Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods (including designer collectible toys) physically exported outside the European Union, provided the supplier or the customer arranges transport and exports them within the statutory periods; intra-EU supplies are zero-rated if the customer supplies a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another EU member state; specified services connected to exported goods or provided to non-EU customers (including design, licencing, and distribution support services) may be zero-rated if the effective use and enjoyment is outside Luxembourg.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Accompanying Document, EAD/D.A.U.)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or comparable bank payment confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract
  • Packing list and insurance/transport documents

Luxembourg does not currently impose mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or fiscal API controls for domestic B2B VAT. E-invoicing is permitted and must comply with EU Directive 2014/55/EU and European standard EN 16931 for public procurement. VAT reporting obligations are periodic via the standard Luxembourg VAT return; from 2024, electronic reporting of cross-border e-commerce transactions under the Import One-Stop-Shop and DAC7 information reporting apply.

Export zero-rating claims are strictly audited by the AEDT. Missing or false export evidence invalidates the zero-rating, causing immediate VAT assessment at 17%, plus late-payment interest at 0.5% per month and a 10% upward tax adjustment; fraudulent claims invoke Luxembourg Criminal Code penalties up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment, retrospective assessment extended to 10 years, and loss of entitlement to exercise a taxable profession; clawback also applies if the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is not met without justified currency-control or commercial grounds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg uses the euro (EUR) as legal tender and maintains full, unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and EU Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no exchange controls, no import/export currency licensing requirements, no conversion quotas, and no restrictions on profit distribution, dividend remittance, intercompany loan repayment, or repatriation of dissolution proceeds to non-resident shareholders. The only tax-layer remittance hurdle is the Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15% (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder holds at least 10% for 12 months and is EU-resident; treaty reductions available for third-country residents). A designer-blind-box SARL may lawfully hold EUR, USD, CNY, and other foreign-currency accounts, contract in any currency, and convert freely; banks are required to report cross-border flows to the BCL solely for euro-area balance-of-payments statistics, not for approval purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Luxembourg. A resident exporter may retain 100% of foreign-currency export earnings indefinitely in domestic or offshore accounts, convert at market rates at its discretion, and use such proceeds directly for overseas trade payables (including PRC contract manufacturers). The sole administrative obligation is indirect: the Luxembourg account-holding bank performs BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting (reporting thresholds and periodicity per BCL circulars) and standard AML record-keeping. Exports of vinyl designer collectibles (HS 9503/9506) are not subject to export licensing or dual-use controls, though EU customs export declarations and Intrastat/EXT statistics filings apply above exemption thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) — the national central bank of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and a member of the Eurosystem; monetary policy authority rests with the European Central Bank (ECB), while the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) supervises banks, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions (EMIs) and enforces AML/CTF rules under the Law of 12 November 2004 as amended and CSSF Regulation 12-02.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — legally viable only as a complementary payments and FX-conversion layer, not as the statutory banking backbone. Luxembourg recognises EU-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) through the EBA single-passport regime under PSD2, and the CSSF licenses domestic PIs/EMIs (including Professionals of the Financial Sector). Digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, Currenxie, Ebury) may hold multicurrency operational balances, settle Shopify/Stripe payouts, and offer competitive SME FX for supplier payments to PRC or the US. However, an EMI cannot (i) issue the blocked-capital attestation required by the Luxembourg notary for SARL incorporation or capital increase, which demands a licensed credit institution; (ii) provide trade finance such as Letters of Credit, guarantees, or working-capital facilities; (iii) guarantee onshore RMB clearing through CIPS with full counterparty strictness. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory minimum share capital for a Luxembourg SARL is EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up (cash or in-kind) before the notarial deed is executed. Compliant routing: (i) the subscribing shareholder originates a SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA) or SEPA Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPITAL INCREASE' / remittance reference to the deed of subscription, into the SARL's blocked escrow account at a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank; (ii) the depositary bank issues a blocked-funds attestation (attestation de blocage) naming the Luxembourg notary and confirming the funds are frozen until incorporation; (iii) the notary files the deed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register and the RBE register, after which the bank automatically releases funds to the operational account. No exchange-control permit is required; the bank codes the inward flow for BCL balance-of-payments statistics as an equity capital inflow. Amounts equal to or above EUR 10,000 trigger standard AML verification (source of funds), not FX approval. Additional capital increases follow the same block-and-release mechanics with an extraordinary shareholder resolution and updated notarial deed.Clearing rails for trade settlements from the Luxembourg SARL: (a) Eurozone suppliers, logistics, and service providers — SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) via TARGET2, with execution within one business day; (b) PRC contract manufacturers — SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer in USD or EUR via correspondent bank channels, or RMB settlement through CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) via a clearing-capable Luxembourg bank, with irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600 advised/confirmed by the beneficiary's PRC bank for large production runs; (c) US distributor and e-commerce payouts — SWIFT MT103 in USD through a US correspondent, or ACH via the EMI partner for small-value remittances; (d) intra-EU liquidity — SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for same-second value for working-capital concentration. All outbound MT103 messages must carry precise purpose codes (e.g., 'GOODS PURCHASE', 'INTRA-GROUP SERVICES') and invoice references; the Luxembourg bank reports each flow to the BCL for statistical purposes and applies CSSF-mandated transaction-monitoring filters on trade corridors.Deliverable EUR/USD outright forward and FX swap contracts quoted by the Luxembourg Tier-1 bank or an EU credit institution, hedging payable and receivable conversion cycles typically up to 12 months.Deliverable EUR/CNY forward contracts executed via a bank with onshore CNY clearing capability (through a PRC correspondent or CIPS member) for hedging monthly conversion of RMB supplier payments.EUR/USD and EUR/CNY European-style FX options (purchased only, non-leveraged) permitting the SARL to cap conversion cost while preserving upside on e-commerce revenue streams.Offshore EUR/CNH Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) referencing the offshore CNY (CNH) fixing where onshore deliverability is impractical or the counterparty cannot execute CNY delivery; permitted for hedging genuine commercial exposure.Cross-currency basis swaps denominated in EUR/USD or EUR/CNY for matched medium-term trade-payable versus receivable flows, provided the notional does not exceed the projected commercial exposure and the transaction is documented under an ISDA or bank master agreement.Named ineligible derivatives: speculative positions, uncovered currency positions exceeding 12-month projected trade flows, and any instrument involving crypto assets or digital tokens, which would contravene CSSF suitability and prudent-credit standards.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — legally viable only as a complementary payments and FX-conversion layer, not as the statutory banking backbone. Luxembourg recognises EU-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) through the EBA single-passport regime under PSD2, and the CSSF licenses domestic PIs/EMIs (including Professionals of the Financial Sector). Digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, Currenxie, Ebury) may hold multicurrency operational balances, settle Shopify/Stripe payouts, and offer competitive SME FX for supplier payments to PRC or the US. However, an EMI cannot (i) issue the blocked-capital attestation required by the Luxembourg notary for SARL incorporation or capital increase, which demands a licensed credit institution; (ii) provide trade finance such as Letters of Credit, guarantees, or working-capital facilities; (iii) guarantee onshore RMB clearing through CIPS with full counterparty strictness. Recommended structure: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank for statutory capital, treasury, and trade finance; a single reputable EU-passported EMI for e-commerce settlement, low-value global payouts, and favourable wholesale FX spreads.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 intercompany transactions (Local File); EUR 50 million consolidated group turnover (Master File); EUR 750 million group revenue (CbCR)

Economic substance scope: No standalone ESR regime in Luxembourg; substance requirements embedded in the Law of 21 June 2019, GAAR, and ATAD, requiring staff, premises, and strategic decision-making in Luxembourg to benefit from tax treaty and EU directive protections; empty shell structures trigger re-qualification and denial of benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000, imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years, disgorgement of evaded tax, plus monthly late interest and late payment surcharges

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal criminal liability, asset attachment, travel bans, and civil restitution for unpaid corporate taxes under Luxembourg case law.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation as an abuse of law, denial of exemption, retroactive withholding tax recapture, and penalties up to 10% of the recomputed tax base plus interest

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil allows tax authorities to hold directors jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts; management disqualification or travel restrictions may also apply.
AML / KYC Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines by the CSSF up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years for serious non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined, prohibited from exercising managerial functions, and held civilly liable for losses resulting from AML compliance failures.

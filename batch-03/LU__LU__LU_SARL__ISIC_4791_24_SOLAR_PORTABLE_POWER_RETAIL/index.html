Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum share capital, fully subscribed and paid in cash. Subscription monies must be placed on a blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution until the RCS registration certificate is issued, after which the account is released to the company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des Contributions Directes, ACD) for direct income/corporate taxes — https://impotsdirects.public.lu; VAT and registration taxes: Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — https://pfi.public.lu (VAT e-filing, OSS/IOSS portal); central business formalities portal: https://guichet.lu; Companies Register: Luxembourg Business Registers, https://www.lbr.lu.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary, deposit minimum capital (EUR 12,000 for a standard SARL), and file the articles with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) to obtain the RCS registration number. Step 2: ACD automatically allocates the 11-digit direct-tax matricule/TIN upon registration; confirm the matricule via the guichet.lu eSpace. Step 3: Apply to the AEDT for a VAT identification number in the format 'LU' + 8 digits (the same number can be used for OSS/IOSS if the entity elects distance-sales rules); VAT registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply in Luxembourg and can be completed electronically within days. Foreign shareholders are permitted without local residency; the SARL must appoint a resident manager (gérant) satisfying substance requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is a fully opaque Luxembourg tax-resident corporation taxed on worldwide profits; no domestic pass-through treatment applies. Qualifying dividends and capital gains may benefit from the Luxembourg participation exemption; no pure territorial regime and no special tax-exempt zone applies to the entity.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (1) fiscal integration (tax consolidation) for a 95%-owned group of Luxembourg subsidiaries; (2) application for a VAT group (unité TVA) with AEDT approval; (3) OSS/IOSS registration so that intra-EU distance sales and certain imports are declared centrally instead of through destination-country VAT registrations; (4) binding tax rulings and advance pricing agreements with ACD; (5) election to apply the Luxembourg participation exemption on substantiated shareholdings; (6) mandatory ATAD/hybrid mismatch analysis where a foreign parent's check-the-box or similar classification could recharacterize the SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Luxembourg-established SARL: mandatory VAT registration from the first euro of taxable turnover (no general registration threshold). A small-business exemption ('franchise en base') is available up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover, but it does not apply to cross-border distance sales or OSS-managed supplies. For intra-EU distance sales of goods: EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide threshold applies; above that threshold, VAT is due in the destination EU Member State. Non-established foreign sellers must register as soon as they make their first taxable supply in Luxembourg unless OSS/IOSS or a marketplace deemed-supplier rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with credit) applies to: (a) exports of portable battery generators and solar panels to non-EU destinations under Art. 41 Loi TVA, provided the goods are physically dispatched outside the EU customs territory within 3 months of the supply and conclusive customs/transport evidence is retained; (b) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered businesses under Art. 43 Loi TVA, requiring the buyer's valid EU VAT ID, physical movement to another Member State, and EC Sales List reporting; (c) certain export-related services such as freight and forwarding directly linked to the exported goods. Supplies to private EU consumers are not zero-rated: cross-border distance sales exceeding the EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide threshold are taxable in the consumer's destination Member State and reportable through OSS. Because Stripe and Affirm BNPL act as payment intermediaries and not online marketplaces, the deemed-supplier regime does not displace the SARL's VAT liability on such sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic DAU filed via Luxembourg Customs PLDA or the export declaration of the Member State of export)
  • Commercial invoice, order confirmation and packing list evidencing the buyer, goods and sales terms
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / air waybill or equivalent carrier-issued proof of dispatch from EU to non-EU destination
  • Bank transfer, Stripe settlement record or other payment-rail evidence of inward remittance
  • For intra-Community zero-rating: purchaser's valid VAT identification number and the periodic EC Sales List / VIES record

Luxembourg currently has no real-time fiscal clearance system, no mandatory e-invoicing, and no digital stamping or API clearance mandate. E-invoicing is permitted and Peppol BIS, UBL, Factur-X and EN 16931 formats are accepted; paper invoices remain lawful for B2B and B2C transactions. A mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime is expected under the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package, with Luxembourg transposition planned on the 2030/2035 horizon; until then, taxpayers must retain full invoice archives for 10 years and file periodic VAT returns, recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) and OSS returns through the AEDT eSpace portal.

Export zero-rating claims are jointly audited by AEDT (VAT) and customs authorities. Failure to evidence physical export within the 3-month dispatch period, or failure to retain supporting documents for the statutory 10-year retention period, triggers retrospective VAT reassessment at 17% plus monthly arrears interest of 0.8% and administrative fines from EUR 750 to EUR 12,500 per infringement under Art. 65 Loi TVA. Intentional or fraudulent zero-rating, including sham exports, participation in missing-trader fraud chains and false EC Sales Lists, is criminally sanctionable with fines up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment of responsible managers for up to 5 years; unduly paid refunds are clawed back with additional late-payment penalties up to 10% of the assessed VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full currency convertibility with no exchange-control approval requirements for current-account or capital-account operations. The euro is fully convertible; capital injections, shareholder loans, trade payments, intercompany disbursements and profit repatriation require no central bank permit and are unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The only operational friction for profit remittance is the domestic 15% dividend withholding tax, reducible to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, plus standard CRS/FATCA documentation duties. All balances may be held or converted in any currency through CSSF-licensed credit institutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no compulsory repatriation timeline exist in Luxembourg. Export receipts derived by the SARL from distance sales of portable solar power equipment may be retained indefinitely in EUR or foreign currency in any CSSF-licensed bank account. The only compliance duties are indirect BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting performed by the bank for individual transactions at or above EUR 12,500, together with standard EU VAT distance-selling and One-Stop-Shop (OSS) declarations.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; day-to-day supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); monetary and foreign-exchange competence is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) and the European Union institutions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a payment-acquiring rail, LOW as a primary treasury account. EU/EEA-authorized digital EMIs operating in Luxembourg under PSD2/EMD2 passporting (Stripe, PayPal and Wise Europe based in Ireland/Belgium) may lawfully process online card flows and Affirm BNPL settlements, but balances held with these non-bank institutions are not safeguarded under the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL). Offshore non-EEA EMIs have no legal standing to provide core banking or FX clearing services to a Luxembourg SARL and cannot be used for the capital escrow or for shareholder distributions. Consequently, the SARL should maintain Tier-1 bank accounts at BIL/BCEE as the sole treasury ledger and automate same-day sweeping from the EMI collection balances.

Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Ministry of Justice, Grand Duchy of Luxembourg.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Law of 19 December 2020 on the digitalisation of the Luxembourg Business Registers and the Registry of Commerce and Companies; Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 September 2020 on electronic filing and publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Acte constitutif) containing the Articles of Association (Statuts), or private deed executed before an authorised civil-law notary, filed electronically with the RCS via the www.lbr.lu portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited in an escrow/blocked account opened in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution, with a blocking certificate issued to the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) valuation report filed with the deed.

Funding & unlock path: General municipal fire safety certificate issued by the mayor (bourgmestre) for the registered office premises based on building code compliance; occupational health and safety risk assessment and workplace clearance under Articles L.312-1 et seq. of the Labour Code (Code du travail); no industrial structural load inspections or heavy machinery clearances apply.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions must be deposited in an escrow/blocked account opened in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution, with a blocking certificate issued to the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's (réviseur d'entreprises agréé) valuation report filed with the deed.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the newly incorporated Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), governed by the Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, hereby: (1) confirms the subscription and full cash payment of the statutory share capital of EUR 12,000, deposited in a blocked Luxembourg bank account; (2) adopts the registered office at [address], Luxembourg; (3) appoints [Manager Name] as Gérant, with full powers to represent the Company, sign patent and trademark licensing agreements, and open bank accounts; (4) authorizes the Gérant to file the articles with the Register of Commerce and Companies, complete RESA publication, and effect all ancillary tax and social registrations; and (5) ratifies all pre-incorporation acts. This resolution is adopted by unanimous consent of the shareholder for immediate execution.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - the entity operates exclusively as a non-industrial intellectual property holding company (ISIC 6420 / NAICS 533110); no manufacturing unit, workshop, or classified installation is established in Luxembourg, and no authorisation from the Ministry of the Economy (Ministère de l'Économie) is required for the ownership or cross-border sub-licensing of patents and trademarks.

Category: None - no operating or manufacturing permit is required under Luxembourg law for pure IP holding and licensing activities. The company may, however, obtain a discretionary written confirmation from the Ministry of the Economy for double-tax-treaty or cross-border royalty clearance purposes, although this is not a statutory licence.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDTV) for VAT and indirect taxes. Official e-portal: guichet.lu and MyGuichet.lu (papiertax / eCDF filing channels).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first complete commercial registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the RCS to obtain a Luxembourg registration number (matricule). Thereafter, apply to the AEDTV using the standard VAT registration form (Form 700) for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). Non-resident entities carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg must register before commencing activity; proof of identification, articles of association, power of attorney, and evidence of economic nexus or permanent establishment must be submitted. For direct tax purposes, a foreign corporate entity deriving Luxembourg-source income may require a Luxembourg tax number via the ACD and must file corporate tax returns accordingly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first complete commercial registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the RCS to obtain a Luxembourg registration number (matricule). Thereafter, apply to the AEDTV using the standard VAT registration form (Form 700) for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits). Non-resident entities carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg must register before commencing activity; proof of identification, articles of association, power of attorney, and evidence of economic nexus or permanent establishment must be submitted. For direct tax purposes, a foreign corporate entity deriving Luxembourg-source income may require a Luxembourg tax number via the ACD and must file corporate tax returns accordingly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A Luxembourg SARL is a resident corporation taxable entity-level on worldwide income. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT grouping election (article 60 VATL) for closely linked VAT-registered persons; (ii) the election to become a single taxpayer under the newly introduced fiscal unity regime (article 164bis LIR) for qualifying 95% Luxembourg- and EEA-held groups, applicable for CIT, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; (iii) the election to apply the Luxembourg IP box regime (article 50ter LIR) through a qualifying patent or software license; (iv) option for non-resident VAT status under article 57 VATL for small enterprises below the EUR 25,000 threshold; and (v) treaty and ATAD hybrid election notifications under Luxembourg domestic implementation, including dividend and interest hybrid mismatch maker rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual taxable turnover for the small business exemption (VATL art. 57). Above EUR 25,000, VAT registration is compulsory for Luxembourg-established suppliers. Foreign entities engaged in taxable supplies performed in Luxembourg are required to register with the AEDTV if they do not benefit from the reverse charge or simplified distance sale rules; there is no de minimis threshold for activities that are physically performed or located in Luxembourg. Intra-EU cross-border acquisitions and reverse-charge services likewise have no low-value threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: a 0% Luxembourg VAT rate applies to export supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported to a non-EU destination, provided the supplier retains shipment evidence within the statutory time limits. For international transport and ancillary services, zero-rating similarly applies. For Luxembourg IP licensing (patents, trademarks, software), the B2B place-of-supply rule applies: where the recipient is a VAT-registered business in another EU Member State, the supply is sited in that recipient's territory and subject to reverse charge; where the recipient is a non-EU business, the supply is outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT and no Luxembourg VAT is chargeable, which operates as an effective zero-rated outcome without a formal export certificate. Zero-rating of goods additionally requires the goods to be commercially exported and the foreign purchaser to be the exporter or the Luxembourg exporter to evidence movement out of the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ENS) with customs office proof of exit and movement reference number (MRN)
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or rail consignment note showing departure from Luxembourg and arrival outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and purchase/export contract
  • Proof of payment and, where applicable, completed Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer receipts evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

Luxembourg does not currently impose a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, clearance stamping, or B2B e-invoicing clearance mandate. B2G invoicing is mandatory in an electronic format through the Peppol network for public tenders above the EU thresholds. For B2B, Peppol BIS 3.0 interoperability was introduced as a voluntary 'cross-border sealed' format in 2023, with a projected mandatory intra-EU B2B e-invoicing obligation expected under EU ViDA by 2030. No API-based national fiscal stamping platform is in production; taxpayers are expected to preserve standard XML/PDF and audit-file records, and to comply with Luxembourg ACD audit requirements including the production of VAT listings, extract files, and electronic audit trails upon request.

Luxembourg tax authorities may apply retrospective VAT adjustments, reassessment periods of up to 10 years for deliberate fraud (standard 5 years where returns are duly filed), and a 100% VAT clawback plus interest at the statutory monthly rate of 0.65% (or 0.6% per month) for documentary failures or fraudulent export zero-rating claims. Criminal penalties for VAT and customs fraud, falsification of documents, and use of fake export certificates include fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, with senior officers jointly and severally liable; VAT credit refunds may also be suspended pending substantive audit verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current and capital accounts. The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender. Royalty inflows, intercompany licence fees, equity injections, loan disbursements, profit distributions, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable without exchange-control authorization, currency quotas, or advance approval under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Bank accounts may be denominated in EUR, USD, GBP, or any freely convertible currency. Practical hurdles are limited to banking AML/KYC screening, CSSF oversight, BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the paying bank, and the domestic statutory withholding tax regime (15% royalty WHT under LIR Section 153, reduced or exempt under the EU Interest and Royalties Directive and applicable double-tax treaties). There are no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend upstreaming, or capital repatriation for a Luxembourg resident SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion rules apply to export proceeds or royalty receipts in Luxembourg. There is no mandatory timeline for converting foreign-currency receipts into euro and no minimum conversion ratio. Non-financial corporates have no individual balance-of-payments filing obligation; however, Luxembourg credit institutions must report aggregated payment transactions to the BCL for statistical purposes under Regulation (EC) No 184/2005, with individual payments at or above EUR 12,500 typically requiring the bank to record the ECB/BCL statistical purpose code. Retaining proceeds offshore or invoicing in USD is permitted without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the national central bank of Luxembourg and a member of the Eurosystem; monetary policy and foreign-reserve functions are delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB). Luxembourg has no separate foreign-exchange administration; financial-sector conduct supervision is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for ancillary receivable collections, but Low viability for principal treasury and clearing functions. Luxembourg-authorised EMIs (CSSF-licensed under the law of 10 April 2020 on payment services) and EEA-passported EMIs may offer multi-currency IBANs with segregated safeguarding accounts, but they cannot provide operating credit, deposit protection, FX derivatives, ISDA master agreements, or large-value TARGET2 settlement. Post-Brexit, UK FCA-authorised digital EMIs no longer benefit from EU passporting into Luxembourg; an offshore EMI outside the EEA must establish a CSSF-licensed branch or subsidiary, which is commercially disproportionate for an IP licensing treasury. A SARL undertaking global IP patent licensing should use a Tier-1 Luxembourg bank for its principal accounts; an EEA-licensed EMI may be limited to small third-party sub-licensee collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial SARL share capital (statutory minimum EUR 12,000) and subsequent equity or shareholder-loan injections uses SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers settled through TARGET2 for EUR, or correspondent-bank clearing (CHIPS/Fedwire) for USD. Before notarial deed signature, the initial capital must be deposited into a blocked account at a Luxembourg credit institution or, failing that, at the Caisse de Consignation. All deposits require the SWIFT MT103 message to include the bank-specific BCL/ECB statistical purpose code for equity capital or subordinated shareholder advances, a board resolution authorizing the transaction, the notarised deed, and the RCS registration extract. Central-bank approval is not required, and no FX conversion declaration is needed. Verification for anti-money-laundering purposes includes the source-of-funds statement of the contributing shareholder and the account-beneficiary match with the mandate card.The primary clearing rails for outbound licensing disbursements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for USD and non-EUR royalty payments to non-resident licensors, distributors, and third-party service providers; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for intra-EEA EUR payments below the large-value threshold; (iii) TARGET2 Large-Value Payments via the bank for FX trade settlements, intercompany loan repayments, and royalty settlements above EUR 100,000; and (iv) MT103 dividend distributions and capital-reduction payments carrying the bank-specific BCL purpose code for dividends. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) and documentary collections are generally inapplicable to patent-licensing and royalty-style service flows and should be expressly rejected as non-compliant clearing instruments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Combined marginal rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City: 17% corporate income tax plus 7% solidarity surcharge (1.19%) plus 6.75% municipal business tax. CIT alone: 17% for taxable income exceeding EUR 200,001.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends distributed by a SARL to non-resident shareholders; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (direct holding of at least 10%, minimum 12-month holding period) or under a double tax treaty if a 0% rate applies; treaty rates commonly 5% for portfolio holdings and 0% for qualifying substantial holdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File applies when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Local File is required for the Luxembourg entity when related-party transactions exceed de minimis thresholds established by ACD Circular LIR 171/2: EUR 100,000 for services, royalties, interest and other financial transactions and EUR 1,000,000 for goods transactions. The SARL must maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation even if revenue is below these thresholds when the ACD requests it.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no economic substance regulations modeled after offshore jurisdictions, but applies the ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule (EU Directive 2016/1164), OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty abuse tests, and ACD administrative practice. A holding or licensing SARL must demonstrate adequate substance in Luxembourg (own office, qualified staff, local board decision-making, management control over the IP risk and economic activity). The tax administration may deny treaty benefits, DTA relief, or parent-subsidiary exemption if the SARL is considered a conduit or shell entity without commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of under-declared tax plus a 100% penalty, 0.8% monthly late interest and the 10% 'droit en sus'. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of one month to five years and a fine of up to EUR 250,000, or both.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or participate in the fraud are personally criminally liable, face imprisonment and fines, are barred from managing companies, and are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, interest and penalties.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ACD adjustment under Article 56 LIR; additional tax assessment; penalty up to 12% of the reassessed tax where the mispricing results from gross negligence; 0.8% monthly interest; documentation fines of EUR 250 to EUR 5,000 per missing deliverable.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transfer pricing declaration knowing that the prices were abusive are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest and penalties; repeated violations trigger personal fines and disqualification.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the Luxembourg entity for tax purposes; denial of DTA and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits (0% dividend relief withdrawn, 15% WHT applied retroactively); recharacterization of royalty income to the actual beneficial owner; dissolution and compulsory deregistration from the Luxembourg RCS.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution of unpaid WHT and taxes, personal joint liability under Article 266 of the Law of 10 August 1915, and travel bans or prohibition orders in criminal investigations for false declarations.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Transparency Breach

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004, as amended: administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal entities in serious cases; per-violation RBE fines of EUR 500 to EUR 25,000; criminal imprisonment of up to five years for responsible individuals; freezing of accounts and prohibition of transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal criminal liability, imprisonment, and personal fines; they may also face civil recovery of illicit gains, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, and disqualification from holding office in financial-service companies.

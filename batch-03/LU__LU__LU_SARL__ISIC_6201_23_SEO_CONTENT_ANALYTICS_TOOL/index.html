Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); notaries act as mandatory e-filers for incorporation acts.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended; SARL provisions under Articles 104 et seq.; registration provisions under Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) rules and the Law of 14 August 2000 on trade and company registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) and articles of association (statuts), lodged with the RCS by the notary; mandatory publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés (RES).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 statutory minimum for a Luxembourg SARL; the entire capital must be fully subscribed and paid up in cash before notarization; funds are held in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution and are released only upon RCS registration. A special auditor's report is required only for non-cash contributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: registered office must secure a municipal fire-safety certificate for commercial occupancy, including emergency exits, extinguishers, and electrical installation conformity. Occupational: ITM workstation compliance under the Luxembourg Labour Code, including ergonomic equipment, electrical safety, and office ventilation; structural load inspections are not required for standard software-office use. No special civil-defense authorization is needed for a non-industrial office workplace.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 statutory minimum for a Luxembourg SARL; the entire capital must be fully subscribed and paid up in cash before notarization; funds are held in a blocked escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution and are released only upon RCS registration. A special auditor's report is required only for non-cash contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direct taxes: Administration des contributions directes (ACD / Luxembourg Inland Revenue) — impotsdirects.public.lu. Indirect taxes: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — pfi.public.lu. Unified e-filing and business portal: myguichet.lu / guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL is incorporated through the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which immediately grants the statutory 13-digit matricule (national tax ID) used for direct-tax, social security and statistical filings. Upon completion of incorporation registration, the AEDT automatically assigns the VAT identification number in the format LU 0000 0000 where the company carries on taxable activities; where automatic assignment does not occur, the SARL must file VAT registration Form 100 (resident) via myguichet.lu. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment does not obtain a LU VAT ID for pure B2C e-service traffic: it uses the non-Union OSS (third-country supplies) or Union OSS (intra-EU supplies). A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg permanent establishment registers via Form 100A and must appoint a VAT representative in Luxembourg only if it has no EU establishment and would otherwise become liable for import VAT or direct registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is a resident company (LIR section 159: registered office and central administration in Luxembourg) taxed on worldwide income, with no pass-through eligibility. The complete charge comprises corporate income tax (17% headline), unemployment fund surcharge (7% on CIT) and municipal business tax; combined statutory headline approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City (rates vary by municipality), plus net wealth tax. — A Luxembourg SARL is incorporated through the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), which immediately grants the statutory 13-digit matricule (national tax ID) used for direct-tax, social security and statistical filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is a resident company (LIR section 159: registered office and central administration in Luxembourg) taxed on worldwide income, with no pass-through eligibility. The complete charge comprises corporate income tax (17% headline), unemployment fund surcharge (7% on CIT) and municipal business tax; combined statutory headline approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City (rates vary by municipality), plus net wealth tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-transparent election is available; the SARL is per se opaque. Applicable statutory elections: (i) fiscal unity / tax consolidation under LIR section 164ter (95% direct or indirect shareholding held for at least one uninterrupted fiscal year, covering CIT and municipal business tax); (ii) VAT group assessment (solidarité) under Article 52 of the VAT law, allowing the SARL and its Luxembourg sub-entities to file under one VAT number; (iii) Union OSS / IOSS election for intra-EU B2C e-services and distance sales, displacing Luxembourg place-of-supply rules; (iv) participation exemption (LIR section 166) applying automatically to qualifying substantial holdings, with a formal declaration option for the capital-gains limb; (v) binding tax rulings for advanced confirmation of hybrid financing structures; and (vi) ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply mandatorily, with grandfathering elections only for pre-2016 structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established businesses (raised from EUR 30,000 effective 1 January 2023); EUR 10,000 calendar-year threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C electronically-supplied services below which Luxembourg VAT applies locally and above which destination-state VAT applies via Union OSS; no registration threshold for foreign suppliers established outside Luxembourg (registration from the first euro of taxable supplies unless the non-Union OSS is used).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to export supplies to non-EU destinations and intra-Community supplies within the EU when the goods leave Luxembourg under transport evidence; export must be evidenced within 90 days from the time of supply (Implementing Regulation 282/2011). Digital SaaS services (SEO crawler and keyword analytics tool): supplies to non-EU business and private customers are outside Luxembourg VAT scope because the place of supply is the customer's establishment or residence (no Luxembourg output VAT and no refund claim needed); supplies to EU business customers are reverse-charged at the customer's member state upon presentation of a valid EU VAT ID; supplies to EU consumers are VAT-liable in the consumer's state and reported through Union OSS once the EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold is met. The exporter must satisfy the invoice-content requirements of Articles 57-61 of the Luxembourg VAT law and hold commercial evidence proving the foreign destination and the customer's non-EU status.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) endorsed by the customs office of exit from the EU
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR/road consignment note evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial invoice containing the Luxembourg VAT ID and express mention of the zero-rate or outside-scope treatment
  • Evidence of the customer's non-EU establishment (valid foreign VAT/tax ID or, for B2C, payment-leg data and geolocation/IP evidence)
  • Executed SaaS licence or service contract and customer acceptance certificate
  • FX inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement proving receipt of the foreign currency proceeds from the foreign buyer

No real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or SDI-style approval system applies to B2B/B2C invoicing. Mandatory PEPPOL-based e-invoicing applies only to B2G supplies (EN 16931/UBL) since 1 January 2021. B2B e-invoices are optional but must guarantee authenticity, integrity and legibility (EDI or qualified electronic signature). VAT returns are filed monthly or quarterly via myguichet.lu (deadline: 15th of the month following the period), EC sales lists are filed monthly/quarterly per value thresholds, and OSS returns are filed quarterly via the EU OSS portal. Records must be retained for 10 years; no digital stamp or clearance code is issued by the AEDT.

The AEDT may, on audit, require the complete zero-rating/outside-scope evidence file. Missing, forged or contradictory export or FX documentation retroactively destroys the zero-rating, resulting in reassessment of 17% Luxembourg VAT, default interest, and administrative fines under Article 66 of the VAT law (per-offence fines) and criminal prosecution under Article 69 (imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and/or substantial criminal fines) where fraudulent intent is demonstrated. The assessment/recovery window is generally three years from the end of the year in which the tax became chargeable and is extended to ten years in cases of fraud or intentional concealment. Company directors are jointly liable for the reinstated VAT, interest and penalties, and repeat offenders may face a public blacklisting effect within the AEDT enforcement file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a founding member of the Eurozone and fully applies the free movement of capital under TFEU Article 63. No distinction exists between current-account and capital-account convertibility; both are unrestricted. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred without exchange-control approval. Subject only to standard tax formalities and AML verification, no prior or post-transaction FX authorizations are required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements in Luxembourg. Enterprises may retain export revenues in the currency of receipt and may continue to hold any freely convertible currency without prior authorization. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL applies above material thresholds, with strict data confidentiality.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in cooperation with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. Luxembourg recognizes Electronic Money Institution (EMI) and Payment Institution (PI) status, governed by the CSSF under PSD2. An offshore digital EMI can offer virtual IBANs and facilitate cross-border collection from Stripe/Paddle, but it cannot provide core deposit-account services and must not hold client funds as bank deposits. For a low-risk SaaS analytics tool, an online EMI is a viable ancillary collection rail, yet the capital escrow account and primary operating liquidity must remain with a Tier-1 Luxembourg commercial bank to satisfy statutory public-deposit and substance expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder wiring must be directed to the designated share capital escrow account at a Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. The SWIFT transfer must clearly reference the shareholder corporate name and use the purpose code 'Capital contribution / Share issuance' (purpose code GR005 under EU statistical standards). Third-party payments are strictly prohibited. When the shareholder is resident outside the EEA, the bank requires a resolution approving the capital increase, notarized subscription deeds, and evidence of the funding source. For USD-denominated injections, the bank executes a spot conversion; for repeated USD funding, a EUR/USD forward can be pre-arranged.For a low-risk MarTech SaaS entity with no physical goods, the primary outbound rails are SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments, SWIFT MT103 for USD and all non-EUR currencies, and SWIFT GPI for instant and traceable priority settlements. Recurring license or software procurement payments are executed via standard MT103 with 'SHA' charges and the advantageous correspondent-bank route (e.g., EUR via TARGET2/ECMS, USD via BNY Mellon or BNP's New York branch). A documentary Commercial Letter of Credit may be used for large bespoke hardware or infrastructure procurement, though it is not needed for recurring subscription-based disbursements. For intra-EU suppliers, the SEPA Direct Debit B2B scheme is permitted for invoiced amounts, while cross-border vendor payments should be settled only via MT103 to avoid unnecessary deductions.EUR/USD forward outrights (offered by all Luxembourg Tier-1 banks under ISDA or local master agreements; must be booked through the corporate treasury account).EUR/USD FX swaps (used for short-term liquidity and margin management without EPU exposure).EUR/USD currency options (covered over-the-counter with CSSF reporting; requires an investment approval board resolution).ISDA-form long-term currency swaps (suitable for EUR/USD interest-rate and principal conversion on shareholder loans or vendor financing).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for EUR/USD because the euro is fully convertible; NDF instruments are permissible only for exotic non-convertible remuneration currencies and then require BCL statistical reporting.

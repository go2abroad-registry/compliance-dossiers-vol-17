Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés — RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the supervisory authority of the District Court of Luxembourg (Tribunal d'arrondissement de Luxembourg); post-registration publication in the Electronic Collection of Companies and Associations (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations — RESA).

Governing statute

Luxembourg amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as modernized by the Law of 23 September 2016; SARL-specific provisions at Articles 710-1 to 710-25 (incorporation deed, share capital, shares, management by gérant, and statutory audit regime); ancillary filing rules under the amended Law of 19 December 2002 on the trade and companies register (RCS).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (incorporation deed) executed either as a notarized authentic deed where a contribution in kind, real-estate contribution, or statutory-auditor report is involved, or as a private deed (acte sous seing privé) for a purely cash-subscribed SARL; the deed embeds the annexed articles of association (statuts) and is filed at the RCS with a subsequent mandatory excerpt in the RESA.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 53 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum statutory share capital is EUR 1.00 (one euro) under Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended; the subscribed capital must be fully paid up in cash before incorporation and deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bancaire bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL at a Luxembourg or EU/EEA credit institution; the bank's blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) must accompany the RCS filing and the funds are released only upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and clearance certificate issued by the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours — CGDIS) covering extinguishers, emergency exits, and dry-storage fire risks; structural load certification by an approved engineering office for the warehouse slab, racking, and shelving; workplace occupational safety clearance by the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (Inspection du travail et des mines — ITM) under the amended Law of 17 June 1994 on safety and health at work, including the appointment of a safety coordinator where construction-phase obligations arise.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory share capital is EUR 1.00 (one euro) under Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915, as amended; the subscribed capital must be fully paid up in cash before incorporation and deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bancaire bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL at a Luxembourg or EU/EEA credit institution; the bank's blocked-funds certificate (attestation de blocage) must accompany the RCS filing and the funds are released only upon registration.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) through the MyGuichet.lu single official digital portal.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT and direct tax purposes if it has no permanent establishment. For a local SARL, the entity is incorporated before a notary, registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and automatically assigned a national business number (numéro national) and VAT number by the AEDT. The foreign entity must submit the standard registration form (Formulaire 106 VAT) via MyGuichet.lu or the Luxembourg registration desk, provide the notarized incorporation deed, articles of association, identity documents of directors/shareholders, proof of address, and RCS extract. The AEDT issues the VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) upon verification, typically within two to four weeks; no minimum capital or residency conditions apply for the foreign-owned SARL beyond regulatory corporate requirements.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident entity must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT and direct tax purposes if it has no permanent establishment. For a local SARL, the entity is incorporated before a notary, registered with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and automatically assigned a national business number (numéro national) and VAT number by the AEDT. The foreign entity must submit the standard registration form (Formulaire 106 VAT) via MyGuichet.lu or the Luxembourg registration desk, provide the notarized incorporation deed, articles of association, identity documents of directors/shareholders, proof of address, and RCS extract. The AEDT issues the VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) upon verification, typically within two to four weeks; no minimum capital or residency conditions apply for the foreign-owned SARL beyond regulatory corporate requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg applies an entity-level corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) with no general check-the-box regime. However, certain elections are available: (a) a fully taxable Luxembourg SARL may elect to join a Luxembourg fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) with 95% or more owned resident subsidiaries for corporate income tax and municipal business tax purposes, effectively offsetting profits and losses; (b) qualifying entities may elect joint venture status under Article 175bis LIR; (c) a Luxembourg SARL may opt for the special VAT franchise (régime de la franchise) only below the small-enterprise threshold, and may elect to remain outside the VAT reverse-charge rules by registering; (d) hybrid mismatch elections are limited to aligning classification under Luxembourg domestic law and the relevant double tax treaty where the entity is fiscally transparent in another jurisdiction, but a Luxembourg SARL as a corporate entity is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Luxembourg imposes no general VAT registration threshold for businesses established in Luxembourg; any person carrying out taxable economic activity must register for VAT regardless of turnover. A non-established foreign supplier may be required to register from the first euro of taxable supplies in Luxembourg subject to the distance-selling thresholds under EU VAT rules (EUR 10,000 cross-border intra-EU distance sales threshold for non-established EU suppliers; non-EU suppliers must register without threshold for B2C supplies of electronically supplied services under the special schemes or the general reverse-charge rules may apply for B2B supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Luxembourg to a non-EU destination qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 44 of the Luxembourg VAT Law (loi TVA) provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier within the statutory time frame (90 days from supply). The supplier must obtain and retain proof of export (justificatifs de transport/exportation). Offshore services (e.g., services related to goods exported outside the EU) may also qualify for zero-rating under the place-of-supply rules where the customer is established outside the EU or the services are directly related to exported goods. For the bulk wholesale export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals under ISIC 4631, the zero-rating criterion is satisfied only if the SARL retains full documentary evidence that title passed to the non-EU buyer, the goods left the EU, and the export declaration matches the commercial invoice in quantity, value, and product description.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via the Luxembourg Single Administrative Document or the electronic Customs Declaration System of the EU – ICS/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading (Airway Bill or CMR consignment note depending on the mode of transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT proof of payment or bank statement confirming receipt of funds from the foreign buyer)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1 or non-preferential certificate where required)
  • Phytosanitary Certificate and Organic Certification (EU Organic Regulation 2018/848) for herbal botanicals
  • Proof of Logistics Contract with named transporter and route
  • Proof of Insurance/Carriage Contract
  • Intrastat declaration (when applicable)

Luxembourg does not currently mandate a national e-invoicing clearance model for domestic B2B transactions. E-invoicing is permitted under EU Directive 2014/55/EU and the Luxembourg e-invoicing framework (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0), and invoices must be issued within the standard B2B 30-day deadline. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory government API stamping, and no digital-fiscal-stamp requirement for standard agricultural or wholesale transactions. However, if the SARL uses certified billing software, the software must ensure data integrity and auditability; for cross-border supplies, the recapitulative statement (Intrastat and EC Sales List) must be filed electronically via the AEDT e-file system.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may retrospectively deny zero-rating and reclassify the supply as taxable at the standard 17% VAT if the documentary evidence is incomplete or inconsistent. The taxpayer bears the burden of proof. Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating claims include: (i) payment of the under-assessed VAT plus default interest at the statutory rate; (ii) an administrative fine of up to EUR 250,000 or 10% of the tax due where negligence exists; (iii) criminal sanctions for tax fraud under Article 16 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code and the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), including imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 125,000; (iv) exclusion from VAT number validity and mandatory security deposit requirements for continued registration; and (v) liability of the managing director for the company's VAT debt. A clawback is not limited to the transaction in question — the authorities may extend audit scrutiny to all intra-EU and export sales, apply penalty interest, and dissolve the SARL for serious management failures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Luxembourg applies the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) and TFEU Art. 63; no exchange controls or approvals for current or capital transactions. Profit remittances are unrestricted; dividend payments may be subject to Luxembourg withholding tax (15% standard, 0% under participation exemption where conditions met, reduced under DTTs). For balance-of-payments statistics, transactions >EUR 12,500 must be reported to BCL using standard purpose codes, but this is a statistical filing duty, not a regulatory barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg has no surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement for export proceeds. Exporters may retain currency balances in foreign-currency accounts held with Luxembourg banks or abroad.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); supervisory authority for credit institutions and payment/EMI institutions is the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with legal constraints. A Luxembourg-authorised or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2 can be used for compartmentalised payment flows, but is not a viable sole treasury vehicle for a wholesale SARL engaged in physical commodity trade. Constraints: EMIs cannot pay interest, extend credit, or accept deposits; they face strict safeguarding obligations; trade finance instruments (LCs, guarantees) are not within the standard EMI licence. An offshore digital EMI (e.g. non-EEA) is generally not viable for primary settlement due to AML/KYC equivalence, CSSF supervision gap, and bank de-risking reluctance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 with a BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment (commonly 'S301 – Equity capital for a direct investment' or equivalent code as specified by the BCL statistical reporting circular). Verification: copy of the share subscription resolution, notarized incorporation deed, bank proof of funds from the remitting shareholder, and confirmation that the credit to the account in Luxembourg is not below the statutory minimum capital for an SARL. Transfers above EUR 12,500 must be reported to BCL by the Luxembourg receiving bank via the central reporting system; no prior authorisation is required.For settlement of international trade obligations, the preferred rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments with mirrored invoice and transport documents retained in the bank's trade verification file; (ii) Commercial documentary letters of credit (LC) issued by the Luxembourg bank (UCP 600) for high-value shipments, especially where the overseas buyer requires bank assurance; (iii) SWIFT MT202/MT210 for cover payments between banks. All outbound disbursements require the standard supporting packet: purchase agreement, supplier invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, and where applicable phytosanitary or conformity certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if the entity's annual turnover exceeds €10 million or if related-party transactions exceed €1 million. Master File required if the group's consolidated turnover exceeds €50 million. Documentation must be prepared by the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in geographically mobile activities (e.g., IP holding, financing). For a wholesale trading company, substance is evidenced by local management, employees, office premises, and decision-making. Failure to meet substance may lead to recharacterization and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to €250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 15% withholding tax on deemed dividends. Administrative fines up to €50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they fail to ensure substance. In severe cases, criminal charges for fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for legal entities, plus suspension of business license. Criminal prosecution of responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors can face imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines up to €500,000 for AML violations.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of the adjustment, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing. In egregious cases, criminal tax evasion charges.

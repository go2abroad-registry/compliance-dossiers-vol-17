Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the Guichet Unique Électronique at lbr.lu and MyGuichet.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Part VI governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), Articles 710-1 to 710-24, read together with the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the electronic filing and publication regime of the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (notarial deed of incorporation, executed before a Luxembourg notary; a written private deed is permissible for a single-member SARL), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 37 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (unless the simplified Sàrl-S regime with EUR 1 capital is adopted). The entire capital must be subscribed; each quota must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value in cash into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution prior to incorporation. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report and must be fully transferred within five years. The escrowed funds are released only upon effective RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification of the professional premises is required from the communal fire brigade or the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS), including an approved safety dossier and functioning fire detection and extinguishing systems; the building must possess a structural conformity certificate or valid occupancy permit under the municipal building regulations. The workplace must be declared to the ITM under the Law of 17 June 1994 on occupational safety and health, and a workplace risk assessment must be prepared and submitted to the accredited occupational health service (service de santé au travail) prior to hiring the first employee.

Paid-in capital rule: Statutory minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 (unless the simplified Sàrl-S regime with EUR 1 capital is adopted). The entire capital must be subscribed; each quota must be paid up to at least 25% of its nominal value in cash into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution prior to incorporation. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report and must be fully transferred within five years. The escrowed funds are released only upon effective RCS registration.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Category: Autorisation d'établissement (Establishment Licence) for the regulated intellectual professional activity of marine insurance claims surveying, vessel hull and cargo damage inspection, issued under the Law of 2 September 2011 regulating access to the professions of craftsman, trader, industrialist and certain liberal professions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties, and excise. Primary e-portals: MyGuichet.lu, ACD eCDF professional filing portal, and AEDT eTVA portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the articles of association, deposit the minimum capital, and file the incorporation dossier with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the RCS registration number. Step 2: Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns the national 13-digit business registration number (matricule), formally communicated by the competent bureau d'imposition. Step 3: Deliver Form 1 TVA (VAT registration application) to the AEDT together with the RCS extract, certified identity documents of managers, registered office proof, and a statement of intended activity; obtain the LU VAT identification number (LU plus 8 digits) normally within 5 to 10 working days before commencing any taxable activity. Step 4: Remain registered with the ACD for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; no separate direct tax certificate is required beyond the matricule.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident SARL (société à responsabilité limitée) is a full taxpayer under Article 159 LIR, subject to corporate income tax on worldwide profits, with no fiscal transparency or pass-through allocation to shareholders. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident SARL (société à responsabilité limitée) is a full taxpayer under Article 159 LIR, subject to corporate income tax on worldwide profits, with no fiscal transparency or pass-through allocation to shareholders. Combined Luxembourg City burden for 2025: corporate income tax 17%, solidarity surcharge 7% of CIT, municipal business tax 6.75%, aggregating to approximately 24.94%, before the 75% municipal business tax deductibility adjustment.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by commercial law. Specific elections available: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR, requiring direct or indirect participation of at least 95% in taxable Luxembourg subsidiaries for private equity and industrial groups; (2) VAT grouping election under Article 60 VAT Law; (3) IP Box election under Article 50bis LIR granting an 80% exemption on qualifying intellectual property income subject to nexus substance requirements; (4) optional deemed dividend distribution and tax consolidation elections are not available to SARLs; hybrid mismatch outcomes are statutorily neutralized under ATAD I and II, and no elective opt-out exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established foreign businesses: any non-resident entity making taxable supplies of goods or services in Luxembourg, or receiving intra-EU acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000 per annum, has an immediate VAT registration obligation with no minimum turnover threshold. The domestic EUR 35,000 small-business exemption is unavailable to foreign entities and to a marine surveying SARL.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 55(1) of the Luxembourg VAT Law applies to goods physically dispatched or transported out of the EU and to services directly connected with the export chain, including vessel hull and cargo damage marine inspection and surveying services, provided: (i) the underlying goods are declared for export and leave the EU within a reasonable period (AEDT circular reference of 90 days), (ii) the surveyor holds documentary evidence identifying the export declaration, the goods, and the exporter, and (iii) the service is contractually linked to that specific export transaction. For cross-border B2B services where the customer is identified with a valid EU VAT identification number, zero-rating is achieved through the reverse charge mechanism under Articles 58 to 60 of the VAT Law, without requiring customs proof.

  • Formal customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) accepted and cleared by the EU customs office of exit
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract incorporating Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR/rail consignment note evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing the receipt of export proceeds
  • Surveyor's cargo damage inspection report, weather and seaworthiness log, and vessel hull assessment certificate
  • Marine insurance open cover or single-voyage policy endorsing the surveyor engagement
  • Exporter's signed declaration confirming the goods have left EU customs territory and the VAT number of the exporter where applicable

Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing introduced by the law of 13 June 2024, phased from 1 January 2026 for taxpayers issuing domestic B2B invoices above EUR 100,000 per year, and from 1 January 2027 for all taxpayers. Invoices must be issued via PEPPOL-compliant EN 16931 structured formats. Structured electronic reporting (e-reporting) of transactional VAT data to the AEDT becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027. No real-time continuous transaction controls (CTCs) or digital fiscal stamping regime is currently in force, and export invoices remain outside the domestic e-invoicing scope.

The AEDT requires contemporaneous retention of all export substantiation for at least 10 years under Article 55(4) of the VAT Law and AEDT administrative circulars. Failure to produce credible evidence triggers rectification under Article 34 AO, reassessment of VAT at 17% on the contract price, monthly late-payment interest of 0.6% under Article 61 AO, and administrative fines up to EUR 250,000 under Articles 61 to 63 AO. Fraudulent zero-rating claims with intent to evade are prosecuted under Article 137 AO, carrying imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and fines up to EUR 1,000,000. Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark serves as an internal export documentation standard, and abnormal delays in receiving export consideration may be recharacterized under Article 164bis LIR as an interest-free loan or deemed dividend, attracting thin capitalization review and net wealth tax consequences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg does not operate a foreign exchange control regime. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, and capital movements, payments, dividends, and profit repatriations are free under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Cross-border transfers in EUR or other convertible currencies may be executed without exchange-control approval. Profit remittances from a Luxembourg SARL are subject only to corporate taxation and dividend withholding tax under Luxembourg domestic law and applicable tax treaties; there are no foreign-exchange conversion or prior-authorization hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no compulsory foreign-conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation of export receipts. A marine insurance surveyor invoicing non-resident underwriters or shipowners may retain export revenue in a foreign-currency account at a Luxembourg bank. The only residual obligations are the AML/KYC documentation duties imposed on credit institutions and BCL statistical balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border flows above applicable reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for bank licensing, AML/CFT supervision, and foreign exchange and balance-of-payments compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Luxembourg-licensed or EU/EEA-passported electronic money institutions may provide transaction accounts and payment services to a Luxembourg SARL, but their legal use is limited to operational expense payments. Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary treasury flows and low-value claims disbursements; Low for holding underwriter premiums or acting as a client-money escrow. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport are not permitted to provide payment services in Luxembourg. Marine claims inspection by its nature involves regulated insurance-sector payments, so funds received from underwriters should be held at a fully regulated credit institution rather than an EMI to preserve segregated-client-money protection and CSSF comfort.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Luxembourg SARL, an inbound capital injection from non-resident shareholders is executed by a standard SWIFT MT103 credit to a blocked capital-escrow account opened in the company's name with a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire reference must clearly state the counterparty, the legal entity name, and the commercial purpose, for example 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION — LU_SARL' or an equivalent ISO 20022 remittance structure. The bank issues a funds-confirmation certificate to the Luxembourg notary before the execution of the notarial deed of incorporation or capital increase. The institution records the transfer for AML/CFT purposes, and the BCL may collect the transaction through its balance-of-payments statistical framework. No exchange-control authorization or administrative permit is required for euro-denominated or convertible foreign-currency capital inflows.Outbound payments from the marine-survey SARL are cleared primarily through SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR-denominated payments within the European Economic Area and through SWIFT MT103 messages for non-EUR or third-country settlements. For claim settlements collected from underwriters and disbursed to surveyors, attorneys, marine casualty experts, or equipment suppliers, the SARL should use its corporate account with an IBAN and clearly structured remittance fields showing the underlying claims file number, insured vessel, and policy reference. A commercial letter of credit may be used where an overseas supplier insists on bank payment undertaking for physical survey equipment; however, standard maritime inspection-services disbursements do not require documentary letters of credit. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Paper returns: due 30 June following the fiscal year-end. Electronic returns: generally due 31 December of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; the ACD annually confirms this automatic extension. For a calendar-year FY ending 31 December 2024, the e-filing deadline is 31 December 2025.

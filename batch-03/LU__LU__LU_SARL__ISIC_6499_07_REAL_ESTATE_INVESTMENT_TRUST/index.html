Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) (direct corporate income tax, net wealth tax) and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) (VAT, registration duties), both under the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance; primary official portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu; corporate legal entity registration: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL, the statutory sequence is: (i) execute a notarized deed of incorporation in Luxembourg; (ii) file with the RCS/Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the legal entity 'matricule' registration number; (iii) obtain the 11-digit Luxembourg identification number (numéro d'immatriculation) from the Luxembourg direct tax administration (ACD); (iv) register for VAT with AEDT using the standard VAT registration form (Form n°1 or electronic channel via MyGuichet), receiving a Luxembourg VAT identification number formatted as LU + 8 digits; (v) complete employer and withholding tax registrations if hiring staff; (vi) activate the electronic filing credential (TVA eCDF / MyGuichet authentication). Non-resident corporate investors may directly register through an accredited tax representative when required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Luxembourg SARL, the statutory sequence is: (i) execute a notarized deed of incorporation in Luxembourg; (ii) file with the RCS/Luxembourg Business Registers to obtain the legal entity 'matricule' registration number; (iii) obtain the 11-digit Luxembourg identification number (numéro d'immatriculation) from the Luxembourg direct tax administration (ACD); (iv) register for VAT with AEDT using the standard VAT registration form (Form n°1 or electronic channel via MyGuichet), receiving a Luxembourg VAT identification number formatted as LU + 8 digits; (v) complete employer and withholding tax registrations if hiring staff; (vi) activate the electronic filing credential (TVA eCDF / MyGuichet authentication). Non-resident corporate investors may directly register through an accredited tax representative when required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply US-style check-the-box classification. Under Luxembourg law, a SARL is per se subject to entity-level corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax, with imputation of withholding tax on dividends. Available elective mechanisms include: (i) Luxembourg fiscal unity / tax consolidation for CIT purposes (intégration fiscale) by a resident parent holding at least 95% of a resident subsidiary; (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) per Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; (iii) election of the 80% IP exemption box regime (R&D qualifying assets); (iv) ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch restriction disclosures; (v) participation exemption election for dividends and capital gains under strict holding thresholds and substance requirements. No full tax-transparency election is available for an SARL under Luxembourg domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business franchise (régime de la franchise) below which small established Luxembourg businesses may be exempt, subject to option to be taxed; non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Luxembourg do not generally benefit from a quantitative threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT from the first taxable transaction in Luxembourg, unless a special simplification regime (typically one-stop-shop or reverse-charge) applies. Threshold figures and franchise provisions are subject to annual EU small-business harmonization updates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Luxembourg VAT is available when the title/vendor supplies goods that are physically transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union (export zero rate), or when goods are dispatched to another EU Member State in an intra-Community supply where purchasing VAT ID is valid and the EC Sales List / recapitulative statement is filed. Services are zero-rated or exempt when the place of supply is treated as outside Luxembourg (e.g., B2B services supplied to a non-EU customer where reverse-charge transfers liability, or services directly linked to exported goods under Article 162 of the VAT Code). Conditions include proof of physical dispatch within statutory time limits, retention of transport and commercial documents, and good-faith compliance with the standard VAT invoice requirements; the exporter must be the VAT-registered taxable person and act in a business context.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Exit Summary Declaration), customs endorsed exit evidence from the EU border
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (AWB) evidencing international transport from Luxembourg
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank payment/swift confirmation evidencing the economic transfer of proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, the zero-rated supply invoice, and transport contract (CMR or CIM)
  • Proof of export regularity (export holder authorizations, lodged T1 / T2 documents when relevant)

As of the filing date, Luxembourg does not operate a mandatory real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC) clearance mechanism for business-to-business invoices. E-invoicing is permitted under EU VAT invoice rules, and electronic invoices are accepted if authenticity/legibility requirements are met. For public procurement (B2G) invoices, Luxembourg mandates e-invoicing through the national Peppol-compliant platform. No API stamping or real-time clearance with AEDT is required for domestic B2B invoices yet; taxpayers must nonetheless retain full invoice data for audits. Luxembourg is aligning with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposal, under which future electronic invoice reporting obligations will be introduced.

Luxembourg VAT authorities conduct targeted scrutiny of export zero-rating claims, cross-referencing customs data, transport and payment documentation. Where zero-rating is denied, the full VAT is reassessed with late-payment interest from the due date; penalties range from administrative fines (usually EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 per breach) to tax fraud penalties of up to EUR 250,000, plus possible imprisonment for up to 5 years, with retrospective clawback of input VAT recovery and extraterritorial or group liability under joint and several liability rules. Deductions taken against a fraudulent export claim may be disallowed, and the limitation period for tax fraud investigations may be extended to 10 years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full current- and capital-account convertibility without foreign-exchange controls, licensing, surrender, or prior-approval barriers on investment, financing, distribution, or liquidation flows. The euro is freely convertible for all treasury operations of this real estate investment trust. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the FX/treasury level, but dividends paid by the SARL attract Luxembourg withholding tax at 15% (potentially 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders, or reduced under applicable double tax treaties). Capital gains, principal repayments, and liquidation proceeds are not subject to withholding tax and are freely transferable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Luxembourg. The REIT may retain, convert, or reinvest foreign-currency income without authorization. Cross-border payments are reported statistically by the executing Luxembourg credit institution to the BCL under the Eurosystem balance-of-payments (BPM6) framework via purpose codes embedded in SWIFT/SEPA messages; direct BCL declarations may be triggered for external financial positions such as intercompany debt or foreign property acquisitions above the applicable BCL threshold. No reporting duty restricts liquidity mobilization.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); exchange and capital-movement supervision is shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while monetary policy and TARGET2 settlement are administered by the European Central Bank within the Eurosystem. The Luxembourg Ministry of Finance retains residual competence for exceptional safeguard measures under TFEU Articles 64-66.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium, usable only as an operational cash-flow overlay and never as the primary treasury or escrow bank. A Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) under PSD2 and the Law of 10 November 2009 may hold e-money and execute payment transactions but cannot grant credit, issue guarantees or letters of credit, or provide the escrow, custody, and depositary services required for regulated REIT or RAIF/SIF structures. Third-country EMIs are commonly declined for Luxembourg real estate SPVs because they lack BCL balance-of-payments reporting connectivity and cannot satisfy CSSF-expected custodial controls. EMIs remain acceptable for small operational rails such as property-management fees, utility payments, and contractor rebates, provided the main acquisition, escrow, and dividend flows clear through a Tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital (share capital, share premium, and shareholder loans) must be routed exclusively into the dedicated Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Luxembourg Tier-1 bank. Remittances are executed by the investor via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for cover aggregations), with the beneficiary field displaying the exact legal name, the LU IBAN, and the escrow sub-account reference. The remitter's bank must be instructed to include the BCL/Eurosystem balance-of-payments purpose code for 'Direct Investment – Equity' or 'Direct Investment – Intercompany Lending' in the structured reference field; where the remitter's clearing system requires an ISO 20022 purpose code, the equivalent code is entered at the executing bank's discretion. Before escrow release to the operational account, the bank's compliance function verifies: (i) the board resolution approving the capital call; (ii) the notarized subscription deed or loan agreement; (iii) the RBE extract evidencing beneficial owners; (iv) source-of-funds documentation for amounts above EUR 1,000,000; and (v) sanctions and adverse-media screening. Settlement is executed via TARGET2 for EUR or via the bank's USD/EUR correspondent network; SEPA Credit Transfer may be used for euro contributions from EEA shareholders where eligible, but MT103 is preferred for auditability. Intra-group funding must be contracted on arm's-length terms and supported by transfer pricing documentation to satisfy both the bank and Luxembourg tax authorities.EUR-denominated disbursements to EEA contractors, property managers, insurers, and tax authorities are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/SCT Inst) and SEPA Direct Debit (SDD Core/B2B). Large acquisitions and refurbishment contracts settled outside the EEA are executed through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers; where documentary security is required, the bank issues or advises commercial letters of credit under MT700/MT707 (UCP600) for imports of materials and construction equipment, with transport documents collected through the presenting bank. Dividend and profit distributions are remitted via SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA beneficiaries, or via SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV for non-EEA investors; for institutional US recipients, the bank's US correspondent clears distributions through ACH/Fedwire rails. The distribution sub-account is funded at least one TARGET2 business day before payment date. Every outgoing cross-border instruction carries the appropriate BCL balance-of-payments purpose code and is screened against sanctions, PEP, and fraud lists prior to release.Outright single-currency FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF) with tenors up to 24 months, hedging rental income, purchase prices, and distribution cash flows; executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA margin and EMIR clearing requirements.Cross-currency interest rate swaps converting foreign-currency acquisition debt (USD/GBP) into EUR floating-rate exposure, subject to EMIR clearing and margin rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without deliverable offshore markets, where an underlying real-estate rental or sale exposure exists and physical settlement is legally impaired.Vanilla FX options (EUR call/put) purchased to protect minimum repatriation yields on foreign-currency distribution streams; writing uncovered options is not permissible without CSSF consent and an economic-hedge memorandum.Short-dated FX swaps for liquidity management between Treasury sub-accounts at the same bank, used exclusively to settle cross-currency positions without speculative open positions.

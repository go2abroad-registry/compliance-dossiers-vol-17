Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Grand-Duchy of Luxembourg – Value Added Tax is administered by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), 1-3 Avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg; direct taxes are administered by the Administration des contributions directes (ACD), 33 Rue de la Congrégation, L-1352 Luxembourg; all official registrations, declarations, and taxpayer communications are channelled through the MyGuichet.lu professional portal and the eCDF electronic filing platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities: (1) before making any taxable supply in Luxembourg, file VAT registration Form 1 with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu Espace professionnel, attaching power of attorney, constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax residence, and projected Luxembourg turnover; (2) the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits, normally within 5-10 business days; (3) if a fixed establishment or branch is created, register the branch with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL) to obtain the corporate matricule, and obtain a business establishment permit (autorisation d'établissement) where applicable; (4) for direct tax purposes, a non-resident entity with Luxembourg-source business income or a permanent establishment must register with the ACD (Forms 660/670) and file annual corporate income tax and municipal business tax returns; resident SARLs are registered automatically upon notarial incorporation and obtain both the matricule and VAT number ex officio.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – The SARL (société à responsabilité limitée) is a legally opaque Luxembourg-resident capital company subject to corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC), the solidarity surcharge, net wealth tax, and VAT. From 1 January 2025 the CIT headline rate is 16%, the solidarity surcharge is 7% of CIT, and the ICC in Luxembourg City is approximately 6.75%, producing an aggregate combined rate of approximately 23.87%. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with relief for foreign permanent-establishment income; the SARL is not pass-through eligible, and the wholesale dairy commodity trade is ordinary commercial income not situated in any tax-exempt zone. — Foreign entities: (1) before making any taxable supply in Luxembourg, file VAT registration Form 1 with the AEDT via MyGuichet.lu Espace professionnel, attaching power of attorney, constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax residence, and projected Luxembourg turnover; (2) the AEDT issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits, normally within 5-10 business days; (3) if a fixed establishment or branch is created, register the branch with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL) to obtain the corporate matricule, and obtain a business establishment permit (autorisation d'établissement) where applicable; (4) for direct tax purposes, a non-resident entity with Luxembourg-source business income or a permanent establishment must register with the ACD (Forms 660/670) and file annual corporate income tax and municipal business tax returns; resident SARLs are registered automatically upon notarial incorporation and obtain both the matricule and VAT number ex officio.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – The SARL (société à responsabilité limitée) is a legally opaque Luxembourg-resident capital company subject to corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC), the solidarity surcharge, net wealth tax, and VAT. From 1 January 2025 the CIT headline rate is 16%, the solidarity surcharge is 7% of CIT, and the ICC in Luxembourg City is approximately 6.75%, producing an aggregate combined rate of approximately 23.87%. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with relief for foreign permanent-establishment income; the SARL is not pass-through eligible, and the wholesale dairy commodity trade is ordinary commercial income not situated in any tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid transparency election exists; fiscal classification is determined mandatorily by legal form. Available elections and regimes include: (i) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 61 of the VAT law for closely linked Luxembourg-established persons; (ii) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR, permitting CIT and ICC consolidation among companies in a 95% direct or indirect ownership chain; (iii) option to tax certain VAT-exempt supplies, such as immovable property leasing, under Article 50 of the VAT law; (iv) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS/IOSS) registration for EU distance sales and certain B2C services; (v) SOPARFI participation exemption, which applies automatically by statute to qualifying dividends and capital gains without election; and (vi) mandatory application of ATAD and EU anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Law of 21 December 2018, with no elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None for non-established foreign entities – VAT registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply in Luxembourg. Resident small traders may claim the Article 57 VAT small-business franchise for annual turnover below EUR 35,000 (excluding construction services); the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales of goods; intra-Community acquisitions by a taxable person below EUR 10,000 per annum do not trigger Luxembourg VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of raw whole and skimmed milk powder (HS 0402.10 / 0402.21) to non-EU buyers qualify for the 0% VAT rate (exemption with right to deduct input VAT) under Article 54 of the Luxembourg VAT law if: (i) the goods physically leave EU customs territory within the statutory period, generally within three months of the supply date; (ii) the export is validated by an EU customs export declaration accepted and released through the Automated Export System (AES) with exit confirmation; (iii) the sale is made to a non-EU consignee and the transport is performed by the supplier, a third party, or the foreign purchaser; (iv) the supplier obtains and retains the complete documentary evidence file. Where goods are held in customs warehousing or in the Luxembourg Freeport prior to export, VAT may be suspended until the export zero-rating is substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU – Document Administratif Unique) accepted and released through the EU Automated Export System (AES), including the Export Control Reference (ECR) and exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR waybill (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – SWIFT/MT103 bank credit evidence proving receipt of the export sale proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice stating Incoterms, HS code 0402.10/0402.21, quantities, weights, and values
  • Purchase order or export sales contract, together with packing list
  • T1 transit declaration or customs warehouse exit records where the export movement used transit suspension

Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing applies under the Law of 15 July 2023 (draft law n. 8032) and its implementing regulation: from 1 January 2025, all Luxembourg-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 January 2026, they must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies. The mandated format is EN 16931 / PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0, transmitted via PEPPOL access points or compliant digital transmission platforms; Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls (CTC) model, and no QR stamping or government pre-clearance of invoices is required. VAT returns (monthly or quarterly) and EU recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the eCDF portal, and wholesale commodity traders must maintain SAF-T-compatible accounting records for audit.

The AEDT may audit export zero-rating claims within the standard five-year VAT assessment limitation period, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or serious irregularity. Failure to produce the complete export documentation bundle within the administrative deadline leads to denial of the 0% rate and assessment of VAT at 16% on the invoice value, plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month and administrative penalties under the Luxembourg General Tax Law ranging from EUR 250 up to EUR 100,000; proven fraudulent export claims expose the taxpayer to criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Retrospective clawback also disallows the corresponding input VAT deductions, reinstates net wealth tax and corporate income tax adjustments, and may trigger an extended statute of limitations and publication of the penalty decision.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is guaranteed for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty framework on free movement of capital, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and the Luxembourg Law of 19 March 2004 on the status of the BCL. There are no exchange-control restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, or settlement of trade obligations. The sole fiction is that all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 125,000 must be declared to the BCL for statistical balance-of-payments data (BOP filings); such reporting is not an approval gate, but a post-period openness requirement, and non-filing can result in late penalties after the 15th business day of the following month. In practice, foreign investors must brief their paying bank with the applicable EU BOP purpose code at the time of transfer to avoid administrative delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory repatriation period, and no conversion ratio requiring export receipts to be surrendered to the BCL or any licensed bank. Dairy commodity exporters may retain EUR, USD, or third-currency balances in any SEPA-connected or SWIFT-connected account. The only obligation is the standard commercial accounting requirement to book invoiced receivables in EUR for financial statement purposes and to observe any bank-negotiated repayment covenants on trade finance facilities. As a Eurozone Member State, there is no central-bank obligation to convert export proceeds into EUR; however, if the SARL incurs a natural FX risk on USD-denominated receipts and EUR-denominated costs, the treasury risk must be managed voluntarily with permitted hedging instruments.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and under the prudential/compliance framework of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Supervisory Authority of Luxembourg (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for SEPA-denominated in/out flows and ordinary corporate disbursements, but Low-Medium for agricultural commodity trade requiring SWIFT MT103/LC issuance. Licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the PSD2/CSSF regime in Luxembourg are legal and passportable across the EEA. They may efficiently onboard the SARL and provide multi-currency e-money wallets, but they cannot legally extend credit, issue documentary letters of credit, or act as clearing agents for large physical milk-powder trade settlements. For the expected trade size, a tier-1 credit institution remains mandatory; fintech rails should be considered only for ancillary working-capital movements, not for primary clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) all capital contributions exceeding EUR 1,000 must be remitted via wire from an account held in the name of the registered shareholder, no cash or third-party payments; (ii) use SWIFT MT103 for large amounts (EUR 50,000+), otherwise SEPA CT is acceptable for EUR-dominated contributions from EEA account holders; (iii) include the BCL statistical purpose code for direct investment capital participation — accepted codes are 'D.1.1.1' (quoted and unquoted shares) or 'D.1.2.1' (repayment of equity-financing liabilities) as applicable; (iv) attach an opening brief containing the notarised incorporation deed, registration with the Luxembourg Registre de Commerce (RCS), and the bank's own compliance form; (v) mandatory attribution: the credit to the account must be titled 'Capital Contribution under Articles of Incorporation' with the transaction reference For dairy commodity purchases, the primary clearing rails are: (i) direct documentary letters of credit (LC) governed by UCP 600, the bank issuing a commercial LC to the seller's bank in Switzerland, Belgium, or overseas, covering shipment of blank milk powder (HS 0402.10 / 0402.21); (ii) SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for the post-shipment settlement, with purpose code 'Trade in Goods — Imports' (BOP code 'G.2.1') where the underlying invoice refers to ISIC 4633; (iii) SWIFT MT101 for direct operator-originated payments from the SARL's account; (iv) risk-reducing contingent MT798/MT799 guarantees for quality-quantity warranties. For transactions with Turkish or African buyers, a confirmed LC from a first-class EU bank is the standard instrument to avoid cross-border non-payment risk. Settlement currency is EUR for intra-EEA counterparties and USD for international counterparties, with EUR/USD conversion executed only through the account-holding bank or an approved FX broker.Deliverable over-the-counter (OTC) EUR/USD FX forwards entered into pursuant to an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or local master FX agreement with a licensed credit institution or investment firm, subject to EMIR mandatory clearing/screening at the clearing threshold if the aggregate notional exceeds the applicable CSSF/ESMA thresholds.Direct EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, and EUR/GBP FX swaps used only for cash-flow management, aligned with the SARL's documented treasury policy and subject to BOP reporting thresholds.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for multi-year USDA-denominated dairy supply contracts, where the notional exchange is physically settled at maturity; such swaps are permissible only where the rationale is hedging the currency risk on the underlying commodity trade and are not permitted for speculative directional carry.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies subject to offshore restrictions (e.g., CNY) — permissible only for hedging genuine trade flows denominated in that currency, and must be entered into with a Luxembourg-authorised credit institution or a validly passporting EU bank; any NDF must be documented with the underlying trade invoice to avoid recharacterisation as a regulated leveraged derivative.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% effective aggregate rate in Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; 0% for dividends meeting the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and the Luxembourg participation exemption (holding at least 10% or an investment of at least EUR 1.2 million held for an uninterrupted 12-month period).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the close of the fiscal year; for calendar-year companies the tax administration normally requires filing by 31 December of the following year; extension available upon reasoned request.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline aligned to the CIT return deadline (31 December following the fiscal year-end; extensions available on request).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File triggered when aggregate intra-group transactions of the Luxembourg entity exceed EUR 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; Master File required where the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000 (the CbCR threshold). Documentation must be contemporaneous and drafted in English, French, or German.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style ESR schedule, but it enforces economic substance through the OECD substance doctrine, the Luxembourg anti-abuse clause, EU ATAD, and Circulars LIR 56/1 and LIR 56bis-1. To secure treaty benefits and participation exemption, the trading SARL must demonstrate local staffing, strategic decision-making, operational risk, and physical office presence. A letterbox company without genuine substance is denied treaty relief and subject to retroactive reclassification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deliberate Under-Declaration and Fraudulent Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg tax fraud penalties: fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years, plus an administrative surcharge of 10% to 100% of the evaded tax depending on gravity, monthly late-payment interest of 0.8%, and automatic rejection of deductible expenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be held personally liable for the SARL's unpaid tax up to the amount of the tax debt when they knowingly file false returns; criminal courts may order a travel ban, asset freeze, and a professional ban of up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities disregard the corporate veil, reallocate all Luxembourg-source commodity profits to the beneficial owner, deny treaty and participation exemption, impose an additional net wealth tax assessment, and levy a civil fine up to EUR 25,000; registration in the RCS may be struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors allowing the company to operate as a shell become jointly responsible for corporate liabilities if the company is clearly insolvent; the court may order liquidation, directorship disqualification, and personal civil liability for the unpaid tax and damages.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Unsatisfactory Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax office may adjust the taxable margin to an arm's length level, reclassify the difference as a constructive dividend, apply a 10% surcharge for non-reporting, and impose a fine up to EUR 50,000 for missing documentation; default interest runs from the original assessment's due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations may be personally assessed for the additional tax under the directors' civil negligence standard; administrative sanctions may include removal from office and a ban from board membership in a Luxembourg company for at least five years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance in Cross-Border Flows

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF regulations: criminal imprisonment of up to five years and a fine up to EUR 1,250,000; administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; automatic withdrawal of the commodity-trading license relevant to ISIC 4633 dairy distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in AML breaches are liable for damages to the company and third parties, may be subject to travel bans pending criminal investigation, and can be barred from exercising any corporate mandate for up to 10 years; personal bankruptcy and asset confiscation may be ordered in severe cases.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) / Luxembourg Inland Revenue; registration portal: MyGuichet.lu and the Registre national des sociétés et associations (RCSL); indirect tax authority: Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) with VAT registration via MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must incorporate via a notary public and register with the RCSL (Luxembourg Business Registers). Following incorporation, the entity must file a request for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) with the AED, and obtain a corporate tax ID assigned by the ACD upon filing the first tax return or through the provisional registration process. Non-resident companies with no permanent establishment but with Luxembourg-source income must register directly with the ACD for a Luxembourg tax reference number and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must incorporate via a notary public and register with the RCSL (Luxembourg Business Registers). Following incorporation, the entity must file a request for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) with the AED, and obtain a corporate tax ID assigned by the ACD upon filing the first tax return or through the provisional registration process. Non-resident companies with no permanent establishment but with Luxembourg-source income must register directly with the ACD for a Luxembourg tax reference number and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not maintain a check-the-box regime. A SARL is per se subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) at entity level, plus municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and the solidarity surcharge. Eligible groups may elect for fiscal unity (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for resident companies, subject to a 95% direct or indirect participation and a five-year commitment. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (Article 168ter LIR) apply and may be disallowed for payments involving hybrid instruments or hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for Luxembourg-established suppliers; any person carrying out taxable transactions in Luxembourg must register. Non-established businesses supplying distance sales to Luxembourg consumers are subject to the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme once intra-Community distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 annually across the EU. An EU-established supplier exceeding EUR 100,000 of intra-Community supplies must register in Luxembourg or use the OSS for non-Union B2C electronic services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations may be zero-rated provided proof of export is evidenced (actual departure from the EU within 90 days for transport documentation, or within the time limits of the fiscal receipt). Advertising, DSP and SSP services supplied to non-established business customers are generally taxable at the Luxembourg place of supply (B2B general rule) and therefore not automatically zero-rated unless the customer belongs outside the EU and the service falls under Article 17(2) or 17(3) LTA (e.g., advertising services). For B2B services, Luxembourg VAT applies where the customer is established; consequently cross-border ad-tech services exported to non-EU clients are generally outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT. Services physically performed or electronically supplied to non-EU private consumers may be zero-rated or outside scope under the place-of-supply rules, subject to supporting business evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Accompanying Document - EAD)
  • Transport Documents evidencing departure (CMR, Bill of Lading, Air Waybill)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of Payment / Bank Transfer Records
  • Purchase Orders and Contracts
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (where FDI approval triggered) or bank inward remittance advice for non-EU settlement

Luxembourg does not currently mandate a nationwide real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC) clearance or clearance stamping regime for standard B2B domestic invoicing. Electronic invoicing is permitted under general VAT rules subject to authenticity, integrity and legibility; Peppol BIS Billing is the standard used for public procurement through the eFiling platform. Businesses must file periodic VAT returns electronically, and e-invoice data must be retained for the statutory ten-year period.

Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims may result in reassessment of VAT plus default interest at 0.8% per month. Intentional VAT evasion triggers criminal tax penalties of up to double the evaded tax and potentially imprisonment. Administratively, the AED can impose fines up to the amount of tax evaded and may revoke the right to apply the zero rate; directors may be held jointly liable. For goods, failure to satisfy export documentation within prescribed deadlines triggers reverse charge VAT at 17% plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Eurozone membership and Articles 63-66 TFEU guarantee unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. Luxembourg imposes no exchange-control licensing, no retention quotas, no minimum-holding periods, and no ordering or prioritisation of remittances; the EUR/USD and EUR/GBP markets are fully deliverable without any BCL involvement. Profit-remittance gates are non-regulatory and are driven by corporate law and tax law: (i) the S.à r.l. minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 must remain intact, and distributions must not reduce net assets below the share capital plus non-distributable reserves; (ii) a solvency / balance-sheet test applies to each distribution; (iii) the standard 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax applies to outbound profit distributions, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders and to reduced treaty rates (e.g., 5% or 0% under the Luxembourg-US DTT) for non-EU investors; and (iv) intra-group interest and royalty flows must satisfy arm's-length transfer-pricing rules to preserve deductibility. No prior central-bank approval is required for any currency conversion or for the repatriation of profits, dividends, loans, or service fees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg statutory law and BCL regulation impose no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, and no statutory repatriation deadline for service receipts. A Luxembourg S.à r.l. earning USD from US programmatic ad networks, DSPs, and publishers may retain USD indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts without penalty. The only attached compliance duties are: (1) mandatory BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting, which the bank executes automatically on the basis of the BOP purpose code stated on each cross-border payment instruction; (2) the EUR 10,000 physical-cash declaration threshold under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672; and (3) originator and beneficiary travel-rule data transmission under Regulation (EU) 2015/847 for all wire transfers. Neither the BCL nor the CSSF requires hedging, conversion, or surrender of proceeds on any timeline.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the national central bank of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg and the Eurosystem member for Luxembourg; operates TARGET2-LU settlement and holds the statutory balance-of-payments statistics mandate. Prudential supervision and AML/KYC enforcement over credit institutions is shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), the financial-sector regulator.

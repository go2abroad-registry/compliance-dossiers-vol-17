Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital for a SARL is €12,000 fully subscribed and paid in cash or in kind; funds must be held on a blocked capital-deposit account with a Luxembourg credit institution until RCSL registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the competent commune with expert review by the Luxembourg fire and rescue service (CGDIS) covering escape routes, extinguishing systems and hazardous-materials storage; structural-load inspection certificate issued by a registered building engineer; and occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), including electrical equipment compliance and workplace ventilation standards.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for a SARL is €12,000 fully subscribed and paid in cash or in kind; funds must be held on a blocked capital-deposit account with a Luxembourg credit institution until RCSL registration is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) / Luxembourg Inland Revenue, online portal: Guichet.lu and MyGuichet.lu; VAT matters administered by Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Luxembourg SARL must complete notarized incorporation before the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL), file with the Registration Department (AEDT) for VAT purposes on form 1027, and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (Numéro d'identification à la TVA) and a national business number (n° matricule). Registration must occur prior to commencing taxable activities; non-established taxpayers may require a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing a Luxembourg SARL must complete notarized incorporation before the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCSL), file with the Registration Department (AEDT) for VAT purposes on form 1027, and obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number (Numéro d'identification à la TVA) and a national business number (n° matricule). Registration must occur prior to commencing taxable activities; non-established taxpayers may require a Luxembourg tax representative for VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg applies a classical corporation tax system with no check-the-box pass-through election for SARLs; SARL is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). No hybrid mismatch election is available for SARL classification under domestic law; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed via ATAD-aligned rules and OECD BEPS Action 2 domestic provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration; registration mandatory for any taxable person established or whose permanent establishment in Luxembourg carries out taxable transactions, subject to small business exemption rules, and compulsory for foreign suppliers of distance sales exceeding EUR 10,000 or any B2C electronic services under the EU One-Stop-Shop regime)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf (Article 44 of the Luxembourg VAT Law). The exporter must hold proof of export and evidence that the goods physically left the EU within the statutory period (generally 90 days from supply, extendable to 180 days). Services related to exported goods (incoterms DDP handling, transport, ancillary services) may qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is established outside the EU and the service is directly linked to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document or electronic customs declaration summary declaration export under EU UCC)
  • Commercial invoice and proof of contract
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill with carrier's dated receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (evidence of foreign exchange repatriation from overseas buyer)
  • Cargo movement evidence (transit/pre-consignment records, logbook, GPS trace) where automated controls have been de-authorized
  • Export declaration acceptance acknowledgment (LR/IE-599 electronic acknowledgment)
  • Proof of physical exit (certificate of arrival or customs stamp from destination country when available)

E-invoicing is not mandatory for domestic B2B transactions; Luxembourg mandates the issuance of structured electronic invoices for B2G contracts (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 format) from 2023. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping (such as SAF-T continuous transaction controls) is required at national level; VAT returns must be filed electronically via eCDF平台 (MyGuichet.lu) on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis, with EC Sales Lists and Intrastat filings as applicable for cross-border transactions.

Luxembourg VAT authorities may disallow zero-rating and retroactively invoice VAT at 17% (or applicable rate) plus late interest and penalties up to 10% of the tax due per month of delay (maximum 50%), and may apply fines up to EUR 250,000 for serious export-fraud cases. Fraudulent zero-rating schemes trigger criminal sanctions, imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years, and joint and several liability for directors. Export documentation must be retained for at least 10 years; failure to substantiate exports results in VAT clawback, interest at 0.8% per month (statutory rate), and disqualification from refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls for residents or non-residents on capital or current account transactions under the EU Treaty and consolidated capital movement rules. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty, and interest repatriation are free of foreign exchange approvals, subject only to corporate governance, anti-money laundering, and tax compliance (including withholding tax). The EUR is a fully convertible currency; cross-border settlement in EUR or foreign currency is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export contract proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts with Luxembourg banks; only statistical reporting obligations under BCL balance-of-payments rules apply for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 or specified monthly thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as national central bank and member of the Eurosystem; foreign exchange administration operates under EU capital movement liberalisation, with CSSF supervising financial markets and money laundering controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to medium for offshore digital electronic money institutions. Luxembourg entities may hold e-money accounts with CSSF-authorised EMIs, but an offshore EMI not passporting under PSD2 into Luxembourg cannot offer regulated payment services to a Luxembourg-domiciled company; the entity would face restrictions on receiving client funds, LC issuance, and high-value treasury transactions. Such EMIs are better suited as auxiliary payment accounts, not as primary clearing rails.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-border ownership with ultimate beneficial owner in a non-EEA tax haven or high-risk jurisdiction.

Root cause: Cross-border ownership with ultimate beneficial owner in a non-EEA tax haven or high-risk jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Notarised corporate charters, current extract of Luxembourg trade register, apostilled passport copies, bank reference letters, detailed source-of-wealth statements, group structure chart, evidence of commercial substance (office lease, local payroll, telephone/fax), and recent audited financial statements of parent entities.
Expected high-value wire flows from government buyers and procurement ministries for life-support medical devices.

Root cause: Expected high-value wire flows from government buyers and procurement ministries for life-support medical devices.

Defense evidence: Executed supply contracts or framework agreements, EU CE marking certificate, ISO 13485 certificate, proforma invoices, end-customer due diligence file for counterparties, sanctions screening report, and procurement tender documentation explaining the nature of fund flows.
Use of third-party payment agents or distribution intermediaries for export deals.

Root cause: Use of third-party payment agents or distribution intermediaries for export deals.

Defense evidence: Duly notarised agency and distribution agreements, KYC file on each agent including beneficial ownership, copies of agent licences, commission schedules, and a description of how agent commissions are calculated and settled.
Rapid movement of capital injection from a non-banking corporate loan rather than equity.

Root cause: Rapid movement of capital injection from a non-banking corporate loan rather than equity.

Defense evidence: Loan agreement, board resolutions approving the loan, proof of source of lender funds, financial statements, and an explanation of the commercial rationale for debt rather than equity.

Compliant inbound routing uses SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA transfers or SEPA Instant/TARGET2 for EUR transfers from EEA countries. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if the group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million and related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million (goods) or EUR 500,000 (services). Master File required if group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in IP holding, financing, and other mobile activities. Manufacturing entities (e.g., medical device production) are expected to have substantial substance, including physical premises, qualified employees, and core decision-making functions in Luxembourg.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 100% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years, and mandatory publication of the judgment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they participated in or knowingly allowed the evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment are possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, recharacterization of income, imposition of penalties up to 10% of the adjusted income, and potential nullification of tax rulings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if the corporate veil is pierced due to fraudulent or abusive conduct.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million for legal entities, imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals, and revocation of business licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability, including imprisonment, if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent AML breaches. Travel bans and asset freezes may also apply.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalties up to 10% of the adjusted amount, and interest on underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may be pursued.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they approved or ignored non-compliant TP policies, especially in cases of gross negligence.

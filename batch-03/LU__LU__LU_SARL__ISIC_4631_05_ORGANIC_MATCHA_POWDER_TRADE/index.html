Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT; national e-portal MyGuichet.lu and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity not established in Luxembourg, VAT registration is mandatory before any taxable supply if no local fixed establishment exists. The foreign SARL (or its VAT representative) must file Form 102 via MyGuichet.lu, attach statutory incorporation documents, proof of economic activity, and power of attorney if a fiscal representative is appointed. Upon approval, the AED issues a Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + 8 digits) and a national business registration number is obtained from the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime. A SARL is automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Elective mechanisms include tax consolidation under Article 164 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR) for resident companies and the option to form a VAT group under specific conditions. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by EU ATAD provisions as transposed into Luxembourg law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established foreign entities: no registration threshold (mandatory from the first taxable transaction). For resident small enterprises: annual turnover exemption threshold of EUR 35,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier or its agent proves that the goods were physically dispatched or transported outside the EU. Evidence must include a customs export declaration, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and, when relevant, proof of payment. Intra-EU supplies of matcha powder may also be zero-rated where the customer is VAT-registered and the goods are dispatched to another EU member state.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for business-to-government contracts in Luxembourg under PEPPOL/EN 16931. There is no general real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or continuous transaction control regime for B2B or export VAT. VAT returns must be filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu with periodicity determined by annual turnover.

The Luxembourg VAT authorities may require evidence of export within 90 days of the supply and have power to reassess and claw back zero-rated treatment with statutory interest. False or unsupported export zero-rating claims are subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 10,000 per violation, criminal penalties including imprisonment for tax fraud, and full repayment of input VAT recovered on the underlying goods. Although no statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline exists for VAT purposes, absence of an Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent banking proof is a documented audit red flag and may trigger detailed forensic review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible, and Luxembourg maintains fully liberalized current and capital accounts with no administrative approval, licensing, or registration requirement for inbound equity injection, shareholder loans, trade settlement, or dividend/profit remittance. An SARL may freely receive and disburse EUR, USD, JPY, and other currencies through any CSSF-supervised credit institution. The only fiscal friction on outbound profit is Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (15% statutory; reduced to 0–5% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU or applicable double-tax treaties). Statutory legal-reserve rules (allocation of 5% of annual net profit until the reserve reaches 10% of paid-up capital, pursuant to the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended) must be observed before any discretionary dividend distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg and the Eurozone impose no statutory surrender ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion obligation for export proceeds. Receipts from matcha powder re-exports may be retained indefinitely in the settlement currency (EUR, USD, JPY, or other) in onshore or offshore accounts; the only requirement is statistical reporting of cross-border transactions to the BCL (effected by the domestic bank) under applicable ECB balance-of-payments regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the national central bank within the European System of Central Banks; the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises exclusive Eurosystem authority over monetary policy and official foreign-exchange operations. Financial-sector conduct and AML/CFT supervision are vested in the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg enforces no standalone foreign-exchange control law; cross-border capital movements are governed by the EU free-movement-of-capital regime (Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium for operational collections/disbursements; Low for capital escrow and trade-finance instruments. CSSF-licensed payment institutions and electronic-money institutions (Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services as amended; CSSF Circulars 18/698 and 23/811) may lawfully provide multi-currency IBANs, virtual accounts, and FX conversion with client funds held in segregated safeguarding accounts. However, an EMI cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot deliver the notary-grade blocked-funds attestation (attestation de blocage) required for SARL incorporation, and is not an acceptable primary banking relationship for a distribution company; non-EU digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer) are permissible only as supplementary collection rails, with settlement waterfalls directed to a tier-1 Luxembourg credit institution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), plus a 7% solidarity surcharge (adding 1.19 percentage points) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal). The aggregate statutory headline rate for a Luxembourg City-registered SARL is 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% under Luxembourg domestic law. The rate is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of the SARL for at least 12 months; tax-treaty rates typically range from 0% to 15% depending on the residence state and holding percentage.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A statutory transfer-pricing documentation file must be maintained where aggregate annual cross-border transactions between the SARL and related parties exceed EUR 450,000. A Country-by-Country Report and corresponding Master File are required where the group's consolidated annual revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime as applied in zero-tax centres. Instead, economic substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit), the GAAR implementing ATAD, the participation-exemption substance conditions and the Principal Purpose Test embedded in Luxembourg's tax treaties. The SARL must demonstrate local substance through physical office premises, operational staff, board meetings held in Luxembourg, genuine decision-making and commercial activity commensurate with its matcha trading function; absence of such substance may trigger denial of treaty or directive benefits, recharacterization of transactions and reassessment under the arm's length standard.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax increase of 10% to 100% of the evaded tax plus monthly default interest of 0.6%. Criminal conviction for tax fraud (Article 396 LIR) carries fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false returns become personally liable for the evaded tax, civil restitution and criminal penalties; the ACD may issue against them personal tax enforcement orders, and the competent court may impose travel bans and arrest warrants during the criminal proceedings.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and EU Directive benefits, disallowance of the participation exemption, recharacterization of the structure under the GAAR and abuse-of-law doctrine, and reassessment of the full income with 0.6% monthly interest and surcharges up to 100%.

Director personal liability: De facto and de jure directors may be held jointly liable for the additional corporate tax liabilities when they knowingly implemented the artificial structure; disqualification from board mandates and criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion may follow.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the law of 13 January 2019 ranging up to EUR 1.25 million for legal persons; the commercial court may suspend the SARL's activity; CSSF and banking partners may freeze payment rails and terminate banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally subject to fines of up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment of up to two years for deliberate non-compliance; in addition, directors can be ordered by the court to remedy the registration under penalty payments per day of delay.
Hidden Profit Distribution and Undisclosed Dividend

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the payment as a constructive dividend subject to 15% withholding tax plus default interest of 0.6% per month; the SARL may be assessed as agent for the uncollected WHT; the shareholder payment loses deductibility and attracts tax increases of up to 100%.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax where they knowingly arranged the undisclosed distribution; liability extends to civil restitution to the tax authority, and criminal sanctions under Article 396 LIR including fines and imprisonment up to five years.

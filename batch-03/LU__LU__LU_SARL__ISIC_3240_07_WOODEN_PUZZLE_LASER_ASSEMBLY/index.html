Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital for a SARL; at least 10% of the subscribed capital (with an absolute floor of EUR 12,000) must be deposited into a blocked escrow bank account in Luxembourg before the notarial deed is signed, and the blocked funds are released to the company only upon successful RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Corps (Service incendie et secours – SIS) covering premises and CO2 laser machinery; structural load inspection by the municipal authority confirming suitability of the existing floor for heavy equipment and storage; occupational health and safety clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) after verification of machine guards, emergency stops, local exhaust ventilation for fumes and dust, and laser-shielding compliance with the Luxembourg regulations on machinery safety; final clearance to operate is evidenced by a certificate of conformity from ITM and a fire safety opinion from the local commune.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital for a SARL; at least 10% of the subscribed capital (with an absolute floor of EUR 12,000) must be deposited into a blocked escrow bank account in Luxembourg before the notarial deed is signed, and the blocked funds are released to the company only upon successful RCS registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct corporate income tax; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; national business e-government portal: Guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL) for legal entity registration.

E-government filing portal

A Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) is incorporated by notarial deed and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL), obtaining a national business registration number. After incorporation and for VAT purposes, the entity must obtain a Luxembourg VAT identification number. Resident established taxable persons apply for a VAT number through the AEDT/RCSL procedures, while non-resident taxable persons apply directly to the AEDT using the simplified VAT registration form; non-EU applicants may be required to appoint a VAT fiscal representative approved by the AEDT and provide security for potential VAT liabilities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification elections; the SARL is per se subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax as a capital company. Available statutory elections include: the option to form a VAT group under Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for closely linked resident taxable persons; the option for corporate income tax consolidation under Article 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law for resident companies; and the application of the participation exemption through relevant anti-hybrid notifications, in compliance with ATAD/MLI hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for resident small enterprises under the Luxembourg franchise regime; no registration threshold for non-resident taxable persons supplying goods or services taxable in Luxembourg; EUR 10,000 Union distance-sales/OES threshold for intra-community distance selling and certain services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for VAT applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third party to a destination outside the European Union. Evidence must prove the goods physically left the EU within stated deadlines. Certain services related to exported goods (e.g., transport, ancillary services) and services directly tied to non-EU tangible movable property qualify for zero-rating where the recipient is established outside the EU or the service is effectively used wholly outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ENS single administrative document or electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice showing seller, buyer, description, export nature and terms
  • Packing list
  • Proof of arrival / foreign customs clearance at destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-based proof of export payment received from non-resident counterparty

Luxembourg does not currently mandate real-time VAT clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for domestic B2B invoices. E-invoicing is legally accepted if authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are ensured. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies through the PEPPOL network and associated Luxembourg public-procurement portals. No fiscal API clearance or pre-clearance is imposed before invoice issuance.

Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to immediate VAT clawback at 17% plus late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) and administrative penalties. Tax fraud or wrongful intent may trigger criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment under Articles 62 and 63 of the Luxembourg VAT Law. The AEDT may extend the ordinary reassessment limitation period from five years up to ten years where fraud or serious infringement is established; retrospective clawbacks may cover the full VAT amount, and indirect liability may extend to directors or fiscal representatives involved in improper export substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Luxembourg applies Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital) and the OECD Capital Movements Code; current-account and capital-account operations are unrestricted. EUR receipts and foreign-currency balances may be freely converted, held in any currency, or repatriated without regulatory approval. Profit and dividend remittances face no FX conversion hurdle; the only frictions are fiscal and administrative: (i) the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax, reduced to 0% for EU corporate shareholders meeting the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions and further reduced or exempted under applicable double tax treaties; (ii) BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for each cross-border financial transaction of EUR 12,500 or more; and (iii) the obligation to demonstrate genuine economic substance in Luxembourg to defend corporate tax residence and anti-abuse positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export collections may be retained indefinitely in any currency in any EU-authorized credit institution. The sole compliance overlay is the monthly BCL balance-of-payments statistics return: transactions with a given non-resident counterparty of at least EUR 12,500 must be declared under the goods-export category (BPM6 line 1.A.B.1), and supporting SEPA/SWIFT advices and Amazon Pay/Stripe settlement reports must be retained for five years under the AML law of 12 November 2004, as amended.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the Eurosystem national central bank for monetary policy implementation and balance-of-payments statistics, with prudential and AML/CFT supervision of banks and payment/EMI institutions carried out by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No bespoke foreign-exchange administration exists in Luxembourg; FX and capital-movement regulation is governed by EU law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium — conditional, secondary-rail only. Under PSD2 as transposed by the Luxembourg Law of 1 April 2019, CSSF-authorised and EEA-passported EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Revolut Business, Wise Business) may open corporate accounts with fully remote KYC. For this SARL, an EMI is legally viable as a low-limit collections and disbursements conduit for Amazon Pay/Stripe sweeps and supplier refunds, but it must not serve as the sole settlement bank because: (i) e-money float is not covered by the Luxembourg deposit guarantee fund (FGDL) and benefits only from safeguarding arrangements; (ii) credit facilities and documentary L/C capacity are unavailable or materially restricted; (iii) TARGET2 RTGS participation and high-value SWIFT MT103 processing are not offered; and (iv) BCL balance-of-payments reporting becomes fragmented across custodians, increasing compliance cost. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 Luxembourg bank for escrow, settlement, and trade finance; one EEA EMI for operational float, swept daily to the Tier-1 account via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer.

Annual accounts filing (dépôt des comptes annuels) and publication in RESA Within 7 months of the financial year-end; for a 31 December fiscal year end, the filing deadline is 31 July of the following year. $54 USD Failure to file allows the tribunal de commerce to order filing under a coercive periodic fine (astreinte); additional administrative penalties up to EUR 25,000 and individual criminal sanctions for directors may apply.
Combined annual corporate income tax (CIT), municipal business tax (MBT), and net wealth tax (NWT) declaration Due by 31 May of the year following the relevant tax year; a filing extension to 31 December is generally available upon electronic submission or prior request. $0 USD Late filing results in default interest at 0.8% per month on any unpaid tax, a late-filing penalty of up to EUR 1,500 per return, and possible official tax assessment by the ACD followed by enforced collection.
Annual beneficial-owner confirmation (confirmation RBE) Within one month after each anniversary of the initial RBE registration; any shareholder or UBO data change must additionally be notified within one month of the change. $0 USD Non-response to the annual confirmation triggers administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 and up to 6 months of imprisonment for intentional omission by directors or managers; repeat breaches may lead to RCS strike-off procedures.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), 2 Avenue John F. Kennedy, L-1855 Luxembourg; electronic filings at rcs.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), notably Articles 710-1 et seq. (Société à Responsabilité Limitée), Article 710-9 (minimum subscribed capital), and Article 710-12 (payment of cash contributions); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies and the Luxembourg Business Registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation — 'Acte constitutif' (Articles of Association) executed before a Luxembourg notary and filed electronically with the RCS, accompanied by the bank blocking certificate for the share capital and the statutory contributions auditor's report where in-kind contributions are made.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,100) minimum subscribed capital; capital must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked Luxembourg bank account in favor of the notary, with at least 25% (EUR 3,000) paid up at incorporation and the balance payable within five years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and supported by a report of a réviseur d'entreprises (approved statutory auditor), unless waived by unanimous partner resolution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: prior approval of the competent communal fire-prevention service (Service de prévention et d'extinction d'incendie) based on a fire-safety report covering fire-resistance classes of materials, emergency egress width, and extinguisher coverage. Structural load: signed attestation by a civil engineer validating floor-load capacity for embroidery machines, compacted storage racks, and ultrasonic equipment. Occupational: workplace clearance by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), confirming CE-marking of all embroidery and ultrasonic machines under the Law of 4 December 1974 on dangerous machinery, noise exposure not exceeding 87 dB(A) peak, dust control below 10 mg/m³ occupational exposure limit for textile fibers, and a compliant Document Unique de Sécurité.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,100) minimum subscribed capital; capital must be fully subscribed. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA – AEDT) for VAT; Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority (Administration des contributions directes – ACD) for corporate income tax; unified public portal: MyGuichet.lu (myguichet.lu)

E-government filing portal

For an LU SARL, incorporation is effected by notarial deed and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). Upon registration, the entity automatically receives a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) and is registered with the ACD for direct tax purposes. VAT registration with the AEDT is mandatory if the annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, if intra-Community acquisitions or distance sales are conducted, or if the entity engages in import/export activities; registration is also available on an optional basis to recover input VAT. Foreign entities establishing a branch or permanent establishment follow the same registration steps and may be required to appoint a local VAT agent or direct-tax representative. The resulting VAT ID takes the format LU followed by an eight-digit identifier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an LU SARL, incorporation is effected by notarial deed and registered with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR). Upon registration, the entity automatically receives a 13-digit national identification number (matricule) and is registered with the ACD for direct tax purposes. VAT registration with the AEDT is mandatory if the annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000, if intra-Community acquisitions or distance sales are conducted, or if the entity engages in import/export activities; registration is also available on an optional basis to recover input VAT. Foreign entities establishing a branch or permanent establishment follow the same registration steps and may be required to appoint a local VAT agent or direct-tax representative. The resulting VAT ID takes the format LU followed by an eight-digit identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Election to constitute a fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (LIR); option to form a VAT grouping as a single taxable person under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; waiver of the small-enterprise VAT exemption to apply standard VAT rules; application of the participation exemption regime for qualifying dividend and capital-gain income; and hybrid mismatch adjustments pursuant to the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) provisions as enacted in the LIR.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established small businesses under Article 7a of the Luxembourg VAT Law; no threshold applies to non-established foreign suppliers or to businesses engaged in intra-Community acquisitions, import operations, or export operations requiring VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the goods are physically dispatched from Luxembourg within 90 days of the supply and the supplier holds and preserves conclusive export evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods to EU business customers are also zero-rated where the customer supplies a valid EU VAT identification number, the goods are transported to another EU member state, and the transaction is correctly reported in the EC Sales List. Certain offshore B2B services (including services related to tangible goods exported outside the EU) are zero-rated based on place-of-supply rules under Article 14 of the Luxembourg VAT Law.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of export issued by the customs system (SAD export document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT payment evidence

Luxembourg does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or mandatory digital stamping regime for domestic B2B transactions. Electronic invoicing in standard formats is accepted for B2B purposes, and e-invoicing is mandatory for B2G contracts via the PEPPOL network. VAT returns, periodic recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists), and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu and the AEDT online systems, with a statutory retention period of six years for all invoicing and export records.

The AEDT may retroactively reassess VAT and claw back the zero rate where export documentation is absent, incomplete, false, or unsubstantiated. Unsupported zero-rated export declarations are treated as domestic taxable supplies, triggering additional VAT, interest at 0.6% per month, fines ranging from 10% to 200% of the tax due in cases of intentional misdeclaration, and possible criminal sanctions for tax fraud under Article 109 of the Luxembourg General Tax Law. Exporters must retain all proof of export and payment evidence for at least six years. Failure to document FX repatriation within the 180-day benchmark may, in substance-over-form review, lead to denial of the zero-rating benefit and imposition of clawback liabilities together with reputational and audit escalation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the domestic currency. Luxembourg applies the EU free movement of capital regime under Article 63 TFEU, providing full current-account and capital-account convertibility without central bank exchange-control approval. Profit remittance from the SARL is unrestricted from a foreign-exchange perspective and is subject only to standard corporate law distribution formalities, tax compliance, and any treaty-based or domestic withholding tax on dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply in Luxembourg or the euro area. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in euros or any foreign currency. Resident exporters are only subject to statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to the BCL for external transactions, and these do not create conversion or repatriation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent central banking, banking supervision, and anti-money laundering supervisory authority in Luxembourg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary collections of platform payouts and low-value receivables; Low for use as the SARL's primary treasury, trade settlement, or capital escrow rail. Legal constraints: EU/EEA electronic money institutions and payment institutions may passport into Luxembourg subject to CSSF notification and AML supervision, but they are not credit institutions and cannot hold commercial deposits, issue documentary letters of credit, or access central bank liquidity. Offshore and non-EEA digital EMIs have no passporting rights into Luxembourg and face significant correspondent-banking restrictions, making them unsuitable for the long-term cross-border treasury needs of a manufacturing SARL.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For euro-denominated shareholder capital contributions, use a TARGET2 or SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) with the remittance reference reflecting the corporate purpose and the SARL's registration number. For non-euro injections, use a SWIFT MT103 wire with the applicable BCL balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment and full bank routing data. Verification by the receiving Luxembourg bank will require the updated RCS extract, UBO declaration, notarised capital-contribution deed, shareholder resolution, and source-of-funds documentation before crediting the capital account.For standard supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with full beneficiary bank routing, remittance details, and the underlying purchase-order reference. For high-value or risk-sensitive trade settlements, use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT710 with the Luxembourg bank as advising or confirming bank.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties, and excise. Official e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu (centralized administrative portal); VAT and corporate tax returns are submitted via the ACD's eTVA module and MyGuichet.lu with national identification number authentication.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL obtains its Luxembourg tax identity as follows: (1) Execute deed of incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and deposit statutory capital (minimum EUR 12,000; EUR 12,000 paid-in for SARL); (2) Notary registers the company with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) within one month, obtaining the RCS registration number (B number) and the national identification number; (3) The RCS automatically transmits registration data to the ACD and AEDT, which issue the direct tax number and the VAT number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) For direct VAT registration by a non-resident entity without RCS registration, file form 5200 with the AEDT via MyGuichel.lu, appoint a fiscal representative if no permanent establishment and no EU-established person, and provide articles of association, proof of foreign registration, and intended activity description; (5) Apply for a separate intra-Community operator status (in-scope of the same VAT number) by declaring prospective intra-EEA distance sales and services to the AEDT; the same VAT number serves for both domestic and intra-Community purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SARL obtains its Luxembourg tax identity as follows: (1) Execute deed of incorporation before a Luxembourg notary and deposit statutory capital (minimum EUR 12,000; EUR 12,000 paid-in for SARL); (2) Notary registers the company with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) within one month, obtaining the RCS registration number (B number) and the national identification number; (3) The RCS automatically transmits registration data to the ACD and AEDT, which issue the direct tax number and the VAT number in the format LU + 8 digits; (4) For direct VAT registration by a non-resident entity without RCS registration, file form 5200 with the AEDT via MyGuichel.lu, appoint a fiscal representative if no permanent establishment and no EU-established person, and provide articles of association, proof of foreign registration, and intended activity description; (5) Apply for a separate intra-Community operator status (in-scope of the same VAT number) by declaring prospective intra-EEA distance sales and services to the AEDT; the same VAT number serves for both domestic and intra-Community purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply the check-the-box regime; the SARL is a taxable entity subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available elective regimes include: (a) group integration under Article 164bis L.I.R. (fiscal unity for 95% or more of share capital and voting rights); (b) VAT grouping election (assujettis uniques) under Article 62 of the VAT law allowing a single taxable person for related entities; (c) the IP box regime (80% exemption and 0% effective tax on qualifying IP income) requires an election and R&D nexus tracking; (d) participation exemption (parent-subsidiary) election for substantial shareholdings (>=10% or EUR 1.2M acquisition cost, held >=12 months); (e) advance tax agreement (tax ruling) procedure before the ACD for economic substance, transfer pricing, and hybrid mismatch treatment; ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply and no unilateral hybrid election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual business turnover for locally established taxable persons (franchise exemption under Article 57 VAT law); no mandatory registration threshold below EUR 35,000 if the business is exclusively outside the scope of VAT. Non-resident taxable persons making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register immediately from the first euro. For intra-Community distance sales and cross-border digital B2C services, the EU distance sales threshold (EUR 10,000) applies, but the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme must be used for OSS-registered businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods dispatched to a destination outside the EU: zero-rating under Article 56 of the Luxembourg VAT law applies if physical removal from Luxembourg/EEA is evidenced by transport documents and the goods leave the EU within 90 days of dispatch. For software/AI video services: B2B exports to customers outside the EU are outside the territorial scope of Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rule where the customer is established; no zero-rating claim is required but the supplier must obtain the foreign customer's B2B evidence and business status. B2C digital services sold to non-EU consumers are treated as performed outside the EU and therefore outside Luxembourg VAT. For intra-EU B2B SaaS supplies, zero-rating is replaced by reverse charge with a valid EU VAT number and by submitting a recapitulative statement; failure to satisfy the documentary threshold converts the supply to a domestic taxable transaction at 16%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX1 or customs-stamped export certificate)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical escape from the EU
  • Commercial invoice (with reference to the 0% rate and place-of-supply rule)
  • Proof of payment: inward remittance certificate (SWIFT or bank confirmation), together with contract and the foreign customer's VAT registration/identification evidence
  • For digital SaaS exports: signed client agreement, customer self-declaration of non-EU establishment, and technical license/hosting/IP transfer documentation showing the place of beneficial usage
  • Intra-EU supplies: recapitulative statement (ESL) with destination customer VAT numbers and evidence of reverse-charge notification

As of the effective date, Luxembourg does not mandate universal B2B e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance. Mandatory e-invoicing applies solely to public-sector invoices under the EN 16931 / PEPPOL format (EU Directive 2014/55/EU). For AI avatar SaaS cross-border sales, the taxpayer must issue technically compliant electronic invoices in EU standard format for intra-EU B2B supplies (reverse charge), submit recapitulative statements (deferred until the 10th of the following month) via eTVA/MyGuichet.lu, and retain real-time transaction log access for audit. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) framework will require standardized e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting for intra-EU B2B supplies by 2030 (targeted 2027-2028); actionable compliance readiness is required on that horizon.

The AEDT conducts documentary audits, clawing back erroneously zero-rated output VAT (16% plus late-payment interest at monthly statutory rates, currently 0.8% per month) if export evidence is absent or deficient. Negligent or fraudulent claims attract a pecuniary fine of EUR 250 to EUR 10,000 per offense, a fine representing up to three times the evaded tax, and criminal liability (imprisonment up to five years) under Articles 396 et seq. of the General Tax Law and Article 65 of the VAT law. For SaaS/digital exports, a retroactive reclassification of zero-rated B2C sales to the Luxembourg or recipient-state rate may also trigger OSS obligations and additional penalties; the clawback period extends five years from the end of the taxable year in ordinary cases, and ten years in cases of proven fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full currency convertibility under EU free movement of capital rules (TFEU Article 63). There is no legal distinction between current and capital account transactions for exchange control purposes. Profit remittances and distributions may flow freely to resident and non-resident shareholders, subject only to corporate law solvency tests, applicable dividend withholding taxes, and standard AML/KYC verification by the paying bank. No prior exchange-control authorization is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or requirement to repatriate export proceeds into EUR. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in Luxembourg bank accounts. Cross-border payments and external positions remain subject solely to BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting as requested by the reporting bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (Central Bank of Luxembourg, BCL), acting in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and conduct-of-business matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for offshore digital EMI accounts; Medium for EU-licensed electronic money institutions passporting into Luxembourg under the Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2). Offshore EMIs without a Luxembourg or EU licence are not acceptable treasury counterparties for capital escrow, account freezing certificates, or corporate settlement accounts. Payment facilitation via Stripe and Paddle is commercially viable for card and online collection, but the SARL's primary treasury and reserved capital accounts must be held at a licensed bank in Luxembourg or an EU/EEA credit institution passporting into Luxembourg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial SARL capital, the subscribing shareholder must transfer cash in EUR to the Luxembourg bank's capital escrow or ordinary blocked account before or immediately after notarisation of the articles of association. Use SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA inbound wires or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-origin payments. The payment purpose and bank reference must clearly state 'Opération en capital / Capital contribution' and the SARL corporate name and registered corporate number. The receiving bank applies the BCL Balance of Payments functional classification 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital.' Verification evidence includes the notarial deed of incorporation, capital subscription form, bank account blocking certificate, shareholder payment instruction from the originating bank, KYC documentation for the ultimate beneficial owner, and confirmation that the minimum statutory capital of €12,000 is fully paid and available free of encumbrance.For cross-border trade and SaaS operational disbursements, Luxembourg treasury operations should use SEPA Credit Transfers for EU suppliers and SWIFT MT103 for non-EU payments in foreign currency or where the receiving bank is outside the SEPA zone. Commercial Letters of Credit are appropriate only for physical goods scenarios; for software, royalty, licence, cloud-infrastructure, and subcontractor payments, standard telegraphic transfers with correct corporate purpose codes are the preferred rail. High-value EUR settlements are cleared through TARGET2, with SWIFT for message routing.

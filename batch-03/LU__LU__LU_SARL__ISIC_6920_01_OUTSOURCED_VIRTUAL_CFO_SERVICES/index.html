Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Ministry of Finance — Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de la TVA (Luxembourg VAT Authority) for indirect taxes; official filing and registration portals are myGuichet.lu, impotsdirects.public.lu, vat.public.lu, and the eCDF electronic VAT filing interface.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident establishing or registering a Luxembourg SARL obtains its statutory identifiers in a five-step sequence: (1) notarized incorporation and approval of the articles of association, followed by registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés — RCS), which allocates the RCS registration number; (2) filing of the legal publication formalities and deposit of the annual accounts; (3) registration with the social security authorities (Centre Commun de la Sécurité Sociale — CCSS), producing the employer identification number; (4) application to the ACD for the Luxembourg national tax number (numéro d'identification national / matricule) used for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; and (5) application to the Administration de la TVA for the Luxembourg VAT identification number (prefix LU + 8 digits), which is mandatory whenever taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or, irrespective of threshold, when taxable supplies are made in Luxembourg. Non-established foreign operators supplying services exclusively to Luxembourg taxable persons remain outside the Luxembourg VAT net under the reverse-charge mechanism; a foreign entity making domestic taxable supplies in Luxembourg must appoint a fiscal representative if it is established in a third country that has no mutual-assistance agreement with Luxembourg. All registrations and filings are executed electronically through myGuichet.lu and eCDF using authenticated e-Citizen or professional e-identity credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg is a codified civil-law jurisdiction and does not offer any 'check-the-box' pass-through election. The SARL is mandatorily a resident corporate taxpayer subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income, with territorial relief available for certain foreign branch income and gains. Available opt-in regimes include: (i) the participation exemption for qualifying dividend income and capital gains under Article 166 LIR, subject to an advance ruling application; (ii) the tax consolidation election (intégration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax purposes between a Luxembourg parent and its directly or indirectly 95%-owned resident subsidiaries; (iii) the VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') allowing financially and legally connected Luxembourg taxable persons to be treated as a single taxable person; (iv) advance pricing agreements and unilateral tax rulings on transfer pricing, substance, and hybrid-mismatch neutrality; and (v) anti-hybrid and ATAD-driven classification results, which are mandatory and not elective. Pass-through classification of a SARL is legally unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual taxable turnover exclusive of VAT; below this threshold the small-business franchise exemption may apply, while mandatory VAT registration is triggered once the threshold is exceeded; non-established suppliers remain registerable irrespective of turnover when making a taxable supply in Luxembourg, subject to reverse-charge relief for B2B supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: the export exemption (0% VAT) under the Luxembourg VAT law applies only where (a) goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting in their name; (b) the goods physically leave the EU, generally within 90 days of dispatch (extendable to 12 months for approved movements); and (c) the exporter holds the customs export declaration validated by Luxembourg customs together with transport documents proving EU exit. For services: zero-rating is achieved through the place-of-supply rule — B2B services supplied to a taxable customer established outside the EU are deemed supplied outside Luxembourg and therefore remain outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT; the supplier must evidence the customer's non-EU business establishment through a valid foreign VAT identification number or equivalent business-register proof, the service contract, itemized professional invoices, and the bank settlement trail. Where the customer is a non-taxable person outside the EU, professional consulting and accounting services are generally taxed in Luxembourg unless a specific derogation or use-and-enjoyment rule applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration — fully validated electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD/EAD) accepted by the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration
  • Transport evidence — original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note showing dispatch from Luxembourg and physical exit of the goods from the EU within the statutory period
  • Commercial documentation — signed sales contract, pro forma or commercial invoice, packing list, and stated Incoterms
  • Non-EU customer status evidence — foreign VAT registration number or official extract from the customer's business register, required for zero-rating of services
  • Inward remittance certificate and corresponding bank statements evidencing receipt of the export consideration from the foreign buyer within the repatriation deadline

There is no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based pre-clearance regime in force for B2B invoices in Luxembourg. Electronic invoicing between private businesses remains optional; where used, e-invoices must comply with the European standard EN 16931 and applicable EU e-invoicing rules. B2G e-invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities is mandatory via the Peppol network. All taxable persons must issue VAT-compliant invoices, retain them in their original format for at least ten years, and transmit the underlying turnover data through the periodic VAT return, the recapitulative statement, and Intrastat/introduction declarations, all filed electronically via the eCDF interface and myGuichet.lu.

Luxembourg's VAT and direct tax authorities audit zero-rated export claims under a general five-year assessment limitation period, extended to ten years where fraud or willful omission is established. If the 0% rate is applied without complete documentary substantiation, the supply is recharacterized as taxable in Luxembourg, VAT is reassessed at the standard rate, default interest accrues from the due date, and administrative fines are imposed for each infringement. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating — including fictitious exports, falsified transport documents, or sham non-EU customer structures — constitutes criminal tax fraud, exposing the legal representatives and any appointed jointly and severally liable fiscal representative to criminal fines and imprisonment, retrospective clawback of VAT credits, and professional disqualification, in addition to the reassessed VAT and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a Eurozone member in which both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible by virtue of EU free movement of capital (art. 63 TFEU). The Euro has no purchase or sale restrictions; profit remittances from a SARL are subject only to the 15% statutory dividend withholding tax (reduced by tax treaty or parent-subsidiary exemption) and to underlying substance requirements. Neither central bank notification nor exchange control approval is required for bona fide remittances. Non-resident shareholders and lenders may convert and repatriate funds freely, but the BCL collects balance-of-payments statistical data on cross-border flows exceeding EUR 100,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export proceeds surrender or conversion duty; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% and there is no statutory deadline for repatriation or conversion of export receipts. Exporters must, however, support BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting for flows above EUR 100,000 and retain export invoices, bank statements and SWIFT confirmations for the general 5-year documentation period under the Luxembourg AML/CFT law of 12 November 2004.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) (Central Bank of Luxembourg)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium for operational treasury flows, Low for statutory capital receipt. A non-Luxembourg EMI such as Wise may passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 to provide transaction accounts, but it cannot deliver the credit-institution block-attestation required by Luxembourg notaries to verify full payment of the SARL's minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 into an account opened in the company's name. An EMI may be used for ancillary collection and disbursement purposes provided its safeguarding account is held with an EU credit institution, but it must not be the sole treasury rail for the regulated accounting and tax service activities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City combined effective rate, comprising 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and 6.75% municipal business tax; the aggregate may vary marginally across communes)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (standard Luxembourg withholding tax on distributed profits to non-resident and resident shareholders); reduced to 0% for qualifying EU parent-subsidiary directive distributions and for substantial participation relief where the parent holds >=10% or an exemption certificate is granted; treaty rates commonly 0%, 5% or 15% depending on the jurisdiction

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

31 March of the year following the fiscal year-end for paper returns; the tax administration usually grants an automatic extension for e-filed returns, commonly to 30 June, with further extensions available upon reasoned request

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 (country-by-country reporting threshold for multinational groups); Luxembourg does not impose a separate statutory Local File / Master File threshold, but the tax administration may require contemporaneous OECD-compliant TP documentation whenever related-party transactions materially affect the taxable base; in practice, prepare a Local File when annual controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 or when management/administrative service fees are invoiced intercompany

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean-ESR style, but robust economic substance is a decisive condition for treaty benefits, participation exemption, IP box access, and avoidance of GAAR/ATAD recharacterization; the SARL must evidence real office, autonomous board decisions, qualified staff, transactional risk control, client-facing operational functions, and local bank accounts to prevent classification as a letterbox vehicle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the full taxable base, monthly late interest, 10% late-payment surcharge, administrative fines up to EUR 250,000, and criminal exposure to imprisonment from one month to five years under the Luxembourg General Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns incur joint civil liability for the recovered corporate tax; the corporate veil may be pierced, allowing seizure of personal assets, travel bans pending criminal investigation, and disqualification from exercising corporate board functions.
Letterbox / Artificial Conduit Company

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR/ATAD and the Luxembourg abuse-of-law doctrine; tax authority recharacterizes the payments as taxable Luxembourg-source income, denies treaty relief and participation exemption, reassesses up to six years, and imposes a surcharge of up to 50% of the corrected tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately maintain a letterbox structure can be ordered by a Luxembourg court to compensate the tax administration personally for lost revenue; travel restrictions and passport surrender are available in serious audit and criminal proceedings.
AML / CDD Failures and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Luxembourg AML law of 12 November 2004, as amended, ranging up to EUR 250,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; laundering proceeds attracts criminal fines and imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML breaches are personally and criminally liable; courts may impose confiscation orders on personal assets, prohibit leaving Luxembourg, and bar the director from practicing as a regulated accountant or CFO advisor.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to taxable income at the arm's-length price, payment of the tax shortfall plus late interest, administrative penalty of 10% of the adjustment where documentation is absent, and potential requalification of loans into equity under thin-capitalization principles.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve demonstrably aberrant pricing without economic analysis are liable for the additional tax and interest if they fail to act as prudent persons; serious noncompliance combined with personal benefit may lead to civil restitution, travel bans, and criminal sanctions for deliberate tax evasion.

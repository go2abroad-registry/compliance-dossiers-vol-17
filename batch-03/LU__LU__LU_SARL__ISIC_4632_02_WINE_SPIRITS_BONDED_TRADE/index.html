Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate taxes and the national TIN; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT registration, collection, and audits; Administration des douanes et accises (Customs & Excise Agency) for excise duty, bonded warehouse licensing, and export customs control. Principal portals: myguichet.lu and the professional taxpayer portal eCDF (Espace professionnel des professionnels).

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL: (1) execute the incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and file it with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); the RCS commercial registration number becomes the national taxpayer identification number (TIN) automatically aligned with the ACD. (2) The VAT identification number (format LU12345678) is obtained separately from the AED by filing the standard VAT registration via the eCDF portal or myguichet.lu; resident taxable persons register from their first taxable supply and no economic threshold applies to a SARL holding a bonded trade licence. (3) For wine/spirits bonded trade, register as tax warehousekeeper with the Administration des douanes et accises, obtaining an excise ID and permission to operate an entrepôt fiscal. (4) Foreign non-established entities must appoint a Luxembourg-established VAT fiscal representative and apply using the non-resident registration form; EU-established foreign entities may alternatively report distance sales under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS), but any supply involving a local excise warehouse requires a full Luxembourg VAT and excise registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Luxembourg SARL is an opaque, full-resident company subject to corporate income tax (IRC at 17%), the unemployment fund surcharge (7% of IRC), and municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City, yielding a combined effective headline rate of approximately 24.94%), plus net wealth tax (0.5%) on net assets. Territorial scope is worldwide taxation for residents; the participation exemption may exempt qualifying dividends and capital gains. The customs/excise bonded warehouse suspension does not confer income tax exemption.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections for a SARL include: (i) fiscal unity / tax consolidation under Section 164bis of the Income Tax Law (LIR) for corporate income tax, requiring at least 95% EU-resident shareholding; (ii) VAT grouping election under Article 61 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, treating the SARL and its qualifying Luxembourg affiliates as a single taxable person; (iii) neutrality elections regarding hybrid mismatch treatment under the ATAD provisions (Articles 168sexies through 168novodecies LIR), requiring alignment of the Luxembourg entity classification with the foreign jurisdiction to eliminate deduction/non-inclusion outcomes; (iv) no transparency/opacity election is available to SARLs (such elections exist only for partnerships such as SCSp/SCS).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for resident taxable persons; a Luxembourg SARL is mandatorily VAT-registered from the first taxable transaction. Non-established EU sellers have a EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold before mandatory Luxembourg registration under the OSS regime; intra-Community acquisitions become taxable in Luxembourg above EUR 10,000 per calendar year. A SARL operating a bonded wine/spirits wholesale trade is compulsorily registered because it holds an excise and customs warehouse licence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is granted where the SARL proves physical export of the goods out of the EU customs territory to a non-EU destination, normally evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documentation showing dispatch within a reasonable period (usually up to three months). Supplies of goods to another EU Member State are zero-rated when the customer provides a valid VAT identification number and the SARL complies with EC Sales List and Intrastat obligations. White wine and spirits transferred under duty suspension from the Luxembourg entrepôt fiscal to another EU tax warehouse are exempt from excise and outside the VAT charge; when subsequently exported from bonded storage outside the EU, both excise exemption and VAT 0% are claimed, with the SARL retaining the warehouse removal register, the export copy of the e-AD, and proof of physical exit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Export Declaration via the EU Automated Export System - AES)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT confirmation or equivalent proof of foreign settlement)
  • EMCS Electronic Administrative Document (e-AD) with Administrative Reference Code for excise-suspended alcohol movements
  • Bonded warehouse removal register and gauging/weighing record for wine and spirits release

Luxembourg does not impose national real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (as of 2025). B2B e-invoicing is optional and PEPPOL-based invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions under public procurement. Invoices must comply with Articles 108-110 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; XML/e-invoices with qualified electronic signatures are accepted. For duty-suspended wine and spirits, mandatory fiscal control is performed through the EU Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS), requiring digital Electronic Administrative Documents (e-ADs) bearing an Administrative Reference Code (ARC) submitted via the customs 'e-douanes' portal/API. Bonded warehouse stock records must be maintained digitally; release of alcoholic products into free circulation may require digital fiscal stamps/banderoles as instructed by the Customs & Excise Agency.

The AED and the Customs & Excise Agency perform risk-based export audits reconciling VAT returns, EMCS stock registers, customs export declarations, and bank settlement flows. If zero-rating is disallowed, the VAT is retrospectively clawed back with statutory late-payment interest and procedural fines within a general reassessment window of 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraud or wilful misdeclaration. False export declarations additionally attract criminal tax penalties (imprisonment up to 5 years and monetary fines proportional to tax evaded), customs fraud sanctions under the national Customs Code, and suspension or revocation of the bonded warehouse authorisation and any Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) certification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible in Luxembourg and across the euro area under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). There is a unitary regime for current-account and capital-account transactions with no general exchange control approval, no restriction on profit repatriation, and no obligation to obtain central bank clearance for dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, equity redemption, or liquidation proceeds. The only meaningful frictions are (i) compliance with EU and UN financial sanctions, (ii) application of Luxembourg fair-tax and EU anti-abuse rules (ATAD / DAC-6), (iii) mandatory AML/CTF due diligence, and (iv) statutory withholding taxes on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties where a tax treaty or EU Parent‐Subsidiary Directive benefit does not apply. Corporate treasury operations may freely hold, convert, remit, and hedge EUR and foreign currencies, including USD, GBP, and CHF, without Ministry of Finance or BCL approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds, whether in EUR or foreign currency. Under the euro legal framework and Luxembourg commercial law, export receipts may be retained onshore or offshore in any designated currency, and may be immediately used for working capital, supplier settlement, loan repayment, or treasury investment without central bank reporting except for statistical balance-of-payments purposes. However, for bonded wine and spirituous beverages warehoused under excise-duty suspension, the debtor-creditor relationship under the customs/excise regime requires clear documentary identification of each movement; export proceeds should be traceable to the corresponding e-AD (electronic Administrative Document) and customs declaration to support VAT and excise discharge within the timelines set by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) and the Customs and Excise Agency.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as national central bank of the Eurosystem, in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for licensing and prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment services; monetary policy and foreign exchange interventions fall under the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-High for payment-only routing, but Low for primary treasury concentration and Medium-Risk enhanced-due-diligence-bounded operations. Luxembourg-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the CSSF and EU-passporting EMIs (under the revised Electronic Money Directive 2009/110/EC) may legally open multi-currency FX accounts and process retail or trade payments without a traditional full banking license. However, for a bonded wine and spirits wholesale SARL with ISIC 4632, the corporate account should be held at a fully-licensed credit institution for mainstream trade settlement, excise transactions, letters of credit, and documentary collections. An EMI may serve only as a secondary disbursement channel, subject to the following legal constraints: (i) no deposit-taking beyond e-money float limits, (ii) no commercial lending or L/C advising without a banking partner, (iii) EMI providers must comply with Luxembourg AML/CFT law and CSSF Regulation 12-02 which may put stricter risk appetite on cash-intensive or excise-sensitive sectors, and (iv) the SARL must maintain nexus with a supervised ACPR/CSSF entity. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are generally not viable for Luxembourg-incorporated operating entities due to the lack of passporting rights, regulatory uncertainty, and bank-intermediary acceptance issues.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital injection to the Luxembourg SARL must be routed through a fully licensed credit institution using SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (correspondent bank transfer) with a precise purpose-code reference. The recommended routing is: (a) direct EUR transfer from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's account at a Luxembourg bank via TARGET2/SEPA (IBAN-LU), with external reference 'CAP_INCREASE' and clear field 70 (Remittance Information): 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION – CAPITAL INCREASE – DIRECT INVESTMENT – BOP CODE 401'. For non-EUR injections (USD/GBP), use SWIFT MT103 with field 72 mention 'CROSS-BORDER DIRECT INVESTMENT', and ensure that the underlying corporate deed (notarial resolution) is lodged with the Luxembourg trade and companies register (RCS) before value date. The bank will require the board resolution, updated shareholder register, SARL bylaws, and a certificate of the BCL statistical reporting if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 (intra-EEA) or any amount outside the EEA for balance-of-payments statistics. No exchange-control approval is required; the only threshold is the bank’s internal KYC/AML risk confirmation.Outbound disbursements for the purchase of bonded Scotch and Bordeaux wines and spirits shall be settled through the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic wire transfers to non-EEA suppliers, with remittance detail referencing invoice number and the customs/excise e-AD; (2) TARGET2 and SEPA (EBA CLEARING EURO1/STEP2 or CSM) for intra-EEA creditor payments in EUR, mandatory when both parties maintain IBAN accounts; (3) documentary settlement via Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued by the Luxembourg relationship bank and advised through a correspondent bank at the supplier's location, especially for high-value consignments (e.g., > EUR 250,000) where a documentary examination protects against trade fraud; (4) for bonded transfers, use the European EMCS e-AD followed by a separate SWIFT settlement; and (5) for goods sold onward from the SARL's bonded warehouse to EU customers, the underlying excise duty is suspended under the e-AD procedure and the commercial invoice settlement is a normal MT103/SEPA transaction. Luxembourg-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the CSSF and EU-passporting EMIs (under the revised Electronic Money Directive 2009/110/EC) may legally open multi-currency FX accounts and process retail or trade payments without a traditional full banking license. However, for a bonded wine and spirits wholesale SARL with ISIC 4632, the corporate account should be held at a fully-licensed credit institution for mainstream trade settlement, excise transactions, letters of credit, and documentary collections. An EMI may serve only as a secondary disbursement channel, subject to the following legal constraints: (i) no deposit-taking beyond e-money float limits, (ii) no commercial lending or L/C advising without a banking partner, (iii) EMI providers must comply with Luxembourg AML/CFT law and CSSF Regulation 12-02 which may put stricter risk appetite on cash-intensive or excise-sensitive sectors, and (iv) the SARL must maintain nexus with a supervised ACPR/CSSF entity. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are generally not viable for Luxembourg-incorporated operating entities due to the lack of passporting rights, regulatory uncertainty, and bank-intermediary acceptance issues.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) for entities engaged in specific activities (e.g., financing, holding, IP); for trading companies, substance is assessed based on premises, employees, and decision-making in Luxembourg; failure to demonstrate substance may lead to reclassification as a shell company and denial of treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; additional 10% penalty on evaded tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential extradition
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, 10% penalty on re-assessed tax, and potential criminal charges for abuse of law

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for taxes due if they fail to ensure substance; civil penalties and potential criminal prosecution
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment

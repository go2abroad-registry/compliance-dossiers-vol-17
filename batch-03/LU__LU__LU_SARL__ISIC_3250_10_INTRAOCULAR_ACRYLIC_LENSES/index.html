Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office with no physical presence is not permissible for a Luxembourg SARL engaged in medical device manufacturing. Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 requires the registered office to be located at a real address in Luxembourg where the corporate records, statutory books and accounting documents are physically kept and where competent authorities may effect service. A domiciliation arrangement with a licensed Luxembourg corporate-services provider is allowed, provided the service provider holds the requisite professional authorization, the domiciliation agreement identifies the company and its managers, and the address is a genuine operational reception address. Nevertheless, for a production company under ISIC 3250 manufacturing intraocular acrylic lenses, the RCS and health inspectorate expect an actual operating manufacturing facility or distribution facility in Luxembourg; reliance solely on a domiciliation address would trigger adverse commercial-substance findings, tax residence challenges, and enhanced AML scrutiny.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, 14 rue Erasme, L-7270 Luxembourg-Hamm, operating the e-RCS electronic filing portal (www.rcsl.lu).

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales (Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended), Articles 710-1 to 710-25 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), together with the Loi modifiée du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés (Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must have a stated share capital of at least EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 1.00. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked bank account in the name of the company or the notary before the deed is signed; the bank confirmation of deposit is annexed to the deed. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor’s report. The capital cannot be reduced below the legal minimum and distributable reserves must be maintained as required by Article 710-2 of the Law of 1915.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety conformity certificate issued by the municipal fire service, including structural load inspection of the cleanroom and lathe-turning floors; occupational workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering machine guarding under Directive 2006/42/EC, ventilation, emergency exits, and chemical safety data sheets; occupational medicine programme with a licensed occupational physician before commencement of operations.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must have a stated share capital of at least EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 1.00. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked bank account in the name of the company or the notary before the deed is signed; the bank confirmation of deposit is annexed to the deed. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor’s report. The capital cannot be reduced below the legal minimum and distributable reserves must be maintained as required by Article 710-2 of the Law of 1915.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and indirect tax registration; unified online portal: guichet.lu / MyGuichet (https://guichet.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing economic activity or a permanent establishment in Luxembourg, the applicant must file the standard business registration form with the AED via MyGuichet, appoint a Luxembourg-resident VAT representative (responsable solidaire) for non-established taxable persons, and annex the notarised articles of incorporation, proof of identity of the legal representative, and documentation evidencing the intended taxable activity (e.g. supply of intraocular acrylic lenses into Luxembourg/EU markets). The AED issues the national matricule and a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix; the ACD subsequently assigns the corporate income tax reference upon filing the declaration of commencement of activity. Non-resident companies with no local establishment but making distance sales or imports into Luxembourg must register for VAT through the EU MOSS/non-established person procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) is mandatorily opaque and is fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICB) and net wealth tax (IChN). No hybrid mismatch election is available to a SARL as such; ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules (Art. 168ter LIR) are applied to neutralise mismatches on cross-border hybrid instruments or payments. The statutory election to be taxed as a corporate entity under Article 6 LIR is available only to fiscally transparent partnerships (SCS/SCSp), not to a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-enterprise exemption (Art. 57 Loi TVA). Non-established taxable persons supplying goods or services in Luxembourg are subject to mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable supply, without benefit of the EUR 35,000 threshold. EU-distance-sales thresholds apply as set out in Directive 2006/112/EC (EUR 10,000 cross-border intra-community threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: the 0% export rate is available for intraocular acrylic lenses physically transported outside the European Union within 3 months of supply, provided the exporter holds the customs SAD EXIT confirmation and appropriate transport evidence. Intra-Community dispatches to a customer with a valid EU VAT identification number are zero-rated provided the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) is filed. Services: 0% or reverse charge applies to cross-border B2B services where the place of supply is outside Luxembourg under Article 14 of the Loi TVA (e.g. contract manufacturing, toll conversion, logistical services, licensing of lens-related IP), evidenced by commercial contracts, proof of recipient's business status, and financial flows. All export zero-rating claims must be documented in a complete 'export file' kept at the disposal of the AED for a minimum of 10 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD form, Export/EX item with EXIT confirmation from the Luxembourg or EU customs system)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order (or pro forma invoice) describing the intraocular acrylic lenses, quantity, value and HS/SAD classification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer/payment proof evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-EU customer

Mandatory B2G electronic invoicing via the PEPPOL network applies to suppliers providing goods/services to Luxembourg public-sector contracting authorities. No general real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime is operational for B2B commercial invoices in Luxembourg as of 2025. Invoices must nonetheless satisfy EU authenticity and integrity verification rules, and must be retained for a minimum of 10 years for VAT audit purposes. The AED retains the right to require XML/e-reporting submissions in the context of specific investigations or for operators in certain regulated sectors.

Luxembourg tax practice subjects export zero-rating claims to strict documentary review by the AED. Missing, contradictory or falsified customs/transport evidence triggers retrospective VAT clawback (the standard-rate VAT becomes due at the time of supply), default interest accruing from the original due date, and administrative penalties of up to twice the VAT evaded. Fraudulent use of forged customs stamps or false export declarations constitutes criminal tax evasion (escroquerie en matière de TVA) punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and/or criminal fines up to EUR 25,000, in addition to complete exclusion of the taxpayer from future zero-rating schemes and debarment from customs simplified procedures. Companies failing the 180-day inward remittance substantiation may also face transfer pricing adjustments and corporate income tax penalties for unrepatriated export revenue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg franc is fully superseded by the euro; foreign-exchange controls are abolished. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible and unrestricted, with no requirement for central-bank approval for cross-border transfers. The only obligations are statistical: balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL, EMIR trade reporting for derivatives, and CRS/FATCA transparency. Profit remittance is frictionless from an FX standpoint; the sole domestic structural cost is a 15% withholding tax on dividends, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% qualifying holding for 12 months) or under an applicable double-tax treaty. No Luxembourg withholding tax applies to royalties paid to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Luxembourg imposes no statutory requirement for surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds. Export receipts in USD, CHF, CNY, or other convertible currencies may be retained freely in any jurisdiction. Compliance is limited to: (i) BCL balance-of-payments statistical declarations for cross-border transactions above the applicable reporting threshold; (ii) VAT/intrastat filing obligations for intra-EU dispatches; and (iii) documentation of the underlying trade transaction where payment flows exceed €10,000, as required by CSSF AML circulars.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with prudential supervision exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); Luxembourg's exchange-control powers operate within the EU Treaty framework (Article 63 TFEU, free movement of capital) and the Luxembourg AML/CTF law of 12 November 2004.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Luxembourg fintechs holding a CSSF payment-institution (PI) or e-money institution (EMI) licence under PSD2 (Law of 10 November 2009) may provide current-account-style holding, issuance, and SEPA/EUR clearing only. They cannot execute USD correspondent clearing, documentary letters of credit, or ISDA derivatives, and are therefore suitable only for small-value auxiliary treasury buckets. Offshore (non-EU) digital EMIs are not viable: PSD2 passporting does not permit payment services into Luxembourg without a local licence or a registered agent arrangement, and mainstream Luxembourg banks will not accept first-tier account relationships with a high-risk medical-device manufacturer channelled through an unlicensed offshore EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the LU_SARL's paid-in capital injection (minimum subscribed capital €12,000 under the Law of 23 June 2016, fully paid and deposited with the bank), the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 credit in EUR (or SEPA Credit Transfer where the parent is EU-domiciled). The purpose code must follow the BCL balance-of-payments taxonomy generically as 'C00 / Equity Capital Investment — Capital Contribution — Share Subscription'; the reference line must state 'LU_SARL CAPITAL INCREASE / SHARE PREMIUM / ESCROW — [NOTARY DEED REF].' The bank will require: the notarial or private deed of capital increase; resolution of the shareholders' meeting; proof of identity of the remitter and the UBOs; confirmation that funds originate from a regulated banking institution; and, where the injection exceeds large-transaction thresholds, an automated BCL statistical report and an internal source-of-funds statement. Proceeds must remain segregated in a capital escrow account until the RCS filing is completed and the register of beneficial owners is updated.All outbound trade settlement must be channelled through the relationship bank's cash-management and documentary trade desks. Standard rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for EUR and USD wires, with SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for domestic/EU payments; (ii) commercial letters of credit, both sight and usance, issued/advised through the bank's trade module and confirmed by a top-tier EU or US correspondent bank for non-EU buyers; (iii) standby letters of credit and tender guarantees for hospital procurement contracts; (iv) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for recurring distributor orders; (v) supply-chain finance and reverse factoring for inbound component purchases; (vi) USD settlement via the bank's New York correspondent (CHIPS/Fedwire) and CNY settlement via CIPS where applicable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Moderate. Luxembourg fintechs holding a CSSF payment-institution (PI) or e-money institution (EMI) licence under PSD2 (Law of 10 November 2009) may provide current-account-style holding, issuance, and SEPA/EUR clearing only. They cannot execute USD correspondent clearing, documentary letters of credit, or ISDA derivatives, and are therefore suitable only for small-value auxiliary treasury buckets. Offshore (non-EU) digital EMIs are not viable: PSD2 passporting does not permit payment services into Luxembourg without a local licence or a registered agent arrangement, and mainstream Luxembourg banks will not accept first-tier account relationships with a high-risk medical-device manufacturer channelled through an unlicensed offshore EMI.

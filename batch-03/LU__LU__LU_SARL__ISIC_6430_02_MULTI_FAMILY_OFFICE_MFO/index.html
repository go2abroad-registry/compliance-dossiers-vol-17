Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), 3, rue du Parc des Expositions, L-1113 Luxembourg; electronic filings through the e-RCS web portal.

Governing statute

Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 sur les sociétés commerciales (Luxembourg Companies Act of 10 August 1915, as amended, notably by the Law of 10 August 2016 on modernisation of company law) – SARL governed by Articles 710-1 to 710-22 (formation, minimum capital, gérance, shareholders' general meetings) and general formation provisions in Articles 1 to 3; Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector (Articles 24-28 applying to investment firms) governs the regulated multi-family office activity; Law of 2 September 2011 on access to professions governs the Autorisation d'établissement.

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif notarié (notarially executed deed of incorporation containing the French-language Articles of Association, 'Statuts') deposited with the RCS for immatriculation of the Luxembourg SARL.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 29 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 under Article 710-1 of the Companies Act of 10 August 1915. The SARL capital must be fully subscribed at formation; cash contributions must be released to at least 20% of the nominal value of the shares, although standard banking practice requires the full EUR 12,000 to be deposited into a blocked escrow current account ('compte bloqué') opened in the name of the company en formation at a Luxembourg credit institution. The bank's blocking certificate is remitted to the notary, and the funds are released to the company upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD - Luxembourg Inland Revenue) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for RCS company registration. Operational portals: myguichet.lu (e-RCS and eTVA electronic filing), guichet.lu (public administrative guide), and the ACD e-filing channel via mybusiness.lu.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SARL and register in the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via e-RCS on myguichet.lu; the automatically attributed national business registration number (numéro d'identification / matricule) serves as the corporate TIN for direct tax purposes. 2) Register for VAT with the AED (format LU + 8 digits) using the standard electronic FEL registration form; non-resident taxable persons performing taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AED from the first euro, while resident SARLs register for VAT upon incorporation if taxable activity is expected. 3) The ACD opens the corporate income tax file automatically for resident companies upon RCS registration; no separate direct tax registration is required for a Luxembourg-resident SARL.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the resident SARL is per se an opaque corporate taxpayer subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) on worldwide income and net wealth, with treaty and domestic relief for foreign-source income; no transparent or pass-through classification is available for a SARL under Luxembourg law.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box regime. Elective mechanisms include: (a) tax consolidation (fiscal unity / intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for a Luxembourg-resident group of companies at least 95% owned by a common parent; (b) election to treat a foreign permanent establishment as a Luxembourg PE for treaty-attribution purposes under Article 48 LIR with an optional consistency election under ATAD 2; (c) a hybrid-alignment election under the Law of 21 December 2020 implementing ATAD 2 to recharacterise hybrid entities/instruments and avoid double non-taxation; (d) the VAT small-business exemption election under Article 57 of the VAT Law for annual turnover below EUR 35,000 (once elected, generally binding for a period).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the domestic small-business exemption (Article 57 VAT Law); no registration threshold applies to non-resident suppliers performing taxable supplies in Luxembourg, who must register from the first euro unless the supply is subject to reverse charge with a Luxembourg VAT-registered recipient; intra-EU distance-selling and OSS thresholds follow EU rules (EUR 10,000 cross-border intra-EU annual threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies on the dispatch/transport of goods to a non-EU destination provided the exporter retains full customs export evidence (SAD/ECS exit confirmation); domestic supplies to an export vendor or freight forwarder for subsequent export are exempt under Article 151 of the VAT Law. Services: cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-Luxembourg taxable person are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (reverse charge at 0% in the customer's jurisdiction), while B2C services to non-EU customers qualify for zero-rating when the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU (Article 148 VAT Law). For an MFO SARL, discretionary portfolio advisory and management fees invoiced to a non-EU fund, family vehicle, or non-established investor will qualify for 0% where the place-of-supply rules locate the service abroad and the exporter holds adequate documentary proof of the customer's foreign establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS) with Movement Reference Number and customs exit confirmation
  • Transport document evidencing physical export: Bill of Lading (sea) or CMR/AWB (road/air)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying advisory or portfolio management contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of consideration from abroad
  • Proof of customer's non-Luxembourg establishment (foreign VAT registration certificate or official certificate of residence)

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing (EN 16931 / PEPPOL-compliant) for domestic B2B transactions enters into force on 1 January 2026 under the Luxembourg e-invoicing reform, with sanctions phased from 2027. No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping/API clearance regime is currently implemented; VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically (eTVA) on a monthly, quarterly, or annual cycle with aggregate declaration data; invoice-level transaction reporting to the tax administration is not yet mandatory except for intra-EU EC Sales Lists.

Luxembourg VAT law requires strict documentary substantiation for zero-rated exports; if the export bundle is incomplete, the AED will reclassify the supply as taxable at 16% and claw back the VAT together with statutory late-payment interest of 0.8% per month, a 10% late-payment surcharge, and administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 per offence. Fraudulent zero-rating schemes or contrived export arrangements constitute tax fraud punishable by imprisonment (8 days to 5 years) and criminal fines, with the normal assessment period extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful concealment; transfer-pricing and substance audits may further recharacterise long-outstanding unrepatriated export consideration as a deemed shareholder distribution or indirect capital contribution.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Luxembourg. Current-account items such as management fees, advisory fees, interest, salaries, custody fees, and dividend payments are freely payable. Capital-account items such as equity injections, shareholder loans, portfolio transfers, and direct investments require no BCL authorisation. Profit remittance is not foreign-exchange blocked; the practical hurdles are documentary and tax-related: banks require evidence of source of funds, economic substance, valid shareholder resolutions, tax residence certificates, and CSSF compliance. Dividend distributions may be subject to Luxembourg withholding tax at 15% domestic rate, 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when conditions are satisfied, or reduced under double-taxation treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion into euro, and no repatriation deadline. Export receipts, fee income, or advisory proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the market rate through a licensed credit institution. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the BCL may be required, and all cross-border transfers remain subject to standard AML/CFT controls.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); banking prudential and AML/KYC supervision: Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). There is no separate Luxembourg foreign-exchange control authority; EU capital-movement liberalisation under Articles 63-66 TFEU applies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for truly offshore digital EMIs; MEDIUM only for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions that validly passport into Luxembourg under PSD2 and the EU Electronic Money Directive. A non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully provide principal corporate treasury services to a Luxembourg SARL, and funds held with an unregulated offshore EMI will not satisfy the blocked-capital verification required by a Luxembourg notary for an SARL capital escrow. Any permitted EMI must be supervised in an EEA state, passported to Luxembourg, maintain safeguarding of client funds, settle through Target2 or SEPA, and submit to equivalent KYC/AML diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections must be routed through a Luxembourg or EU-regulated credit institution. For initial SARL capital, transfer funds to a blocked 'société en formation' account at a tier-1 Luxembourg bank; the bank issues a blocked-funds certificate to the notary; after notarial deed execution and RCS registration, funds are released to the operational account. For subsequent equity or shareholder-loan injections, use SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the EEA, with ISO 20022 structured remittance and a purpose description such as 'Share capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement'; where the receiving bank requires a Luxembourg balance-of-payments code, the bank will map the transaction to direct-investment capital categories. Verification package: notarially certified shareholders' resolutions; updated RCS and RBE filings; source-of-funds evidence; prior share-subscription statements; transfer-pricing documentation for interest-bearing shareholder loans; and no further BCL approval is required.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT gpi customer credit transfers via MT103, together with MT202 COV for interbank cover, for non-EEA payments in any convertible currency; SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for euro disbursements within the EEA; commercial letters of credit issued by SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP600, with MT707 amendments and MT752 reimbursement instructions; documentary collections using MT400/MT410 when no LC is required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (Luxembourg City; cumulative corporate income tax 17% + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19%, plus municipal business tax 6.75% on deductible base; rates vary by municipality)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% (domestic rate; reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when parent holds ≥10% for ≥12 months; treaty relief may reduce to 5% or 10% for direct shareholdings below 10%; a standard 0% is available for distributions to qualifying Luxembourg resident corporate shareholders)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year company, the return must be filed by 30 June of the following year; for off-calendar fiscal years, within 6 months after the fiscal year-end. Extensions (e.g., to 31 December) may be granted upon formal request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required where (a) the Luxembourg entity’s gross annual turnover exceeds EUR 10 million (Local File) and (b) the group’s consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million (Master File); the Luxembourg entity must also submit a CbC Report if the group’s consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million; document acquisition, financing, IP, and management services with OECD-aligned functional and risk analysis.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are applicable to Luxembourg entities performing core income-generating activities (including holding, financing, and intellectual property exploitation). A Multi-Family Office providing discretion portfolio advice must evidence: (i) local senior management and decision-making, (ii) adequate and proportioned staff, board meetings, and physical offices, (iii) active control of principal risks and core profit-generating assets; failure to substantiate economic substance exposes the entity to recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, and sanctions under EU/OFAC/ATAD rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 10,000 plus interest (0.5%/month); criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and disqualification from holding public office; the tax assessor can also reassess within 10 years in the case of fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to pay unpaid corporate taxes if they knowingly permit false filings; civil restitution of misappropriated assets, travel bans, and extradition risks arise under joint criminal proceedings.
Artificial Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as tax-transparent; denial of participation exemption, NID, and treaty relief; retroactive corporate income tax of 24.94% plus municipal business tax, 15% dividend WHT, penalties up to EUR 1,250 per infraction, and possible suspension of the business license for the regulated multi-family office activity.

Director personal liability: Directors face separate liability for penalties and interest; under the CSSF supervisory framework, failure to enforce substance may lead to withdrawal of the professional license and personal bans on holding a management position in a regulated entity.
AML/CTF Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative (CSSF) fine up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover, whichever higher; criminal fine up to EUR 250,000; imprisonment up to 5 years for willful money laundering; freezing of assets and revocation of the statutory license of the MFO.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for negligence; they may be prohibited from exercising roles in financial institutions; travel bans are imposed pending criminal investigation; civil liability for restitution of laundered funds is joint and several.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Tax authority recharacterizes the transaction, adds back excessive deductions, calculates additional CIT and MBT at standard rates, imposes 10% late-filing penalty and 0.5% monthly interest; penalties may be doubled if no documentation is produced; the company is additionally exposed to CbC reporting non-compliance penalties up to EUR 4,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm’s length agreements may be jointly liable for the shortfall in corporate taxes; courts may apply the 'alter ego' doctrine to pierce the veil if funds are drained to related parties without consideration, leading to personal asset seizure and restraint orders.

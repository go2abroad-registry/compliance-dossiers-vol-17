Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000 (Article 710-1, Companies Act 1915 as amended by the Law of 23 July 2016), fully subscribed by quotas of equal nominal value; at least 25% of the nominal value of each cash quota (aggregate minimum EUR 3,000 on the statutory minimum capital) must be paid up at formation by deposit into a blocked 'compte de consignation' with a Luxembourg credit institution, evidenced by an 'attestation de blocage'; the unpaid balance must be settled within five years of incorporation; contributions in kind require a valuation report by an independent réviseur d'entreprises; blocked funds are released only upon completion of RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct corporate and income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for Luxembourg VAT; Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu as the integrated corporate and tax registration portal.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL must first be incorporated before a notary and filed with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS). Upon RCS registration, the ACD automatically assigns a Luxembourg national tax identification number (NIF) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes. For VAT, the SARL, or any foreign taxable person making taxable supplies in Luxembourg, must file a VAT registration application with the AEDT, providing its RCS excerpt, articles of association, proof of Luxembourg business address, identity of directors/managers, and, where required, a Luxembourg VAT representative. The AEDT then issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format LU + 8 digits). Non-established foreign suppliers have no turnover threshold and must register for Luxembourg VAT before making their first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL is mandatorily classified as a fully taxable corporate entity subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Relevant elective mechanisms available include the Luxembourg VAT grouping regime (assujetti unique) for VAT consolidation, and the group taxation regime under Article 164bis LIR for resident companies holding at least 95% direct or indirect participations. Hybrid mismatch elections are not discretionary; Luxembourg implements the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules, and no substantive pass-through election is available to a SARL.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg, as a member of the European Union and the eurozone, maintains full capital-account and current-account convertibility in accordance with Articles 63 and 64 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are subject only to standard corporate withholding tax procedure (15% domestic rate reduced by tax treaties, participation exemption or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where applicable) and are not subject to exchange-control approval. The euro is fully convertible; no surrender requirements or central bank authorization are imposed on current or capital transactions for residents. Administrative formalities may include the completion of the CSSF/BCL balance-of-payments reporting (BOP) framework for movements exceeding thresholds, but these are statistical, not restrictive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Luxembourg. Air cargo charter brokering receivables denominated in foreign currency (e.g., USD) may be retained externally, converted at commercial market rates, or used directly for operational and settlement purposes. For statistical balance-of-payments reporting, resident companies must declare individual cross-border payments in euro and foreign currencies to the BCL under the CSSF/BCL BOP regulation (Regulation EEE 2408/92 and national RGD 27 May 1997), but no surrender or forced conversion to EUR applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB), with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the prudential supervisor for regulated financial activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium—viable only for settlement of client receivables/payables in currencies without unlicensed restrictions under a Luxembourg or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence (e.g., under PSD2). Legal constraints: (i) the EMI cannot grant credit or hold client funds longer than 180 days unless client elects deposit; (ii) corporate cash management limits mean EMIs usually cannot support large air-cargo charter broker fund flows without a sponsoring credit institution; (iii) CSSF treats EMIs as high-risk for collective investment-like or escrow flows, requiring robust AML/KYC and transaction monitoring; (iv) Fedwire membership is generally unavailable for EMIs, so USD-clearing requires correspondent bank sponsorship. Onboarding viability: Low for primary treasury accounts; Medium for ancillary payment processing or merchant settlement platforms.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 or Fedwire with unambiguous purpose codes and remittance information. For Luxembourg, use the EU Balance of Payments (BOP) codes: for direct investment equity — 11010 (equity capital); for shareholder loans — 12010 (loans from parent); for trade/miscellaneous operating payment — 10000 services. The receiving bank will require the underlying instrument (share subscription agreement, loan facility agreement) and the CSSF/BCL BOP declaration will be made automatically via the bank. State the full legal name and SARL 'LU' Vat number (LU + n° d'identification fiscale) in the remittance information. Wire format must include IBAN for EUR shipments; for USD, ensure the ABA routing number of the intermediate correspondent is provided. The transaction reference must include the charter invoice number and the statement 'AIR CARGO CHARTER BROKERAGE — OPERATIONAL FUNDING'.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for all EUR and USD outbound trade disbursements (charter payments, fuel, crew, insurance, landing fees); (ii) Commercial letters of credit (L/C) under UCP 600 for high-value sub-charter transactions requiring documentary comfort, especially when acting as intermediary between aircraft operator and shipper; (iii) SEPA CT and SEPA Instant for eurozone counterparties; (iv) Fedwire for same-day USD priority disbursements requiring immediate value; (v) Correspondent banking through one of the tier-1 banks' downstream correspondent network (e.g., BGL BNP Paribas Fortis US branch or JP Morgan/Citi).

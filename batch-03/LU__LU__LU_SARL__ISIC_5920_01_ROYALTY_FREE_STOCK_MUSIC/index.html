Applicable AML statute: The principal legal framework is: (i) the amended Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, as amended, governing the SARL; (ii) the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, implementing EU Directive (EU) 2015/849 and Directive (EU) 2018/843; (iii) the amended Law of 13 January 2019 establishing the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE), integrated into the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR); (iv) the Law of 19 December 2020 on financial sanctions; and (v) Grand-Ducal implementing regulations and CSSF AML/CFT circulars. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permitted only in the form of a professional domiciliation arrangement with a qualified corporate-service provider maintaining a physical Luxembourg address where legal notices can be served and the governing-body authorized representatives can be contacted. A mere post-office box or non-physical virtual address is not acceptable for RCS registration. For a fully digital royalty-free stock-music licensing marketplace, a professional domiciliation address is acceptable if complemented by real commercial substance: a Luxembourg bank or EMI account, board minutes taken in Luxembourg, contracting counterparty records, and a responsible management function effectively exercised in Luxembourg.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), Luxembourg — Service de la TVA (VAT registration, OSS filings and indirect tax audits), portal: MyGuichet.lu / eTVA (https://myguichet.lu, https://pfi.public.lu); direct tax competence: Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD), portal: https://impotsdirects.public.lu; customs competence: Administration des douanes et accises (https://douanes.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

Resident SARL: incorporation filing at the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via MyGuichet.lu triggers attribution of the 13-digit national business number (matricule), which serves as the national TIN and as the basis for the Luxembourg VAT identification number (LU + matricule). Non-resident foreign supplier: file the AEDT non-established taxable person VAT registration request (digital 'VAT identification' form published by the AEDT via the PFI/eTVA portal), attaching a certified copy of the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutive documents, proof of identity and authority of the legal representative, foreign VAT ID (for EU suppliers), NACE/ISIC description of the audio-licensing activity, evidence of any Luxembourg fixed establishment, and bank details; the AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT number. Digital-services suppliers may alternatively submit a Union/Non-Union OSS registration through the national OSS portal for B2C intra-EU electronic supplies. VAT registration is mandatory before issuing compliant invoices and claiming input VAT deduction under section 25 of the Luxembourg VAT Law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Luxembourg; the SARL is an opaque, fully taxable company subject to corporate income tax (IRC), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax. Available elections relevant to cross-border structures: (i) fiscal unity election (intégration fiscale) under section 164bis LIR for resident companies held directly or indirectly at not less than 95%; (ii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under section 25a of the VAT Law; (iii) IP box election under section 50bis LIR granting an 80% exemption on qualifying digital/audio royalty income; (iv) small-trader franchise option under section 28 VAT Law below the EUR 35,000 turnover threshold; (v) OSS/Union scheme election for distance sales and electronically supplied services; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules are applied statutorily under ATAD (section 168quater LIR) with no elective carve-out for hybrid instruments or payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (annual national taxable turnover for the small-trader franchise exemption, section 28 VAT Law); EUR 10,000 (Union-wide annual turnover of intra-EU distance sales and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons, above which destination-country VAT applies via OSS or local registration, section 58(2) VAT Law)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: dispatch of physical merchandise from Luxembourg to non-EU destinations qualifies for the 0% rate when the electronic export declaration (IE-599/SAD) is accepted by the Administration des douanes et accises and transport evidence is retained within the statutory 90-day proof window. Digital services (royalty-free stock-music licensing and subscriptions): supplies are classified as electronically supplied services (ESS). B2B supplies to customers established outside Luxembourg are zero-rated/outside Luxembourg VAT (place of supply at the customer's establishment; foreign customer VAT ID or residence certificate required). B2C supplies to consumers outside the EU are outside Luxembourg VAT. B2C supplies to EU consumers exceeding EUR 10,000 Union-wide are taxable in the destination Member State through the OSS rather than Luxembourg zero-rating; supplies below that threshold remain taxable at Luxembourg rates unless the destination-state option is elected.

  • Customs Export Declaration (IE-599 / Single Administrative Document) cleared by the Luxembourg Administration des douanes et accises
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch from Luxembourg to a non-EU country
  • Commercial invoice and executed licence/subscription agreement describing the royalty-free music exploitation rights and consideration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing settlement of the foreign sales proceeds
  • Foreign customer's valid VAT identification number or certificate of residence establishing B2B status for place-of-supply purposes
  • OSS quarterly returns and transaction-level digital records where B2C electronic supplies to EU consumers are reported

B2G: mandatory e-invoicing since 1 January 2024 for invoices addressed to Luxembourg public authorities (Peppol network, EN 16931, Factur-X/UBL format). B2B: e-invoicing remains voluntary; no national real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, QR code or daily electronic transaction transfer obligation applies to commercial audio-licensing invoices. Tax control is enforced through periodic e-filed VAT returns (monthly/quarterly/annual) on MyGuichet/eTVA, the AEDT's standard-audit-file inspection practice, and 10-year record-retention duties. EU ViDA real-time digital reporting and a general EN 16931 e-invoicing mandate are expected from approximately 2030.

The AEDT may retroactively review VAT positions for 5 years, extended to 10 years where concealment or fraud is established. Unsupported export zero-rating is clawed back by reassessment at the domestic Luxembourg VAT rate (currently 17%) together with monthly statutory late-payment interest and administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000 per taxpayer per infringement. Willful electronic-service VAT evasion is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and court-imposed fines; fraudulent OSS declarations can trigger joint liability for VAT of all destination Member States, exclusion from the OSS regime, and compulsory posting of security.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies no exchange controls. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittances are unrestricted under Luxembourg and EU capital movement rules, subject only to ordinary corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and, where applicable, withholding tax obligations. No prior administrative approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or royalty outbound payments, provided that AML/KYC reporting thresholds and BCL balance-of-payments statistical reporting are satisfied. The Luxembourg entity operates in EUR; settlement of USD or other foreign-currency licensing receipts is freely permitted and may be retained in foreign currency or converted without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds or foreign-currency receipts. The SARL may retain offshore or onshore funds in any currency, subject to corporate substance, transfer pricing, and controlled-foreign-corporation considerations arising under the tax law of the foreign revenue jurisdiction.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; prudential supervision of banks is shared with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Limited-Low as primary treasury rail; Medium-Only as a receivable collection channel. Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) proceeding under PSD2 and CSSF supervision may provide multicurrency virtual IBANs and low-cost cross-border collection, but they are not full credit institutions and cannot support capital escrow arrangements, commercial letters of credit, or material FX derivative negotiations. Unregulated foreign EMIs, including non-Luxembourg PSPs such as Stripe and PayPal, are acceptable only for marketplace collections, not as sole treasury counterparties; all major liquidity and hedging operations must be conducted through a BCL-supervised credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route initial share capital and subsequent capital increases by SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA remitter) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EEA remitter) using the SARL’s Luxembourg IBAN at the onboarding bank. The payment must include the beneficiary legal name exactly matching the KYC profile, the corporate registration number RCS, and a purpose code falling within BCL balance-of-payments nomenclature for direct investment/capital participation; acceptable descriptors include 'CAPT' or 'INVS' (capital injection/investment). The bank will require the notarized deed of incorporation or capital-increase resolution before accepting the wire. For amounts above €10,000 or any risk-sensitive jurisdiction, source-of-funds evidence must be pre-submitted. After settlement, verification is completed by reconciling the bank credit advice with the notarial certificate, registering the capital increase with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and updating the RBE register if ownership changed.Royalty and licensing disbursements from the SARL to foreign music copyright owners, distributors, or co-licensors settle primarily through SWIFT MT103 covered transfers, with field 72 purpose code reflecting 'ROYL' (royalty/license fee) or 'INTC' (intra-group services) where applicable. Within the eurozone, low-value disbursements (<€100,000) can use SEPA Instant Credit Transfer or standard SEPA CT; outside the EEA, a conventional SWIFT EUR/USD payment is mandatory. For high-value contractual trade settlements requiring documentary protection, a Commercial Letter of Credit (import/export LC) or documentary collection against presentation of licence acceptance certificates and delivery confirmations is the appropriate clearing rail. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate in Luxembourg City: 17% national corporate income tax, plus 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT, plus municipal business tax of 6.75%; municipal rate varies by commune.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on Luxembourg-source dividends. Rate is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder holds at least 10% and meets substance/anti-abuse conditions; applicable DTA often reduces the rate to 5% for substantial holdings and 15% for portfolio dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a tax year ending on 31 December, the return is due by 31 December of the following calendar year unless ACD notifies a different filing date or an extension is granted. E-filing is mandatory for companies; non-receipt of a pre-printed form does not discharge the filing obligation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are triggered for Luxembourg members of MNE groups with consolidated group turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000 under Article 56bis LIR and OECD TP documentation standards. Below this threshold, the SARL must still produce contemporaneous evidence of arm's-length pricing when intercompany transactions exist, and the Controlled Transactions Annex to Form 705 must be completed where applicable.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no separately named Economic Substance Regulations, but the same substance principle is enforced through tax residence rules, Article 56bis LIR, EU ATAD GAAR, Luxembourg administrative circulars, and treaty-beneficial-ownership requirements. The SARL must maintain a registered office, active director decisions, employees or subcontracting substance, a Luxembourg bank account, and commercial activity. A mailbox-only structure with no real management of music-licensing contracts will be disregarded and denied treaty and participation-exemption benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: ACD will apply reassessment of the full omitted income, late-payment interest, and administrative penalties. Criminal conviction under Luxembourg General Tax Law can lead to imprisonment of up to five years and substantial criminal fines commensurate with the evaded tax, in addition to full restitution.

Director personal liability: The director or manager who signs or directs the false return is the liable natural person. Criminal asset freezes, travel bans, and seizure of personal bank accounts may be ordered. In insolvency, the director may be ordered to contribute personally to the SARL's liabilities under Luxembourg company-law management-liability rules.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: ACD and tax courts may disregard the structure as abusive, deny the tax residence certificate, deny treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, reallocate income to the true owner or controlling shareholder, and treat previously exempt dividends and gains as fully taxable. Administrative penalties and default interest apply; the company may also be struck from the Luxembourg trade register for failure to maintain lawful corporate substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or maintain an artificial shell may be held civilly liable for resulting tax losses, subject to court-ordered restitution and asset freezes. In serious cases involving intentional abuse, criminal fines and travel restrictions can be imposed on the responsible individuals.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with RBE and AML/CFT filing obligations attracts criminal sanctions under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 and the RBE Law, including fines and imprisonment for natural persons. Administrative sanctions may be imposed separately on the SARL, and the authorities may enroll the company in a public non-compliance registry.

Director personal liability: The managing director, as the registered responsible officer, is personally exposed to criminal fines and imprisonment. Prosecutors may obtain freezing orders over the director's assets and prohibit departure from Luxembourg while an AML investigation is pending.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Abusive Royalty-Free Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Article 56bis LIR permits ACD to re-determine taxable income by applying the arm's-length principle and adjusting the SARL's profit upward. The adjustment may be accompanied by default interest, late-payment penalties, and, where the transaction is part of a fraudulent avoidance scheme, criminal tax penalties. Failure to produce TP documentation upon request is independently sanctionable.

Director personal liability: A director who approves biased intercompany transfer prices without supporting economic analysis can be jointly liable for the additional corporate taxes, interest, and penalties. In fraud-based adjustments, the director's personal assets may be subject to attachment and the individual may be barred from leaving Luxembourg during criminal proceedings.

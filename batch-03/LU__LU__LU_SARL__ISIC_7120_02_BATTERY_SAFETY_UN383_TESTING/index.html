Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), 12, avenue de la Liberté, L-1931 Luxembourg; electronic filing portal: www.lbr.lu

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 to 710-26 (SARL provisions); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register (Loi modifiée du 19 décembre 2002 concernant le registre de commerce et des sociétés et la comptabilité annuelle des entreprises), Articles 9, 16 and 18.

Formation / constitutional filings

Demande d'inscription au Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (application for registration in the Trade and Companies Register) filed electronically via the LBR e-formalities module with the certified notarial deed of incorporation annexed, followed by mandatory publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum EUR 12,000 share capital for an SARL, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation; the funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and released only upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 710-6, amended by the Law of 23 September 2016). A capital between EUR 1 and EUR 12,000 is admissible only if the articles of association restrict distributions until net assets exceed share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) and the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for lithium battery storage rooms, requiring EI 60 fire resistance, hydrogen gas detection and Class D fire suppression. Structural load approval for the drop-test rigs and vibration/isolation benches must be issued by the competent building control service. Workplace clearance under the Luxembourg Labour Code covers electrical safety of high-voltage test benches (IEC 61010), emergency evacuation planning, personal protective equipment protocols, and first-aid provisions for thermal battery incidents.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum EUR 12,000 share capital for an SARL, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation; the funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and released only upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate (Law of 10 August 1915, Article 710-6, amended by the Law of 23 September 2016). A capital between EUR 1 and EUR 12,000 is admissible only if the articles of association restrict distributions until net assets exceed share capital.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Luxembourg law; available elections include VAT grouping (assujetti unique), the corporate income tax fiscal consolidation regime for 95%-owned resident subsidiaries, the IP box election for qualifying intellectual property income (80% exemption), and various ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch structuring options; Luxembourg does not provide a unilateral pass-through election for SARLs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for small businesses; non-resident suppliers have no threshold for supplies taxable in Luxembourg; EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance sales threshold remains relevant for e-commerce

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (a) dispatch/transport of goods outside the EU supported by a customs export declaration (SAD) and transport evidence; (b) services closely linked to exported goods and performed directly for the goods; (c) testing and analysis services supplied to a taxable customer established outside the EU where the place of supply is outside Luxembourg, provided the testing relates to goods destined for non-EU delivery or to services effectively enjoyed outside the EU; proof of customer's non-EU status and export destination must be maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; mandatory structured electronic invoicing (PEPPOL-BII 3.0) for B2G contracts; B2B and B2C e-invoicing is permitted but not mandatory; VAT returns and EC sales lists must be filed electronically; the Luxembourg standard audit file for tax (SAF-T / fichier d'écritures comptables) is required for tax audit purposes for certain taxpayers.

Improper or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT adjustments over a five-year limitation period (extended to ten years for fraud), a 10% tax increase for late or incomplete reporting, late-payment interest at the legal rate, administrative fines up to EUR 12,500, and criminal penalties of 8 days to 5 years imprisonment; the Luxembourg tax administration may revoke the zero-rating clearance and hold directors personally liable for unpaid VAT in case of repeated infringements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully integrated eurozone jurisdiction with unrestricted currency convertibility. Both current and capital account transactions are free from exchange controls under EU Capital Movement Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC) and Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and repatriation of invested capital are freely permitted subject to standard tax withholding obligations, corporate formalities, and CSSF/BCL statistical reporting thresholds; no prior administrative authorization or repatriation permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists under Luxembourg law. Exporters are freely entitled to retain foreign currency in external/offshore accounts, to convert to EUR at market rates, or to settle liabilities directly in foreign currency. However, under ECB Balance of Payments Statistics Regulation (Regulation EC No 184/2005), direct reporting on cross-border payments and transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be furnished to the BCL for statistical compilation. No percentage or timeline for mandatory conversion is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting under the European Central Bank (ECB) framework, together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking and financial market supervision and the Ministère des Finances for capital movement statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating for the entity is 'High' for inbound low-value disbursement flows and 'Medium' for statutory capital escrow functions. Luxembourg recognizes European Economic Area (EEA)-licensed E-Money Institutions (EMIs) under Directive 2009/110/EC and Payment Services Directive (PSD2) via passporting. A Luxembourg SARL may open accounts at EEA EMIs such as Wise (European Banking Authority passporting from Belgium, Ireland, or other EU member states). Legal constraints include: (i) EMI accounts cannot hold investment-grade securities; (ii) client money safeguarding obligations may restrict sub-account concentration; (iii) high-value battery testing lab capital injections above EUR 15,000 usually require a licensed credit institution for SWIFT and letters of credit; (iv) EMI onboarding may not satisfy escrow account provisions in third-party capital commitment agreements; (v) KYC and AML requirements under CSSF Circular CSSF 12/552 apply fully to EMIs, requiring enhanced due diligence where shareholders or beneficiaries are not EU tax residents. EMIs are not substitutes for full commercial banking relationships when the entity must issue Commercial Letters of Credit, operate as a non-bank importer/exporter, or rely on an overdraft/credit line.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection to the SARL's Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account is performed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder’s bank to the Luxembourg credit institution. Eligible purpose codes must be classified in the BCL's Balance of Payments Manual: for initial equity subscription the transaction falls under 'Direct investment – Equity capital' (BoP code 5101 or equivalent under the ECB BPM6 classification); for shareholder loans, current account advances, or convertible instruments, the codes correspond to 'Loans – Non-Financial Sector' (e.g., BoP code 6101). The remitter must provide the definitive document (share subscription agreement, notarized deed of contribution, or board minutes) and ensure the MWG/MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) cites the precise invoice/reference number and the phrase 'INCREASE OF SHARE CAPITAL' or 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION' to avoid AML flagging. Verification by the Luxembourg bank will normally require: (i) a certified share subscription deed; (ii) source-of-funds documentation for the remitting account holder; (iii) CSSF/BCL statistical reporting declarations if cumulative cross-border payments exceed EUR 12,500; (iv) a signed confirmation that the SARL has no illegal or unlicensed activity under Luxembourg commercial law; and (v) in case of non-EU remitter, an anti-money-laundering check on the originator's bank.Outbound settlement for test laboratory equipment, calibration services, and subcontracting is governed by the SARL’s treasury policy. Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for ordinary supplier payments, direct debits, and pre-payments. For high-value or documentary trade transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) can be issued by the Luxembourg account bank in favor of foreign suppliers, payable at sight via a confirming bank in the supplier’s country. The SARL may also use TARGET2 (T2) and SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) for EUR-denominated low-value disbursements to EU beneficiaries. Treasury instructions must specify the ICA (International Code Area) or BT (Bank Transaction) in the instruction, provide the beneficiary bank BIC and IBAN, and maintain a risk-based threshold under which no additional FX conversion is needed. For cross-currency disbursements, the bank will convert EUR to USD/CNY/GBP at spot using the ECB reference rate plus spread; the SARL may request a forward rate when payment exceeds EUR 100,000 and the delivery horizon exceeds 60 days. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months of the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year SARL the return is due by 31 December of the following year (e.g. FY2025 return due 31 December 2026). Extension may be requested by a reasoned application before the deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory if related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 per transaction category (goods, services, intangibles, financial transactions) in the tax year. Master File mandatory when the group’s consolidated global turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) threshold: consolidated group turnover of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have a standalone codified ESR similar to the UAE; however, substance is governed by the General Tax Law (AO) and the effective-management test. For a battery-safety testing SARL, economic substance requires: own or leased laboratory premises in Luxembourg, full-time qualified technical staff performing the UN38.3 and CE-RED tests, laboratory insurance, genuine client contracts, and board decisions taken in Luxembourg. A letter-box entity without such substance will be denied treaty benefits under the domestic GAAR (Art. 6 AO / fraude à la loi) and may be reassessed as subject to tax only on a deemed-basis income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude à la loi / Déclaration frauduleuse)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 396 L.I.R.: fine up to EUR 25,000 (or twice the evaded tax), imprisonment from 1 to 5 years (up to 10 years for aggravated evasion), full restitution of evaded taxes, plus late-payment interest at 6% p.a.

Director personal liability: The SARL’s gérant and the statutory directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax underpayment if they signed false declarations, concealed transactions, or actively participated in the fraud. Direct recourse on personal assets, restriction of movement and, in severe cases, custodial sentences for the directors.
Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Loss of Luxembourg tax residency or denial of treaty benefits; recharacterisation of profits under GAAR; disallowance of the full intercompany fee deduction; administrative penalty of 10% of the recalculated tax, plus late interest. In case of non-filing or false substance declarations, criminal prosecution for misuse of rights can follow.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held civilly liable for all taxes, penalties and interest re-assessed; they may be banned from professional activity and, if complicit, face criminal sanctions for false certification and tax fraud.
AML/CFT Violation – Battery Testing Laboratory

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended): administrative fines of up to EUR 1,250,000 or 10% of turnover for serious breaches; criminal money-laundering penalties up to 5 years’ imprisonment and a criminal fine of up to EUR 500,000. The banking AML risk tier for the entity is low-risk, but obligations remain.

Director personal liability: The gérant, compliance officer, and the statutory directors are personally liable for monetary penalties; they may be arrested and extradited for money-laundering offences, be subject to a professional ban, and be liable for damages suffered by victims or the State.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 5,000 for failure to produce TP documentation; transfer pricing adjustment upward with an additional 10% penalty on the corrected taxable income (where the adjustment is for cross-border non-arm’s-length transactions); 6% p.a. late interest; and possible additional cascading penalties for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and the 10% penalty if they failed to establish and retain proper TP documentation; in cases of gross negligence, the tax authorities may refer the file for criminal tax-evasion prosecution.

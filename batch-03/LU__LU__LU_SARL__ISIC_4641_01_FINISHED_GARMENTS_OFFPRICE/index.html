Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income/corporate taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT and registration duties; official portal: guichet.lu / myguichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

For a SARL, file incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet; obtain the RCS matricule and the Minister of Economy establishment authorization if required. For VAT, submit the Luxembourg VAT registration application using Form 563 to the AED; the AED issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number. A foreign entity without a Luxembourg permanent establishment but making taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register for VAT without benefiting from the domestic EUR 35,000 small-enterprise threshold; non-residents with no EU establishment may be required to appoint a VAT representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; SARL is automatically opaque. Elective fiscal unity (tax consolidation) under LIR Art. 164bis for 95%-owned Luxembourg resident companies for CIT and municipal business tax; elective VAT grouping under LVA Art. 60; hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under ATAD apply and no elective hybrid-mismatch regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for established Luxembourg small businesses (optional registration); no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers—any Luxembourg taxable supply or intra-EU acquisition triggering local VAT requires registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically leaving the EU to non-EU destinations are zero-rated under Luxembourg VAT law. Eligibility requires that the VAT-registered supplier makes the supply, the goods are exported by the supplier or on its instructions, and the supplier holds contemporaneous transport and customs evidence proving removal from the EU. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers may also be zero-rated with valid EU VAT identification evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export data/exit notification)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport evidence (air waybill, CMR, courier track-and-trace)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and inward remittance certificate/payment receipt

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction controls; no digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G supplies to Luxembourg public entities via Peppol; domestic B2B e-invoicing is optional if accepted by the counterparty; VAT returns and recapitulative statements are filed electronically via MyGuichet with audit-readiness of accounting records and SAF-T data.

Luxembourg tax authorities scrutinize export files; failure to maintain the required export and payment evidence triggers reversal of zero-rating, assessment of VAT at 17%, late-interest accrual, and penalties up to 100% of the evaded tax for fraudulent concealment; criminal sanctions may also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Luxembourg maintains no exchange controls on either current-account or capital-account transactions, as guaranteed by Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Council Directive 88/361/EEC of 24 June 1988. For the LU_SARL, there are no exchange-approval requirements for inbound equity injections, outbound trade settlements, dividend distributions, or loan repayments. Profit remittance is operationally unrestricted; the sole fiscal impediment is the Luxembourg dividend withholding tax of 15%, which is reduced to 0% for corporate shareholders meeting the conditions of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or mitigated under an applicable double tax treaty, provided the beneficial ownership file is documented. Full current- and capital-account liberalisation applies, and no separate foreign-investment licence or central bank permit is triggered by the wholesale apparel liquidation activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion obligations exist in Luxembourg. Export proceeds from the liquidation of branded overstock apparel into non-EU markets may be credited in full to EUR-denominated or foreign-currency bank accounts, retained abroad, or converted freely, with no statutory timeline and no minimum conversion ratio. The only compliance burden is indirect: the Luxembourg credit institution must report cross-border payment data to the BCL for euro-area balance-of-payments statistics; the SARL itself is not required to file any export-proceeds declaration or central bank form.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); banking prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while the Cellule de Renseignements Financiers (CRF) acts as the Luxembourg financial intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary payment rail and Low as the sole treasury anchor. An EU-authorised electronic money institution passporting into Luxembourg, or a Luxembourg-licensed EMI under PSD2, can cost-effectively run SEPA instant transfers, virtual IBANs, card acceptance, and lighter receivables collection for the apparel trading operation. Legal constraints: an EMI is neither a credit institution nor a deposit taker; it cannot hold the SARL's initial share-capital escrow for the notarial deed, cannot grant overdrafts or trade credit, and is not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme. Most EMIs also lack a full SWIFT MT103 correspondent network and cannot provide documentary letters of credit or cross-currency treasury desks. Conclusion: retain one Tier-1 Luxembourg bank as the primary treasury and clearing anchor and use an EMI only for niche receivables collection in EU markets.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge (1.19%) + Luxembourg City municipal business tax (6.75%) = aggregate headline statutory rate of 24.94% for tax year 2024; outside Luxembourg City the municipal multiplier varies typically between 6.65% and 11.05%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory withholding tax on dividends and other profit distributions paid by a Luxembourg SARL, unless reduced by a double tax agreement or eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for an EU resident corporate shareholder holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period and satisfying the anti-abuse substance test); under the Luxembourg-China DTA currently in force the rate is capped at 5% for a beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the capital and 10% otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within five months after the close of the financial year (31 May for a calendar-year SARL); the ACD generally grants an automatic extension to 31 December of the following year in practice, and additional extensions must be requested electronically before the expiry of the deadline.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A / Box 1 — Identification Company legal name, SARL form, registered office address in Luxembourg, RCS (Luxembourg business register) number, VAT identification number and ACD tax file number. A non-resident company operating through a Luxembourg branch or permanent establishment must insert the branch's local address, the Luxembourg VAT number and the ACD tax reference, and must attach the parent company's certificate of existence. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section B — Financial statements and tax reconciliation Attach the annual accounts prepared under Luxembourg GAAP (balance sheet, profit and loss account, notes); reconcile accounting profit to taxable income by adding back non-deductible expenses (CIT itself, certain penalties, excessive interest) and deducting exempt income (participation-exempt dividends and gains, 80% IP income). The Luxembourg PE must present separate accounts for the branch activity, with transfer pricing adjustments documented for intercompany transactions with the foreign head office. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section C — Depreciation, provisions and inventory Computation of tax depreciation of fixed assets (linear or declining balance per ACD tables) and tax provisions; inventory of off-price overstock garments may be written down to net realizable value, and doubtful trade receivables may be provisioned if documented. Fixed assets of the PE are depreciated according to Luxembourg tax tables; inventory located in Luxembourg is valued on the same prudent commercial basis. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section D — Tax losses carried forward Statement of opening and closing loss carryforward, current-year usage and any forfeiture; utilization is limited to EUR 5,000,000 plus 70% of the taxable income exceeding EUR 5,000,000; continuity of the same commercial activity must be demonstrated. Only losses generated by the Luxembourg PE are available; foreign head-office losses are not deductible in Luxembourg. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section E — Municipal business tax (IBT) Determination of the IBT taxable base and application of the municipal multiplier of the commune where the SARL's registered office or PE is located (Luxembourg City 6.75%). A PE is subject to IBT in the commune where the fixed place of business is situated; use that commune's multiplier and file the related IBT annex in Form 600. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section F — Net wealth tax (NWT) Computation of taxable net wealth (assets minus liabilities, after the exempt amount) at 0.5% per year, with a minimum net wealth tax of EUR 4,815 for 2024 companies with a balance sheet total above EUR 350,000; minimum of EUR 535 for dormant micro-companies. The PE's Luxembourg-situs assets are subject to NWT; the applicable DTA may reduce the rate but the minimum NWT remains enforceable. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section G — CIT, solidarity surcharge and advance payments Computation of CIT at 17%, solidarity surcharge at 7% of CIT, deduction of quarterly prepayments already made, and declaration of the remaining balance due or refund claimed. The branch itself files quarterly prepayments under its Luxembourg tax reference; prepayments made by the foreign head office do not belong to the PE. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.
Section H — Declaration, signature and annexes Electronic declaration signed by the authorized director (gérant) attesting accuracy; annexes include annual accounts, directors' report, tax elections and any requests for administrative rulings. A resident tax representative may sign for the PE; the representative's name, address and professional license number must be identified. Late filing triggers a statutory reminder (mise en demeure), possible taxation by default (imposition d'office) on the ACD's own estimated basis, a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax, default interest of 0.6% per commenced month, and administrative fines between EUR 250 and EUR 2,500 for breach of the mandatory e-filing obligation; unpaid taxes may be collected through garnishment and seizure of bank accounts and inventory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg has no statutory Local File or Master File filing threshold for ordinary taxpayers; however, multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 must file Country-by-Country notifications and returns with the ACD; for all related-party transactions, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File following the OECD TP Guidelines and the EU Transfer Pricing Documentation standard) is required in practice to avoid reversal of the burden of proof in a tax audit.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Partially permissible. The SARL must have a registered office (siège social) in the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg (Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915). A purely virtual office without operational presence may be accepted at registration but will be challengeable in VAT, customs and corporate income tax audits.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Amended Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Title IV governing Sociétés à Responsabilité Limitée (Articles 100 et seq.); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; Grand-Ducal Regulation of 23 June 1983 on special and exceptional transports for the underlying heavy-haul activity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Authentic notarial deed of incorporation (acte de constitution) together with the articles of association (statuts), filed electronically through the LBR eJustice portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Since the 2016 Luxembourg company-law reform, the SARL has no statutory minimum share capital; however, the articles must fix a stated capital with a nominal par value per share, and the full subscribed capital must be paid up and deposited into a blocked bank account ('attestation de blocage') with a Luxembourg credit institution before the notarial deed is executed. The blocked funds may not be released until the RCS registration certificate is issued. No state escrow account is imposed by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance by the communal authority with CGDIS certifying building evacuation, fire alarms, and extinguishers; structural stability certificate of any leased warehouse or yard; occupational health and safety inspection by the ITM (Inspection du travail et des mines) covering internal regulations, first-aid equipment, safe load-handling procedures, and the annual risk assessment under Article L.312-1 of the Luxembourg Labour Code.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for business registration; unified e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (guichet.lu).

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated SARL is registered by the notary with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR), obtaining the national matricule / business number, which serves as the base TIN for all tax filings. The SARL must then file VAT registration form 645 with the AEDT (via MyGuichet.lu) to obtain the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format 'LU' + 8 digits). Non-resident foreign entities without a Luxembourg establishment performing taxable supplies in Luxembourg must register directly with the AEDT and, if established outside the EU, appoint an approved Luxembourg tax representative. The SOPARFI holding regime is confirmed through the company's constitutional object and compliance statement, not a separate registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box classification. Available elections: (i) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) election under Art. 60 of the VAT Law for 95%-controlled integrated entities; (ii) fiscal integration (tax unity) election for a 95%-owned Luxembourg subsidiary consolidation for corporate income tax; (iii) participation-exemption application for qualifying 10% holdings (or EUR 1.2M acquisition cost); and (iv) ATAD anti-hybrid election/notification for hybrid mismatch arrangements. No pass-through or transparent status is available for SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

16%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-resident suppliers: EUR 0 / no threshold - registration is mandatory for any taxable B2B or B2C supply or distance sale into Luxembourg; resident small enterprises: EUR 35,000 annual turnover franchise exemption (optional and never available to non-established foreign entities under Art. 55bis VAT Law).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export of goods under Art. 44(I)(1)(a) VAT Law where goods are dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier or a third party acting on the supplier's behalf within 90 days of supply. Directly related international logistics services (OOG heavy-haul project cargo transport, loading, securing, handling, and freight forwarding) linked to the physical export movement also qualify for 0% VAT, provided the supplier holds documentary evidence of the transport to a third-country destination. Exports through an approved customs warehouse (regime 51/53), followed by external transit (T1), are eligible where the customs export declaration is lodged with Luxembourg Customs and the exit is confirmed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX form) accepted by Luxembourg Customs, with MRN of export
  • Bill of Lading (seaway/ocean) or CMR waybill confirming physical exit from EU customs territory to third country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, contract of carriage, and proof of insurance for OOG heavy-haul cargo
  • Cargo securing and stowage survey reports supporting the transport service nature
  • VAT recapitulative statement and export journal evidencing zero-rated supply

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is currently enforced in Luxembourg. E-invoicing follows EU VAT Directive rules: both paper and structured electronic invoicing (EDI, XML, PDF) are accepted; VAT returns and recapitulative statements (ESL) must be filed electronically via the AEDT MyGuichet.lu eFiling portal with mandatory monthly/quarterly periodicity depending on annual turnover; Intrastat reporting applies above EU turnover thresholds. Luxembourg is preparing a future mandatory structured e-invoicing framework, but no operative API clearance gateway exists as of the current effective tax period.

Luxembourg AEDT and Customs may reassess zero-rated exports where the supplier fails to obtain proof of exit within the statutory 3-month period, restoring standard 16% VAT plus late-payment interest at 0.8% per month (9.6% per annum) from the due date. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Art. 199 of the General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), exposing directors to fines of up to five times the evaded VAT and imprisonment terms of 8 days to 6 months; clawback assessments may be raised for 5 years (10 years in cases of evasion), with denial of deduction for related input VAT and publication of the corrective assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies the euro as legal tender; exchange controls are fully abolished under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, including profit repatriation, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and capital redemptions. No general license is required for FX conversion or cross-border transfers. De minimis balance-of-payments statistical reporting may apply, and payments to sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions are constrained by EU sanctions and AML/CFT surveillance, but no convertibility or remittance restrictions apply to lawful commercial transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any mandatory export proceeds repatriation, surrender, or conversion obligation. Exporters are free to invoice and receive payments in any currency, hold foreign-currency accounts, and convert or maintain balances without statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios. The only obligations are accounting, tax documentation, and compliance with EU sanctions and AML/CFT rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and under the monetary competence of the European Central Bank (ECB); banking supervision and AML/CFT enforcement for credit institutions and electronic money institutions are exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary settlement, low-value sourcing, and expense flows; Low for primary receivables and high-value project cargo disbursements. Legal constraints: an EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) may provide services in Luxembourg on a cross-border services passport or through a CSSF-authorised branch; non-EEA offshore EMIs without a passport cannot lawfully undertake payment services for Luxembourg entities without CSSF authorisation. Additional constraints include client-fund safeguarding, no credit-taking, and elevated AML/KYC expectations. For heavy-haul project logistics, an EMI is acceptable only as an auxiliary rail, never as the sole clearing channel.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Holding-company structure with non-resident UBOs and multi-layer ownership

Root cause: Holding-company structure with non-resident UBOs and multi-layer ownership

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation; certificate of good standing; up-to-date shareholders' register and UBO declaration filed with the Luxembourg trade and companies register (RCSL); full corporate tree with jurisdictional rationale; board resolution appointing authorised signatories; professional reference from a regulated EU financial institution; audited financial statements or opening balance sheet.
High-value cross-border project cargo payments to third parties in geographies with perceived sanctions exposure

Root cause: High-value cross-border project cargo payments to third parties in geographies with perceived sanctions exposure

Defense evidence: Executed project logistics, construction, or sales contracts naming the beneficiaries; commercial invoices and bills of lading / CMR waybills; shipping and customs documentation; supplier due-diligence file including licenses, ownership, and sanctions screening; evidence that no sanctioned person has a beneficial interest; narrative justification for each wire underlying the genuine trade.
Unusual payment frequency or large milestone wires inconsistent with working capital

Root cause: Unusual payment frequency or large milestone wires inconsistent with working capital

Defense evidence: Project timeline and milestone schedule; cash-flow forecast; agreed commercial milestone triggers; client invoice register; prior bank statements; management accounts; written explanation of payment velocity acceptable to the bank's transaction-monitoring unit.
Politically exposed person (PEP) or family member of PEP as shareholder or director

Root cause: Politically exposed person (PEP) or family member of PEP as shareholder or director

Defense evidence: PEP and fraud self-declaration; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds reports prepared by a licensed auditor or attorney; documented legitimate business background; notarized identification and proof of address; board and UBO approvals; independent compliance review by external counsel.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Standard deadline: 31 May following the end of the tax year for taxpayers whose fiscal year coincides with the calendar year (31 December); extended automatically to 30 June; further monthly extensions available upon reasoned request (up to a maximum of 6 months, subject to a final deadline of 30 November of the following year); payments of CIT and municipal business tax are due based on quarterly provisional instalments (10 March, 10 June, 10 September, 10 December) with the final balancing payment due with the assessment notice

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1a Entity identification: legal form (SARL), company name, registered office address, corporate registration number (RCS), tax identification number (matricule) – preprinted by the e-filing system Non-resident SARL with a Luxembourg permanent establishment must indicate its foreign head-office address and obtain a Luxembourg TIN prior to filing
Box 2a Tax year period: start date and end date of the fiscal year (must match the articles of association unless changed by shareholder resolution) Non-resident filer uses the Luxembourg fiscal year of the permanent establishment; where the foreign head office uses a different fiscal year, the Luxembourg PE must file based on the PE's accounting period as presented in the local books
Box 3a Basis of assessment: confirm whether the return covers a full 12-month period or a short first fiscal period State clearly if the PE is newly established or ceases activity mid-year; short-period returns are not prorated for tax rate purposes
Box 5a Commercial balance sheet profit/loss before tax as per Luxembourg GAAP (annual accounts) The PE must keep and submit separate local accounts; foreign head-office accounts are not sufficient
Box 7a Add-back of non-deductible expenses: fines, penalties, corporate income tax itself, municipal business tax, 50% of certain entertainment expenses, excessive director fees, and payments to non-cooperative jurisdictions All disallowed amounts incurred by the Luxembourg PE or allocated from head office must be added back; transfer pricing adjustments require simultaneous corresponding adjustments
Box 8a Deductible items: interest subject to thin capitalisation limits, royalties, business expenses wholly and exclusively incurred for the Luxembourg business Interest on shareholder loans must satisfy the arm's length principle and the 30% EBITDA limitation; the PE may deduct only expenses attributable to the PE
Box 9a Tax-exempt income: participation exemption dividends and capital gains, IP box income, notional interest deduction Non-resident shareholders cannot apply the participation exemption to Luxembourg source dividends paid by the PE; the exemption applies only at the level of the Luxembourg corporate taxpayer
Box 10a Taxable income computation: net taxable amount after adjustments and deductions Luxembourg-sourced income of the PE is taxable; foreign-sourced income is outside the PE base unless attributable under the OECD model
Box 11a Tax credits: withholding tax credits on Luxembourg source dividends/interest, foreign tax credit for taxes on foreign income included in the Luxembourg tax base Use the foreign tax credit mechanism to avoid double taxation; maximum credit equals Luxembourg CIT attributable to the same income
Box 12a Assessment of corporate income tax (17% base rate), unemployment surcharge (7% of CIT), municipal business tax Municipal business tax applies to the PE if the PE is situated in a Luxembourg commune; the surcharge and municipal tax are calculated on the same taxable base
Box 13a Tax losses available for carryforward (5 years) and loss carryback (no statutory carryback) Losses of a Luxembourg PE may be carried forward only against future Luxembourg PE profits; losses of the foreign head office are not attributed to the PE

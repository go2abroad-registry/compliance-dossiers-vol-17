Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must have a minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000. At incorporation the entire capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid up; cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution or placed in notarial escrow, and may be released to the company only after RCS registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates a fully liberalised capital and current account regime by virtue of EU obligations under Articles 63/64 TFEU and the Schengen/Euro area arrangements. The euro is fully convertible, with no restrictions on current account transactions, portfolio investment, direct investment, or the repatriation of profits, dividends, liquidation proceeds, or loan repayments for non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is subject only to compliance with corporate formalities (approval of annual accounts) and applicable withholding tax obligations under Luxembourg domestic tax law and double tax treaties. No prior authorisation from the BCL or the Ministry of Finance is required for cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, mandatory sale, or time-limit repatriation rules for export proceeds in Luxembourg. Exporters may retain, reinvest, or transfer export receipts in any currency without conversion obligations. Banks are, however, obliged to report suspected anti-money laundering and terrorist financing incidents under the Luxembourg Criminal Code and the Law of 12 November 2004, and to collect statistical Balance of Payments (BoP) data for the BCL under ECB reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential supervision of banks and financial intermediaries is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). Luxembourg has no separate central bank foreign exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for Luxembourg-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) using the EU Single European Payment Area (SEPA) and PSD2 passport. A fully Luxembourg-licensed EMI with an EBA-compliant e-money authorisation can open virtual accounts and conduct EUR and cross-border settlement, but the SARL's commercial banking function for trade finance, escrow, and documentary credit will require a regulated credit institution. Third-country/offshore EMIs (non-EEA, e.g. some Asia-Pacific or Caribbean e-money operators) are not recognised as qualified payment institutions for Luxembourg corporate treasury purposes and are legally constrained by the Luxembourg Banking Act 1993 and PSD2 restrictions; they cannot lawfully act as the primary clearing rail for the SARL's export distribution without a local branch or agent.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Capital injection from a low-tax/offshore jurisdiction without clear economic nexus

Root cause: Capital injection from a low-tax/offshore jurisdiction without clear economic nexus

Defense evidence: Certified source-of-funds letter from the originating bank, audited financial statements of the injecting shareholder, signed intercompany loan/subscription agreement, group organisational chart, business plan for the Luxembourg SARL, and tax domicile certificates for all UBOs.
High transaction velocity inconsistent with an ISIC 4638 wholesale seasoning/export distribution business

Root cause: High transaction velocity inconsistent with an ISIC 4638 wholesale seasoning/export distribution business

Defense evidence: Full trade cycle documentation, including commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, customs declarations, warehouse receipts, inventory forecasts, dispatch schedules, and sales contracts with end buyers, supplemented by an independent audit confirmation of the expected annual merchandise flow.
Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctioned-adjacent beneficial owner

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or sanctioned-adjacent beneficial owner

Defense evidence: PEP declaration, updated CV and business references from an EU-regulated financial intermediary, independent source-of-wealth report prepared by a Luxembourg counsel or certified auditor, UBO register (RBE) extraction, and avoidant sanctions screening reports against EU/UN/US/OFAC consolidated lists.
Proposed settlement via unlicensed or non-bankable payment service provider in the inbound trade chain

Root cause: Proposed settlement via unlicensed or non-bankable payment service provider in the inbound trade chain

Defense evidence: Luxembourg legal opinion confirming the PSP's licensing/exemption status, evidence of authorization in the PSP's home jurisdiction, PSP due-diligence file, transaction flow diagram, and contractual undertakings restricting payments to segregated client accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions to the SARL's escrow account must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer using the company's IBAN at a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire must reference the BCL/ECB BoP statistical purpose code corresponding to direct investment equity capital (e.g., the applicable EU/ECB code for 'Direct investment — equity capital' as instructed by the onboarding bank) and include a written remittance narrative: 'Contribution to share capital of LU_SARL, ISIC 4638 wholesale seasoning goods distribution.' The receiving bank will verify the UBO, the triggering ESG/AML screen, and the corporate authorization (boarding resolution). For CNY or USD injections, the aggregation custodian bank should pre-clear the conversion rate and the temporary custody arrangement before credit is allocated to the operational account.Primary clearing rails for export trade settlements are SWIFT MT103 single-customer payments for full pre-payment, SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank cover, documentary collections under URC 522 for low-risk open account flows, and irrevocable commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for buyer-payee risk. For EU/SEPA counterparties, a SEPA Credit Transfer with the related reference 'Hotpot Base Brick Wholesale Distribution' is sufficient. For Chinese or Hong Kong settlement nodes, RMB/CNH clearing through Bank of China Luxembourg or CIPS-participant correspondent accounts may be used, supported by underlying trade documents and VAT/customs evidence to avoid FX revert or trade-based money-laundering red flags.Outright FX forward contracts (EUR/CNY, EUR/USD, CNY/USD) entered into with an authorised Luxembourg or EU credit institution under ISDA terms with documented underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/CNY or EUR/USD) for hedging long-term working capital requirements and trusted debt-service cash flows, subject to EMIR clearing and variation margin obligations.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on offshore CNY (CNH) where a leg or notional conversion is subject to partial convertibility barriers of the underlying Asian currency, only with a licensed global FX clearing bank and ISO 20022 payment reconciliation.

24.94% aggregate effective CIT rate in Luxembourg City (17% corporate income tax + 7% solidarity surcharge = 18.19%, plus municipal business tax of 6.75%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic withholding tax on resident and non-resident dividend recipients; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% interest held ≥12 months or a 24-month indicative holding); most income tax treaties reduce the rate to 5% for portfolio investors and 0% for qualifying corporate shareholdings

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File mandatory where the group's consolidated annual turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50 million (Law of 23 June 2016); Country-by-Country Report mandatory where the group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750 million; documentation must include functional analysis, benchmark study, and intercompany contracts in EUR

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone ESR statute comparable to the UAE, but substance is decisive through several binding channels: the judicial anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit), ATAD rules (30% EBITDA interest limitation, CFC rules, general anti-abuse provision), participation-exemption conditions, and the requirement of real activity for a Luxembourg Tax Residence Certificate. For a SARL operating as a wholesale exporter of pre-packaged hotpot soup base, tested substance includes a Luxembourg office, qualified sales and logistics staff, genuine negotiation and invoicing activity, local ERP and bank accounts, warehousing or third-party logistics contracts, risk assumption, and board meetings in Luxembourg. A pure letterbox distributor could lose treaty benefits and the participation exemption, and face re-assessment of profits as if earned by a fully substantive local distributor.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration (Tax Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and a fine of EUR 250 to EUR 125,000 per infraction under Article 1193 of the Luxembourg General Tax Code; in aggravated or organised fraud, fines up to EUR 250,000, confiscation of proceeds, and publication of the judgment; the underpaid tax is recaptured with monthly interest of 0.8% and penalty interest

Director personal liability: Any director or gérant who signs or causes the false return is criminally liable as co-principal, with personal imprisonment, fines, and a management ban (interdiction de gérer); the investigating judge may seize the director's passport (travel ban); civil liability attaches for the underpaid tax plus damages under Article 510-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit and Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The Luxembourg tax administration recaptures the avoided tax at the statutory 24.94% aggregate rate, applies 0.8% monthly late-payment interest, denies treaty benefits and the participation exemption, and may issue a penalty assessment under the GAAR; cross-border information exchange (DAC6, CbCR) increases detection probability

Director personal liability: Directors remain personally liable for intentionally facilitating sham structures: joint and several liability for the recaptured corporate tax and WHT, disqualification from corporate office, personal fines, and imprisonment where the arrangement is qualified as tax fraud; asset tracing and freezing orders may target the director's personal bank accounts
Administrative Non-Compliance: Late Filing and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Ex-officio assessment with complete disallowance of undocumented deductions; penalty interest of 0.8% per month compounded; procedural fines up to EUR 2,500; forced recovery through garnishment of the SARL's bank accounts and seizure of goods; non-declaration of WHT triggers a specific charge of 10% of the withheld amount plus the monthly interest

Director personal liability: If the company's tax debts arise from intentional or negligent conduct, directors may be declared jointly liable for the full unpaid tax debt upon liquidation or insolvency; the director's personal assets can be attached; travel bans are possible in enforcement proceedings when the director leaves Luxembourg to frustrate collection
AML Non-Compliance in Trade Payment Processes

Statutory sanction: Under the Luxembourg AML framework, legal persons face administrative fines up to EUR 5 million and criminal sanctions including confiscation of transaction funds; the business licence (autorisation d'établissement) can be revoked, freezing the company's operations; repeated violations trigger automatic blacklisting by the banking sector

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or permit non-compliant cross-border payments are criminally liable with imprisonment up to 5 years and personal fines; civil liability covers the company's AML penalties; the CSSF and CRF can order the director's removal from management and impose a professional ban; passport seizure may accompany criminal investigations

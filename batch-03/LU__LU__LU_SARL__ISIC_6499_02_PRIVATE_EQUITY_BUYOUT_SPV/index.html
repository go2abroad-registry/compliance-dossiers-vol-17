Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office is not acceptable as the sole statutory registered office of a Luxembourg SARL. Article 710-2 of the Law of 10 August 1915 requires a registered office in Luxembourg with a physical address. A shared office space or dedicated corporate-services address may satisfy the legal requirement only when actual business records are maintainable at that address and the entity has local substance indicators: local management meetings, Luxembourg-resident director capacity, locally retained accounts, and an economic presence proportionate to the private equity buyout activity. A fully-virtual mailbox without any local operational presence will expose the entity to UBO-register challenge, tax substance controversy, and reclassification risk.

Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and municipal business tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT, registration duties and stamp taxes; Customs and Excise Agency (Administration des douanes et accises, ADN) for customs and excise. Electronic filing and taxpayer portals: myTax.lu, MyGuichet.lu, and Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file the notarial deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the RCS registration number and matricule, which is the master corporate identifier for all tax accounts. 2) Apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA, 'LU' + 8 digits) electronically via MyGuichet.lu on AEDT Form 106 as soon as taxable supplies commence; a non-resident entity without a Luxembourg establishment making taxable supplies must register directly with the AEDT and, if established outside the EEA, appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative. 3) Obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Agency (ADN) for all customs import/export formalities. 4) The corporate income tax and municipal business tax accounts are set up automatically from the RCS registration; annual tax returns are filed under the RCS/matricule reference. 5) If the SPV has employees or payroll, register separately with the Luxembourg Social Security Centre (CCSS).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL is an opaque juridical person subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur la collectivités) on worldwide income, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and annual net wealth tax; it has unlimited tax liability as a Luxembourg tax resident. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under Luxembourg domestic law, and a US 'check-the-box' election has no effect on Luxembourg entity classification. — 1) Incorporate the SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file the notarial deed with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the RCS registration number and matricule, which is the master corporate identifier for all tax accounts. 2) Apply for a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA, 'LU' + 8 digits) electronically via MyGuichet.lu on AEDT Form 106 as soon as taxable supplies commence; a non-resident entity without a Luxembourg establishment making taxable supplies must register directly with the AEDT and, if established outside the EEA, appoint a Luxembourg VAT fiscal representative. 3) Obtain an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Agency (ADN) for all customs import/export formalities. 4) The corporate income tax and municipal business tax accounts are set up automatically from the RCS registration; annual tax returns are filed under the RCS/matricule reference. 5) If the SPV has employees or payroll, register separately with the Luxembourg Social Security Centre (CCSS).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL is an opaque juridical person subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur la collectivités) on worldwide income, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and annual net wealth tax; it has unlimited tax liability as a Luxembourg tax resident. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under Luxembourg domestic law, and a US 'check-the-box' election has no effect on Luxembourg entity classification.

Available election forms

Fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election allowing a 95%+ resident parent and its subsidiaries to be treated as one taxable entity for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; optional VAT group (assujetti unique) election for closely connected taxable persons under the transposed EU VAT Directive; participation exemption election under the 10% shareholding / 12-month holding condition; no domestic check-the-box election exists; hybrid mismatch elections are neutralized by Luxembourg ATAD implementation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general compulsory turnover threshold: any taxable person carrying on a taxable supply of goods or services in Luxembourg must register for VAT, subject to the optional small-business franchise exemption of EUR 30,000 annual turnover; non-established EU traders registering under the OSS/Distance Sales scheme benefit from the EUR 10,000 intra-Community distance-selling threshold; intra-Community acquisitions by non-taxable persons are subject to a EUR 10,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the supplier proves, through the export customs declaration and transport documentation, that the goods physically left the EU within 3 months of the supply. Certain internationally-linked services are also zero-rated, notably sea and air transport of goods, related loading/unloading and ancillary handling services, and services directly performed on movable goods destined for export. Cross-border B2B services are outside Luxembourg VAT scope and shift to the recipient under reverse charge. For a private equity buyout SPV, pure financial and fund-management services are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated; such exempt services can be structured through separate cost-bearing entities to optimize irrecoverable VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) lodged electronically in the Luxembourg AES/ECS customs system
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR (road), Air Waybill (air) or postal/courier receipt evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of Origin or EUR.1 movement certificate, if preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Incoterms-based sale contract and order documentation substantiating the export transaction

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime is in force in Luxembourg. Electronic VAT filing and EC sales listings are mandatory through the AEDT e-filing portal (myTax/eTVA). E-invoicing is mandatory only for B2G public procurement via the national PEPPOL infrastructure in Factur-X/BIS format since 1 March 2020; private B2B e-invoicing remains optional and subject to recipient consent, with paper and PDF invoices still legally accepted for non-public supply chains.

The AEDT conducts targeted documentary audits on high-risk export zero-rating claims; insufficient or inconsistent proof triggers retroactive VAT assessment with statutory interest. The standard limitation period is 5 years, extended to 10 years for fraud or deliberate evasion. Fraudulent zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to full clawback of the VAT, late-payment interest, penalties up to 100% of the evaded tax, administrative fines of up to EUR 250,000, and criminal prosecution (including imprisonment) under Luxembourg tax-fraud provisions; the burden of substantiating the export and the repatriation of consideration rests entirely on the supplier.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a fully liberalised euro area jurisdiction. The euro (EUR) is freely convertible on both current and capital account; Articles 63-66 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC guarantee free movement of capital. No exchange control approval, no convertibility restrictions, and no profit remittance licences apply to non-resident investors. Dividends, redemption proceeds, liquidation surpluses and loan repayments can be remitted abroad without BCL authorisation, subject only to corporate law formalities (notarised shareholder resolutions, statutory audit where legal thresholds are exceeded), the 15% domestic dividend withholding tax (reducible by treaty or the Luxembourg participation exemption provisions) and bank-side BOP statistical declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for trade receipts. The BCL requires only statistical balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border settlements. For a private equity buyout SPV under ISIC 6499, there is no export trade cycle; acquisition financing, capital call subscriptions and disposal proceeds must be identified in SWIFT remittance fields as financial-account BOP categories (F.1 direct investment equity, F.112 direct investment loans) rather than trade-related receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential supervision of credit institutions delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The BCL operates TARGET2-Luxembourg settlement, manages euro monetary operations, collects balance-of-payments statistics under Regulation (EC) No 184/2005, and co-owns the domestic AML/CFT framework with the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance and CSSF.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability with significant legal constraints. CSSF-authorised or EU-passported electronic money institutions (Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services) can provide a Luxembourg IBAN operational account and low-value administrative disbursements. However, an EMI cannot act as settlement account provider for syndicated loan wires, cannot directly hold client escrow without a BCL-licensed sponsoring credit institution, and has no direct TARGET2 access. Non-EEA offshore EMIs lack EU passporting rights and will be declined by Luxembourg banks for a Medium-Risk-Enhanced PE vehicle. The blueprint permits EMIs solely for pre-funding professional fees and operating expenses, while all acquisition capital, escrow and clearing balances remain on domestic Tier-1 banking rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections arrive via SWIFT MT103/MT202 into the EUR operational or escrow account, with field 70 remittance information stating the notarised capital subscription deed reference or shareholder loan agreement reference, the beneficiary account number, funding jurisdiction code, and the ECB BOP classification. Equity contributions must be coded as F.1 (direct investment equity); shareholder loans as F.112 (direct investment loans). Funds can be injected as equity (notarised capital increase registered with the RCS) or as documented shareholder loans with arm's length interest. For each injection exceeding EUR 10,000, the bank requires a written facility agreement or notarised deed, pre-cleared with the relationship manager; the BCL quarterly BOP declaration is automatically generated by the bank from these remittance classifications.Outbound settlement flows use three clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for purchase price payments, capital distribution and fund disbursements; (ii) SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for interbank cover payments; and (iii) commercial or standby letters of credit (MT700 under UCP 600/ISP98) for post-closing price adjustments, warranty collateral or vendor securities. EUR payments clear through TARGET2-Luxembourg or SEPA Instant for EEA counterparties; USD and GBP leg settlement occurs through correspondent banking chains. Every disbursement requires a transaction purpose code in field 70 referencing the underlying acquisition agreement, the BOP classification (equity sell-down, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayment), and the tax clearing certificate where withholding obligations apply. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File required if group consolidated revenue ≥ €50 million and related-party transactions exceed €1 million; CbC report required if group revenue ≥ €750 million

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for certain activities (banking, insurance, distribution, service centers, financing, leasing, holding companies); PE SPVs must demonstrate substance (office, employees, decision-making) to avoid being classified as shell and to benefit from tax treaty and EU directives

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 or 10% of evaded tax; criminal prosecution for fraud; additional penalties up to 100% of tax due

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful evasion
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, participation exemption, and EU directives; reclassification of income; penalty up to €250,000; mandatory liquidation if deemed abusive

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly operated a shell; travel bans and asset freezes possible
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment)

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification from serving as directors

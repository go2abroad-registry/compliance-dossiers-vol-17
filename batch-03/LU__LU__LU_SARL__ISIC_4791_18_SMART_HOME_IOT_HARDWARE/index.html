Pre-Incorporation & Know-Your-Customer

UBO transparency, Apostille authentication chain, official name clearance restricted wordlist, and pre-incorporation statutory fee budget.

UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Law of 12 November 2004 on the fight against money laundering and terrorist financing, as amended, notably by the Law of 13 February 2018 transposing the 4th and 5th EU AML Directives; Law of 13 January 2019 and Law of 25 March 2020 establishing and governing the Luxembourg Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE); CSSF Regulation No. 12-02 of 14 December 2012 as amended; Grand-Ducal Regulation of 1 February 2018 on the identification of beneficial owners. Under these instruments, direct or indirect ownership of more than 25% of shares, voting rights, or control by other means constitutes beneficial ownership. A Luxembourg notary and the opening credit institution must conduct full KYC/CDD on the shareholders, managers, and ultimate beneficial owners before incorporation, and the entity must file its UBO particulars with the RBE within one month of incorporation.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office consisting only of a PO Box or a non-existent physical location is not permissible. Luxembourg law requires the SARL to maintain a real and determinant registered office ('siège social') within the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg. For an e-commerce IoT hardware retail entity, the use of a licensed domiciliation agent ('bureau de domiciliation') with a physical Luxembourg address is accepted, provided the company keeps its statutory books, corporate records, and management accessibility at that address and does not create a mere letterbox arrangement. Banks and payment processors such as Stripe and Shopify Payments will ordinarily require a verifiable physical address and may independently verify the substance of the registered office; the Luxembourg tax administration may apply abuse-of-law and substance doctrines if the office is purely artificial.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) – Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), Luxembourg, with mandatory e-filing via the LBRonline portal.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended – Articles 180 et seq. (SARL regime); Law of 23 September 2020 on digitalisation of the Trade and Companies Register; Law of 14 December 2022 on modernisation of corporate law.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) executed before a Luxembourg civil-law notary and filed electronically with the RCS; legal effect against third parties upon publication in the Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed and fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 (each share minimum EUR 1.00); 100% of the subscribed capital must be deposited into a blocked account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before notarization; the deposit is released only upon production of the RCS registration certificate – no separate state escrow account applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Fire and Rescue Service (Service d'incendie et de secours – SIS) for the registered office and any storage site; structural load inspection certificate for warehouse racking systems; workplace occupational safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) confirming compliance with the Labour Code and the Law of 17 June 1994 on health and safety at work.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed and fully paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 (each share minimum EUR 1.00); 100% of the subscribed capital must be deposited into a blocked account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before notarization; the deposit is released only upon production of the RCS registration certificate – no separate state escrow account applies.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDTVA) for value added tax; unified official filing and management portal: MyGuichet.lu; employer/income tax e-filing portal: e-Tax (offer.public.lu).

E-government filing portal

For a resident SARL, the statutory business identification number (Numéro d'identification national, 'matricule') is issued automatically upon incorporation through the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); the matricule forms the basis of the Luxembourg VAT identification number (format 'LU' + 8 digits) and the corporate income tax reference, with subsequent notification from the ACD/AEDTVA requiring no separate application. For non-resident foreign entities, the SOP is: (1) submit a VAT registration application to the AEDTVA (electronically via MyGuichet.lu or on prescribed paper form) attaching the RCS/foreign registry extract, evidence of an economic presence or a Luxembourg permanent establishment where required, and, when the foreign entity has no establishment in Luxembourg but performs taxable distance sales or imports into Luxembourg, documentation appointing a Luxembourg fiscal representative or confirmation of eligibility under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme; (2) upon approval, receive the LU VAT identification number enabling EC Sales Listing, Intrastat filings, and import VAT accounting; (3) where a non-resident company holds a Luxembourg permanent establishment, additionally register with the ACD via a declaration of permanent establishment and appoint a local tax representative for corporate income tax and municipal business tax filings. Resident SARLs are automatically tax-registered; no further TIN procurement step is necessary beyond RCS formalities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg operates a classical entity-level corporate tax system with no US-style check-the-box election. Available elections and options include: (1) election for fiscal unity (tax consolidation) among a Luxembourg resident parent and its 95%-or-more held Luxembourg subsidiary companies (Article 164bis LIR), permitting offset of profits and losses within the group; (2) participation exemption election under Article 166 LIR exempting qualifying dividend income and capital gains; (3) intellectual property box election under Article 50quater LIR providing an 80% exemption on qualifying net IP income; (4) ATAD hybrid mismatch elections and the associated transitional grandfathering positions for legacy hybrid instruments; (5) optional renunciation of the small-business VAT franchise so as to remain in the normal VAT regime; and (6) election to constitute a VAT group (assujetti unique) among closely linked Luxembourg taxable persons.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small-business franchise exemption; mandatory VAT registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or upon voluntary waiver of the franchise. Foreign suppliers making intra-EU distance sales of IoT hardware into Luxembourg must register once cross-border distance sales to non-taxable customers exceed the EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold, except where the destination-country OSS registration is used. Imports of IoT hardware from non-EU countries into Luxembourg trigger import VAT registration and declaration obligations regardless of the franchised turnover threshold. Luxembourg applies the EU EUR 100,000 distance-sales threshold for sales from other EU member states where the seller does not possess an establishment in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available for: (1) exports of goods — including smart-home IoT hardware — physically transported out of Luxembourg to a destination outside the European Union (Article 43 Loi TVA), provided the export is supported by conclusive proof of dispatch/transport and the goods leave the EU within the statutory 90-day period from the supply; (2) intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number, where the supplier fulfils the EC Sales Listing and recapitulative statement duties (Article 42 Loi TVA); and (3) services directly connected with exported goods and supplied to non-EU recipients where the place of supply is outside the EU. For IoT hardware sold via Stripe or Shopify Payments, the 0% rate is denied unless per-parcel evidence documents the physical exit of each consignment: carrier tracking data, delivery address outside the EU, customs export declaration receipts, and sales contract/payment documentation matching the exporter of record.

  • Luxembourg Customs Export Declaration filed through the national electronic customs platform (NCTS / Single Administrative Document), evidencing acceptance and exit certification from the customs authority
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note showing transport of the goods from Luxembourg to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank MT103/payment-rail settlement records (Stripe/Shopify Payments payout logs) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the SARL's Luxembourg bank account
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract/purchase order identifying the exporter, foreign buyer, incoterms, and product description
  • Customs exit confirmation message or equivalent proof of physical exit of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Warehouse dispatch records, inventory movement report, and authorised warehousekeeper certificate where export stock is held in Luxembourg

Luxembourg imposes no general real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital-stamping regime on ordinary B2B/B2C invoicing. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies to B2G contracts: suppliers must transmit structured e-invoices (EN 16931 / PEPPOL UBL or CII format) to Luxembourg public contracting authorities through a certified PEPPOL access point; there is no central government clearance API. For B2B and B2C invoices, electronic issuance is lawful subject to buyer consent and to guarantees preserving authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility; the AEDTVA recognises standardised e-invoicing formats, summary invoicing, and archival within Luxembourg or EEA cloud storage. No advance authorisation or digital stamp is required for VAT invoice numbering or for application of the 0% export rate.

The AEDTVA subjects export zero-rating claims to rigorous document-based audit, routinely cross-checking the customs exit certification against transport contracts, payment receipts, and warehouse records within a three-year assessment window, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate concealment. Failure to maintain or produce the mandatory substantiation triggers reassessment of output VAT at the 17% standard rate plus statutory default interest, and any input VAT recovered in reliance on the zero-rated export is clawed back. Administrative fines ranging from EUR 750 to EUR 1,500 per false or incomplete declaration are imposed under the Loi TVA penalty provisions. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, falsified export documentation, or fictitious export schemes constitute criminal tax fraud, punishable by substantial monetary penalties, custodial sentences under Luxembourg criminal law, forfeiture of export/Oss registration privileges, customs enforcement action, and enhanced exchange-of-information scrutiny under EU and OECD mutual assistance instruments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised under Articles 63-66 TFEU. There are no exchange-control approvals, quantitative limits, licensing regimes or convertibility barriers for trade settlements, dividends, loan repayments or equity capital flows. Profit remittance by a Luxembourg SARL is unrestricted, subject only to the 1915 Companies Law distribution/balance-sheet test, corporate tax filings and EU sanctions screening. Residual friction is limited to AML/KYC documentation (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended; CSSF Regulation 12-02), the beneficial-owner register (Law of 13 January 2019) and sanctions-related due diligence on non-EEA counterparty banks. Invoicing may be freely denominated in EUR or any other convertible currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation window or mandatory conversion ratio for export/trading receipts. The SARL may freely retain EUR, USD or other currency balances in Luxembourg or EU bank accounts. For balance-of-payments statistics, each cross-border transfer must be tagged with the BCL/ECB purpose code; the servicing bank normally discharges this reporting obligation on behalf of the entity, but the BCL may require direct external-statistics reporting if annual cross-border payment flows are material or on specific request. No conversion obligation exists under any statutory timeline.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the Eurosystem and the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM), jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for AML/CFT and financial-sector supervision. No separate foreign-exchange administration exists in Luxembourg; exchange-rate policy is euro-area competence, and the BCL operates TARGET2-LUX. The Luxembourg Ministry of Finance's financial-sanctions unit enforces EU/UN asset freezes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium as a secondary treasury rail; do not use as primary bank. Luxembourg-licensed and EU-passported EMIs under PSD2 (EU 2015/2366) and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Qonto) offer multi-currency virtual IBANs and cheaper FX conversion for Stripe/Shopify USD and GBP collections. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL) and are safeguarded, not lent; (ii) Luxembourg Tier-1 banks may treat EMI inflows as higher-risk and request source confirmation; (iii) EMIs cannot provide credit, overdrafts or documentary trade finance; and (iv) EMI AML/CFT files do not satisfy the notary or the formation bank for inbound capital validation. Blueprint: hold 1-3 weeks of working liquidity in a regulated EMI for FX efficiency, sweep residual balances daily to the BIL/BCEE operating account, and keep all equity and trade-finance flows at the Tier-1 bank.

Non-resident UBOs or shareholders domiciled in third-country or higher-risk jurisdictions; bank demands source-of-wealth evidence.

Root cause: Non-resident UBOs or shareholders domiciled in third-country or higher-risk jurisdictions; bank demands source-of-wealth evidence.

Defense evidence: Passport and proof of residential address (<3 months), bank reference letter from the UBO's primary bank, CV and business-activity narrative, corporate group organigram, RBE extract from the Luxembourg register, notarised source-of-funds declaration, and audited financial statements of any corporate parent.
High-volume card-not-present receivables via Stripe and Shopify Payments, perceived as aggregated third-party funds.

Root cause: High-volume card-not-present receivables via Stripe and Shopify Payments, perceived as aggregated third-party funds.

Defense evidence: Stripe/Shopify merchant statements, PCI-DSS Attestation of Compliance (AOC) for the SARL's e-commerce environment, refund and chargeback policy with <0.5% chargeback ratio, logistics/delivery evidence (courier reports or bills of lading), 12-month revenue forecast, business plan, and a signed representation that platform payouts represent genuine retail sales only.
Payments to contract manufacturers in China or Hong Kong triggering sanctions screening and payee KYC hurdles.

Root cause: Payments to contract manufacturers in China or Hong Kong triggering sanctions screening and payee KYC hurdles.

Defense evidence: Commercial invoice, purchase order, Incoterms contract (EXW/FOB), transport documents (MAWB/house airway bill), supplier incorporation certificate and bank account certificate, EU consolidated sanctions-list screening result, completed supplier KYC questionnaire, and HS tariff classification for each IoT hardware SKU.
Initial capital subscription in cash or via third-party financing; bank blocks release of the formation escrow account.

Root cause: Initial capital subscription in cash or via third-party financing; bank blocks release of the formation escrow account.

Defense evidence: Notarised articles of association evidencing the subscribed capital (minimum EUR 12,394.68), deposit receipt issued by the notary or Caisse de Consignation, shareholder payment records, source-of-funds statements of each subscriber, and a written declaration under Article 710-13 of the 1915 Companies Law that the capital is fully paid in.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

At formation, the full SARL stated capital (minimum EUR 12,394.68) must be paid in cash into a blocked 'SARL en formation' escrow account at a Luxembourg credit institution or lodged with the notary before execution of the notarial deed; the bank releases the funds only upon RCS registration. Shareholder wires must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA if EEA) with the BPM6 purpose code 'equity - shares and other equity' (direct investment) and the company name, notary reference and subscription allocation in remittance information. The bank issues a release letter to the notary upon final credit. For subsequent capital increases, a notarised deed and RCS re-registration are required; each subscriber's wire must exactly match the notarial deed, the updated RBE extract and the UBO file. A dual-account structure is recommended: a zero-balance blocked capital account and a live EUR operating account, with all movements recorded in annual accounts to avoid Luxembourg CAMT inconsistencies.For non-EEA suppliers, settle via SWIFT MT103 (USD/CNY) with full remittance data including purpose code, purchase order and invoice number; for EEA suppliers, use SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03) or Instant SEPA through TARGET2-LUX. For large initial IoT hardware orders, open an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (UCP 600) via BIL/BCEE with 30-90 days tenor, or a Bank Payment Obligation under ICC URBPO; where advance payment is required, obtain a standby LC or advance-payment guarantee from the supplier's bank. For e-commerce operational flows, Stripe and Shopify Payments net consumer card transactions in EUR and payout to the principal IBAN; when USD/GBP wallets are enabled, direct funds to the bank's multi-currency sub-account or an EMI, then execute a same-day bank FX swap rather than accepting on-platform conversion spreads. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium as a secondary treasury rail; do not use as primary bank. Luxembourg-licensed and EU-passported EMIs under PSD2 (EU 2015/2366) and the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Qonto) offer multi-currency virtual IBANs and cheaper FX conversion for Stripe/Shopify USD and GBP collections. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are not covered by the Luxembourg deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDL) and are safeguarded, not lent; (ii) Luxembourg Tier-1 banks may treat EMI inflows as higher-risk and request source confirmation; (iii) EMIs cannot provide credit, overdrafts or documentary trade finance; and (iv) EMI AML/CFT files do not satisfy the notary or the formation bank for inbound capital validation. Blueprint: hold 1-3 weeks of working liquidity in a regulated EMI for FX efficiency, sweep residual balances daily to the BIL/BCEE operating account, and keep all equity and trade-finance flows at the Tier-1 bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% CIT + 7% solidarity surcharge on CIT + municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City); aggregate headline rate up to 24.94%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% at source, reduced to 0% for qualifying intra-EU subsidiaries under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; treaty rates generally 5%, 10% or 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750 million (consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent triggering mandatory Master File and Local File duties under EU administrative cooperation framework); otherwise documentation must still be contemporaneously maintained upon a specific request from Luxembourg tax authorities under Art. 56bis LIR.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to offshore centers, but substance must be present under the OECD/BEPS nexus approach, ATAD general anti-abuse rule, and the Luxembourg tax administration's substance doctrine; the SARL must prove real premises, employees, management presence and business activity in Luxembourg to be resident, avoid CFC/GAAR challenges and retain treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Reassessment with 100% penalty for bad faith, 10% late filing surcharge, monthly interest 0.6%, and criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg Criminal Code and direct tax law: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the tax underpaid; courts may order travel bans, seizure of passports and personal assets, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies for up to 10 years.
Artificial Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust the profit to arm's length, apply 25% administrative fine on the reassessment, and impose the EUR 1,000,000 CbCR penalty if documentation is missing.

Director personal liability: In cases of intentional transfer pricing misstatement, directors may be held personally accountable for the incremental assessed taxes and fines; an international arrest warrant or travel ban may be issued under a Grand-Ducal extradition process for serious tax fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under LOB/PPT rules, reclassification of the company as tax-transparent, disallowance of loss carry-forwards, and net wealth tax minimum assessments; criminal anti-shell fines up to EUR 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficiaries face piercing of the corporate veil under the principle of simulation: civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 1 year for knowingly misleading tax authorities under the Luxembourg Criminal Code.
AML and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: CSSF sanctions for AML violations: fines up to EUR 1 million or 5% of annual turnover for legal persons; confiscation of proceeds and enhancement of tax penalties to 200%.

Director personal liability: Individual liability of compliance officers and directors under the Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004: imprisonment up to 5 years, personal fines, and travel bans; passports may be withdrawn pending criminal proceedings.

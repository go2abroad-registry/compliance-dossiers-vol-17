Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permitted. Luxembourg law requires a SARL to have a registered office in the Grand Duchy but does not require the office to maintain physical operating assets. A licensed Luxembourg domiciliation agent may provide the registered seat and letterbox facility. For an ISIC 6311 real-time video matching and social telephony business, a virtual office is acceptable at the incorporation stage and for RCS, tax and UBO filing purposes. However, to sustain Luxembourg corporate tax residence and economic substance, the strategic management functions such as board decisions, IT administration, contractual negotiations and risk oversight must be demonstrably exercised in Luxembourg. A pure virtual letterbox without substance gives rise to profit allocation, transfer-pricing and general anti-abuse rule exposure.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Statutory minimum share capital: EUR 12,000 (approx. USD 13,200), fully subscribed at formation. Each cash share must be paid in at least 20% at subscription (minimum EUR 2,400 effective paid-up); in-kind contributions must be fully freed within five years. The capital is blocked in a Luxembourg bank account opened in the name of the 'société en formation' until the notary executes the deed; no statutory escrow agent is mandated, but the notary requires the bank's blocked-funds certificate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; unified electronic portal via Guichet.lu / MyGuichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first incorporate with the Luxembourg Business Register (RCSL) through a statutory corporate agent, obtaining the national matricule number. After incorporation, VAT registration is completed by filing Form A with the AEDT Bureau d'imposition TVA through MyGuichet.lu, disclosing the intended taxable activities, place of management, and estimated turnover. Non-resident non-established taxable persons may register directly with AEDT for a Luxembourg VAT identification number, subject to submission of proof of foreign corporate existence and identification of a fiscal representative within Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; an SARL is inherently a non-transparent company subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Available elective regimes include VAT group treatment under Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law, income tax fiscal unity under Article 164bis LIR for resident group companies, and the election to treat a civil partnership (SCSp) as transparent for tax purposes where appropriate construction is chosen. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by ATAD implementation and the Luxembourg anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual turnover for resident small-enterprise VAT exemption under Article 57 VAT Law; non-EU and non-established foreign suppliers have no general threshold and must register from the first taxable supply in Luxembourg unless covered by the EU One-Stop-Shop or reverse charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union, provided the supplier obtains and retains conclusive documentary proof of export within statutory deadlines. Export-related services and services supplied to non-resident taxable persons may be outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules; digital services to non-taxable customers outside the EU may also fall outside Luxembourg VAT. Goods exported from Luxembourg to EU Member States are taxable in the destination state rather than zero-rated in Luxembourg except where transitional distance-sale or OSS rules apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export System / ECS)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, rail waybill, or air waybill evidencing physical movement
  • Proof of exit from the European Union endorsed by customs authorities
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement confirming receipt of payment from abroad
  • VAT registration certificate of the foreign purchaser where relevant

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping exists in Luxembourg. B2B e-invoicing is not generally mandatory, but electronic invoicing on the PEPPOL network (PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0) is required for supplies to Luxembourg public authorities. Tax invoices must comply with Articles 68 and 69 VAT Law; the VAT return and listing are filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis depending on taxable turnover.

The AEDT scrutinizes zero-rating claims and may retrospectively clawback VAT where export documentation is missing, incomplete, or not obtained within the 90-day documentary deadline, adding default interest calculated at the legal rate. Deliberate disguise or misuse of export zero-rating constitutes tax fraud and attracts administrative fines under Article 68 of the General Tax Law and criminal sanctions under the Luxembourg Criminal Code. False documentation may also trigger joint liability of any customs agent and a five-year clawback period in normal cases, extended to ten years in the event of proven fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full currency convertibility as a euro-area member state. Capital account and current account transactions are unrestricted under EU Treaty free movement of capital provisions. Profit remittances are free of exchange controls, subject only to standard corporate tax compliance, withholding tax clearances, and AML/KYC verification at the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts in EUR or any freely convertible currency may be retained in any bank account. No reporting obligation applies to the per-transaction level except under EU statistical balance-of-payments reporting thresholds administered by the BCL.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) acts as the financial sector regulator, and Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EU-licensed or passporting electronic money institutions and payment institutions operating under a Luxembourg licence or valid EU passport. Low for offshore digital EMIs without CSSF licensing or EU passporting. Offshore EMIs cannot provide regulated payment services in Luxembourg; any such arrangement must be structured through a licensed local agent or sub-license, with full KYC/AML due diligence at the parent bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.80% aggregate headline rate for Luxembourg City (CIT 15% + solidarity/unemployment surcharge 7% of CIT = 1.05% + municipal business tax 6.75%; municipal business tax varies by commune, is deductible for CIT, so the effective combined burden is slightly below the headline; rate applicable to FY beginning 1 January 2025).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% final Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends paid by an SARL; reduced to 0% for qualifying distributions to EU/EEA parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% participation) and under certain DTAs; treaty rates generally 5% (10%+ capital/quota) or 15% (portfolio) where the recipient is a beneficial owner in a DTA state.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 30 June of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (fiscal year = calendar year for the SARL unless otherwise authorized); extension to 30 September is generally granted for e-filing, and to 31 December where a registered approved tax advisor (fiduciaire/cabinet d'avocats) files the return. Quarterly prepayments of CIT/municipal business tax fall due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December of the fiscal year; the remaining balance follows the final tax assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File (Form/annex records) must be prepared and filed with the tax declaration where intragroup transactions exceed the following thresholds per fiscal year: goods EUR 5,000,000; services EUR 1,000,000; financial transactions (loans, interest, guarantees) EUR 250,000; IP/royalty transactions EUR 250,000. Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least EUR 50,000,000; Luxembourg CbC reporting obligations mirror EU Directive 2016/881/EU for groups above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no ESR law of the UAE/offshore type, but powerful substance-based anti-abuse doctrines apply: (1) EU ATAD GAAR (Art. 6) and the 2019 tax-amnesty/circular practice recharacterize artificial arrangements lacking commercial justification; (2) treaty-benefit and participation-exemption claims require economic substance (local management, employees, decision-making, and bank accounts in Luxembourg); (3) the pending EU Unshell Directive (ATAD 3) indicators scrutinize 'letterbox' SARLs with passive income, nominee directors and no local premises; (4) for the video-match app, the ACD expects genuine content-moderation, sales, IP-administration and treasury functions to be performed in Luxembourg to support IP-box and PE-protective outcomes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full tax at the 22.80% combined rate; additional tax up to 100% of the evaded tax; 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; criminal fines (Criminal Code and Section 395 AO) of up to EUR 250,000 plus imprisonment of up to 5 years for controllers; confiscation of criminal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors/managers who signed the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and fines; Luxembourg criminal procedure permits travel bans (interdiction judiciaire de sortie du territoire) and provisional custody pending trial; a conviction automatically disqualifies the individual from managing a Luxembourg commercial company.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment restoring arm's-length income; secondary adjustment (imputed dividend or loan) with 15% WHT exposure; 0.6% monthly interest; negligence surcharge of up to 50% of the additional assessment; criminal escalation where the TP file is falsified.

Director personal liability: Managers approving the pricing are personally liable vis-à-vis the SARL for damages under company-law standards (faute de gestion) and face criminal liability if the documentation is knowingly fraudulent; ACD officials may refer the file to the State Prosecutor (Parquet).
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse (Lack of Substance)

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization of the SARL as a sham, denial of treaty benefits and of the participation exemption/IP-box, disallowance of deductible payments (with reclassification as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT), retroactive reassessment, and administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 for procedural non-compliance; possible compulsory strike-off from the RCSL.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors are jointly liable for taxes re-assessed due to the arrangement; criminal sanctions follow if the shell was used to commit fraud; directors may be ordered by the commercial court to pay the company's tax liabilities as civil damages and may be subject to exit visits/restrictions while under investigation.
AML / UBO Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the AML Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended) up to EUR 250,000 for legal persons; RBE registration failure penalized at EUR 125-EUR 250,000 per infraction; criminal sanctions for money laundering (Criminal Code Art. 506-1 et seq.) with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines for legal persons up to EUR 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer may be criminally prosecuted where willful blindness to suspicious flows is established; Luxembourg courts have imposed personal fines, seizure of assets, travel bans and disqualification orders against non-compliant managers; directors remain liable for the SARL's AML fines unless they prove delegation to a suitable and independent compliance function.
Withholding Tax and Constructive Dividend Failure

Statutory sanction: The SARL as the withholding agent is liable for the full un-withheld 15% WHT, plus 0.6% monthly interest and a withholding penalty of up to 10% of the amount; the recipient shareholder's treaty/PSD claim is lost if the statutory formalities were not met.

Director personal liability: The manager who caused the distribution without WHT filing is personally liable to the Treasury for the un-collected WHT (recourse of the company's liability); prosecution is possible for fraudulent reduction of movable assets subject to WHT; disqualification from office follows an unsatisfied tax liability declaration.

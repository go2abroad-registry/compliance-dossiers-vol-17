Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The S.à r.l. requires minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be deposited before execution of the deed in a blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution in the name of the company to be formed; each subscriber must pay up at least 25% of the nominal value of the subscribed cash shares at subscription, and contributions in kind must be fully paid up immediately. The blocked account may be released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the CGDIS fire and rescue service, including cold-room escape-door and fire-alarm compliance; structural load clearance by a certified engineer for heavy racking and carcass storage; occupational health and workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) covering low-temperature work, permitted working hours, emergency rescue protocols and worker welfare in cold environments.

Paid-in capital rule: The S.à r.l. requires minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000. Cash contributions must be deposited before execution of the deed in a blocked account with a Luxembourg credit institution in the name of the company to be formed; each subscriber must pay up at least 25% of the nominal value of the subscribed cash shares at subscription, and contributions in kind must be fully paid up immediately. The blocked account may be released only upon RCS registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member and applies full convertibility under Articles 63–66 TFEU on both capital and current accounts. Profit remittance and dividend distribution require no prior exchange-control approval, but are conditioned on corporate-law solvency tests, audited accounts where statutory thresholds are met, and applicable withholding tax documentation. Payments to sanctioned counterparts or to persons on FATF high-risk lists may be frozen under EU restrictive measures without a convertibility issue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory foreign-currency surrender, no minimum conversion percentage, and no holding-period restriction on export receipts. All settlements can be received in EUR through SEPA, in EUR by SWIFT, or in any freely convertible currency. Euro-denominated cash deposits of €10,000 or more trigger CSSF AML/CTF reporting, and all transactions are subject to EU sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); prudential supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), and the Ministry of Finance sets exchange-control policy; no separate foreign-exchange inspectorate exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary receiving/payout rail for small-value transactions, but Low as the primary treasury rail. Legal constraints under PSD2 and Luxembourg law of 21 July 2010: any non-EU/offshore digital EMI must be authorized or passport into Luxembourg through the CSSF; unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot issue EU-registered IBANs, cannot access BCL settlement systems, cannot legally operate corporate current accounts for a Luxembourg SARL, and are unacceptable as counterparties for trade finance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The shareholder injects equity via SWIFT MT103/MT202 using purpose code S010 (capital subscription) and must cite the notary's escrow account reference and the SARL's VAT/EORI identifiers. The receiving Luxembourg credit institution will treat the transfer as a capital inflow subject to standard AML due diligence; a copy of the notarised incorporation deed and a board resolution approving the capital call must accompany settlement. There is no exchange-control recognition required, but the bank must verify the remitter's authority and the final UBO layer.For procurement of frozen beef and seafood, the standard clearing rails are: (1) irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued by the Luxembourg relationship bank via SWIFT MT700, with a confirming bank in the exporter's jurisdiction and reimbursement through MT103/MT202; (2) direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer against a pro-forma invoice and sanitary certificate; (3) SEPA credit transfer (SCT/SDD) for EU counterparties; and (4) domiciliated direct debit to the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Administration for the payment of import duties and VAT. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% Corporate Income Tax (IRC) for FY 2024; plus 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC (1.19%) and communal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at the Luxembourg City rate of 6.75%, producing an aggregate headline burden of 24.94% for a Luxembourg City SARL.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard domestic rate on dividends paid by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders; 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; treaty rates are commonly 5% for shareholdings of at least 10% and 15% otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

31 December of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (e.g., FY 2024 return due 31 December 2025); taxpayers represented by an approved tax adviser receive an automatic extension; quarterly advance payments fall due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory EUR threshold for Local File or Master File in Luxembourg domestic law; OECD-aligned TP documentation under Article 56 LIR and Circular LIR n° 56/1 must be produced within 30 days of an ACD request. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. The Form 100 e-filing includes a cross-border related-party transaction disclosure schedule applicable where aggregate related-party flows exceed EUR 100,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; substance requirements derive from tax residence (Article 159 LIR: registered seat plus central administration or effective management), the GAAR codified under ATAD, Luxembourg's EU Code of Conduct Group commitments, and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. For a meat-importing SARL, the ACD expects local premises, operational management decisions, cold-chain or logistics capability, and payroll substance to support treaty access and the participation exemption; letterbox distribution companies are re-characterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative majoration of 10% of the evaded tax plus late-payment interest; additional fine of up to 100% of evaded tax; criminal sanctions of imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and/or fines up to EUR 25,000 under the Luxembourg General Tax Code; confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers who sign false returns face joint civil liability for evaded tax, personal criminal fines, asset seizure, travel bans, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence certificate, revocation of participation exemption, GAAR re-allocation of profits, loss of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, reassessment with 10% surcharge and 0.3% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Nominated directors of substance-less companies become personally liable for resulting tax liabilities if they knowingly signed declarations of conformity; regulatory removal by the Luxembourg Business Registers and potential criminal prosecution for aiding abusive arrangements.

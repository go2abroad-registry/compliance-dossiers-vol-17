Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) for income taxes, and Administration de l'Enregistrement, des Domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; electronic portal: guichet.lu

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a fixed establishment in Luxembourg must apply for VAT registration on guichet.lu using Form 104 VAT. The entity must provide constitutional documents, proof of foreign VAT number, identity of the legal representative, and a description of taxable activities. If the entity has no establishment in Luxembourg and no local representative, the AEDT may require appointment of a fiscal representative authorized to receive tax assessments. The application must be submitted before any taxable supply is made in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity without a fixed establishment in Luxembourg must apply for VAT registration on guichet.lu using Form 104 VAT. The entity must provide constitutional documents, proof of foreign VAT number, identity of the legal representative, and a description of taxable activities. If the entity has no establishment in Luxembourg and no local representative, the AEDT may require appointment of a fiscal representative authorized to receive tax assessments. The application must be submitted before any taxable supply is made in Luxembourg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A SARL is inherently subject to entity-level corporate income tax. Available statutory elections include: VAT group registration (assujetti unique), fiscal unity for corporate income tax purposes in respect of 95%+ owned resident subsidiaries, and group loss relief elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD must be considered for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the small enterprise exemption; however, non-established suppliers generally cannot rely on this exemption and must register from the first euro of taxable supply unless an EU one-stop-shop or special scheme applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to the sale and dispatch of goods to destinations outside the EU, provided the supplier obtains and preserves documentary evidence that the goods left EU territory within the legally prescribed period, generally within three months of the supply. Offshore services provided to a recipient established outside the EU qualify for zero rating under Luxembourg VAT law where the place-of-supply rules confirm that the services are deemed supplied outside the Luxembourg/EU territory. The e-commerce MRO activity must maintain a complete export evidence file, including the export customs declaration and transport documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD) validated by Luxembourg Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR international consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing FX receipt
  • Proof of customs entry or delivery acknowledgement in the destination country
  • Evidence of non-EU customer status or export order confirmation

Luxembourg mandates PEPPOL-compliant electronic invoicing for all invoices issued to central and local public authorities. There is no general real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no mandatory API-based clearance for B2B invoices, and no digital stamping regime. B2B suppliers must ensure authenticity, integrity, and legibility of electronic invoices and retain them in VAT-compliant form.

Fraudulent or substantively defective export zero-rating claims are disallowed by the AEDT, resulting in reassessment of VAT at the standard rate, default interest from the date the VAT was originally due, and administrative fines which may reach EUR 25,000 for serious violations. Deliberate non-compliance may expose the taxpayer to criminal penalties under Luxembourg tax law, including imprisonment for individual officers. The AEDT conducts targeted audits of export claims and requires retention of the full documentary bundle for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates under the euro and the EU framework of free movement of capital (Articles 63-65 TFEU, EU Capital Movement Directive). Both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no administrative FX license or prior authorization. Profit remittance and dividend distributions require no exchange-control approval; the only structural conditions are standard corporate law formalities: annual account filing, availability of legally distributable reserves, dividend withholding tax at 15% (subject to domestic exemptions or treaty relief), and submission of a Luxembourg tax residency certificate where beneficial ownership is asserted. No currency window, repatriation, rationing, or compulsory conversion constraints exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export receipts to the banking system and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency earned on exports. A Luxembourg SARL may freely retain export receivables in EUR, USD, GBP, or any other convertible currency in domestic or EU-licensed bank accounts, provided the account is reportable for balance of payments statistics on a monthly or quarterly basis to the BCL.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) jointly with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking conduct, prudential supervision and anti-money laundering compliance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU-licensed Fintech and EMI providers, but Low for offshore digital EMIs. A Luxembourg SARL may open treasury accounts with CSSF-authorized payment institutions and electronic money institutions that hold an EU passport, yet those entities cannot take deposits and lack Luxembourg deposit guarantee coverage. EMIs are acceptable as secondary payment collection rails for B2B customers, but are not viable for capital escrow, trade LC issuance, or the primary cross-border clearing spine. Offshore EMIs based in Switzerland, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Hong Kong, or the United States cannot passport into Luxembourg under CSSF rules and present prohibited restrictions on accepting repayable funds, thus causing significant legal and liquidity risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injections are executed as SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfers from the investing parent to the SARL's euro-denominated operational or escrow account at a Luxembourg tier-1 financial institution. No central bank approval is required, but the receiving Luxembourg bank will need the reference to the corporate registration number (RCSL), the capital increase deed, and the subscription statement. The bank will apply the BCL external statistics reporting code for direct investments; for EU cross-border transfers this is captured under the BCL monthly external position reporting, with the SARL commonly asked to confirm the economic nature of the payment in the purpose field: 'Capital subscription / equity injection'. The settlement rail is TARGET2 with instant euro finality.Supplier payments are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor wire transfers, or through SWIFT MT700 for commercial letters of credit issued in favor of foreign MRO suppliers under UCP600. For EU counterparties, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers and TARGET2 are the standard rails. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 per tax year; Master File is required for Luxembourg group entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million; extended documentation triggers also apply for financing or royalty-rich structures above EUR 100,000 of intragroup interest/royalty flow

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation in Luxembourg, but the general anti-abuse provisions and OECD/ATAD case law require substance: physical premises, qualified full-time employees, and decision-making in Luxembourg; pure shell structures are denied treaty benefits and DTA relief; IP-heavy traders must show active management, dept of R&D, and risk-bearing capacity under the Luxembourg patent box rules

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €25,000 and imprisonment from 1 to 5 years; administrative assessment of the entire hidden tax plus late payment interest; penalties up to 10% of the deficient tax per late month; confiscation of the benefit and potential publication of the court decision

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or implement the fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the concealed tax, penalties, and interest; travel bans and freezing of personal assets may be issued; criminal conviction is not dischargeable in bankruptcy and can lead to disqualification from corporate management for up to 10 years
Shell Company and Sham Business Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of corporate personality for tax purposes under Luxembourg GAAR; disallowance of treaty relief and EU directive exemptions; recharacterization of the SARL as a transparent entity or disposition by the tax administration; 5% to 25% correction penalties on re-assessed taxable profits

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable to third-party creditors under the doctrine of abuse of legal personality; personal restitution for unlawful distributions; court-ordered liquidation and legal action by the Luxembourg Public Prosecutor; criminal liability for fraud where intent is shown
AML / KYC Failure in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of worldwide turnover under the Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004; temporary suspension or revocation of the payment service licence; administrative seizure of funds; regulatory censure by the CSSF/CRF

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches where they failed to establish adequate controls; civil damages to defrauded clients; criminal sanctions including imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering offences; travel bans and extradition possibilities under Luxembourg legal cooperation
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Unjustified Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with resulting tax plus late interest; penalty up to 25% of the under-assessed taxable income; in severe cases, the adjustment is combined with a 50% fraud penalty; denial of deduction for absent documentation where unable to demonstrate arm's length nature

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation knowing it is false commit criminal tax fraud; joint and several liability for the additional corporate tax and penalties; prohibition from serving as director in a Luxembourg commercial company; imprisonment for repeated breaches; personal recourse orders by creditors in insolvency

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) - Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 180-190-1 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Register of Commerce and Companies; EU AML framework as transposed.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation and articles of association (acte constitutif and statuts), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL minimum subscribed capital is EUR 12,000; at least 25% of the capital must be paid in on incorporation and held in a blocked Luxembourg bank account until the RCS certificate is issued; cash contributions may remain callable thereafter subject to the Company Law capital maintenance rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) for storage areas and retail dispatch premises; occupational health inspection by Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM); structural load inspection for shelving and mezzanines; public liability and employer liability insurance coverage confirmed.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct income taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes; unified digital taxpayer portal: MyGuichet and guichet.lu; corporate registry: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR/RCS) via eJustice.

E-government filing portal

For the SARL: (1) execute the notarial deed of incorporation with minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 fully subscribed and paid up; (2) file for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through the eJustice/LBR electronic platform, which automatically issues the national business matricule (numéro d'identification) and transmits data to the ACD and AEDT; (3) the matricule functions as the tax identification number (TIN) for income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes; (4) for VAT, submit the VAT registration application (Form 100) to the AEDT Service de la TVA via MyGuichet, which issues a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix plus eight digits; (5) foreign/non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a local establishment must register directly for VAT online; non-EU suppliers must appoint an approved Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT unless covered by the EU OSS/IOSS or electronic services simplification; (6) activate the MyGuichet business account for electronic filing of VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and audited financial statements.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is a resident capital company (société de capitaux) fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) at 17% plus a 7% solidarity surcharge, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at rates varying by commune (typically 6.75%-11.2%, approx. 6.75% in Luxembourg City), and annual net wealth tax (0.5% on excess net wealth above EUR 500,000). Pass-through treatment is not available. Territorial_Scope: resident SARL is taxed on worldwide income, mitigated by the participation exemption (exemption of qualifying dividends under Article 166 LIR and of qualifying capital gains under Article 166bis LIR subject to a 10% shareholding or EUR 6 million acquisition price and 12-month holding period) and by the European Parent-Subsidiary Directive eliminating withholding tax on outbound profit distributions. Tax_Exempt_Zone: no general tax-free zone applies in Luxembourg. — For the SARL: (1) execute the notarial deed of incorporation with minimum share capital of EUR 12,000 fully subscribed and paid up; (2) file for registration with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS) through the eJustice/LBR electronic platform, which automatically issues the national business matricule (numéro d'identification) and transmits data to the ACD and AEDT; (3) the matricule functions as the tax identification number (TIN) for income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax purposes; (4) for VAT, submit the VAT registration application (Form 100) to the AEDT Service de la TVA via MyGuichet, which issues a separate Luxembourg VAT identification number with the 'LU' prefix plus eight digits; (5) foreign/non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a local establishment must register directly for VAT online; non-EU suppliers must appoint an approved Luxembourg fiscal representative for VAT unless covered by the EU OSS/IOSS or electronic services simplification; (6) activate the MyGuichet business account for electronic filing of VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and audited financial statements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is a resident capital company (société de capitaux) fully subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) at 17% plus a 7% solidarity surcharge, municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at rates varying by commune (typically 6.75%-11.2%, approx. 6.75% in Luxembourg City), and annual net wealth tax (0.5% on excess net wealth above EUR 500,000). Pass-through treatment is not available. Territorial_Scope: resident SARL is taxed on worldwide income, mitigated by the participation exemption (exemption of qualifying dividends under Article 166 LIR and of qualifying capital gains under Article 166bis LIR subject to a 10% shareholding or EUR 6 million acquisition price and 12-month holding period) and by the European Parent-Subsidiary Directive eliminating withholding tax on outbound profit distributions. Tax_Exempt_Zone: no general tax-free zone applies in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg law. Available elective regimes: (1) fiscal unity / group taxation election under Article 164bis LIR for EU-resident groups with a 95% direct or indirect holding between a Luxembourg parent and Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries or Luxembourg permanent establishments of EU companies, enabling income and losses to be pooled; (2) election to apply the single taxable person (assujetti unique) VAT grouping regime under Article 60 of the VAT law for affiliated entities; (3) application of the participation exemption is made through the corporate income tax return (Form 400/410) with supporting evidence, not by separate filing; (4) option to apply the deemed interest deduction (notional interest deduction) on new equity capital in the tax return; (5) hybrid mismatch neutralization elections and adjustments are mandated under the ATAD Grand-Ducal regulations of 2019, with no elective opt-out for in-scope mismatches; (6) by-erogation transactions relief: an entity may apply for an advance tax ruling from the ACD (tax rulings commission) to confirm the tax classification of cross-border flows before implementing the structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for taxable persons established in Luxembourg: VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply (effective from inception for a Luxembourg-resident SARL). For intra-EU distance sales of goods (B2C e-commerce) to consumers in other Member States, the pan-EU threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; below this threshold, the supply remains subject to Luxembourg VAT; above it, the destination Member State's VAT applies via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS). For imports of low-value consignments (intrinsic value up to EUR 150), the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) regime is available. Non-resident suppliers have no de minimis threshold for registration where they make any taxable supply in Luxembourg without OSS coverage.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) is available for: (1) direct exports of goods by the SARL to customers outside the EU, where the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting on their behalf, with proof of physical exit from EU territory retained before the end of the reporting period deadline; (2) intra-EU B2B dispatches of goods where the SARL obtains the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, issues an accurate invoice, and reports the supply in the EC Sales List and Intrastat where thresholds apply; (3) certain services directly related to exported goods or supplied to non-EU business customers under the general place-of-supply rules (B2B services to non-EU recipients are outside Luxembourg VAT), which cover merchant fees and ancillary logistics services. B2C online retail exports to non-EU consumers qualify for 0% only where the shipment is accompanied by verifiable destination-country delivery evidence; shipments merely declared as export without independent transport confirmation are denied. Intra-EU B2C distance sales are not zero-rated but are taxable at destination and reported through OSS. The substantiation record must be kept for at least 10 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/electronic export declaration filed through Luxembourg Customs / ICS2) with the MRN (Movement Reference Number) for each shipment outside the EU
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or postal/courier export receipt showing the actual dispatch route
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract with clear identification of buyer and consignee
  • Proof of arrival at destination (delivery confirmation, signed POD, customs entry in the destination country, or courier tracking report)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment/foreign exchange receipt evidencing settlement of the export proceeds in the SARL's Luxembourg bank account
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and, where applicable, Intrastat declarations and EC Sales List references for intra-EU movements

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), API stamping, or digital stamping regime is currently in force in Luxembourg. E-invoicing (facturation électronique) is legally accepted where the authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are preserved through reliable audit trails. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to national public authorities via the PEPPOL network (national eFactura platform). Luxembourg has not yet passed domestic legislation mandating structured e-invoicing for all B2B transactions; however, under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) proposal, mandatory e-invoicing for intra-EU B2B/B2C transactions and related reporting is expected from 2028 (subject to final adoption), and the AEDT is preparing its ICT infrastructure accordingly.

The AEDT and Luxembourg Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims with particular attention to consumer-electronics e-commerce flows. Any failure to produce the mandatory customs documentation bundle, or any inconsistency in dates, volumes, or consignee identity, triggers retroactive reassessment at the standard 17% VAT rate, denial of the corresponding input VAT deduction, and collection of default interest on the under-declared VAT (statutory monthly late-payment interest accruing from the original due date until payment). Administrative fines range up to EUR 100,000 depending on the intent classification and the amount of tax evaded. Deliberate fraudulent schemes (forged customs declarations, insertion of fictitious export consignees, artificial reversal of circular transactions, or sham warehouse operations outside the EU) constitute criminal tax fraud under Luxembourg tax criminal law, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years and monetary penalties of up to EUR 250,000 per offence, plus the publication of the sanction, disqualification of managers, and clawback of all VAT exemptions and refunds previously granted for the audited period. The same documentation deficit can trigger cross-border customs duty recovery and notification to other Member States under VAT administrative cooperation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg operates no exchange-control statute. Both current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU); the euro is fully convertible. Interest, royalties, dividends and liquidation proceeds may be paid to any non-resident without BCL authorisation. The only practical hurdles to outward profit remittance are (i) documentary AML/CFT compliance under the Law of 12 November 2004 and CSSF Regulation 12-02, and (ii) Luxembourg dividend withholding tax at a 15% standard rate, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to lower treaty rates for treaty-protected investors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory repatriation timeline or obligatory conversion ratio applies in Luxembourg. The LU SARL may retain USD proceeds derived from Stripe and PayPal settlements indefinitely and convert them at its commercial discretion. Statutory friction is limited to balance-of-payments statistical reporting: Luxembourg-based payment service providers must report to the BCL, under Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 as implemented by BCL circular instructions, each cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 with an economic-purpose classification; no conversion of export receipts is mandated by law.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), the Eurosystem national central bank for the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg; prudential supervision of banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions is delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), while the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises ultimate monetary and foreign-reserve policy authority for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Luxembourg recognises e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorised by the CSSF under PSD2 (Law of 1 August 2018 on payment services and e-money, Directive (EU) 2015/2366), and EMIs established elsewhere in the EEA may passport their services into Luxembourg. Legal constraints: an EMI may not take deposits or grant commercial loans; client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts at an EU credit institution or be covered by equivalent insurance; and direct TARGET2 access is generally unavailable, requiring an intermediary banking partner. For the SARL's treasury, a CSSF-licensed or passported EMI can optimise currency conversion and accelerate the Settlement of Stripe and PayPal payouts, but core clearing, documentary-credit issuance and capital-escrow functionality require a full commercial-banking relationship at BCEE, BIL, BNP Paribas Luxembourg, Société Générale Bank & Trust, ING or Raiffeisen. The recommended structure therefore uses an EMI as a secondary liquidity accelerator, not as the primary clearing rail.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque shareholder chain or non-EU beneficial owners

Root cause: Opaque shareholder chain or non-EU beneficial owners

Defense evidence: Notarised chain-of-title organisational chart; Luxembourg RCS extracts (extrait du registre de commerce et des sociétés); updated register of shareholders; passport and proof-of-residence for every UBO holding 25% or more; source-of-wealth memorandum for each UBO; independent legal opinion if any corporate intermediate is incorporated in a high-risk jurisdiction; the complete trace of all funding wires.
Unverifiable source of the SARL's initial capital subscription

Root cause: Unverifiable source of the SARL's initial capital subscription

Defense evidence: Notarised deed of incorporation and subscription; bank confirmation of the exact capital deposit; certified bank statements of the contributing shareholders; board resolution approving the capital call; where contributed by a parent entity, the parent's audited financial statements and a formal intra-group contribution or loan agreement; RCS-filed UBO declaration.
High-frequency micro-payment flows and chargeback volatility from Stripe/PayPal aggregation

Root cause: High-frequency micro-payment flows and chargeback volatility from Stripe/PayPal aggregation

Defense evidence: Signed merchant agreements with Stripe and PayPal; 12-month projected settlement and chargeback model; documented transaction-monitoring policy aligned with CSSF Regulation 12-02; e-commerce refund and dispute-resolution procedure; daily reconciliation of aggregator settlement files against the SARL's accounting ledger.
Sanctions, PEP or negative-news screening hits on counterparties, UBOs or beneficial owners

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP or negative-news screening hits on counterparties, UBOs or beneficial owners

Defense evidence: Official sanctions-screening output from EU consolidated lists, UN lists and OFAC SDN where applicable; negative-news search report; sworn non-PEP declarations; for identified PEPs, submission to the bank's senior-management approval with an enhanced-monitoring plan under Article 5 of the Law of 12 November 2004.
Trade-diversion and grey-market risk inherent to consumer-electronics retail

Root cause: Trade-diversion and grey-market risk inherent to consumer-electronics retail

Defense evidence: Supplier onboarding file with invoices, Incoterms and HS codes (8471 automatic data-processing machines, 8517 telephone sets); distributor and end-customer country-screening; customs-broker compliance letters for export shipments; audit-ready record of each high-value shipment to justify the low-risk e-commerce classification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's capital-escrow or operational IBAN at the Luxembourg credit institution, with an ISO 20022 purpose code CAPA (capital payment) or EQUI (equity-related flow) and remittance text restricted to the corporate name, RCS number and reference to the notarised deed. The receiving bank verifies the originator against the filed UBO and shareholder register and records the flow in the BCL balance-of-payments statistical return; funds are released to the operational account after the notarial deed and applicable confirmations are sighted. For intra-group shareholder loans, the route instead uses purpose code LEND and must be supported by a written intercompany loan agreement to avoid recharacterisation by the Luxembourg tax administration; third-party contributions in cryptocurrency or from non-bank channels are not accepted by clearing banks.EU supplier payments are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) to the supplier's IBAN; extra-EU supplier invoices are settled by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers; high-value supplier contracts and cross-border import transactions above the bank's threshold are supported by commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP 600, issued by BCEE, BIL or BNP Paribas Luxembourg with the SARL's operational account as applicant. Daily proceeds from Stripe and PayPal are swept by the aggregators' acquiring banks via SEPA or SWIFT to the SARL's operational account with descriptive settlement files; the SARL then applies its liquidity policy by maintaining a chargeback buffer in EUR and converting surplus USD through the relationship bank's FX desk. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% national corporate income tax, plus municipal business tax (average 6.75%) and solidarity surcharge (7% of national CIT), yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 24.94% (2024).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% domestic Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions to shareholders, unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (condition: 10% shareholding, 12-month holding period, no abusive or artificial arrangement), or under Luxembourg participation exemption rules when the receiving EU parent is in comparable form.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed by the fifth month following the end of the fiscal year (e.g., 31 May for calendar-year taxpayers). Extensions may be granted upon proof of underlying financial statements; no automatic extension beyond 6-12 months without application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File arise when (i) consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 750 million, and (ii) the Luxembourg entity has cross-border related-party transactions whose aggregate value exceeds EUR 150,000; a simplified three columns report is sufficient below such threshold. Benchmarks: EUR 150,000 (aggregate related-party transactions).

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg has no standalone FDI/ESR regime, but the NPR (national legislation) imposes a general anti-abuse rule (Steueroasen-Abgabeverordnung, section 6 AbgO) and OECD Pillar Two GloBE requirements assume substance. The EU ATAD incorporates a substance test for treaty and participation claims; a SARL in digital retail must maintain actual senior management, employees, decision-making, office premises, and operational risk controlling outsourced logistics or warehousing. Local substance thresholds for TP documentation vary, but any inconsistency between substance and profit allocation is an immediate enforcement target.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine from EUR 25,000 to EUR 1,000,000; administrative re-assessment with a 10% tax addition and 0.6% monthly interest; confiscation of goods and trading bans. Repeat offenders may have tax returns voided and assets attached.

Director personal liability: Directors and de-facto managers face personal civil restitution for lost taxes; Luxembourg courts may order travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment of up to 10 years for fraudulent bankruptcy-aggravated evasion, and manager ban from corporate boards for 5 years.
Transfer Pricing / Substance Abnormalities

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to arm's length with a 20% surcharge (taxe additionnelle); interest at 0.6% monthly; denial of participation exemption and of EU Parent-Secondary Directive benefits; reclassification of service fees as deemed dividends or hidden distributions subject to 15% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors signing an upwards adjustment waiver or knowingly executing a value-draining IP royalty scheme become jointly liable for the additional CIT, municipal business tax, net wealth tax and WHT; can be ordered to repay sums to the corporate estate in civil proceedings, and a criminal migration ban is possible.
Abusive Shell/Holding of Bank Account / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Dissolution of the SARL (striking-off) with a penalty of EUR 10,000–100,000 under Luxembourg AML law (Loi du 12 novembre 2004, as amended); the beneficial owner is reported to the FIU (CRF); account frozen and seizure powers available; directors are subject to administrative fines of EUR 5,000–500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign bank attestations of untruthful BO declarations expose themselves to personal criminal liability for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment) and personal civil liability for the sums laundered; they may be prohibited from travelling to the EU under Schengen-wide judicial co-operation frameworks.
Incorrect Dividends / Unjustified Participation Exemption

Statutory sanction: Retroactive WHT at 15% plus 50% penalty for abusive deduction; disallowance of the EU directive benefits; interest from actual payment date. Luxembourg tax authorities may request compulsory audit and seizure of bank guarantees.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial-owner forms or misleading holding certificates are personally liable for the unpaid WHT, sanction surcharge and interest; Luxembourg case law has allowed criminal recovery against directors for restitution of the tax loss, with imprisonment of up to 5 years.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA – AEDT) for VAT; Direct Tax Administration (Administration des contributions directes – ACD) for corporate income tax; single digital portal: MyGuichet.lu and guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity that is not established in Luxembourg but makes taxable supplies of dangerous-goods forwarding services must obtain a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA) from AEDT via the 'Demande en vue d'obtenir un numéro d'immatriculation TVA' (Form 710). The application must indicate a Luxembourg tax representative if the entity has no permanent establishment and no fixed establishment. Supporting documents include notarized/chamber-certified certificate of incorporation, statute, board resolution appointing the representative, a completed NACE/ISIC classification (5229 corroborating DG forwarding), and evidence of the cross-border transaction structure. For a resident SARL, the RCS (Luxembourg Business Registers) matricule number serves as the national tax ID; the ACD assigns a corporate tax file number on incorporation. The VAT number is issued in the format LU12345678 and conferred immediately upon registration; approval generally takes 5–15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg-resident SARL is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide profits; branch/PE of a non-resident is taxed on Luxembourg-source income; no territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg-resident SARL is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (CIT) on worldwide profits; branch/PE of a non-resident is taxed on Luxembourg-source income; no territorial exemption or tax-exempt zone applies. For VAT, the SARL is a taxable person under Article 4 of the VAT Law; the DG forwarding activity is an established business in Luxembourg.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime is available. Luxembourg permits a group VAT registration (unification de la TVA) under Article 61 of the VAT Law, allowing related taxable persons to file as a single VAT person. For direct tax, a resident SARL may elect fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) with a qualified Luxembourg parent under Article 164bis LIR for CIT/NAM. Hybrid Mismatch Elections: taxpayers must document and disclose hybrid mismatch arrangements under ATAD (Articles 60bis–60quater LIR); no elective exemption is permitted, but a 'reverse hybrid' treatment is automatically applied when the entity is treated as transparent in its U.S./foreign jurisdiction and the SARL is an EU resident company — note that Luxembourg is not a check-the-box jurisdiction, and entity classification for U.S. Federal purposes follows treasury regulations, not Luxembourg election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-resident suppliers that have a fixed establishment or that supply services subject to Luxembourg VAT at the place of supply; Luxembourg-established small businesses with annual turnover (excluding VAT) of EUR 30,000 or less qualify for the small-business exemption under Article 57 of the VAT Law and may be exempted from VAT registration. The DG forwarding contract value will almost always exceed this, and for dangerous-goods services VAT registration is mandatory irrespective of threshold because the customer is usually a taxable person with the place of supply in Luxembourg or another EU Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 42 of the VAT Law (exportation), provided the goods physically leave the EU and the exporter retains evidence of departure. Freight, packing, insurance, or forwarding services directly linked to exported goods are zero-rated under Article 46. Intra-Community dispatches to a taxable person in another EU Member State are exempt under Article 43 only if the supplier records the customer's validated EU VAT ID and the goods are transported to that state. For DG/Hazmat forwarding, the zero-rating defence requires a consignment-specific dangerous goods declaration (DGD) and transport document proving actual cross-border movement; if the service relates to goods stored or consumed in Luxembourg, Luxembourg VAT at 17% applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD – Single Administrative Document) filed via the EU Export System (ICS/ECS) including MRN; for dangerous goods, also the consolidated dangerous goods declaration and IMDG/IATA documentation.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document showing physical exit from the EU; for DG freight this must include UN numbers, Class labels, and proper shipping name.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / Letter of Credit evidence of payment received from the foreign buyer in convertible currency (where applicable; Luxembourg does not impose FX controls, but banks require underlying transaction documents).

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time electronic fiscal clearance, digital stamping, QR-code coding, or a continuous API-based clearance/reporting system for B2B VAT. Electronic invoicing is standard and legally valid under Article 14 of the VAT Law; invoices may be issued via PEPPOL, EDI, or signed PDF. For B2G (public-sector contracts), electronic invoicing via the national Peppol infrastructure is mandatory for invoices over EUR 3,000 (Grand-Ducal Regulation). Taxpayers must retain a SAF-T (Standard Audit File for Tax) export capability upon AEDT request; there is a statutory requirement to preserve invoices in original form (XML/PDF/EDI) and to grant access to fiscal auditors. In practice, DG freight contracts should include invoice lines with 0% VAT codes and the customer's EU VAT ID to substantiate intra-Community treatment.

Fraudulent or untrue zero-rating declarations trigger mandatory VAT repayment to the State plus default interest of 0.8% per month (or part thereof) from the VAT due date, administrative fines up to EUR 2,500 or, in serious cases, 10% of the evaded VAT under Article 211 of the VAT Law; if goods do not leave EU within the documented period, the zero-rating is retroactively cancelled and a 17% VAT assessment issued. Under the Customs Law (Code des douanes), false declarations are punishable by confiscation of goods, fines up to three times the evaded duties, and criminal penalties for smuggling; AEDT retains a five-year limitation period for assessments, extendable to ten years in cases of fraud or omission. For DG freight, document retention of at least ten years is mandatory for explosion and environmental safety records under national transport legislation, and the tax audit will reconcile the DG manifest with export declarations and freight receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a eurozone member and applies the full EU Treaty principle of free movement of capital. There are no exchange controls, no convertibility restrictions, and no prior authorisation for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred in EUR or any foreign currency, subject solely to standard corporate law formalities (annual account approval, distributable reserves) and to the reporting obligations under BCL Balance of Payments statistics. The domestic law, primarily the Luxembourg Law of 5 September 2016 on the free movement of persons and capital, confirms the absence of any administrative transfeison limitation. In practice, funds are converted via commercial banks with standard anti-money laundering (AML) checks, but there is no reliance on central bank approvals or perpetual exchange control hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules exist in Luxembourg or under EU law. Luxembourg does not require exporters to repatriate trade receivables within a specific timeline, nor to convert receivables into euro or any other currency. For statistical purposes, payments for exports of goods exceeding a certain threshold form part of the BCL's quarterly balance of payments data collection and must be reported by resident banks or, in certain circumstances, by the resident enterprise under the Grand-Ducal Decree of 23 October 2002 relating to external trade statistics. However, these reporting obligations do not create mandatory conversion ratios, holding period limits, or surrender duties. A dangerous-goods forwarder may therefore maintain cash flows in original invoicing currency and execute spot conversions only when operationally required.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for banking supervision and the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and euro-system legal framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low-to-Medium for a Luxembourg-domiciled SARL active in hazardous goods forwarding. Luxembourg recognises authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) supervised by the CSSF, as well as EU/EEA EMIs passporting under PSD2. Such institutions can be used narrowly for client this reason. For payments, however, major Luxembourg clearing and settlement infrastructure. For operational disbursements and receipt of trade proceeds, banks provide a broader range of clearing rails. The core impediment is not electronic money licensing per se but the high-risk classification of the ISIC 5229 / dangerous goods model, which multiply the obligational scrutiny of the EMI's own correspondent banking network. Most non-bank payment institutions terminate or heavily restrict onboarding because of the perceived sanctions and trade-based money laundering exposure. The use of an offshore digital EMI (outside EU / EEA) is generally commercially impractical because no Luxembourg private equity or lending counterparty will accept a non-bank offshore account as the primary treasury vehicle for statutory capital, LC issuance, or supplier guarantees, constraining liquidity and clearing efficiency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is normally routed through SWIFT MT103 payments denominated in EUR or major currencies from the counterparty bank to a Luxembourg credit institution where the SARL maintains an opening account. The reference must include an unambiguous sender-to-receiver or beneficiary reference matching the capital contribution and the entity's commercial unique code. For statutory equity injection, the bank applies the BCL Individual Balance of Payments (BoP) category code 7021 for direct investment equity, while remuneration for operational prepayments or inbound prepayments of freight charges are attributed to codes under trade in services (e.g., 619 for transport services). Verification protocol combines SWIFT network tracing, sender / receiver name matching, and a segregated inward debit advices used by the statutory auditor to certify the capital under the Luxembourg law. For crypto assets or funds routed via shadow clearing or stablecoin bridges, no credible Luxembourg bank will accept the inbound as proper capital or trade settlement; these are categorically rejected on AML grounds. All inbound flows must come from a bank account held in the name of the investor or customer and cannot be aggregated or remitted from opaque intermediary holding accounts without a full explanation and evidence of the underlying relationship. Inward funds subject to foreign exchange conversion must be matched against a proactive tenor-limit spot or forward in a designated corporate dealing account and cannot be placed through a spread-only fintech engine without a valid client-pass documentation.Outbound trade disbursements by the Luxembourg SARL will be executed through commercial bank clearing rails. Preferable for settlement with ocean / air carriers, customs authorities, and sub-contractors is the SWIFT MT103 direct correspondent clearing network, using the stated across-border BIC/IBAN. This supports wire transfers in EUR, USD, and other freely convertible currencies. For larger consents covering charters, consolidation, and COA (Contract of Affreightment) payments, or to harmonise delayed settlement conditions with suppliers, the commercial letter of credit (LC) is the principal clearing instrument, issued by the Luxembourg bank in favour of carriers and terminal operators. The bank will apply ICC UCP 600 and eUCP 2.0 rules, and documentation will include AWS and original transport documents. The company must establish separate subsidiary treasury operational policies so that outbound disbursements for freight and handling use reality but exact original invoice mapping; outlay for customs duties is made directly to the customs authority via designated account, never to a third-party broker unless under strong for services. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low-to-Medium for a Luxembourg-domiciled SARL active in hazardous goods forwarding. Luxembourg recognises authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) supervised by the CSSF, as well as EU/EEA EMIs passporting under PSD2. Such institutions can be used narrowly for client this reason. For payments, however, major Luxembourg clearing and settlement infrastructure. For operational disbursements and receipt of trade proceeds, banks provide a broader range of clearing rails. The core impediment is not electronic money licensing per se but the high-risk classification of the ISIC 5229 / dangerous goods model, which multiply the obligational scrutiny of the EMI's own correspondent banking network. Most non-bank payment institutions terminate or heavily restrict onboarding because of the perceived sanctions and trade-based money laundering exposure. The use of an offshore digital EMI (outside EU / EEA) is generally commercially impractical because no Luxembourg private equity or lending counterparty will accept a non-bank offshore account as the primary treasury vehicle for statutory capital, LC issuance, or supplier guarantees, constraining liquidity and clearing efficiency.

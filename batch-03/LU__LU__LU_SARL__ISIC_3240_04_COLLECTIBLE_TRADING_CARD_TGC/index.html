Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) — competent authority for Luxembourg VAT registration, VAT returns and indirect tax enforcement; Administration des Contributions Directes (ACD) — competent authority for Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; official digital portals: guichet.lu and MyGuichet.lu (business e-government single access point); electronic VAT filing and registration are processed through the AED e-filing module (eTVA).

E-government filing portal

The foreign investor forms the SARL before a Luxembourg notary, subscribes the fully paid minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, and files incorporation formalities with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS/LBR), which issues the business registration certificate, the RCS number and the national identification number. The SARL must then apply to the AED for VAT registration using Form 010/010D, filed electronically through MyGuichet.lu or the Entreprise Desk prior to commencing taxable activity; the AED issues the VAT identification number in the format LU + 8 digits. Non-established foreign suppliers without a Luxembourg permanent establishment must appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative and register directly with the AED, or alternatively use the Union OSS/IOSS simplification for distance sales and imports of low-value consignments. Social security registration (CCSS) and statistical registration (STATEC) follow concurrently. The VAT number must be quoted on all invoices, and the SARL must be entered in the Luxembourg VAT register before any deductible input VAT may be claimed.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is an opaque limited-liability company (société de capitaux) subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), for a combined headline CIT rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City. No pass-through, transparent treatment, or US-style check-the-box election is available for an SARL under Luxembourg domestic law. Resident SARLs are taxed on worldwide income where registered office and central administration are in Luxembourg; non-resident shareholders are not directly taxable on the SARL's profits until distributions occur, and dividend withholding rules apply separately. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is an opaque limited-liability company (société de capitaux) subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) and the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), for a combined headline CIT rate of approximately 24.94% in Luxembourg City. No pass-through, transparent treatment, or US-style check-the-box election is available for an SARL under Luxembourg domestic law. Resident SARLs are taxed on worldwide income where registered office and central administration are in Luxembourg; non-resident shareholders are not directly taxable on the SARL's profits until distributions occur, and dividend withholding rules apply separately. There is no tax-exempt free zone in Luxembourg; however, the participation exemption (100% exemption for qualifying dividends and capital gains) and the IP box (80% exemption for qualifying IP income) constitute preferential regimes requiring substance and holding-period compliance.

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elective classification regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 60 LTVAT for financially, economically and organisationally linked persons; (ii) waiver of the small-business VAT franchise so as to exercise input VAT deduction; (iii) corporate income tax consolidation election (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for groups with direct or indirect shareholdings of at least 95% in Luxembourg-resident subsidiaries; (iv) qualification for the participation-exemption regime subject to a 12-month holding period and substance conditions; (v) mandatory application of ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch rules (Law of 21 December 2017), which deny deductions for hybrid payments and contain no grandfathering or elective opt-out; (vi) ordinary regime elections such as the fixed-asset depreciation schedule and R&D tax credit options. Tax-exempt-zone elections do not exist in Luxembourg.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover registration threshold for Luxembourg-established taxable persons: any person carrying out an independent economic activity in Luxembourg must register for VAT before commencing activity, regardless of projected turnover. A domestic small-business franchise exemption (Article 57 LTVAT) applies to annual VAT-exclusive turnover not exceeding EUR 35,000, but a taxable person established as an SARL may elect to waive the franchise and register normally. For non-established EU suppliers, intra-community distance sales to Luxembourg trigger registration when annual distance sales exceed EUR 100,000, unless the Union OSS scheme is used; for non-EU suppliers, VAT registration is generally required from the first taxable supply unless IOSS applies to low-value imports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Articles 39 and 40 LTVAT. Direct exports of tangible collectible trading cards dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf qualify for the 0% rate (Article 39). Intra-EU dispatches to a customer holding a valid VAT identification number in another Member State qualify for zero-rating under Article 40, subject to EC sales-list reporting, transport evidence, and the customer's VAT number appearing on the invoice. Export-linked services (e.g., printing, finishing, packaging and logistics) are zero-rated where they are performed directly for an EU/non-EU counterparty in connection with the goods placed outside the EU or temporarily moved within the EU, subject to the AED's documentation criteria. The burden of proof of physical exit and economic substance rests on the exporter; failure to substantiate the zero-rating converts the supply into a domestic Luxembourg supply taxed at 17%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A) or Exit Summary Declaration lodged through the Luxembourg/European e-customs system
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing dispatch of the goods to a destination outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the collectible trading cards, quantities, unit values, Incoterms and export customs classification codes (ISIC 3240 / corresponding HS code)
  • Purchase order, supply contract and transport instruction chain evidencing the foreign customer relationship and arm's-length pricing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 transfer advice or credit confirmation evidencing settlement of the export invoice and foreign-currency repatriation
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) and Intrastat declarations, where the supply is an intra-EU dispatch rather than a direct export

Luxembourg currently enforces no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance regime for B2B transactions. Electronic invoicing to Luxembourg public-sector entities via the PEPPOL network is mandatory under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. B2B VAT invoices must be issued no later than the 15th day of the month following the taxable event and must contain the mandatory Article 57 LTVAT particulars; paper, PDF or structured EDI invoices are equally acceptable pending transposition of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package. Taxpayers must retain complete invoice trails, purchase ledgers, sales ledgers, and electronic audit files (SAF-T components) for AED inspection; future ViDA-derived e-invoicing and digital reporting obligations will apply upon national transposition.

The AED performs risk-based audit verification of zero-rated export claims, examining customs interfaces, transport contracts, terminal receipts, carrier statements and matching bank remittances. Where export status is not substantiated, the AED reassesses VAT at 17% with statutory monthly late interest, disallows the corresponding input VAT deduction and may impose administrative penalties on undeclared or misdeclared tax. Fraudulent export zero-rating — false customs declarations, fictitious transport documentation, circular payment schemes, or sham foreign customers — engages Articles 109 and 214 LTVAT and the Luxembourg Customs Code: criminal fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years in aggravated cases, joint and several liability of the gérant (managers), extension of the standard five-year assessment limitation period to ten years where fraud is established, retrospective clawback of all VAT and interest from the opening of the audit window, de-registration from VAT and OSS/IOSS schemes, and referral of money-laundering indicators to the Luxembourg Financial Intelligence Unit. Luxembourg imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day inward-remittance benchmark set in this dossier serves as the banking, transfer-pricing and audit evidentiary standard for demonstrating genuine export consideration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg franc was fully replaced by the euro (EUR) on 1 January 1999, and EUR is the sole legal tender; Luxembourg maintains complete current-account and capital-account convertibility, unimpeded by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, which guarantees free movement of capital between Member States and third countries. No exchange-control authorization, capital-export license, advance approval, or foreign-investment screening applies to a SARL's profit remittance, dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, intercompany treasury settlements, or the conversion of USD income into EUR. The only operational constraint is the standard documentary confirmation of the economic purpose of a transfer and, where relevant, BCL statistical reporting of cross-border direct-investment and intercompany financial flows for euro-area Balance of Payments (BoP) data under BCL Circular 2020/01 and related reporting instructions; the de minimis reporting threshold is generally EUR 12,500 per transaction, filed by the resident credit institution or corporate reporting unit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or foreign-currency holding restriction applies to a Luxembourg SARL. Invoicing for the collectible trading card business may be denominated freely in EUR, USD, or any other currency, and receipts may be retained in dedicated multi-currency operational accounts with eligible credit institutions. The only affirmative duties are: (i) accurate bookkeeping of trade receivables under Luxembourg GAAP or IFRS as elected; (ii) treatment of the proceeds for corporate income tax and VAT purposes at the calendar-year close; and (iii) submission of monthly/quarterly Intrastat and customs declarations for goods imported from and exported to EU and non-EU jurisdictions, administered by the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) and the Luxembourg Customs and Excise Agency (ADA).

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem, with prudential control of credit institutions delegated by statute to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) and the BCL jointly; the Luxembourg Ministry of Finance retains policy oversight of the anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing (AML/CFT) legal framework, principally the Law of 12 November 2004, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) is Medium for merchant-acquiring settlement (e.g., PayPal (Europe) S.a r.l., Mangopay S.A., or EU-passported EMIs), but Low for the SARL's primary operating account. A Luxembourg-regulated payment institution or electronic money institution may open the account remotely, expedite onboarding, and provide direct integration with card acquirers; however, an EMI cannot grant credit, has narrower treasury product coverage, lacks documentary trade finance capacity, and the group treasury must still hold its irrevocable operational liquidity in a fully licensed credit institution to satisfy counterparty risk and notarial attestations. Legal constraints: CSSF authorization or passport notification, client-money safeguarding rules under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services as amended, segregation of collected funds from the EMI's own insolvency estate, and absolute prohibition of credit extension by EMIs; accordingly, the recommended structure is a cross-border EMI for card settlement receiving rails (Stripe/Authorize.Net payouts) combined with a Tier-1 bank account for main cash concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for share-capital injection uses an irrevocable SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer where both banks are in SEPA) executed by the shareholder's bank, with instruction: beneficiary = 'LU_SARL', field 59 showing the exact legal name and IBAN (LU + 3-digit BIC of the receiving bank), field 72/remittance information containing 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION — LU_SARL — NOTARY DEED [date] — [notary file no.]', and the transaction classified as BOCODE 'S1.1 direct investment equity' in the BCL statistical declaration. The funds land in the blocked sub-account (compte d'attente / compte bloqué) bearing the notary's reference; the bank issues an attestation de blocage confirming the minimum EUR 12,000 is fully paid; the notary then draws on this attestation to execute the deed of incorporation, after which the bank converts the blocked account into an operational current account. For shareholder or intercompany loans, the inbound message must reference a signed intercompany loan agreement, bear the purpose code 'S1.4 direct investment intercompany loan', and, if cross-border and above the BCL threshold, be reported to the BCL BoP system; no central-bank approval is required, but the underlying documentation must be retained for the annual audit and CSSF AML files.The preferred clearing rails are, first, SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001 ISO 20022 XML) for routine EUR payments to EU suppliers of foil, ink, packaging and logistics; second, SWIFT MT103 for non-EU trades in EUR, USD or GBP, using the bank's correspondent network; third, irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) for large purchases from China or Hong Kong, with MT700 issuance, involvement of the finance department in checking the shipping, insurance, and quality certificates against the LC terms, and MT202 COVER for the reimbursement if cross-currency; fourth, documentary collections (DP/DA, MT400/MT422) for recurring mid-value orders; and fifth, settlement of Stripe/Authorize.Net consumer receivables by merchant-payout or SWIFT USD transfer into the SARL's multi-currency account, with same-day value via Fedwire/CHIPS and subsequent customer-facing conversion only where the designated settlement currency is EUR. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating for offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) is Medium for merchant-acquiring settlement (e.g., PayPal (Europe) S.a r.l., Mangopay S.A., or EU-passported EMIs), but Low for the SARL's primary operating account. A Luxembourg-regulated payment institution or electronic money institution may open the account remotely, expedite onboarding, and provide direct integration with card acquirers; however, an EMI cannot grant credit, has narrower treasury product coverage, lacks documentary trade finance capacity, and the group treasury must still hold its irrevocable operational liquidity in a fully licensed credit institution to satisfy counterparty risk and notarial attestations. Legal constraints: CSSF authorization or passport notification, client-money safeguarding rules under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services as amended, segregation of collected funds from the EMI's own insolvency estate, and absolute prohibition of credit extension by EMIs; accordingly, the recommended structure is a cross-border EMI for card settlement receiving rails (Stripe/Authorize.Net payouts) combined with a Tier-1 bank account for main cash concentration.

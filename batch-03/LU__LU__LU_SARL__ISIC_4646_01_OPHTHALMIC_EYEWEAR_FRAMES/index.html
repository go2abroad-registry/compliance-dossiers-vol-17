Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in-kind at incorporation. For cash contributions, the full amount must be deposited with a Luxembourg bank and evidenced by a banker's certificate; the funds remain blocked until the entity obtains RCS registration. No separate statutory escrow account is required beyond this blocked deposit mechanism.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety declaration for business premises, if open to the public or comprising a warehouse, under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 3 November 2008 on fire safety; structural load certification for storage racking; occupational workplace risk assessment and ITM registration under Articles L.312-1 et seq. of the Luxembourg Labor Code.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in-kind at incorporation. For cash contributions, the full amount must be deposited with a Luxembourg bank and evidenced by a banker's certificate; the funds remain blocked until the entity obtains RCS registration. No separate statutory escrow account is required beyond this blocked deposit mechanism.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current account. Luxembourg is part of the Eurosystem; the euro is fully convertible and no prior exchange-control authorization is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayment, or capital repatriation by an ordinary wholesale SARL. Dividends and profit distributions are subject only to Luxembourg corporate formalities: ordinary general meeting approval, the Article 710-6 balance-sheet/solvency test of the Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies, and CSSF AML/CFT payment screening. Foreign-currency receipts (USD, CNY) are freely depositable and convertible into EUR at the foreign-exchange desks of Luxembourg credit institutions without surrender obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg does not impose any statutory timeline for repatriation, liquidation, or conversion of export proceeds. An ISIC 4646 wholesale exporter is free to retain USD/CNY export receipts in multicurrency accounts for constructive matching of procurement payables. The only compliance obligation is the bank's periodic Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting to the BCL for cross-border transactions above applicable per-transaction thresholds; no mandatory conversion percentage applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration. EU free movement of capital and payments (Articles 63-66 TFEU) applies, with bank supervision and AML/CFT enforcement by the CSSF under the amended Law of 12 November 2004.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with strict limits. Luxembourg-licensed payment institutions and e-money institutions under the Law of 1 August 2018 (implementing PSD2) can open EUR IBAN accounts and execute SEPA, SEPA Instant, and SWIFT cross-border payments for low-value open-account disbursements (supplier prepayments, platform payouts, refunds). However, an EMI is neither a credit institution nor a provider of documentary trade finance; it cannot issue standby letters of credit, activate MT700 documentary credits, or process principal trade settlement flows for ISIC 4646 volumes without a sponsoring Tier-1 bank. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK/US fintech platforms not passporting under the CSSF) have low viability for principal flows due to correspondent banking friction, no Luxembourg RBE nexus, and the CSSF's territorial prudential scope; the blueprint therefore reserves platform rails for ancillary disbursements only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: the parent/shareholder remits the subscription proceeds by SWIFT MT103 in EUR (or USD converted at the receiving bank) to the SARL's blocked capital account (compte de consignation/attestation) opened at the Tier-1 Luxembourg bank prior to notarial incorporation. The MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) must state: 'Capital subscription – [SARL full name] – RCS number – shareholder identity'. The bank will apply the BCL Balance-of-Payments statistical classification for direct-investment equity and will release the block only upon the notary's attestation of receipt. The statutory minimum is €12,000 nominal capital, with at least 25% of each subscribed contribution paid up (€3,000 minimum in cash) at formation; after incorporation, additional injections are booked as capital increase or shareholder current-account loans and require a general meeting resolution and, for loans, a written advance agreement with arm's-length interest to prevent recharacterization as permanent equity. Funds from outside the EEA are further subject to standard correspondent-bank UBO verification, sanctions screening, and the CSSF's know-your-fund analysis; no exchange-control license is required.Open-account settlements for ISIC 4646 frame purchases are executed by SWIFT MT103 (country-wide payment) from the SARL's EUR or USD operational account, with the remittance information containing the commercial invoice number, the LC reference where applicable, and the purpose classification for foreign trade (import of goods, i.e., BPM6 goods category). EU suppliers are paid by SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT or SCT Inst) using beneficiary IBAN. For contractually secured trade, the bank issues documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700 and acts as reimbursing bank under MT740; confirmations by Asian seller-side banks are arranged to mitigate country and availability risk. Typical LC documentation for ophthalmic frames: commercial invoice, full-set original bill of lading or house air waybill, packing list, and beneficiary certificate of origin. Platform_Pay rails, where adopted, are restricted to low-value disbursements (sales commissions, catalogue design payments, customs-broker fees) through a regulated Luxembourg payment institution and are reconciled invoice-by-invoice against the enterprise treasury ledger.FX Forwards (EUR/USD and EUR/CNY) — vanilla deliverable forwards concluded under bilateral confirmations and, where applicable, an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement; permitted for hedging documented invoice and purchase-order exposures only.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — EUR/CNY and similar onshore-restricted pairs, permitted only through CSSF-licensed counterparts and subject to EMIR trade reporting and essential-hedging documentation.Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) — permitted for treasury balance-sheet hedging of USD receivables against CNY payables; must be assessed against EMIR clearing thresholds and non-cleared margin rules (Article 11 EMIR) if the counterparty exceeds relevant thresholds.FX Options and Zero-Cost Collar Structures (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) — permitted under a board-approved treasury mandate, treated as cash-flow hedges under IFRS 9 when matched against the expected wholesale sales and procurement pipeline, and subject to strict market-risk limits.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate statutory headline for a Luxembourg City SARL in FY2025 (17% CIT + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax; reduced 15% CIT applies to the first EUR 200,000 taxable income under LIR art. 174).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% statutory dividend withholding tax on SARL distributions to resident and non-resident shareholders (LIR art. 67); 0% applies where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions are met (10% holding, 12-month minimum, beneficiary ownership).

Section E1 - Non-deductible expenses Income taxes and communal business tax, 50% of representation costs, excessive interest limitation disallowance, late-payment surcharges, and non-commercial gifts. Same adjustment rules apply to PE results. Late filing: formal mise en demeure; administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 per return; where no return is supplied, ACD assessment based on estimated profits plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.) on overdue CIT; intentional concealment triggers criminal tax fraud sanctions (up to EUR 250,000 fine and imprisonment).
Section E2 - Tax-exempt income and deductions Participation exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains, IP box deduction, investment tax credits and EU-source income relieving. Treaty and EU directive claims require a valid foreign TRC and 12-month holding evidence. Late filing: formal mise en demeure; administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 per return; where no return is supplied, ACD assessment based on estimated profits plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.) on overdue CIT; intentional concealment triggers criminal tax fraud sanctions (up to EUR 250,000 fine and imprisonment).
Section F - Tax loss carry-forward (reports en avant) Application of prior-year losses against current taxable base. Carry-forward applies only to losses suffered by the Luxembourg tax entity. Late filing: formal mise en demeure; administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 per return; where no return is supplied, ACD assessment based on estimated profits plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.) on overdue CIT; intentional concealment triggers criminal tax fraud sanctions (up to EUR 250,000 fine and imprisonment).
Section G - Net taxable income Final taxable base before application of CIT, solidarity surcharge and municipal business tax rates. Final balance determines Luxembourg PE nexus and WHT computation. Late filing: formal mise en demeure; administrative fine of EUR 250 to EUR 2,500 per return; where no return is supplied, ACD assessment based on estimated profits plus late-payment interest of 0.6% per month (7.2% p.a.) on overdue CIT; intentional concealment triggers criminal tax fraud sanctions (up to EUR 250,000 fine and imprisonment).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory fixed EUR turnover threshold for Master File/Local File preparation in Luxembourg; in practice, an intercompany wholesale distributor such as the SARL must hold contemporaneous TP evidence on request by the ACD (typically within 30 days). Mandatory CbC notification and CbC Report apply where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; a low-volume optical frames distributor will ordinarily only maintain a simplified Local File for intra-group purchases and financing above EUR 100,000 per transaction.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a Cayman/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but imposes OECD-aligned and ATAD-compliant substance requirements: the Participation Exemption, EU Parent-Subsidiary benefits, treaty relief and IP box advantages require the SARL to demonstrate physical premises, qualified staff, real wholesale distribution functions, commercial decision-making power, banking substance and an absence of artificial conduit arrangements; the ACD applies LIR art. 6 (abuse of law), the GAAR inherited from ATAD and circulaire LIR 264/1 when testing holding and finance structures. A letter-box distributor is exposed to re-characterisation of its profit allocation, denial of treaty/DTA benefits and reclassification of payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Corporate Income Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud sanction: imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 250,000; administrative add-ons: penalty of 10% to 200% of the evaded CIT/municipal business tax, late-payment interest of 0.6% per month, and restitution of the shortfall; repeated or organised fraud increases penalties and triggers publication of the sanction.

Director personal liability: Directors, gérants and de facto managers who signed false declarations or ordered the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the evaded taxes; aggravated cases lead to personal arrest warrants, travel and Schengen exit bans, disqualification from office and imprisonment.
Shell / Letterbox Company and GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: ACD recharacterisation of taxable profits into the beneficial owner's main jurisdiction; denial of DTA/EU Directive benefits and of the participation exemption; reclassification of financing or service payments as disguised dividends triggering the 15% WHT; administrative fine for non-filing and restitution of all undue refunds.

Director personal liability: Directors may be found personally liable for the re-assessed CIT and surcharges where the letter-box arrangement was used to defraud third parties or the state; civil restitution claims, travel bans and disqualification measures follow in bankruptcy or insolvency of the SARL.
Transfer Pricing and Substance Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative re-pricing by the ACD with an upward adjustment of the taxable base, denial of deductibility for the disallowed margin, interest of 0.6% per month; fines up to EUR 250,000 for CbC non-compliance in relevant groups; publication of adjustments; prolonged non-compliance may be escalated to tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified transfer pricing documentation knowing it to be incomplete are jointly liable for the additional tax, withholding taxes and default interest; travel bans and imprisonment are available in cases of serious deliberate misconduct.
AML/CFT and UBO Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Law of 12 November 2004 as amended by the 2020 AML/CFT package: up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious institutional breaches; individual criminal penalty of up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine for intentional money laundering; automatic freezing and confiscation of the object of the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil confiscation orders, travel and banking prohibitions and accumulated director disqualification; knowing participation in a laundering scheme pierces the SARL's limitation of liability.

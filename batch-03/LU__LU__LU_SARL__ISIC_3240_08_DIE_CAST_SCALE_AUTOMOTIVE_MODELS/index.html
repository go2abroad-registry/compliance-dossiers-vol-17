Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de commerce et des sociétés - RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through the myRCS e-filing portal and the Guichet.lu single digital gateway.

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), as amended, notably Articles 1-9 (formation requirements), Title IV, Articles 179-192 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée); complemented by the Law of 19 December 2020 facilitating electronic filing and the Law of 13 January 2023 on the business registers.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) containing the articles of association (statuts), filed with the RCS by the Luxembourg notary and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 49 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000 (approximately USD 13,200), which must be fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be deposited and irrevocably blocked in a credit institution account in Luxembourg or with a Luxembourg branch of a foreign bank, evidenced by a banker's certificate (attestation bancaire de blocage) lodged with the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report. Funds are released only after RCS registration is confirmed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and clearance issued by the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS) including approved fire doors, extinguishing equipment, emergency exits, and an evacuation plan for the foundry floor; structural load and occupancy clearance from the competent municipal building authority (commune) under the modified Building Regulation of 26 May 2011 (ABRO/ABR); occupational health and safety clearance from the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM) covering machine guarding for die-casting presses, ventilation of fume and solvent areas, noise control measures below the 85 dB(A) exposure action value, provision of personal protective equipment for molten metal and ink handling, and medical surveillance of industrial employees; the ITM work permit (permis de travail santé et sécurité) must be obtained before commissioning of the production line.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital of EUR 12,000 (approximately USD 13,200), which must be fully subscribed and fully paid up at incorporation in cash or in kind. Cash contributions must be deposited and irrevocably blocked in a credit institution account in Luxembourg or with a Luxembourg branch of a foreign bank, evidenced by a banker's certificate (attestation bancaire de blocage) lodged with the notary; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report. Funds are released only after RCS registration is confirmed.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Ministère de l'Économie - Direction générale de l'industrie (Department of Industry) and Direction générale des classes moyennes (authorisation d'établissement); environmental supervision by the Administration de l'environnement; inspections by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM).

Category: Autorisation d'établissement - Establishment and Operating Permit for Industrial Manufacturing (covering zinc alloy die-casting, tampo printing, assembly and wholesale/retail trading of scale automotive models), issued by the Ministry of the Economy under the Law of 2 September 2011 on the regulation of access to artisan, commercial and industrial professions.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; both are accessible through the unified MyGuichet.lu taxpayer portal, with eTVA for VAT returns and EC Sales Lists, eCDF for corporate income tax and municipal business tax filings, and RCS-linked registration via guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), the SARL automatically receives its 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which serves as the direct tax ID. VAT registration is then completed separately with the AEDT through the eTVA portal on Form 563, resulting in an LU+8-digit VAT identification number. A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg must register for VAT from its first taxable supply, with EU suppliers registering directly and non-EU suppliers appointing a fiscal representative (responsable solidaire) unless the reverse charge or OSS regime covers the supply.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is a full resident corporate taxpayer with unlimited worldwide tax liability for corporate income tax (IRC at 17% plus 7% solidarity surcharge), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax; no pass-through treatment is available for a Luxembourg SARL. — Upon notarial incorporation and registration with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS), the SARL automatically receives its 13-digit national identification number (matricule), which serves as the direct tax ID. VAT registration is then completed separately with the AEDT through the eTVA portal on Form 563, resulting in an LU+8-digit VAT identification number. A foreign entity not established in Luxembourg must register for VAT from its first taxable supply, with EU suppliers registering directly and non-EU suppliers appointing a fiscal representative (responsable solidaire) unless the reverse charge or OSS regime covers the supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is a full resident corporate taxpayer with unlimited worldwide tax liability for corporate income tax (IRC at 17% plus 7% solidarity surcharge), municipal business tax (ICC) and net wealth tax; no pass-through treatment is available for a Luxembourg SARL.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Luxembourg; however, a SARL may elect fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 164bis LIR for subsidiaries at least 95% owned, join a VAT group under Article 60 LVD, and claim the participation exemption (10% shareholding or EUR 6 million acquisition cost plus a 12-month holding period) in its corporate income tax return. The passive Société de Participations Financières (SPF) regime is unavailable to an active die-casting manufacturer, and EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules apply mandatorily without elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis threshold applies: VAT registration is compulsory from the first euro for every person carrying out taxable supplies in Luxembourg. The only turnover-based relief is the Article 57 LVD small-business exemption (EUR 35,000 annual turnover) reserved exclusively for Luxembourg-established businesses. Cross-border B2C distance sales and electronically supplied services trigger Luxembourg registration or OSS filing once pan-Union turnover exceeds EUR 10,000; non-EU suppliers have no threshold and must register immediately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of zinc alloy die-cast scale models to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 53 LVD provided the goods physically leave the EU and the supplier substantiates that departure with transport documents within the customary audit benchmark of 90 days, extendable to 180 days for consolidated shipments. Intra-EU supplies qualify for zero-rating under Article 35 LVD when the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. Export-related logistics and ancillary services rendered to non-EU customers are likewise zero-rated. A Luxembourg-established supplier or a locally appointed fiscal representative is a prerequisite for recovering any associated input VAT through the refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD filed through the Luxembourg ECS/DEU export processing system)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing FX settlement of the export proceeds
  • Proof of Arrival in the destination market (customs-stamped delivery note or arrival certificate)
  • EC Sales List / VIES recapitulative statement containing the customer's valid EU VAT ID (for intra-EU supplies)
  • Intrastat Dispatch Declaration where applicable under the prevailing simplification threshold

Luxembourg imposes no mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or transaction-based clearance API as of the 2025 tax year; e-invoicing in EU-compliant formats is freely permitted. B2G invoices addressed to Luxembourg public administrations must be submitted in PEPPOL BIS format under EU Directive 2014/55/EU. VAT returns, VIES/EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations are filed electronically through the eTVA portal, and Luxembourg is preparing for the EU ViDA framework, which is expected to introduce mandatory e-invoicing and digital reporting in stages from the 2030s once adopted.

The ACD/AEDT may reassess a claimed zero-rating within the standard five-year limitation period, extended to ten years where VAT fraud is established, and claw back the output VAT at the 17% standard rate plus monthly default interest of 0.6%. Documentary insufficiency alone, such as missing export stamps, bills of lading or remittance certificates, triggers retrospective reversal of the zero-rating without the need to prove intent. Administrative fines may reach 100% of the evaded tax, and criminal tax fraud under Article 397 of the General Tax Law is punishable by fines of up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, with joint and several liability of management and any appointed fiscal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility under EU free movement of capital; no exchange controls or prior administrative authorizations apply to equity contributions, dividends, or trade-related payments; profit remittance is unrestricted subject to corporate formalities, bank-level sanctions screening, and anti-money laundering checks; the euro is the sole legal tender and all resident entities may transact in foreign currencies through licensed banks without central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or repatriation timeline exists; no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR applies; exporters may retain balances in foreign currency accounts at authorized Luxembourg credit institutions or route proceeds as commercially agreed, subject only to EU sanctions enforcement and KYC transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), with Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as supervisory authority for credit institutions and payment services

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating in Luxembourg under the passporting regime of Directive 2009/110/EC; Low to Medium for offshore non-EU EMIs because they generally require a Luxembourg or EEA branch license and cannot rely solely on remote cross-border arrangements; non-EEA digital EMIs without a local passport or agent are legally unable to provide resident corporate accounts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL, unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a parent holding at least 10% of share capital for 12 months, or reduced under an applicable double tax treaty (typically 5% to 15% for portfolio investors).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by 31 December of the following calendar year. For a fiscal year ending on another date, the return must be filed by 31 December of the next calendar year. Extensions must be expressly granted by the ACD.

Charges invoiced by foreign affiliates are added back unless the foreign affiliate provides original invoices, proof of actual services, and an arm's length analysis. Late filing triggers an ACD reminder followed by an administrative fine of EUR 100 to EUR 10,000, an automatic 10% increase of the final CIT assessment, and collection interest at 3% per annum. In cases of gross negligence or intentional concealment, civil penalties range from 30% to 300% of the additional tax, with criminal referral for tax fraud.
Box 300 Deductions: tax losses carried forward, participation exemption under Art. 166 LIR, IP box deduction under Art. 50bis LIR, notional interest deduction on eligible new equity, and investment tax credit. Dividends received from non-resident and resident subsidiaries are exempt only if the participation conditions are met; attach beneficial ownership declarations and TRCs for foreign-source income. Late filing triggers an ACD reminder followed by an administrative fine of EUR 100 to EUR 10,000, an automatic 10% increase of the final CIT assessment, and collection interest at 3% per annum. In cases of gross negligence or intentional concealment, civil penalties range from 30% to 300% of the additional tax, with criminal referral for tax fraud.
Box 400 Taxable income after municipal business tax allocation among operating municipalities; application of the aggregate statutory rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City. Non-resident shareholders without a Luxembourg permanent establishment have no Luxembourg-sourced manufacturing income from this box; any sales commission attributed to a Luxembourg dependent agent must be included. Late filing triggers an ACD reminder followed by an administrative fine of EUR 100 to EUR 10,000, an automatic 10% increase of the final CIT assessment, and collection interest at 3% per annum. In cases of gross negligence or intentional concealment, civil penalties range from 30% to 300% of the additional tax, with criminal referral for tax fraud.
Box 500 Credits and prepayments: foreign tax credit, dividend withholding tax credits, tax prepayments, balance due or refund. Non-resident beneficial owners attach Form 21 or equivalent TRC for withholding tax reclaims; a reimbursed withholding tax is itself treated as an additional dividend subject to CIT and municipal business tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CbC report mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. There is no separate statutory monetary threshold for the Local File/Master File, but the ACD expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any material related-party transaction upon request, generally to be submitted within 30 days of an audit demand.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime akin to offshore financial centres, but applies the OECD-aligned GAAR, ATAD substance-over-form jurisprudence, and permanent establishment case law. A Luxembourg SARL must demonstrate real registered office, local decision-making, adequate employees, and an actual business rationale. A letterbox entity using a shell structure to obtain treaty or EU directive benefits will be denied those benefits, and intragroup transactions may be recharacterized as hidden dividend distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Art. 393-394 LIR: fine of EUR 1,250,000 or five times the evaded tax; imprisonment of up to 10 years for aggravating circumstances; civil tax increase of 30% to 300%; seizure of bank accounts and company assets; compulsory dissolution in exceptional cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations face criminal conviction, imprisonment, civil restitution of evaded tax and interest, personal liability for corporate tax debts where corporate assets are insufficient, and travel bans or exit restrictions pending criminal tax proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Arm's length re-fixing by the ACD with upward adjustment of Luxembourg taxable income; 10% administrative increase; late-payment interest at 3% per annum; penalty of 30% to 50% of additional tax where records are missing; possible publication of tax arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly endorsed non-arm's length contracts are jointly and severally liable for the resulting additional CIT and interest; personal negligence claims under company law, professional disqualification, and criminal liability for false certification may follow.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence; denial of participation exemption and IP box; denial of treaty and EU directive benefits; recharacterization of payments as undisclosed dividends subject to 15% withholding tax; penalties and interest on reassessed tax; possible cancellation of the commercial register registration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be stripped of limited liability where the entity is a mere sham; courts may impose civil restitution for diverted funds, personal payment of unreported WHT, travel bans while fraud investigations are active, and imprisonment for abuse of company law.

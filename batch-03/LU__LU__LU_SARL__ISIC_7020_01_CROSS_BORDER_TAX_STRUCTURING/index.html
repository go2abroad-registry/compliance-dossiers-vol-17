Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for value added tax. Primary electronic portals: impotsdirects.public.lu, guichet.lu, and MyGuichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL is automatically issued a national business registration number (NIF / matricule) upon incorporation via the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). The SARL must then apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the AEDT using the VAT registration declaration (Formulaire 1041/C), submitted online through MyGuichet.lu together with the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, RCS extract, proof of the legal representative's authority, and, where applicable, a copy of the client engagement letters substantiating cross-border services. For a foreign entity supplying taxable services in Luxembourg without an establishment, a Luxembourg VAT ID is obtained from the AEDT by the same electronic route; no local legal establishment is required, but a fiscal representative may be mandated for non-EU residents.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Luxembourg SARL is automatically issued a national business registration number (NIF / matricule) upon incorporation via the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) and registration with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). The SARL must then apply separately for a Luxembourg VAT identification number with the AEDT using the VAT registration declaration (Formulaire 1041/C), submitted online through MyGuichet.lu together with the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, RCS extract, proof of the legal representative's authority, and, where applicable, a copy of the client engagement letters substantiating cross-border services. For a foreign entity supplying taxable services in Luxembourg without an establishment, a Luxembourg VAT ID is obtained from the AEDT by the same electronic route; no local legal establishment is required, but a fiscal representative may be mandated for non-EU residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box or multi-entity classification election. A SARL is statutorily opaque and fully subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités) and municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), with no look-through treatment for Luxembourg resident owners. Limited statutory optionality exists under the ATAD implementation law of 28 December 2018 to elect hybrid-mismatch safe-harbor alignment, the group 75% participation-exemption regime, and the notional interest deduction on new equity. A Luxembourg permanent establishment may elect to compute its branch taxable base according to treaty-relevant allocation principles if consistent with the Luxembourg-Non-Resident tax treaty and OECD transfer pricing guidelines.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises under the simplified small-business exemption; no threshold applies for non-resident suppliers not established in Luxembourg, who must register for VAT as soon as they make any taxable supply of goods or services in or from Luxembourg, subject to place-of-supply rules and reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Luxembourg SARL providing cross-border tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services may benefit from an effective 0% VAT treatment when the service is supplied to a taxable business customer established outside the European Union. This follows the B2B place-of-supply rule (Luxembourg VAT Act Article 34, transposing EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC Article 44), under which the supply is outside the Luxembourg territorial scope. For goods exported outside the EU, zero-rating applies at 0% if the SARL evidences dispatch from Luxembourg within 90 days and holds proof of transport out of the EU. Services ancillary to exported goods, transfer of licences, and financial facilitation services to non-EU counterparties are also outside the Luxembourg VAT net provided contractual and payment flows substantiate the non-EU recipient status.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Luxembourg has no real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-based reporting, or mandatory digital stamping regime as of the current year. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices through the national Peppol-based e-Lux platform (issued via MyGuichet). Domestic B2B invoicing may continue in paper or digital form, but structured formats aligned with EN 16931 are recommended. Luxembourg is expected to implement mandatory EU-wide B2B e-invoicing and digital real-time reporting upon adoption of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, projected by calendar year 2028.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow zero-rating or outside-scope treatment if the exporter cannot produce credible evidence of physical exit of goods or of the non-EU establishment of the service recipient. Penalties and consequences include: (i) reassessment of VAT at 17% on the full invoice value; (ii) late-payment interest at 5% for the first month and 1% for each additional month, capped at a total of 20%; (iii) administrative fines of up to EUR 12,500 for incorrect invoicing and up to double the evaded tax in cases of fraudulent intent; (iv) joint and several liability of the SARL's directors and designated VAT representative; and (v) a clawback prescription window of 5 years, extended to 8 years where concealment, fraud, or intentional obstruction is established. Export refund claims are routinely selected for risk-based documentary audits by the AEDT customs unit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full capital and current account convertibility as a eurozone member state. There are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, or capital, provided that standard tax, AML/KYC, and statistical reporting obligations are satisfied. Foreign-owned SARLs may remit dividends and liquidation proceeds freely via the TARGET2 payment system, subject to beneficial ownership documentation and applicable withholding tax formalities under Luxembourg domestic law and tax treaties. No prior central bank approval is required for cross-border transfers, but intra-EU and third-country payments must be reported for balance-of-payments statistics under BCL Circular 2015 and related CSSF regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no statutory surrender requirements on export receipts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed timeline for repatriation, and no obligation to sell foreign currency earned from exports to the BCL or any authorized bank. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency accounts at Luxembourg credit institutions or maintain balances abroad, subject to OECD transfer pricing rules and the arm's length principle for related-party transactions. For tax purposes, export revenues are taxable in Luxembourg to the extent attributable to the Luxembourg entity under the territoriality rules of the Luxembourg income tax law (LIR), as refined by case law and administrative practice.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Legally feasible only through Luxembourg-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) or EU-passported EMIs registered with the CSSF. The viability rating is Medium for operational liquidity platforms and Low for capital escrow functions. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs are generally not acceptable for SARL corporate account opening because they lack a local or EU banking license, cannot clear through TARGET2, and trigger heightened AML scrutiny. Luxembourg-regulated EMIs such as PayPal Luxembourg, S.A. (if appropriately licensed) or other CSSF-passported players may be used solely for ancillary payment collection, not for substantial treasury holdings. Any use must be supplementary to a credit institution headquartered in the EU.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank account to the SARL's euro-denominated account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The wire must be titled 'Paid-in capital contribution' or 'Share capital deposit' and include the SARL's name and LEI (549300LUX) to facilitate reconciliation. The payment purpose code should be selected as 'Share capital injection into a non-financial corporation' under the ECB's Balance of Payments reporting (BOP) framework; in practice the bank will map this to code T110 or similar under the BCL's reporting schema. The contribution must be supported by (i) a notarized deed of incorporation or minutes of the extraordinary general meeting resolving the capital increase, (ii) a statement from the registered auditor or expert confirming the consideration is fully paid up, (iii) proof of the shareholder's identity and source of funds, and (iv) a bank certificate confirming receipt and crediting to the restricted capital escrow account, if applicable. The BCL statistical report must be filed by the receiving bank within the mandatory timeliness window (normally five business days). For injection in currency other than EUR, conversion must be executed at the prevailing ECB reference rate or a bank-offered spot rate, with conversion documentation retained for AML and tax purposes.Outbound trade payments are settled primarily through SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro-denominated low-value flows within SEPA. For high-value or third-country trade settlements, an MT103 with full cover or an MT202COV may be used. Commercial letters of credit (LCs) can be opened through the Luxembourg bank's trade finance desk, using the UCP 600 rules; documentary collections are cleared via SWIFT MT798/MT799 sequences. For consulting services, the disbursement is typically an invoice settlement via MT103 or SEPA CI (Credit Transfer) with explicit reference to the invoice number, ISIC 7020 service description, and VAT treatment. The bank will require the underlying service contract, invoice, and AML/KYC documentation for the payee; payment purpose codes must align with the EU Balance of Payments statistic categories. Any disbursement above EUR 50,000 is subject to enhanced due diligence. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate corporate tax rate in Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 7% unemployment surcharge on the CIT + 6.75% municipal business tax); in other municipalities the CIT plus unemployment surcharge is 18.19%, plus municipal business tax ranging from 6.75% to 10.50%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends paid by a Luxembourg resident company to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% or 25% participation test) or under an applicable DTA (often 0% or 5%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers, the completed tax return must be filed by 31 December of the year following the tax year; fiscal-year taxpayers must file within five months after the close of the fiscal year unless an extension is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local/Master File monetary threshold; TP documentation must be maintained upon request under OECD TPG and Luxembourg administrative circulars (LIR 56/1-56/2). Mandatory CbC report for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000; the local file is an expected part of any TP audit of related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a Gulf-style ESR regime; instead, the general anti-abuse rule (abuse of law) and beneficial-ownership requirements make economic substance (local employees, premises, strategic decision-making in Luxembourg) an essential prerequisite for treaty relief, participation exemption, and tax residency certificates. Passive holding, financing and IP companies are audited for substance, especially in hybrid mismatch and interest/royalty flow structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal Fiscal Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 125,000 (or up to double the evaded tax in certain circumstances) plus imprisonment from 1 month to 5 years; the tax administration may also impose an increase of up to 50% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted and personally fined or imprisoned; courts may impose a travel ban; the director can be required to pay the evaded tax, late interest and principal penalties from personal assets under the theory of faulty representation.
Failure to File / Late Filing of Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine from EUR 100 to EUR 25,000; default assessment may be issued using estimated income; late payment interest runs at 0.8% per month on the assessed tax.

Director personal liability: The director may be held personally liable for the tax if the company's non-compliance resulted from a clear personal intentional breach; in egregious cases a travel ban may be issued during criminal investigation.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the Luxembourg entity for tax purposes, recharacterisation of income, denial of treaty benefits, standard reassessment plus interest; civil penalties up to 25% of the additional tax (or 50% when evasion is proven).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly established or operated a conduit without substance may be personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies and can be barred from acting as statutory managers by the competent civil/commercial court.
AML / Corporate Licence Violation

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the operating licence; fines under anti-money-laundering law up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal entities; criminal sanctions including imprisonment for the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors face fines, imprisonment, and disqualification from managing Luxembourg companies; travel bans can be imposed pending AML proceedings; the court may order confiscation of illegally obtained advisory fees.

Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, 'RCS'), administered under the District Court of Luxembourg through the Luxembourg Business Registers ('LBR') electronic e-filing platform.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (notably by the Law of 23 September 2020 modernizing the SARL); SARL regime under Articles 710-1 to 710-23, particularly Articles 710-3 (minimum capital), 710-8 (subscription and payment of shares), and 710-11 (management), together with the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and Company Accounting.

Formation / constitutional filings

Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif) comprising the coordinated Articles of Association (Statuts coordonnés), filed via RCS electronic Form F1, followed by mandatory publication in the official electronic journal (Recueil Électronique des Sociétés et Associations, 'RESA').

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital, fully paid in cash and deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before incorporation; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's certificate from a réviseur d'entreprises; the blocked funds are released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office requires a municipal fire-safety conformity statement under the Grand-Ducal Regulation on fire protection; structural load clearance is limited to standard office-use certification in the building safety file. Workplace clearance and occupational health compliance are audited by the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM) under the Luxembourg Labor Code, including electrical installation checks and ergonomic workstation assessments; no heavy industrial civil-defense permits apply.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000 minimum subscribed capital, fully paid in cash and deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a Luxembourg credit institution before incorporation; in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's certificate from a réviseur d'entreprises; the blocked funds are released only after RCS registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
At a constitutive meeting held in Luxembourg City on [date], the founding shareholders resolved: (1) the share capital of EUR 12,000, divided into 1,000 shares of EUR 12, is fully subscribed and paid; (2) the Articles are adopted and the registered office is fixed in the Commune of Luxembourg; (3) [Name] is appointed as Manager (Gérant), with sole authority to open bank accounts, execute the lease, and hire staff; (4) the Manager is authorised to complete the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register filing and the RESA publication; and (5) the EdTech language-learning business under ISIC 6201 and the Apple IAP/Google Play subscription model are approved.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable; no sector-specific industrial licensing board mandates apply to pure software and interactive language-learning services. The general business establishment permit is governed by the Ministère de l'Économie (Direction générale de l'industrie), while the trade registry and employment oversight rest with the RCS and the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM).

Category: No sectoral operating or manufacturing permit required; only the general establishment permit (autorisation d'établissement) for the Manager and standard RCS/company-law registrations are applicable to the EdTech subscription activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified filing via MyGuichet.lu portal and electronic filing services (eCDF).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated LU SARL, the notary files the incorporation deed with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), which triggers issuance of a national business identification number (matricule). The entity must file a registration form with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or upon the first taxable transaction. Non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies register directly through MyGuichet.lu; a fiscal representative may be required unless the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident company subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; worldwide income taxable with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through election for SARL). — For a newly incorporated LU SARL, the notary files the incorporation deed with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), which triggers issuance of a national business identification number (matricule). The entity must file a registration form with the AEDT to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 35,000 or upon the first taxable transaction. Non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies register directly through MyGuichet.lu; a fiscal representative may be required unless the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident company subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax; worldwide income taxable with foreign tax credit relief; no pass-through election for SARL).

Available election forms

VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Art. 60 LTV; fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Art. 164bis LIR for corporate income tax and municipal business tax requiring a 98% plus participation; optional EU OSS/non-Union OSS scheme for digital services and distance sales; no check-the-box regime under Luxembourg direct tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for small-business exemption (Art. 57 LTV), except for certain supply categories; EUR 10,000 annual turnover threshold for pan-EU distance sales and digital B2C services subject to the One-Stop-Shop; no mandatory registration threshold for intra-Community acquisitions other than the EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to dispatches of goods to destinations outside the EU (Art. 52 LTV) and to services directly linked to export transactions. B2B services to non-EU recipients are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT under the place-of-supply rules (Art. 53 LTV); B2C digital services to non-EU customers likewise fall outside Luxembourg VAT. Zero-rating requires proof of physical export (or direct link for services) and receipt of payment from the non-resident customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (with DAE reference number) or electronic export declaration lodged with Luxembourg/other EU customs authority
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document evidencing physical movement of goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or foreign-exchange confirmation showing payment received from the non-resident buyer)

No nationwide real-time fiscal clearance or pre-clearance API mandate. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via the PEPPOL network (since 1 March 2019) for invoices to public entities. B2B/B2C e-invoicing is optional; accepted formats include Factur-X, UBL, and CII; digital signatures or EDI controls must be implemented to guarantee authenticity and integrity of invoices.

Luxembourg tax authorities may disallow zero-rating if substantiation documents are missing, incomplete, late, or contradictory, triggering reversed VAT assessment at 17% plus default interest of 0.6% per month. Negligent non-compliance may attract fines of 10%–20% of the tax at stake; deliberate fraud or forgery in export substantiation exposes the taxpayer to penalties up to 100% of the evaded VAT and criminal prosecution under Art. 65–69 of the Luxembourg VAT Law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Luxembourg has no exchange-control legislation restricting inbound capital injection, outbound trade disbursements, dividend distribution, interest payments, or liquidation proceeds. Free movement of capital is constitutionally embedded via Articles 63-66 TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements; no prior licensing, notification, or surrender approval is required for EdTech subscription royalties, Apple IAP / Google Play Billing receivables, or intercompany settlements. Profit-repatriation frictions are limited to tax friction only: (i) 15% Luxembourg withholding tax on dividends (reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU for qualifying parent entities, or reduced under bilateral double-tax treaties); (ii) 15% withholding tax on interest on profit-sharing loans (non-applicable to arm's-length ordinary shareholder debt); and (iii) statutorily stronger substance expectations for holding/treasury functions under Luxembourg domestic substance rules (Law of 21 December 2017 and circulars), not as a capital-control measure. No restrictions apply to conversion of USD-subscription proceeds into EUR at market rates; banks apply standard FX spread with no spread cap other than market transparency rules under MIFIR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg imposes zero export-proceeds surrender obligations, zero mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Software licensing and EdTech subscription export proceeds (ISIC 6201) may be retained indefinitely in EUR, USD, GBP, or any freely convertible currency. The only administrative obligation is statistical: BCL balance-of-payments (BoP) and international investment position (IIP) reporting is executed by the relevant bank on its own initiative for direct investment and services flows above de-minimis thresholds; the SARL has no client-side filing duty for ordinary subscription collections.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy, FX operations, and TARGET2 settlement. Prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions and financial-sector entities is exercised by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), applying the Law of 5 April 1993 on the financial sector, CSSF Regulation 12-02, and CSSF Circulars 20/744 and 22/810.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Luxembourg-authorised or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under PSD2 may lawfully hold the SARL's operational funds, segregate client funds, and route cross-border payouts via SEPA/SWIFT; their viability is acceptable for collection-clearing and disbursement-of-royalty rails only. Legal constraints: (1) EMI funds are not deposits and are protected at a lower tier than credit institutions – the FGDL (Fonds de garantie des dépôts Luxembourg) EUR 100,000-per-depositor guarantee applies only to licensed banks; (2) EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide unsecured FX credit lines, making them unsuitable for trade settlement and capital-escrow functions; (3) treasury cash concentration above EUR 100,000 at an EMI would be unprotected and is sub-optimal for a Luxembourg parent treasury; (4) CSSF Circular 20/744 vigilance duties apply equally to EMIs, so no KYC relief is obtained. Recommended structure: use a Tier-1 bank as the primary treasury and capital-escrow provider; use an EMI only for instant-settlement of app-store or affiliate micro-disbursements with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the Tier-1 bank, thereby limiting counterparty risk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium. Luxembourg-authorised or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under PSD2 may lawfully hold the SARL's operational funds, segregate client funds, and route cross-border payouts via SEPA/SWIFT; their viability is acceptable for collection-clearing and disbursement-of-royalty rails only. Legal constraints: (1) EMI funds are not deposits and are protected at a lower tier than credit institutions – the FGDL (Fonds de garantie des dépôts Luxembourg) EUR 100,000-per-depositor guarantee applies only to licensed banks; (2) EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide unsecured FX credit lines, making them unsuitable for trade settlement and capital-escrow functions; (3) treasury cash concentration above EUR 100,000 at an EMI would be unprotected and is sub-optimal for a Luxembourg parent treasury; (4) CSSF Circular 20/744 vigilance duties apply equally to EMIs, so no KYC relief is obtained. Recommended structure: use a Tier-1 bank as the primary treasury and capital-escrow provider; use an EMI only for instant-settlement of app-store or affiliate micro-disbursements with daily zero-balancing sweeps to the Tier-1 bank, thereby limiting counterparty risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 10 million of total revenues for the Luxembourg entity triggers the mandatory Local File. Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million. Intercompany transactions above EUR 1 million per category (goods, services, intangibles, financing) require contemporaneous TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies OECD ATAD substance requirements. A SARL must demonstrate adequate physical presence (stand-alone office), qualified local employees, and substantive decision-making in Luxembourg to access tax treaty and EU Directive benefits. Absent such substance, the entity may be considered a shell company, leading to denial of treaty relief, recapture of withholding tax, and potential reclassification of the entity for Luxembourg tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; civil penalty of 10%–20% of evaded tax plus default interest at 0.6% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax filings are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; they may face criminal prosecution and imprisonment. Under the Luxembourg Company Law, the corporate veil is pierced if the director acted in bad faith.
Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief; recapture of withholding tax on distributed dividends; imposition of additional CIT/MBT on profits attributable to Luxembourg substance; potential administrative fine of €10,000–€100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate tax under veil-piercing doctrine if the shell company is used to evade public obligations; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under EU Directive 2014/42/EU.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment of understated profits; penalty of up to €25,000 for missing documentation; default interest at 0.6% per month; on concealment, a penalty of 200% of the tax understated.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the adjustment amount if they orchestrated the mispricing without legitimate business purpose; criminal negligence can lead to imprisonment for up to 3 years.
AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines from €10,000 to €250,000, or up to 5% of annual turnover for serious breaches; criminal sanctions for institutional offenses under Luxembourg AML law; business license revocation for payment processor relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to personal fines up to €1,000,000, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for gross negligence in failing to prevent money laundering through the entity's payment rails.

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD), Luxembourg City (direct taxes: corporate income tax, municipal business tax, net wealth tax); Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) (VAT and registration duties); Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR / RCS) at lbr.lu for statutory incorporation and foreign-branch registration; unified e-filing portal via myguichet.lu and eCDF (Electronic Customs and Fiscal Documentation).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the SARL (Societe a Responsabilite Limitee) is an opaque capital company resident in Luxembourg (seat or central administration in Luxembourg) and is fully taxable at entity level on worldwide income under Articles 159-164 LIR. It is subject to corporate income tax (IRC: 15% on the first EUR 175,000 of taxable profit, 17% above, plus a 7% solidarity surcharge), municipal business tax (impot commercial communal, municipality-dependent rate), and net wealth tax (impot sur la fortune, 0.5%). No pass-through or tax-transparent status is available for a SARL; profits are taxed at entity level, and distributions may benefit from the participation exemption where applicable.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime: entity classification follows the statutory legal form registered with the RCS, and a SARL is always opaque for Luxembourg tax purposes. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal unity / group consolidation under Article 164bis LIR (irrevocable 5-year election, horizontal group possible); (ii) VAT grouping under Article 60 LTVT (authorisation of the AED, requiring a relationship of financial, economic and organisational interdependence); (iii) participation exemption under Article 166 LIR (election to exempt qualifying dividends and capital gains, holding of at least 10% or an acquisition cost of at least EUR 1.2 million and a 12-month holding/commitment period); (iv) merger and cross-border restructuring relief under EU Directive 90/434/EEC as implemented. Hybrid mismatch exploitation is restricted by the ATAD implementing law of 25 February 2021: no hybrid entity election is available, and mismatched deductions are denied or correspondingly included where a hybrid instrument or hybrid transfer gives rise to a deduction/no-inclusion outcome.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg applies full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions and the euro-area single currency system. No general exchange control authorization is required for foreign direct investment, loan repayment, or profit remittance. Dividends, branch profits, interest, royalties, and service income may be freely repatriated to non-resident shareholders and creditors, subject only to standard corporate tax, withholding tax, and beneficial ownership documentation requirements. For an enamel pin trading SARL, no administrative approval is required for the conversion of EUR into USD or other currencies for commercial transactions; banks merely require a valid underlying transaction document under their internal anti-money-laundering (AML) and sanctions protocols.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no timing deadlines under Luxembourg law. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in a foreign-currency account indefinitely or converted to EUR at the discretion of the SARL. For balance-of-payment statistical reporting, the BCL may require direct or bank-mediated data reporting for cross-border transactions exceeding certain thresholds, but this is a statistical obligation, not a surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) and Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); exchange controls are integrated within the euro-area framework of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — Luxembourg-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by the CSSF under the PSD2 regime, as well as EU/EEA EMIs passporting into Luxembourg, may offer streamlined online merchant accounts and virtual IBANs for low-risk e-commerce receivables. Cross-border digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA generally cannot operate in Luxembourg without obtaining a Luxembourg EMI license or an EU passport; fully offshore non-EU digital banks and payment processors are not viable as primary clearing rails due to CSSF restrictions on unlicensed deposit-taking and payment services. A regulated EMI can serve as an operational layer for Etsy and Stripe settlement acceleration, but the SARL should maintain at least one full-service Luxembourg commercial bank account for capital escrow, tax payments, and high-value trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be executed via SWIFT international wire transfer in EUR to the SARL's Luxembourg capital escrow account. The remitting bank should use a coherent purpose-of-payment code; for an equity subscription from a shareholder, the purpose code 'S110 - Receipts from capital participation in a resident company' or the commonly used 'Equity Capital Contribution' in the SWIFT MT103 field 70 is accepted, while the BCL statistical report may treat it as a direct investment inflow. The receiving bank wil verify the UBO match against the RCS filing, the articles of association, the notarized subscription certificate, and the board resolution. All inbound capital contributions must be lodged through a bank holding a Luxembourg banking license or a recognized European authorized credit institution; a third-party payment platform cannot be used for statutory capital injection.Trade payments to overseas suppliers and logistics providers must be cleared through SWIFT MT103 single payment instructions for the exact settlement amount, supported by the purchase order, proforma invoice, and contract. Smaller operational payments such as courier fees, customs duties, and raw-material purchases may be made through SEPA Instant Credit Transfer within the eurozone or via MT103 to non-EU counterparties. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% (aggregate rate for a limited liability company in Luxembourg City, comprising 17% corporate income tax, 7% solidarity surcharge, and 6.75% municipal business tax; lower 22.80% aggregate rate applies to taxable income below EUR 175,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within five months after the close of the fiscal year (e.g., by 31 May for a 31 December year-end); extensions may be granted for up to seven months upon written request.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: required when the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; Local File: required where the total related-party cross-border transactions exceed EUR 100,000 per fiscal year. Thresholds are applied in euros, and currency conversion is made at the applicable ECB rate.

Economic substance scope: Although Luxembourg has no formal Economic Substance Regulations as in the UAE, the tax authorities require genuine substance (adequate physical premises, employed staff, and management oversight) for the SARL to be recognized as resident and to access treaty/EU directive benefits. Purely artificial or letterbox arrangements linked to enamel pin manufacturing are challenged through general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction for tax evasion: imprisonment of liable individuals for up to 5 years and/or a fine up to EUR 250,000 or double the evaded tax. Administrative surcharge of up to 20% of the under-declared corporate income tax plus monthly late interest at 0.6%.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent or reckless conduct; directors face civil restitution of the unpaid tax, potential travel bans, disqualification from management for a period of 10 years, and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the PPT clause and loss of Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption; the tax authority may re-characterize the income and impose additional corporate income tax and municipal business tax of up to 24.94%, plus an administrative penalty of 10% on the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate the shell structure become jointly and severally liable for the tax deficit, may be personally assessed on the profits, and can be barred from serving as directors in Luxembourg for a period of 15 years.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Luxembourg Law of 12 November 2004 (as amended): for legal persons, fines up to EUR 1 million; administrative fines up to EUR 1 million imposed by the Luxembourg supervisory authority. Additionally, the court may order closure of the business premises or confiscation of funds.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment for up to 5 years and personal fines up to EUR 250,000; severe breaches trigger travel bans, asset freezes, and personal liability if the company was insolvent and the director acted in bad faith.

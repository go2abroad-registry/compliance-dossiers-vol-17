Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties. Primary e-portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) with ACOSET business registration and VAT registration modules.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Luxembourg SARL must first file the incorporation deed and obtain a legal entity identifier from the Luxembourg Business Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS). Through the MyGuichet portal, the SARL applies for a national company tax number (numéro d'identification, NIF) and, if engaged in taxable activities, for a Luxembourg VAT number (numéro de TVA, format LU + 8 digits). The application requires proof of RCS registration, articles of association, identification of directors and shareholders, description of intended activities, and the appointment of a local tax representative if applicable. VAT registration is typically completed online via MyGuichet and must be initiated before or simultaneously with the first taxable supply in Luxembourg.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A Luxembourg SARL is by default a resident corporate entity fully taxable in Luxembourg on its worldwide income. It is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IRC), the municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC), and the net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune, IF). Territorial scope applies to non-resident companies, but a resident SARL may benefit from the Luxembourg participation exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A Luxembourg SARL is by default a resident corporate entity fully taxable in Luxembourg on its worldwide income. It is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IRC), the municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal, ICC), and the net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune, IF). Territorial scope applies to non-resident companies, but a resident SARL may benefit from the Luxembourg participation exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains. No pass-through taxation is available to an SARL under domestic law; the entity is a separate taxpayer.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not provide an elective check-the-box classification regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) the option for joint and several VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 59 of the VAT law, allowing related persons to be treated as one taxable person; (ii) the election for VAT on real estate leasing or other occasional transactions; (iii) the election for horizontal and vertical fiscal consolidation under Article 164bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law (intégration fiscale), enabling resident group members and qualifying 75%-owned Luxembourg subsidiaries to consolidate taxable results; and (iv) certain ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch declarations for cross-border arrangements. These elections require formal submission to the AEDT or ACD and must be documented in advance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover in Luxembourg for supplies of goods and services (small business exemption threshold). Cross-border supplies may trigger registration at lower limits, including the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance sales threshold; inbound intra-EU acquisitions exceeding EUR 10,000 per year require Luxembourg VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Luxembourg SARL performing airline inflight catering may zero-rate supplies of goods (food, beverages, and ancillary provisions) loaded onboard aircraft operating on international routes, thereby qualifying as supplies for the fuelling and provisioning of aircraft operated by airlines where the aircraft is engaged in international transport or the goods are removed from the EU. This exemption/zero-rating is grounded in Article 148(a) of the EU VAT Directive and Article 36 of the Luxembourg VAT law, subject to direct evidence that the goods are transported outside the EU by the aircraft before consumption. Supplies to airlines for strictly intra-EU or domestic flights remain subject to standard or applicable domestic VAT rates. The supplier must retain proof of the aircraft origin, departure point, route, and the actual loading of the catering goods; simple domestic delivery without physical export does not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) or transit/exiting summary declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill/Air Coupon confirming the goods were loaded on the identified aircraft
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or documentary evidence of payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and unitary export contract with the airline
  • Proof of aircraft departure from EU territory and international flight routing

No general real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing clearance regime is currently imposed by the AEDT for domestic B2B or B2C supplies. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies for Luxembourg public-sector procurement, requiring electronic invoices in PEPPOL format ('e-invoicing'). For commercial export and airline catering transactions, electronic invoices are acceptable if they comply with EU Directive 2022/55 and Luxembourg VAT invoicing rules; digital stamps and government fiscal XML clearance are not required.

The AEDT may retrospectively audit export zero-rating claims and recharacterize invalid claims as taxable domestic supplies, clawing back the unpaid VAT for up to 5 years (extended to 10 years in the event of fraud or intentionally concealed transactions). A fraudulent export VAT claim attracts statutory late-payment interest of 0.8% per month, administrative penalties of up to 100% of the evaded VAT, and additional fines. In serious cases, criminal prosecution under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung) and the Criminal Code may lead to imprisonment and further judicial fines. The VAT authorities also apply a generalized anti-avoidance stance requiring robust evidential files; where documentation is incomplete, the reversal of zero-rating may be combined with joint and several liability for holders of the goods and any transport participants.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg is a euro-area member. Under TFEU Articles 63-66 capital and payments are fully free. There is no exchange-control regime, no central bank approval for inbound investment, and no restriction on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted from an FX perspective. Only tax withholding aspects remain relevant, chiefly the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the 10% participation threshold is met) and ordinary corporate income tax (combined rate approximately 24.94%).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Luxembourg imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion into EUR, and no statutory repatriation period. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency bank accounts abroad or in Luxembourg. ECB balance-of-payments reporting and Intrastat statistical declarations apply for the purpose of EU-wide economic statistics, but these are informational and do not constrain currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL); principal macroprudential and eurosystem authority; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) regulates bank conduct, AML/CTF, and payment and settlement systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Ramp Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMI; Medium for EEA-authorized EMI using passporting into Luxembourg under the Luxembourg law of 1 November 2009 on payment services as amended. An EMI can collect client funds and execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, but it cannot issue formal letters of credit, act as a settlement bank for aviation ramp deliveries, or provide treasury credit lines. For capital escrow and operational accounts of an airline inflight catering SARL, a licensed Luxembourg deposit-taking bank is mandatory to preserve UCP600 LC capability and to maintain a clean correspondent banking profile.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 450,000 for related party transactions (goods) and EUR 450,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for entities engaged in certain activities (e.g., holding, financing, IP). For an inflight catering operation, substance is inherent through physical premises, staff, and operational activity. Must maintain adequate personnel, premises, and decision-making in Luxembourg to avoid shell company characterization.

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of tax evaded; criminal fines up to EUR 250,000; imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment if involved in fraudulent acts
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of tax benefits, recharacterization of transactions, penalties up to EUR 100,000; potential criminal charges for tax fraud

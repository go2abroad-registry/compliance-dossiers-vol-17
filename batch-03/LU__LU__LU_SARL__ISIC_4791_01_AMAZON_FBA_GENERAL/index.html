Competent corporate registry

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed and fully paid-up capital of EUR 12,000 (cash or in-kind); cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution, with the blocking certificate delivered to the notary prior to execution of the deed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: inspection and approval by the Luxembourg fire and rescue corps (Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours – CGDIS) for any office/warehouse premises, including an emergency evacuation plan, fire alarm and extinguisher installation certificates (certificat de sécurité incendie) issued before occupation; structural load clearance: engineer's attestation for mezzanine shelving or racking in the fulfilment area confirming compliance with Luxembourg building codes (règlement des bâtisses); occupational clearance: workplace risk assessment (évaluation des risques) and health-and-safety registration with the ITM (Inspection du travail et des mines), plus a medical aptitude declaration for the applicable employees; if more than 15 employees are engaged, the employer must establish a mixed works council (comité mixte) and a safety delegate.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed and fully paid-up capital of EUR 12,000 (cash or in-kind); cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation at a Luxembourg credit institution, with the blocking certificate delivered to the notary prior to execution of the deed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) – Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority, for VAT matters; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) – Direct Tax Administration, for corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax; Administration des douanes et accises (ADA) – Customs and Excise Administration, for customs, excise and import VAT; unified access portal: MyGuichet.lu (https://guichet.public.lu) and the AEDT e-space; official legal references: amended Luxembourg VAT Law of 12 February 1979 and General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung, AO) of 22 May 1931.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and file the incorporation dossier with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) through MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the business identification number (matricule d'identification). Step 2 – File the initial VAT registration form (Form 214 / demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA) with the AEDT electronically via MyGuichet, citing the RCS matricule, registered office address, actual business activity code (ISIC 4791), expected annual turnover and the names of the natural person responsible for VAT; the AEDT issues the Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA, format 'LU' + 10 digits) normally within 10–15 business days. Step 3 – Request an EORI number from the Customs and Excise Administration (ADA) for import/export activities (format 'EO' + matricule); a Luxembourg EORI is a prerequisite for lodging export declarations and for operating under any inward/outward processing or customs warehousing procedure. Step 4 – Register for social security with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) for employer obligations; this is not a tax formality but is required for payroll compliance. Step 5 – Non-resident entrepreneurs (including non-resident shareholders acting as managers) must obtain a Luxembourg residential tax number (numéro d'identification, 13 digits) from the ACD for personal income tax and employer withholding registration; this number is also used for the municipal business tax file. Step 6 – File the annual VAT and CIT returns through the MyGuichet portal using the taxpayer's space; foreign sellers with no Luxembourg fixed establishment may alternatively use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales and the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) for low-value consignments, but companies with an RCS-registered SARL in Luxembourg are treated as established and must register directly with the AEDT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable resident company (société de capitaux) under Article 159 LIR; it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, 17% headline including the COVID solidarity surcharge of 7% on the base CIT) and to municipal business tax (taxe commerciale communale, rate varies by commune; in Luxembourg City approx. 6.75%, producing a combined statutory rate of roughly 24.94%); net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) applies at 0.5% generally, with a reduced 0.05% regime for qualifying asset holdings and an exemption threshold of EUR 500,000 for the 2024 assessment year; territorial scope: resident companies are taxable on worldwide income, with a foreign permanent establishment exemption available under Article 182(8) LIR only if the foreign PE income is subject to a comparable income tax in the source country; the SARL is not a pass-through entity – no partnership transparency whatsoever, and no tax transparency election exists under Luxembourg domestic law; the business scope (online marketplace FBA retail trading) is a fully taxable commercial activity with no special tax-incentive zone applicable to Luxembourg City. — Step 1 – Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and file the incorporation dossier with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) through MyGuichet.lu, obtaining the business identification number (matricule d'identification). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a Luxembourg SARL is a fully taxable resident company (société de capitaux) under Article 159 LIR; it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, 17% headline including the COVID solidarity surcharge of 7% on the base CIT) and to municipal business tax (taxe commerciale communale, rate varies by commune; in Luxembourg City approx. 6.75%, producing a combined statutory rate of roughly 24.94%); net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) applies at 0.5% generally, with a reduced 0.05% regime for qualifying asset holdings and an exemption threshold of EUR 500,000 for the 2024 assessment year; territorial scope: resident companies are taxable on worldwide income, with a foreign permanent establishment exemption available under Article 182(8) LIR only if the foreign PE income is subject to a comparable income tax in the source country; the SARL is not a pass-through entity – no partnership transparency whatsoever, and no tax transparency election exists under Luxembourg domestic law; the business scope (online marketplace FBA retail trading) is a fully taxable commercial activity with no special tax-incentive zone applicable to Luxembourg City.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate the US check-the-box regime, but the following elections are available: (i) fiscal unity / consolidation under Article 164bis LIR for corporate income tax and Article 164ter LIR for municipal business tax – a single group election may be made by a resident parent company holding at least 95% of the voting rights in one or more resident or EEA subsidiaries, submitted annually with the tax return; (ii) VAT grouping (assujettis uniques) under Article 60 of the VAT Law – by application to the AEDT, financially, economically and organisationally linked resident entities may form a single taxable person with one VAT number; (iii) PE exemption election under Article 182(8) LIR – the taxpayer may elect to exempt qualifying foreign permanent establishment profits instead of claiming tax credits; (iv) capital allowances elections under Articles 32 and 42bis LIR (declining-balance depreciation, investment tax credit of up to 15% for eligible digitalisation assets); (v) notional interest deduction election under Article 168bis LIR – an optional deduction on new equity capital (currently capped at 0.816% for 2024); (vi) the R&D super-deduction election (Article 57ter LIR) allows up to 100% additional deduction for R&D expenditures; (vii) hybrid mismatch elections are not available – Luxembourg implemented ATAD 2 via the Law of 21 December 2018, and the anti-hybrid rules automatically override any contrary contractual or statutory election; (viii) a 'self-employment option' is not applicable because SARL shareholders cannot be treated as self-employed for income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EU-wide threshold applicable to intra-Community distance sales of goods: EUR 10,000 per calendar year (Article 357 VAT Directive; Article 58(vt) Luxembourg VAT Law) – if total distance sales from Luxembourg into other EU member states (including those deemed from Amazon FBA warehouses) remain below EUR 10,000, Luxembourg VAT at 17% applies; above the threshold, the seller must either register in each destination member state or use the OSS (non-Union/Union scheme). For purely domestic supplies from a Luxembourg-established SARL, there is no registration threshold – the company must be VAT registered from the day the first taxable transaction is performed; an optional small-business exemption (régime de la franchise) up to EUR 35,000 annual turnover may be applied but is not commercially viable for a marketplace FBA operator because it precludes input VAT deduction. Import VAT registration is required from the first import (no de minimis threshold) unless the IOSS scheme is used for consignments not exceeding EUR 150. Non-established foreign sellers stocking goods under Amazon FBA in a Luxembourg warehouse are registrable in Luxembourg regardless of the EUR 10,000 threshold because the place of supply of the onward sale is where the goods are located at the time of sale; Amazon's fulfilment services themselves are taxable B2B services located in the customer's jurisdiction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically delivered from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU customs territory qualify for zero-rating at 0% under Article 152-I-1° of the VAT Law provided: (i) the goods leave EU territory by means of transport of the seller or a third party acting on the seller's behalf within 90 days from dispatch (as a commercial reality rule; the law requires proof of exit, not a fixed 90-day customs deadline, but the AEDT applies the 90-day presumption); (ii) the seller holds the export declaration in its own name or in the name of an authorised customs representative; (iii) the seller retains in its books the transport contract, bill of lading/AWB/CMR and written purchase order identifying a non-EU buyer; and (iv) the export is made for the benefit of a non-EU purchaser or for a non-EU customer's account. For cross-border e-commerce: B2B services supplied to a non-EU established customer are zero-rated under the place-of-supply rule (Article 77(1) of the VAT Law); B2C digital services supplied to a non-EU consumer are also zero-rated; goods sold via Amazon FBA to EU consumers in other member states are NOT exported from Luxembourg and must be declared via OSS at the destination state's VAT rate; goods sold to a non-EU consumer (e.g., the UK after Brexit or the US) and physically exported directly from the Luxembourg logistics center qualify for zero-rating; if the goods are warehoused outside Luxembourg (e.g., an Amazon FBA centre in Germany) and then sold to a third-country customer, the export is zero-rated in the member state where the goods left the EU, and the Luxembourg seller must register there or use the OSS only within the EU.

  • Electronic export declaration lodged in the Luxembourg customs import/export system (MSIA-AI / EU AES) through the ADA, containing the EORI number, HS codes, declared commercial value and the unique Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit certificate (PDG – preuve de sortie) issued by the customs office at the EU border, or the AES post-departure message confirming exit with date of departure
  • Transport document: original Bill of Lading for sea freight, CMR waybill for road freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or international consignment note (rail/parcel), indicating the consignor (Luxembourg SARL), consignee (non-EU buyer) and destination outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice with the export declaration number, Incoterms 2020 rule (EXW, FOB, or DAP), the buyer's non-EU tax/VAT code, and a statement that goods are exported outside the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103, bank confirmation, or Foreign Exchange Deal ticket) evidencing the receipt of foreign currency for the export sale in the seller's Luxembourg bank account
  • Amazon FBA shipment manifest (inventory transfer report, closure order, shipment event history) proving that the goods left the EU and matching the date of sale to the buyer
  • Freight forwarder's shipping instructions, packing list, and destination customs entry (import declaration from the destination country) as supplementary proof of actual arrival in the non-EU market

Luxembourg has no general real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no digital tax stamping, and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing or API-based pre-clearance for commercial invoices. Mandatory structured electronic invoicing applies only to B2G transactions: since 18 March 2021, suppliers to the Luxembourg State and public-sector buyers must issue e-invoices through the PEPPOL network using the PEPPOL BIS 3.0, UBL (OASIS), or Factur-X formats; compliance is monitored by the Treasury (Trésorerie de l'État) and the Centre des technologies de l'information de l'État (CTIE). For B2B invoices, the VAT Law permits electronic invoicing provided the authenticity of origin and integrity of content are guaranteed through a qualified electronic signature, EDI, or a documented audit trail (Articles 92–93 of the VAT Law); paper invoices remain acceptable. The AEDT may require extraction in SAF-T LU format (audit-file standard) upon initiation of a tax audit; as of 2024 no live clearance API exists for VAT purposes. Operators using Amazon Seller Central are reminded that Amazon's marketplace seller portal issues invoices on behalf of the seller for EU supplies and provides the AEDT with seller data on request, which does not alter the seller's statutory obligation to maintain original commercial documentation.

The AEDT's export audit programme scrutinises the documentary chain for artificial fulfilment structures (especially Amazon FBA operations where the marketplace acts as seller of record for certain programmes). If the exporter cannot demonstrate (i) physical exit of goods, (ii) settlement of the invoice in foreign currency within a commercially reasonable period (the AEDT applies a 180-day presumption in export refund controls), or (iii) a genuine non-EU customer, the zero-rating is denied, input VAT previously deducted on related purchases is clawed back, and the VAT due is assessed at 17% with interest at the legal rate of 0.8% per quarter (Article 31(3) VAT Law), plus a late-payment penalty of 10% of the tax due (Article 25 amending Article 56 of the VAT Law). Where fictitious exporters claim refunds on the basis of forged transport documents, the criminal law applies: Article 396(2) of the General Tax Law provides fines of EUR 250 to EUR 250,000 for negligence or wilful misdeclaration; Article 397 AO provides for imprisonment of eight days to five years and/or fines of EUR 25,000 to EUR 250,000 for intentional tax fraud; in addition, the AEDT may revoke the exporter's EORI, require a bank guarantee up to 150% of the disputed amount, and reassess within a limitation period of five years (ten years in cases of fraud or concealment). The Luxembourg fiscal authorities cooperate with the EU Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF) and the Amazon tax information sharing programme; the director of a Luxembourg SARL who signs a knowingly false export declaration bears personal criminal liability, while the company is jointly liable for the VAT clawback and fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Luxembourg imposes no exchange controls. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital within the EU and with third countries, subject only to EU-wide sanctions screening. The capital account is fully open: equity injections, loans between affiliated undertakings, dividend distributions, and profit repatriation are unrestricted. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax clearance and reporting duties: (i) 15% domestic dividend withholding tax (5% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive with a 10% shareholding and 12-month holding period; 0% further where a Luxembourg participation-exemption regime applies), (ii) annual BCL direct-investment (EPURE) statistical declarations, and (iii) absence of any currency rationing, surrender, or prior authorization for e-commerce receipts. Current-account payments (Amazon FBA settlement remittances, supplier invoices, platform fees) are freely tradable and payable in EUR, USD, CNY, or any other freely usable currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist for Luxembourg exporters. All Amazon marketplace receipts, marketplace payouts, and unrelated export proceeds may be held indefinitely in foreign currency without conversion. A Luxembourg SARL is free to convert 0% or 100% at its commercial discretion. Applicable obligations are limited to (i) banking record retention (Articles L. 100-116 of the Luxembourg Commercial Code), (ii) CSSF AML transaction scrutiny, and (iii) BCL quarterly direct-investment and external-asset/liability position reporting for cross-border settlements exceeding EUR 12,500 per single transaction in some statistical classes. No within-365-day mandatory repatriation or conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the monetary authority, responsible for balance-of-payments reporting, prudential oversight of banks operating in Luxembourg, and EU TARGET2 settlement participation. Banking conduct and AML/CFT supervision is discharged by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). No separate Ministry of Finance exchange-control approval is required for current or capital transactions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate controlled transactions exceed €1,000,000; Master File is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least €50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report is required when group revenue exceeds €750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg applies the Economic Substance Regulations through administrative circular LSC 2021 and EU ATAD/GAAR; a SARL must maintain real local office premises, qualified local employees, substantive commercial activity, and board decisions made in Luxembourg; thin capitalization restricts interest deductibility to 30% of EBITDA (leverage cap).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to €250,000 or twice the evaded tax, additional interest of 6% per annum, and potential liability for personal wealth tax recalculations.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or approve fraudulent filings may be jointly and severally liable for the evaded corporate tax; severance of the corporate veil leads to personal asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of Luxembourg tax residence, denial of benefit of participation exemption, imposition of a 30% administrative penalty, and voidance under GAAR with reassessed taxes due.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement a letterbox structure may incur personal civil restitution for the reclassified tax liability, travel restrictions, and disqualification from board functions.
AML / Financial Integrity Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering under the Luxembourg Penal Code, plus administrative sanctions imposed by the CSSF; financial integrity lapses may lead to immediate bank account freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction, confiscation of personal assets, travel bans while proceedings are pending, and aggravated liability if the corporation is deemed a mere instrument of criminal activity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to €10,000 for documentation non-compliance, upward adjustment of taxable profits by 10%, and potential loss of foreign tax credit; the tax authority may apply an arm's length re-determination.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign misleading documentation may be held personally liable for penalties and civil damages; deliberate disregard of TP rules can be treated as fraudulent mismanagement leading to director disqualification and court-ordered compensation.

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR)

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (loi modifiée du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register and the Résultats, Enregistrement, Sécurité et Informatique system.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) with coordinated articles of association (statuts coordonnés), filed electronically with the RCS and published in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL requires a minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000. The capital must be fully paid in cash at incorporation into a blocked bank account opened in the company name; the bank account release occurs only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification of the registered office is required from the competent communal fire authority. Structural-load and building-occupancy clearance is required only when a physical office is leased; the local commune issues the occupancy certificate. For any employees, occupational-safety registration must be made with the Labour and Mines Inspectorate (ITM). No civil-defense license is required for software-only activity.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL requires a minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000. The capital must be fully paid in cash at incorporation into a blocked bank account opened in the company name; the bank account release occurs only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's valuation report.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The shareholders of Luxembourg SARL, duly convened, hereby resolve: (i) the subscription and full payment of 12,000 registered shares of one euro each, constituting the minimum capital, such funds being deposited in a blocked bank account in the company's name for release upon registration; (ii) the adoption of the registered office at [address], Luxembourg; (iii) the appointment of [Name] as manager (gérant) with sole signature powers; (iv) the shareholders' meeting reserves the right to amend the articles of association; and (v) the manager is authorized to execute all filings with the Trade and Companies Register and to publish in the RESA.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — the no-code website-builder business under ISIC 6201 does not constitute industrial or manufacturing activity. General administrative oversight for software activities is exercised by the Luxembourg Ministry of the Economy and the competent municipal administration.

Category: Not applicable — no operating, manufacturing, or commercial-operating license is required for a no-code visual website builder under Luxembourg law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg direct tax authority; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) — Luxembourg VAT and registration duties authority. Both operate through the national e-government portal MyGuichet.lu (www.myguichet.lu) and the AEDT secure VAT e-filing platform; official references: acd.public.lu and aedt.public.lu. The two administrations are in a legislated merger process toward a unified Luxembourg fiscal administration.

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg-resident SARL, the national matricule (TIN) is assigned automatically upon notarial incorporation and filing with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through MyGuichet.lu. For a foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment, VAT registration in Luxembourg is obtained by filing Application Form 1021 (Demande de numéro d'identification à la TVA) via MyGuichet.lu with the AEDT, attaching certified articles of association, proof of foreign tax registration, identity documents of legal representatives, and evidence of the intended taxable supplies; a Luxembourg tax representative must be appointed where required by law for non-established suppliers. VAT number format: LU + 8 digits; processing time generally 2–4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is a fully tax-opaque resident corporation: it is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) on its worldwide profits, with treaty-based territorial relief for foreign permanent establishments and qualifying participation exemptions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Luxembourg SARL is a fully tax-opaque resident corporation: it is subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) on its worldwide profits, with treaty-based territorial relief for foreign permanent establishments and qualifying participation exemptions. The SARL is not eligible for partnership-style pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections: (i) group taxation / fiscal integration — election by a resident parent directly or indirectly holding at least 95% of a subsidiary's shares and voting rights, with a binding five-year commitment period and a minimum 95% integration ratio; (ii) VAT grouping as a single taxable person (assujetti unique) subject to AEDT approval for closely linked entities; (iii) OSS/IOSS special schemes for EU distance sales and digital B2C services; (iv) optional recharacterisation and adjustment elections required to comply with ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, with DAC6 mandatory disclosure obligations where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 (exclusive of VAT) annual turnover threshold for the small-enterprise VAT exemption in Luxembourg; VAT registration becomes mandatory from the first euro of turnover exceeding EUR 35,000. Non-established suppliers of digital services and distance sellers have no local registration threshold: they must account for Luxembourg VAT from the first taxable supply unless they use the OSS/IOSS scheme; digital no-code/SaaS B2C supplies are taxable where the consumer is established.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to: (a) export of goods physically dispatched or transported from Luxembourg to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documentation in the name of the supplier or its behalf; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods where the goods are transported to another EU Member State, the customer is a VAT-registered operator, and the customer's EU VAT number is validated (VIES); (c) specified services directly connected with exported/imported goods (e.g. freight, handling, ancillary transport services). Digital no-code/SaaS subscriptions do not qualify as goods exports: B2B services to non-EU business customers fall outside Luxembourg VAT under place-of-supply rules; B2B services to EU customers are reverse-charged; B2C digital services are taxed at the consumer's EU Member State via OSS. The supplier must retain complete, chronological, verifiable evidence of the export chain to sustain the 0% rate upon audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic ECS declaration in the Union Customs Code environment)
  • Transport documentation proving departure from the EU — Bill of Lading (sea), CMR/air waybill or equivalent
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) where ancillary to the transaction

No comprehensive national B2B electronic-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate currently applies in Luxembourg; there is no digital stamping or continuous transaction controls regime. B2G e-invoicing to Luxembourg public authorities is mandatory in PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format (phased in from 2024). VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and OSS filings must be submitted electronically through MyGuichet.lu / AEDT systems; the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' initiative will introduce standardised EU-wide B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting obligations around 2030, with Luxembourg transposition pending.

Where zero-rating is claimed without complete export/transport proof, the AEDT may retroactively assess VAT at the standard rate (17%) from the invoice date, disallow associated input VAT deductions, and charge statutory late-payment interest. Administrative penalties for defective or false documentation range from EUR 50 to EUR 4,000 per offence and are doubled for repetition. In cases of fraudulent export zero-rating, the administration may impose penalties of up to 200% of the evaded VAT, and criminal liability (fines and imprisonment) under the Luxembourg Criminal Code can be pursued. The AEDT conducts post-clearance audits using customs data and EU administrative cooperation (Regulation (EU) No 904/2010) to detect fictitious or sham exports; taxpayers must maintain all export and banking records in Luxembourg for inspection for at least the statutory 10-year retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Luxembourg has no foreign-exchange control statute, no central bank approval for foreign investment, and both capital-account and current-account transactions are liberalised under Article 63 TFEU, Luxembourg's long-standing policy of full liberalisation since the abolition of exchange controls in the early 1990s, and its OECD commitments. Inbound equity, shareholder loans, licence fees, and dividends may be freely credited or debited in EUR or any other convertible currency. Profit remittance is not FX-restricted; the only practical hurdles are (i) the Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (15% standard, 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable tax treaty), (ii) AML source-of-funds verification by the paying bank, and (iii) the statutory requirement to have financial statements approved by the shareholders' meeting confirming distributable reserves before a dividend distribution. No BCL or CSSF authorisation is required for a routine dividend payout.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Luxembourg and the Eurosystem impose no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. The de facto rule is a zero-day holding period and a 0% mandatory conversion ratio; the SARL may retain export proceeds in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency. The sole substantive compliance requirement is statistical: under EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 and BCL balance-of-payments instructions, Luxembourg credit institutions report cross-border payment details for resident non-banks, with the BCL's declarative threshold at EUR 12,500 per reporting item. This is a data-reporting obligation imposed on banks, not an exchange-control restriction affecting the SARL.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); credit-institution supervision and AML/CFT enforcement are delegated to the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF), which issues banking licensing, prudential supervision, and compliance directives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium viability, with clear legal subordination. Paddle and Stripe Treasury operate as EEA-authorised Payment Institutions or E-Money Institutions under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and may lawfully extend a segregated client-money account to the Luxembourg SARL for card acquiring, subscription billing, and cross-border payouts through their partner settlement banks. However, an EMI is not a credit institution under the CRD framework and does not provide deposit-guarantee protection (DGSD EUR 100,000) for the SARL's balances; client funds are pooled and ring-fenced, creating counterparty and insolvency exposure. The CSSF expects the SARL to maintain its principal treasury, statutory capital, and VAT payments in a fully licensed EEA bank. Practical recommendation: retain Stripe/Paddle solely as collection and disbursement rails and run an automated daily SEPA/API sweep of balances into the BGL BNP Paribas or BIL operational account; do not rely on a third-country EMI (for example, a UK or UAE digital bank) as the primary treasury because post-Brexit passporting limitations and CSSF/bank KYC concerns will create friction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital: remit EUR via SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA zone) or SWIFT MT103 (non-SEPA) to the SARL's blocked capital-subscription account at a Luxembourg credit institution, using an ISO 20022 purpose code acceptable to the bank for equity contributions (typically EQUI, or INTC for intragroup capital movements) and including the required originator data under Regulation (EU) 2015/847; the remitter name must match the registered shareholder exactly, and the beneficiary reference field must quote the SARL's RCS registration number. The blocked funds are released only upon the Luxembourg notary's certificate confirming payment of the minimum share capital and filing of the incorporation deed. Shareholder loans: use a separate MT103 with purpose code LOAN, attach the executed intercompany loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate, and document the source of funds with 12 months of bank statements. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% (2024) on taxable income exceeding EUR 175,000; 15% on the first EUR 175,000; from FY 2025 the schedule becomes 16% above EUR 200,000. Add the 7% Employment Fund solidarity surcharge on CIT and municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), yielding a combined marginal effective rate of approximately 24.94% (2024) / 23.87% (2025) in Luxembourg City.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on Luxembourg-source dividends paid by a SARL; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the corporate beneficial owner holds >=10% or a EUR 1.2M acquisition cost for at least 12 months and is a qualifying EU/EEA resident; 0% for Luxembourg-resident corporate shareholders under the domestic parent-subsidiary regime; treaty rates generally 5%-15% (e.g., 5% under many DTAs for holdings above 10%, 15% portfolio) subject to the principal-purpose test.

Calendar-year SARLs: return due by 31 December of the year following the tax year (e.g., FY 2024 by 31 December 2025); non-calendar accounting periods: due by 31 December of the year following the closing date, with extensions on reasoned request. Section I - Balance due and prepayments Quarterly prepayments declared and residual CIT payable after offset. Pay the residual within one month of the assessment; unpaid balances accrue 0.6% monthly interest (7.2% per annum). Box 1 - Net book assets Total Luxembourg and foreign assets after depreciation and provisions. Unitary basis with no worldwide consolidation; intercompany loans to the parent are subject to 0.5% unless the 12-month participation exemption applies. Late filing and understatement trigger the same regime as Form 1002: fine up to EUR 25,000, 10% surcharge, 0.6% monthly interest; deliberate omission is treated as tax evasion.
Box 2 - Deductible participations Qualifying shareholdings (>=10% or EUR 1.2M) excluded from the net wealth base. Box 3 - Net wealth taxable base Result after participation relief and EUR 500,000 exempt allowance for businesses. The allowance and minimum tax apply at entity level; no consolidation with the foreign parent. Late filing and understatement trigger the same regime as Form 1002: fine up to EUR 25,000, 10% surcharge, 0.6% monthly interest; deliberate omission is treated as tax evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where intra-group transactions within one category exceed EUR 100,000 per year (financial transactions, IP transfers and licences, goods, services, management fees) or EUR 50,000 for guarantee transactions. Master File and Country-by-Country Report: for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Defensive documentation and a benchmarking study are recommended for a no-code SaaS SARL even below the thresholds, and are required for any APA or unilateral ruling request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate UAE/BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is enforced through the statutory general anti-abuse rule (Article 6 of the 1934 Tax Adaptation Law, reinforced by the 2016 reform), the EU ATAD interest-limitation and anti-hybrid rules, and the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test applicable to DTAs. A SARL carrying the no-code website-builder business must maintain premises, a Luxembourg-based gerant taking commercial decisions, employees or contractors whose number and skill profile correspond to the activity, and contemporaneous board minutes. Absent such substance, the ACD may ignore the Luxembourg entity, reattribute its profits to the foreign parent, deny treaty and directive benefits, and extend the assessment period from 5 to 11 years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion - suppression of subscription revenue

Statutory sanction: Reassessment plus late/incomplete-return fine up to EUR 25,000, 10% surcharge, 0.6% monthly interest, and criminal prosecution under Article 392 ff. LIR: imprisonment of 8 days to 5 years and fines of EUR 2,500 to EUR 50,000, with the court able to order publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: The gerant or director who signs a knowingly false return is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties and interest; personal assets can be seized, and the court may impose a management ban applicable across the EU through mutual recognition.

Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), Articles 710-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), together with Articles 8, 9, and 24 on capital, formation formalities, and publication in the RESA.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (acte constitutif) filed electronically by the Luxembourg notary via eJustice for RCS registration and publication in the Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations (RESA).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must have a fully subscribed minimum share capital of EUR 12,000. At least 25% of the nominal value of each cash share must be paid up before notarial execution. The cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened in the company's formation name at a Luxembourg credit institution, and the bank's blockage certificate is released only after RCS registration. Contributions in kind must be fully vested and are subject to statutory auditor valuation if required.

Funding & unlock path: The studio and office premises must obtain fire safety approval from the Corps grand-ducal d'incendie et de secours (CGDIS), a building occupancy permit from the competent commune, and workplace safety documentation filed with the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM). Employer affiliation with an approved occupational health service is compulsory once staff are hired.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must have a fully subscribed minimum share capital of EUR 12,000. At least 25% of the nominal value of each cash share must be paid up before notarial execution. The cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened in the company's formation name at a Luxembourg credit institution, and the bank's blockage certificate is released only after RCS registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) — Luxembourg Direct Tax Authority, 4 rue de la Congrégation, L-1352 Luxembourg — for corporate income tax, net wealth tax, withholding tax and transfer pricing; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT), 1-3 avenue Guillaume, L-1651 Luxembourg — for Value Added Tax (TVA) and registration duties; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) — for commercial (R.C.S.) registration and the national identification number; single digital filing gateway: MyGuichet.lu (business eSpace), supplemented by the eCDF electronic tax-filing module of the ACD/AEDT.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and file the incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through MyGuichet/eJustice; the LBR automatically assigns an 11-digit national business registration number (matricule / numéro d'identification national), which serves as the master tax identity before the ACD. Step 2 — No separate direct-tax registration is required for a resident SARL: the ACD uses the matricule for all corporate income tax, municipal business tax and net wealth tax assessments; annual returns are filed electronically through the MyGuichet/eCDF platform. Step 3 — Obtain the VAT ID by filing Form 640 (Déclaration pour assujettissement à la TVA) with the AEDT; resident SARLs receive a VAT identification number of the format LU + 8 digits with a modulo-97 checksum; registration is due before the first taxable supply and, for a resident SARL, no turnover de-minimis applies unless the small-enterprise franchise under Article 57 of the 1979 VAT Law is claimed. Step 4 — For a non-resident foreign entity without a Luxembourg establishment, registration with the AEDT via Form 640 is required when the entity makes taxable supplies in Luxembourg (e.g., intra-EU distance sales, B2B services subject to Luxembourg reverse-charge rules, or supplies of e-services to Luxembourg consumers above the OSS threshold); a non-EU applicant must normally appoint a Luxembourg VAT representative; the AEDT then issues a stand-alone Luxembourg VAT ID without an RCS number. Step 5 — Register as an employer with the Centre commun de la sécurité sociale (CCSS) if staff are engaged, and open the ACD e-filing account to receive pre-printed returns, notices and VAT statements; registration is processed typically within 5–15 business days where the file is complete.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the Luxembourg SARL is an opaque, fully tax-resident corporation under Article 159 LIR and is subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability, with the only territorial carve-out being treaty-based exemption for profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment. The standard Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) is 15% on the first €175,000 of taxable profit and 17% on taxable profit above €175,000, plus a 7% employment solidarity surcharge calculated on the IRC, plus municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) of 6.75% in Luxembourg City, resulting in an aggregate headline effective rate of approximately 24.94% on profits above €175,000. The SARL is also liable to a minimum annual net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) of €3,500, increased to €4,815 when the balance-sheet total exceeds €350 million. Profit is taxed on accrual, not distribution: dividends declared by the SARL are subject to a 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the domestic participation exemption when the 10% shareholding/e1.2m acquisition-cost and 12-month holding tests are met. The SARL is therefore not a pass-through, hybrid or disregarded entity for Luxembourg tax purposes; the tax classification is mandatory and not elective, and non-resident ownership of the SARL does not alter its status. — Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL by notarial deed and file the incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) through MyGuichet/eJustice; the LBR automatically assigns an 11-digit national business registration number (matricule / numéro d'identification national), which serves as the master tax identity before the ACD. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Luxembourg SARL is an opaque, fully tax-resident corporation under Article 159 LIR and is subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability, with the only territorial carve-out being treaty-based exemption for profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment. The standard Luxembourg corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités, IRC) is 15% on the first €175,000 of taxable profit and 17% on taxable profit above €175,000, plus a 7% employment solidarity surcharge calculated on the IRC, plus municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) of 6.75% in Luxembourg City, resulting in an aggregate headline effective rate of approximately 24.94% on profits above €175,000. The SARL is also liable to a minimum annual net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune) of €3,500, increased to €4,815 when the balance-sheet total exceeds €350 million. Profit is taxed on accrual, not distribution: dividends declared by the SARL are subject to a 15% Luxembourg withholding tax, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the domestic participation exemption when the 10% shareholding/e1.2m acquisition-cost and 12-month holding tests are met. The SARL is therefore not a pass-through, hybrid or disregarded entity for Luxembourg tax purposes; the tax classification is mandatory and not elective, and non-resident ownership of the SARL does not alter its status.

Available election forms

Check-the-box and similar pass-through elections do not exist in Luxembourg's civil-law unitary tax system; the SARL is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available statutory elections: (1) VAT group election under Article 60 of the 1979 VAT Law — resident entities with economic, financial and organisational links may apply to be treated as a single taxable person (assujetti unique) with joint and several liability, allowing consolidated intra-group VAT invoices; (2) Fiscal unity for direct tax under Article 164bis LIR — a Luxembourg-resident parent holding directly or indirectly at least 95% of a Luxembourg subsidiary may elect full absorption of the subsidiary's taxable results from the first tax year of the election; (3) Participation-exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains under Articles 166 and 168 LIR, requiring a durable 10% shareholding or an acquisition cost of at least €1.2 million and a 12-month holding period measured at the relevant assessment date; (4) Option to waive the small-business VAT franchise (Article 57 VAT Law) in order to charge VAT and recover input VAT; (5) OSS (One-Stop-Shop) election for intra-EU B2C distance sales and telecommunications/broadcasting/e-services, filed electronically through MyGuichet; (6) hybrid-mismatch elections are not available: the Law of 23 December 2016 implementing EU ATAD/ATAD2 imposes mandatory anti-hybrid adjustments, and a Luxembourg SARL cannot elect to be disregarded, transparent or treated as a branch of its shareholder; (7) where applicable, the EU cross-border loss-relief election and the arm's-length advance pricing agreement route are available with the ACD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-enterprise franchise threshold: €35,000 (EUR) of annual taxable turnover excluding VAT, under Article 57 of the 1979 VAT Law, below which a Luxembourg-established business may be exempt from VAT; above this threshold mandatory registration applies unless the exemption is waived. Intra-EU B2C distance-selling and digital e-services threshold: €10,000 (EUR) of cumulative annual European turnover for distance sales of goods and supplies of telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services by a Luxembourg supplier; below €10,000 Luxembourg VAT is chargeable, at or above €10,000 the supply is taxed in the consumer's member state and the OSS return mechanism must be used. Non-established foreign suppliers: no registration threshold applies and a Luxembourg VAT ID is required from the first euro of Luxembourg-taxable supply, except for supplies covered by the €10,000 OSS regime or by the reverse-charge mechanism; intra-EU goods distance sales by non-established EU suppliers are each governed by the national €10,000 OSS ceiling as implemented in their own state. VAT registration is compulsory, not discretionary, when any threshold is exceeded, and VAT must be accounted for from the date the threshold is crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with credit for input VAT) applies to: (a) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf (Article 156(1)(b) of the 1979 VAT Law), subject to proof of removal; (b) intra-EU supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid VAT identification number in another member state, subject to VIES validation, EC sales list reporting and invoice references to the customer's EU VAT ID; (c) supplies of services directly connected with exported goods, such as transport, handling and ancillary services physically performed in Luxembourg in relation to goods leaving the EU; and (d) supplies placed under customs warehousing, inward processing or similar customs regimes. For the SARL's business of automated subtitle transcription, AI voice cloning and post-production dubbing, the normal outcome is territoriality rather than a classical zero rate: B2B supplies of such services to a customer established in another member state are taxable in the customer's state under the reverse charge (no Luxembourg VAT), and B2B supplies to a customer outside the EU are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT; B2C supplies of e-services to EU consumers below the €10,000 threshold bear Luxembourg VAT, above the threshold they are taxed at destination via OSS, and B2C supplies to non-EU consumers fall outside Luxembourg VAT. The SARL retains the right to deduct input VAT attributable to these export-like supplies, and a non-EU-established customer may recover improperly incurred Luxembourg VAT through the 13th Directive procedure before the AEDT.

Luxembourg has not introduced real-time continuous transaction controls: there is no mandatory fiscal-clearance API, no government digital stamping, and no BTC/B2B e-invoicing mandate for private-sector invoices. B2G electronic invoicing to central, regional and municipal authorities is mandatory through the PEPPOL network in the EN 16931 data model pursuant to Luxembourg's transposition of Directive 2014/55/EU, with phased enforcement against suppliers to the national administration. B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and contractually driven, although structured electronic invoices (EDI, PEPPOL, Factur-X) are accepted. All VAT periodical returns (monthly or quarterly depending on prior-year turnover), recapitulative statements (EC sales lists), Intrastat declarations and requested Luxembourg SAF-T standard audit files must be submitted electronically through MyGuichet/eCDF; the VAT books and source evidence must be retained for at least 10 years. The payment rails identified (Stripe, Wise) operate solely as payment service providers: they are not deemed suppliers, not electronic-interface marketplaces under the VAT e-commerce package, and do not trigger a VAT collection, OSS or deemed-supply obligation for the SARL beyond the obligation to invoice and account for the underlying supply.

Luxembourg domestic law imposes no exchange-control or mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day standard recorded in this field serves as the administrative benchmark for export-proceed substantiation, transfer-pricing documentation and ACD risk review. The AEDT and ACD enjoy a general audit window of five years from the end of the relevant assessment year, extended without limit in cases of fraud, wilful default or failure to file. Where zero-rating or export exemption is claimed without the required customs, transport, VIES or payment evidence, the administration clawbacks VAT at the standard rate of 17% (or at the applicable reduced rate) on the full supply value, together with statutory default interest, a late-payment surcharge of 10% and administrative fines of up to €25,000 per adjustment; criminal tax-fraud proceedings may additionally result in imprisonment of up to five years and further judicial fines. On the direct-tax side, the ACD may recharacterise funds or receivables that have not been repatriated or economically justified as hidden dividend distributions or disguised profit distributions under Article 56 LIR and the ATAD general anti-abuse rule, triggering reassessment of corporate income tax, municipal business tax, a 15% dividend withholding tax, interest and penalties. To mitigate audit exposure, the SARL should maintain a per-transaction export evidence file, including customs declarations, transport documents, VIES screenshots, contracts, delivery certificates, licence confirmations and bank/remittance records, retained for at least ten years and reconciled to the VAT return, EC sales list and annual accounts.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full current-account and capital-account liberalisation under Articles 63-66 TFEU, the OECD Capital Movements Code, and the application of the euro as legal tender. There are no exchange-control approvals, no FDI licensing requirements for post-production subtitle and AI voice-cloning services, and no prohibition on inward capital or outward profit remittance. Dividends and profit shares may be freely transferred in EUR or convertible foreign currency once corporate law distribution tests are met and the paying agent's AML/KYC obligations are satisfied. The only material obstacles are tax-driven, not FX-driven: a Luxembourg Sàrl distributing dividends is normally subject to Luxembourg withholding tax of 15% (subject to treaty reductions or the Parent-Subsidiary Directive where applicable), and shareholders' loan repayments must comply with thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing rules. Inconvertibility risk is negligible because EUR is freely traded and the BCL has never imposed surrender or rationing measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in Luxembourg. Export proceeds derived from subtitling, dubbing, and AI transcription services may be retained in any foreign currency in bank accounts without compulsory sale, surrender, or BCL authorisation. Banks acting as reporting agents must provide transaction-level balance-of-payments statistics to the BCL for eligible cross-border payments, typically above the EUR 12,500 reporting threshold, and the SARL must preserve underlying invoices, contracts, and transaction narratives for at least five years. These obligations are statistical and AML/CFT-based, not exchange-control restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque Centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) is the national central bank and Luxembourg's monetary and balance-of-payments authority; the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) is the integrated financial sector supervisor competent for banking, AML/CFT supervision, and payment/e-money institution oversight. There is no separate exchange-control administration; the BCL operates within the Eurosystem and the CSSF enforces prudential and AML rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for inbound collection and subcontractor payout routing; Low as a substitute for a licensed credit institution or statutory share-capital escrow. A Luxembourg-authorised payment institution or e-money institution supervised by the CSSF may hold payment or electronic-money accounts but may not take deposits or create bank credit. International EMIs such as Wise and Stripe may be used as regulated settlement/technical payment processors for trade receivables if the serving entity is authorised or passported in the EEA under PSD2 and subject to safeguarding rules. However, the civil-law formation procedure of a Luxembourg Sàrl requires the paid-up share capital to be indefeasibly evidenced, typically through a blocked account in the name of the company at a Luxembourg/EU credit institution certified to the notary; a pure EMI balance cannot satisfy that formal proof. Offshore digital EMIs without passporting, deposit-taking capability, or a local AML nexus create a legal concentration risk and should not be treasury counterparties for material balances.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly incorporated Sàrl with non-resident director and no demonstrated local economic substance

Root cause: Newly incorporated Sàrl with non-resident director and no demonstrated local economic substance

Defense evidence: Registered lease or commercial sub-lease agreement; utility invoice and internet/telephony service contract in the company's name; local substance description including Luxembourg-based manager, service agreements, accounting firm relationship, payroll or corporate tax filings; organisational chart identifying management and employees; signed board resolution approving account opening and treasury mandate.
AI voice-cloning, subtitling, and post-production business classified as intellectual-property-centric digital activity with cross-border revenue flows

Root cause: AI voice-cloning, subtitling, and post-production business classified as intellectual-property-centric digital activity with cross-border revenue flows

Defense evidence: Portfolio of binding client contracts and purchase orders; detailed workflow description distinguishing AI-generated transcription, human post-editing, and localisation; IP ownership or licensing chain, including copyright registrations and assignment deeds; software and data licences; data-processing agreements and GDPR or DPIA documentation evidencing lawful handling of actor and author data; projected transaction flows with counterparty jurisdictions.
Frequent outbound payments through Stripe, Wise, or similar payment aggregators to numerous subcontractors and voice studios in multiple countries

Root cause: Frequent outbound payments through Stripe, Wise, or similar payment aggregators to numerous subcontractors and voice studios in multiple countries

Defense evidence: Whitelist of subcontractors with full legal names, registration numbers, and address verification; master subcontracting agreements and per-project statements of work; invoice integrity tracking and PO-to-invoice-to-payment reconciliation; sanctions and PEP screening results for each counterparty; explanations for any unusual or split payments; confirmation that all funds are settled through EEA-regulated institutions.
Ultimate beneficial owner located in, or nationality aligned with, a FATF-high-risk monitoring jurisdiction or a jurisdiction requiring enhanced due diligence

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner located in, or nationality aligned with, a FATF-high-risk monitoring jurisdiction or a jurisdiction requiring enhanced due diligence

Defense evidence: Certified passport copy and proof of residential tax residence; sworn declaration of legitimate wealth and source of funds; audited or bank-supported evidence for the capital injection; curriculum vitae and business substance narrative; corporate registers and ownership chain documentation demonstrating transparency; consent to enhanced monitoring and periodic Source-of-Funds/Wealth re-verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the SARL's paid-up share capital, the inward transfer must be executed before notarisation to a blocked escrow account in the company's name at a Luxembourg/EU credit institution. If the transfer is in EUR within the euro area, use a SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) with the remittance narrative "Capital subscription - Société à Responsabilité Limitée en formation"; if in USD or other foreign currency, use SWIFT MT103 with a corresponding-bank clearing path and the balance-of-payments flag for direct investment equity capital. The bank must provide a blocked-funds certificate or irrevocable confirmation used by the Luxembourg notary to authenticate payment of the statutory minimum capital. After registration, the funds are released to the operational account on the company's instruction. For subsequent intra-group debt or equity injections from a foreign parent, use SWIFT MT103 or SEPA SCT with the appropriate balance-of-payments category and ensure invoices, shareholder resolutions, and intercompany loan agreements are retained to demonstrate commercial purpose and financing substance.For subcontractor payments, royalty remittances, AI platform licensing fees, and operating expenses, the primary clearing rails are SEPA SCT for EUR payments within the EEA, SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD or non-EEA payments, and SWIFT MT202/COV for correspondent-bank settlement where required by the receiving institution. Trade-related documentary settlement is generally unnecessary for intangibles and services, but where the counterparty requires assurance for high-value technology or post-production deliverables, a standard commercial letter of credit may be issued through SWIFT MT700/MT707; in the digital services context, bank-guaranteed payment undertakings and standby LCs are more practical.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés – RCS), operated by the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the Ministry of Justice

Governing statute

Luxembourg Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended, Articles 710-1 et seq. governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); complemented by the Law of 19 December 2019 on the Register of Commerce and Companies (RCS) and the Law of 13 January 2019 on the establishment of the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) electronic filing framework

Formation / constitutional filings

Acte constitutif (Notarial Deed of Incorporation containing the Articles of Association/Statuts), filed electronically via the e-RCS portal of the Luxembourg Business Registers

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000 (fully subscribed at incorporation); at least 25% of each cash contribution, i.e. a minimum of EUR 3,000, must be paid up at signing; in-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation; payment is evidenced by a Luxembourg bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) or a notary-held escrow certificate; no mandatory long-term escrow account is required after RCS registration

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (attestation de sécurité incendie) issued by the municipal fire prevention service and the Grand-Ducal Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) for the registered office; structural load inspection and building occupancy certificate from the municipal building authority; workplace health and safety clearance from the Inspection du Travail et des Mines (ITM), covering ergonomic workstations, electrical safety, evacuation signage, first-aid equipment, and compliance with OSH standards equivalent to the European framework.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed share capital of EUR 12,000 (fully subscribed at incorporation); at least 25% of each cash contribution, i.e. a minimum of EUR 3,000, must be paid up at signing; in-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation; payment is evidenced by a Luxembourg bank blocking certificate (attestation de blocage) or a notary-held escrow certificate; no mandatory long-term escrow account is required after RCS registration

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT; unified corporate registration through Luxembourg Business Registers (RCSL) portal (https://www.lbr.lu) and eCDF tax filing portal (https://ecdf.public.lu)

E-government filing portal

For a Luxembourg SARL, the statutory procedure is: (1) execute the incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and file the articles of association with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); (2) obtain the national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule) assigned by the RCSL; (3) register for VAT with the AEDT by filing the VAT registration form via MyGuichet to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits); (4) the ACD automatically registers the company for corporate income tax and municipal business tax upon RCS filing; non-resident entities without a Luxembourg establishment that have a direct tax liability or VAT taxable event must register directly with the AEDT/ACD and, if VAT-liable in Luxembourg, may be required to appoint a local tax representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL is a taxable company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on a worldwide basis, subject to participation exemption, IP box, and permanent establishment exemptions; no tax-exempt zone regime applies) — For a Luxembourg SARL, the statutory procedure is: (1) execute the incorporation deed before a Luxembourg notary and file the articles of association with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); (2) obtain the national identification number (numéro d'identification / matricule) assigned by the RCSL; (3) register for VAT with the AEDT by filing the VAT registration form via MyGuichet to obtain a Luxembourg VAT number (LU + 8 digits); (4) the ACD automatically registers the company for corporate income tax and municipal business tax upon RCS filing; non-resident entities without a Luxembourg establishment that have a direct tax liability or VAT taxable event must register directly with the AEDT/ACD and, if VAT-liable in Luxembourg, may be required to appoint a local tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident SARL is a taxable company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax on a worldwide basis, subject to participation exemption, IP box, and permanent establishment exemptions; no tax-exempt zone regime applies)

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal unity (tax consolidation) election under article 164bis LIR for resident companies that hold at least 99% of the subsidiary's capital and voting rights; (ii) hybrid mismatch re-characterisation elections under the ATAD implementation, including options to align payment treatment for interest restriction and controlled foreign company rules; (iii) the option for securitisation vehicles to ring-fence assets and liabilities under the Securitisation Law; (iv) election to apply the IP box regime for qualifying intellectual property income at 80% exemption; (v) VAT grouping is available under specific conditions for Luxembourg-established VAT taxable persons.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for the small-enterprise franchise regime under Article 57a of the Luxembourg VAT Statute; non-resident taxable persons supplying goods or services in Luxembourg are required to register for VAT without any threshold, unless a special EU scheme applies (e.g., One Stop Shop).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (i) exports of goods physically sent to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier holds sufficient documentary evidence of dispatch and transport; (ii) services supplied to a customer established outside the EU where the place-of-supply rules allocate the supply to the third-country destination; (iii) certain international transport and related services. For the royalty financing fund, financial services are generally exempt without credit and not zero-rated; zero-rating may be claimed only for ancillary services (e.g., advisory fees, collection agency services) that qualify as B2B services supplied to non-EU counterparties under the place-of-supply rules.

  • EU Single Administrative Document (SAD) / Customs Export Declaration
  • Signed Bill of Lading (B/L) or CMR transport waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the receiving bank or bank statement showing foreign currency settlement
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Proof of ownership transfer and transport insurance documents
  • SWIFT MT103 or equivalent wire transfer evidence
  • Foreign exchange conversion confirmation, if applicable

No real-time continuous transaction controls (CTC), digital stamping, or fiscal clearance mechanism is currently enacted for Luxembourg domestic B2B invoicing. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only to public procurement invoices issued to Luxembourg public authorities, which must be submitted through the Luxembourg e-Procurement platform in PEPPOL BIS format. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and Intrastat filings must be submitted electronically via eCDF/MyGuichet; tax clearance is performed ex-post through periodic VAT audits and risk-based inspection.

Luxembourg tax authorities may reassess zero-rated supplies retrospectively if export evidence is deficient, and are entitled to claw back input VAT together with interest at 0.8% per month and administrative fines ranging from 5% to 100% of the VAT understated. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, including falsified export documentation or artificial circular transaction structures, constitute tax fraud ( escroquerie fiscale) and are punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to €500,000, as well as forfeiture of deductibility and solidary liability for company directors and representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains fully liberalised capital and current account movements under TFEU Article 63 and the EU's free movement of capital acquis. The euro is fully convertible with no exchange controls, no BCL pre-authorisation for cross-border flows, and no restrictions on profit or dividend remittance. Fiscal hurdles only: 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax may apply on distributions, but is eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for qualifying 10% shareholdings) or reduced under double tax treaties; non-profit-linked interest payments to non-resident lenders are exempt from withholding tax, while IP royalties are subject to 15% WHT unless exempt under the EU Interest & Royalties Directive (2003/49/EC) or an applicable DTT. Balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations rest on the banking counterparties, not on the SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The Eurosystem and Luxembourg law impose no surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds. Export receipts in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency may be held offshore or onshore without BCL approval; only the collecting bank's statistical BOP reporting applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), together with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for bank licensing and AML/CTF supervision; Luxembourg operates within the Eurosystem and settles large-value euro payments via the ECB's TARGET2 platform.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. CSSF-authorised payment institutions or EMIs (e.g., MANGOPAY S.A., EDEA PAY S.A.) may operate only as segregated collection rails for royalty receipts via SEPA Direct Debit and card pay-ins; they may not grant commercial credit, hold the SARL's financing capital as escrow, or execute FX forwards under the Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services. EU passported digital banks/EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Bunq) can serve as secondary operating accounts for day-to-day expenses but are unsuitable as the primary treasury bank for a lending-like royalty financing vehicle. A full Luxembourg or EU credit institution remains mandatory for treasury, lending disbursements, and hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from shareholders must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (non-SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR to the SARL's IBAN at the receiving Luxembourg credit institution. The payment must carry an ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'EQUI' for equity subscription or 'LOAN' for intercompany shareholder loans; the receiving bank will map this to the ECB/BOP category for direct investment or other investment-loans for BCL statistical reporting. Remittance fields must quote the notarial capital-increase deed date and notary reference, the SARL's RCS number, and the share premium account. The bank will require a certified copy of the notarial deed of capital increase, the updated extract of the RCS, and the RBE filing confirmation before final credit. For a capital increase, the contribution must be fully paid into a Luxembourg bank account or a blocked escrow referenced in the deed; MT202 COV should be avoided unless the beneficiary detail in the cover payment exactly mirrors the SARL's legal name and IBAN.Disbursements to supported digital brands within the EEA are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant; non-EEA and non-EUR payouts use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 referencing the disbursement contract and underlying revenue-sharing agreement. USD, GBP, and CHF outflows clear through Fedwire/CHIPS, CHAPS, and SIX via the Luxembourg bank's correspondent network. Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) are available where trade collateral exists but are non-standard for royalty financing; the primary rail is a clean MT103 transfer. Collections from merchants are best structured as SEPA Direct Debit B2B mandates routed to a dedicated EMI or collection account, then swept daily via SEPA into the treasury account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

17% Luxembourg corporate income tax (IRC) above EUR 175,000 taxable profit, plus 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC; municipalities levy an additional municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal) at rates between 6.75% and 12.64%. The aggregate statutory headline rate in Luxembourg City is approximately 24.94%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% on gross dividends distributed by a Luxembourg SARL to non-resident shareholders. The rate is 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the domestic participation exemption, and may be reduced to 0%-15% under applicable double tax treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed by 30 June of the calendar year immediately following the fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the statutory deadline is 30 June. Approved tax advisers may request extensions through the electronic portal.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required where the multinational group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Local File required for a Luxembourg entity when its aggregate related-party transactions in a relevant category exceed EUR 2,500,000, or when the Luxembourg entity itself is significant within the group. Country-by-Country Report filing applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore ESR regimes, but substance requirements are embedded in Art. 56 LIR, judicial anti-abuse doctrines, ATAD general anti-abuse rules, and treaty limitation-on-benefits analysis. A Luxembourg SARL engaged in royalty financing and fund activities must demonstrate local substance including board control, senior management, staff, office premises, risk management, and actual economic ownership of the financial and IP assets; otherwise tax deductions, treaty benefits, and Luxembourg tax residence may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Best-judgment tax assessment, 0.6% monthly arrears interest, fraud surcharges up to 100% of the evaded tax, criminal fines, and imprisonment up to 5 years. The tax administration may also publish the judgment and disqualify the company from public contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations or knowingly cause the company to evade taxes are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax, interest, and penalties. Criminal courts may impose imprisonment, fines, asset seizure, and exit or travel bans. Civil liability under Luxembourg principles of faute de gestion permits creditors or the tax administration to pursue personal claims against directors.
Abusive shell company and artificial royalty/financing structure

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, recharacterisation of royalty payments as dividends or hidden equity distributions, imposition of Luxembourg dividend withholding tax up to 15% on the recharacterised amount, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, and application of ATAD CFC rules attributing income to the controlling shareholder.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is a pure façade or the directors knowingly participate in an abusive circular arrangement, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold the directors personally responsible for unpaid taxes and restitution. Travel bans and criminal sanctions may apply where fraudulent intent is established.
Transfer pricing non-compliance and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under Art. 56 LIR to a deemed arm’s length profit, revocation of deductions for excessive payments, 0.6% monthly arrears interest, and general penalties applicable to understated income. Deliberate documentation falsification escalates the case to criminal tax fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for approving non-arm’s length pricing and for destroying or manufacturing TP documentation may become personally liable for the tax deficiency and for damages to the company. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment and fines for forgery and tax fraud.
AML, UBO register non-compliance, and unlicensed financial activity

Statutory sanction: The UBO register omission exposes the company and its managers to administrative fines. Money laundering constitutes a criminal offence under the Luxembourg Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines reaching the value of the laundered assets. Unregulated financial activity may trigger CSSF enforcement, asset freezing, and compulsory dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for money laundering if they knowingly process illicit proceeds; civil restitution orders, confiscation of assets, travel bans, and disqualification from holding managerial office may be imposed even where the SARL’s liability is limited.

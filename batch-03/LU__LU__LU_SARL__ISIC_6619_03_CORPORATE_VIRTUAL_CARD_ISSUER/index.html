Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), operated by Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) under the Ministry of Justice through the eJustice single-window portal at www.lbr.lu.

Governing statute

Law of 10 August 1915 on Commercial Companies, as amended (Loi du 10 août 1915 concernant les sociétés commerciales), notably Title VII on the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), Articles 710-1 to 710-26 as amended by the Law of 23 August 2023 (in force since 12 December 2023); complemented by the Law of 19 December 2002 on the Trade and Companies Register (RCS) and the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 1 February 2003; electronic filing regime under the Law of 2 September 2011 on eJustice and the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial Deed of Incorporation (Acte constitutif), executed before a Luxembourg notary and transmitted electronically to the RCS via eJustice, followed by mandatory publication in the Electronic Compendium of Companies and Associations (Recueil électronique des sociétés et associations, RESA).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 56 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

EUR 12,000.00 minimum share capital for a SARL under Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended by the Law of 23 August 2023, in force 12 December 2023). The capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid in cash at incorporation and deposited in a blocked subscription account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank issues a blocking certificate to the notary, and the funds are automatically released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety attestation under the Grand-Ducal Regulation of 13 February 2014 on fire prevention; structural-load and electrical-installation verification for the registered office; workplace risk assessment and ITM occupational clearance under the Law of 17 June 1994 on safety and health at work; public-liability and workplace insurance cover as a condition of the establishment permit.

Paid-in capital rule: EUR 12,000.00 minimum share capital for a SARL under Article 710-1 of the Law of 10 August 1915 (as amended by the Law of 23 August 2023, in force 12 December 2023). The capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid in cash at incorporation and deposited in a blocked subscription account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company in formation with a Luxembourg credit institution; the bank issues a blocking certificate to the notary, and the funds are automatically released only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for direct taxes; Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT and indirect taxes; integrated electronic portal: MyGuichet.lu / guichet.lu.

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg SARL must first complete incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national registration number (matricule), which functions as the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for direct taxes, VAT, and social security. Where the SARL is owned by foreign persons or the entity is a foreign permanent establishment, the foreign parent must additionally register with the Luxembourg direct tax authorities using the Form 8 non-resident tax registration or similar, and file a VAT registration application (Form 1) with the AEDT when a taxable activity in Luxembourg is carried out. For non-established foreign entities, appointment of an approved VAT representative may be required; documentary proof includes constitutional documents, powers of attorney, evidence of place of effective management, and proof of the Luxembourg establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Luxembourg SARL taxable on worldwide income; non-resident entities taxable only on Luxembourg-source income; SARL is subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax). — A Luxembourg SARL must first complete incorporation with the Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) to obtain the national registration number (matricule), which functions as the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for direct taxes, VAT, and social security. For non-established foreign entities, appointment of an approved VAT representative may be required; documentary proof includes constitutional documents, powers of attorney, evidence of place of effective management, and proof of the Luxembourg establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident Luxembourg SARL taxable on worldwide income; non-resident entities taxable only on Luxembourg-source income; SARL is subject to corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax).

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include the VAT grouping election (assujetti unique), the corporate income tax fiscal unity election for resident entities held directly or indirectly at least 95%, and elections for certain ATAD hybrid mismatch adjustments. Certain Luxembourg-specific regimes such as the participation exemption and financial instruments provisions require timely election in the annual tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for Luxembourg-established small enterprises; non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold and must register in Luxembourg when making taxable supplies there unless the reverse charge or EU place-of-supply rules shift the liability.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT if the supplier proves physical dispatch or transport outside EU territory within the legally prescribed period and retains transport/customs evidence. Cross-border B2B services to EU customers are reverse-charged to the customer; B2B services to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT (effectively zero-rated) where the customer receives the service at its non-EU establishment. Financial card-issuing and payment processing services in the scope of Article 135 VAT Directive are generally VAT-exempt without credit; export services of technical, electronic, or management nature require written customer declarations, contracts, and bank/payment records demonstrating the non-Luxembourg establishment of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (AES/DMS customs electronic declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Airwaybill
  • Transport Evidence (packing list, container tracking, delivery confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Payment/SWIFT Evidence for settlement of the export sale
  • Written Customer Statement or Contract for non-EU service recipient status

Luxembourg does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, stamping, or mandatory API-based approval of e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is mandatory in the B2G sector using PEPPOL/Factur-X templates; for B2B transactions, electronic invoices are freely issued without clearance if the recipient consents. Monthly and annual VAT returns, recapitulative statements (ECS), and Intrastat filings are submitted electronically through the AEDT portal. E-invoices must be retained in original form for 10 years.

Luxembourg authorities may retrospectively assess VAT and claw back zero-rating benefits if export formalities or proof of physical export are deficient. The ordinary reassessment period is 5 years; in cases of intentional fraud, the period is 10 years. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to default interest (0.6% monthly), fines of 50% to 100% of the evaded VAT, criminal penalties including imprisonment, and joint and several liability of statutory directors. Substantiation requires full chain of custody documents, bank settlement records, and contemporaneous transport evidence to survive audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro convertibility on both current and capital account. No exchange controls, no licensing of FX operations, and no restriction on dividend or profit remittance for the SARL. The only fiscal friction is the 15% Luxembourg dividend withholding tax (reduced to 0% for qualifying parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or Luxembourg participation exemption, and minimised under tax treaties). Cash transfers out of Luxembourg are unrestricted provided Regulation (EU) 2015/847 (wire transfer information) and the amended Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT are satisfied. No distinction between resident and non-resident accounts for convertibility purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Luxembourg imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to route export receipts through BCL. Corporate distribution and settlement receipts may be held freely in EUR or foreign currency. The residual compliance duty is statistical: the receiving bank files the BCL balance-of-payments declaration (ECB/BCL direct investment and services codes) for each qualifying cross-border transaction, and the SARL must maintain accurate back-up documentation for five years for CSSF and tax audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) as the national central bank, with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as the competent licensing and AML/CFT supervisory authority for payment institutions and e-money institutions, and the European Central Bank (ECB) for single monetary policy and TARGET2 oversight. Luxembourg maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration; capital-movement rules derive from EU Treaty Articles 63–65 and the amended Law of 10 November 2009 on payment services and e-money.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Under the amended Law of 10 November 2009 and PSD2, an EU/EEA licensed EMI may provide accounts in Luxembourg through a passported branch or cross-border service, but a non-EU offshore digital EMI without substance cannot serve as the sole liquidity or safeguarding bank for the SARL because client card-funds must be safeguarded with a CSSF-licensed credit institution or equivalent. A licensed Luxembourg or passported EMI is acceptable for operational e-wallet functions only where it independently satisfies CSSF outsourcing and safeguarding requirements; unregulated offshore EMIs are contractually and legally unacceptable as the primary clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The statutory €12,000 minimum share capital and any additional shareholder pre-funding must be transferred by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's blocked capital account at a Luxembourg credit institution. The remittance must carry the reference 'apport en capital – souscription d'actions SARL' together with the notary deed date and RCS file number, and the paying bank must transmit the full originator and beneficiary information required by Regulation (EU) 2015/847. The receiving bank will classify the transaction under the ECB/BCL balance-of-payments code for inward direct investment-equity capital for statistical reporting; funds remain frozen until the notarial deed is filed with the RCS. For intra-group shareholder loans above de-minimis, the bank requires the signed loan agreement, an interest-rate schedule, and a statement of the intended use to prevent Luxembourg thin-capitalisation recharacterisation. Capital can be received in any convertible currency without conversion obligation, although the bank may set a foreign-currency holding policy; the notary's attestation and the RBE extract must accompany the first draw.EEA disbursements are executed via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for small-value flows and TARGET2/RTGS for high-value same-day settlements; non-EEA supplier payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with the contracting party's IBAN/BIC and structured remittance fields. For high-value procurement of physical goods or regulated services, the SARL may use SWIFT MT700/MT710 documentary credits with the sponsor bank holding the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and certificate of origin; this is mandatory where the supplier requires payment-against-documentation. Card-network interchange is settled through the Mastercard/Visa clearing-and-settlement interface directly into the SARL's dedicated settlement account at the sponsor bank with D+1 net settlement and irrevocable clearing per network rules; any delay in funding the settlement account attracts a network chargeback and liquidity fine. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. Under the amended Law of 10 November 2009 and PSD2, an EU/EEA licensed EMI may provide accounts in Luxembourg through a passported branch or cross-border service, but a non-EU offshore digital EMI without substance cannot serve as the sole liquidity or safeguarding bank for the SARL because client card-funds must be safeguarded with a CSSF-licensed credit institution or equivalent. A licensed Luxembourg or passported EMI is acceptable for operational e-wallet functions only where it independently satisfies CSSF outsourcing and safeguarding requirements; unregulated offshore EMIs are contractually and legally unacceptable as the primary clearing bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue triggers CbC/Master File obligations; a Local File should be contemporaneously prepared for the SARL when the entity's related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 in a fiscal year, and is mandatory in practice where card scheme fee, technology licence, or treasury transactions exceed EUR 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not operate a formal ESR scorecard regime akin to UAE Economic Substance Regulations, but applies a substantive anti-abuse and substance doctrine through domestic GAAR, ATAD-transposed rules, DTA limitation-of-benefits clauses, DAC6, and the requirement that a société à responsabilité limitée have real local management, decision-making, staff, office premises, and operational control over card programme contracts, scheme management, settlement accounts, and treasury functions.

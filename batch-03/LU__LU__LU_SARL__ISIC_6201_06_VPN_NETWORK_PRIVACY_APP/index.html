Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum fully subscribed and paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL; the capital must be deposited before notarization in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and is released to the company only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense certification is triggered for a standard office tenancy without public access. The employer must comply with the general occupational health and safety duties enforced by the Inspection du travail et des mines (ITM), including a workplace risk assessment, fire safety measures (accessible extinguishers, escape routes) and, if staff are hired, a declaration to the CCSS; fire service approval is required only if the premises belong to the class of établissements recevant du public (ERP) or exceed specified floor areas.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum fully subscribed and paid-up share capital of EUR 12,000 for an SARL; the capital must be deposited before notarization in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Luxembourg credit institution and is released to the company only upon RCS registration.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for direct taxation and Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AED) for VAT; unified company and tax registration portal: guichet.lu / Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) via the Single Registration Desk (RBE).

E-government filing portal

A Luxembourg Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) must first complete company incorporation with the Trade and Companies Register (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS), obtaining the Luxembourg national identification number (matricule d'identification). VAT registration is then mandatory for taxable economic activities; the foreign or newly resident entity files the VAT registration application electronically via the guichet.lu business portal, with the AED issuing a Luxembourg VAT identification number (numéro de TVA LU). Non-established foreign entities that make taxable supplies in Luxembourg without a fixed establishment must register directly for VAT and may be required to appoint an approved VAT representative in Luxembourg; there is no de minimis VAT registration threshold for non-established suppliers. The same matricule is used as the national tax identification number, and the VAT number must be quoted on all invoices and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. A SARL is classified as a fully taxable resident corporate entity from the moment of incorporation, subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur le revenu des collectivités), municipal business tax (impôt commercial communal), and net wealth tax (impôt sur la fortune). Available statutory elections relevant to taxation include the election to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 56bis of the VAT law, and the option to enter the Luxembourg corporate tax consolidation regime for resident group members with at least 95% ownership, allowing income and expense aggregation for CIT purposes. No fiscal transparency election is available for a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover for the Luxembourg small-business VAT exemption for domestic established businesses; EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-sales threshold for B2C electronic and VPN/privacy services supplied to EU customers via the One Stop Shop (OSS); zero registration threshold for non-established foreign businesses making taxable supplies in Luxembourg.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating on exports to non-EU destinations applies when the supplier exports the goods itself, exports them on behalf of the non-resident customer, or otherwise proves that the goods have physically left the EU within the legally required time limits, accompanied by a valid customs export declaration. For services: VPN, encryption software, and related digital/network services supplied to a non-EU business customer fall outside Luxembourg VAT under the general B2B place-of-supply rule unless the effective use and enjoyment is located in Luxembourg; services supplied to non-EU non-taxable persons are outside Luxembourg VAT when the customer is resident outside the EU and the effective use is outside the EU. VPN privacy services supplied to EU private consumers remain taxable in Luxembourg or via OSS/e-commerce simplification, excluding 0% zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (Electronic Export / Export Accompanying Document lodged through Luxembourg Customs / EU Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing delivery to the non-EU destination
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank payment confirmation establishing receipt of the foreign sales consideration
  • Commercial invoice, proforma invoice, packing list, and proof of transport (CMR, sea waybill, airway bill or port receipts)

Luxembourg currently has no general real-time continuous transaction control, fiscal clearance, or mandatory digital stamping regime for B2B or B2C VAT invoices. Mandatory electronic invoicing applies only for the public procurement (B2G) context through the PEPPOL network. There is no API-based pre-clearance requirement for issuing invoices; however, all invoices must satisfy Articles 56-59 of the Luxembourg VAT law, including integrity-of-content and authenticity requirements, and standardised invoice fields with the LU-VAT number and seller identification. Future EU-wide e-invoicing and e-reporting standards based on the VAT in the Digital Age package will progressively replace current rules.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive documentary audit by the Luxembourg VAT administration. If the supporting export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, or fraudulent, the AED will recharacterise the supply as locally taxable at 17% VAT and issue an assessment for the tax due, plus default interest of 0.8% per month or part thereof for late/VAT withheld amounts, and administrative fines ranging from approximately 10% to 100% of the underpaid tax depending on good faith, negligence, or intentional concealment. Criminal penalties may be imposed for VAT fraud, including imprisonment and fines under Luxembourg fiscal criminal law, and the limitation period for reassessment is extended to 10 years in cases of fraud. Export zero-rating refunds may be clawed back with retrospective effect, including rejection of exporters' refund claims and inclusion of the taxpayer's name in customs risk-targeting databases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Luxembourg franc has been superseded by the euro; Luxembourg, as a member of the euro area, imposes no foreign exchange controls on either current account or capital account transactions. Capital movements, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds are freely convertible and remittable, subject to EU anti-money laundering, sanctions, and tax compliance obligations. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted, but a 15% domestic withholding tax applies to dividends unless reduced pursuant to the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% under qualifying conditions), a bilateral double tax treaty, or the Luxembourg tax exemption regime. The Luxembourg tax administration may require evidence of economic substance, beneficial ownership declarations, and compliance with anti-abuse rules (e.g., GAAR) before treaty benefits or withholding tax relief are granted. Practical hurdles are mainly documentary, not regulatory, including the need to file annual accounts with the Luxembourg Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés and to maintain appropriate substance for a société à responsabilité limitée (SARL).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements in Luxembourg or the euro area. Export receipts, including revenues from VPN subscription licences, technical support services, and software royalties, may be fully retained in any currency, held on EUR or foreign-currency accounts, and freely used for working capital or investment. No mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline exists. For balance-of-payments statistical reporting, resident companies must report cross-border transactions above applicable thresholds to the Banque centrale du Luxembourg on a quarterly or annual basis, but such reporting is purely statistical and does not restrict currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) acting within the Eurosystem and in conjunction with the European Central Bank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore non-EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally recognised as credit institutions for general corporate banking purposes in Luxembourg; they cannot open a domestic payment account with a Luxembourg IBAN unless licensed under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) or operating through an EEA-licensed partner. EEA-licensed fintech and EMI platforms can passport their services into Luxembourg and may provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, payment processing, and FX conversion; however, corporate treasury operations, capital escrow arrangements, and material liquidity buffers are better held at a Luxembourg-authorised bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-regulated EMIs when used as auxiliary disbursement rails, but Low for offshore non-EEA EMIs as a primary treasury solution due to regulatory non-recognition, correspondent banking risk, and AML scrutiny.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Anonymity-enhanced payment flows and cryptocurrency denomination of subscription fees

Defense evidence: Detailed transaction monitoring controls, an anti-money laundering policy covering crypto-asset red flags, complete list of accepted payment processors (e.g., Stripe, Paymentwall), copy of the merchant agreements, risk assessment showing no crypto-outset or mixing activities, and user onboarding logs proving source-of-funds verification for any large B2B payments.
High-risk counterparty or customer base in jurisdictions with weak AML standards

Root cause: High-risk counterparty or customer base in jurisdictions with weak AML standards

Defense evidence: Country-by-country customer penetration analysis, IP-geolocation evidence, export-control classification for encryption software, acceptable-use policy prohibiting sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctions screening results (EU, UN, OFAC), and a contractual clause requiring resellers and distributors to comply with EU restrictive measures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Luxembourg SARL should be routed via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (EUR, within EEA) or SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments in USD or GBP. The remittance instruction must specify the payment purpose using the EU Balance of Payments coding convention: for equity capital, the purpose code 'E' (Direct Investment – Equity) or the IMF BPM6 code 'Direct Investment' is appropriate, accompanied by a shareholder resolution and subscription agreement. Luxembourg banks require verification of the source of funds through bank statements, tax returns, or audited financial statements of the contributing shareholder. The corporate bank must be notified under KYC procedures before the transfer; copies of the signed board minutes approving the capital increase, the notarised deed if a SARL statutory capital change necessitates it, and the registration filing with the Luxembourg Register de Commerce et des Sociétés must be provided. For inbound investment in the form of a shareholder loan, a written loan agreement and a detailed purpose code for 'Other Investment – Loans' must be submitted. Direct investment capital flows are freely permitted, but Luxembourg banks may request an economic substance declaration confirming the SARL's administrative and operational presence in Luxembourg.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade and vendor payments is the SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated disbursements to EEA beneficiaries, with SWIFT MT103 used for non-EUR and non-EEA transactions. For larger SaaS infrastructure purchases or hardware-related licensing, a commercial letter of credit (LC) may be used if the supplier requires documentary collection, but Luxembourg commercial practice for IT services is generally via irrevocable telegraphic transfer. All outbound payments must be supported by an underlying commercial invoice, contract, purchase order, or service agreement; the bank will apply sanctions screening and AML transaction monitoring. For recurring disbursements to cloud providers, data centre operators, and international bandwidth carriers, standing instructions with predetermined thresholds and sub-authorisation rules can be established. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% aggregate effective rate for Luxembourg City (FY2024), composed of corporate income tax (IRC) at 15% on the first EUR 175,000 of taxable income and 17% above EUR 175,000, plus a 7% solidarity surcharge on the IRC, plus municipal business tax (ICC) at the Luxembourg City rate of 6.75%. The aggregate marginal rate is projected at 25.28% for FY2025 when the solidarity surcharge rises to 9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a SARL to its shareholders. The rate is reduced to 0% for qualified corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% shareholding held for at least 12 months) and to 5% or 15% under applicable double taxation agreements where the shareholder is the beneficial owner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed by 31 May of the year following the tax year. Automatic extension to 31 December is granted where the return is filed electronically by an approved tax adviser; a further reasoned postponement may be requested from the Administration des contributions directes (ACD).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the Luxembourg SARL's annual net turnover exceeds EUR 1,000,000 and the entity engages in controlled cross-border transactions with related enterprises. Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: mandatory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Electronic CbCR filing is made through the ACD's dedicated e-filing channel; documentation must be produced within 30 days upon request.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but substantive economic activity is enforced through: (i) the codified general anti-abuse rule (article 6 of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law, as applied through the abuse-of-law doctrine); (ii) the requirement of genuine management and real activities to establish Luxembourg tax residence and to access treaty benefits (including the MLI Principal Purpose Test); (iii) ATAD implementation, namely the 30% EBITDA interest limitation rule, CFC rules, anti-hybrid rules, and exit taxation; and (iv) administrative practice requiring substance for APA applications, transfer pricing adjustments, and the benefit of tax incentives. A SARL operating a VPN/privacy software business must demonstrably perform strategic management, contracting, staff supervision, and R&D decisions in Luxembourg, maintain an active bank account, hold periodic board meetings with actual participation, and employ adequate qualified personnel, otherwise it is exposed to recharacterization as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Ordinary tax fraud under Article 176 LIR: fine of EUR 10,000 to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years. Aggravated tax fraud involving cross-border artificial arrangements or a tax loss exceeding EUR 25,000: imprisonment from 1 to 10 years and fine up to EUR 250,000, in addition to reassessments, a 10% penalty, monthly interest at 0.8%, and possible publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Managers (gérants) and directors who knowingly sign or cause the filing of false declarations are criminally liable and may be subject to joint and several civil liability for the tax deficit under the action en comblement du passif; in serious cross-border cases, travel bans, provisional arrest, and enforcement against personal assets are available.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to the arm's length result, with a penalty surcharge of 10% on the additional assessment; late-payment interest at 0.8% per month; failure to file a valid CbCR or documentation can attract fines up to EUR 250,000; artificially structured mispricing may be recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to 15% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the incremental tax and penalties where they caused or tolerated the incorrect transfer pricing policy without professional advice; criminal liability arises if the documentation is deliberately falsified; civil seizure of personal property is possible where the company is insolvent.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of Luxembourg tax residence, recharacterization under the GAAR, application or denial of treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test, reassessment of profits with 0.8% monthly interest and a 10% penalty; possible loss of the net wealth tax SME exemption and exposure to full 0.5% net wealth tax; the Luxembourg authorities may also strike the company from the RCS for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners risk personal liability for the company's unpaid taxes where the corporate form is used as a fraudulent device; criminal sanctions for falsification of financial statements, travel restrictions, and prohibition from managing companies in Luxembourg may be imposed.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Constructive Dividends

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full 15% withholding tax on the payer SARL, plus a 10% penalty and monthly late interest; where the foreign shareholder lacks treaty substance, the treaty relief is rejected and the standard 15% WHT is recovered from the distributing company; penalties for failure to withhold are assessed directly on the directors.

Director personal liability: The manager who makes a distribution without collecting the WHT becomes personally liable to the tax authorities for the full withheld amount; criminal fines of EUR 1,000 to EUR 5,000 apply to recalcitrant officers; in fraud cases, imprisonment and travel bans are possible.
AML and Payment Processing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Luxembourg AML Law of 12 November 2004 as amended and CSSF Regulation 12-02 impose administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; if the SARL is deemed to engage in crypto-asset services without authorization, cease-and-desist orders and sanctioning of the payment service providers follow; freezes of funds and termination of processing agreements by Stripe and Paymentwall are typical operational consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted under Articles 506-1 to 506-8 of the Luxembourg Criminal Code for money laundering, with imprisonment up to 5 years and criminal fines up to EUR 1,250,000; civil seizure, travel bans, and prohibition on exercising commercial functions apply; supervisors and compliance officers are individually liable for the absence of effective AML controls.

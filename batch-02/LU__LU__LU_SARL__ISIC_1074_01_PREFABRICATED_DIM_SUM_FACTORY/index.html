Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Luxembourg Inland Revenue (Administration des contributions directes, ACD) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax; Registration Duties, Estates and VAT Authority (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA, AEDT) for VAT. Electronic portals: MyGuichet (myguichet.lu) and guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

A foreign founder must first incorporate a SARL before a Luxembourg notary and file the articles of association with the Luxembourg Business Registers (RCS), obtaining a 13-digit RCS registration number which constitutes the national business/matricule number. The RCS number is then used as the TIN for direct tax purposes without separate registration, but the company must file a corporate taxpayer profile with the ACD. For VAT, the SARL must submit Form 640 (VAT registration application) to the AEDT before starting taxable activities, accompanied by the RCS extract, identity documents of directors/partners, evidence of business premises, and a description of intended supplies. Non-resident entities without a fixed establishment must appoint a Luxembourg tax representative unless using the OSS/IOSS scheme; the AEDT then issues a Luxembourg VAT number beginning with 'LU' followed by eight characters.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Luxembourg has no U.S.-style check-the-box or pass-through election for SARLs; SARLs are transparently not considered. Statutory elections available include: election for fiscal unity (integration fiscale) for corporate income tax and municipal business tax among at least 95% owned resident group companies; option to form a VAT group; election to apply the special IP (patent and IP box) regime for qualifying intangible income; irrevocable election for the notional interest deduction; and option for the simplified VAT flat-rateâreimbursement in certain sectors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 30,000 annual taxable turnover for the small undertaking exemption; VAT registration becomes mandatory above this threshold. Non-resident taxable persons supplying goods or services in Luxembourg are required to register from the first euro unless the supply is covered by OSS/IOSS or is a B2B service where the reverse charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided (i) the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party acting on behalf of the customer; (ii) export occurs within the legal time limits (normally goods must leave the EU within 90 days of the supply date or the discretionary longer period must be documented); (iii) the supplier obtains and retains documentary proof of export, including customs exit confirmation; and (iv) the invoice clearly states that the supply is an export subject to zero-rating. Offshore services, ancillary transport, loading, warehousing, and processing services directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated, or are outside the scope of Luxembourg VAT where the place of supply is located outside Luxembourg under B2B place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) with exit stamp or electronic PLDA export confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or other transport document (CMR waybill, AWB, rail consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of payment for the exported goods
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Export/sanitary certificate for food products (including EU health and phytosanitary certificates for buns/dim sum)

Luxembourg does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no API-based invoice stamping, and no digital mark or QR code mandate for B2B/B2C invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only for B2G invoices to public authorities, which must be submitted electronically via the PEPPOL network through the Invoice.lu platform. All VAT invoices must follow EU invoicing rules, and copies must be retained for at least six (or, in practice, ten) years for audit purposes.

The AEDT may subject zero-rating claims to documentary audit and may retrospectively claw back VAT at the standard 17% rate plus default interest if the exporter cannot demonstrate physical export within the statutory period or if documentation is incomplete, false, or fraudulent. Penalties under Luxembourg VAT law include administrative fines up to EUR 2,500, a recovery surcharge of 10-20% of the tax due, and late-payment interest running from the original due date. Intentional VAT evasion or the deliberate use of fake customs declarations constitutes criminal tax fraud, punishable on conviction by imprisonment of up to five years and/or penal tax fines, and extends the reassessment limitation period from five to ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full convertibility of both current and capital account transactions for EUR and all freely convertible currencies. No prior administrative authorisation is required for payment of imports, repatriation of proceeds, dividends, interest, loan repayments or liquidation distributions to non-resident shareholders, subject to corporate law formalities and tax compliance. Profit remittance requires audited statutory accounts, a board decision proposing distribution, a general meeting resolution declaring the dividend, and compliance with Luxembourg dividend withholding tax and any relevant tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may freely retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts, convert at commercial bank rates, or net them against import obligations. All such transactions must merely be reported by the local bank for BCL balance-of-payments statistical purposes pursuant to the BCL external transactions reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), in conjunction with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) as banking and AML supervisory authority; foreign exchange controls are abolished under EU free movement of capital rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a sole or primary banking relationship. Luxembourg law, via the amended Law of 10 August 1915 on commercial companies and the PSD2 implementing Law of 8 June 2017, permits electronic money institutions (EMIs) licensed by the CSSF or a passporting EU authority to provide payment accounts; however, a Luxembourg SARL's subscription capital must be paid into a bank account, and standard banking KYC/reference requirements under domestic AML law (Law of 12 November 2004, as amended) do not afford EMIs the same unsecured credit depth as Tier-1 banks. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport are not legally viable as an account provider for a Luxembourg holding/operating entity and will be rejected by auditors and counterparties. An EU-licensed EMI may serve only as a supplementary settlement sub-channel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% for Luxembourg City (17% CIT + 1.19% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); the aggregate rate ranges across communes with varying local multipliers.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% standard Luxembourg dividend withholding tax; 0% for qualifying Luxembourg/EU parent companies with at least 10% shareholding held for at least 12 months under the domestic participation exemption and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; DTA rates may reduce dividend WHT to 0-15% depending on ownership, residence, and beneficial ownership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR; mandatory OECD Local File/Master File is expected when the Luxembourg entity exceeds at least two of the 'large undertaking' thresholds (total assets > EUR 4,400,000; net turnover > EUR 8,800,000; average employees > 50) or when the multinational group must file CbC reports (consolidated revenue >= EUR 750,000,000); below these thresholds the ACD may still demand contemporaneous documentation within 60 days.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a UAE-style ESR, but economic substance is decisive under ATAD, BEPS Action 5, tax abuse case law, and the participation exemption. A frozen-food operating SARL must maintain in-country premises, operative staff, inventory, decision-making board meetings, local bank accounts, logistics control, and real financial risk; a conduit arrangement will be disregarded for tax purposes and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty of up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 250,000 or up to half of the evaded tax; automatic 10% administrative surcharge; 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; full reassessment and loss of beneficial tax regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations become personally criminally liable and may be banned from managing Luxembourg companies; the corporate veil can be pierced where funds are diverted, enabling civil restitution, asset seizures, and travel bans.
Artificial or shell company arrangement (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: The ACD disregards the artificial entity under ATAD/GAAR and abuse-of-law doctrine; profits are reallocated to the true economic owner, DTA and EU directive benefits are denied, plus 10% surcharge and 0.6% monthly late-payment interest; administrative fines for false documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for tax debts where the shell structure is used to strip assets; Luxembourg courts can disqualify directors, impose civil restitution obligations, and issue travel bans during fraud investigations.
Transfer pricing failure

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to the arm's-length price; secondary recharacterization as hidden dividend/contribution; penalty of 10% on the excess tax and interest at 0.6% per month; failure to supply documentation reverses the taxpayer's evidentiary position and triggers daily fines.

Director personal liability: Senior managers are personally accountable for maintaining contemporaneous TP documentation; if penalties force insolvency, directors may face creditor claims for wrongful management and travel bans during judicial examinations.
AML / sanctions risk in trade finance and wire settlements

Statutory sanction: Under Luxembourg AML/CFT law, fines up to EUR 2.5 million for legal persons and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; obligatory disgorgement of proceeds; freezing of company bank accounts; removal from commercial registry and regulatory disqualification.

Director personal liability: AML duties cannot be fully delegated; directors are subject to personal fines and disqualification; investigating judges may impose asset freezes, passport surrender, and travel bans where asset flight is feared.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean law imposes no minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa LLC. The subscribed capital must be fully paid at incorporation in cash or kind, but no blocked escrow account is required. For laboratory operation, the company must, however, maintain a funded current account sufficient to pay the ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation fee and civil defense installation costs.

Funding & unlock path: Must obtain a Fire Safety and Fire Resistance Certificate from the National Fire Agency under the Fire Service Act, covering structural load inspection of the test floor, fire-rated wall integrity, emergency exit signage, automatic fire suppression for battery test cells, and ventilation for hazardous materials storage. Additionally, must obtain an Occupational Safety and Health clearance from the Ministry of Employment and Labor (MOEL) certifying compliance with Workplace Health Standard No. 29027, including laboratory chemical exposure limits, health surveillance for employees handling lithium batteries, and ergonomic design of drop-test stations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic filing and registration portal: HomeTax (Hometax) at www.hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service for export declarations through the UNI-PASS single-window system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity obtains its Korean tax identity either by incorporating a Yuhan Hoesa or by registering a domestic place of business of a foreign corporation. The NTS assigns one 10-digit Business Registration Number that functions as the corporate TIN, VAT registration number and employer registration number. The exhaustive procedure is: (1) complete incorporation of the Yuhan Hoesa or register the foreign corporation's domestic place of business with the competent registry; (2) appoint a Korean tax representative with a domestic address and execute a registered power of attorney if the foreign entity has no fixed domestic business establishment; (3) file the NTS-prescribed Business Registration Application with the competent District Tax Office, either through HomeTax or in person, within 20 days of commencing business or beginning VAT-taxable supplies; (4) attach the certified articles of incorporation, apostilled or consularized foreign registration certificate, proof of the Korean business address (lease or ownership), proof of foreign direct investment or share issuance, and the representative's identity documents; (5) for an export-only laboratory, identify the export zero-rating category on the VAT registrations and retain the planned evidence file; and (6) upon acceptance, receive the Business Registration Certificate with the final 10-digit number and confirm the active VAT registration in HomeTax. A foreign non-resident entity that has no Korean business establishment and supplies only electronic services to Korean consumers may instead use the simplified HomeTax registration procedure for non-resident electronic service suppliers, where that simplified category applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box or similar pass-through election. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income. The only optional corporate election relevant to a wholly-owned domestic group is the consolidated corporate tax return regime for a domestic parent and its 100%-owned domestic subsidiaries. No hybrid mismatch election is available; hybrid mismatch adjustments operate mandatorily under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No revenue-based registration threshold (KRW 0) for a domestic corporation: a Yuhan Hoesa must register for VAT from the commencement of business regardless of turnover. For individual sole traders only, the simplified taxable person threshold is KRW 80,000,000 in annual taxable supplies, but such persons still obtain a business registration number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rating applies where: (a) goods are physically exported from Korea and the export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service; (b) services are supplied to a non-resident and the place of supply is deemed outside Korea, including qualifying technical testing and analysis services performed in Korea for an overseas principal when the results are delivered abroad and the consideration is paid in foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank; (c) services are supplied for the export of goods, such as inspection and testing of export-destined batteries; and (d) qualifying offshore digital services are effectively used and enjoyed outside Korea. Zero-rated export status must be substantiated in the VAT return and preserves the input VAT deduction and refund entitlement. Supplies of otherwise zero-rated services to Korean consumers, or offshore digital services effectively used and enjoyed in Korea, remain subject to the standard 10% VAT and may require the non-resident entity to register under the simplified non-resident electronic service supplier procedure.

  • Korea Customs Service accepted Export Declaration (export clearance confirmation) generated in UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading for sea shipment or Air Waybill for air shipment showing the non-resident consignee and the Korean exporter
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the exported goods or re-exported test samples
  • Foreign exchange bank settlement confirmation or inward remittance receipt proving collection of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency (Korean equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • For exported testing services: signed service contract with the non-resident principal, delivery evidence of the test report or results, and foreign exchange bank remittance certificate showing payment for the service

The Value-Added Tax Act mandates the electronic tax invoice system. All VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices in the NTS-prescribed format and transmit them to the National Tax Service through HomeTax or through an NTS-certified ERP/API integration. The NTS validates the received data and uses the central electronic tax invoice matching database as the anti-fraud fiscal clearance mechanism; there is no separate physical fiscal stamp in Korea. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted no later than the day following the supply date, except where the NTS quarterly or monthly aggregate filing rules apply, and B2C supplies are additionally reported through the mandatory NTS cash receipt system. Failure to transmit or non-compliant invoicing triggers statutory additional taxes and may lead to business suspension or criminal referral.

Export zero-rating claims are jointly scrutinized by the National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service. The taxpayer must retain the complete export evidence file for the statutory record-keeping period and make it available on demand. If physical export is not proven, the service is found to be effectively used in Korea, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, the NTS will disallow the zero-rating, assess the unpaid VAT at the standard 10% rate, and impose statutory additional taxes. The misreporting surcharge can be as high as 40% of the underreported VAT for fraudulent non-declaration or false documentation, and up to 20% for negligent underreporting; the NTS may reopen a tax return for 5 years in ordinary cases and up to 10 years for fraudulent or omitted returns. In serious cases, the file is referred to criminal enforcement under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and corporate officers or the Korean tax representative may be subject to imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised capital account with a rigorous reporting and surveillance framework rather than direct exchange controls. The Korean won is convertible for current account payments, trade settlements, service fees, dividends, and branch profit remittances, provided the transaction is routed through a designated foreign exchange bank and coded under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital account inflows, including equity contributions to a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC, require either prior approval or a post-transaction filing with the designated bank, and the resulting foreign investment must be reported because the ultimate foreign owner is non-resident. For profit remittance, the blocking points are corporate tax clearance, audited financial statements, a board resolution approving the distribution, and documented underlying receivables; the bank will also query tax payments before any dividend outflow. Export proceeds in the service-export model represented by battery safety testing must be collected within 180 days of the service invoice date and repatriated to Korea unless the bank grants a foreign-currency retention approval. Although convertibility is high, inconsistent corporate purpose codes, late surrender of export proceeds, or unexplained cross-border flows will trigger financial intelligence unit reporting and account suspension, so treasury must maintain a complete evidentiary trail for every leg of the transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment or, for services, from the date the service is completed and the invoice is issued. The proceeds must be surrendered to a designated foreign exchange bank and converted into KRW unless the exporter holds an approval to maintain a foreign currency account; standard mandatory conversion is effectively 100% in the absence of such approval. For UN38.3 battery testing and CE-RED electromagnetic laboratory services, each incoming payment must be matched against the testing service contract and filed under the current-account service purpose code, with test certificates and itemised invoices available for the bank's FEIS declaration and audit trail.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the statutory foreign exchange policy administrator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; operational execution by designated foreign exchange banks and the Korea Customs Service through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury onboarding and prohibited for statutory Korean banking functions. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut are not licensed as designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot file FEIS declarations, issue Korean capital verification certificates, process KYC for a Korean resident corporation, or serve as the settlement account for Korean won disbursements. Wise may be used only as an auxiliary non-KRW payment rail for overseas vendor payments where the Korean entity maintains its principal corporate account at a licensed tier-1 bank and the bank-side reconciliation, beneficial owner verification, and tax declaration obligations remain at that licensed bank. This conditional use is not a substitute for the legally recognised banking infrastructure required for cross-border capital transactions in Korea.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder should execute a SWIFT MT103 transfer to the Korean entity's Capital Escrow Account at a designated foreign exchange bank, using the direct-investment or equity-contribution purpose code required by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The bank will check the remitter and beneficiary names, UBO declaration, board resolution, and source of funds, then issue a certificate of foreign exchange purchase after the funds are credited. The next step is capital verification by a certified Korean public accountant or bank treasury officer, followed by FIPA registration with the Help Center for Investment or the designated bank if the equity contribution exceeds the statutory foreign investment threshold; lower-value contributions must still be declared through the Foreign Exchange Information System. Only after capital verification should the funds be swept into the operational KRW account, and every inbound payment must be traceable back to the approved capital injection to avoid future dividend-repatriation complications.Outgoing trade and service payments should use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with the correct Korean foreign exchange purpose code and supporting invoices, contracts, and, where applicable, customs declarations. For large purchases of battery testing hardware or CE-RED measurement equipment, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued through the tier-1 Korean bank is the preferred clearing rail because it protects advance payments and holds the supplier to documentary compliance. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for established suppliers with a reliable trade history where full L/C cost is unjustified. For all outbound payments, the bank will require the underlying service agreement, supplier due diligence, and proof that the payment is consistent with the entity's declared testing laboratory operations; the business manager must retain copies of every certificate and test report related to the settled invoice.Deliverable KRW/USD forward contracts of up to 12 months for hedging genuine current-account exposures arising from export invoices and equipment import settlements, permitted through the designated bank upon submission of underlying trade contracts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on KRW/USD are available only where the counterparty is offshore and the Korean entity proves a genuine underlying business exposure, with the transaction documented and reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act derivative business rules.Cross-currency interest rate swaps and plain-vanilla currency swaps are permissible solely for hedging real cross-border loan principal and interest positions, not for speculative profit, and require board approval, bank suitability assessment, and derivative transaction reporting to the authorities.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury onboarding and prohibited for statutory Korean banking functions. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut are not licensed as designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and cannot file FEIS declarations, issue Korean capital verification certificates, process KYC for a Korean resident corporation, or serve as the settlement account for Korean won disbursements. Wise may be used only as an auxiliary non-KRW payment rail for overseas vendor payments where the Korean entity maintains its principal corporate account at a licensed tier-1 bank and the bank-side reconciliation, beneficial owner verification, and tax declaration obligations remain at that licensed bank. This conditional use is not a substitute for the legally recognised banking infrastructure required for cross-border capital transactions in Korea.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% national corporate income tax for taxable income exceeding KRW 200 million; 10% bracket below KRW 200 million; 20% bracket between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion; plus 10% local surtax on national tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax + 2% local surtax) on cash and constructive dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% in certain tax-free reorganisations only after pre-approval by the Ministry of Economy and Finance

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation when the aggregate amount of controlled transactions (including sales of goods, purchases of services, intangible licence fees, and loans) exceeds KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year; Master File required when the aggregate amount exceeds KRW 50 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting required for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue above KRW 1 trillion (or USD 850 million equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Although South Korea does not apply a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, the substance-over-form principle is embedded in the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act. A battery safety testing lab performing UN38.3 drop tests must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea: possession of licensed test chambers, qualified electrical safety engineers, operational decision-making offices in Korea, physical performance of drop, shock, vibration and crush testing, and maintenance of project data and test reports on Korean soil. If the Korean entity merely forwards invoices to a foreign related office while testing is performed elsewhere, the NTS may recharacterise the revenue as foreign PE income, impose TP adjustment, deny the treaty benefit, and treat the entity as a shell liable for penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Test Certificate Fabrication and Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Under Article 103 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and Article 66 of the Corporate Tax Act, the National Tax Service may impose a negligence penalty of 40% of the underreported tax and an unlawfully claimed refund penalty of 40%; criminal fine up to 5 times the evaded amount and seizure of the testing equipment and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or participated in falsification are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of all evaded tax and penalties; the NTS can impose a travel ban (destination restriction) and blacklist the director as habitual defaulter; criminal conviction carries imprisonment for a term of up to 3 years (or 5 years for aggravated fraud under the special tax offence law).
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 11: infringement surcharge of up to 30% of the transaction value and an income adjustment year causing an additional tax of 10% of the adjustment amount; Article 18 thin capitalisation rule disallows interest on loans exceeding twice the equity investment from a non-resident controlling shareholder, adding the excess to taxable income and applying a 22% withholding tax on deemed dividend.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for gross negligence in maintaining transfer pricing documentation, and the court may pierce the corporate veil if the entity is operated as an alter ego of the foreign parent. Principal directors face civil asset restitution up to the total unpaid amount and a criminal sentence of imprisonment up to 1 year for failure to comply within 60 days of a demand order.
AML and Shell Company Concealment – Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Articles 9 and 17 impose a fine of up to KRW 100 million, and the Financial Intelligence Unit may suspend payment rails; proceeds hidden in overseas accounts are subject to forfeiture; a criminal penalty for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act Article 25 carries a non-conditional fine of up to KRW 200 million and imprisonment for a term of not less than 3 years up to a maximum of 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directorship in a shell where the actual economic owner is concealed converts limited liability into unlimited personal liability; courts order the director to restore all repatriated sums, place the director on the FIU blacklist banning bank transfers and international travel, and if the concealment is convicted as wilful money laundering the director faces imprisonment for a minimum of 3 years, asset confiscation, and disqualification from serving as a corporate director for 10 years.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation must specify the number of equity interests and the amount of each member's contribution, and each member must fully perform the contribution before incorporation registration. Cash contributions must be deposited in a bank subscription account in the company's name and evidenced by a contribution certificate; no escrow or blocked-account arrangement is mandated by Korean law. In-kind contributions must be appraised by a court-appointed expert. The Korean court registration fee is not flat: registration license tax is calculated ad valorem on declared capital at 0.4 percent, subject to a statutory minimum of KRW 22,500, plus 20 percent local education tax; therefore, no fixed USD state fee is reported in the fee field.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no factory, warehouse, chemical installation, or large physical facility requires a Civil Defense fire permit or structural load inspection. As a standard office employer, Yuhan Hoesa must still comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act by providing a safe workplace, fire extinguishers, evacuation routes, and basic workplace safety instruction, and the leased building must maintain its own fire safety certification under the Act on Fire Prevention and Safety.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) via the HomeTax electronic tax portal; customs declarations and export clearances are processed through Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS

E-government filing portal

For a domestic Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the foreign investor must first file an initial foreign investment notification with KOTRA or through an authorized foreign exchange bank, then submit the standard Application for Business Registration to the competent district tax office serving the entity's registered address. The application must include the articles of incorporation, a copy of the director's identification, a lease agreement or title evidence for the domestic business address, and a statement of business purpose. Once verified, NTS issues the 10-digit business registration number and the Business Registration Certificate, which serves as the entity's Korean Tax Identification Number for corporate income tax and VAT. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Korean permanent establishment, a domestic tax representative must be appointed to complete the registration, retain VAT records, issue electronic tax invoices, and communicate with NTS through HomeTax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not operate a check-the-box election for ordinary limited liability companies. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is automatically taxed at the entity level under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and there are no hybrid mismatch elections for this standard commercial entity. Certain specially regulated investment vehicles have separate optional tax regimes, but they are inapplicable to a hyper-casual game publishing company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. All businesses, including foreign-invested domestic companies, must register for VAT from the commencement of business irrespective of revenue volume. A business that expects annual taxable supplies below the statutory simplified taxpayer ceiling may be treated as a simplified taxpayer, but registration itself is still mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to the export of tangible goods where the goods are actually shipped outside Korea and an export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service. For services, zero-rating is available for specifically enumerated service categories, including international transportation and services physically or legally consumed entirely outside Korea; in the hyper-casual mobile game context, advertising and service revenues derived from overseas publishers may qualify as zero-rated exported services when the recipient is a non-resident business, the services are used outside Korea, and payment is collected through a foreign exchange license or approved domestic settlement method. The exporter must retain documentary evidence of the export transaction, the tax invoice, and proof of foreign currency repatriation.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic Export Declaration and export clearance certificate
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing actual departure from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the exported goods or service scope
  • Foreign Exchange Repatriation Confirmation issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing inward remittance of export proceeds

Tax invoices must be issued electronically through an NTS-certified e-tax invoice system and transmitted to NTS through HomeTax. Electronic invoices must be reported to NTS no later than the day following the issuance date. Korea does not operate a real-time transaction-level fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; the mandate is a near-real-time electronic tax invoice reporting regime. For exports, an electronic export declaration is also filed with Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, and the data is made available to NTS under an integrated information-sharing mechanism.

NTS and Korea Customs Service maintain digital integration of export declarations, e-tax invoices, foreign exchange settlement records, and corporate VAT returns. If the exporter cannot substantiate zero-rating through the required documentation bundle, NTS will deny the 0% rate and assess VAT at 10% on the gross export amount, with statutory additional taxes and interest calculated from the date the refund was paid or the zero-rating was claimed. Deliberate submission of false export declarations, fabricated foreign exchange settlement certificates, or overdeclared export values exposes the company to criminal tax investigation, customs record falsification penalties, revocation of export benefits, and clawback of all refunds received together with penalties up to the amount of tax evaded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized managed-float currency regime. The Korean won (KRW) is materially convertible for both current account items, such as advertising, royalty, and software licensing payments, and capital account items, including foreign direct investment equity subscription and shareholder loans, subject to the declaration, reporting, or license duties imposed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. There is no mandatory surrender of export or service proceeds to the central bank; instead, foreign currency receipts may be held in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts at authorized foreign exchange banks. Profit remittance is permitted once solvency, audit, and corporate income tax obligations are demonstrated, but the offshore parent must provide board minutes, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and the underlying economic substance documentation for ad-network revenue to prove the outflow is a genuine distribution or service payment. Capital account transactions, including cross-border loans, acquisitions, and equity injections, are separately reportable to MOEF/BOK through the reporting bank. The practical constraint is not absolute convertibility but documentary completeness, strict foreign exchange purpose coding, and the statutory requirement that every material transaction be routed through a domestically licensed foreign exchange bank. Entities using foreign unlicensed EMI accounts for settlement or unnecessarily converting KRW offshore may encounter anti-money laundering scrutiny, delayed repatriation, and potential administrative penalties. Therefore, treasury design should treat KRW as freely usable inside South Korea but operationally dependent on a licensed domestic bank for seamless regulatory reporting and compliance clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion or surrender requirement for export/service proceeds under the Korean framework. A resident exporter must collect foreign currency revenues through an authorized foreign exchange bank and must, as a general rule, complete collection within five years from the date of export declaration for physical goods; for digital gaming services and ad-network proceeds, the collection window is managed under the same foreign exchange bank supervision, with extension available upon justifiable documentary request. The foreign currency may remain held in a foreign-currency deposit account at a domestic Tier-1 bank, and converted to KRW when operational liquidity requires. Conversion must be reported under the approved foreign exchange purpose code, and any divergence between the invoiced contract, the settlement instruction, and the underlying ad-network payout log may trigger a customs or BOK examination.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central execution authority, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting as the supreme foreign exchange administrative agency under the framework of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Licensed only as an auxiliary tool for international executives' discretionary expenses, an offshore digital EMI is legally impracticable as the primary clearing rail for this Korean entity. Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, any inbound capital injection must be notified through a designated domestic foreign exchange bank, and the company's capital verification certificate must be issued by a Korean account bank. Foreign EMIs without a Korean banking license cannot register the FDI notification, cannot issue valid domestic securities subscription receipts for capital verification, and are not admissible as a licensed foreign exchange bank for tax filings or trade settlement. South Korean supervisory practice effectively requires statutory account operation to reside onshore with a Tier-1 bank; using an EMI for core treasury would create regulatory discomfort, purpose-code failures, and AML red flags on aggregated ad-marketplace payouts. Thus, the blueprint should use domestic Tier-1 banks for both escrow and operational rails, with EMI accounts optional only for non-operational administrative expenses and never for proceeds collection or equity injection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent investor must file the Foreign Investment Notification through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank before the equity wire is released. The remitting bank will transmit a SWIFT MT103 with the investor's exact corporate name and a strict purpose code corresponding to equity investment in a Korean resident enterprise; the receiving Korean bank credits the capital escrow account under the name of the Korean entity and issues the Certificate of Import of Capital Goods where applicable. After the equity funds are credited, the bank verifies the amount against the FDI declaration, obtains a Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation, and issues the capital verification certificate required by the Commercial Act. Prior to distribution to the operational account, the bank requires the final UBO structure, the board resolution approving the share subscription, and the executed articles of incorporation. For any shareholder loan component, the offshore lender must also file a loan agreement and purpose code under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and the debt facility must be distinguished from equity in internal treasury records to avoid accidental dividend withholding characterizations.Settlement for non-digital goods and services involving Korean suppliers should use SWIFT MT103 for immediate operative balance transfers, with reference to the correct outbound foreign exchange purpose code and the supporting contract, invoice, and customs report. For trade involving disruptive logistics, Korean trade partners, or higher-ticket transactions, the preferred rail is an Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit substantiated by a sales contract, packing list, commercial invoice, and clean transport document. Documentary Collections may be used only where the counterparty has a proven settlement history and the bank's compliance committee accepts the trade documentation. For recurring international payments to the ad networks and global app stores, the treasury team should execute automated SWIFT payment instructions from the domestic KRW account to the parent or overseas service provider's account, each instruction backed by a vendor invoice, monthly settlement statement, ad-network payout report, and withholding tax computation where Korea domestic withholding rules apply. Turnover: Under Article 2 of the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (as amended by the current enforcement decree), a Yuhan Hoesa is required to appoint a certified public accountant as external auditor if any one of the following thresholds is met: (i) balance-sheet total assets at the preceding fiscal year-end equal or exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9,000,000); (ii) annual turnover/revenue equals or exceeds KRW 20,000,000,000 (approximately USD 15,000,000); or (iii) the average number of full-time employees during the preceding year equals or exceeds 150. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum national corporate income tax rate for taxable income above KRW 300 billion; with the 10% local income tax surtax, the top combined statutory rate is 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on gross dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, composed of 20% national dividend withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax, unless an applicable bilateral income tax treaty reduces the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is required when cross-border related-party transactions with a single foreign related party exceed KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year; Master File preparation is required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; Country-by-Country reporting is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation for ordinary gaming companies, but the statutory substance-over-form doctrine, general anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership tests, and judicial corporate veil-piercing doctrine are applied strictly. A Korean hyper-casual game publisher must demonstrate core income-generating activity in Korea through local staff, offices, books, bank accounts, and management decisions. A shell entity that merely receives AdMob or AppLovin proceeds and remits profits offshore will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, or disregarded for Korean tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Offshore Digital Game Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: The Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, applied with the Framework Act on Taxes, authorizes criminal penalties of imprisonment and fines equal to several times the evaded tax, plus NTS additional tax of 40% on fraudulent underreporting. The underpaid tax is assessed retroactively with interest and daily delinquency surcharges.

Director personal liability: The responsible director or officer is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, additional tax, and collection costs. NTS may attach personal bank accounts, blacklist the director on the serious delinquent taxpayer registry, impose an overseas travel ban, and the director may be criminally sentenced to imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Default on Non-Resident Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax plus an additional tax of up to 50% of the unremitted amount under the Framework Act on Taxes and the Corporate Income Tax Act. The underlying payment is disallowed as a deduction and the missing tax remains collectible from the Korean payer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or direct the unlawful remittance are personally liable for the tax shortfall. NTS asset attachment, travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may result in imprisonment of the responsible director or financial officer.
Treaty Abuse, Shell Company and AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Korea applies its domestic general anti-abuse rule and the OECD-inspired principal purpose test to deny treaty benefits. The income is recharacterized as Korean-source taxable income of the ultimate beneficial owner, subject to 22% withholding or corporate income tax as applicable, plus a 40% fraud surtax and foreign exchange violation penalties.

Director personal liability: Under the judicial corporate veil-piercing doctrine and the Korean Commercial Act, directors who created or maintained the sham structure may be personally liable for the withheld tax and penalties. Their personal assets can be seized, they may be blacklisted and banned from overseas travel, and repeated or aggravated conduct may lead to criminal imprisonment.

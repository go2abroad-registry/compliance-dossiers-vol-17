A stand-alone virtual office or commercial mailbox address is not accepted for the statutory registered office of a Yuhan Hoesa because the Commercial Act requires the headquarters to be located at the registered office, and the National Tax Service requires a real place of business for business registration. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a highly liberalized foreign exchange control regime governed by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercising regulatory oversight. Current account transactions such as trade settlements, seafarer payroll remittances, and profit dividends are generally freely permitted upon submission of supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections and external borrowing, require prior notification, declaration, or post-transaction reporting to the Foreign Exchange Information System, administered by Korea Customs Service. The Korean won is fully convertible for bona fide current account operations, but the government retains policy tools to impose temporary countermeasures during extraordinary volatility. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender ratio, but every receipt must be reported and physically collectible within the statutory settlement period. Profit remittance hurdles are relatively low for a resident Korean limited liability company, providing the entity maintains audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance certificates, and underlying commercial or payroll service contracts. The overarching compliance burden is documentary rather than restrictive, with banks acting as the front-line reporting agents. This regime is therefore assessable as business-friendly for a crew recruitment agency, provided all underlying maritime employment service agreements are free of sanctions and flag-state violations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected within twelve months from the date of the original export declaration, extendable by up to two years upon approved justification. There is no mandatory conversion percentage to Korean won; export foreign currency receipts may be credited in full to a domestic foreign currency account or converted at the exporter's discretion. The receipt must be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System within thirty days to maintain regulatory transparency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are not licensed as deposit-taking banks by the Financial Supervisory Service and are legally barred from providing domestic Korean-won corporate transactional accounts or payroll clearing services to a resident Korean entity. They may function only as ancillary remittance aggregators or payment facilitators bridging account holders to licensed Korean banks. They cannot be considered a viable primary treasury rail for a Yuhan Hoesa operating in South Korea. The optimal approach is to maintain operational treasury accounts at a tier-1 domestic commercial bank and use the EMI only for spot foreign-currency conversion if the EMI is backed by a licensed overseas bank and the underlying data flow satisfies the bank's audit trail requirements.

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit and Accounting, an independent audit by a certified public accountant is mandatory for (i) all listed Korean companies; (ii) all financial investment companies and financial institutions; and (iii) any other Korean incorporated entity, including a Yuhan Hoesa limited liability company, whose total assets at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 8.9 million). The law does not impose a general separate turnover or headcount audit threshold for a private LLC, but a maritime crew agency may be required by the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries to obtain a CPA audit as a licence-renewal condition where annual payroll handled exceeds 1,000 seafarers or where annual foreign-currency remittances exceed KRW 15,000,000,000 (approximately USD 11.2 million). The quantitative triggers are therefore balance-sheet assets of KRW 12 billion or more, enhanced payroll/remittance operating thresholds for maritime licence continuation, and any designation as a public-interest company by the Financial Services Commission.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory when the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions exceeds KRW 50 billion (approximately USD 40 million) in a fiscal year; Master File documentation is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 1 trillion (approximately USD 800 million).

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Korea apply via judicial anti-avoidance doctrines and the Substance over Form rule. For free-zone or tax-holiday entities, the actual performance of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea, such as decision-making, management, crew payroll administration, and client contracting, must be verifiable. In transfer pricing audits, the Korean tax authority also applies the OECD BEPS Action Plan 6/8 principles to recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, Article 3 - the evader is liable for imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine not exceeding 3 times the evaded amount, plus the underlying unpaid tax plus interest surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the tax evasion are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and interest, and may face criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years (up to 10 years if the evasion exceeds KRW 3 billion). A Korean court may also issue a travel ban and the director can be blacklisted for departure with a view to enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Failure / Incomplete Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, Article 116, a penalty of up to 30% of the under-reported taxable income may be imposed (50% in case of intentional disregard), and in addition a documentation penalty of KRW 3 million to KRW 10 million per requirement. Transfer pricing adjustments can be made regardless of the foreign affiliate's profit rate, and secondary adjustments (deemed dividends) may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to maintain required accounting records or give false instructions to omitting documentation are personally liable for the related underestimated corporate tax and surcharges under the Corporate Income Tax Act's joint liability rules. In serious cases, directors may be prosecuted for breaching the Framework Act on National Taxes, leading to criminal fines and imprisonment.
Shell Company Abuse / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the former Article 17(2) of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the substance-over-form doctrine developed by Korean Supreme Court precedent, the tax authority may disregard the shell arrangement, recharacterize the income as attributable to the Korean PE, and impose up to 50% penalty on the additional assessed tax. If the shell structure is used to evade foreign exchange or licensing rules, the Maritime and Labor statutes may also trigger administrative revocation of the crew manning license.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the shell has no autonomous economic activity; directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax, employer payroll withholding obligations, and statutory crew welfare contributions. Civil restitution orders extend to the assets transferred out of the shell. Directors may be blacklisted (travel ban) and face imprisonment under the Criminal Code for fraud (Article 347) if they knowingly used the shell to defraud the tax authority.

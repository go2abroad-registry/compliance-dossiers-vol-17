Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total stated capital and each member's contribution units. Cash contributions must be paid to a bank before incorporation, and the bank's Contribution Payment Certificate must be submitted to the Registry Office. There is no blocked escrow account requirement. In-kind contributions require a factual or independent valuation according to the in-kind contribution valuation rule of the Commercial Act; no escrow is prescribed. The registry's filing fee is expressed as USD 0 in this normalized schema because the actual amount is a registration license tax of 0.4 percent of the stated capital plus a local education surtax and a court fee, determined by the Registry at filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, electronic portal HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); export clearance and customs validation are supported by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For the Yuhan Hoesa (Korean LLC), the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 10-digit Business Registration Number issued by the NTS district tax office with jurisdiction over the registered business address. Procedure: (1) prepare the Articles of Incorporation, business place lease evidence, director or shareholder passport identification, and company seal; (2) file an online Business Registration Application with HomeTax within 20 days of commencing the game publishing business; (3) receive the NTS Business Registration Certificate, which serves as VAT ID, corporate TIN, and e-tax invoice issuer ID. For a foreign non-resident vendor with no Korean registered place of business but making taxable supplies into Korea, the same HomeTax platform is used to apply as a foreign entrepreneur under the Value-Added Tax Act; the foreign entity must submit a power of attorney and appoint a Korean tax representative if no domestic fixed establishment exists, together with a foreign corporate certificate (apostilled or consularized), articles of incorporation, and evidence of Steam and PlayStation Store distribution contracts. NTS will assign a separate foreign business registration number; ongoing compliance requires the foreign supplier to file VAT electronically and maintain a local KRW payment account for tax collection.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Yuhan Hoesa is a resident corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxable on its worldwide income; it is not an eligible pass-through entity).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective tax classification regime in Korea. Classification follows legal form under the Commercial Act. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are made only under the Adjustment of International Tax Act, and no elective hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for corporate taxpayers: any person supplying taxable goods or services in the Republic of Korea is required to register for VAT. Taxpayers other than corporations with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000 may qualify for simplified taxable person filing, but that status does not eliminate the registration duty; the Yuhan Hoesa is outside the simplified category because it is an incorporated entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, 0% VAT applies to (i) goods exported by leaving the Korean customs territory under an accepted customs export declaration and (ii) services supplied to non-residents, non-domiciled persons, or foreign exchange-earning service transactions performed overseas. For Yuhan Hoesa PC and console game publication, digital game licenses and downloadable software transferred to Valve Steam, PlayStation Store, or directly to non-resident end customers qualify as zero-rated export services or export of digital goods only if the customer is a non-resident person, the service is not consumed in Korea, the consideration is received from a foreign payer in foreign currency, and the transaction is evidenced in Steam/PlayStation payout reports. Sales to Korean end users through domestic storefronts or local platform branches remain domestic supplies taxable at 10%.

  • Accepted KCS Export Declaration (UNI-PASS export declaration acceptance document and export performance confirmation), required only for physical game discs, collector editions, or hardware crossing the Korean customs border; not required for pure digital download supplies.
  • Steamworks payout report or PlayStation Store sales and payout statement identifying the non-resident contracting entity, the gross game license consideration, and the underlying digital distribution contract.
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate of Received Export Proceeds issued under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (the Korean statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate or FIRC) showing the matching foreign currency credit received in Korea from Steam, Valve, PlayStation, or the foreign buying platform.
  • Commercial invoice and, where physical goods are exported, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill; for digital exports, the corresponding SWIFT/MT103 credit advice or foreign exchange purchase contract showing conversion to KRW.

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory for all registered businesses. Tax invoices must be issued as HomeTax e-tax invoices and transmitted to NTS through the HomeTax portal, NTS certified integration, or the official HomeTax open API; the e-tax invoice carries a nationally issued invoice number and digital signature code matched by NTS with VAT returns, customs declarations, and bank statements. NTS performs near-real-time cross-matching of e-tax invoice data, cash receipt declarations, export cargo records, and foreign exchange receipts. For B2C sales through Steam or PlayStation Store where cash payment is accepted, the Cash Receipt System requires issuance of a cash receipt and transmission to HomeTax at the point of sale. There is no physical fiscal stamp or full blockchained real-time clearance system, but the HomeTax digital invoice registry operates as the statutory anti-fraud fiscal clearance infrastructure.

The NTS will disallow zero-rating if the foreign recipient, place of supply, contract, performance, or foreign-exchange receipt is not contemporaneously documented. The supply will be recharacterized as taxable at 10%, unpaid VAT will be assessed with statutory interest, and additional tax will be imposed: generally 10% of the underpaid VAT for negligent underpayment, up to 20% for non-filing or unfounded refund claims, and up to 40% with criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for intentional false e-tax invoices, forged export declarations, or fraudulent export refunds. Already refunded input VAT is retrospectively clawed back with interest. The statutory assessment period is five years from the filing due date, extended to ten years for deliberate concealment or false documentation; a criminal tax fraud conviction removes the ordinary limitation period for recovery of the fraudulently obtained refund.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a free-floating exchange rate regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions such as software licensing, digital game publishing, royalty payments, and platform payouts, provided the underlying real economic transaction is documented and a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank files the required trade declaration. Capital account transactions are largely liberalized but remain subject to notification or reporting for foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, securities, and derivatives. Export proceeds do not have to be surrendered to the central bank; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in designated foreign currency accounts or convert them to KRW at their bank. Profit remittances by a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa require board-authorized dividend minutes, audited financial statements, withholding tax payment confirmations, and a bank-filed foreign exchange declaration. The administration of FX controls is therefore transparent but compliance-documentation intensive. Domestic banks require purpose-of-payment codes for all wires, robust beneficial ownership files, confirmation that the transaction has a real underlying commercial contract, and sanctions screening. Profit repatriation is legally unrestricted for current account profits, yet the foreign investor must hold a valid report of foreign direct investment or the remittance may be suspended pending additional review. Practical hurdles are mainly KYC topicality, beneficial ownership transparency, source-of-funds evidence, and exact classification of the payment purpose under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act rather than quantitative convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in Korea must be collected within 90 days of the export date unless a longer payment term is approved by a licensed foreign exchange bank due to commercial requirements. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; 100% of export proceeds may be retained in a foreign currency account and converted to KRW only as the exporter elects. The exporter must file receipt confirmation with the attestation institution and submit evidence of collection when the proceeds are credited to the foreign currency account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, together with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance and the Foreign Exchange Information System administered under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Overseas fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking banks or authorized foreign exchange banks in South Korea and cannot legally provide KRW account servicing, KRW IBANs, or primary cross-border payment clearing for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa. An offshore EMI may act only as an ancillary receivable channel if it partners with a licensed Korean commercial bank, but regulatory exposure remains too high for core treasury operations. The corporate treasury must place all KRW and foreign currency clearing through Tier-1 domestic banks; using an unlicensed EMI for Steam or PlayStation payouts creates automatic AML and FX declaration failures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders must be routed through a licensed domestic Korean foreign exchange bank after registering the foreign direct investment with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance and the receiving bank's foreign exchange system. The remitter must transfer funds via SWIFT MT103 with a precise payment narrative referencing the foreign direct investment report number, the ultimate beneficial owner, the Yuhan Hoesa corporate name, and the purpose of remittance as equity contribution. Upon crediting the capital escrow account, the bank issues a certificate of receipt and the capital verification is completed by matching the CFR code with the shareholder ledger. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion in a fiscal year trigger mandatory preparation and submission of transfer pricing documentation, including Local File and summaries of core transactions; Master File is required when the Korean entity is a constituent unit of a multinational group with aggregate annual consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion, and Country-by-Country reporting applies to the ultimate parent at the same group threshold.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the UAE/Caribbean format of Economic Substances Regulations, but Korean tax law and tax treaty jurisprudence require actual commercial substance of a Korean entity. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa engaged in Steam and PlayStation publishing must have a substantive place of management, local board decisions, full-time employees exercising core income-generating activities, actual execution of contracts with rights holders, and bank accounts in Korea. Absence of these indicators can cause a foreign parent to be treated as having a Korean permanent establishment, or permit the tax authority to disregard the Korean entity as an artificial conduit under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs and OECD anti-abuse principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under the Corporate Income Tax Law

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3: imprisonment for a term not exceeding three years or a criminal fine not exceeding five times the evaded tax; aggravated evasion of an amount exceeding KRW 500 million in a three-year period may lead to higher imprisonment terms. Additionally, the National Tax Service imposes a 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty plus local income tax, daily interest, and publication of non-compliant taxpayers.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced: directors who directed or participated in the concealment are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, surcharges, interest, and civil restitution. Assets and bank accounts can be seized; the director and representative shareholder can be blacklisted and subject to a travel ban. Criminal imprisonment is the direct personal sanction for intentional evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: Under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, failure to submit or prepare transfer pricing documentation can trigger a penalty equal to 3% of the undisclosed international transaction amount, subject to statutory maximums; tax authorities may reallocate taxable income under the arm's-length principle, impose a 20% deficiency surcharge, assess daily late-payment interest, and deny foreign tax credits.

Director personal liability: Korean tax collection rules permit the National Tax Service to collect the deficiency from a director or controlling shareholder where the corporate assets were dissipated before the assessment. If invoices, contracts, or Board minutes are falsified, the responsible director may face criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment, and the court may pierce the veil to impose personal civil liability.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and Treaty Relief Fraud

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Article 98 and Local Income Tax Act Article 110 impose withholding obligations on the Korean payer; the withholding agent becomes personally liable for the unwithheld amount together with a 10% un-collected withholding penalty, interest, and a 20% understatement penalty where the failure was wrongful. Criminal fraud may be charged where the same act involves false or fake taxation documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dividend resolutions or direct payment disbursements are statutory withholding agents and are jointly and severally liable for the shortfall. The tax authority may attach personal assets, impose a travel ban and blacklisting, and seek criminal imprisonment for intentional non-withholding. Civil claims for restitution of the unpaid dividends may also be brought against directors for breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment.

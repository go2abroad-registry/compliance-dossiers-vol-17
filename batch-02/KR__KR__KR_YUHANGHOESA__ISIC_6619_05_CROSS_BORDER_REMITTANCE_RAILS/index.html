A virtual office or commercial-mailbox-only arrangement is not permissible for a Yuhan Hoesa conducting cross-border remittance services. The Financial Services Commission, KoFIU, Korea Customs Service, and National Tax Service require a fixed physical place of business in Korea with a compliance officer or responsible manager, complete books and records, physical AML transaction-monitoring infrastructure, and accessible customer due diligence files. National Tax Service registration presumes fixed business premises; a P.O. For this high-risk special-license industry, virtual offices are categorically rejected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalized yet declaratory foreign exchange regime. Current-account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, dividends, interest, and generally recurring business payments, are freely convertible for resident companies and non-resident investors within the scope of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account transactions are permissible but require prior reporting, registration, or execution through an authorized foreign exchange bank for categories such as foreign direct investment, cross-border financing, and acquisition of securities. There is no general forced surrender for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in licensed domestic foreign currency accounts after collection, but every transaction must be routed through and declared to an authorized foreign exchange bank. Profit remittances are processed without central bank pre-approval once the bank verifies the audited financial statements, the tax withholding certificates, and the board resolution approving the dividend. The practical convertibility experience is efficient for current-account items, while capital-account items demand strict documentary discipline to avoid the reactive controls or administrative penalties that FETA can impose for unreported or mischaracterized transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect foreign currency export and service proceeds within one year from the date of export declaration or from the completion date of the exported service, unless the Ministry of Economy and Finance grants an extension under FETA. Upon collection, the exporter must deposit the full amount in a domestic foreign currency account with an authorized foreign exchange bank or sell the proceeds to that bank; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero because 100 percent may be retained in foreign currency, but the funds must flow through an authorized foreign exchange bank and be reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), functioning as the central bank in cooperation with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the authorized foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) for the administration, surveillance, and enforcement of the Korean foreign exchange control system.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; lower brackets apply: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion; 21% between KRW 20 billion and KRW 300 billion. An additional 10% local income surtax is imposed on national tax payable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income surtax on the national tax, resulting in an effective statutory 22% withholding on outbound dividends paid by a Yuhan Hoesa to non-resident shareholders when no treaty relief applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The return and tax payment are due within three months from the fiscal year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the deadline is 31 March of the following year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Law for the Coordination of International Tax Affairs, a Korean entity must prepare and submit a Master File and Local File when the aggregate volume of international related-party transactions exceeds KRW 50 billion in the preceding business year. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for a Korea-resident constituent entity of a multinational group whose consolidated revenue is at least KRW 100 billion in the preceding fiscal year. Even below the threshold, cross-border remittance fee arrangements must be supported by an arm's length benchmark study.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Korean tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Abuse Rule under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 17. Cross-border remittance income will be recharacterized where the Korean entity lacks Core Income-Generating Activities such as employees, contracts, risk management, settlement operations, and licensing. A Yuhan Hoesa that is only a booking center with no operational substance will be treated as a conduit, the income may be attributed to the overseas principal, and a permanent establishment of the foreign enterprise may be found under the DTA and Korean domestic PE rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: The Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding three times the grossly evaded tax. The Corporate Tax Act applies a 40% penalty surcharge for fraudulent filing and a 20% penalty for negligent filing. The National Tax Service may also revoke licenses under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act in connection with unregistered remittance activity.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the director used the Yuhan Hoesa as an alter ego, personally ordered the concealment, or stripped the company of assets. The director is jointly and severally liable for corporate tax, interest, and penalties; the National Tax Service may impose a travel ban, blacklist the director, seize personal bank accounts and real property, and obtain civil restitution by provisional attachment and execution. The director may serve up to 5 years of imprisonment and pay a fine equal to three times the evaded amount.
Shell Company / Abuse of Treaty Benefits in Remittance Structure

Statutory sanction: Korean tax courts apply substance-over-form and the OECD BEPS principle to deny treaty benefits. The National Tax Service will reclassify the intermediary's income as Korean-source, apply the 22% statutory withholding instead of the claimed 10% treaty rate, impose the 40% fraudulent-return penalty, and refer the matter to the prosecution service under the Act on Reporting and Using Specific Financial Transaction Information. The shell will be blacklisted for foreign exchange settlement and the remittance license may be cancelled by the Financial Services Commission.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the shell invoice or established the conduit as a nominee are jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax and interest. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 23 permits administrative fines and penal detention for directors who allow an unreported cross-border remittance through a shell. Personal accounts are frozen by the Financial Intelligence Unit during the AML blacklist period; the director faces up to 3 years of imprisonment for participating in an unauthorized remittance business and civil asset restitution for fraudulent transfers.
AML Violation / Unlicensed Cross-Border Money Transmission

Statutory sanction: Unauthorized money transmission is a criminal offense under the Act on Reporting and Using Specific Financial Transaction Information and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of up to KRW 50 million. The Financial Services Commission may impose an administrative fine of up to KRW 100 million, and organized habitual violations subject directors to aggravated punishment under the Act on the Punishment of Specific Financial Crimes.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced against directors who controlled the remittance platform: each director faces up to 5 years of actual imprisonment, a KRW 50 million fine, confiscation of illegal commissions, civil asset restitution to harmed customers, and a travel ban. The director may be barred from financial institution management for up to 5 years. The Financial Intelligence Unit can enforce asset freezing against the director's personal bank accounts and real property for up to four days or longer during prosecution.

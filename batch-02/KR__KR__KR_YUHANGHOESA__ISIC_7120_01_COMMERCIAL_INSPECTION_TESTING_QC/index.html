Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The Commercial Act does not impose a minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC). However, the initial capital must be fully paid in at the time of incorporation, and the paid-in amount must be deposited into a bank account in the name of the company (or a designated escrow account) until the registration is completed. The deposit certificate must be submitted to the registry. For foreign investors, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act requires a minimum foreign investment of KRW 100 million (approximately USD 75,000) to qualify for FIPA incentives, but this is not a statutory incorporation requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (소방안전증명) from the National Fire Agency (소방청) for the registered office and any laboratory premises, including compliance with the Fire Safety Standards for Buildings (e.g., fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and evacuation plans). Occupational Safety and Health Administration (KOSHA) approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, including a workplace safety inspection and a written safety management plan if the company employs 5 or more workers. For on-site factory inspections, a Certificate of Liability Insurance for professional indemnity may be required by clients.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 80,000,000 in annual taxable revenue (approximately USD 60,000); voluntary registration is allowed below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act and its enforcement decree, zero-rating applies to exported goods and exports of certain services provided to non-residents, including cross-border quality-control and inspection services where the service is physically performed in Korea but the recipient is situated abroad and the consideration is paid in foreign currency. The exporter/importer must prove that the goods or services were actually supplied outside Korea or for a non-resident, the foreign payment was received, and all customs/export formalities were completed. For services to a foreign customer, a written contract and invoice showing the services are provided to a non-resident are typically required.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration (export bill of entry / export declaration number)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing international carriage
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate / FIRC)

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for all B2B transactions. Every electronic tax invoice must be issued and transmitted to the HomeTax system in real-time via the authorised NTS API or e-invoice service provider. The invoice includes a National Tax Invoice number and an electronic signature; no separate anti-fraud stamp or fiscal clearance by the tax authority is required, but submission is monitored in real time for mismatch detection.

Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger a reassessment of unpaid VAT plus a statutory additional tax: 10% of the wrongfully credited zero-rated tax for mere reporting defects; 20%-40% for negligence/fraud (approach depending on the seriousness). The NTS may also seek criminal sanctions for VAT evasion and impose a collection surcharge where repayment periods exceed 180 days. Claims must be supported by the export declaration, payment evidence and foreign exchange receipts; otherwise the zero-rating is clawed back retrospectively with penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberal but documentary-heavy foreign exchange regime. Current account receipts and payments in ordinary trade and services are freely convertible through licensed foreign exchange banks; no prior MOEF approval is normally required, although each remittance must be supported by the underlying contract or invoice and must be reported through the bank FETA platform. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, intercompany loans, and acquisition of foreign securities, are also convertible but require filing or review with MOEF and BOK through the designated bank, especially when the amount exceeds regulatory thresholds or the foreign investment exceeds KRW 100 million. The system does not impose forced surrender of foreign exchange for resident manufacturing or quality-control businesses. There are no recurring KRW conversion quotas; exporters may retain foreign currency in onshore foreign currency deposit accounts. The main friction is documentary rather than convertibility: profit and dividend remittance requires the Korean LLC to have paid withholding tax and filed national tax clearance, and capital repatriation must be traced to the original FDI report. In practice, BOK does not act as a day-to-day censor for valid inspection, testing, and quality-control service payments when the underlying trade evidence is clear.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA and its delegated regulations, export proceeds for services and pre-shipment inspection or quality-control contracts must be collected within 180 calendar days after the service completion date or the contractual settlement date, unless the responsible foreign exchange bank grants an extension. The mandatory conversion ratio to KRW is 0 percent for ordinary exporters; there is no standing requirement to sell foreign exchange earned from inspection services to the central bank or a licensed bank. The exporter may retain the proceeds in a designated foreign currency deposit account with a Korean foreign exchange bank. The bank requires underlying service contracts, inspection reports, invoices, and a receipt confirmation. If an invoice is settled in KRW, the bank applies the market exchange rate and records the transaction under FETA reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Fintech EMIs such as Wise are not licensed in Korea as foreign exchange banks under FETA and cannot open the type of corporate FDI capital account, report capital transactions to Korean regulators, or perform withholding tax settlement required for a Yuhan Hoesa. They may be used for low-value ancillary payments through bank-sponsored payment services, but the statutory corporate account and all equity injection must remain at a domestic commercial bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Accounting Firms, a Yuhan Hoesa must obtain an independent certified CPA audit when any of the following conditions is met: (i) the entity is a listed company or a securities issuer registered with the Financial Services Commission; (ii) total assets at the end of the preceding fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 12 billion, approximately USD 9 million; (iii) the entity is a financial company, investment company, collective investment scheme, or other regulated entity designated by Presidential Decree regardless of asset size; (iv) the entity voluntarily elects to be audited under its articles of incorporation or by member resolution. There is no standalone revenue or employee-headcount trigger that creates an audit obligation for a non-regulated private Yuhan Hoesa; revenue and headcount are nevertheless used by the Presidential Decree to determine SME classification and to decide whether the simplified audit procedures are available. Forced audit can also be ordered by the court or tax authority if statutory records are unreliable or public interest requires an independent examination.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5 billion per foreign related-party transaction type for mandatory Local File preparation and KRW 10 billion of aggregate related-party transactions or KRW 100 billion of revenue for mandatory Master File preparation; country-by-country reporting attachs when the Korean group generates consolidated revenue equal to or above KRW 1 trillion.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not legislate a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the tax administration applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, Principal Purpose Test and Core Income-Generating Activities analysis through the International Tax Adjustment Act and tax treaties; the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must operate actual inspection and quality control functions through its own employees, premises, equipment, contracts and revenue-bearing activities, and the actual directors must control the core business decisions, otherwise the entity may be re-characterised as a conduit or shell for treaty and anti-avoidance purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent understatement attracts an additional tax of 40% of the evaded tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes and, on referral, criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act of imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to KRW 100 million; confiscation of the evaded corporate assets is authorized.

Director personal liability: The National Tax Service pierces the corporate veil, assesses the directors personally for the evaded corporate tax and local surtax, files civil restitution claims for stripped assets, imposes a travel ban/blacklist on directors residing in Korea, and a court conviction disqualifies the director under the Commercial Act.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority denies treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test and applies the domestic 22% withholding plus accuracy penalties; the Corporate Tax Act and International Tax Adjustment Act allow upward tax adjustment for the full deemed under-withholding and recharacterize the intermediary entity.

Director personal liability: Directors are held jointly and severally liable for the entire tax deficiency under the veil-piercing doctrine; the NTS issues a departure ban and asset seizure order, and directors face criminal exposure for aiding a fraudulent tax claim where they knowingly execute the sham structure.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-disclosure of beneficial ownership in cross-border payments violates the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Financial Transactions Reports Act, carrying fines up to KRW 100 million and an additional tax levy for undisclosed income, with referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering assessment.

Director personal liability: Personal oversight exposes directors to asset freeze, FIU blacklisting, departure restrictions, disqualification from corporate governance roles, and imprisonment of up to five years for concealment of criminal proceeds or aiding money laundering; personal assets are attachable when the corporate treasury has been depleted.

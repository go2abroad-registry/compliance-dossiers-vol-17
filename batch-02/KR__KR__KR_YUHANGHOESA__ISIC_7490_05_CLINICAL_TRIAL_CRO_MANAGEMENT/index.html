A virtual office, commercial mailbox, or registered-agent-only address is not permissible as the sole registered office of a clinical-trial CRO in South Korea. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean Yuhan Hoesa is not subject to a statutory minimum paid-in capital, and the Korean Commercial Act does not require a blocked capital escrow account as a matter of law. In bank practice, however, the subscription proceeds should be deposited into a dedicated pre-incorporation escrow account in the Company's intended name and remain blocked until the court issues the certificate of registration. The Articles of Incorporation must state the exact amount of contribution capital and the number of contribution units, and no capital may be distributed to founders before registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with the electronic portal Home Tax Service (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr; export customs clearance is administered through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor must first incorporate a Korean Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act; the company is then a Korean domestic entity. Tax registration is completed through the National Tax Service. The company or its authorized tax agent must file the electronic business registration application through Home Tax Service within 20 days from the date of commencement of business. The submission pack must include: certified/apostilled formation documents of the foreign investor, articles of incorporation and court registry certificate of the Korean Yuhan Hoesa, evidence of the registered business address, power of attorney designating a Korean tax agent, foreign investment declaration certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act when applicable, and any clinical trial service authorization issued by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety if already obtained. The competent district tax office verifies the information and assigns a 10-digit business registration number, which is simultaneously the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the VAT registration number, and issues the business registration certificate. A foreign entity operating directly through a Korean branch rather than a Korean subsidiary must register the branch as a domestic place of business under the same process and must obtain the same business registration number before making taxable supplies in South Korea.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold: any business maintaining a domestic place of business in South Korea must register for VAT before taxable supplies begin; a Korean corporation is always a general VAT taxpayer, and the simplified taxpayer threshold of KRW 80,000,000 applies only to individual business owners, not to a Yuhan Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the zero-rating provisions of the Value-Added Tax Act and its Enforcement Decree for physically exported goods, services that qualify as foreign currency earning services supplied to a non-resident business with no Korean domestic place of business, and services physically performed entirely outside South Korea. For a clinical trial CRO, the qualifying supply must be documented contract research services to a non-resident pharmaceutical sponsor, invoiced in foreign currency, with proceeds received through a Korean foreign exchange bank and repatriated into South Korea within the statutory period; the zero-rated exporter must issue a 0% e-tax invoice and maintain the service agreement, protocol, deliverable reports, and foreign currency remittance certificate. If the sponsor has a Korean domestic place of business or the service is invoiced to a Korean resident, the supply is subject to 10% VAT. The zero-rated exporter may apply through Home Tax Service for early refund of attributable input VAT.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic export declaration for any clinical trial materials, biological samples, or equipment physically exported from South Korea; pure service-only CRO zero-rating does not require a customs declaration, but any accompanying goods must be covered by this export declaration.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of trial goods or samples to the foreign destination.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with HS codes and clinical trial reference numbers for all exported materials.
  • Service agreement and clinical trial protocol with the non-resident sponsor identifying the non-resident recipient, scope, and foreign currency payment terms.
  • Inward foreign currency remittance certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank proving receipt and repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days.
  • Korean electronic tax invoice issued through Hometax showing the zero rate for the qualifying supply.

The Korean entity must issue electronic tax invoices through NTS Home Tax Service using the official Hometax API or EDI channel. Each e-tax invoice is transmitted to NTS and assigned a unique NTS management number, which acts as the anti-fraud digital stamp and enables the NTS to cross-match buyer and seller invoices in its fiscal clearance system. A corporation cannot use paper tax invoices except for statutory exceptions that do not apply to a corporation. VAT returns are filed semi-annually with a preliminary return for each half-year, due 25 April for January-March and 25 October for July-September, and a final return, due 25 July for January-June and 25 January for July-December; all e-invoice and cash-receipt data must be reconciled to the returns and retained for at least five years.

The NTS may scrutinize export zero-rating claims through document review, bank confirmation, and exchange of information with the sponsor's tax treaty jurisdiction. Failure to maintain the required contract, e-tax invoice, customs evidence, or foreign currency receipt invalidates the zero rating; the NTS will retroactively assess 10% VAT on the supply, add additional tax up to 40% of the underreported VAT for false or negligent reporting, charge statutory delay interest, claw back any refund already paid, and may refer the case to prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. The statutory assessment period is normally five years but extends to ten years where the false zero-rating is intentional concealment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains an open current-account system, accepting IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations, but retains a reporting-based framework for capital movements. Current-account payments, including clinical-trial service fees, subcontractor invoices, dividend distributions, and interest payments, can be converted at market rates upon presentation of a customs declaration, VAT invoice, contract, or audited resolution. Capital inflows for equity injection into the LLC must first be registered under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; once a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate is issued, inbound USD is converted to KRW at a domestic foreign-exchange bank using the direct-investment FX purpose code, and the proceeds can be used for business operations. Profit remittances require evidence of payment, tax withholding confirmation, audited financial statements, and no prohibition on the receiving jurisdiction. Outbound payments to foreign CRO sites or laboratories are permissible as invisible trade, subject to contract, shipping or milestone evidence. The Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance may impose temporary capital-flow management measures in crisis scenarios, but the current regime imposes no surrender obligation and no quantitative caps on conversion for legitimate transactions; practical friction is driven by bank-level AML evidence collection rather than by central-bank exchange allocation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, export proceeds must be collected no later than 90 days after the export declaration/loading date unless an extension is approved in advance. The statutory surrender ratio is 0% under the post-1998 liberalization regime; Korean exporters are not required to sell export proceeds to the Bank of Korea. However, the foreign currency received must either be sold to a licensed foreign-exchange bank to obtain KRW at the market rate or be credited to a domestic foreign-currency account in the name of the exporting LLC. The settlement is recorded by the bank in the electronic trade-settlement system for Korea Customs Service verification. A failure to collect within the statutory timeline without prior approval must be reported and may result in administrative fines or loss of trade-settlement facilitation benefits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the sovereign monetary and foreign exchange authority, with supervisory and administrative powers delegated under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and licensed foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and effectively not viable as a primary or complementary settlement rail for the Korean LLC. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or other non-bank electronic-money institutions are not licensed under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to execute foreign-exchange business for a Korean resident entity. The Act confines foreign-exchange dealing, cross-border transfer and settlement to licensed foreign exchange banks with a Korean banking resolution. An offshore EMI cannot obtain Korea Financial Supervisory Service approval nor use the Korean clearing and reporting channels; attempting to use an EMI to collect sponsor milestone payments or to fund the capital account would fail statutory reporting and expose the LLC to fines. Domestic internet-only banks such as Toss Bank or Kakao Bank could technically onboard the LLC, but they are not universally accepted for FDI Capital Escrow Accounts and are not included in this Tier-1 blueprint.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national CIT headline for tax base exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local surtax resulting in 26.4% aggregate top marginal rate; lower statutory progressive brackets are 9% (up to KRW 200 million), 19% (from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion), and 21% (from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22.0% aggregate (20% national withholding rate for non-resident corporate shareholders under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act plus 2% local income tax surtax, which is 10% of the 20% national tax). For Korean resident individual shareholders the statutory dividend withholding environment is different, but the outbound non-resident dividend repatriation rate applicable to Yuhan Hoesa is 22% in the absence of treaty relief.

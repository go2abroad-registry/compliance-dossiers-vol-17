Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. South Korean law sets no minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa, and the capital amount is determined solely by the Articles of Incorporation. However, the full declared paid-in capital must be deposited into a domestic Korean bank escrow account prior to court registration, the bank must issue a certificate of capital deposit in the name of the company, and the funds must remain blocked and unavailable for withdrawal until the court-issued incorporation registration certificate has been obtained.

If a rendering server room is installed, the company should obtain an electrical load inspection and seismic secondary-fixation certification from a licensed electrical engineer.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); customs export declarations are filed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal.

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed Korean Yuhan Hoesa (domestic limited liability company), apply for a Business Registration Number (BRN) with the district tax office having jurisdiction over the registered head office, or through the HomeTax portal using the corporate Digital Certificate, within 20 days after business commencement. The BRN is simultaneously the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the Value-Added Tax (VAT) registration number. Required documents include: certificate of incorporation and certified Korean translation; articles of incorporation; board resolution appointing the representative (and, where required, the tax representative); power of attorney; evidence of domestic place of business (lease agreement or certificate of office occupancy); foreign business registration certificate or equivalent; and identity documentation of the appointed representative/shareholders. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic place of business, the entity must appoint a tax representative under the Act on the Coordination of Tax and International Transactions and file the same BRN application before making taxable supplies in Korea; the tax representative submits the application via HomeTax on behalf of the foreign entity. Where the foreign entity only supplies services to Korean business customers in respect of which Korea imposes reverse-charge VAT, registration is ordinarily not required; however, a foreign supplier of digital/electronic services to Korean non-business consumers is required to register under the simplified VAT registration for foreign electronic service suppliers and discharge VAT through HomeTax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax law does not provide a check-the-box or entity classification election; a Yuhan Hoesa is automatically treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act. No hybrid mismatch or pass-through election is available. Corporate tax consolidation under the Corporate Income Tax Act may be elected only by a qualifying parent company controlling 100% of domestic subsidiaries, but it does not alter the entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold. VAT registration is mandatory upon commencement of any business making taxable supplies in Korea, regardless of revenue. Simplified taxable person status is limited to individual sole proprietors whose prior-year taxable supplies do not exceed KRW 80,000,000; a corporate entity, including a Yuhan Hoesa, is always an ordinary VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the zero rate applies to exports of goods, supplies of services to foreign countries, and other supplies of goods or services prescribed by Presidential Decree as foreign currency earning activities. For a Korean 3D animation/VFX production company, services to non-resident clients will qualify as zero-rated service exports when the contract is with a non-resident, the consideration is paid in foreign currency, and the services are materially performed for the foreign client's use or exploitation outside Korea, including delivery of animation/CGI files or digital broadcast/streaming content to an overseas buyer. The claim must be substantiated by retaining contractual, payment, and work-product delivery evidence; no domestic VAT is chargeable on qualifying zero-rated exports.

  • UNI-PASS export declaration certificate (Export Declaration Acceptance/Number) issued by Korea Customs Service for physical goods exported from Korea
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Korean Yuhan Hoesa to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and transport document (bill of lading, air waybill, or international courier delivery record) showing the goods left Korea
  • Bank-issued Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance/Receipt Certificate or foreign exchange purchase receipt from a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing settlement of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Original service/export contract with the non-resident client, delivery acceptance record, and digital file transfer logs for services and digital animation deliverables; a customs export declaration is not required for pure service exports, but the export service documentation bundle must evidence the foreign recipient and foreign use
  • Zero-rated VAT tax invoice (export tax invoice) issued through HomeTax with the statutory zero-rating exemption code

B2B supplies must be evidenced by a tax invoice issued in accordance with the VAT Act. Electronic tax invoices (ETI) are mandatory for all VAT-registered business operators, and each ETI must be transmitted to NTS through HomeTax. ETIs are submitted in structured XML form; high-volume taxpayers may integrate accounting or ERP systems with the HomeTax EDI/API interface. NTS validates and cross-matches buyer and seller ETI data; there is no fiscal digital stamping or real-time pre-clearance denial mechanism. Late submission, non-submission, or submission of false ETI data triggers VAT Act penalty taxes and administrative surcharges.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match zero-rating claims with export declarations, transport documents, foreign exchange settlement receipts, and the relationship between the Korean supplier and the foreign client. If export zero-rating is claimed without lawful substantiation, or if export proceeds are not received in foreign currency within the statutory settlement period, NTS can reverse the zero rate, assess VAT at 10% on the supply value, add statutory penalty taxes (including understatement penalties for unpaid or underpaid VAT and penalties for unissued or false tax invoices), and charge statutory interest. For fraudulent claims, the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may impose criminal fines of up to three times the evaded tax or imprisonment, and the assessment limitation period extends to 10 years where fraud or dishonest concealment is shown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a regulated but broadly convertible foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercising supervisory authority. Current account transactions, including trade in services, royalties, animation production fees, and ordinary profit remittances, are generally classified as freely convertible and may proceed through authorized foreign exchange banks subject to documentary verification and submission of prescribed foreign exchange reports. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections and external borrowings, are not fully liberalized and require notification, registration, or prior approval depending on the transaction type. Export proceeds are not forcibly surrendered to the central bank, but exporters must ensure that foreign currency receipts are collected within statutory periods, properly received through domestic banks, and recorded for customs and tax reconciliation. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is permissible after satisfaction of corporate income tax, tax clearance certification, and submission of board resolutions, financial statements, and auditor confirmations where applicable. Practical hurdles arise from stringent anti-money laundering documentation requirements, the need for foreign exchange transaction reports, and the necessity to evidence the commercial substance of cross-border service transactions involving intangible digital assets such as 3D animation and VFX deliverables. Korean authorities scrutinize mis-invoicing, round-tripping, and shell-company structures, requiring treasury officers to maintain complete underlying contracts and settlement evidence. Overall, Korean won is convertible for operating business transactions, but treasury planning must incorporate reporting lead times and bank-level compliance gates for capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must collect export proceeds within 6 months from the date of export or the date of loading, unless an extension is approved by the governing foreign exchange bank. Korea does not presently impose a mandatory conversion ratio requiring surrender of export receipts into Korean won; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts, but the receipt must be reported through an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank and trade settlement documentation must evidence the underlying export transaction. Delayed collection, shortfall, or substitution with alternative payment methods requires submission of a written explanation and supporting shipping documents to the bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low Direct Legal Viability: South Korean law does not classify foreign digital EMI platforms such as Wise or Revolut as authorized banks or licensed domestic money service businesses eligible to provide payment accounts and FX settlement services to a resident Yuhan Hoesa entity. Foreign EMIs are not supervised by the Financial Supervisory Service for domestic account holding and cannot independently supply Korean won clearing, FX purpose code reporting, or export proceeds collection confirmation. They may be used only as supplementary payment origination rails for low-value non-regulated disbursements, but the primary treasury relationship must reside with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank to satisfy the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and AML KYC obligations. Any arrangement presenting a foreign EMI as the main clearing channel would be structurally non-compliant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity investments from non-resident shareholders must be routed through a SWIFT MT103 wire to a domestic Tier-1 bank account referencing the foreign direct investment notification or registration issued under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remittance should carry a clear payment narrative such as 'Paid-in capital for Yuhan Hoesa investment' and the appropriate bank code for capital receipts. After the funds arrive, the receiving bank will issue a foreign exchange purchase confirmation and the company must obtain a capital verification or investment certificate from the bank or a certified public accountant before remitting any dividends. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national top marginal corporate income tax rate; plus 10% local income tax surtax for a combined 26.4% top marginal rate. Lower brackets: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion, 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Non-resident dividends are subject to 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax, producing a combined statutory rate of 22% on gross dividend distributions; a lower rate may apply under an applicable double tax treaty if the recipient is the beneficial owner and a certified tax resident of the treaty jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due within three months after the end of the fiscal year; a calendar-year Yuhan Hoesa must file by 31 March of the following year. Interim prepayment returns are due within two months after the first six months of the fiscal year; any tax due is payable by the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate overseas related-party transactions reach KRW 5 billion or more in a business year; Master File is mandatory when the Korean taxpayer has related-party transactions of KRW 50 billion or more and global revenue of KRW 100 billion or more; CbC Report is required for ultimate parent groups with consolidated group revenue of KRW 2 trillion or more.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain an enumerated Economic Substance Regulations regime similar to the UAE, but the NTS applies the substance-over-form doctrine, the Korean General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the OECD BEPS/MLI minimum standards and the beneficial ownership test to recharacterize shell structures. For a commercial 3D animation company, the core income-generating activities consist of script and character development, digital asset creation, VFX rendering infrastructure, licensing negotiation, IP management and post-production supervision; these activities must be conducted in Korea by substantive employees and assets to avoid attribution of income to an offshore principal and to preserve treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Offenses Act: imprisonment with labor for up to five years or a fine not exceeding five times the amount of tax evaded; civil additional tax of 40% of the under-reported tax, plus daily late payment surcharges; immediate criminal referral to the prosecution service and forfeiture of the proceeds of the concealment.

Director personal liability: The Korean corporate veil may be pierced where the Yuhan Hoesa is used as a mere instrument to evade tax; directors who participated in the fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties from personal assets; directors face asset attachment, travel bans and immigration blacklisting for tax arrears exceeding KRW 50 million, and criminal imprisonment upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: International Tax Coordination Act articles 4 through 6 give the NTS authority to re-determine the arm's length price and assess corporate tax on the adjusted income; failure to preserve or submit contemporaneous TP documentation can trigger an additional penalty of 30% of the tax understatement, along with late payment surcharges of approximately 0.022%-0.027% per day.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false transfer pricing documentation is treated as a co-participant in tax evasion and may be jointly and severally liable for the entire tax shortfall, penalties and interest; personal bank accounts, real property and dividends may be seized, the director may be prohibited from leaving Korea, and criminal imprisonment may be imposed where the TP mispricing was designed solely to strip Korean profits offshore.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Default and Treaty or AML Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Korean payor as withholding agent is liable for the uncollected WHT plus an additional penalty of 10%-20% for failure to withhold and remit; abusive DTA claims are recharacterized under general anti-avoidance rules and BEPS/MLI provisions; the false use of treaty relief constitutes fraud and triggers criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act together with Anti-Money Laundering referrals for structured cross-border wires.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate officers of the Korean withholding agent bear personal civil and criminal liability if they knowingly authorize non-resident payments without WHT; the corporate veil is pierced for improper use of the entity, making the director jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT, local surtax and penalties; directors may face personal asset execution, travel bans, blacklisting by Korean tax and immigration authorities, and imprisonment for the fraudulent non-compliance.

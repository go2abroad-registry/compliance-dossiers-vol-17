Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under Article 287 of the Commercial Act, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company). The capital amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid-in to the domestic bank escrow (designated deposit) account before the incorporation registration. Korean regulations require the paid-in amount to be deposited in a Korean commercial bank (e.g., Hana Bank, Kookmin Bank, Shinhan Bank) in the name of the company or its promoter, with a balance certificate (잔액증명서) issued for the court filing. Non-resident promoters may open the escrow account through a duly appointed proxy holder or with an Alien Registration Card, and the funds must remain blocked until the court completes the incorporation registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Export customs clearance is administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UniPass (www.unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the tax registration procedure is: (1) incorporate with the Korean corporate registry; (2) commence the business and within 20 days file the Business Registration application with the competent district tax office via HomeTax, using the statutory electronic form, digital certificate, and the Korean tax agent's credentials; (3) attach the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, foreign-equity ownership registry, business license, and lease document; (4) after verification, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both corporate TIN and VAT registration number; (5) register the entity in the e-tax invoice system and the KCS exporter identification in UniPass; and (6) open a Korean won corporate bank account and a foreign-currency account for export and offshore service proceeds. For a foreign non-resident entity that itself engages in Korean taxable supply without a Korean branch, the NTS requires registration of that foreign entity and application for a foreign business registration number via a duly authorized tax agent; the agent identifies a resident place of business or, if none exists, designates a tax representative pursuant to the Framework Act on National Taxes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold in KRW for corporate VAT registration. Any person carrying on a taxable business in South Korea must register with the NTS within 20 days of business commencement. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the 0% VAT rate applies to (i) goods exported from South Korea, where export declaration and actual departure are evidenced, and (ii) qualifying services supplied to non-residents, paid in foreign currency, and actually performed, used, or consumed outside Korea, including database, API monitoring, and other developer infrastructure cloud services. The supply must be documented by a zero-rate tax invoice issued under the VAT Act. Payment must be received into a Korean bank account, observable in the foreign exchange records, within the statutory export-proceeds period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) / Export Clearance Certificate issued by the Korea Customs Service through UniPass.
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) naming the foreign buyer or consignee and evidencing departure of goods from a Korean port or airport.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Bank Receipt proving repatriation and settlement of export proceeds in Korea.
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act documentation supporting the link between the foreign customer's payment and the zero-rated supply.

South Korea operates a real-time electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) reporting regime through the NTS HomeTax system. Corporate taxpayers are required to issue e-tax invoices and transmit the e-tax invoice data to the NTS via HomeTax open API, EDI/SFTP, or accredited ASP providers. Transmission is due the day after issuance. The NTS does not grant a pre-issuance authorization or physical fiscal stamp; anti-fraud integrity is maintained through mandatory digital-certificate authentication of the issuer, NTS-assigned e-tax invoice unique numbers, and automatic cross-matching of transmitted invoices to the VAT return and periodic tax invoice statements. Any invoice not reported to the NTS before the VAT filing deadline exposes the taxpayer to statutory surcharges, non-declaration penalties, or reduction or denial of input VAT deduction.

NTS audits cross-check the Customs Export Declaration, foreign exchange remittance data, and e-tax invoice records. Absent a complete documentation bundle, the export is recharacterized as a domestic supply, causing 10% VAT to be assessed at the export invoice value plus surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes and statutory interest. Intentional fraudulent export claims can attract criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and extend the tax reassessment period from 5 years to up to 10 years. Refunded input VAT is clawed back with interest if the export zero-rating is disallowed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised but regulated foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is convertible on the current account for trade and service payments, subject to submission of underlying documentation and prior bank screening. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment and inbound or outbound loans, require a foreign exchange declaration or a certificate of notification to a designated FX bank; conversion of KRW to foreign currency for capital purposes requires evidence of the authorised transaction. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds, but exporters are subject to statutory settlement-period limits. Profit remittances by foreign-invested companies are permitted under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, provided the company files tax returns in Korea and demonstrates that the earnings have been legitimately generated and taxed; withholding tax at the applicable treaty rate is deducted at source. Dividend remittances require evidence of foreign investment registration, a board resolution declaring dividends, and audited financial statements. In practice, banks apply strict transaction-purpose reviews, and any attempt to convert KRW for speculative use or without an underlying trade or investment event is rejected. The effectiveness of the system is high, with the Bank of Korea acting as the central repository of FX data and the Ministry of Economy and Finance issuing policy directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters and service providers must settle export proceeds through a licensed foreign exchange bank within the statutory due date, generally within 6 months from the date of export or service completion. If proceeds cannot be collected in that period, an extension for up to 18 months may be granted with the approval of the foreign exchange bank upon submission of justification. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the entire export receipt may be retained in a foreign currency account. However, the exporter must report the settlement through the bank's foreign exchange database and provide export customs clearance documentation or equivalent service export evidence to the settlement bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and licensed foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability. South Korea operates a permission-based foreign exchange system; non-licensed offshore EMIs cannot legally provide multi-currency accounts or payment services to Korean entities without being registered as a foreign exchange business handler or electronic financial transaction operator under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Domestic fintechs such as Toss (Viva Republica) operate under domestic licenses, but offshore platforms like Mercury, Stripe in its EMI capacity, or similar digital lenders are only permissible for auxiliary invoicing or collection if they partner with a licensed domestic bank; they cannot serve as the primary clearing rail for FX transactions, capital escrow, or regulatory filings. Use of such EMIs as the principal treasury account would create immediate regulatory non-compliance and AML rejection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity subscriptions must be routed through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. The investor or the Korean entity submits a foreign exchange declaration to the designated FX bank, obtains the bank's confirmation of receipt, and causes the Korean entity to open a capital escrow account in foreign currency or KRW. The bank issues a foreign investment notification certificate after verifying the incoming wire; this certificate underpins the entity's ability to later repatriate profits. A corporate resolution authorising receipt of funds and share allocation is required. For every capital injection, the sending bank must transmit a SWIFT MT103 with the investor's full name and the appropriate Bank of Korea balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment equity. After receipt, the Korean entity must complete the foreign investment registration within the statutory time limit and ensure that the received amount exactly matches the subscribed capital schedule.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

National CIT rate bands: 10%/20%/22%/25% plus 10% resident surtax on national CIT, producing an aggregate top marginal headline rate of 27.5%; lower aggregate effective rates are 11.0%, 22.0%, and 24.2% at the respective brackets.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory aggregate outbound dividend withholding tax rate is 22%: 20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax. This rate applies unless an applicable DTA reduces or eliminates the dividend withholding tax for a beneficial owner with sufficient shareholding and substance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The corporate income tax return must be filed within 60 days after the end of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year Korean entity the final return is due by May 31 of the following year, with non-resident branch quarterly prepayments and final adjustment integrated into the same HomeTax filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion of aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation in Korea; a multinational group with KRW 100 billion or more consolidated group revenue is subject to Country-by-Country Reporting notification and filing. NTS may also request local documentation below these thresholds during a transfer pricing audit.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not administer a formal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing, but the substance-over-form principle, the general anti-abuse rule in the Framework Act for National Taxes, and the abuse-of-rights doctrine under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act apply fully. The Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate real employees, premises, decision-making, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in the jurisdictions where cloud pricing, database infrastructure contracts, intellectual property ownership, and financial risk are managed; a shell with no operational reality will lose DTA protection and Korean tax deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion on Korean-Source Cloud Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3, fraudulent evasion of corporate income tax is punishable by imprisonment for up to five years (seven years in aggravated high-value cases) and a fine of up to three times the evaded tax. NTS additionally imposes a 20% fraudulent understatement surcharge, daily late-payment interest, and may seize Korean bank accounts and receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the false return can be personally joint and severally liable under the secondary taxpayer provisions of the Framework Act for National Taxes if corporate assets are dissipated before collection; they face personal bank account and real estate seizure, NTS overseas travel restriction (blacklisting), and criminal imprisonment up to five years for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Excessive Royalty Loading

Statutory sanction: Under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act Articles 4 and 13, NTS re-determines the arm's-length profit, denies interest deductions under thin capitalization, imposes transfer pricing document penalties of up to 10% of the transaction amount, and adds an understatement surcharge of 20%-40% on the resulting tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: A director found to have signed the manipulated intercompany contracts, or to have actively shifted revenue to a controlled offshore corporation, can be assessed as a secondary taxpayer, lose limited liability protection where the corporation is treated as an alter ego, suffer travel blacklisting and asset freezing, and face imprisonment up to five years for evasion.
Treaty Shopping and Beneficial Ownership Abuse in Dividend and Royalty Conduit Structures

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act for National Taxes general anti-abuse rule and the Adjustment of International Taxes Act treaty-shopping provisions, NTS denies the DTA benefits, recharacterizes the conduit as the taxable recipient, imposes the full 22% WHT, adds a 30%-40% reckless avoidance surcharge, and disallows the related intercompany royalty deductions in Korea.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly using a conduit to avoid Korean WHT are jointly and severally liable for the unwithheld tax as withholding co-obligors, may be blacklisted under the NTS travel restriction program, and can be prosecuted for aiding and abetting tax evasion, with penalties including imprisonment up to five years and fines of up to KRW 20 million.

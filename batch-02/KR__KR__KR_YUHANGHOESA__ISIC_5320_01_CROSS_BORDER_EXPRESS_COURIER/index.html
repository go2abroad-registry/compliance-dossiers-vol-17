Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. A Yuhan Hoesa has no statutory minimum paid-in capital under Korean law. The authorized contribution units and capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must, however, be fully paid by each subscribing member into a dedicated capital deposit escrow account maintained with a licensed commercial bank. The bank-issued deposit balance certificate is a mandatory attachment to the incorporation filing, and no withdrawal is permitted until the completion of court registration and issuance of the corporate registration certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, through the HomeTax electronic tax portal (HomeTax System, hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a domestic tax representative or establish a domestic place of business, executing a power of attorney in favor of the representative if no permanent Korean business address exists. Step 2: Submit the Business Registration Application to the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean business location, or file electronically through HomeTax, attaching certified copies of the foreign company incorporation documents, articles of association, evidence of the Korean representative/office appointment, and ownership or lease documents for the business premises. Step 3: The NTS examines the application and issues a single statutory 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the universal Korean Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), Value Added Tax (VAT) registration number, and corporate identity number for all national tax filings and customs procedures. Step 4: The foreign non-resident entity is then registered as a taxable business operator and obtains electronic tax invoice capability in HomeTax without any minimum capital requirement and regardless of non-resident ownership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None; no economic turnover threshold applies under the Korean VAT Act. Any person or foreign entity commencing a taxable business in Korea must register for VAT before the taxable supply activity begins. Simplified taxpayer rules for small individual businesses are not applicable to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC operating cross-border express courier services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT when legal title to the goods passes to a non-resident purchaser and the goods are physically transported from Korea under a valid Korea Customs Service export declaration. International transport services, including the cross-border express courier movement of cargo from Korea to another country, are zero-rated under the Korean VAT Act where the service is performed wholly or partly outside Korea and consideration is received from a non-resident customer. The exporter must retain the export declaration, transport document, foreign exchange receipt, and trade settlement proof demonstrating that the goods left Korea and the sale proceeds were repatriated to Korea through the foreign exchange banking system.

  • Korea Customs Service electronic export declaration via UNI-PASS (export Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods from Korea to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of delivery for the international courier consignment, including internationally recognised shipment delivery signature or electronic delivery confirmation records

All VAT-registered businesses, including foreign-owned Korean LLCs, must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system for taxable supplies exceeding KRW 300,000. Electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS on the day following issuance, using the prescribed XML format either directly in HomeTax or via a certified API integration. Each electronic tax invoice receives an NTS-issued electronic tax invoice number and is centrally matched against VAT returns for anti-fraud control. No separate fiscal stamping device is mandated; the HomeTax electronic tax invoice number constitutes the statutory fiscal clearance marker. Export zero-rating is substantiated by linking the electronic export invoice to the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export declaration and the associated foreign exchange settlement record.

If the NTS determines that export zero-rating was claimed without the mandatory customs export declaration, transport document, or foreign exchange repatriation proof, the 0% rate is revoked and the VAT is retroactively assessed at 10% on the export consideration for the original return period. Additional tax penalties are imposed under the Korean VAT Act, including up to 40% of the tax evaded for fraudulent or deliberately unverifiable zero-rating claims. False export declarations or facilitation of unrecorded export movement additionally expose the entity to criminal penalties under the Customs Duties Act and the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, including fines and imprisonment of responsible officers, as well as NTS collection enforcement and business registration revocation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is classified as a freely convertible currency for current account transactions, but the regime imposes a structured reporting and declaration framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. Trade-related current account receipts and payments are largely liberalised, requiring only post-event reporting through designated foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions, by contrast, require prior declaration or notification, especially for foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and securities investment. The currency is subject to managed floating, with the BOK intervening only to smooth excessive volatility. Profit remittances, dividends, and loan repayments to non-residents are permitted but must be supported by audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and evidence of source funds; the remitting bank acts as the gatekeeper. For a non-resident owned express courier entity, the practical hurdles are not legal conversion limits but documentary substantiation: each remittance must map to verified trade or service contracts, and the bank will scrutinise the underlying commercial rationale. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds; these may be held in foreign currency accounts domestically, but all inflows must be reported to the BOK through the designated bank within the statutory window. Fractional conversion is possible at any time, with the spot rate determined by the Seoul interbank market. The regime is therefore not a hard-convertibility denial system, but a compliance-intensive system with robust AML/CTF enforcement, demanding a high degree of transparency for both inbound and outbound liquidity flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 15-3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of exportation; extensions may be granted by the designated bank for up to a year. There is no mandatory conversion of foreign currency proceeds into KRW; exporters may retain balances in Foreign Exchange Demand Deposit Accounts. However, every receipt must be reported to the BOK through the bank within 20 business days of receipt. The reporting must include the export declaration number, invoice, B/L or airway bill, and proof of receipt. Failure to meet the six-month collection deadline triggers automatic reporting to the BOK and may result in administrative penalties unless an approved extension is obtained.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. South Korean regulatory architecture does not recognise offshore digital EMI licences for domestic corporate banking. The Banking Act reserves deposit-taking and foreign exchange services for licensed banks, while the Electronic Financial Transactions Act authorises non-bank e-money issuers only for prepaid payment instruments and domestic settlement, not for multi-currency nostro accounts, trade finance, or cross-border liquidity management. A foreign fintech EMI cannot independently maintain a KRW account or access the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) rails. Any viable fintech solution must partner with a tier-1 bank as a sub-accounting layer, which does not reduce the bank KYC burden and may even introduce additional third-party risk. Accordingly, the treasury blueprint should rely on direct commercial bank relationships for all operational and capital flows.

Turnover: Under the Korean Act on External Audit of Stock Companies (recently amended and effective for fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2019), a compulsory independent certified CPA audit is mandated: (i) for every stock company with total assets exceeding KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 8.9 million) as of the close of the preceding fiscal year; (ii) for any stock company, including a Yuhan Hoesa, if the balance sheet total exceeds KRW 12 billion, if annual turnover exceeds KRW 10 billion (approximately USD 7.4 million), or if it has 100 or more employees; (iii) for companies listed on a Korean stock exchange, regardless of size; (iv) for companies with total assets of KRW 12 billion or more but below KRW 10 trillion, external audit is required under the same Act; (v) for private companies whose total assets are below KRW 12 billion but that fall under the asset, debt, turnover, or headcount thresholds as prescribed by Financial Services Commission regulations, where the general test commonly includes a balance sheet total of over KRW 7 billion and annual revenue of over KRW 8 billion or more than 50 employees; (vi) for any company that requests credit financing from Korean financial institutions exceeding KRW 6 billion, for which a certified audit is conditionally required; and (vii) for any company required by its constitutional documents, banking covenants, or government courier license terms to present audited financial statements.

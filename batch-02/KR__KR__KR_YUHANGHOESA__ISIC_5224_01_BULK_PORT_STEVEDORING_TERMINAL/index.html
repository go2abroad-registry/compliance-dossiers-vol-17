Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The capital amount specified in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid into a Korean bank settlement or escrow account and evidenced by a bank certificate before the court registration application is filed. There is no official blocked-escrow account rule for ordinary stevedoring companies; however, a foreign investor must report inbound equity investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and maintain evidence of receipt. The ad valorem registration tax is calculated at 0.4 percent of subscribed capital plus a 20 percent education surcharge, so with zero subscribed capital the statutory fee component is zero; fixed court, notary, and seal certification charges still apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalized foreign exchange system underpinned by the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and regulations promulgated by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, with the Bank of Korea serving as the central bank and enforcing reporting requirements through designated foreign exchange banks. The won is fully convertible on current account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, dividends, and loan interest, subject to submission of supporting documents and foreign exchange purpose codes. Capital account transactions are generally convertible but require prior declaration or reporting to the Bank of Korea or a designated foreign exchange bank; inbound foreign direct investment is reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion or surrender to the central bank; however, exporters must receive payment within a statutory period and report settlement evidence. Profit remittance by a foreign-invested company is permissible once audited financial statements and tax clearance are provided, but requires bank confirmation of the foreign investment report and proof of dividend source. Temporary restrictions may be invoked in balance-of-payments emergencies, but current practice imposes no material convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, an exporter of goods must collect the full export proceeds within 18 months from the date of export clearance, unless a temporary permit for extension is granted by the Minister of Economy and Finance through a designated foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio: the statutory surrender requirement was abolished in 1999. Exporters may maintain foreign currency receipts in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts, convert to KRW at a bank rate, or use them for approved overseas purposes; however, the receipt itself must be reported and settled through a designated foreign exchange bank so that the Bank of Korea can monitor balance-of-payments flows. Failure to meet the collection deadline triggers customs export license rectification, a fine under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and adverse KYC impact.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Foreign digital EMI and neobank service providers are not authorized to open won-denominated current accounts, process trade settlements, or provide corporate treasury rails for Korean-resident entities unless they separately hold a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and are registered as a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. No major offshore Tier-1 EMI currently satisfies this dual licensing requirement; therefore, fintech EMI viability is Low and legally constrained. The port stevedoring LLC must rely on domestic Tier-1 commercial banks for escrow, settlement, FX conversion, and trade clearing.

Turnover: A Yuhan Hoesa is legally required to obtain an independent certified CPA audit under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, Etc. where, at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year, the company's total assets are at least KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9.0 million at 1,340 KRW/USD). The Act does not separately impose a turnover or headcount threshold for a private port stevedoring LLC; the mandatory quantitative balance-sheet trigger is total assets of KRW 12 billion or more. In addition, a statutory audit is required where the company is a financial service company, designated by the Financial Services Commission as a public-interest company, files consolidated financial statements under K-IFRS, or is a subsidiary of an audited parent that is consolidated into a K-IFRS reporting group. A Yuhan Hoesa with total assets below KRW 12 billion and no public-interest designation is exempt from mandatory independent CPA audit, although members may voluntarily appoint an auditor.

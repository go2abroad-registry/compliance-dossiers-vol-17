Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, before the establishment registration is filed, the initial contribution capital must be fully subscribed by the members and actually deposited into a designated Korean commercial bank account, and the bank issued deposit certificate must be attached to the registration filing. If any contribution is in kind, a court appointed examiner or certified appraiser must verify the specific property value. Once the company registration is completed, the deposited funds are available for ordinary business use and no further blocked escrow period is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), operating the HomeTax e-Tax portal (www.hometax.go.kr); cross-border export clearance and customs supervision are handled by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS portal (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that commences a taxable business or establishes a domestic place of business in South Korea must file a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office, or through the HomeTax online portal, within 20 days of business commencement. For non-resident entities with no Korean address, the application must be accompanied by a Power of Attorney appointing a tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes, an apostilled certificate of incorporation, board resolution authorizing the Korean business activity, evidence of the fixed domestic place of business, and identification of the responsible representative. On acceptance, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number that serves as both the corporate TIN and VAT ID. This registration is mandatory for domestic exporters: the locally incorporated Yuhan Hoesa, as a Korean-resident exporter, obtains its own corporate Tax Identification Number and VAT registration through this business registration procedure and must separately be registered in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for a Yuhan Hoesa; a domestic incorporated entity is always classified as a separate corporate income taxpayer. Korean law applies hybrid mismatch rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act, but no elective pass-through or hybrid entity classification is available. Qualifying domestic groups may elect consolidated corporate tax filing, but this statutory consolidation does not change the entity-level taxation classification of each subsidiary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary VAT registration threshold for a Yuhan Hoesa: mandatory VAT registration is triggered by the first taxable supply or the opening of a business in South Korea, regardless of turnover. The KRW 80 million annual turnover threshold applies only to the simplified taxpayer regime for individual sole proprietors and is not available to corporate entities. Even export-only turnover requires VAT registration because only a registered VAT taxpayer can issue valid tax invoices, file periodic VAT returns, and claim the 0% export VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies mandatorily to exported goods, including dried goji berries and herbal botanicals supplied in the ordinary export trade, under Article 24 of the VAT Act. The statutory requirements are that the seller is a registered Korean VAT taxpayer, the goods physically depart the Republic of Korea, and the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. A domestic delivery to a foreign buyer, or a sale where the goods do not actually leave Korea, is standard-rated at 10%. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-resident customers and used wholly outside Korea may also be zero-rated, but the present goods export derives its 0% treatment from the physical export declaration rather than an e-service rule.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) accepted by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the international carrier evidencing physical export
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the dried goji berries and herbal botanicals
  • International Sales Contract / Purchase Order executed with the overseas buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • UNI-PASS export clearance result data confirming the outgoing cargo movement and customs release

South Korea does not operate a Latin-American-style real-time fiscal clearance system with server-side digital stamping. Instead, the NTS enforces a mandatory electronic tax invoice regime: registered VAT taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices through the HomeTax electronic tax invoice system or an NTS-approved open API-integrated ERP system, and transmit the data to the NTS by the day following the date of issue. The NTS validates invoices electronically for duplicates, mismatches, and fraud patterns, and for B2C supplies retail sellers must issue electronic cash receipts where payment is made without a tax invoice. Export declarations must be cleared electronically through the KCS UNI-PASS system, which records the outward movement of goods and is the anti-fraud backbone for zero-rated export VAT.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly verify the export declaration, bill of lading, cargo tracking, foreign exchange settlement, and the identity of the offshore purchaser. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine physical exportation, or if documents are false, the NTS will retroactively claw back the 0% rate and assess the 10% VAT, plus statutory additional tax, collection interest, and overdue penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Willful misconduct can be prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, carrying imprisonment of up to three years and fines up to three times the evaded tax. The Korea Customs Service may additionally impose smuggling or false-declaration customs penalties up to a multiple of the evaded customs duty, and the exporter must retain all supporting export, customs, transport, and foreign exchange documents for at least five years after the VAT filing due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime and has achieved full current-account convertibility; most trade-related and service-related transfers are unrestricted and can be executed through designated foreign exchange banks without prior approval. Capital-account operations, however, including foreign direct investment, equity subscription, cross-border loans, and certain portfolio transactions, remain subject to notification, reporting, or approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, as administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and monitored by the Bank of Korea. A foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa can open Korean won and foreign currency accounts and may receive capital contributions in USD or KRW after filing an initial foreign-investment report with a Korean foreign exchange bank; foreign currency borrowing beyond statutory thresholds must be reported to the Bank of Korea or the relevant regulatory authority. Export proceeds are generally received and retained in foreign currency within a designated foreign exchange account, and conversion to Korean won is at market rates without automatic compulsory surrender, but reporting violations can trigger administrative penalties. Profit repatriation is permitted after certified financial statements are issued, the final foreign-investment report is filed, and corporate income tax obligations are settled; however, dividend remittance can be delayed by documentation checks and requirement to prove that the original capital entry was properly registered and that the underlying earnings originated from bona fide trade receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must collect export proceeds within 15 months from the date of exportation; extensions are possible for unavoidable causes with approval from the Ministry of Economy and Finance or the Bank of Korea. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, meaning exporters are not forced to convert foreign currency into Korean won; however, once received, 100% of export foreign currency proceeds must be credited to a designated foreign exchange bank account or converted into Korean won through a licensed bank, and the receipt must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEBIS).

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) – Foreign Exchange Administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, jointly administered with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Global digital EMIs and offshore electronic money institutions are not licensed to hold deposits or maintain domestic corporate current accounts in South Korea. Financial operations require a banking license under the Banking Act or Express Money Transfer Business license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and foreign EMIs generally cannot provide the statutory foreign-exchange bank relationship needed for Korean trade settlement, capital verification, FEBIS reporting, or profit repatriation. A Yuhan Hoesa should use a tier-one domestic commercial bank for all corporate treasury activity; fintech EMIs may be used only as ancillary payment initiation tools where the underlying liquidity stays in a licensed Korean bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all inward equity and shareholder loan transfers in USD, EUR, or KRW via SWIFT to the Yuhan Hoesa's designated foreign-exchange bank account under the direct investment purpose code for a Korean foreign-invested company. The foreign investor must file the initial foreign-investment report with the receiving Korean foreign-exchange bank or through the Foreign Exchange Information System before or at the time of remittance; bank-level recognition of the report number is required for the wire to be classified as legally admitted capital rather than personal transfer. After the inward remittance is credited, the receiving bank confirms the payment, and a Korean certified public accountant issues the paid-in capital verification certificate confirming the share issuance or capital increase. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Deficit in Corporate Income Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 3(1): imprisonment up to 2 years (aggravated up to 5 years if evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million) or fine up to 3 times the evaded amount; plus the National Tax Service imposes 40% of the understated tax as a negligence penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 48(2), and 0.66% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 39(1), the director may be jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax if the director grossly neglected duties. Civil asset restitution includes seizure and sale of personal bank accounts, vehicles, and real estate. Criminal criminal proceedings: travel ban (출국금지) under Article 26 of the Tax-Related punishment procedures; obstruction of a tax officer may lead to imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced where the corporation was used as a shell for tax fraud; the representative director and any financial officer physically signing the fraudulent return face criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years and personal restitution for all penalized tax.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: LCITA Articles 4–6: NTS makes a transfer pricing adjustment equal to the arm's length price; grossly unfaithful transfer pricing documents may attract a penalty of KRW 30 million or 2% of the adjusted income. Interest on the unpaid tax accrues at 0.66% per month; additionally, a 10% penalty for inappropriate intercompany transactions may be imposed. Thin capitalization recharacterization: deductible interest denied and treated as constructive dividend subject to withholding tax of 25% plus surtax.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for tax assessments arising from transfer pricing adjustments if they signed the transfer pricing documentation or the corporate return without reasonable cause. The NTS may issue an asset preservation writ (압류), a travel ban, and a criminal referral for 'fraudulent evasion' if the price manipulation was deliberate. The court may uphold unlimited personal restitution and disqualification from serving as director for up to 5 years under the Commercial Act Article 622 (unlawful conduct by directors).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance Facilitating Tax Treaty Abuse or AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Framework Act on National Taxes Article 76-5 permits outright denial of treaty benefits; the National Tax Service may assess additional corporate income tax, 20% negligence penalty, and withhold tax at 25% (plus 2.5% surtax) on disguised distributions. Violations of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act for cross-border payments without a full declaration are subject to a fine up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow shareholders of a Korean shell company are jointly and severally liable under the Commercial Act Article 623 for actions performed in the company's name. Piercing the corporate veil: court may send the corporate assets into receivership and impose personal civil restitution for dividends deemed repatriated abroad.

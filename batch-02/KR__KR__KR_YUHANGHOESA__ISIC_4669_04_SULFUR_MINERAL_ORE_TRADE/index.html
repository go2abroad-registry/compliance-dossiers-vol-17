Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None under the Korean Commercial Act. The Articles of Incorporation may fix any capital amount. Nevertheless, before a company registration is accepted, the entire subscribed capital must be paid in full and evidenced. Cash contributions must be deposited into a Korean Banking Business Act licensed bank or securities company, which issues a contribution payment certificate (paid-in capital deposit certificate). This certificate is the proof required by the court; it operates as a restricted/escrow verification because funds remain traceable from the account of the incorporator into the contributed capital ledger. In-kind contributions require an independent appraiser's report if not a listed security. For a corporate-registration fee illustration, a capital of KRW 10,000,000 (about USD 7,500) yields registration tax and education surcharge of roughly KRW 48,000 (about USD 36), plus court fixed fees, totaling about USD 72; the actual amount is computed by the registry on the declared capital.

When vessels are engaged, the ocean carrier must hold SOLAS, International Maritime Dangerous Goods, and a valid Port State control certificate for bulk sulfur cargo, and the broker shall not charter a vessel without those certificates.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic tax portal; customs export control and clearance supervision are exercised by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system.

E-government filing portal

Yuhan Hoesa LLC, as a Korean domestic limited liability company, must apply for a business operator registration number through the NTS HomeTax system or its competent district tax office. The application must be submitted within 20 days after the commencement of VAT-taxable business activity. The registration dossier includes the certificate of incorporation, certificate of registered matters from the Korean registry, articles of incorporation, foreign shareholder identification documents, proof of capital where applicable, address proof for the domestic place of business, and appointment of a domestic tax representative if required by the non-resident shareholder structure. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic establishment that nonetheless imports goods or supplies Korean VAT-taxable services, the NTS issues a separate non-resident business operator number after a domestic tax representative is appointed; that representative files VAT returns, issues tax invoices, and pays VAT on behalf of the non-resident. The same 10-digit business operator number is the statutory TIN, VAT ID, and withholding-agent registration number, and is electronically verified via the HomeTax portal and the UNI-PASS customs clearance network.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korea does not operate a check-the-box regime. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Tax Act and cannot elect pass-through treatment. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary VAT registration threshold applies to a corporation in South Korea; VAT registration is mandatory upon the commencement of taxable supplies of goods or services. The simplified VAT rules for small businesses are available only to individual business operators and do not apply to Yuhan Hoesa LLC, which must therefore register before the first export transaction, irrespective of expected turnover in KRW.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods delivered to a place outside Korea are eligible for the 0% VAT rate when the exporter files a valid export declaration with Korea Customs Service and the goods are physically removed from Korea to a foreign destination. For the dry bulk granular sulfur ore trading model, Yuhan Hoesa must demonstrate that title and risk passed to the foreign buyer, that the ore was loaded and shipped from a Korean seaport under a marine bill of lading, that the sales contract contains a clear export destination, and that the foreign exchange proceeds are received through a Korean licensed foreign-exchange bank. Export brokerage and freight-arrangement services separately contracted in respect of the export may also qualify as zero-rated export services, whereas domestic inland freight, warehousing, and port handling are standard-rated Korean VAT supplies. The zero-rated export schedule is supported by the quarterly VAT return, and an export VAT refund of attributable input VAT may be claimed when input VAT exceeds output VAT.

  • UNI-PASS Export Declaration issued by Korea Customs Service evidencing export clearance of the granular sulfur ore shipment
  • Marine or combined transport Bill of Lading naming the foreign buyer as consignee or notify party and confirming shipment from a Korean port to a destination outside Korea
  • Original commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the customs declaration in product description, gross/net weight, packaging, and contract value
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or electronic foreign-exchange transaction record issued by a Korean Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing actual receipt of the USD proceeds within the statutory collection period
  • Signed export sales contract / purchase order and an independent pre-shipment surveyor certificate confirming sulfur content, moisture, weight, and quality

All Korean VAT taxable businesses must issue tax invoices and report the invoice data to the NTS through HomeTax. Legal entities are mandatorily required to issue electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) through the HomeTax system and transmit each e-invoice to the NTS no later than the day after issuance. Individual taxpayers fall under the mandatory e-invoicing requirement when prior-year revenue exceeds the statutory threshold of KRW 100,000,000, but a limited liability company is always subject to the electronic invoicing mandate. ERP and accounting platforms issuing Korean VAT e-invoices must integrate with the National Tax Service HomeTax e-invoice API using the standard XML request/response schema. Credit-card sales, cash-receipt transactions, and point-of-sale data are transmitted to the NTS in real time through licensed VAN networks. Korea does not use a physical fiscal seal or mechanical invoice-stamping device; anti-fraud substantiation is achieved through real-time digital matching of HomeTax e-invoices, credit-card and cash-receipt data, bank foreign-exchange settlement records, UNI-PASS export declarations, and international trade statistics.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims by cross-matching UNI-PASS export declarations, HomeTax e-invoices, marine bills of lading, vessel movement records, foreign-exchange settlement reports, and buyer payment accounts. If the documentary bundle is incomplete, inconsistent, or the goods are found not to have been physically exported, the NTS retroactively revokes the 0% rate and assesses VAT at 10% on the full export invoice value, together with penalty tax and statutory interest under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Deliberate submission of forged export documents or false zero-rated refund claims constitutes tax evasion and may be referred to prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, exposing the entity and responsible officers to criminal fines and imprisonment, while the limitation period for tax collection on fraudulent evasion is extended to 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal but closely monitored foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for current-account transactions such as trade settlement, service payments, dividends, and royalties, provided that a domestic foreign-exchange bank receives the required declaration or report under Article 6 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital-account transactions are generally subject to a positive-list system that requires prior or ex post reporting, registration, or licensing depending on the nature of the transaction; inflows such as equity subscription proceeds require a foreign investment report, while loan drawdowns require a loan agreement registration and, when applicable, separate BOK/MOEF approval. Export proceeds are not confiscated or forcibly converted, but they must be received into a domestic foreign-exchange bank account within the statutory timeline of one year from the export date unless a written extension is granted by the foreign exchange bank; the exporter may retain the proceeds in a foreign-currency account and convert into KRW when required. Profit remittance hurdles are moderate: a foreign-invested company may remit offshore dividends upon completing the statutory audit, proving tax reconciliation, and presenting the foreign-investment registration certificate and distribution resolution; the remittance is subject to transaction purpose-code filings. Withholding tax of up to 20 per cent applies unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and the bank will require certificate-of-residence documentation. Although there is no blanket surrender requirement, banks apply stringent anti-money-laundering and source-of-funds checks on every conversion, and Financial Action Task Force standards are enforced by the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit. Overall, the won is convertible for genuine trade and investment purposes, but treasury management must route all settlement activity through regulated onshore banking channels to avoid unapproved currency swaps or unlicensed offshore settlement constructs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transaction Regulations, resident exporters must collect export proceeds within one year from the export declaration date unless a written extension is approved by an authorized foreign-exchange bank for justifiable causes such as extended payment terms or deferred collection; the proceeds must be received in a foreign-currency account at an authorized domestic bank or sold promptly to an authorized foreign-exchange bank, and the onshore receipt must be reported using the standard export-receipt code in the bank's electronic system. No compulsory percentage conversion into KRW is mandated; the exporter may retain 100 per cent of the foreign-currency receipts in a foreign-currency account, but any conversion to KRW must be documented with the matching export customs declaration and commercial invoice.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Investment Review Council under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-nil for a Korean-domiciled operating entity. Overseas digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Wise generally lack a domestic foreign-exchange bank license under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and therefore cannot lawfully maintain full KRW settlement, issue Korean disbursement instructions, or act as the primary settlement bank for export-receipt collection. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa must route cross-border payments, LC issuance, and foreign-currency balances through an authorized domestic bank or a licensed domestic electronic-financial-transaction provider holding BOK/MOEF registrations. An EMI can be used only as a supplementary remittance channel for minor, pre-approved low-value disbursements, and even then only if the EMI holds a foreign-exchange business registration with the Bank of Korea and the customer passes the onshore bank's reimbursement and AML controls. In practice, compliance officers will reject an EMI as the main treasury channel, and counterparties in the sulfur bulk trade will refuse to accept letters of credit issued by an unlicensed platform.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign investment into the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must comply with the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA). The non-resident investor opens a temporary foreign-currency receipt account at an authorized domestic foreign-exchange bank, wires the USD, EUR, or CNY capital under the correct SWIFT purpose code for inward foreign investment, and files the required foreign-investment report with the Bank of Korea or Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA), either before or simultaneously with the share subscription. After the report is accepted, the bank issues a foreign-investment registration certificate and the funds are converted into KRW at the onshore market rate; conversion is settled against the foreign-investment report and certificate. Although no custodial capital escrow account is statutorily mandated, most banks hold the initial subscription in a segregated non-interest-bearing account until the share subscription is executed and the business registration is filed. The capital verification for treasury and accounting purposes is evidenced by the foreign-investment registration certificate, the remittance SWIFT MT103 showing the investor as remitter, the foreign-exchange contract rate and confirmation, and the board resolution approving the subscription.For the bulk dry-shipping and brokerage activity, outbound settlements should be executed through onshore clearing rails only. First, for advance payments to foreign suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 from the Korean bank account to the receiving bank, annotating the transfer with the true BOK trade purpose code and attaching the commercial contract and customs declaration in the bank transfer file. Second, for documentary shipments, issue an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit through the opening Korean bank in the exporter's favour, with the bank advising through a first-class correspondent; this is the preferred rail for high-value elemental sulfur cargoes because it conditions payment on presentation of on-board Bills of Lading, quality certificates, and invoices. Third, for open-account or collections flows, use Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A), under which the Korean bank forwards title documents to the collecting bank and releases documents against sight payment or acceptance; this is permissible for lower-value or repeated broker flows but carries payment risk. The Korean entity may also make KRW vendor payments to domestic freight and handling partners, while foreign-currency payments must be reported in the bank's foreign-exchange transaction system and the bank will retain a full cash-flow audit trail.Onshore USD/KRW deliverable forwards are permitted only when there is a genuine current- or capital-account performance obligation; the bank requires a signed underlying trade contract and proof of export or import exposure to support the hedge.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) trades are allowed only for documented trade exposure via a bank licensed to operate the onshore NDF market; resident entities are expected to use deliverable forwards, and speculative offshore NDF leverage should be avoided because it violates the anti-speculation principle.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps are permissible for hedging medium-term foreign-currency loan principal and interest exposures when the underlying borrowing has been registered with the authorities and the swap is executed with an authorized bank or licensed broker.Domestic FX option strategies such as purchased USD/KRW calls or puts are permissible for trade hedging, subject to the same real-demand documentation rule; uncovered option selling is not permitted for standalone profit generation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-nil for a Korean-domiciled operating entity. Overseas digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Wise generally lack a domestic foreign-exchange bank license under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and therefore cannot lawfully maintain full KRW settlement, issue Korean disbursement instructions, or act as the primary settlement bank for export-receipt collection. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa must route cross-border payments, LC issuance, and foreign-currency balances through an authorized domestic bank or a licensed domestic electronic-financial-transaction provider holding BOK/MOEF registrations. An EMI can be used only as a supplementary remittance channel for minor, pre-approved low-value disbursements, and even then only if the EMI holds a foreign-exchange business registration with the Bank of Korea and the customer passes the onshore bank's reimbursement and AML controls. In practice, compliance officers will reject an EMI as the main treasury channel, and counterparties in the sulfur bulk trade will refuse to accept letters of credit issued by an unlicensed platform.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.5% top combined corporate income tax rate (25% national corporate tax plus 2.5% local income tax surcharge, computed as 10% of the 25% national CIT); lower progressive national rates of 9% on up to KRW 200 million and 19% on the KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion tranche apply with an equivalent 10% local surcharge

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File documentation become compulsory when the Korean company's annual turnover equals or exceeds KRW 100 billion and total cross-border related-party transactions equal or exceed KRW 50 billion; a simplified International Transactions Annex is also required in the corporate tax return when transactions with a single foreign related party exceed KRW 5 billion, and a Country-by-Country Report is required when the consolidated group revenue reaches KRW 1.5 trillion.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Korean tax evasion law, in particular the fraudulent-evasion offense under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Evaders, provides imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 200 million, with additional administrative penalties of 40% of the understated tax and daily late-payment surcharges; the NTS is empowered to redetermine the taxable base for six years in fraud cases and to enforce collection through seizure of corporate and personal assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when a director personally directed the concealment; the director becomes secondarily liable for the unpaid corporate tax under the National Tax Collection Act, may be blacklisted and placed on the NTS overseas-travel restriction list until the tax is paid, can have personal bank accounts and real estate attached, and may be criminally convicted to imprisonment; civil restitution to damaged creditors and the tax authority is also ordered by Korean courts.
Shell Company and Conduit Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS will disregard or re-characterize the intermediary company under the substance-over-form doctrine embedded in the Framework Act on National Taxes and may apply the Principal Purpose Test contained in Korea's tax treaties; denied expense deductions will be restored to taxable income, outbound payments will be re-tolled as dividends subject to 22% withholding, and a penalty of 40% of the false documentation or understatement may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false board minutes, substance certificates, or treaty-relief applications can be held personally and jointly liable for all additional corporate taxes, interest, and penalties; they face asset forfeiture, travel bans, and blacklisting by the NTS; if they knowingly cause abuse of a tax treaty, they are exposed to criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, with imprisonment terms of up to 3 years under Korean tax-coordination law, and foreign parent indemnities will not protect them from Korean personal liability.

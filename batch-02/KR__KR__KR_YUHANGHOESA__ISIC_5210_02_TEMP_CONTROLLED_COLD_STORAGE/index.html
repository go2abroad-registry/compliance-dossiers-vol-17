Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); customs export clearance and zero-rating evidence are administered by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean limited liability company (Yuhan Hoesa) owned by non-residents, the company must first complete its Commercial Act incorporation registration at the competent district court. The legal entity must then file an application for business registration with the local district NTS office via HomeTax within 20 days of business commencement. The filing requires articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, board resolution or ownership/ownership time details, evidence of the fixed place of business such as a lease contract or ownership certificate for the temperature-controlled cold storage facility, and the passport/foreign corporate certificate of the non-resident shareholders. The NTS will issue a permanent 10-digit business registration number (business operator registration number), which simultaneously functions as the VAT registration number and the corporate annual income tax identifier. For any foreign non-resident entity without domestic incorporation, a Korean tax representative must be appointed and notified to the relevant district tax office under the Framework Act on National Taxes before applying for a business registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean tax system does not provide a check-the-box-style election; a legal entity organized under the Korean Commercial Act as a Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act, regardless of the tax classification of the entity in its home jurisdiction. Separate statutory elections are available only in specific areas such as consolidated corporate tax filing between a domestic parent and its wholly owned domestic subsidiaries, and thin-capitalization or transfer-pricing advance arrangements, but no general entity-classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies; every business operator carrying on a trade in the Republic of Korea is obliged to register for VAT within 20 days from the date of business commencement, regardless of annual revenue. A simplified taxpayer filing regime applies to qualifying small businesses with annual taxable revenue not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but VAT registration remains mandatory. For foreign non-resident suppliers of remote digital services, a separate simplified VAT registration regime applies once the preceding calendar-year supply of such services to Korean customers crosses the statutory threshold set under the VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Korean VAT Act applies to goods physically exported from the Republic of Korea to a foreign country, as evidenced by an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. In the context of temperature-controlled cold storage logistics for perishable pharmaceuticals, the physical sale and delivery of the goods into Korean customs-bonded or export-procedure movement will qualify for 0% VAT only when the goods are actually exported and the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to Korea. Warehousing and storage services rendered to a non-resident customer in connection with goods that remain in Korea are not automatically zero-rated; such services are zero-rated only if they are supplied to a person outside Korea, are directly linked to the exported goods, and are paid for in convertible foreign currency under the foreign exchange regulations and the conditions of Article 24 of the VAT Act and its Enforcement Decree. An exporter must retain full documentary proof of physical export, foreign currency invoicing, and overseas payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS system (export declaration number),
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the international carrier evidencing delivery outside Korea,
  • Commercial Invoice signed by the exporter and the overseas buyer,
  • Detailed Packing List,
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Foreign Currency Receipt Confirmation issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank,
  • Inward Remittance Advice / bank credit confirmation showing the export payment received from the overseas buyer,
  • Cold-chain temperature records and refrigerated transport logs for the perishable pharmaceutical cargo (sector-specific evidentiary support)

All registered business operators are required to issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax system or through an approved e-tax-invoice program. The e-tax invoice must include the customer business registration number, supply date, supply value, VAT rate, and VAT amount. E-tax invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS by the day following the date of issuance. Cold-storage and warehousing services provided to VAT-registered customers must be covered by an electronic tax invoice; the NTS performs real-time cross-matching of declared purchases against issued invoices, and the absence of a valid e-tax invoice will deny the customer an input VAT deduction. The Korea Customs Service uses the UNI-PASS system for all export declarations, with electronic exchange of the export declaration number, and no separate physical fiscal stamp is used; anti-fraud enforcement is effected through NTS invoice matching, annual reconciliation, and penalty regimes.

The NTS has the statutory right to audit export transactions and to request the original customs declaration, bill of lading, foreign exchange bank confirmation, and payment records. If zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary evidence, or if export proceeds are not repatriated to Korea within the applicable foreign exchange settlement deadline (generally 180 days from export, subject to extension), the supply is re-characterized as a taxable domestic supply and the exporter becomes liable for 10% VAT on the supply value plus additional tax penalties and interest for negligent or intentional non-payment. False or fraudulent export invoices and fabricated zero-rating claims are subject to fraudulent tax invoice penalties under the VAT Act, under-assessment and tax evasion penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes, export and currency violation sanctions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and, where the tax underpayment exceeds established thresholds, criminal prosecution sanctions may also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized but monitoring-based foreign exchange system. Current account transactions are effectively convertible: receipts and payments for goods, services and ordinary business income may flow through licensed foreign exchange banks without individual approval. Capital account flows, however, are not unconditional; inbound foreign direct investment and outbound financial transfers require notification to the reporting authority through a designated foreign exchange bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The Korean won is managed through a free-floating exchange rate regime, and there is no statutory forced surrender of export proceeds. In practice, export collections must be brought into a domestic foreign exchange bank account within settlement timeframes and can be converted to won at market rates. Profit remittance for a foreign-invested Korean LLC is lawful once corporate formalities are satisfied, but banks require proof of tax payments, audited financial documents, and an approved foreign investment report. Administrative discipline is exercised through the Foreign Exchange Information System, so wire origin and purpose codes must be clearly documented. Overall, the regime places strong emphasis on evidentiary trail rather than quantitative rationing of currency, making corporate treasury planning feasible through mainstream domestic banks rather than offshore EMIs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Regulation on Foreign Exchange Transactions, export proceeds must be collected and repatriated into the exporter domestic foreign exchange bank account within 180 days from the shipment date unless an extended settlement period is approved by the Korea Customs Service, the Bank of Korea, or a foreign exchange bank with delegated authority. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may retain foreign currency in a designated foreign currency account or sell it to the bank in the KRW market. For cold-chain services exported to non-resident customers, the treaty trade settlement must match the declared commercial payment term and be supported by invoices, bills of lading or electronic service performance records.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, as the central monetary authority, operating in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; authorized foreign exchange banks administer reporting and settlement duties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and not legally viable for the Korean Yuhan Hoesa as a primary banking channel. Domestic fintech electronic money issuers licensed under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act are not full banks and cannot open a corporate foreign-invested capital escrow account or execute foreign exchange contracts under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Offshore digital EMIs without a Korean money exchange license or foreign exchange bank license are generally barred from servicing Korean entities with corporate accounts. Such providers may at most support ancillary card-issuance or payment facilitation, but the treasury for this entity must remain with tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder should file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with a domestic foreign exchange bank or KOTRA. After the report is granted, capital must be remitted through an international bank wire using the shareholder's own account in the country of origin. The SWIFT MT103 must include the approved report number as reference, and a clear purpose code corresponding to equity capital for a logistics enterprise. On receipt, the Korean bank credits the capital escrow account and later issues the foreign investment certificate after verifying the remittance, share issuance and valuation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal national corporate income tax rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; below the threshold, rates are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion and 21% up to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on dividend distributions, including deemed profit repatriation, to non-resident shareholders; bilateral treaty reductions are available only after procedural DTA relief is approved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 5,000,000,000 (KRW 5 billion) in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and, where the foreign group has consolidated revenue exceeding KRW 100 billion, Master File preparation; an International Transaction Summary Report and transfer pricing method disclosure must also be filed under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs.

Economic substance scope: Although Korea has not adopted Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Korean tax law applies the substance-over-form doctrine, the OECD Multilateral Instrument principal-purpose test, and general anti-avoidance rules to deny treaty and incentive benefits where the entity lacks genuine business purpose. For a refrigerated logistics company, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Korea: physical cold-storage capacity, qualified local staff, commercial risk, client contracts, daily temperature-control operations, and strategic board decisions. A Yuhan Hoesa whose board, treasury, contracts, and management decisions occur outside Korea and that merely holds title to a warehouse will be reclassified as a shell and lose tax treaty and free-zone incentive protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Income Omission

Statutory sanction: Article 9(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment for up to 3 years or a fine of up to 3 times the amount of tax evaded; additional tax of 20% for unjustified under-reporting under Article 47-4 of the Framework Act on Taxes; local income tax surcharge and daily delayed-payment interest are collected on top of the reassessed national tax.

Director personal liability: Where corporate formalities are used as an instrument of fraud, the National Tax Service and courts pierce the corporate veil and hold the representative director jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and local surcharges; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, confiscation of personal and foreign assets, travel blacklisting, and seizure of bank and investment accounts held in the director's name.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Economic Substance Abusive Structure

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs for non-submission of documentation and false reporting, plus reassessment using the best method selected by the National Tax Service; non-arm's-length income adjustments are taxed at source with 10%-20% under-reporting additional tax and interest; intra-group service and royalty deductions are denied where service value is not substantiated.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the submission of false transfer pricing documentation may be prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; personal asset restitution may be ordered, and directors may be declared jointly liable for the adjusted corporate tax, with travel bans and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years in willful evasion cases.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The Korean payer is liable to collect the unwithheld 22% national and local tax; under the Corporate Tax Act and Local Tax Act, the withheld tax is recovered from the payer together with negligence additional tax of up to 20% and daily late-payment interest; treaty reduction is denied ex post and the funds are reassessed with an additional 40% false-claim penalty.

Director personal liability: The representative director and finance officers who directed the non-withholding or treaty abuse are personally jointly liable for the unpaid corporate and local withholding taxes; criminal indictment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may impose imprisonment, asset freezing, confiscation of foreign accounts, and blacklisting; civil actions by the state may attach the directors' personal real estate, vehicles, and pension assets to satisfy the tax claim.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; statutory electronic tax portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Export declarations and customs-related export evidence are handled by the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

The Yuhan Hoesa must complete the Korean Business Registration procedure under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes before or no later than 20 days after commencing business. The LLC files the Business Registration Application with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its registered office, or electronically through NTS HomeTax. Required supporting documents include: articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, director identification and Korean address evidence, lease or office-occupancy documentation, a written application signed with the corporate seal or authorized electronic certificate, and, for foreign-invested LLCs, foreign shareholder and power-of-attorney records. The NTS issues a 10-digit corporate Business Registration Number, which functions as both the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT registration number. A foreign non-resident entity that has no Korean place of business but makes Korean taxable supplies of electronic or digital services must separately register in HomeTax using the foreign business-operator registration pathway; the NTS requires a Korean tax representative, a duly notarized power of attorney, the foreign certificate of incorporation, overseas tax identification data, and evidence of Korean digital supply. If the foreign entity only holds shares in or receives payments from the Yuhan Hoesa, no separate Korean VAT ID is required, but withholding tax and corporate income tax reporting obligations apply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary exemption threshold in Korean won applies to a domestic Yuhan Hoesa; any taxable commercial supply triggers mandatory VAT business registration from commencement of business. Foreign non-resident suppliers without a Korean fixed place of business that supply electronic or digital services to Korean recipients must likewise register from the time they commence Korean taxable supply; no general de minimis registration threshold exists in local currency. The KRW 80,000,000 simplified taxable person regime affects calculation and reporting simplification for very small domestic businesses, not exemption from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the export of goods from Korea and services supplied to a foreign country or foreign resident are zero-rated. For the ISIC 7490 localization business, translation, subtitling, dubbing, voice-over, media localization, linguistic QA, and related professional services qualify for 0% VAT when they are supplied under a contract with a foreign counterparty, the services are actually performed for and used outside Korea, and the consideration is paid in foreign currency and repatriated to Korea. Physical media exported by customs also qualifies if a valid KCS export declaration and transport documents exist. The taxpayer must retain the complete NTS evidentiary package set out below.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) accepted by the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS, required for physical media and tangible deliverables; for pure digital service exports the equivalent electronic delivery evidence must be retained.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Korea to a foreign destination whenever physical media, masters, or localized materials are exported.
  • Commercial invoice and signed localization or dubbing services agreement describing the service, foreign beneficiary, and foreign-currency consideration.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate) issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank, showing receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign client.
  • Digital release and acceptance log for electronically delivered content, including delivery timestamps, file checksums, or platform acknowledgement confirming offshore use.
  • Foreign client's certificate of tax residence, business registration extract, or equivalent documentary proof that the recipient is not a Korean resident.

Mandatory e-Tax Invoice filing through NTS HomeTax. Every taxable B2B supply, including zero-rated export services, requires an electronic tax invoice issued through the HomeTax e-Tax Invoice system or an NTS-accredited ERP/ASP connector using the Korean standard electronic tax-invoice XML schema and HomeTax authentication certificates for API-based submission. There is no real-time per-invoice government clearance before issuance, but each e-tax invoice receives an NTS verification code and is subject to full digital validation, purchaser verification through HomeTax, automatic matching with VAT returns, and anti-fraud verification-code stamping. Issuance details and correction notices must be submitted electronically according to the statutory VAT return cycle; failure to issue or transmit an e-tax invoice triggers penalty taxes.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service may jointly audit export zero-ratings by matching export declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, e-tax invoices, and bank records. If the documentation bundle is incomplete or the foreign-exchange repatriation occurs after the statutory 180-day period without NTS or regulatory approval, the zero-rating is disallowed retroactively. The NTS will assess the 10% VAT on the original invoice amount, add penalty tax for unfaithful returns up to 40% of the deficient VAT in concealment or serious negligence cases, impose late-payment surcharge interest, and claw back any refund previously given with interest. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may additionally trigger enforcement under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and intentional fabrication of export documents is subject to criminal sanctions under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and related statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains an OECD Article VIII recognized current-account convertible regime. The Korean won is fully convertible for current transactions such as royalty payments, service fees, and dividend remittances once a foreign exchange bank confirms the underlying trade contract and purpose code. Capital-account transactions, including foreign equity injection and cross-border loans, are not prohibited by tariffs or surrender quotas but are subject to mandatory reporting/declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; they require either a prior filing, post-facto declaration, or in certain cases approval from MOSF or the Bank of Korea. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary: the paying entity must have completed audited financial statements, a certificate of tax payment for the dividend withholding tax, a corporate seal certificate, and the foreign investor must present the foreign investment registration certificate. There is no statutory cap on remittable ordinary profits once corporate income tax, dividend tax, and settlement fees are satisfied. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a foreign currency deposit at a licensed domestic bank or convert at the market rate through an authorized foreign exchange bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be received in Korea within eighteen (18) months from the date of export declaration, unless an extended settlement period is reported to the Korean customs office and the authorized foreign exchange bank. The mandatory conversion ratio in Korean won is zero percent (0%) under the fully liberalized current-account regime: exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in a foreign-currency deposit account, or sell them to the authorized bank at the prevailing market rate. If the export declaration amount exceeds USD 5,000 equivalent, the exporter must submit an Export Proceeds Settlement Confirmation to the bank in compliance with Article 17-2 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) acting jointly with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations; the BOK operates the national FX reporting system, while authorized foreign exchange banks execute convertibility and settlement operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low legal viability as the primary settlement rail. South Korea does not grant EU-style electronic money institution licenses; a digital EMI cannot hold Korean-won deposits or issue Korean bank account numbers under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act unless it is authorized as a small-payment agency or licensed financial company. Wise B2B and Payoneer may be used as supplementary disbursement rails for low-value localization vendor payments outside Korea, but they are not authorized to serve as the corporate bank of record, capital escrow vehicle, or FX settlement counterparty for the Yuhan Hoesa account. The entity must maintain a Tier 1 Korean commercial bank account for statutory FC/FRN reporting, FDI capital verification, tax withholding, and dividend remittance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is to file the Foreign Investment Report (FDI report) with KOTRA or an authorized foreign exchange bank before the SWIFT wire is sent, using the statutory purpose-code category for foreign direct investment in equity capital. The remittance should be a telegraphic transfer from the foreign shareholder's own bank account to the bank-designated escrow/foreign-investment registration account, with the payment narrative clearly stating 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - Yuhan Hoesa Capital Account' and referencing the FDI report number. After the funds are credited, the bank verifies the inflow, issues the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, and the capital amount is booked as paid-in capital. Conversion into Korean won must occur at the authorized bank's prevailing customer spot rate; the bank maintains the required report to the Bank of Korea through the FX information system. Wires must not be split into odd amounts, and no third-party trust funds may be used without a supplemental AML declaration.Outbound trade disbursements for localization services should be executed via SWIFT MT103 priority remittance through the Tier 1 bank, with the correct service-fee purpose code, supporting invoices, and tax invoices attached within the bank's reporting threshold. For high-value or repeat deliveries of licensed media content, an irrevocable documentary credit (Commercial LC) can be established by the Korean buyer or the entity's foreign client; however, since ISIC 7490 is a service activity, documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are uncommon and should only be used when accompanied by a delivery confirmation certificate, a service acceptance certificate, and the underlying contract.

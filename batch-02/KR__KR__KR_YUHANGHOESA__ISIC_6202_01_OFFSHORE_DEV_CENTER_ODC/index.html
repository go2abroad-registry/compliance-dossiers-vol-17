Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - no statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS for export clearance; Foreign Investment Ombudsman / Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) for foreign investment reporting.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Korean Yuhan Hoesa, if the non-resident investor acquires 10% or more of voting shares, file a Foreign Investment Report with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy through KOTRA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and obtain a Foreign Investment Report Certificate. Incorporate the legal entity under the Commercial Act at the local court registry; then apply for Business Registration with the competent district tax office within one month of business commencement under the Framework Act on National Taxes, presenting the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, share-capital remittance certificate, leased place of business contract, and foreign investment report certificate. NTS issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number; the BRN is activated in HomeTax. For a non-resident company without a Korean place of business that makes Korean VAT-taxable supplies, the non-resident must register as a non-resident business operator under Article 53 of the Value-Added Tax Act through a tax representative in Korea and is issued a supplier tax code for VAT purposes.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - registration is mandatory for every domestic corporation from the commencement of business regardless of turnover; no statutory turnover threshold exists for Yuhan Hoesa. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Korean consumers are also required to register for VAT without a de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act and Article 31 of its Enforcement Decree, exports of goods are zero-rated. Services, including offshore software engineering/maintenance, are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident who receives the services wholly outside Korea, the consideration is paid in convertible foreign currency, and documentary evidence of export is retained. The Korean ODC must issue a valid electronic tax invoice (export), and the corresponding foreign exchange receipt must be evidenced.

  • Export Declaration issued and accepted by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS where physical goods or media are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing cargo movement from Korea to the foreign destination when physical goods are involved
  • Written Offshore Dev Center Services Contract between KR_YUHANGHOESA and the non-resident client, defining scope, deliverable location, terms and payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice in convertible foreign currency matching the contractual services and bank receipts
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate (equivalent to Inward Remittance Certificate, FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank confirming the export proceeds were received in foreign currency
  • Electronic Tax Invoice / e-Sacgi for the zero-rated supply, transmitted to the NTS via HomeTax
  • Bank remittance advice and any supplementary evidence of actual use of the software/service by the foreign principal outside Korea

Under Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act and NTS e-Sacgi regulations, all B2B suppliers must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices to HomeTax using structured XML/API by the day after the supply amount is settled; NTS operates near-real-time automated cross-verification. B2C supplies require cash receipts through the mandatory Cash Receipt System. No physical fiscal stamping device is used; the e-invoice is the fiscal clearance act.

NTS can deny the 0% rate where export and FX documentation is absent or inconsistent; the company becomes liable for 10% VAT, plus additional tax of up to 60% of the tax due for cheating under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and a 2% added tax for an unverifiable false e-tax invoice under Article 69 of the Value-Added Tax Act. Fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to criminal sanctions (imprisonment or up to 3 times evaded VAT) and retrospective clawback of input VAT credits. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 540-day period triggers administrative fines and interest under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is convertible for current-account transactions, and software development and export service fees may be collected in foreign currency and held in Korean foreign-currency deposit accounts. Korea does not impose mandatory surrender or fixed conversion quotas on export proceeds; receipt, retention, and repatriation are processed through banks supervised by the Bank of Korea. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment into the Korean LLC, are not unregulated; they require a notification or declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but eligible software engineering services do not require investment approval. The initial equity contribution must be linked to a Foreign Investment Promotion Act notification filed before the inward wire through an authorized foreign-exchange bank or KOTRA. Dividends and branch profits may be remitted after corporate tax settlement using audited financial statements, tax clearance, shareholders' resolutions, and remittance evidence reviewed by the settlement bank. The practical constraints are documentary and procedural rather than prohibitive: every cross-border remittance needs an underlying contract or invoice, a purpose code, a domestic bank relationship, and positive due diligence results. There are no balance-of-payments restrictiveness penalties for a properly notified offshore development center, and no requirement to force KRW conversion at a below-market rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations require goods export proceeds to be collected generally within 180 days of the export customs date and service export proceeds generally within 90 days of service completion, or by the contractual due date if shorter; extensions may be granted by the foreign-exchange bank upon proper justification before the deadline. The mandatory surrender ratio is zero: exporters may retain 100 percent of foreign-currency receipts in a foreign-currency on-call deposit or designated account, and conversion to KRW is at the market rate. All receipts must be reported under the applicable balance-of-payments settlement code so the Bank of Korea can monitor abnormal delays, reversals, or trade-mismatch patterns.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Legally constrained. Wise Business and Payoneer are not licensed Korean banks and cannot open domestic KRW corporate accounts, issue local sort codes, or perform the statutory foreign-exchange reporting that the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act requires a local foreign-exchange bank to perform. They may be used only as auxiliary billing rails for small foreign-currency payments to overseas contractors; all statutory capital transactions, KRW settlements, Korean payroll, VAT payments, and profit remittances must be cleared through a Bank of Korea-supervised tier-1 commercial bank. No offshore EMI can serve as the primary treasury relationship for this ODC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first submit a Foreign Direct Investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through KOTRA or an authorized foreign-exchange bank, receiving a Foreign Investment Notification certificate. The inward payment is then sent by SWIFT MT103 to the Korean ODC's domestic bank account, using the FDI purpose code and referencing the notification certificate. The receiving Korean bank posts the funds with the Bank of Korea's balance-of-payments reporting code, verifies the remitter against the notification, and issues or records the capital verification/foreign investment registration evidence. The ODC may convert the foreign exchange to KRW at the market rate without any mandatory surrender. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% national corporate income tax plus 10% local income tax surcharge, yielding an aggregate 27.5% headline rate for a resident Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national dividend withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge, for a 22% aggregate statutory rate on non-resident shareholders. Qualifying dividends paid to resident corporate shareholders may be exempt under the corporate dividend exemption regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, the Local File is mandatory where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion in the taxable year; Master File preparation is required for Korean groups with annual consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or more, and Country-by-Country reporting applies where the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches KRW 1 trillion. The NTS may still request contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation below those thresholds based on transaction risk and comparability indicators.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not operate a statutory offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code of the type seen in certain financial centers, but the substance-over-form and GAAR doctrines under the Corporate Tax Act and OECD BEPS Action 6 require an Offshore Development Center to maintain genuine presence in Korea: substantive employees, premises, payroll, board meetings, management, and Core Income-Generating Activities. The CIGA standard under applicable treaty Article 7 attributes profits to the Korean permanent establishment only where key entrepreneurial and R&D decisions are made in Korea. A shell with passive directors and no CIGA will be disregarded, treaty relief will be denied, and income will be reallocated to the ultimate shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion, Shell Company Arrangement, and Revenue Understatement in Offshore Development Center

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act assessment adding unreported income and disallowing sham deductions; 40% dishonest-unfiled penalty on the understated tax; 10% local income tax surcharge; International Tax Adjustment Act TP documentation penalties; criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment or fine up to 3 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Korea's corporate veil will be pierced where the LLC is a sham or alter ego of its shareholders. Directors face civil asset restitution, joint personal liability to the Korean tax authority under Commercial Act Article 401, travel blacklisting for unpaid taxes exceeding KRW 100 million, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years, increased to up to 5 years when evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million.
Abuse of Bilateral Double Tax Treaty Relief and False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Denial of the DTA relief under the limitation-on-benefits and GAAR rules; recharacterization of the payments as made to the ultimate non-resident shareholder; imposition of the full 22% aggregate withholding tax plus 40% underreporting penalty, interest, and local surcharge; criminal tax-evasion fines of up to 3 times the tax at risk for false documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial-ownership or TRC certificates are personally liable for the under-withheld corporate taxes, subject to civil damages, asset freezes, and travel blacklisting; they face criminal imprisonment of 3 to 5 years for tax fraud, and officers of both the Korean payer and the foreign conduit may face cross-border mutual-assistance and extradition exposure.
AML and Foreign Exchange Evasion on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act: imprisonment of up to 5 years or fine up to KRW 200 million for unregistered international remittance. Financial Transactions Reports Act penalties apply for failure to file unusual-transaction or suspicious-transaction reports. The NTS may add 20%-40% of the undisclosed income as an unfounded penalty and impose daily surcharges on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of the illegally remitted corporate profits; they face criminal fines, travel bans/passport revocation under tax arrears enforcement, and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Where the corporate form was used as a facade for personal profit shifting, civil veil-piercing makes directors jointly liable for all repatriated amounts and the associated tax penalties.

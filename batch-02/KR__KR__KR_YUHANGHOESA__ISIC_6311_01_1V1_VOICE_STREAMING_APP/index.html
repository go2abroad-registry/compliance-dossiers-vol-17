Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. If capital is declared in the Articles of Incorporation, each subscriber must fully pay the subscribed amount into a domestic Korean bank account before registration; the bank's deposit certificate must be attached to the registration application. If no capital is declared, no blocked escrow account or capital deposit certificate is required, but bank counterparties may still require a funding threshold. Non-resident subscribers may contribute foreign currency subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero threshold for a domestic Yuhan Hoesa engaged in taxable supplies (registration is mandatorily required from the first day of business and is not turnover-dependent). Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act and the relevant NTS guidance, cross-border supplies of voice streaming services (a category of digital services) qualify for 0% VAT treatment if (i) the service is acquired by a foreign non-resident business or foreign individual; (ii) the service is practically consumed outside Korea substantiated by IP geolocation of the user at the time of streaming; (iii) the consideration is remitted in foreign currency and repatriated to a Korean foreign exchange bank account; and (iv) the supplier retains documentary evidence of non-resident status. The service is deemed to be an exported service and not subject to the 10% VAT when the recipient is located in a foreign jurisdiction and the payment is not sourced from Korea.

  • Written service contract or purchase order executed with the non-resident customer and specifying the foreign-currency consideration
  • Commercial invoice issued in the foreign currency, cross-referenced to the bank remittance
  • Foreign Currency Deposit Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt and conversion of the export proceeds
  • Digital service delivery log and platform-generated copy of streaming usage reports geolocated to the foreign customer's IP address
  • Proof of non-resident status of the customer, such as a foreign corporate tax registration certificate, passport copy, or beneficiary tax ID in the recipient jurisdiction
  • If physical goods are bundled in the same transaction (not applicable to pure voice streaming, but required when app is distributed through a physical SDK), the Customs Export Declaration and Bill of Lading shall be added.

All VAT-registered taxpayers in South Korea are obliged to issue and transmit electronic tax invoices through the National Tax Service HomeTax platform using the NTS-certified XML or JSON schema. The invoice data is cleared in near-real time by the NTS Integrated System for E-tax Invoices; there is no additional physical fiscal stamping. For B2C voice-streaming transactions where the customer is a domestic consumer and payment is processed through Apple or Google billing platforms, the app-store operator is treated as a VAT-collecting agent for its foreign branch, and must report the gross sales through its own NTS electronic filing portal. The domestic app provider must nevertheless record each transaction in its VAT ledger and issue an electronic cash receipt in cases where the transaction is not already reported by the payment gateway.

The NTS may audit the zero-rating claim at any time within a 5-year limitation period for negligent misreporting or 10 years for fraud. If the documented foreign currency is not received in Korea within the 180-day repatriation window, the zero rating is voided from the original date, and the NTS will assess the 10% VAT plus inflation interest. Additional penalties apply under Article 60 of the VAT Act: 10% of the underpaid tax for oversight, 40% for non-wilful but negligent misdeclaration, and 80% for fraudulent or concealment-driven claims. Criminal indictment for tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may add imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to KRW 100 million. Any refund already obtained through a zero-rated export return must be repaid with interest, and the refund right is retroactively cancelled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalised but strictly supervised Foreign Exchange Transactions Act framework. The Korean won (KRW) is freely convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods and services, royalty payments, dividends, interest, and platform settlement fees; these flows are subject only to mandatory reporting through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) and do not require prior administrative approval. Capital account transactions, by contrast, remain under a negative list system: inward foreign direct investment must be filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and outward transfers beyond regulatory thresholds require prior reporting to an authorised foreign exchange bank. There is no statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds in the sense of mandatory conversion into KRW; exporters may retain foreign currency balances domestically or abroad, but the receipt must be evidenced within the statutory collection period. Profit remittance is permitted once the Korean entity files audited financial statements, pays corporate tax, and settles the applicable dividend withholding tax, generally 22% for a non-resident corporate shareholder without treaty relief. The practical friction is documentary rather than regulatory: banks require source-of-funds evidence, beneficial ownership disclosure, and the underlying FDI report before executing a large KRW conversion and outbound remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must normally be received within six months from the date of export declaration or from completion of the exported service, with extension available through an authorised foreign exchange bank for bona fide trade reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may keep export proceeds in foreign currency in domestic foreign-currency accounts, but all receipts must be reported via FEIS. If the proceeds are converted into KRW, the receiving bank records the spot transaction under the relevant FX reporting code, and the retained foreign currency is subject to annual foreign-account reporting if held overseas.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the designated foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) for bank conduct supervision and the Korea Customs Service for trade settlement data monitoring

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. South Korea reserves foreign exchange business to authorised foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) do not hold a banking licence and cannot open corporate demand deposit accounts, execute FX conversions, or operate clearing rails for equity capital and trade settlements. An offshore digital EMI may only provide invoicing, payment-card facilitation, or expense sub-accounting if it partners with a licensed domestic bank, but all legal inbound and outbound settlement flows must go through a Tier-1 authorised bank. Treating an EMI as a primary treasury rail would trigger FEIS non-reporting, bank account freezing, and FSS enforcement risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed through an authorised foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The remitter should send a SWIFT MT103 in USD or another freely convertible currency with the remittance information field stating 'Paid-in Capital - Foreign Direct Investment' and referencing the FDI report number issued by MOEF/Invest Korea or the delegated bank. The receiving bank credits the foreign investor's capital escrow account, executes KRW conversion at the market rate, and issues the foreign investment certificate after matching the remittance to the board resolution, the FDI report, and the investor's bank reference. Capital verification is completed when the deposited amount equals the declared equity contribution and the bank obtains a written capital release instruction from the investor and the Korean entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 1 billion in annual cross-border related-party transactions triggering mandatory Local File preparation, and a KRW 100 billion consolidated group revenue threshold triggering Master File documentation; additional disclosure obligations apply to each international transaction exceeding KRW 100 million in the annual corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not follow UAE-style ESR, but applies the substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), and the beneficial ownership requirement under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs. A Yuhan Hoesa must conduct genuine Korean Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as developer selection, user ToS execution, content moderation, payment revenue collection, and server contracting; a paper entity with no employees, no board meeting records, or no economic activity will be disregarded, its income re-attributed to the foreign shareholder and subjected to 22% withholding plus 20% penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding 50 times the evaded tax; under Article 80 of the Corporate Tax Act, penalty tax of 40% of the omitted tax plus late-payment surcharges and a prohibition on future tax deduction benefits.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors personally ordered, concealed, or profited from the evasion; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and civil restitution, can be subjected to a travel ban/blacklisting, and may be arrested and held without bail during criminal tax investigation.
Shell Company Substance Failure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The NTS will apply the substance-over-form doctrine and article 21-2 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs to deny treaty benefits, the shell structure is ignored, all income is recharacterised as a Korean PE profit and assessed with 22% withholding, plus 40% penalty for fraudulent structuring; corporate registration may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors are held civilly liable for restitution of the assets stripped from the shell, are jointly liable for the reassessed corporate tax, face departure prohibitions while a tax avoidance investigation is pending, and may be personally indicted for fraud and tax evasion, with imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Withholding Tax and Tax Remittance Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Withholding Tax provisions and Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, the withholding agent is personally liable for the unremitted tax, a penalty of 20% of the unpaid amount, and criminal punishment of up to 2 years imprisonment or a fine of up to 30 million KRW.

Director personal liability: Because the director or officer acts as the statutory withholding agent, he/she must pay the withheld amount from personal assets; NTS attaches a travel ban automatically for cumulative unremitted withholding tax above KRW 100 million, and a criminal conviction disqualifies the director from serving as an officer of any Korean entity and permits seizure of personal property.

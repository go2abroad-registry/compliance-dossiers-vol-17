Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL must have a minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked capital account at a Luxembourg credit institution until the RCS registration certificate is issued; in-kind contributions require valuation by a statutory auditor unless an exemption applies. After registration the blocked account is released to the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Luxembourg Fire and Rescue Corps (CGDIS) after inspection of alarm systems, extinguishers, hydrants and emergency exits for hot-water pressure-cooking and aseptic canning lines; structural load inspection by an engineer approved by the Administration des bâtiments publics for autoclaves and retorts; occupational health clearance through an approved labour-medicine doctor and CCSS workplace registration; ALVA HACCP inspection for food-contact surfaces and sterilization records.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL must have a minimum share capital of EUR 12,000, fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked capital account at a Luxembourg credit institution until the RCS registration certificate is issued; in-kind contributions require valuation by a statutory auditor unless an exemption applies. After registration the blocked account is released to the company.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for income and net wealth taxes; Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for VAT and registration duties; integrated corporate e-filing portal: MyGuichet.lu (with e-TVA and eCDF modules); company register portal: Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR - lbr.lu)

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation of the SARL, the notary files the deed and articles of association with the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the Luxembourg Business Registers, which then issues the national business identification number (n° d'identification). This number constitutes the corporate tax reference for the ACD, eliminating any separate CIT application. For VAT, the SARL must file electronic registration form Formulaire 640 (inscription à la TVA) with the AEDT before commencing taxable operations and in all cases within 15 days of exceeding the EUR 35,000 threshold; the VAT number is issued in the LU + 8-digit format (with modular check digit). Non-resident shareholders are fully permitted without prior investment license, but the SARL must provide a registered office in Luxembourg, appoint an authorized local manager (gérant), open a Luxembourg bank account, and obtain a food business establishment approval from the Veterinary and Food Administration (ALVA) under Ministry of Health regulation for the canned boba tapioca production plant.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident fully taxable SARL, conclusive corporate opacity; no pass-through election available). The SARL is subject to corporate income tax (17%), municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), and the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), producing a combined statutory headline rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, with relief for foreign permanent establishment income under the exemption-with-progression method and the participation exemption for qualifying 10% stakes held for 12 months. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident fully taxable SARL, conclusive corporate opacity; no pass-through election available). The SARL is subject to corporate income tax (17%), municipal business tax (6.75% in Luxembourg City), and the solidarity surcharge (7% of CIT), producing a combined statutory headline rate of 24.94% in Luxembourg City. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, with relief for foreign permanent establishment income under the exemption-with-progression method and the participation exemption for qualifying 10% stakes held for 12 months. No territorial carve-out or tax-exempt zone applies to food manufacturing.

Available election forms

Luxembourg does not operate a check-the-box regime; an SARL is irrebuttably allocated to the corporate tax column. Statutory elections include: (i) group taxation (intégration fiscale), consolidating 95% of each subsidiary's results among at least 95%-owned resident entities, requiring a 5-year commitment; (ii) participation exemption election for dividend and capital-gain relief (10% shareholding, EUR 1.2 million acquisition price or 10% book value, 12-month holding requirement); (iii) allocation of notional interest deduction (NID) on qualifying new equity capital; (iv) co-ownership tax election for certain joint ventures; (v) ATAD hybrid mismatch adjustment elections requiring the taxpayer to prove either inclusion or corresponding deduction pursuant to Articles 168bis to 168septies LIR.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 35,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for the small business franchise exemption per Article 57 of the Luxembourg VAT Law; registration is mandatory notwithstanding this threshold for (i) any person making intra-Community acquisitions above the EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold, (ii) any non-established supplier distance selling into Luxembourg, and (iii) the importation of goods. Given the export manufacturing and intra-EU distribution model, the SARL must register as a normal VAT taxpayer from the commencement of its production and sale operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of canned boba tapioca pearls to non-EU third countries are zero-rated under Article 39 of the Luxembourg VAT Law where the goods are dispatched or transported out of the EU within the statutory 90-day period (extendable to 180 days upon justified commercial grounds) and the supplier holds probative transport documents. The supply qualifies for the 0% rate only if title and risk have passed to the buyer and the SARL retains evidence that the goods were delivered to a third country or to the EU customer's non-EU consignee. Intra-Community supplies to customers with a valid LU/EU VAT number equally benefit from 0% where the SARL issues a compliant invoice, files the EC Sales List, and includes the transport documentation in its VAT records. Ancillary aseptic tubing, food safety testing, and cold-chain logistics services performed directly in respect of the exported goods qualify as linked export services eligible for the 0% rate, subject to the same documentation requirements. The SARL must obtain and archive the export evidence before the expiration of the third month following the month of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Accompanying Document / EAD with Master Reference Number MRN) evidencing exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) for ocean freight or Air Waybill (AWB) / CMR international consignment note for road transport to non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 credit advice from the Luxembourg clearing bank) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, audited packing list, certificate of origin, and insurance certificate matching the customs declaration weights and HS codes
  • Intra-Community Sales List (EC Sales List), Intrastat dispatch declarations, and copies of the customer's VAT registration certificate for EU-destined zero-rated deliveries

Luxembourg currently imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, PEPPOL-only mandate, or digital stamping/API clearance for B2B e-invoicing. The SARL must file periodic VAT returns electronically through the e-TVA channel of MyGuichet.lu (mandatory for companies), submit monthly or quarterly EC Sales Lists by the 10th of the following month (mandatory for intra-EU zero-rated supplies), and file monthly Intrastat declarations where the annual intra-EU trade value exceeds EUR 1.5 million on arrivals or EUR 5.5 million on dispatches (2024 thresholds). The EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform agenda will progressively introduce structured e-invoicing and transaction-based digital reporting obligations to Luxembourg by 2030; the SARL's ERP should be ViDA-ready.

The AEDT may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate and reassess VAT at 17% if the documentary bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or the goods failed to leave the EU within the legal time limit; such reassessment carries statutory default interest (intérêts de retard) and administrative fines graded up to 100% of the evaded VAT for negligent failure and up to 300% for deliberate concealment. Under the Luxembourg General Tax Law (Abgabenordnung), VAT fraud (fraude fiscale) is punishable by criminal fines up to EUR 125,000 and imprisonment for 8 days to 5 years, or higher where determined by the criminal court, with individual criminal liability for the gérant and responsible officers. The normal 5-year statute of limitation for VAT assessment is extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or documentary suppression, and the AEDT may obtain criminal referral and publish adverse decisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is the lawful tender. Luxembourg applies full current and capital account convertibility under EU Treaty free movement of capital provisions. No quantitative limits apply to dividend repatriation, trade payments, or shareholder loans. Withholding tax on outbound dividends to non-EU jurisdictions may apply if a double tax treaty is not applicable, but no exchange control authorization is required. Restrictions may apply where EU/UN sanctions or Luxembourg anti-money-laundering risk-based safeguards are invoked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Luxembourg. Exporters may hold and dispose of EUR or foreign currency receipts freely. BCL statistical reporting monthly thresholds apply to intra-Community trade over EUR 10,000; non-EU trade receipts require no prior authorization. EU anti-money-laundering (AML) obligations require retention of underlying trade documentation for five years.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL) with prudential and anti-money laundering oversight by Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF); Ministry of Finance supervises capital market integration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate, low to mid order probability. Fully-licensed EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into Luxembourg may open operational accounts and hold escrow balances. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed in any EEA Member State cannot lawfully provide payment services to a Luxembourg corporate without a Luxembourg/EU electronic money or payment institution authorisation, as per EU PSD2 and Luxembourg Law of 10 November 2009. For a food manufacturing entity requiring documentary credits and import/export settlement, reliance solely on a fintech EMI is not recommended; EMIs are viable only for technobridge sub-accounts and not for final clearing or regulatory deposit balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For subscription or shareholder loan funding from non-EU principals, standard SWIFT MT103 must be used with a purpose code S001 (Capital payments) or RA001 (Investment in equity), or the Luxembourg central bank statistical code counterpart. The remitting bank must clearly identify the final beneficiary as 'LU_SARL' and include the notary reference. The wire must be directed to the dedicated capital escrow account within a credit institution having its registered office in the EEA. Before any utilization, the SARL must file the notarial deed and updated articles to RCS; BCL and CSSF may require confirmation of the ultimate origin of funds, but no foreign exchange authorization is required in the Eurozone.For payments to the boba material supply chain, permissible clearing rails are: (1) standard Telegraphic Transfer/SWIFT MT103 (TT wire) for spot payment with EUR or USD; (2) SWIFT MT700 (Documentary Credit) for commercial Letters of Credit to Chinese tapioca starch suppliers; (3) MT202COV for outer funds settlement; (4) batch-cleared in TARGET2 if EUR-denominated. The exporter’s bank will require a commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and any quality certificates to align with the underlying ISIC 1030 manufacturing process. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24.94% combined effective headline rate for Luxembourg City (15% corporate income tax + 1.05% solidarity surcharge + 6.75% municipal business tax); base corporate income tax is 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% withholding tax on Luxembourg-source dividends; reduced to 0% for qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (at least 10% direct shareholding held for 12 months); treaty rates generally range from 0% to 15%, commonly 5% for direct shareholdings of at least 5-10%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Luxembourg TP documentation follows OECD guidelines; mandatory Master File and Local File are generally expected for taxpayers forming part of a group with consolidated annual net turnover of at least EUR 50 million and significant intra-group transactions, and Country-by-Country Report is mandatory where consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 750 million. Transactions below this threshold should nevertheless be supported by adequate contemporaneous TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in UAE/Cayman, but substance requirements are imposed through settled case law on abuse of law, circular LIR 56/1, EU ATAD, and the principal purpose test under treaties; for a manufacturing SARL, physical premises, qualified staff, strategic production decisions, management meetings in Luxembourg, and full-risk operating activity are essential to support economic substance and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment plus late-payment penalties; administrative penalty of up to 10% of tax due; criminal fines up to EUR 250,000 per offence per taxable period, and imprisonment for principals up to 5 years (Luxembourg General Tax Code, art. 413 et seq.).

Director personal liability: Directors who instigate or tolerate evasion are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax, interest, and fines; travel bans and investment disqualification orders may be imposed; custodial sentences up to 5 years apply for criminal fraud.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization and recapture of income; treaty benefits denied under principal purpose test; abuse-of-law penalty equal to 50% of the tax advantage (Luxembourg law of 15 January 2018); loss of participation exemption or IP regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the unpaid tax advantage if they signed declarations without genuine corporate activity; management bans and claw-back remedies apply.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: AML fines under the Luxembourg law of 12 November 2004 and FATF enforcement, up to EUR 1,250,000 for legal persons; additional sanctions for false RBE filings include fines of up to EUR 250,000 and publication of the sanction; frozen bank accounts and revocation of business licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for breach of AML reporting duties; can be criminally prosecuted with custodial sentences up to 5 years and disgorgement of laundering profits; travel bans and extradition procedures may apply.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Burden of proof shifts to taxpayer; tax administration may adjust taxable income to a deemed arm's-length price; administrative penalties up to 10% of the adjustment; failure to supply documentation in response to ACD request may trigger an additional fine up to EUR 2,500; in severe cases the adjustment is treated as a dividend distribution subject to withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's-length pricing or refused to cooperate after official request are jointly and severally liable for the assessed additional corporate tax, interest, and penalties; potential professional ban and criminal liability where TP misstatement amounts to tax fraud.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA (AEDT) for indirect tax and registration duties; Administration des contributions directes (ACD) for corporate income tax; Luxembourg Business Registers (LBR) for commercial registration and the statutory national identification number; e-portals: https://espace.etat.lu (AEDT/ACD e-filing), https://www.lbr.lu, https://guichet.public.lu.

E-government filing portal

The SARL is incorporated by notarial deed and the notary e-files the incorporation dossier with the Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). Upon RCS registration, the Luxembourg Business Registers issues the 11-digit national matricule which constitutes the corporate direct-tax identification number. VAT registration is a separate step: the appointed manager submits Form 619 via MyGuichet or the AEDT e-space, attaching the RCS extract dated no more than 3 months, associated articles of incorporation, identity documents of managers and ultimate beneficial owners, registered-office proof, and bank details. The AEDT issues the LU VAT number (format LU00000000) within approximately 2–3 weeks. A non-established foreign entity with no fixed establishment in Luxembourg registers under Article 58 of the Luxembourg VAT Law using the AEDT non-established taxable person form, annexing proof of foreign VAT registration and the foreign entity's constitutive documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: SARL is a capital company subject to Luxembourg corporate income tax, municipal business tax, and net wealth tax. Pass-through treatment is not available for an SARL. Territorial_Scope: resident companies are taxed on worldwide profits, with extensive exemptions for substantial participations and foreign permanent establishments under tax treaties; quasi-Territorial_Scope: the safe-harbor participation exemption under Articles 166 and 166bis of the Luxembourg Income Tax Law. Tax_Exempt_Zone: none – Luxembourg operates no general free-zone or offshore VAT exemption; selective statutory regimes (deduction at 80% for intellectual-property income, alternative investment fund regimes) are not territorial exemptions.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification is determined by legal form. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) pursuant to Article 60 of the Luxembourg VAT Law for closely linked persons; (ii) corporate fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) election for direct-tax income consolidation among 95%-owned Luxembourg subsidiaries; (iii) election to apply the Luxembourg intellectual-property box with an 80% net-income deduction subject to full documentation; (iv) option to apply the EU OSS/IOSS distance-selling mechanisms instead of local VAT registration; (v) hybrid-mismatch compliance elections per the Law of 25 July 2020 implementing ATAD (alignment of accounting period, treatment of hybrid instruments/entities, and related taxpayer statements).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

17%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: EUR 35,000 of annual turnover (VAT-exclusive) for established suppliers, with an annual declaration requirement; no de minimis threshold for non-established foreign suppliers – any taxable supply in Luxembourg triggers VAT registration; EU intra-Community distance-selling threshold for goods: EUR 10,000 (annual, pan-EU), above which the OSS regime or local LU VAT registration becomes mandatory; B2B intra-Community acquisitions are reverse-charged by the Luxembourg customer without a local supplier registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 54 of the Luxembourg VAT Law and Articles 146–147 of Directive 2006/112/EC: (i) export of tangible goods (including self-heating instant food containers) physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or on its behalf – 0% VAT with right to input VAT credit; (ii) intra-Community supply of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number, combined with EC Sales List filing and transport evidence – 0%; (iii) certain cross-border B2B services supplied to taxable persons or non-EU recipients under the place-of-supply rules – 0% or outside scope. The zero-rating is denied where the supplier cannot prove, from contemporaneous and consistent documents, that the goods left the EU and were received by the stated customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (PLDA ECS / Export Summary Declaration data from Luxembourg Customs giving the MRN – Movement Reference Number)
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from EU territory: Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, Air Waybill, or equivalent carrier certificate
  • Commercial invoice (with incoterms, goods description, quantity, and value) in the name of the exporter
  • Proof of delivery into the non-EU destination market: signed delivery note, customs entry declaration of the destination country, warehouse receipt, or customer confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing the repatriation of export proceeds (where available as supplementary proof)

No general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies to B2B/B2C VAT invoices as of the current date. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing (European Standard EN 16931 / PEPPOL) applies to public procurement. All VAT invoices must satisfy Luxembourg formal requirements (supplier/customer name and address, supplier and customer VAT numbers, invoice date, description, quantity, unit price, VAT rate, VAT amount, and reference to the applicable exemption). The taxpayer must retain SAF-T-LU (Standard Audit File for Tax) in XML format, and annual VAT returns, EC sales lists, and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically through the AEDT 'TVA e-filing' system with Luxtrust/LuxID authentication.

On any deficiency of export evidence, the AEDT reassesses the missed output VAT at 17% of the full invoice amount, plus late-payment interest at 0.6% per month, a filing-default penalty of 10% of the tax due (increasing up to 50% for willful concealment). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute Luxembourg tax fraud under Articles 395–403 of the General Tax Law, punishable by fines up to EUR 25,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; the assessment limitation period extends to 10 years in fraud cases, the manager/director becomes jointly and severally liable, and the AEDT may issue cross-border collection requests and exchange information under Directive 2010/24/EU and the DAC framework.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Luxembourg maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There are no exchange control regulations, currency allocation mechanisms, or approval screens for foreign direct investment with respect to this food manufacturing SARL, except EU sanctions and defensive screenings. Profit distributions to a non-resident parent may be made freely; Luxembourg imposes no withholding tax on qualifying dividend distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% shareholding) or, in limited cases, at the domestic 15% standard reduced to 0% on exemption. No restrictions exist on repatriation of capital contributions, shareholder loans, trade receivable proceeds, or dividend remittance from a Euro-denominated account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations, conversion mandates, or repatriation timeframes for Luxembourg exporters. Export receipts from the manufacture and sale of self-heating instant meals may be retained indefinitely in any currency. Where receivables are credited to a foreign currency account with a Luxembourg bank, the BCL balance-of-payments statistical schedule requires reporting of direct investment and trade transactions above regulatory thresholds; this does not impose a surrender duty.

Central bank supervision: Banque centrale du Luxembourg (BCL), acting within the European System of Central Banks, in coordination with the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF) for credit institution conduct, payment services, and AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low. EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) may offer rapid onboarding and multi-currency IBANs under PSD2, and their safeguarding arrangements are regulatorily compliant, but they are not licensed to provide credit, escrow-segregation in Luxembourg, commercial letter of credit, or FX forward capacity. These rails are acceptable only for minor trade payments and not for the capital escrow account or concentrated treasury flows. Luxembourg-licensed EMIs fall under CSSF supervision; foreign EMIs must rely on home-state passporting and cannot possess a Luxembourg principal-branch presence without prior licensing. AML/KYC harmonization makes document requirements substantially similar to regulated banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For euro-denominated injections, use SEPA Credit Transfer or TARGET2-affiliated correspondent banks with an MT103 only for non-EEA currencies. The SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 mapping must include the BIC of the receiving Luxembourg bank (e.g., BILLLULL for BIL or BCEELULL for BCEE), beneficiary IBAN in tag 59, and the purpose tag 70/PURP indicating ISO External Purpose Code 'EQUI' (equity capital contribution) supported by the BCL balance-of-payments statistical transaction code for direct investment in Luxembourg equity capital. Beneficiary banks will independently verify the notarial capital contribution deed, shareholder resolution and source of funds; totals above EUR 50,000 trigger automatic enhanced due diligence and potential BCL reporting under the balance-of-payments survey.Trade settlement for raw material imports and international distribution contracts should primarily use SWIFT MT103 (with MT202 COV cover payments) routed through the Luxembourg clearing system and the Eurosystem's TARGET2 layer for high-value euro payments. For documentary credits, use UCP600-based commercial letters of credit issued by the buyer's bank, with SWIFT MT700/MT707 flows to a confirming seller-side bank in Luxembourg, and MT760 standby letters of credit where product delivery guarantees are required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Automatic Master File, Local File and CbCR obligations arise only when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (LIR art. 164(2)-(3), law of 18 December 2015, ACD circular of 25 December 2016). Below this threshold, taxpayers remain under the general burden of proof and must hold contemporaneous arm's-length documentation per ACD circular LIR n°164/2 of 28 January 2011, producible within 30 days of an ACD demand.

Economic substance scope: Luxembourg operates no codified ESR regime of the zero-tax jurisdiction type; however, substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (LIR art. 6 b), the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and the participation exemption conditions of LIR art. 166. A compliant SARL must maintain a Luxembourg registered office, hold board meetings in Luxembourg, take management decisions locally, employ local staff and premises commensurate with its manufacturing/distribution functions, keep books and records in Luxembourg, and maintain a local bank account. Absent such substance, the ACD may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize payments as hidden profit distributions under LIR art. 56, and impose enhanced documentation demands.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Law of 23 March 2022 and LIR art. 396: back taxes plus 10% late-filing surcharge, 0.6% monthly late-payment interest, and criminal fines up to EUR 250,000; aggravated cases (use of shell entities, cross-border arrangements) are treated as criminal fraud with higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or approve fraudulent returns face imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax and civil restitution, and prohibition from holding management office in Luxembourg.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the PPT/GAAR; reclassification of receipts as constructive dividends subject to 15% WHT; forfeiture of the participation exemption; net wealth tax minimum raised to EUR 21,150 when the balance sheet exceeds EUR 20,000,000; inclusion in future EU Unshell Directive scope once enacted.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly operating an empty vehicle can be held personally liable for the resulting tax adjustments and interest; subject to fines, debarment from corporate office, and in cases of treaty abuse accompanied by fraud, criminal prosecution and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment to arm's-length results under LIR art. 56; reassessed tax plus 0.6% monthly interest and a 10% surcharge; the burden of proof shifts entirely to the taxpayer; specific penalties may be applied for non-production of mandated documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors approving deliberately mispriced intercompany transactions may be jointly liable for the additional tax and interest; where the mispricing is part of a tax evasion scheme, criminal liability follows with up to 5 years imprisonment and market bans.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Registration Violations

Statutory sanction: Law of 12 November 2004 on AML/CFT and RBE law of 13 January 2019: fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for serious breaches; RBE non-compliance fines up to EUR 125,000; asset freezing and seizure orders; in aggravated cases the entity's transactions are blocked and the commercial license revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors qualify as responsible parties and risk personal criminal sanctions: imprisonment from 8 days to 5 years and/or fines ranging from EUR 500 to EUR 1,250,000; travel bans, restitution orders, and permanent disqualification from management of Luxembourg commercial entities.

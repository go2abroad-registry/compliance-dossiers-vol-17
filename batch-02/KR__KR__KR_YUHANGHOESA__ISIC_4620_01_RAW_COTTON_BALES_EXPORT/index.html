Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registration Division of the District Court exercising jurisdiction over the registered office; for an office in Seoul, the Seoul Central District Court Registration Office; nationwide electronic filing through the Supreme Court Registration Information Service.

Governing statute

Commercial Act of the Republic of Korea (Act No. 1000, promulgated November 23, 1962, as amended), in particular Articles 287-2 through 287-74 addressing the Yuhan Hoesa (a private limited liability company), Article 288 requiring notarized Articles of Incorporation, and Article 331 governing registration of incorporation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the total capital stated in the notarized Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid by bank deposit before the incorporation registration is filed. The capital subscription moneys must be deposited into a blocked escrow account in the name of the company or promoters, and withdrawal is not permitted until incorporation is registered. Non-monetary contributions must be supported by a statutory asset valuation report. An illustrative initial capital of KRW 10,000,000 corresponds to approximately USD 37 in registration duty and educational surtax.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to office-based brokerage and export trading operations. If the company later leases a cotton bale warehouse, it must obtain a fire safety inspection certificate from the local fire station under the Act on Fire Prevention and Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Control of Fire-Fighting Systems, secure a structural load approval from the municipal building office for stacked cotton bales, and register the workplace with the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency, including work environment measurement for dust and fiber particulates under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), accessible through the Hometax national electronic tax portal at www.hometax.go.kr and Korean Customs UNI-PASS for export declarations

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that establishes or operates through a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must cause the Korean company to apply for business registration (Business Registration Number / Sa'eopja Deungrok) with the competent district tax office, or through Hometax using a tax representative where the foreign entity itself carries on a Korea-source VAT taxable business without a domestic place of business. The application must be made within 20 calendar days of commencing business, must include the constitutional documents, registered office evidence, foreign shareholder details, and identity documents of the tax representative where required, and results in a 10-digit Business Registration Number which serves as both the corporate taxpayer reference and VAT identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is classified under the Corporation Tax Act as a domestic corporation taxed on worldwide income; Korea does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election. Foreign tax credit and controlled foreign corporation rules apply at the shareholder level but do not alter the entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold applies; any person, including a non-resident entity making taxable supplies in Korea, is statutorily required to register for VAT upon commencing business. Simplified taxable persons with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 remain registered but may use simplified VAT calculation and reporting rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The zero rate applies to exported goods under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act where the Korean seller transfers ownership of the goods to a foreign purchaser and the goods are physically removed from Korea to a foreign destination under a valid Customs Act export declaration. The raw cotton bales must not be consumed, used, or supplied sub-divisionally within Korea prior to export, and the exporter must retain transport, customs, and settlement records evidencing the actual foreign delivery and foreign payment.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (Export Cargo Clearance Report / Suchool Shin-go Pil-jeung) filed through Korean Customs UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice signed by the Korean seller and the overseas buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carriage to a foreign destination
  • Packing list and certificate of origin if required by the destination customs administration
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate proving receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Letter of Credit or documentary collection documents evidencing the contractual export payment terms

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (HomeTax e-tax invoices) are required for all VAT taxable supplies; taxpayers must issue and transmit e-tax invoices through the National Tax Service HomeTax API or EDI system within the statutory issuance period. Korea does not operate a continuous real-time transaction clearance or centralized digital stamping platform; anti-fraud control is achieved through mandatory e-tax invoice matching, periodic VAT return validation, and cash receipt issuance for B2C supplies.

Export zero-rating is subject to NTS verification of the export declaration, foreign exchange receipts, transport evidence, and payment reconciliation. Any false, unsubstantiated, or fictitious export zero-rating is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply and assessed to 10% VAT, plus additional tax for under-reporting, wrongful refund clawback, late-payment interest, and negligence or fraud penalties under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including corporate fines and criminal liability where deliberate evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current-account operations, including trade in goods, services, and ordinary remittances; no prior approval is required for most current-account receipts and payments. Capital-account flows, however, are governed by a negative-list system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and require notification, reporting, or authorisation through an authorised foreign exchange bank or the Bank of Korea. Non-resident shareholders may inject equity and receive dividends, but profit remittances must be supported by audited or reviewed financial statements, a corporate dividend resolution, a tax withholding payment certificate, and, if applicable, a foreign-exchange purchase certificate issued by the remitting bank. For export activities, proceeds must be repatriated to Korea within the statutory period, but may be retained in foreign currency accounts; there is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into KRW. Overall, Korea is assessed as an open yet administratively controlled jurisdiction, where banking counterparties must verify the transactional purpose and maintain a full paper trail for every cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio to KRW is required; Korean resident exporters may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency deposit accounts held with an authorised domestic foreign exchange bank. However, the full amount of export proceeds must be received or repatriated into a Korean bank account within 5 months from the export declaration date. Any extension beyond 5 months requires the prior approval of the authorised foreign exchange bank, and all receipts must be reported to the BOK Foreign Exchange Information System through the collecting bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting with the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF) as the external foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex are not licensed under the Korean Banking Act or the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to act as foreign exchange banks in South Korea. They cannot provide KRW settlement accounts that are recognised by the BOK-Wire+ clearing system, and they cannot execute statutory FX transaction reporting with the Bank of Korea. A Korean LLC such as Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its principal treasury and foreign exchange accounts with a domestic Tier-1 authorised foreign exchange bank. EMIs may only function as auxiliary collect/payment tools outside Korea and cannot replace the local banking licence requirement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required if the Korean LLC has annual revenue of at least KRW 50 billion and international related-party transaction volume of at least KRW 5 billion. Master File is required if annual revenue is at least KRW 100 billion and international related-party transactions are at least KRW 50 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting applies when the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion. A raw cotton export brokerage LLC below these thresholds must still maintain contemporaneous TP documentation sufficient to demonstrate the arm's-length nature of each intercompany commission or financing arrangement.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation filing, but substance is decisive. The NTS applies the statutory substance-over-form principle and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to disregard entities lacking real offices, employees, contracts, books, bank accounts, and decision-making authority in Korea. For treaty benefits, Korea applies the BEPS Action 6 limitation-on-benefits and principal purpose analysis; the LLC must demonstrate its Core Income-Generating Activities for raw cotton export brokerage are genuinely performed in Korea, not by a foreign affiliate in a low-tax conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion through fraudulent export documentation

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3: imprisonment up to two years or a fine up to twenty times the evaded tax; Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-2 through 47-5 impose an additional 20% deficiency penalty plus daily late-payment interest; aggravated tax fraud thresholds may lead to imprisonment exceeding five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fraudulent invoices or approved the sham contracts are jointly and severally liable under Korean tort and corporate law; under National Tax Collection Act Article 12 they may be designated secondary taxpayers and held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax when corporate assets are insufficient; the NTS may issue a departure restriction travel ban against delinquent directors and criminal conviction disqualifies them from serving as company officers.
Failure to withhold and remit outbound dividend, interest, or royalty withholding tax

Statutory sanction: Corporate Income Tax Act Article 98 and Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-4 make the withholding agent liable for the unwithheld amount plus an additional 10% penalty; Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 4 criminalizes intentional non-withholding, carrying imprisonment up to two years or a fine up to twenty times the withheld tax.

Director personal liability: The director and finance officer who execute the payment instruction are personally liable as withholding agents for the unremitted tax, with joint and several exposure to NTS enforcement; intentional non-withholding constitutes a direct criminal offense triggering personal arrest, asset seizure, departure restriction, and possible imprisonment of the responsible directors.
Shell company and treaty shopping lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: The NTS may disregard the corporate entity under the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 2 of the International Tax Adjustment Act; the result is re-characterization of all income to the real controlling shareholders with an additional 20% penalty on understated tax, interest, and denial of tax treaty benefits under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Korean courts pierce the corporate veil where the company is an instrument for avoiding legal obligations; director and controlling shareholder assets are reached through Commercial Act representative-director liability and secondary taxpayer collection. Criminal prosecution for deliberate shell-company tax evasion may impose imprisonment, fines up to twenty times the avoided tax, travel bans, and disqualification of the directors from holding any future corporate management position.

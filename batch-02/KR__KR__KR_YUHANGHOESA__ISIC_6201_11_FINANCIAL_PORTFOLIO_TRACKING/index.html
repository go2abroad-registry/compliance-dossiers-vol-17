Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online portal: HomeTax (http://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax agent (tax manager) resident in South Korea and file a Business Registration Application with the local district tax office having jurisdiction over that tax agent's place of business, either in person or through the HomeTax electronic portal. Required attachments include an apostilled or notarized original of the foreign business registration certificate, articles of incorporation or equivalent constitutive documents, a resolution appointing the local tax agent, a power of attorney, and a certified copy of the tax agent's identification. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the corporate tax reference and the VAT registration number, normally within 3 to 5 business days. There is no minimum share capital requirement for this registration process. The tax agent must maintain the registration and discharge all ongoing VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax agent (tax manager) resident in South Korea and file a Business Registration Application with the local district tax office having jurisdiction over that tax agent's place of business, either in person or through the HomeTax electronic portal. Required attachments include an apostilled or notarized original of the foreign business registration certificate, articles of incorporation or equivalent constitutive documents, a resolution appointing the local tax agent, a power of attorney, and a certified copy of the tax agent's identification. The NTS will issue a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the corporate tax reference and the VAT registration number, normally within 3 to 5 business days. There is no minimum share capital requirement for this registration process. The tax agent must maintain the registration and discharge all ongoing VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Republic of Korea does not operate a check-the-box regime in the US sense. A Yuhan Hoesa formed under the Commercial Act is treated as a domestic corporation for Korean tax purposes, subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no elective pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold. All business operators, whether resident or non-resident, making taxable supplies in South Korea are required to register for VAT with the National Tax Service. For small business operators with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million, the Simplified Taxpayer regime applies (VAT effectively calculated as 2% of turnover), but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% export zero-rating under Article 24 of the Value-Added Tax Act, the taxpayer must substantiate: (i) for goods, physical exportation from South Korea evidenced by a valid Korean Customs Service export declaration; or (ii) for services, provision to a non-resident client for use or consumption outside South Korea, with the consideration received in freely convertible foreign currency and repatriated into South Korea through an authorized foreign exchange bank. For the relevant business scope (ISIC 6201, cloud-based personal wealth analytics and investment tracking software), qualifying exports include cross-border software-as-a-service subscriptions and digital content services delivered to non-resident users, provided the services are effectively used outside South Korea and the requisite documentary evidence is retained. Domestic B2B digital supplies remain standard-rated at 10%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) issued by the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying contract or service order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Certificate of Foreign Exchange Settlement issued by a licensed foreign exchange bank
  • Digital service delivery records, including user location logs and download or access receipts for electronically supplied services

Electronic Tax Invoice (e-Tax Invoice) transmission via HomeTax is mandatory. All registered taxpayers must issue and transmit an electronic tax invoice through HomeTax no later than the day following the supply date. The system supports NTS-specified API and XML interoperability, but there is no real-time pre-clearance fiscal stamping mechanism as found in certain Latin American jurisdictions. Fraud controls rely on mandatory e-invoice reconciliation, data matching, and the ability of the NTS to demand digital yet detailed supply records.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to periodic NTS desk and field audits. If the required supporting documentation, including the export declaration, transport evidence, and foreign exchange settlement certificate within the 180-day repatriation deadline, is missing, incomplete, or false, the NTS will reverse the zero-rating application and assess VAT at 10% of the supply value, plus additional tax penalties of at least 50% of the amount evaded for negligent non-compliance and up to 100% for deliberate fraud. Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, responsible executives may face criminal liability with imprisonment up to five years or fines up to KRW 100 million. Retrospective clawback periods generally extend to 10 years for fraudulent or willful non-disclosure and 5 years for ordinary unreported under-assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a highly open current account and a substantially liberalized capital account, with the Korean won generally convertible for trade, service payments, and legitimate profit remittance through authorized foreign exchange banks. Current account settlements for software subscriptions, app-store receivables, and cross-border service fees do not require a prior license, but must be processed through a domestic foreign exchange bank and reported under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injection into a Yuhan Hoesa and the subsequent repatriation of dividends or liquidation proceeds, are classified as capital transactions and require a BOK/MOEF foreign exchange transaction report submitted by the receiving bank. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted, but banks require proof of corporate tax withholding, audited financial statements, the certificate of business registration, and documentary evidence that the underlying investment capital was lawfully brought into South Korea. Practical constraints include mandatory beneficial ownership disclosure, strict source-of-funds verification for foreign investors, and the need for the remitter to identify a valid foreign exchange purpose code; failure to present complete documentation can delay inward remittance and capital verification. Although no general surrender requirement exists, foreign exchange banks must still ensure that all capital inflows are traced to a stated legitimate economic purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, Korean exporters, including software and fintech entities, must collect export proceeds through a domestic foreign exchange bank within 6 months from the date of export, unless an extension is approved by the designated bank or by the BOK/MOEF. There is no mandatory conversion to Korean won and no statutory surrender ratio to the central bank; exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency receipts in eligible foreign currency deposit accounts. However, all export proceeds must pass through an authorized foreign exchange bank for trade reporting and cross-border reconciliation, with the underlying export settlement method and payment details recorded in the Foreign Exchange Information System.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), operating under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail.

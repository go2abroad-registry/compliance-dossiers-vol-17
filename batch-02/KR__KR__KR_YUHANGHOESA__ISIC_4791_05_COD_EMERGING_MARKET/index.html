Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Republic of Korea National Tax Service (NTS); electronic portal HomeTax at https://www.hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service single-window clearance platform UNI-PASS at https://unipass.customs.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident Yuhan Hoesa entity must appoint a domestic tax representative under Article 84 of the Framework Act on National Taxes before it can be registered for VAT. Through HomeTax, the tax representative must complete the non-resident business registration (Business Registration Number, BRN) for the foreign entity and submit: (1) apostilled/certified certificate of incorporation and jurisdiction status; (2) foreign registered address and business license evidence; (3) power of attorney naming the Korean tax representative; (4) director identification and ownership certificates of the foreign entity; (5) expected Korean reportable supply schedule for mail-order e-commerce/export business. The NTS normally issues the BRN within 3-5 business days via the portal. Once registered, the BRN is used for VAT returns, e-tax invoice issuance, and the import/export ID required for UNI-PASS clearance. If the entity derives Korean-source corporate income, it must also file non-resident corporation tax returns with the same NTS branch; no separate tax identification code is needed because the BRN serves as the integrated tax identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold for foreign non-resident e-commerce suppliers; all foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT from the first supply. For resident small businesses, the simplified taxation threshold is KRW 80 million of annual taxable supplies, at which point an optional or mandatory simplified return regime and lower collection burden applies, but it does not remove the obligation to be VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 11 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods receive the 0% VAT rate if the taxable person proves that the goods have been exported to a foreign place via a Korean customs office, the goods leave Korea with consideration received from a foreign entity and title/risk passes at the free-on-board or other export incoterms point. Respective goods sold through e-commerce channels including cash-on-delivery mail-order to non-resident buyers qualify when the seller retains proof of the export declaration, foreign buyer's identity and delivery to an overseas terminal. Qualifying services supplied and actually used or consumed outside Korea (including offshore games/digital content provided to foreign account holders) may also be zero-rated as export services when the recipient is not a Korean tax payer and the contract and payment evidence are retained. The zero-rated seller also may claim refund of related input VAT in the same periodic VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration certificate accepted by Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic clearance system; the Korean lawful equivalent of an export Bill of Entry)
  • Original Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill (air freight), or courier consignment note with house/grand number indicating the country of destination and confirming physical export from Korea
  • Commercial invoice packet comprising contract or order, packing list, weight/packaging certificate, incoterms certificate and proof that place of delivery is outside Korea
  • Foreign exchange collection certificate (receipt evidence) issued by a Korean foreign exchange banking institution showing that export proceeds were credited and that settlement was in freely convertible currency; where payment is collected through an Airwallex or logistics/remittance partner, the regulated payment service provider must issue an equivalent foreign exchange remittance statement and the underlying customer remittance records must be available on request
  • Logistics proof of electronic e-commerce shipment showing the final delivered address, proof of delivery signature or tracking scanning record for wholesale or retail export parcels

All VAT-registered business suppliers must issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax (e-Tax Invoice) for B2B supplies and report them to the NTS using the HomeTax EDI, web API or batch submission functions. There is no real-time fiscal printer clearance and no anti-fraud digital stamping requirement for exported or cross-border mail-order supplies. The Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system requires an electronic advance clearance declaration for every export and parcel import shipment; the exporter/importer must supply the BRN or customs ID, customs value, commodity description, incoterms, and transport document reference before any goods move. Foreign sellers selling by mail-order or cash-on-delivery into Korea are subject to customs duty/VAT at import clearance, and the NTS uses data sharing with customs, payment intermediaries and logistics providers to audit underdeclared supplies.

The NTS will reject zero-rating and re-assess the export supply at 10% VAT if any of the above statutory documents are missing, inconsistent, or non-contemporaneous; it will also order a refund clawback of any previously credited input VAT or refunded export VAT with statutory interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Civil surcharges for negligence/unfaithful returns, undelivered tax invoices and late payment apply on the disallowed amounts under the Framework Act on National Taxes. An intentionally false export declaration, forged bill of lading, or fabricated FX remittance certificate may constitute tax invoice or customs fraud under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act and the Customs Act, exposing the directors and tax representative to criminal liability, imprisonment, and fines, in addition to a seven-year look-back period for fraudulent evasion. The NTS also applies enhanced scrutiny to cash-on-delivery e-commerce exporters and may cross-check payment service provider reports with customs export data before granting any refund.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains an essentially open current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Act on Foreign Exchange Transactions. Payments for trade, services, royalties, dividends, liquidation proceeds, and other current transactions may generally be made freely through a designated foreign exchange bank, subject to documentary substantiation and purpose-code reporting. The capital account is also liberalized, but not unconditional: inbound equity investment by foreign owners must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, usually before or at the time of acquisition of shares, and the capital funds must enter through a designated domestic banking channel. Any profit remittance from the Yuhan Hoesa will require a valid board resolution, audited or reviewed financial statements, confirmation of unrestricted distributable profits under the Korean Commercial Act, corporate tax clearance documentation, and the bank's verification of the remittance purpose code. In practice, the main hurdles are not an absolute ceiling on conversion but rather the administrative requirements of proving beneficial ownership, source of funds, and underlying real economic activity. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory percentage surrender; they may be retained in a resident foreign-currency account or converted to Korean won, but they must be received and reported through a foreign exchange bank within the statutory trade-settlement timeline. For an e-commerce and digital-content treasury model, the important constraint is maintaining full auditability of every cross-border receipt and payment so that the foreign exchange bank can complete the required trade/investment reporting and satisfy the Financial Services Commission's anti-money-laundering expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export claims must be collected within 90 days from the export declaration or shipment date unless the designated foreign exchange bank grants an extension for legitimate commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio presently applied to export receipts; the exporter may deposit the foreign currency into a compliant resident foreign-currency account at a domestic bank or sell it for Korean won. The statutory duties are full receipt of the proceeds, prompt settlement reporting through the foreign exchange bank, and alignment with the customs export evidence. For the KR_YUHANGHOESA cash-on-delivery e-commerce operation, the logistics and remittance partners collecting KRW from end customers must deposit the collected funds into the entity's official Korean bank account, and the associated export/digital-content settlement must be traceable through the foreign exchange bank's trade-surveillance framework.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the delegated network of designated foreign exchange banks is the central authority for South Korean foreign-exchange control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Airwallex and equivalent offshore financial technology EMI platforms are not licensed as Korean banks under the Banking Act and cannot legally replace a resident KRW bank account for the entity's local cash-on-delivery settlement flows. Korean logistics and courier partners will almost universally require settlement into a local bank account in the corporate name. A global EMI may be used only as a secondary algorithmic liquidity overlay for cross-border vendor payouts and foreign-currency balances, provided the EMI is not holding resident KRW settlement funds and the Korean bank remains the primary clearing and verification rail. For statutory corporate banking, capital verification, foreign-exchange reporting, tax filing support, and AML transparency, the treasury structure must be anchored in a Tier-1 domestic Korean commercial bank.

For statutory corporate banking, capital verification, foreign-exchange reporting, tax filing support, and AML transparency, the treasury structure must be anchored in a Tier-1 domestic Korean commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is required for a Korean entity when aggregate international related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year. The Master File must be prepared where the multinational group has worldwide consolidated revenue above KRW 100 billion and related-party cross-border transactions above KRW 10 billion. Detailed thresholds and exemptions are contained in the International Tax Adjustment Act and its Enforcement Decree.

Economic substance scope: Korean tax law applies the substance-over-form principle and general anti-abuse rules. The Korean LLC must maintain real offices, employees, payroll, local bank accounts, continuous operational activity, and substantive decision-making functions in Korea to avoid recategorization as a tax sham or transparent entity. Korea does not enact GCC-style economic substance regulations, but the economic substance doctrine operates through Controlled Foreign Company rules, beneficial ownership tests under tax treaties, and criminal commercial misrepresentation rules for shell companies; every Korean entity must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

E-commerce Cash Suppression / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3: imprisonment of up to 2 years or a fine of up to twice the amount of tax evaded for ordinary evasion; aggravated evasion exceeding KRW 500 million carries imprisonment of up to 5 years. The Framework Act on National Taxes additionally imposes a 40% fraudulent under-reporting penalty.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for alter-ego or sham entities: directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and withheld tax. The National Tax Collection Act permits travel bans and the high-value delinquent taxpayer blacklist for unpaid national taxes above the statutory threshold, while criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 5 years and personal asset seizure.
Fraudulent Tax Treaty Relief / Withholding Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny DTA benefits, impose a 40% unlawful-method under-reporting penalty plus a withholding-agent penalty of up to 10% of the unwithheld tax, and apply the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for intentional false claims, with fines up to twice the avoided withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in certifying false ownership or Tax Residency Certificates or directing withholding misrepresentation can be personally assessed for the unwithheld tax with interest and surcharges. They are exposed to travel bans, delinquency blacklisting, civil restitution in shareholder and tax claims, and imprisonment for fraud where the scheme is proven.
Sham Substance / Shell Company Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the International Tax Adjustment Act, the tax authority may recharacterize transactions, disregard the entity, recapture excessive deductions, and impose a 40% negligent or fraudulent adjustment penalty. Such cases are routinely referred for criminal tax proceedings.

Director personal liability: Courts may disregard the corporate personality and hold shareholder-directors personally liable for all unpaid Korean taxes, interest, and penalties. The delinquency list and travel-ban sanctions apply until full settlement, and directors face up to 5 years imprisonment for obstruction of tax collection or fraudulent tax avoidance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose any statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa and there is no blocked escrow account requirement. The members may fix the initial capital freely in the Articles of Incorporation, but the subscribed capital must be paid in full before the incorporation registration application is filed, and a bank-issued payment certificate must be submitted to the registry. If foreign investment is involved, the remitted capital must be received through a designated foreign-exchange bank and reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr); export customs clearances administered by Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa, the tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN), which doubles as the Corporate TIN and VAT ID. The entity must complete Commercial Act incorporation before tax filing. The responsible domestic representative submits a Business Registration Application to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business, via HomeTax or in person. Documents include company certificate or registry extracts, certificate of incorporation and articles of association of any foreign parent (apostilled or notarized with certified Korean translation), board resolution appointing the domestic representative or manager, power of attorney, passport or national ID of the representative, and evidence of the domestic business place such as a lease agreement or office registration. For a non-resident foreign company without a Korean establishment, a domestic tax representative is appointed and the same BRN procedure is completed before the first taxable supply. NTS issues the Certificate of Business Registration; the 10-digit BRN is then activated for electronic tax invoices through HomeTax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Yuhan Hoesa is a separate domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act, taxed on worldwide income at entity level; pass-through or transparent treatment is not available.

Available election forms

None equivalent to a US check-the-box election. Korean law does not permit a business entity to elect pass-through taxation. A statutory group consolidation election is available for eligible 100% owned domestic subsidiaries under the Corporate Tax Act; OECD/hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under the Act on International Tax Coordination apply to cross-border hybrid instruments and cannot be overcome by an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None in KRW: any person who starts a business or makes taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT before business commencement, regardless of turnover. Simplified taxpayer status applies only to low-revenue individual entrepreneurs and does not provide a registration threshold for a corporate Yuhan Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% VAT export rate under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act when they are physically exported from the Republic of Korea under a final export customs declaration, and the Korean supplier is the exporter in the customs record. In the off-price apparel liquidation model, the Korean Yuhan Hoesa must have a genuine sale to an offshore buyer, title and risk transfer outside Korea, and a valid export clearance. Qualifying offshore services, not relevant to this apparel scope, are zero-rated if supplied to a nonresident and paid in convertible foreign currency.

  • Export Declaration (final export customs clearance statement) accepted and transmitted in the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export and showing the Korean Yuhan Hoesa as shipper or consignor and the offshore buyer as consignee or notify party
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, or equivalent trade documentation evidencing the offshore buyer and sale price
  • Foreign exchange collection evidence: Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation, remittance receipt, or foreign exchange bank certificate from a Korean foreign exchange bank (Korean equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate or FIRC)

All VAT-registered suppliers must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS e-Tax Invoice system (HomeTax web, EDI, or API) and transmit invoice details to NTS by the day following issuance. There is no pre-issue government clearance or physical tax stamp; the NTS relies on near real-time post-issuance reporting, automated cross-matching against customs export declarations, and continuous anti-fraud validation processing.

If an exporter cannot substantiate actual export, a genuine offshore buyer, or repatriation of the export proceeds, the NTS will disallow the zero rating, assess VAT at 10% on the supply value, and claw back any refund already paid. The unfaithful return penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes is generally 10% of the unpaid tax, rising to 40% for dishonest or fraudulent returns, plus late-payment interest. Fraudulent export invoices or refund schemes are referred under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act to criminal prosecution. The NTS and KCS retrospectively re-examine prior returns; the national tax collection period may extend to 10 years in cases of fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current account convertibility regime under Article 3 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but residual controls remain for capital account flows and large settlements. Residents and non-resident entities may freely convert KRW for trade and operating expenses, subject to documentary submission to a domestic foreign exchange bank. Export proceeds in foreign currency must be collected through authorized banks; conversion is automatic with no mandatory surrender ratio beyond reporting threshold, yet the receipt must be reported within the statutory deadline. Repatriation of capital, dividends, and liquidation proceeds from a foreign-invested company is generally permitted on a prior-declaration basis, provided the investment was registered with the MOEF under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The Korean won is not fully freely convertible outside Korea; offshore KRW settlement is restricted, and non-resident banks may access KRW only through the FX interbank system. For a wholesale apparel liquidation trading entity, current account convertibility is straightforward, but capital account operations such as capital increase or loan extension require additional declarations to the BOK-MOEF reporting system. The practical hurdle is not convertibility denial but strict evidentiary documentation and timeline compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

South Korea abolished mandatory surrender of export proceeds; however, every export contract exceeding USD 50,000 must be reported to a designated foreign exchange bank within the relevant transaction period, and export receipts must be received into a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days from the date of export, with extensions available for trade credit periods not exceeding 180 days.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, with delegated foreign exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act enforced by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI solutions such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currenxie are not recognized as licensed deposit-taking institutions in South Korea. The Korean banking law reserves current account deposit-taking and KRW FX settlement to licensed banks and specialized mutual institutions. EMIs may only provide non-account payment initiation or remittance facilitation if partnered with a domestic tier-1 bank, and even then, they cannot open KRW-denominated settlement accounts for an onshore entity directly. For a Korean LLC engaged in apparel off-price wholesale, relying on an EMI would create non-compliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act because export collections must be routed through a Korean foreign exchange bank or its directly corresponding overseas branch. Feasibility is therefore low as a standalone banking rail; EMIs are viable solely as ancillary payment trigger tools after primary treasury accounts are established. Legal constraints require the underlying Korean entity to maintain at least one domestic bank account for statutory reporting and tax verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment from a non-resident shareholder into the Korean LLC must be filed with the MOEF under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, then routed through an internationally authorized bank using wire protocol SWIFT MT103 with standard purpose of claim code F and Korea-required FDI narrative. The bank will issue a certificate of capital importation before the amount is credited to a temporary capital escrow account; after the Capital Verification Report is issued by the authorized bank, the funds can be transferred to the operating account. If the share capital is contributed as a bank loan, the borrower must file a loan agreement with the BOK/MOEF system within 30 days and secure an inward loan registration certificate. All payments must be made in convertible foreign currency; KRW remittances from an overseas-incorporated account are only accepted for investment if the sender holds a MOEF-issued KRW settlement permission. The Korean repatriated treasury account must be classified under foreign exchange purpose code 3011 for equity capital and 3021 for long-term loans.Outbound trade settlements for buying off-price apparel from foreign suppliers should use SWIFT MT103 direct wire for amounts below USD 100,000, with purpose of remittance code A100-FX for trade payments and supporting import declarations submitted via the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS. Larger settlements should follow documentary collection DP/DA through the trading bank or commercial letters of credit where the Korean importer opens an irrevocable LC, in which case the full LC text must be forwarded to the overseas advising bank and the bill of lading must be endorsed to order. The settlement window should be synchronized with the BOK/NBVS statement cycle to avoid late declaration penalties. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies, etc., a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) is subject to mandatory independent CPA audit if its total assets at the end of the relevant fiscal year equal or exceed KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 9.2 million at 1,300 KRW/USD). No purely turnover-based or headcount-based audit trigger exists under Korean law for an ordinary private limited liability company; the balance-sheet asset test is the primary quantitative threshold. Additional mandatory audit is required for listed stock companies, public-interest entities designated by the Financial Services Commission, and certain large business group affiliates regardless of legal form. A small off-price wholesale apparel LLC with total assets below KRW 12 billion is not statutorily required to obtain an external audit, but a certified audit may be required by lenders, investors, or contractual commitments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate for taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surcharge yielding aggregate 26.4% effective top marginal burden

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% national source tax on gross dividends plus 2% local income tax surcharge, aggregate 22% for non-resident shareholders without treaty coverage

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 10 billion in the taxable year simultaneously require submission of Master File and Local File with the annual corporate return; an International Transaction Report (annex to the CIT return) is mandatory for aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 500 million

Economic substance scope: South Korea does not have the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes and OECD BEPS directives apply. Korean tax authorities deny treaty benefits where an off-price liquidator is established as a conduit without real resale, inventory custody, pricing, order, or staff function; this is reinforced by the Multilateral Instrument’s Principal Purpose Test incorporated into Korea’s DTAs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unreported Off-Price Sales / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent underreporting penalty of 40% of the evaded corporate tax, dishonesty penalty of 20%, daily late payment interest of 0.025%, plus collection seizure and freezing of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Under the Punishment Act for Tax Offenses, Art. 3, responsible directors face imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax. Civil personal liability attaches where directors structured the concealment, and the NTS may obtain travel bans and asset preservation orders.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: Korean tax office re-determines the arm's-length margin, disallows the intercompany invoices, imposes additional corporate tax up to 24%, adds a 20% negligence penalty, and asserts foreign withholding tax on imputed dividends or branch remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish an economic-substance-free structure are jointly liable for the assessed deficiencies, may be subject to investigation for violation of the International Tax Adjustment Act, and face imprisonment up to 3 years where deliberate false transfer pricing documentation is used to conceal tax avoidance.
AML Offenses in Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to report or providing false information triggers imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to KRW 500 million; the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit can order account seizure, criminal confiscation of proceeds, and permanent revocation of banking licenses for collaborating institutions.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers who order or permit the circumvention of the AML reporting mandate are personally criminally liable, face exit controls, and are subject to civil forfeiture of all illicit enrichment derived from the unreported remittance flows.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Korean withholding agent is held jointly liable for the unpaid WHT, a 10% negligence penalty, daily 0.025% overdue interest, and denial of the treaty rate if the principal purpose test is violated. The Korean payor entity is also liable for the grossed-up reimbursement amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false withholding certificates or who misuse a DTA while knowing the foreign recipient lacks economic substance incur personal civil restitution to the tax office; under the Punishment Act for Tax Offenses, they may be sentenced to imprisonment up to 3 years, and a travel ban is automatically applied during prosecution.

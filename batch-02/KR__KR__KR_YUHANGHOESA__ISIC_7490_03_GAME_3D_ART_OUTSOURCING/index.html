Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The subscribers must fully pay the subscribed capital before filing the incorporation registration. For cash contributions, the full amount must be deposited into a corporate bank account opened in the name of the proposed company in the Republic of Korea, and the deposit receipt certificate must be attached to the registration application. There is no legal requirement for a blocked escrow account, but the contribution must be actually and irrevocably made before the court registry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS), administered through the HomeTax electronic tax administration portal (hometax.go.kr), with jurisdiction over corporate income tax, VAT registration, electronic tax invoices, domestic source income, and withholding tax; Korea Customs Service cooperates for export declaration data.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean domiciled Yuhan Hoesa LLC, the representative files the statutory business registration application under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the VAT Act with the competent district tax office or via the HomeTax online portal within 20 days from business commencement. The application requires the articles of incorporation, proof of registered office address, identity and appointment of the representative, and certification of the foreign shareholder structure. The NTS then issues the 10-digit business registration number, which is the unified Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number for the entity. For a foreign non-resident entity owning the Korean LLC without a Korean fixed place of business, no separate Korean TIN is required solely for such ownership, but if the foreign entity itself realizes Korean-sourced income or makes taxable supplies, it must register separately on HomeTax as a foreign corporation using its foreign corporate certificate, obtain its own foreign corporation registration number, and apply for VAT registration if it is a taxable supplier. All registrations and filings require the representative's or foreign entity's digital certificate or I-PIN for HomeTax authentication.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover registration threshold: every person supplying taxable goods or services in the course of a Korean business must register for VAT, including newly incorporated corporations, within 20 days of business commencement. A simplified taxable person regime is available only to certain small businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million, but this is a computational status rather than an exemption from VAT registration; corporations are generally excluded from simplified treatment and are normal taxable persons regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export is granted under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act. It applies to goods exported from Korea and to services supplied to non-residents where the services are used or exploited outside Korea, including international transport and other pure offshore services. For the game 3D art outsourcing studio, the supply of commissioned artwork, character sculpting, and rigging services to a non-Korean game developer or publisher qualifies for 0% VAT when the customer's business establishment is outside Korea and the digital asset is contractually delivered for use outside Korea. The exported service consideration must be demonstrably settled in foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank, with wire transfer or PayPal proceeds evidenced in the related receiving account, and all contract records, delivery manifests, and project files must prove the offshore recipient. The zero rating is claimed on the relevant VAT return and is corroborated by the supporting documentation.

  • Korean Customs Service export declaration certificate or export clearance certificate for any physically exported media, samples, or documentation; digital-only service deliveries are evidenced through the service contract and digital delivery logs
  • Commercial invoice and signed international service contract identifying the foreign buyer, scope of 3D art and rigging services, and consideration in a foreign currency
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international transport document for physical shipments; for electronic deliverables, a dated file transfer manifest, cloud delivery receipt, or email log showing transmission to the offshore client
  • Foreign exchange settlement certificate or foreign currency purchase certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, or the bank remittance advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Buyer's business registration or tax identification certificate, or equivalent identification of the non-resident customer, together with a project completion report and IP usage confirmation indicating offshore exploitation

Korean VAT law mandates electronic tax invoice issuance and transmission for business-to-business supplies under the VAT Act. The e-tax invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS through the HomeTax system or an authorized e-invoice service provider and includes the business registration numbers, date of supply, tax base, VAT amount, and a unique electronic serial number. South Korea does not require real-time national fiscal clearance stamping or API approval before invoice issuance; rather, e-tax invoice data must be reported to the NTS by the statutory deadline, generally the 15th day of the month following issuance, after which NTS automated data matching compares seller and buyer declarations. Failure to issue or transmit an e-tax invoice, or issuing without a valid registration number, subjects the supplier to penalty surcharges and may deny the buyer the corresponding input VAT deduction.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service audit export zero-rating by cross-checking export declaration data, e-tax invoices, bank foreign exchange settlement records, and the service contracts retained by the taxpayer. If the 0% rate has been applied but the required export documentation is absent, the foreign exchange receipt is not evidenced, or the exported service is later shown to have been consumed in Korea, the NTS retrospectively assesses the 10% VAT that should have been declared on the supply, together with late-payment interest and statutory penalty surcharges. False documents or intentional mischaracterization of a domestic supply as an export for VAT evasion are additionally subject to penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and can lead to criminal liability, forfeiture, and disallowance of the related input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a free-floating exchange rate regime, with the Korean won (KRW) fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in services, royalties, salaries, and dividends. Capital account transactions are also substantially liberalized but remain subject to a post-transaction reporting framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank; exporters are required to channel foreign currency receipts through designated foreign exchange banks and to report any unusually large or delayed settlements. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders are generally permitted without prior approval, provided that the underlying foreign direct investment was properly registered with the Korean government and the source of the funds is verified against audited financial statements. For a Korean LLC engaged in game 3D art outsourcing, the practical convertibility risk is not legal prohibition but documentation friction: banks will require the underlying service contract, commercial invoices, and proof of service delivery to support each outward remittance. The Bank of Korea does not impose restrictions on the amount of repatriation for legitimate service fees, but banks aggregate transaction data and automatically report any pattern that appears inconsistent with the declared business model. The current account convertibility is effectively full, while capital account transactions such as inbound equity injections or loans require the issuance of a certificate of foreign exchange transaction (외국환거래증빙) for the bank's records. For inbound capital, the foreign investor must obtain a foreign investment registration certificate from a designated domestic bank, and the funds must be wired from an overseas account with a clearly identified investor name. Surrender requirements are minimal, but the Export Proceeds Collection System imposes a collection period of six months from the date of export declaration, during which the proceeds must be received into the domestic account of the enterprise. The system also mandates the closure of the transaction if payment is not received within that window, unless a written extension is approved by the local foreign exchange bank. Overall, conversion for trade and service flows is unrestricted, but every cross-border movement must be traceable through a licensed Korean banking institution, with supporting documents retained for at least five years.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Export Proceeds Collection System, Korean exporters must repatriate foreign currency export proceeds into a domestic foreign exchange bank account within six months from the date of the export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a designated account or convert it at the prevailing market rate, but the receipt must be recorded with the bank and any late collection or shortfall must be justified with a written explanation and supporting trade documents.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF), which oversees foreign exchange policy under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. While global fintech EMIs such as Wise and Revolut Business offer multi-currency accounts, they do not hold a Korean e-money license and are not authorized to provide foreign exchange services to Korean-domiciled legal entities. Korean regulations require any entity transacting in foreign currency to route through a designated foreign exchange bank licensed by the Bank of Korea. An offshore EMI cannot issue a Korean IBAN or provide a local clearing account for KRW settlement, and Korean banks will not accept EMI-issued statements as proof of proper foreign exchange transaction reporting. Using an unlicensed EMI would violate the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and may trigger AML penalties. The only permitted digital alternative is a domestic licensed financial institution's mobile corporate banking platform, not a standalone EMI. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on tier-1 Korean commercial banks for all inbound and outbound cross-border payment flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be executed via a SWIFT wire transfer from the investor's overseas account in the investor's own name, routed to the LLC's KRW-denominated corporate account at a designated Korean foreign exchange bank. The payment must be accompanied by an explicit purpose code for direct investment, typically '1000' (Investment in a Domestic Corporation) under the Foreign Exchange Information System classification. The bank must be provided with the foreign investment registration certificate issued in advance, and the incoming wire should include a reference to the certificate number. After receipt, the bank issues a certificate of foreign exchange transaction detailing the conversion of foreign currency to KRW at the prevailing market rate. For capital verification, the LLC should instruct the bank to produce an official transaction statement and, if applicable, request a certified capital contribution confirmation from the bank. The entire inbound flow must remain free of any third-party payment; mixing personal or client funds would trigger automatic escalation to the financial intelligence unit. All documentation, including the SWIFT MT103, payment proof, and agreement, must be retained to substantiate the tax basis and comply with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.Outbound trade settlements for global client disbursements are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 for standard wire transfers of service fees and for royalty payments. For larger recurring relationships, a commercial letter of credit may be utilized if the client requires a documentary guarantee, although service-based transactions typically use open account settlement with MT103. Documentary collections provide an alternative for signature-based payment security on milestone payments, but are less common in the game art outsourcing sector. All outbound wires must be made from a DDA-funded clearing account, and the bank requires the underlying service contract, validated invoice, and evidence of completed milestones. For disbursements exceeding USD 50,000, banks may require additional supporting documents to confirm the transaction is consistent with the declared business model and to verify that the payment is not a disguised capital outflow. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. While global fintech EMIs such as Wise and Revolut Business offer multi-currency accounts, they do not hold a Korean e-money license and are not authorized to provide foreign exchange services to Korean-domiciled legal entities. Korean regulations require any entity transacting in foreign currency to route through a designated foreign exchange bank licensed by the Bank of Korea. An offshore EMI cannot issue a Korean IBAN or provide a local clearing account for KRW settlement, and Korean banks will not accept EMI-issued statements as proof of proper foreign exchange transaction reporting. Using an unlicensed EMI would violate the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and may trigger AML penalties. The only permitted digital alternative is a domestic licensed financial institution's mobile corporate banking platform, not a standalone EMI. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on tier-1 Korean commercial banks for all inbound and outbound cross-border payment flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income above KRW 300 billion, plus 10% local income tax surcharge, producing an aggregate top effective headline rate of 26.4%; progressive national rates are 9% (up to KRW 200 million), 19% (KRW 200 million-20 billion), 21% (KRW 20 billion-300 billion), and 24% (above KRW 300 billion).

22% aggregate statutory Korean withholding on outbound dividends paid to a non-resident corporation, being 20% corporate income tax withholding under Corporate Tax Act Article 98 plus 10% local income tax surcharge.

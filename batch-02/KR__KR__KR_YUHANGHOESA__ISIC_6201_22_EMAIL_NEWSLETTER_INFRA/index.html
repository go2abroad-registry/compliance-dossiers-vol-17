Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Korean commercial law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa and no blocked escrow account requirement. National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic corporate and VAT administration portal: HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr). Customs export declarations are lodged through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS portal (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For the KR_YUHANGHOESA limited liability company (Yuhan Hoesa), a resident corporation is deemed registered by filing an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office (or the HomeTax System) having jurisdiction over its principal domestic place of business within 20 days after business commencement. For a foreign non-resident entity that has no domestic place of business but is liable for value-added tax (e.g., supplying electronic services to Korean non-registrants), the foreign entity must appoint a tax representative in Korea and complete simplified foreign business registration with the National Tax Service through HomeTax; the NTS then issues a Korean Tax Identification Number (TIN, also used as the VAT registration number). Non-resident entities acquiring a domestic permanent establishment must obtain the corporate registration number and business registration certificate under the same procedure as resident companies. There is no minimum capital condition.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW turnover threshold for VAT registration. All business operators, including foreign suppliers of electronic services with no Korean place of business, must register for VAT before commencing taxable activities in Korea. A simplified taxable status (reduced relief, not exemption) is available for individuals with annual revenue below KRW 80,000,000, but registration is still mandatory. A foreign electronic service supplier is not exempt based on volume below threshold; registration is required for any supply of qualifying electronic services to Korean non-taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, exported goods are zero-rated upon the export declaration to Korean customs. Services, including the bulk transactional email API, email newsletter hosting infrastructure and associated SaaS data processing services, qualify for 0% VAT when exported to non-residents, provided (i) the contract is made with a non-resident counterparty, (ii) the consideration is conclusively received in foreign currency through a Korean foreign exchange bank, and (iii) the service is actually performed or used outside Korea or is provided for the non-resident's use outside Korea. Intra-company overseas service transactions also require documentary evidence of the actual overseas provision.

  • Export Declaration submitted via UNI-PASS for any physical goods components
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing cross-border movement (goods only)
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated tax invoice referencing the export contract
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Purchase Confirmation or Inward Remittance Confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Export contract or service order stipulating the non-resident customer and foreign-currency consideration
  • Evidence of offshore service consumption (e.g., customer performance logs, infrastructure usage records supplied to overseas principal)

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-Tax invoice) must be issued for all B2B VAT supplies and transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system by the day following the supply date. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, government-certified digital stamping, or customs API clearance for normal VAT invoices in Korea. Export declarations are cleared separately via the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system. Large taxpayers may use API/EDI integration with HomeTax for e-invoice submission, but validation is not a pre-supply clearance condition.

The National Tax Service audits zero-rated export claims on a transaction-by-transaction basis, requiring the exporter or service provider to retain and produce the full documentation bundle. If foreign exchange is repatriated after the prescribed deadline or the overseas-use/foreign-customer conditions are not substantiated, the NTS will disallow the 0% rate, assess VAT at 10% on the full consideration, and impose additional tax. False or fraudulent zero-rating claims, including tax invoice forgery and fake export documentation, can trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and penalty taxes under the VAT Act, with additional tax rates commonly ranging from 20% to 40% of the underpaid VAT, depending on intent, plus any customs penalties if the export declaration was falsified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal but regulated foreign exchange regime. Current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act are broadly convertible, meaning the Yuhan Hoesa can receive Stripe settlement proceeds, pay overseas cloud infrastructure vendors, license software, remit royalties, and repatriate after-tax dividends through a designated foreign exchange bank without a blanket prior approval, provided each transaction is correctly classified and reported through the electronic foreign exchange information system. Capital account transactions are less automatic: any non-resident equity injection into the Korean LLC must be declared as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through the bank or KOTRA, and any outward capital transaction such as a foreign-currency loan, guarantee, or material equity investment requires a separate report or approval threshold assessment. The KRW is managed floating and is not subject to an in-country surrender requirement for export proceeds, but every inward and outward remittance must carry a valid MOEF Foreign Exchange Transaction purpose code. Profit remittance is ultimately allowable for a Yuhan Hoesa if accumulated retained earnings are supported by audited tax and financial statements, a board resolution declares the dividend, the Foreign Investment Company registration or FDI notification is current, and the shareholder loan account for capital remittance is in order. In practice the major operational hurdles are document-intensive bank underwriting, stringent beneficial ownership verification for non-resident shareholders, and the bank’s internal requirement that software-subscription revenue models show genuine recurring business contracts rather than ambiguous cross-border collections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services and software-based export proceeds such as email newsletter infrastructure subscriptions, the exporter is required to collect and deposit the foreign currency consideration inside a domestic foreign exchange bank account within the statutory collection period prescribed by the MOEF, generally aligned with the contractual settlement date and not exceeding five years for meritorious trade receivables under standard export claims. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the mandatory surrender ratio is effectively 0%, meaning the company may retain USD or other foreign currency proceeds in a foreign currency account, exchange them into KRW at the prevailing market rate, or use them directly for permissible foreign-currency disbursements. All export receipts, including those received through global payment intermediaries such as Stripe, must ultimately be booked through a Korean designated foreign exchange bank and reported with the correct BOK/MOEF export-revenue purpose classification; extension of the settlement timeline beyond statutory limits requires a documentary evidentiary submission for bank or MOEF approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Korea Customs Service supervises cross-border currency and goods movement at frontier points.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI viability is low and effectively non-feasible for a Korean-resident corporate entity. An offshore digital EMI without a licensed Korean branch cannot lawfully issue KRW-denominated resident corporate accounts, accept deposits, or execute onshore foreign exchange transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Korean Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Only domestic tier-1 commercial banks licensed as foreign exchange banks can legally hold capital escrow accounts, operational currency accounts, and cross-border clearing mandates for the Yuhan Hoesa.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital injection must be routed through a properly filed Foreign Direct Investment notification under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act at a designated domestic foreign exchange bank. The overseas shareholder remits funds from its own bank account, never from an anonymous or third-party pool, via SWIFT MT103, with a clear BKEF or MOEF foreign exchange purpose code matching a non-resident share subscription in a Korean direct-investment target. The bank will book the inward remittance into a temporary foreign-currency settlement account, convert it into KRW when appropriate, and credit a capital escrow account pending issuance of the share capital. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion for the fiscal year; Master File is required when annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; documentation must be submitted with the corporate income tax return and the National Tax Service may request contemporaneous evidence regardless of threshold in an audit.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation code, but the substance-over-form principle, the beneficial ownership requirement in tax treaty relief, the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 5 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs, and controlled foreign company rules require real Korean economic activities; for the email newsletter infrastructure, core income-generating activities such as server hosting, client contracting, data processing, personnel management, and call support must be performed by the Korean LLC and cannot be artificially shifted to an offshore shell with no operational capacity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; punishment may include imprisonment for a term up to five years, or a fine, or both, with aggravated penalties when the evaded tax amount exceeds the statutory criminal threshold; the National Tax Service also imposes additional tax of up to 40% of the understated tax and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors who knowingly sign or submit fraudulent returns may be held jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and civil restitution; the court may order asset seizure and overseas travel blacklisting under the National Tax Collection Act; criminal imprisonment terms apply if the director directed or benefited from the fraudulent scheme.
Transfer Pricing and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Articles 5 and 6 of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs permit the National Tax Service to re-determine the taxable income as if the transactions had occurred at arm's length; related adjustments are subject to penalty taxes, additional tax for under-reporting, and daily late-payment surcharges; criminal prosecution can follow if the taxpayer uses fabricated transfer pricing documents.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors who authorized the artificial pricing structure may be personally liable when corporate assets are insufficient to pay the tax assessment under Article 39 of the Framework Act on National Taxes; they face civil damages for breach of fiduciary duty, travel blacklisting, and imprisonment if the pricing scheme is shown to be a fraudulent tax evasion mechanism.
Withholding Tax / DTA and Shell Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act imposes the 22% statutory withholding obligation on Korean payers; under the Framework Act on National Taxes, the failed withholding agent is liable for the uncollected tax, negligence penalties, and daily late-payment surcharges; the National Tax Service may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership and principal purpose tests and impose additional corporate income tax, penalties, and criminal sanctions for wilful non-collection.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors who cause the Korean LLC to withhold nothing and remit the full amount offshore are personally liable for the withheld taxes and related interest; personal assets can be seized and an overseas travel ban imposed; criminal imprisonment is possible under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act where the payment structure was designed to defeat Korean tax collection.

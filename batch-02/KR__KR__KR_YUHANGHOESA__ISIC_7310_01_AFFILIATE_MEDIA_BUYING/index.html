E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity seeking a Korean Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number must first determine whether it has a domestic place of business (fixed place of business) under Article 119 of the Income Tax Act or a permanent establishment under Article 2 of the Corporation Tax Act. If the entity is engaged in the supply of digital advertising media buying and affiliate lead generation services through a Korean intermediary, a server, or a dependent agent in Korea, registration with the NTS is required. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For corporations, including a Yuhan Hoesa LLC, VAT registration is mandatory from the start of business regardless of turnover; there is no minimum threshold. For individuals and sole proprietors, mandatory registration triggers when taxable turnover in the prior calendar year exceeds KRW 80,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT treatment applies to the export of goods physically exported under customs declaration and to the supply of services and digital services to non-resident recipients for use wholly outside Korea, including digital advertising, affiliate marketing, and lead generation services supplied by a Korean entity to a foreign business recipient. Eligibility requires proof that the recipient is a non-resident without a domestic place of business in Korea, the place of supply is outside Korea, the contract and invoices designate offshore supply, and the services are not rendered to Korean consumers. Advertising services on exported goods and services supplied to foreign clients may also qualify under Article 21 of the VAT Act and the VAT Act Enforcement Decree. In all cases the exporter must hold full documentation substantiating the foreign destination and collection of proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or comparable shipment receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank, or SWIFT/MT103 wire confirmation evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice stating the export price and the 0% VAT treatment
  • Copy of the export contract with the overseas buyer, including Incoterms and place-of-supply terms
  • Korean customs export performance confirmation (Export Performance Certificate)
  • For services: zero-rated electronic tax invoice referencing the foreign recipient's VAT/GST identification number, together with the service contract and evidence that the recipient is a non-resident business and all benefit is derived outside Korea
  • Foreign exchange transaction reporting data from the Foreign Exchange Information System (FXIS) where applicable

All VAT-registered taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices (e-Tax Invoices) under Article 32 of the VAT Act for B2B transactions. Issuance must be transmitted to the NTS through Hometax or an approved e-invoice service provider in XML format. Since 1 July 2024, the mandatory electronic tax invoice system applies fully, and the NTS operates a real-time transmission and validation framework requiring API-based or machine-to-machine integration for high-risk B2B transactions. VAT returns are filed quarterly for corporations, with electronic filing mandatory. Anti-fraud controls include assigned electronic serial numbers, buyer VAT number verification, and 24-hour validation through the NTS tax invoice status transmission service. Non-compliance triggers penalties under Article 32-5 of the VAT Act and Article 125-1 of the Framework Act on National Taxes.

If the documented export is not substantiated by customs declarations, actual cross-border movement of goods, or verifiable foreign exchange repatriation within the statutory period, the NTS may reverse the zero-rating and assess 10% VAT on the supply, plus penalties of up to 40% of the unpaid tax for negligent underreporting or intentional misstatement, 10% for underdeclared tax, and monthly late-payment interest of 0.022% per day from the original due date. Fictitious or fraudulent export invoices can trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, with imprisonment up to 2 years or fines up to KRW 50,000,000. Retroactive clawbacks may include all input VAT credits claimed in connection with the false export, and the statute of limitations extends to 3 years generally, or 5 years for intentional evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal current-account system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but the Korean won is not an offshore freely deliverable currency; KRW convertibility is provided exclusively through authorized foreign exchange banks integrated with the Bank of Korea settlement system. Current-account receipts and payments for trade, service fees, and profit remittances are generally permitted without prior approval, while capital-account transactions, including FDI share purchases and non-resident borrowing, are subject to reporting or approval. A Yuhan Hoesa receiving inbound equity must ensure that every dollar is remitted with a correct foreign-investment report; otherwise the foreign exchange bank will reject the credit or the Bank of Korea will issue a correction order. Remittance of dividends is supervised: the bank will require audited financial statements, evidence of corporate tax payment and withholding, and a shareholder resolution. The practical hurdle is documentary exactness, not convertibility itself. Overseas banks do not hold domestic KRW clearing accounts, and offshore KRW trading is largely an NDF market outside the legal onshore settlement perimeter. Therefore the treasury blueprint must keep the onshore KRW liquidity ring inside regulated banks and route every convertible flow through declared business purposes with full transactional provenance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Regulations, Korea imposes no mandatory cash surrender percentage on export proceeds. Exporters must settle export-related foreign currency receipts through an authorized foreign exchange bank and are required to bring the proceeds onshore within 90 days from export clearance; an extension to 180 days may be granted by the bank if the exporter submits a collection plan and proves an acceptable payment risk profile. The proceeds may be credited to a foreign currency account or converted into KRW at the bank's market rate. Failure to repatriate on time or to file the required extension report triggers administrative reporting to the Ministry of Strategy and Finance and can result in penalties or loss of treasury accommodation.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), with delegated authority from the Ministry of Strategy and Finance (MOSF), is the central foreign-exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act of the Republic of Korea; actual execution and supervision are carried out through authorized foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, non-bank fintech EMIs such as Wise are not licensed to open domestic KRW transaction accounts for regulated corporate entities or to act as the primary foreign-exchange settlement counterparty without a foreign-exchange business license from the Bank of Korea and authorization from the Financial Services Commission. A Yuhan Hoesa registered in the Republic of Korea must maintain its FDI capital escrow and core operating liquidity with an authorized domestic Tier-1 bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity for the Yuhan Hoesa must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's account directly to the FDI capital escrow account opened at an authorized foreign exchange bank. The remittance reason must use a capital-account foreign-exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment, with the FDI report completed and submitted to the bank before the credit is posted. After the credit clears, the bank verifies the capital against the reported amount, issues capital verification and foreign investor registration documents, and releases the funds to the entity's operational KRW account. The bank then files the transaction electronically through the Foreign Exchange Information System. The treasury must never allow the equity to be pre-funded through third-party payment wallets, digital EMIs, or personal accounts.Operational disbursements should flow through the single authorized foreign exchange bank. SWIFT MT103 is the default rail for affiliate commissions, media agency invoices, and recurring cross-border service fees, with payment metadata showing beneficiary bank, exact legal entity beneficiary, and invoice reference. For larger media procurement contracts with recognized vendors, commercial letters of credit or documentary collections may be used where the invoice is contractually supported and the service delivery schedule is specific. Domestic KRW payments to Korean publishers, influencers, and suppliers must clear through BOK-Wire+ or domestic OTC bank transfer rails and be reported as current-account payments. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Republic of Korea's Act on External Audit of Stock Companies and its Presidential Enforcement Decree, a Yuhan Hoesa is legally required to appoint an independent certified public accountant and undergo a statutory audit if, at the end of the immediately preceding fiscal year, total assets equal or exceed KRW 12 billion (approximately USD 8.9 million). The same statutory audit is also mandatory for a Yuhan Hoesa that is formed or continued as an entity subject to external audit by presidential decree, including any entity whose securities are listed or whose shares are traded on a Korean exchange, any entity that falls into special categories such as holding companies with material public interest, and any company that has been ordered by the Securities and Futures Commission to appoint an outside auditor due to failure of internal accounting control. In practice, banks and foreign affiliates may also require a certified audit even below the statutory threshold if the financial report is used for credit extension, overseas remittance, or merger consolidation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax on taxable income above KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surtax, producing an aggregate headline rate of 26.4%; lower resident brackets are 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% up to KRW 20 billion, and 21% up to KRW 300 billion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on gross outbound dividends (20% corporate income tax withholding plus 2% local income tax surtax), subject to reduction under an applicable bilateral double tax treaty, commonly to 5% or 10% for a qualified substantial corporate shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File becomes mandatory when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions reach KRW 10 billion during the fiscal year; Master File may be required when annual revenue reaches KRW 100 billion and related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion; transfer-pricing disclosure is filed with the annual corporate tax return and must be submitted on request within a statutory time limit.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies a substance-over-form doctrine through Corporate Tax Act Article 170 and International Tax Adjustment Act Article 4; for an affiliate media-buying entity, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed by Korean personnel, including contract negotiation, media purchasing decisions, risk assumption, and oversight of affiliate networks; a paper LLC that merely invoices commissions while actual functions remain with shareholders or foreign affiliates will have its transactions recharacterized, profit reallocated, and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and offshore concealment

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Articles 47-2 and 47-3 impose additional taxes of 10% to 40% of the underreported or unreported tax, and Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act imposes imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to ten times the evaded tax, or both.

Director personal liability: Under National Tax Collection Act Article 311, the representative director becomes a secondary taxpayer when the company's assets are insufficient because of intentional evasion; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is a mere instrumentality, and directors face civil restitution for company losses under Commercial Act Article 399, travel bans, asset attachment, and individual criminal liability including imprisonment.
Shell affiliate misuse and transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office may disregard the intermediary under International Tax Adjustment Act Article 4, reallocate profit to Korea, impose an additional tax on intercompany adjustments, deny deductions for the inflated fees, and apply Foreign Exchange Transactions Act penalties for illegal cross-border remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed offshore service agreements without real service substance can be held personally liable for restitution and secondary tax obligations; the representative director may be blacklisted, issued a departure ban, and charged with intentional documentation fraud under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Economic Crimes.
Anti-money laundering and cross-border payment concealment

Statutory sanction: The Specific Financial Information Act imposes criminal liability for AML breaches, including imprisonment and statutory fines, and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act Article 30 penalizes unrecorded outward remittances with fines and confiscation; AML penalties may be assessed on both the company and the directing officers.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors who knowingly direct concealed payment flows are personally criminally liable for violations, individual bank accounts may be frozen, travel bans are imposed during investigation, and civil asset restitution is available to aggrieved counterparties; corporate limited liability will not protect directors once criminal conduct and intentional concealment are proven.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Yuhan Hoesa engaged in oversized-furniture online retail, a pure virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered office. Banks under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act and financial institution AML CDD guidelines will typically reject a standalone virtual-office address for settlement account opening, especially for Stripe and BNPL payment flows, unless a physical lease and proof of control are supplied. Therefore, the company should obtain a registered office with real commercial substance and a resident contact.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: Home Tax Service (hometax.go.kr), including the e-Scro electronic tax invoice system and cash receipt reporting system.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing Yuhan Hoesa or a foreign entity that needs Korean VAT/TIN: (1) appoint a domestic tax representative/manager under the Framework Act on National Taxes; (2) file the Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office before business commencement, either in person or through the NTS Home Tax Service electronic portal; (3) attach the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation, articles of association (foreign registration evidence), board resolution appointing the Korean representative, power of attorney, and proof of the registered domestic place of business; (4) the NTS issues the 10-digit business registration number connected to VAT registration; (5) for foreign electronic-service suppliers without a Korean fixed place of business, the NTS administrative post designated for non-residents performs a simplified VAT registration and issues a dedicated foreign supplier VAT ID, with a domestic tax representative if needed; (6) the same number is used for e-tax invoicing, customs declarations, VAT returns, and corporate income tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) is treated as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and Value-Added Tax Act; no U.S.-style pass-through or check-the-box election is available. Korean hybrid mismatch rules under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act operate as mandatory anti-abuse adjustments, not elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based VAT registration exemption; all business operators, including non-residents making Korean taxable supplies, must register under the VAT Act before/within 20 days of business commencement. Domestic individual micro-businesses below the statutory simplified taxable person ceiling in the VAT Act may use the simplified taxable person declaration regime but are still registered for VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Act, exports of goods from Korea are zero-rated. The supplier must prove that the goods actually left Korea under a customs export declaration, title and risk passed to a foreign buyer outside Korea, and consideration is received from abroad in foreign currency (or as otherwise approved). Services directly connected with exported goods, and qualifying digital services performed and consumed outside Korea, are also taxable at 0% or outside the Korean VAT base if the recipient is a non-resident/non-Korean consumer. For oversized furniture e-commerce, the zero-rating applies to the furniture sale when delivered to a foreign destination through Korean export customs clearance.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (UNI-PASS electronic clearance system)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / multimodal transport document) showing shipment from Korea to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Foreign exchange receipt or settlement certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank (inward remittance advice for export proceeds)
  • Export performance/earnings confirmation from NTS or Korea Customs Service, where necessary to prove collection of export proceeds

Mandatory e-tax invoicing under Article 32 of the VAT Act through the NTS e-Scro system. B2B VAT invoices must be issued and transmitted electronically via Home Tax Service or a certified ERP/API interface; the NTS receives/validates them centrally on transmission. No private third-party clearance or physical fiscal stamp is used; anti-fraud control is enforced by the NTS electronic tax invoice database, mandatory cash receipt reporting for B2C cash sales, and automated data matching against VAT returns and customs declarations.

The NTS may scrutinize export zero-rating claims by matching export declarations, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If the export is not substantiated, the goods are not actually exported, or proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day settlement period without prior approval, the NTS disallows the 0% rate and assesses VAT at 10%, plus statutory additional tax for false/unfaithful returns (up to 40% of the understated tax) and late-payment interest. Where a false VAT refund is obtained intentionally, criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment and fines, may apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised but regulated foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance and delegated to the Bank of Korea and authorised foreign exchange banks. Current account transactions, such as trade-related import and export settlements, service fees, and operational remittances, are substantially free and can proceed through authorised banks with limited documentation. Capital account transactions, including inbound foreign direct investment into the Yuhan Hoesa, foreign loans, and outward investment, remain subject to either a prior report, a simultaneous report, or post-transaction reporting, depending on the size and nature of the transaction. Korean won (KRW) is only fully convertible for onshore residents with a legitimate underlying trade or investment purpose; offshore convertibility is limited, and non-residents generally settle through deliverable or non-deliverable arrangements. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are permitted once corporate tax, invoice verification, and withholding obligations are cleared; the remitting bank will require evidence of the foreign investment registration and a tax clearance statement. There is no bureaucratic surrender of export proceeds, but all FX receipts must be returned to Korea and reported through a designated foreign exchange bank, with an 18-month repatriation deadline for export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No fixed percentage of export receipts must be surrendered to the central bank; instead, the full invoice value of exports must be collected in foreign currency or from an authorised bank account within 18 months of the export date. The exporter must deposit the proceeds into a foreign currency account at an authorised foreign exchange bank and report the receipt and settlement through that bank; if the proceeds are retained abroad, a separate approval is required. The statutory period may be extended by the bank in cases of deferred payment, construction projects, or long-cycle trade contracts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK); the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Bank of Korea jointly administer the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with daily oversight delegated to authorised foreign exchange banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / Prohibited for primary treasury use. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed as banks in Korea and cannot lawfully provide the account holder with a domestic KRW account, a Korean settlement IBAN, or an authorised foreign exchange bank status usable for Foreign Exchange Transactions Act filings. For this Yuhan Hoesa, EMIs cannot receive the FDI capital, cannot be used for capital verification, and cannot execute Korean tax payments. EMIs may be used only as ancillary payment gateways where the settlement occurs through a licensed domestic acquirer, never as the entity's principal liquidity rail.

All new capital injections must first be received in a dedicated foreign currency account at an authorised Korean foreign exchange bank under a Foreign Investment Report filed through KOTRA or the bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion for the business year; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion; a simplified transfer pricing record is required starting at KRW 10 billion of international related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is enforced through Corporate Tax Act Article 14 substance-over-form, the International Tax Adjustment Act general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial ownership tests, and OECD BEPS anti-abuse rules. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa cannot be a letterbox: it must maintain order acceptance, inventory ownership, freight and logistics control, delivery, customer service, warranty management, accounting records, and board decision-making in Korea. For oversized freight e-commerce, the Core Income-Generating Activities include supply-chain negotiation, inventory risk assumption, and delivery settlement functions, all of which must be economically supported by local employees, premises, contracts, and assets.

Failure to withhold and pay Korean tax on outbound distributions, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act Articles 156, 156-2, and 156-6: the withholding agent is personally liable to the NTS for the unwithheld national tax and local income tax surcharge, plus a 10% failure-to-withhold penalty, the daily delinquency surcharge, and mandatory amounts review; in aggravated cases the same conduct constitutes tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: The representative director and any officer responsible for treasury operations can be made personally liable for the unpaid withholding and penalties where corporate assets are insufficient, may be blocked from leaving Korea by the departure ban system, may have personal assets attached, and may face criminal prosecution for assisting non-resident tax evasion, leading to imprisonment.
Shell-company substance abuse, treaty shopping, and transfer pricing fraud

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Article 14, International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 4 and 6-2, and the OECD anti-BEPS limitations authorise the NTS to disregard sham transactions, recharacterise revenue, adjust the arm's length price, deny DTA benefits, impose a 10-40% adjustment penalty, and add statutory daily interest; criminal referral follows when the documentation is fabricated.

Director personal liability: Directors who consciously maintain a sham corporate vehicle or approve non-arm's length related-party contracts can be held civilly liable for the full tax, penalties, and interest through corporate-veil piercing, be subject to confiscation of personal assets and travel restrictions, be blacklisted by the NTS, and, on conviction for fraud, be imprisoned for up to 5 years when the evaded amount is aggravated.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under South Korean law. A Yuhan Hoesa may be incorporated with any contribution amount stated in the articles. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic administration portal: HomeTax (Hometax) at https://www.hometax.go.kr. The NTS is the sole competent tax authority for Korean corporate tax, VAT, business registration, tax invoice processing and electronic tax invoice compliance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Yuhan Hoesa as a domestic limited liability company under the Korean Commercial Act at the competent Register Office and obtain the certificate of business registration. Step 2: Before or within 20 days after the commencement of taxable business, file the statutory business registration through the NTS HomeTax portal or with the local District Tax Office under Article 5 of the Value-Added Tax Act; the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as both the corporate tax ID and the VAT registration number. Step 3: A foreign non-resident entity that operates through a Korean domestic place of business without a separate Korean legal entity must additionally file a Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration under the Corporate Tax Act with the local District Tax Office, obtain the same business registration number for that establishment, appoint a domestic tax representative where required, and register the Korean bank account used for business settlement. Step 4: File an additional application to add the e-commerce business site, postal address, administrators, payment acquireer identifiers, and any cross-border e-commerce platform code to the business registration details; the NTS updates the standardised VAT assessment keys and exporter identifier. Step 5: Submit the certificate of business registration to open the Korean Won corporate bank account and to link the export zero-rating and electronic tax invoice mandates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold applies to a corporate Yuhan Hoesa; all business operators, including all limited companies, must register for VAT under Article 5 of the Value-Added Tax Act within 20 days of the business commencement date. For natural-person sole proprietors only, a simplified taxpayer status exists where annual gross supplies are less than KRW 80,000,000, but this status is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods, including pet supplies and pet care devices, is zero-rated at 0% VAT under Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act when the goods are physically exported from the Korean customs territory and an export customs declaration is accepted by the Korean Customs Service. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated when the services are supplied to a foreign non-resident and are consumed or used wholly by that non-resident outside Korea; this must be supported by a completed foreign-currency contract, invoicing, proof of foreign use or consumption, payment remittance evidence, and, where required, the non-resident recipient's equivalent foreign tax identification number. Online marketplace sales to a foreign buyer qualify as export zero-rating only if the goods are exported in kind and the ordinary export evidence chain is retained; domestic deliveries to Korean consumers, even through a foreign payment processor, remain subject to 10% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration Acceptance Certificate (export clearance result generated by the Korean Customs Service UNI-PASS system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the international carrier
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List consistent with the UNI-PASS export declaration
  • Sales contract, purchase order, or online marketplace order-dispatch reconciliation report identifying the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Exchange Proceeds Receipt Certificate / Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign-exchange bank receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency)

Mandatory electronic tax invoices (e-invoices) through the NTS HomeTax platform or NTS-approved e-invoicing ASP/API channels. A general taxable person, including every Yuhan Hoesa, must issue an electronic tax invoice for B2B supplies of goods and services no later than the day immediately following the supply date and transmit it to NTS through HomeTax; the e-invoice data is cross-matched by NTS against VAT returns, cash receipts, customs declarations and corporate tax filings. There is no step-by-step government invoice blocking or digital stamping in Korea; anti-fraud control is performed by real-time e-invoice submission, a central NTS electronic invoice database, mandatory cash receipt issuance for consumer transactions when requested, and automated risk-scoring before VAT refunds are released.

The NTS and Korean Customs Service run a joint data-matching audit that reconciles the UNI-PASS export declaration, the electronic tax invoice or export evidence, the VAT return, and the foreign-exchange bank receipt. If a 0% export claim fails the statutory documentation or proceeds-repatriation test, the NTS may retroactively reassess the supply at 10% VAT and impose additional tax for unfaithful tax invoices, failure to issue or transmit electronic tax invoices, delayed or omitted VAT reporting, and unlawful refund claims; the refund made from the zero-rated export is clawed back with interest. Fraudulent zero-rating documentation, forged export documents, and intentionally circular export schemes are criminal tax evasion matters under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and may expose the entity and responsible officers to criminal fines and imprisonment; the NTS audit exposure is extended for deliberately concealed or false documentation cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current-account transactions including trade-related receipts and payments, with no surrender requirements on export proceeds. Capital-account transactions are subject to a declaration or a licensing regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA), administered by the MOEF and executed through domestic banking channels. For a newly formed Yuhan Hoesa LLC, inbound equity injections and shareholder loans require a Foreign Exchange Declaration Form (Form 12) filed via a designated foreign exchange bank; approval is generally granted on a post-audit basis. Profit remittance offshore is lawful provided corporate income taxes have been settled and audited financial statements are submitted; no pre-approval or lock-up period applies. However, the FETA imposes a six-month repatriation deadline on export proceeds from shipment date, and any foreign-currency earnings must be received in a domestic bank account unless an extension is obtained. The overall convertibility for this e-commerce exporter is high, but compliance with monthly receipt confirmation through the bank is mandatory to avoid punitive fines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 11 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Korea within 180 days from the date of shipment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into KRW; proceeds may remain in a foreign-currency account at a domestic bank. Extensions beyond 180 days require prior approval from the exporting bank or an application to the MOEF. The exporter must submit a Foreign Exchange Purchase/Repayment Confirmation to the relevant bank within 30 days after receipt for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service (KCS)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Revolut, Wise Business, Airwallex) are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions under the Korean Banking Act or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. They may be used only as a supplementary payroll or procurement rail if they maintain a licensed partnership with a domestic Tier-1 bank, but they cannot replace a domestic corporate bank account for statutory FX settlement, capital verification, or tax payments. Moreover, Korean regulators require all export proceeds to be settled through a resident bank account; therefore, EMI-only treasury management is legally non-viable. Feasibility rating: Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions must be routed via a corporate USD or EUR account at a Korean Tier-1 bank using an MT103 message with the payment purpose code 'A-301' (Direct Investment – Equity). The bank will require the foreign investor's completed and notarized Foreign Exchange Declaration Form (Form 12) submitted within 30 days prior to the injection. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Korean tax resident entity is required to prepare and produce the Korean Local File and Master File when its annual revenue is KRW 100 billion or more and cross-border related-party transaction volume is KRW 5 billion or more in the fiscal year; Country-by-Country Reporting is separately required if the group's consolidated global revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion; all taxpayers engaged in cross-border related-party transactions above KRW 50 million must file the International Transaction Report and may be required to submit an integrated reporting schedule even when full documentation is not mandatory

Economic substance scope: Korea applies substantive economic-substance principles through the Basic Act for National Taxes (unlawful avoidance using no business purpose may be disregarded), the Adjustment of International Taxes Act framework, and the Permanent Establishment attribution doctrine derived from OECD principles; the Korean NTS may deny treaty benefits and recharacterize income if the Korean LLC lacks employees, premises, business decision-making authority, or meaningful management functions in Korea, despite the absence of a formal Economic Substance Regulation regime commonly seen in the Gulf; for e-commerce pet-supply import and distribution activities, the Core Income-Generating Activity in Korea includes procurement of pet products, inventory ownership, order fulfillment, logistics coordination, customer support, and revenue recognition from Korean online sales, all of which must be economically borne by the Korean LLC and not merely documented offshore

Tax Evasion and Digital Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, Article 3 and Article 4, a representative director who evades corporate tax by misreporting or hiding income is criminally liable; the sanction is imprisonment up to three years for ordinary evasion and up to five years for aggravated evasion, plus a fine of up to five times the evaded tax; in addition, the National Tax Service imposes a 20-40% negligence penalty on the underreported income and daily overdue interest of 0.025% on the unpaid principal, and the tax authority may correct the return with an additional local surtax of 10% of the national deficiency

Director personal liability: Where the director authorized or caused the false return, the corporate veil is pierced under the principle of representative secondary liability in the National Tax Collection Act; the director's personal bank accounts, automobiles, and residences can be seized and sold to satisfy the corporation's unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; the director may be placed on the NTS wanted/restricted travel list (blacklisting) for delinquent taxes exceeding the statutory threshold, has his or her passport denied or revoked, and upon criminal conviction is subject to imprisonment; civil restitution to the NTS plus criminal fines can be anchored from offshore assets through international mutual legal assistance treaties

Statutory sanction: The Adjustment of International Taxes of Korea (Articles 3 and 6) empowers the tax authority to compute a normal transaction price and re-assess the Korean LLC's taxable income; the resulting shortage triggers a 10% negligence tax on the corrected intercompany transaction amount, a documentation penalty of up to KRW 30 million, and internal interest for each year from the original filing; if the transfer pricing adjustment exceeds KRW 5 billion and is deemed fraudulent, the criminal tax evasion penalties of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act apply, adding imprisonment and fines up to five times the aggregate additional tax

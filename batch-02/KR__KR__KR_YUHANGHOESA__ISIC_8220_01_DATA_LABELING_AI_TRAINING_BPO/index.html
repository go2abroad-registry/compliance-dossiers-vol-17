Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Commercial Act. However, all contribution units must be fully paid at formation. Cash contributions must be deposited in a licensed Korean bank formation deposit account and evidenced by a certificate of contribution payment issued by such bank. Non-cash contributions are permitted if valued in accordance with the Commercial Act. No blocked governmental escrow or capital maintenance account is required. A non-resident investor may need to file a foreign direct investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and remit through a licensed foreign exchange bank.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax System (https://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company), the entity must complete business registration with the competent district tax office through the HomeTax system or in-person submission within 20 days of commencing business. The application must include articles of incorporation, certificate of corporate registration, lease agreement or ownership document proving the Korean place of business, identity documentation of the representative director, and bank account confirmation. The NTS will issue the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which simultaneously functions as the Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number and the VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a fixed Korean place of business that makes taxable supplies in Korea, Korean VAT law requires the appointment of a domestic tax representative, who must file the same business registration application in the foreign entity's name via HomeTax, attaching a power of attorney, documents proving the foreign entity's legal existence, and evidence of the foreign entity's place of management, after which the NTS issues a business registration number for non-resident taxpayers.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None. The Republic of Korea does not provide check-the-box elections or dedicated hybrid mismatch elections. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No mandatory turnover threshold for corporations; every business making taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT before commencing business. The simplified VAT regime may apply to certain small individual businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported from Korea and the export is supported by an approved customs export declaration. Services, including AI data labeling and BPO services, may qualify for 0% VAT when supplied to a non-resident recipient whose place of business is outside Korea and the service is not deemed supplied in Korea under the place-of-supply rules of the Value-Added Tax Act. The supplier must retain a written contract, evidence of the non-resident status of the recipient, and documentation showing that the consideration is received in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (approved by Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS, including the Export Declaration Number)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) proving physical export from Korea
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list matching the export declaration
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized foreign exchange bank, evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a convertible currency

All VAT invoices issued to other businesses must be transmitted electronically as e-tax invoices through the HomeTax system, which assigns a unique digital approval number to each valid e-tax invoice. Submission may be made via the HomeTax portal directly or through approved ERP-to-NTS API/XML channels. E-tax invoice issuance is also mandatory for zero-rated export transactions. For B2C taxable supplies, cash receipts must be issued under the Cash Receipt System, and all relevant transaction data must be reported to the NTS in the prescribed electronic format. Failure to issue and transmit e-tax invoices subjects the taxpayer to statutory penalty surcharges under the Value-Added Tax Act.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service electronically cross-match the approved customs export declaration, the zero-rated e-tax invoice, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If the physical export is found to be fictitious, the documentation is false, or the foreign exchange proceeds are not received within 180 calendar days from the export clearance date, the zero-rating is retrospectively revoked. The exporter will be assessed output VAT at 10% on the supply, plus statutory penalty surcharges and National Tax interest for underpayment based on the Framework Act on National Taxes. Deliberate documentation fraud triggers criminal penalties and the denial of input VAT refunds, and the responsible parties may be subject to criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a free-floating won with current-account convertibility broadly available to residents and non-residents; service export earnings from data-labeling and RLHF BPO can be received in KRW or foreign currency and converted at any licensed foreign exchange bank without prior licensing. The capital account remains subject to registration, reporting, or approval controls under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, especially for direct equity injections, external loans, and overseas payments exceeding the bank's presumed documentation threshold. There is no blanket surrender rule for service export proceeds, yet the bank must allocate every receipt to the correct transaction classification, apply the source-of-wealth probing rule, and retain underlying contracts and invoices for a minimum of seven years under AML obligations. Profit remittance is legally permitted, but requires audited or reviewed financial statements proving accumulated undistributed earnings, a corporate tax clearance certificate, the annual board resolution, and a foreign exchange bank's certificate showing the initial capital contribution was properly reported; until those documents are clean, remittances will be quarantined. The practical constraint is therefore documentary density and beneficiary KYC rather than administrative prohibition, and any failure to classify cross-border flows accurately can trigger FSS penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export and service proceeds must be settled no later than one year from the date of export declaration or service completion; extensions are possible only with bank approval. For a Yuhan Hoesa engaging in cross-border AI data-labeling and RLHF BPO, 100% of invoice value must be repatriated to a licensed Korean bank's foreign-currency or KRW account; no mandatory sale-to-KRW ratio exists at present and the full amount can remain in a foreign-currency deposit, but any conversion is executed at the bank's prevailing rate and coded as service export settlement. The bank will treat a settlement exceeding one year as a compliance event and require a written explanation, supporting client correspondence, and a bank-approved trade-payment extension.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), central bank and de jure monetary authority, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) jointly administering the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and prudential reporting framework.

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail and only conditional as an ancillary payout edge. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

9% national corporate income tax on the first KRW 200 million of taxable income, 19% on the next KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion tranche, 21% on the KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion tranche, and 24% above KRW 300 billion; a 10% local income tax surcharge is added, producing an effective first-tier headline rate of 9.9% plus an aggregate effective top rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on outbound cash or constructive dividends: 20% national withholding tax under the Corporate Tax Act plus the 10% local income tax surcharge (i.e., 20% + 2% local withholding), absent treaty reduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are statutorily required when annual consolidated revenue reaches KRW 100 billion and foreign related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; all corporations with cross-border related-party transactions above de minimis levels must file the disclosure form, and the NTS can request contemporaneous local documentation within 45 to 60 days for taxpayers carrying lower transaction volumes if audit risk is identified.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the International Tax Adjustment Act and National Tax Basic Act Article 14 impose a substance-over-form rule: a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa must have real Korean offices, local payroll, operational management, and Core Income-Generating Activities for annotation, labeling, RLHF curation, and client contracting; if decision-making and personnel reside abroad and the Korean entity merely passes income and expenses, the NTS will recharacterize the entity as a shell, disregard its separate personality, attribute income to the foreign parent, and apply Controlled Foreign Company rules to offshore retained earnings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false corporate income tax filing and offshore revenue concealment

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1) imposes imprisonment up to five years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax; the Corporate Tax Act imposes a 20% no-return penalty, 10% negligent understatement penalty, and 40% fraudulent understatement penalty; the National Tax Collection Act adds late-payment surcharge of about 0.03% per day until full settlement.

Director personal liability: Korean courts may pierce the corporate veil when the Yuhan Hoesa is used as a mere instrumentality: the representative director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, local tax, penalties, and accrued interest; personal bank accounts and overseas assets can be attached; the NTS may blacklist the director, impose a travel ban, block exit at the airport, and revoke passports; criminal conviction carries imprisonment and disqualification from future corporate directorships.
Artificial profit shifting / shell company lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: International Tax Adjustment Act Article 5 authorizes the National Tax Service to adjust the transaction to an arm's-length price and impute profits to the Korean entity; CFC and GAAR rules reattribute passive offshore retained earnings; transfer-pricing penalty surcharges of up to 20% of adjusted taxaable income and daily late-payment penalties apply, together with refusal to grant deductions for bogus service fees.

Director personal liability: Under National Tax Basic Act Article 39, directors who actually exercise control over funds and assets may be secondarily liable for the corporate tax when the company's assets are insufficient; Commercial Act Article 399 exposes directors to civil damages; aggravating conduct is punished as aiding tax evasion and loots the corporate veil protection, resulting in personal asset restitution, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment for direct participants.
Non-withholding / abusive tax treaty relief on profit repatriation

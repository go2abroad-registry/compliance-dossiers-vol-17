Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr); export customs clearance and export declaration filing administered by Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity forming a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) must first complete the required Financial Services Commission registration/licence for P2P micro-lending. The entity must then file a Foreign Corporation Domestic Place of Business Registration application with the competent NTS district tax office, either in person or through the HomeTax portal, submitting certified English and Korean copies of the foreign certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, memorandum and articles, board resolution appointing the Korean tax representative, power of attorney, proof of the Korean domestic place of business (lease agreement or service-office contract), and the foreign entity's own foreign tax identification number. Upon review, NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (sintae no), which serves simultaneously as the Korean corporate TIN and VAT registration number. If the foreign entity supplies only electronic or digital services and has no Korean fixed place of business, it may instead use the simplified registration procedure for foreign electronic service suppliers, appoint a Korean tax representative, and register through HomeTax before making taxable supplies. The assigned registration number must be used for corporate tax withholding, VAT declarations, e-tax invoice issuance, and Korean Customs Service export filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Yuhan Hoesa formed under Korean law is classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available to foreign members. If the investor instead operates as a branch of a foreign corporation without a Korean entity, Korean branch taxation applies with territorial sourcing rules.

Available election forms

None. Korea does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily entity-level taxable as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act; foreign members cannot elect pass-through treatment. Only standard corporate governance elections (financial year, tax representative appointment, and foreign tax credit claiming) apply; no tax classification election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 for corporations - VAT registration is mandatory from the commencement of any taxable business in Korea. A reduced simplified taxpayer status exists for small individual entrepreneurs with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but a Yuhan Hoesa corporation is not eligible for that simplification and must register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory zero-rating applies to goods actually exported from Korea when the export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service, the exporter is the Korean seller, and the goods physically leave Korea within the statutory period. Services supplied by a Korean resident to a non-resident are zero-rated when performed wholly outside Korea, foreign consideration is paid in foreign currency, and no Korean consumer/property/benefit is involved; qualifying digital electronic services exported to foreign customers may similarly be zero-rated if the foreign recipient and overseas use are evidenced. P2P platform services rendered to foreign lenders/borrowers offshore can qualify for zero-rating if the contractual and actual performance place is outside Korea, while pure lending interest provided to, or consumed by, Korean persons remains VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration via UNI-PASS (export clearance/shipment confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical departure of goods from Korean territory
  • Foreign exchange payment confirmation / Inward remittance certificate issued by a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank showing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice, loan contract, or platform services agreement matching the Korean exporter, foreign counterparty, consideration amount, and invoiced currency

All Korean VAT-registered suppliers must issue tax invoices; e-tax invoices are mandatory for substantially all B2B sales and must be digitally signed using an NTS-approved certificate and transmitted to NTS through the HomeTax interoperability API by the day after supply. The NTS assigns and validates each e-tax invoice number in near real-time for anti-fraud integrity. Korean businesses must also participate in the mandatory cash-receipt system for B2C payments where required. For exports, the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system performs export declaration clearance and data sharing with NTS, creating the fiscal evidence chain for zero-rated export claims.

NTS and Korea Customs Service cross-match export declarations, e-tax invoices, customs clearance records, and foreign exchange bank receipts. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days or the customs/fx documentation is incomplete, the zero-rating is disallowed and the supply is reassessed at 10% VAT with late-payment interest penalties. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims trigger penalty taxes up to 40% of the unpaid tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, retrospective clawbacks for up to five tax years, customs and foreign exchange regulatory fines, and under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, imprisonment or criminal fines for the entity's directors and responsible tax representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely liberalized convertible-currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. The won is fully convertible for bona fide current-account transactions including trade settlements, service receipts, and ordinary dividend remittances, subject to transaction evidence and reporting thresholds. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity investments, acquisition of domestic financial instruments, and loans, are not freely convertible without filing; residents and non-residents must submit advance or post-transaction FX reports through the Bank of Korea's Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). For a P2P micro-lending platform, the regulator's authorization under the Online Investment-Linked Financing Act (P2P Act) is a precondition for any cross-border funding conversion. Profit remittance is permissible after audited financials, corporate tax clearance, and formal dividend resolution; however, the borrowing entity must demonstrate that incoming foreign capital was properly registered and used for licensed lending activities. Although current transfer evidence is normally sufficient, the lending platform's 'high-risk financial' classification will trigger beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds scrutiny at the FX bank before conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected and deposited in a foreign exchange bank within the export collection period of six months from the shipment date unless a bank-approved extension is obtained. No statutory mandatory conversion percentage exists; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency proceeds in a designated foreign currency account, but any conversion into KRW must be executed through a licensed foreign exchange bank and reported through FEIS. If the six-month deadline is missed without an approved extension, the unrepatriated export receipts become a regulatory violation subject to BOK and MOEF enforcement. For the P2P platform, interest, fees, and commissions received from overseas borrowers or service users are treated as current-account service revenue and must also be received through the licensed FX bank with supporting contracts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), acting as the central bank and foreign exchange reporting authority, in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Financial Services Commission (FSC) for P2P platform licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Currenxie, and similar platforms are not licensed foreign exchange banks under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They cannot lawfully open domestic won accounts, access the BOK-Wire+ or KFTC clearing systems, comply with Korea's real-name financial transaction requirements, or service a licensed Korean P2P lending entity's treasury. They should be used only as ancillary downstream payout rails after KRW has been lawfully converted and remitted through a domestic Tier 1 bank. The primary clearing relationship must reside with a Korean commercial bank; reliance on an EMI without such a domestic bank would be a regulatory red flag and is not recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax rate on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion (progressive: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% from KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% from KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax payable, resulting in a combined top effective statutory rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory outbound dividend withholding tax is 20% under the Corporate Income Tax Act / Income Tax Act, plus a 2% local income tax surcharge (10% on the withholding tax), producing an aggregate 22% withholding on dividends paid by the Korean LLC to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced under a bilateral Double Tax Treaty (DTA). Some DTAs reduce the withholding rate to 10% or 15%; parent-subsidiary-type reductions may be available for qualifying shareholdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Triggering thresholds under the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA) and its Presidential Decree: Local File is mandatory for a Korean entity when cross-border related-party transactions in each category are at least KRW 5 billion for goods, KRW 2 billion for services, KRW 2 billion for intangibles, and KRW 3 billion for financing/loan transactions; Master File is required when total aggregate intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report is required for a multinational group with consolidated revenue above KRW 1 trillion. Even below these thresholds, a taxpayer must retain contemporaneous reasonable-basis documentation if claiming a refund or offset.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' statute, but the general anti-avoidance rule under LCITA Article 4 allows the NTS to recharacterize transactions lacking bona fide economic substance, and the Korean CFC regime under LCITA Article 17 will attribute the passive income of an offshore shell entity to the Korean shareholder unless the offshore entity shows real business substance (actual offices, employees, revenues and management). For a P2P micro-lending platform, the Korean LLC must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (lending decisions, loan-servicing algorithms, risk management, treasury and compliance) are performed from Korea with local staff and decision-makers; a bare holding or conduit arrangement without such substance will not qualify for treaty relief on outbound payments and may trigger punitive penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Act on the Punishment of Tax Evaders, Article 3: imprisonment for not more than 5 years or a criminal fine of not more than three times the amount of tax evaded, or both; in addition, the NTS will impose the 40% fraud penalty under the Framework Act on National Taxes and daily late-payment surcharge of 0.022%; the unpaid tax will bear interest and enforcement seizures over the corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Under the National Tax Collection Act Article 11, a director who actively participated in the evasion becomes secondarily liable for the company's unpaid national taxes if the corporate assets are insufficient; the director's personal bank accounts, real estate and vehicles may be seized. Criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years; and the NTS may place the director on the tax-delinquent blacklist and impose an overseas travel ban (immigration restriction) until the assessed tax is deposited.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: LCITA Article 4 (arm's-length adjustment), Article 14 (thin-capitalization interest disallowance), and Article 16 (transfer pricing documentation penalty). The NTS may recompute the taxable income, disallow excess interest, and impose an additional penalty of up to 20% of adjusted tax and an ad-valorem penalty for non-submission of documents; the statute of limitations for TP adjustments is 5 years (10 in fraud cases).

Director personal liability: If a director personally signed false TP documentation or knowingly directed interest payments to a shell entity, the court may classify the action as fraudulent tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and impose imprisonment; the director may also be civilly liable to the Korean entity and minority shareholders for the amount of tax, penalties, and interest through a derivative action. NTS may pierce the corporate veil in extreme cases and recover unpaid taxes from the director if the entity's use of the shell company was so integrated that the corporate structure was a sham; a travel ban and asset freeze are typical collection enforcement measures.
Unlicensed P2P Lending / AML and Financial Regulations

Statutory sanction: The Act on Online Investment-Linked Financing Facilities provides criminal penalties of imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to KRW 100 million; the Loan Business Act imposes the same criminal range for unlicensed lending; the Financial Services Commission may order immediate shutdown and cancellation of registration. Violations of the Proceeds of Crime Act may result in additional imprisonment of up to 5 years and confiscation of the laundered assets; foreign-exchange registration violations add to the exposure.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the director acted as the 'actual operator' (real controlling person). Directors face personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 5 years and massive fines; they are also subject to the confiscation of personal property traceable to the illicit proceeds; the FSC may ban them from all financial-industry positions; and customs/immigration authorities can impose a travel ban during investigation and prosecution. If the company is used purely as a shell, the court will treat the company as the director's alter ego and hold the director personally liable for all unpaid civil damages and refunds to defrauded P2P lenders.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) under the Commercial Act. However, the company must issue at least one share with a par value of KRW 100 or more. Capital may be contributed in cash or in kind, and must be fully paid at the time of incorporation. No escrow account or blocked deposit is mandated; however, if capital is contributed in kind, a court-appointed appraiser must verify the value.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable (no physical manufacturing or warehousing; however, if the company leases an office, it must comply with standard building fire safety regulations and obtain a business occupancy permit from the local fire department, which is typically handled by the building owner. No separate civil defense clearance is required for a service-based entity.)

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, via the Home Tax Service (HTS) electronic portal at www.hometax.go.kr

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) must first obtain a corporate registration number from the local court registry office when incorporating. Following incorporation, the entity must register for tax with the NTS through the Home Tax Service portal (hometax.go.kr) or at the local district tax office. The NTS assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as both the TIN and VAT ID. The foreign owners must submit the articles of incorporation, proof of registration, lease contract for the registered office, and a foreigner's ID number (or alien registration card) if a representative is a non-resident. A designated resident tax agent may be required for entities with no Korean place of business. The BRN is issued within a few business days after verification, and the entity is automatically registered for VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exemption for incorporated companies; all business entities are required to register for VAT prior to commencing business operations in Korea, irrespective of projected turnover. Among individuals and sole proprietors, a simplified taxpayer regime applies for annual supplies below KRW 80 million, but corporations remain standard VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Korean VAT Act, exports of goods are zero-rated (0% VAT) when the goods are delivered to a foreign buyer and leave South Korea. Qualifying export services and certain digital services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated under the VAT Act Enforcement Decree, provided the services are actually consumed overseas and paid for in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / Foreign Exchange Ledger issued by the Foreign Exchange Bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency from the overseas buyer

Since 2021, all VAT invoices must be issued electronically through the NTS Home Tax System. E-invoices are transmitted in real time via the national e-invoice clearance system (including API integration or the free HTS portal). The e-invoice must include the approved digital signature or national tax stamp. Failure to issue or report fraudulent e-invoices triggers digital anti-fraud controls and penalty surcharges.

Exports are subject to mandatory documentary review by the NTS and Korea Customs Service. Where the FIRC/IRC is missing or the foreign currency is not repatriated within the statutory deadline (generally 180 days from the export date, unless extended under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations), the NTS will deny the zero-rating, impose the 10% VAT plus additional tax penalty (typically 10% of the tax shortfall but up to 40% for fraudulent filings), and may impose a separate customs penalty. A retrospective clawback period of five years applies for fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Korean won is freely convertible for all current-account transactions, including services trade, dividends, and ordinary remittances, without prior approval, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and reported through authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital and financial account operations, however, remain subject to a negative-list system requiring submission of a foreign exchange transaction report to the Bank of Korea or the Ministry of Economy and Finance, with certain categories such as domestic money-market investment by non-residents and overseas direct investment above thresholds requiring pre-notification or approval. Repatriation of profits is permitted for foreign-invested companies upon submission of audited financial statements, a foreign investment enterprise registration certificate, and a remittance application evidencing payment of all corporate taxes. There are no surrender requirements for export proceeds, and balances can be held in foreign-currency accounts. In practice, banks apply strict beneficial-ownership verification and require documentary proof of the underlying commercial transaction, but convertibility for service-industry entities is otherwise unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export and service proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export or service performance; extensions may be granted by the authorized bank in justified cases. No mandatory conversion ratio applies because Korea abolished mandatory surrender requirements. Exporters may retain foreign currency earnings in domestic foreign-currency deposit accounts indefinitely, and conversion to KRW is optional.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. South Korea does not recognize foreign electronic money institutions as deposit-taking banks, and the Financial Services Commission and the Banking Act prohibit EMIs without a domestic banking license from offering KRW current or escrow accounts to local companies. Offshore fintech providers such as Wise and Stripe may be used only as ancillary payment collection gateways for cross-border customer receipts, but they cannot substitute for a tier-1 Korean bank for statutory operational, tax, or escrow clearing. Legal constraints under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act require strict licensing for any entity performing payment transaction brokerage; therefore, relying on a foreign EMI for treasury services exposes the Yuhan Hoesa to regulatory and liquidity risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the Yuhan Hoesa's designated foreign-currency or won account at a licensed foreign exchange bank in Korea, with the remittance field citing the appropriate Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) purpose-of-remittance code designated by the Bank of Korea for inward direct investment. The receiving bank issues a Foreign Exchange Bank Confirmation and capital verification certificate after verifying the share subscription agreement and booking the amount against the capital account. If the injection qualifies as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, the company must obtain a foreign-invested enterprise registration certificate from KOTRA or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy before the remittance and file the investment report with BOK. Subsequent profit repatriation must be supported by the prior year's audited financial statements and tax clearance.For services exports and vendor payments, use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border wire transfers with clear invoice references and FETA outbound purpose codes. Domestic KRW settlements are cleared through BOK-Wire+; USD and other currency settlements use correspondent bank networks such as CHIPS, Fedwire, and EURO1/TARGET2. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate; progressive tiers: 9% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 19% above KRW 200 million up to KRW 20 billion, 21% above KRW 20 billion up to KRW 300 billion, 24% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

15% Korean national withholding tax plus 1.5% local income tax surcharge, yielding a combined statutory rate of 16.5% for dividends payable to non-resident corporate or individual shareholders; bilateral tax treaties regularly reduce the rate on dividends to 10-15% under the limitation-on-benefits article.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Korean Local File preparation and filing with the corporate return is mandatory in any fiscal year in which cross-border intercompany transactions with foreign related parties exceed KRW 5 billion; a Master File is required when the Korean corporate group's annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion; separate transaction-by-transaction reporting is required for cross-border transactions with related parties in tax havens above KRW 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Korea enforces Economic Substance Doctrine through the International Tax Adjustment Act, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty context. For a Yuhan Hoesa engaged in outsourced virtual CFO services, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analyzed are the negotiation of client contracts, the making of pricing decisions, the journal entry review and financial statement approval, and the execution of steering-management decisions. These functions must be physically exercised by Korean employees or contractors physically present in the Korean LLC's ordinary business premises, using Korean bank accounts, software licenses, and local management minutes. If the entity operates as a letter-box with only nominee directors and all economic decisions are taken abroad, the NTS will disregard the Korean company as tax resident, deny treaty benefits, and impute income to the non-resident parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Sham Transaction / Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may deny the entity's corporate personality for tax purposes, invalidate treaty withholding relief under the Korean DTA General Anti-Abuse Rule, recharacterize all income as taxable to the ultimate foreign parent, and impose the 40% fraudulent understatement addition on the Korean company plus daily delinquency interest and business registration cancellation; the Regional Tax Office may also impose a 20% computation penalty for failure to maintain books and records.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated statutory auditor may have the corporate veil pierced under the Korean Commercial Act and the Framework Act on National Tax: personal liability extends to unpaid corporate taxes, penalties, and collected interest to the extent the Director signed fraudulent declarations or abused limited liability. Civilly, assets may be attached and bank accounts frozen; criminally, the Director may be prosecuted under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine of up to 500 million KRW; NTS routinely requests departure prohibition (international travel ban) for the Representative Director, blacklisting him in the immigration control database.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Adjustment Act, the NTS is authorized to redetermine the price at arm's length, increase the taxable income, reclassify the payment as constructive dividend, and assess the difference together with a 20%-40% penalty on understated tax; the adjustment may also trigger withholding tax on the reclassified dividend including 10% WHT tax. Failure to submit the required Local File for a transaction above the KRW 5 billion threshold adds an explicit documentation penalty of up to KRW 300 million.

Director personal liability: The Director who negotiates and signs the non-arm's-length intercompany agreement is jointly secondarily liable for the adjusted tax and penalty where the transfer pricing documentation was intentionally false. If the Director is found by the prosecutor to have knowingly prepared false financial records for the purpose of tax evasion, criminal liability reaches 5 years' imprisonment or 500 million KRW fine; in addition, civil restitution claims from the Korean company's creditors may attach the Director's personal assets, and the NTS may impose a blacklist and travel ban pending full settlement.
Failure to Withhold and Divert Outbound Dividends/Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Tax, the Korean corporation is the withholding agent. For failure to withhold, the NTS orders payment of the unpaid withholding amount together with a negligence penalty of 10% (or 20% where no return is filed) and interest surcharges computed daily. If the misclassification is fraudulent, the 40% fraud addition applies on both corporate tax and withheld tax; the tax office may also refuse the deduction of the underlying expense and reclassify it as fringe benefit.

Director personal liability: The Representative Director and the responsible finance officer are personally and primarily liable for collection of the unpaid withholding tax when the company does not pay it: personal assets are placed under seizure, bank accounts are frozen, and the Director may be barred from foreign travel by the NTS. Criminal prosecution under Article 10 punishable for willful non-withholding can result in imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine up to 200 million KRW, and in aggravated cases the court may also order dissolution of the company and disqualification of the Director.

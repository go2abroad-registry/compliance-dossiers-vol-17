None under the Commercial Act. The Yuhan Hoesa has no statutory minimum paid-in capital. The Articles of Incorporation must state each member's contribution value and the method of contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to the core digital operation. No industrial civil-defense clearance is required because there is no physical factory or warehouse, but a simple fire drill log and designated evacuation officer are required under the Fire Safety Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: NTS HomeTax (https://www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident establishing a Yuhan Hoesa (Korean limited liability company) must obtain a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN; the 10-digit taxpayer identification number) from the NTS district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business. Procedure: (1) complete Korean court registration of the Yuhan Hoesa and, if needed, appoint a domestic statutory tax representative for the foreign shareholders; (2) before or within 20 days after business commencement, file the Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office or through HomeTax, attaching the certificate of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, Korean office lease or rental proof, foreign director identification and apostilled corporate documents, and a tax representative appointment power of attorney; (3) receive the 10-digit BRN, which also serves as the VAT registration number, corporate income tax payer ID, and employer withholding tax ID; (4) register in HomeTax for electronic tax invoice issuance, cash receipt card issuance, and direct debit VAT payment. A foreign supplier with no Korean place of business that is nevertheless required to charge Korean VAT on services used in Korea must register through a Korean tax representative on HomeTax and obtain a BRN before issuing tax invoices.

No monetary threshold. VAT registration is mandatory at business commencement for every business operator supplying taxable goods or services in Korea. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a liberalized but notification-driven foreign exchange system. The Korean won is convertible for most current account transactions, including trade settlements and service/business payments, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA). Capital account operations, however, remain subject to a negative-list system requiring prior or post reporting, bank confirmation, or registration depending on the character of the flow. Inward foreign direct investment must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) and registered with a designated foreign exchange bank or Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) before capital is remitted. Dividends, royalties, and legitimate current account payments are generally remittable without substantial restriction after tax clearance and submission of the supporting transactional documents. A statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in the sense that exporters must collect proceeds within a stipulated period; however, mandatory conversion of foreign currency into won is limited to specified service categories and crisis measures. The Bank of Korea may temporarily require surrender during external stress, but in ordinary circumstances exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts. Practical frictions appear in foreign exchange bank due diligence, requiring underlying contracts, real-use certificates, and customs declarations, rather than absolute convertibility denial.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 14 of FETA and related notices, exporters must collect export proceeds within 6 months from the date of customs clearance unless a longer period is approved by the Bank of Korea or a designated foreign exchange bank. In ordinary conditions, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for broadly eligible export receipts; companies may maintain proceeds in foreign currency deposit accounts at domestic banks. However, the Minister of Economy and Finance, upon consultation with the Bank of Korea, may impose a temporary surrender obligation of up to 50% of export receipts during a balance-of-payments crisis. For cross-border MCN service revenue from TikTok, the bank may classify the receipts as service export income and require invoices, settlement statements, and proof of the underlying creator representation contract.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) as central bank; Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as principal foreign exchange policy authority; delegated to designated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment must be reported under FIPA to KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank before the remittance is made. The investor should open a foreign-investor capital deposit account at a Tier-1 Korean bank, wire the capital via SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code for investment funds, and ensure the remitter name matches the actual foreign investor. After the remittance is received, the bank issues the foreign investment registration certificate. Capital verification is completed by reconciling the foreign investment report, remittance advice, bank receipt confirmation, foreign investment registration certificate, and the subsequent corporate registration filing with the Korean court registry. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate international transactions with foreign related parties per fiscal year triggers mandatory preparation and filing of integrated Transfer Pricing Local File and Master File with the National Tax Service; Country-by-Country Report filing is separately required when the ultimate parent group has consolidated annual revenue above KRW 1 trillion

Economic substance scope: Korea does not operate a formal ESR or CIGA filing regime, but economic substance is essential through the related-controlled-tax-transaction arm's-length doctrine, the beneficial-ownership requirement for treaty relief, and the broad anti-abuse and substance-over-form rules under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act; for MCN agency activities, the core income-generating activities of creator scouting, contract negotiation, campaign management, payment collection, sponsorship sourcing, and platform analytics must be performed by Korean decision-makers and employees in Korea; a Korean entity without local staff, operational control, or profit-paying capacity is treated as a conduit shell, its transactions are recharacterized at arm's length, and its members lose limited-liability protection for resulting tax fraud

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Omission of Offshore Creator Payout Revenues

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine not exceeding three times the illegal tax evaded; National Tax Service adds a 40% fraudulent understatement penalty on the omitted corporate income tax, a 0.022% daily late-payment interest charge on the deficiency, and a 10% local income tax surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instigate the offshore revenue concealment are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax and penalties through veil piercing; the National Tax Service may issue a travel ban or blacklisting order, attach personal bank accounts and real property, and criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Missing Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of International Taxes Act Articles 4 and 5 permit the National Tax Service to reassess controlled transactions at arm's length; failure to present required integrated documentation triggers a documentation penalty and a 10% penalty on the unsubstantiated understated tax base, or 40% where false documents are submitted; daily 0.022% late-payment interest is added to the resulting deficiency

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false transfer prices, backdated agreements, or sham invoices for services never rendered are personally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties under the principle of punitive veil piercing; civil asset restitution includes freezing personal property, and the criminal route under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act may impose imprisonment for up to 5 years in addition to fines and travel restrictions
Shell Entity and Treaty Abuse for Dividend Under-Withholding

Statutory sanction: Treaty avoidance is rebutted under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act Article 4, the Framework Act on Taxes Article 47-3, and limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose-test clauses in Korea's tax treaties; the National Tax Service re-imposes the full 22% withholding, adds a 40% fraud penalty, accrues daily interest, and exposes officers to criminal fraud sanctions when the conduit structure is used to evade tax

Director personal liability: A director who designs or operates the conduit shareholder chain cannot hide behind the LLC corporate veil; the National Tax Service will pierce the entity to collect unpaid withholding tax and penalties from personal assets, will impose a travel ban after certifying the director as a bad-faith tax debtor, and criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, additionally revoking the company's access to future tax clearance and cross-border payment licenses

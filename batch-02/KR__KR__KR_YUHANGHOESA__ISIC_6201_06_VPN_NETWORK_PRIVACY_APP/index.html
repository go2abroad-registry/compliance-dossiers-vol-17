Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act does not impose a minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Yuhan Hoesa. Capital may be set at any amount in the Articles of Incorporation. Contributions must be paid in full by each member before incorporation registration; no statutory blocked escrow account is required, but all capital must be deposited into a temporary corporate bank account and remain in the company's possession. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr); export declarations and customs clearance are processed through the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic system.

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed Yuhan Hoesa incorporated under the Commercial Act, the domestic representative must file a Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office (Head of District Tax Office) via HomeTax or in person within 20 days after business commencement. The applicant must submit the corporate registration certificate from the Korean court registry, articles of incorporation, board resolution appointing the domestic representative, lease agreement or occupancy certificate for the registered business address, and a passport or identification card of the representative. Because the entity is a foreign-invested company, the shareholders should also obtain a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and submit the certificate of foreign exchange bank dealing. The NTS issues a single 10-digit Business Registration Number which simultaneously serves as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number. A non-resident foreign corporation without a Korean place of business does not use the ordinary Yuhan Hoesa registration path; it instead registers through a domestic tax representative as a foreign business operator under the Value-Added Tax Act, providing the foreign entity name, overseas address, description of business, global head office details, and the tax representative's Korean information. In the VPN SaaS context, the Korean Yuhan Hoesa is the principal VAT registrant and will carry its own Korean Business Registration Number for all domestic supplies and zero-rated export supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalized current account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, with full convertibility for trade in goods, services, and routine profit remittances. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections, intercompany loans, and portfolio investments, are generally free but subject to a declaration or reporting requirement through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). The Korean won (KRW) is not a freely deliverable offshore currency; non-resident accounts and offshore won trading are heavily restricted. Surrender requirements apply to export proceeds: all foreign currency earned from exports must be either repatriated or deposited into a domestic foreign currency account within 18 months from the date of acquisition, with no mandatory conversion into KRW for proceeds held in foreign currency accounts. However, converting to KRW is required if the entity seeks to fund local payroll and KRW-denominated operating expenses. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted without prior approval, but a foreign exchange bank must verify the source of funds and the underlying tax clearance (e.g., withholding tax certificate under the Korea Tax Treaty). A material hurdle is that any capital contribution exceeding USD 1 million equivalent must be reported to the delegated foreign exchange bank within 30 days of the remittance, and for FDI, a prior notification to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy is required under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Overall, the regime is compliant and functional for legitimate SaaS/network security businesses, but any use of virtual assets, crypto settlement, or privacy wallets will trigger enhanced scrutiny and likely rejection of the banking application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 14 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Enforcement Decree, export proceeds must be collected and repatriated to a domestic foreign currency account or brought into Korea within 18 months from the date of export (with extension possible only for exceptional causes such as arbitration or litigation). There is no mandatory conversion rate for export proceeds; however, if the company needs KRW liquidity, it must sell the foreign currency to any licensed foreign exchange bank at the prevailing market rate. For digital services (SaaS, VPN subscriptions, app sales) treated as exports of intellectual property/software, the 18-month collection period applies, and the service provider must register the transaction with the designated foreign exchange bank using the appropriate revenue code (e.g., case number under 'Earnings from Software' – code 521100). Failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers administrative fines, retroactive tax assessments, and customs investigation referrals.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) operated by the Korea Exchange Bank (KEB Hana) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Low / Non-Viable. South Korean regulations under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Act on Registration of Financial Businesses require any entity providing foreign exchange services or payment settlement to be licensed as a 'Foreign Exchange Business Operator' or registered as an 'Electronic Financial Business Operator' with the Financial Services Commission (FSC). Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury) do not hold a Korean foreign exchange license and are legally barred from maintaining on-shore KRW settlement accounts or providing local IBANs/routing numbers. They can only serve Korean entities as a receiving conduit for offshore USD/EUR, but cannot facilitate KRW payouts to Korean vendors or employees. Therefore, the company must maintain a primary relationship with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. For global collections, the company may use Stripe or Paymentwall as payment gateways that route settlement funds to the company's designated domestic bank account via SWIFT. However, these gateways do not replace the domestic banking relationship; they merely act as payment aggregators collecting customer funds offshore and remitting net proceeds as a single wire. The bank will require a business contract and gateway settlement statements to verify the source of funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 50 billion cross-border related-party transactions with KRW 100 billion revenue threshold for Master File preparation; KRW 10 billion cross-border related-party transaction threshold for Local File preparation; smaller related-party transactions must still be disclosed on the annual corporate tax return schedule and retained in contemporaneous documentation.

Economic substance scope: Although South Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the National Tax Service will test economic substance under the substance-over-form doctrine, Article 52 of the Corporate Tax Act, the international anti-avoidance rules in LEIITA, and treaty limitation-on-benefits/principal-purpose tests. For a VPN network SaaS company, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as contract execution, network management, encryption development, server control and customer acceptance must be conducted by employees in Korea, not merely routed through a paper Korean entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Article 47-3 of the Framework Act on National Taxes: 20% negligence penalty for late/non-filing, 10% understatement penalty (30% if understatement exceeds KRW 300 million), plus daily delinquency charge of 0.03%; Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million.

Director personal liability: Under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act, directors who intentionally or negligently cause the entity to evade tax are jointly liable to the tax authority for the amount of tax plus penalties; the director's name may be placed on the National Tax Service's overdue/blacklist registry, departure from Korea may be restricted, and fraudulent concealment of assets to evade collection is separately indictable.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under LEIITA, the NTS may adjust taxable income and increase the tax paid plus a 20% transfer pricing negligence penalty where bookkeeping documentation is incomplete; excessive interest or royalty payments are denied and 22% withholding tax is additionally collected on the disguised dividend or constructive remittance; non-filing of transfer pricing schedules triggers separate document penalties.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Commercial Act Article 401 permits recovery of additional tax, penalties and interest from directors; foreign directors with expatriate status may be subject to travel bans under the National Tax Collection Act; repeated intentional evasion through abusive transfer pricing can result in criminal indictment and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may apply Article 52 of the Corporate Tax Act and LEIITA general anti-abuse rules to disregard the entity or recharacterise transactions; denial of DTA relief under the principal purpose test, repayment of taxes, and additional underreporting penalties up to 20% (or 30% when the understated tax exceeds KRW 300 million); criminal sanctions under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act if the substance failure is part of a fraudulent scheme.

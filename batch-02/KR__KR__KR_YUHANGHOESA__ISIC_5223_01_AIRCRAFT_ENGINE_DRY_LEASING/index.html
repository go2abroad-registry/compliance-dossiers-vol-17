Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or a commercial post-office mailbox is not permissible for a Korean aircraft-engine dry-leasing Yuhan Hoesa. The National Tax Service (NTS) and District Court registries require a real place of business in Korea where corporate books, records, and management activities are kept. A virtual or mailbox address alone is rejected as a false place of business, and Korean banks will decline corporate account opening under the CDD rules in the Act on the Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Transaction Information. A serviced office with a dedicated lease agreement, meeting rooms, a reception desk, and a local staffing presence is acceptable, provided it is not presented as a purely mail-forwarding point. The registered address must be included in the Articles of Association and in the commercial registration application.

None. The Commercial Act imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa. Subscribers determine the capital amount in the Articles of Incorporation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr); local administrative oversight by the district tax office having jurisdiction over the Korean registered head office; foreign investment reporting by KOTRA or a domestic foreign-exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor forming a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) must first complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act at the local court registry and, where applicable, file a foreign investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. After incorporation, the entity must apply for business registration with the district tax office or through HomeTax within 20 days of commencing business. The application must include the Certificate of Registered Matters, Articles of Incorporation, evidence of registered head-office premises, proof of foreign-investor identity and capital contribution, and authorization of the local tax representative when the foreign shareholder is not a Korean resident. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number, which is simultaneously the VAT registration number, the corporate income tax file number, and the main identifier for electronic tax invoices, customs declarations, and withholding tax reporting. No separate VAT identification number is issued. If the foreign investor carries on activities in Korea through a branch that is not a domestic Yuhan Hoesa, a separate foreign corporation tax registration must also be made with NTS; however, the default Korean entity in this payload is a resident domestic corporation and uses the standard business registration route.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a corporation. Any person supplying goods or services in Korea is a VAT taxable person, and a Yuhan Hoesa is a general taxpayer required to register for VAT from the date it begins business regardless of turnover. The KRW 80 million simplified taxpayer threshold is restricted to qualifying natural persons and is not available to a corporate Yuhan Hoesa.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods definitively exported from Korea under a customs export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service. A 0% rate may also apply to qualifying services supplied to a non-resident foreign customer and consumed or used outside Korea, including the cross-border dry leasing of turbofan aircraft engines to a non-resident lessee, provided the engine is delivered to or used in international aviation or outside the Korean customs territory and the lease consideration is received in foreign currency. The lessor must be able to substantiate the foreign status of the lessee, the actual removal of the leased asset from Korea, the performance of the service abroad, and the repatriation of foreign sales proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service)
  • Commercial Invoice describing the aircraft engine, foreign lessee, lease term, and consideration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving the engine or component physically left Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of lease rentals in convertible foreign currency
  • Executed aircraft engine dry-lease agreement
  • Packing List and engine log or aviation asset certificate where applicable
  • Proof of installation of the engine on an internationally operated aircraft or other evidence of foreign use outside Korea

All tax invoices for supplies made by a Yuhan Hoesa must be issued as electronic tax invoices to the counterparty and must be transmitted to the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice clearing platform no later than the next day after issuance. Transmission is made through the NTS standard API, an NTS-approved EDI/ASP service provider, or direct entry into HomeTax. The NTS platform performs real-time central validation, duplicate detection, input-output reconciliation, and permanent archival. Although South Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamping regime equivalent to the Italian SDI, the electronic tax invoice must contain a unique invoice number, the registered business number of the issuer and recipient, an NTS-validated electronic seal, and full invoice metadata. A general corporation may not issue ordinary paper tax invoices except in limited cases prescribed by the Act, such as temporary system failure authorized by the NTS.

Export zero-rating claims are provisionally accepted but are subject to mandatory audit scrutiny by the NTS and Korea Customs Service. If the zero-rate claim is not proven by the required export documentation, foreign-currency receipt evidence, and overseas-use evidence, the NTS will assess output VAT at the 10% headline rate, deny corresponding input tax credits, and impose additional tax of 10% for negligent understatement or up to 40% for intentional dishonesty, plus daily delay interest on any refund clawback. Fraudulent or fictitious export claims are subject to criminal penalties, fines, and imprisonment under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including retroactive reassessment and immediate set-off of unpaid amounts against VAT refunds or other tax credits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a largely convertible current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, while the capital account remains subject to declaration and monitoring requirements. The Korean won is freely convertible for trade-related payments, service fees, lease rentals, dividends, and interest transfers when supported by genuine underlying documentation. For the aircraft engine dry leasing entity, inbound foreign-direct-investment capital and outbound lease payments to non-resident lessors will be treated as reportable FX transactions, not as pre-approval-restricted items in the ordinary course, provided the beneficiary bank receives the required digital FX transaction reports and the entity maintains a valid foreign exchange registration. Capital account transactions, including direct equity subscription by non-resident shareholders, must be declared to the designated foreign exchange bank and, where applicable, filed through the MOEF and Bank of Korea information systems. Profit and lease rental remittances historically require proof of payment, tax withholding certificates, and the absence of outstanding tax arrears. The entity must retain all underlying lease agreements, engine title documents, and bank remittance confirmations to satisfy foreign exchange verification reviews. Korea does not mandate full surrender of export or service proceeds, but all settlement proceeds must enter Korea through a licensed foreign exchange bank and be either converted to won or held in a foreign currency account within the permitted statutory window. The practical constraints are therefore documentary and administrative rather than absolute convertibility barriers, but enhanced due diligence for the aviation leasing sector can add frictions in the form of extended bank compliance review before first remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Regulations, export-related foreign currency proceeds, including aircraft engine dry leasing service or lease proceeds deemed as exported services, must be received within one year from the underlying export declaration or service contract date unless an extension is granted by the relevant foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio requiring the full surrender of the proceeds into Korean won, but the proceeds must be collected into a licensed foreign currency account or converted through a domestic Tier-1 bank. The entity must ensure the settlement invoice states the correct purpose code and that remittance correspondences are retained for a minimum of five years for MOEF, Bank of Korea, and Korea Customs Service audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as joint FX regulatory authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and foreign fintech payment institutions have Low viability as the sole banking gateway for this South Korean entity. Under the Banking Act and Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-resident-licensed EMI without a Korean banking license cannot legally maintain a primary settlement account in the name of the Korean legal entity, cannot issue Korean IBAN-equivalent virtual accounts, and cannot serve as the receiving bank for statutory capital subscription or foreign direct investment registration. Such EMIs may only serve as auxiliary wallet rails for minor operational expenses if the underlying funds are first converted through a licensed domestic Korean bank, which is generally not recommended for treasury control. The mandatory account relationship for capital verification, FX declaration, and cross-border lease payment routing must reside with a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank holding a foreign exchange business license.

The Korean supplier must maintain contemporaneous proof that the digital content is supplied to an offshore counterparty, that the economic use occurs abroad, and that the consideration is receivable in foreign currency. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea operates a largely liberalized but prudentially supervised foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, administered jointly by the Bank of Korea, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and authorized foreign exchange banks. The won is fully convertible for nearly all current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, royalties, and film post-production fee remittances, with no surrender requirement or mandatory conversion to local currency for export proceeds. However, capital account transactions, including direct equity injections, foreign borrowings, and outbound investments, are subject to mandatory reporting and, in certain cases, registration or approval prerequisites. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are permitted as current account items once the statutory corporate income tax and surtax withholdings are settled, but the underlying investment and the relevant foreign currency purchase must be traceable through a registered foreign exchange bank to avoid misclassification as a capital account outflow. Practical hurdles include the need for a dedicated Foreign Currency Account or a Won-currency Corporate Account with an authorized foreign exchange bank, strict documentary support for the underlying transaction, and the requirement to file a Foreign Exchange Transaction Report for each inbound FDI remittance. Non-resident shareholders can maintain ownership, but inward capital injections must be substantiated with the Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) registration certificate or the foreign exchange bank's inward transaction report to satisfy the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The overall convertibility for the proposed AI subtitle post-production studio is high for genuine trade and service payments, but penalties for unreported capital movements are severe, including criminal liability, making disciplined bank-driven reporting the only compliant treasury path.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, export proceeds must be collected, received, and settled through an authorized foreign exchange bank within six months from the date of shipment, unless an extension is granted by the bank or the Ministry of Economy and Finance for valid business reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won; exporters may retain all foreign currency receipts in a designated foreign currency account at a domestic bank, or convert to won at market rates. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline without an approved extension triggers enforcement measures, potential administrative fines, and adverse risk classification for the entity's future banking relationships.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise or Stripe Treasury have extremely limited viability as primary corporate banking rails for a Korean-resident entity. South Korea's regulatory framework requires that all foreign exchange transactions be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks, which are domestically licensed commercial banks. Foreign EMIs lacking a domestic banking license cannot provide Korean won current accounts, issue Foreign Exchange Transaction Reports, or support Foreign Investment Promotion Act capital entrant registrations. They may be used only as supplementary invoicing or payment collection gateways in the entity's offshore sales channels, and even then the underlying settlement must ultimately be moved through an authorized Korean bank for compliance with the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Consequently, the recommended treasury architecture must maintain core cash management, FX execution, and capital verification entirely within the Tier-1 domestic banking system.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise or Stripe Treasury have extremely limited viability as primary corporate banking rails for a Korean-resident entity. South Korea's regulatory framework requires that all foreign exchange transactions be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks, which are domestically licensed commercial banks. Foreign EMIs lacking a domestic banking license cannot provide Korean won current accounts, issue Foreign Exchange Transaction Reports, or support Foreign Investment Promotion Act capital entrant registrations. They may be used only as supplementary invoicing or payment collection gateways in the entity's offshore sales channels, and even then the underlying settlement must ultimately be moved through an authorized Korean bank for compliance with the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Consequently, the recommended treasury architecture must maintain core cash management, FX execution, and capital verification entirely within the Tier-1 domestic banking system.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Commercial Act imposes no minimum paid-in capital for a Yuhan Hoesa; there is no statutory blocked escrow account. The Articles of Incorporation must state the total amount of each member contribution and the monetary amount per contribution unit on an equal basis. Cash contributions must be deposited into a domestic bank account established in the proposed corporate name before the registration filing, and the bank's contribution receipt must be submitted to the registry. In-kind contributions require an independent valuation certificate and a member resolution. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; operational electronic portals: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr) for business registration, corporate income tax, VAT, e-Tax Invoice transmission; Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr) for export customs clearance and export declaration records.

E-government filing portal

For the Korean Yuhan Hoesa domestic entity: (1) incorporate the Yuhan Hoesa and procure the corporate seal certificate and register with the Commercial Registry; (2) file the Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office through HomeTax or the local tax office; (3) receive the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as the entity's Corporate Taxpayer Identification Number, VAT registration number, and e-Tax Invoice issuer ID; (4) obtain the Korea Customs Service exporter clearance identifier by registering the business number in UNI-PASS, enabling electronic filing of export declarations; (5) register bank account details with a Korean foreign-exchange bank for settlement of export proceeds; (6) if the Korean entity is not yet formed and a foreign non-resident enterprise is supplying goods or services into Korea directly, the non-resident enterprise must appoint a Korean tax representative, submit the foreign registration certificate, articles of incorporation or equivalent, passport or corporate identity documents, a completed application through HomeTax, and obtain a foreign resident business registration number for VAT purposes. The Yuhan Hoesa itself, being locally incorporated, follows the domestic registration route regardless of foreign ownership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Korean Corporate Tax Act does not provide a check-the-box or other domestic entity classification election. A Yuhan Hoesa is a Korean domestic corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no hybrid mismatch election exists. Foreign classification of the entity under a treaty jurisdiction does not override Korean domestic entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a Korean domestic legal entity; VAT registration is mandatory at business commencement. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean establishment, any VAT-taxable supply in Korea creates a mandatory registration obligation; the simplified taxpayer threshold available to Korean micro-entrepreneurs is not available to foreign legal entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21 of the Korean Value Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated where the seller exports to a foreign buyer, the goods are cleared through Korea Customs Service export declaration, title and risk transfer overseas, and payment is received from a non-resident in foreign currency. The zero-rated transaction does not bear output VAT, and creditable input VAT from related purchases is refundable by the National Tax Service. For the current passive electronic component distribution activity, each inventory sale to a non-Korean customer must be physically exported from Korea and supported by the documentation bundle below; sales of the same inventory to a Korean domestic customer are subject to standard 10% VAT.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Customs Declaration (UNI-PASS export clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or multimodal transport document) evidencing physical dispatch from Korea to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Korean Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and HS classification/weight/value records matching the export declaration

VAT-registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system for supplies to other businesses. The e-Tax Invoice data is transmitted to HomeTax and centrally matched by the NTS; large ERP systems may transmit via NTS API or EDI interfaces. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance digital stamp model: the anti-fraud system relies on mandatory issuance and transmission deadlines, unique e-Tax Invoice serial numbers, real-time declaration comparison of sales and purchase ledger information, and penalty surcharges for non-issuance, late issuance, or false issuance.

National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service jointly audit zero-rating substantiation. If any mandatory document is missing, inconsistent, or the export proceeds are not collected through Korean banking channels within the 180-day deadline, the zero-rating is retrospectively disallowed; the taxpayer is assessed 10% VAT on the affected supply, plus penalty surcharges under the Framework Act for National Taxes (generally 10% to 40% of the underpaid VAT, with higher surcharges for intentional concealment). False or forged export declarations can trigger criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, customs export penalties, seizure or denial of exporter privileges, and clawback of any VAT refund with interest. Re-importation or diversion of zero-rated goods into Korean domestic commerce also re-exposes the goods to import duty and VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; capital account transactions are liberalized but subject to reporting requirements and a real-demand identity. Export proceeds must be collected within one year but there is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio; proceeds can be retained in foreign currency settlement accounts at a domestic licensed foreign exchange bank. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted provided the remitting bank verifies the prior filing of the foreign investment report, confirms absence of Korean tax arrears, and receives a certified copy of the distribution resolution. Any capital outflow that lacks a genuine underlying trade or investment contract may be recharacterized as disguised capital flight and denied by the bank. The MOEF and BOK jointly restrict offshore KRW liquidity, and non-resident access to onshore won is limited to legitimate trade-invoiced amounts. A corporate treasurer must therefore maintain a complete documentary trail of each transaction, carefully separate current account and capital account flows, and report all FX position changes in the Foreign Exchange Information Reporting System (FEIRS) to avoid administrative penalties and reputational damage. Central bank oversight is active, with strict audit powers exercised through the Bank of Korea's examination functions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 16 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters must receive export proceeds within one year from the date of export declaration; extensions require MOEF approval. There is no mandatory conversion into KRW and no surrender ratio; exporters may retain funds at a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank in foreign currency, but they must submit a settlement receipt report to the bank and preserve trade documentation for five years. Failure to collect proceeds within the statutory timeline or to report the settlement constitutes an administrative violation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to provide foreign-exchange-linked deposit, escrow, or settlement services to Korean resident entities. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act confers FX banking powers exclusively on licenced domestic banks, and EMIs holding only e-money licenses in their host country cannot clear KRW or hold customer funds for Korean corporations. A fintech EMI may function solely as an introducing agent or software technology vendor to a domestic bank, never as an independent treasury rail. Therefore, cross-border treasury liquidity must be built on Tier-1 Korean bank core custody and Swift-based clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans are routed through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank using the Foreign Exchange Information Reporting System (FEIRS). The investor must first submit a capital transaction report to the bank or MOEF covering the amount and purpose code (e.g., foreign direct investment code 701200 for a stake in a Korean entity). The funds are credited to a dedicated foreign investment deposit account, then converted into won or retained in foreign currency in a basic operational account. Capital verification is accomplished by presenting the bank's certificate of registration, a company registry application, and evidence of the deposit balance to the Korean court registry. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Fraudulent Omission

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, intentional tax evasion carries imprisonment with labor for up to 2 years or a fine up to three times the evaded tax; additionally the Framework Act on National Taxes imposes a 40% fraud penalty plus daily late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or knowingly approved the sham filing lose the corporate veil shield and are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and interest; they may also be blacklisted by the National Tax Service, subject to travel bans, and face criminal imprisonment.
Failure to Withhold on Outbound Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the unwithheld tax, plus an additional tax under the withholding provisions and daily late-payment interest; criminal penalties apply for intentional failure to collect and pay national taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for treasury and disbursements are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax and surcharges; in cases of deliberate diversion, they face asset seizure, exit bans, and prosecution as co-perpetrators of tax evasion.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority can deny treaty benefits, re-characterise the transaction under the statutory anti-abuse rule, reassess income at full statutory rates, impose a 40% unfaithful return penalty, and refer the matter for criminal investigation under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the conduit may face civil restitution of all underpaid taxes and penalties, pierce-the-veil personal liability as persons responsible for management, blacklisting and travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion if they are shown to have directed the artificial arrangement.

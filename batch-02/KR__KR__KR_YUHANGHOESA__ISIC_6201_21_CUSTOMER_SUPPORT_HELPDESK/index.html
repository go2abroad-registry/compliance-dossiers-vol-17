Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic portal for business registration, electronic tax invoices, filing, and payments; Korea Customs Service (KCS) via the UNIPASS system for export declaration and customs clearance evidence.

E-government filing portal

As a Korean Yuhan Hoesa incorporated under the Commercial Act, the entity is a domestic corporation and must register for its 10-digit business registration number, which serves as the unified corporate tax and VAT identification number. The representative director must file the business registration application with the responsible NTS district tax office within 20 days from the commencement of business. Required supporting documents include the articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, commercial registration certificate, lease agreement or title deed evidencing the head-office premises, identification of the representative director, and, if any shareholder is a non-resident, the shareholder corporate certificate and passport of the authorized foreign signatory. Non-resident shareholders may be represented under a power-of-attorney, and no minimum paid-in capital is required under Korean law for the registration itself. Upon completion, NTS issues the business registration number through HomeTax, which is simultaneously recognized for Corporate Tax, Value-Added Tax, and Withholding Tax purposes. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Korean domestic business establishment that supplies electronic services or digital goods to Korean recipients, the entity must instead complete the mandatory simplified VAT registration under the Value-Added Tax Act through HomeTax; this generates a separate simplified VAT registration number and requires the appointment of a tax representative or statutory tax agent in Korea.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean tax classification is not elective. A Yuhan Hoesa is automatically treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level worldwide income taxation under the Corporate Tax Act. There is no check-the-box election, no pass-through treatment analogous to a US LLC, and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election. A group-wide consolidated corporate tax regime exists under the Corporate Tax Act, but it is available only to qualifying domestic corporate groups and is not an elective entity classification mechanism for a standalone LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold. Any person, including a domestic corporation or a foreign non-resident entity, that commences taxable supply activity in Korea is mandatorily required to register for VAT. Foreign non-resident suppliers of electronic services or digital content to Korean consumers are separately required to complete simplified VAT registration without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT treatment is available under the Value-Added Tax Act for goods physically exported from Korea and for services supplied to non-residents or foreign persons where the service is deemed to be consumed or used outside Korea under the statutory place-of-supply rules. For the Helpdesk SaaS cloud business, export zero-rating will apply where the customers are foreign non-residents, the contract is executed from the Korean entity's foreign-facing commercial channel, the assistance and software platform are delivered from cloud infrastructure located outside Korea or used exclusively outside Korea, and the consideration is received in foreign currency. The supplier must demonstrate that the recipient is a non-resident and that the service is not actually consumed in Korea. Services supplied from Korea to Korean residents or performed in Korea for a foreign counterparty do not qualify for zero-rating unless a specific statutory exception applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration Certificate issued by Korea Customs Service through the UNIPASS system for physical goods, evidencing the exportation of goods and matching the commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment of the goods from Korea to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Purchase Order, and executed international sales contract showing the foreign non-resident counterparty
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • For digital helpdesk and SaaS services: service contract with declared non-resident customer, customer identity and registration documents, cloud service usage logs and server location records, IP access evidence, and detailed billing and payment settlement records demonstrating offshore consumption

Every VAT-registered business in Korea is required to issue an electronic tax invoice for taxable supplies made to other registered businesses. The electronic tax invoice must be created through the HomeTax system and transmitted to NTS, using the statutory OpenAPI or EDI channel, by the day following the date the electronic tax invoice is issued. For certain self-billed electronic tax invoices, including those issued by foreign non-resident simplified VAT registrants, transmission occurs on the statutory due date associated with the VAT return. Korea does not operate a real-time pre-clearance digital stamping or fiscal clearance platform; NTS instead performs near-real-time reconciliation of transmitted electronic tax invoices against VAT returns, and each transmitted electronic invoice receives a digital verification number that evidences NTS receipt and integrity.

NTS can challenge an export zero-rating claim in VAT audits and may require production of all supporting export and foreign-exchange documentation for each zero-rated transaction. If the documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, NTS will reclassify the supply as domestic taxable at the 10% standard VAT rate, assess the unpaid VAT, impose penalty surcharges under the Value-Added Tax Act for underreporting, false tax invoices, and negligent non-payment, and add late-payment interest at the statutory rate of 0.03% per day on the unpaid amount. When zero-rated input VAT refunds were previously paid, NTS will issue a reassessment and clawback the refund with these surcharges and interest. In cases of deliberate concealment, forgery, or fraudulent export or remittance documentation, the matter can be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exposing responsible persons to imprisonment and criminal fines in addition to the civil tax liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an open current account regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; KRW is convertible for genuine trade and service payments, but all cross-border transactions are monitored through foreign exchange banks. Current account receipts such as SaaS export proceeds may be collected in foreign currency or KRW, but service exporters must collect proceeds within 180 days. There is no surrender obligation and no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may maintain foreign currency proceeds in designated foreign exchange accounts until used for working capital. Capital account transactions are subject to a negative-list system. Inward foreign investment into Yuhan Hoesa must be notified under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and paid-in capital must be routed through a Korean foreign-exchange bank escrow account before capital verification. Profit repatriation is possible as dividends after audited financial statements, tax clearance, and showing foreign investment registration; remittances for royalties or service fees require underlying contracts and invoice evidence. In practice, banks treat profit remittance from a low-risk helpdesk SaaS entity as standard but will block or defer if the beneficial owner chain is incomplete, tax residency mismatches arise, or the payment purpose code is inconsistent with supporting documents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds, including SaaS and helpdesk service receipts, must be collected through a Korean foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of service completion or invoice date; a delayed collection report or regulatory approval is required beyond 180 days. Mandatory conversion ratio: none. The exporter may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency account or convert to KRW at its discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the foreign-exchange administration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

The receiving bank reports the transaction in the Foreign Exchange Information System and issues the foreign-invested company registration. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and Master File are mandatory when the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction amount for the fiscal year exceeds KRW 50 billion (approximately USD 38 million); Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting is required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least KRW 1 trillion (approximately USD 750 million) with operating revenue from foreign subsidiaries above KRW 100 billion; even below these thresholds, transactional records and arm's-length documentation must be maintained contemporaneously

Economic substance scope: Although Korea does not apply BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Korean tax authorities apply the 'substance over form' doctrine under the Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 14, and the Corporate Tax Act Art. 20/21 rules against sham transactions; a Korean company must carry on its business with actual employees, office premises, management and operational risk in Korea to be recognized as the beneficial owner of income and to be eligible for treaty benefits; Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a helpdesk SaaS provider include the development and maintenance of the software platform, contracting with customers, risk management, billing and support functions, which must be performed in Korea if the entity is the contract party; reliance solely on offshore subcontractors with no Korean substance will trigger re-characterization of revenue, denial of treaty benefits, and be treated as a shell company under the OECD/G20 BEPS MLI

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Omitted or Falsified Digital Service Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act, Art. 3, evading corporate tax by fraud or other unlawful means is penalized with imprisonment for up to 2 years or a criminal fine not less than 50% but not exceeding 200% of the amount of evaded tax; in addition, the NTS imposes a 20% negligence penalty on the omitted tax amount plus daily delinquency surcharges; if the evaded tax exceeds KRW 500 million, the criminal penalty is aggravated to imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine up to 300% of the evaded amount

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: the responsible director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and interest; the NTS can attach director's personal bank accounts, real estate and equity holdings; the director will be placed on the national tax delinquent list, resulting in a travel ban (blacklisting) and restrictions on receiving government licenses; upon criminal conviction, the director faces actual imprisonment without probation, confiscation of profits, and disqualification from serving as a director of any Korean entity for up to 10 years
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting to Offshore Affiliates

Statutory sanction: Under the Korean Corporate Tax Act Art. 20 and Art. 21, the NTS is authorized to re-determine taxable income by applying the arm's-length principle and may impose an underreporting negligence penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted underreported tax, plus 10% for unsubstantiated international transaction disclosures; where a CbC report is required but not filed, the penalty is KRW 50 million for the first non-compliance and KRW 200 million for repeated non-compliance; tax adjustments may also trigger a 60% fraud penalty for intentional manipulation

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized or signed the manipulated transfer-pricing documentation are personally liable for the additional corporate tax, interest and penalties discovered on audit; the NTS may seek indemnity from the director's personal assets; in serious cases, the act is deemed tax evasion and subject to the same criminal imprisonment terms as under the Punishment of Tax Crimes Act; directors may also be debarred from holding office in any company that transacts with Korean financial institutions, and the government may impose an exit ban for pending cross-border tax disputes
Shell Company / Sham Substance and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 14, transactions that are a sham or mere form are disregarded for tax purposes, resulting in full assessment of the hidden profits to the Korean entity; the Council for International Tax Coordination Act (CITCA) and the Korean Multilateral Instrument (MLI) ratification allow the NTS to deny treaty benefits if the main purpose of the arrangement is to obtain treaty relief; a 40%-60% additional penalty may be imposed on abusive tax avoidance transactions; in extreme cases, the criminal anti-tax-evasion penalties described above apply

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the full amount of tax, penalties, and interest if the corporate veil is pierced; courts have historically pierced the veil for gross undercapitalization and absence of separate control; directors face civil liability for asset restitution, loss of limited liability protection, immediate travel restriction order until the tax liability is settled, and criminal conviction for conspiracy to commit tax fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years and a personal criminal fine equal to 300% of the evaded tax; the director's personal passport can be invalidated and the director placed on the Interpol red notice if the liability exceeds KRW 300 million

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (hometax.go.kr). Customs clearance is administered by Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS electronic single-window.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity carrying on domestic business through a Korean place of business (including a Yuhan Hoesa subsidiary or branch) must file taxpayer registration with the competent NTS regional tax office or through HomeTax within 20 days of domestic business commencement. The SOP requires the foreign entity to provide its home-jurisdiction corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, a board resolution approving Korean establishment, a tax representative power of attorney where required, and evidence of the Korean place of business. For the Korean-incorporated Yuhan Hoesa, completion of Commercial Registration Court filing automatically triggers NTS assignment of the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the corporate TIN, VAT registration number, and e-tax invoice identification number. NTS subsequently enrolls the entity in HomeTax and issues the business registration certificate. A foreign non-resident entity with no Korean permanent establishment does not require a Korean BRN merely to export commodities into Korea; the Korean exporter substantiates zero-rated exports with its own BRN and customs export declarations.

Offshore services or services performed entirely outside Korea are also zero-rated under the Value-Added Tax Act; domestic cold-chain wholesale to Korean retailers remains standard-rated at 10%.

Registered taxpayers must issue electronic tax invoices through HomeTax and transmit invoice data to NTS no later than the day after issuance. Korea does not operate real-time per-invoice fiscal clearance or a public digital stamping server; instead the NTS e-tax invoice system performs anti-fraud data matching, mandatory electronic signature verification, and periodic VAT return reconciliation. Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a broadly liberal current-account and capital-account foreign exchange regime. Korean won is convertible with transaction-level monitoring rather than central-bank pre-approval for ordinary trade and operational payments. Craft beer import/export settlement, freight charges, insurance, commissions, royalties, and distributor payments can be executed through authorized domestic foreign exchange banks using statutory declarations. For capital-account transactions such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border borrowing, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act impose mandatory reporting, but do not impose blanket prohibitions. Exporters are not forced to surrender export proceeds; they may retain foreign currency in a depository foreign-currency account or convert it to Korean won. Profit remittance obstacles are limited to standard compliance evidence: audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board resolutions approving dividends, evidence of statutory surplus, and verification that the remitted earnings were generated by legally declared business operations. The principal practical friction for a Yuhan Hoesa engaged in alcoholic beverage distribution is documentary: liquor-related licenses, customs declarations, and the bank's AML/KYC insistence on proving beneficial ownership and economic substance. There is no surrender ratio that would impair treasury planning, and no material hurdle to opening multi-currency accounts with a top-tier domestic bank.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must generally be received within six months from the date of export declaration unless a longer period is approved through the foreign exchange bank or BOK. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign-currency account or sell it to an authorized bank at market rates, so the statutory surrender rate is zero percent. Settlement must be executed through a designated foreign exchange bank and supported by an export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and foreign exchange payment confirmation, with a mandatory notification/reporting duty to the bank and ultimately the BOK for abnormal delays or non-collection.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), serving as the Republic of Korea's central bank and principal foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, in coordinated oversight with the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore digital EMI viability is Low/Negligible for mandatory treasury operations. A non-bank fintech EMI without a Korean banking license cannot legally provide local won settlement accounts or act as a primary foreign-exchange counterparty for a Korean registered entity under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Such platforms can only be used as secondary payment facilitators or invoicing tools when they route through a licensed Korean bank or authorized payment gateway, and they cannot replace a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank account for capital escrow, export proceeds, or corporate treasury. The compliance recommendation is to avoid reliance on any foreign EMI for settlement and to keep all clearing, capital verification, and tax-related payment records within the domestic banking system.

Such platforms can only be used as secondary payment facilitators or invoicing tools when they route through a licensed Korean bank or authorized payment gateway, and they cannot replace a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank account for capital escrow, export proceeds, or corporate treasury. The compliance recommendation is to avoid reliance on any foreign EMI for settlement and to keep all clearing, capital verification, and tax-related payment records within the domestic banking system.

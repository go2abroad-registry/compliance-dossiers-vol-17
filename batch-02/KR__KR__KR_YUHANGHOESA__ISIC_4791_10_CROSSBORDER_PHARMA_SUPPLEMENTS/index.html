Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS), Republic of Korea, operating the HomeTax electronic portal (hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service administers border and export controls through UNI-PASS.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Incorporate the Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) under the Commercial Act by registration with the Commercial Registration Court registry. Step 2: File an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office of the principal place of business, including the corporate articles of incorporation, commercial registration certificate, lease evidence, and, if foreign-owned, the foreign investment report filed with the Minister of Economy and Finance / KOTRA Invest Korea under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Step 3: The NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number (taxpayer identification number) and a Business Registration Certificate; this single number covers corporate income tax, value-added tax, and withholding registration. Step 4: For a non-resident foreign entity without a domestic fixed place of business, the foreign entity must appoint a domestic tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes, submit the same registration application through the agent, and obtain a foreign resident tax registration number before making taxable supplies in Korea. Step 5: License-specific registration is separately required for health functional food retailers under Ministry of Food and Drug Safety rules before NTS final recognition is effective.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. The Republic of Korea does not have check-the-box pass-through elections; a Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a domestic corporation on worldwide income. A 100% domestic subsidiary group may elect consolidated corporate tax filing under the Corporate Tax Act, but that election does not alter the entity-level tax classification or VAT identity of the LLC.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No registration exemption: every entrepreneur conducting taxable supply in Korea must register from the first supply. KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable supply is the simplified taxpayer (small taxpayer) classification boundary only, not a VAT registration threshold. Foreign non-resident electronic suppliers to Korean consumers must register under the electronic services special VAT treatment before the supply begins and cannot use the KRW 80,000,000 threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 11 of the Value-Added Tax Act if the goods physically leave Korea through legitimate customs export clearance and ownership passes to a non-resident. For nutraceutical e-commerce, the seller must show the product was exported under the seller's exporter declaration, not merely delivered to a domestic forwarding warehouse. Qualifying offshore digital services that are supplied to non-resident users and effectively used outside Korea may also be zero-rated, but the supplements transaction is subject to the physical export rule. The exporter may claim input VAT refunds attributable to zero-rated export sales; the claim must be substantiated with the documents listed below.

General taxable persons must issue tax invoices electronically through NTS HomeTax or a certified Automated Service Provider API; e-tax invoice data must be transmitted to NTS for validation and storage. Korea does not operate a pre-clearance tax stamp system, but the NTS e-Tax Invoice System assigns acceptance serial numbers, performs matching checks between issuers and VAT returns, and flags anomalies in real time. For B2C sales, the seller must issue a Cash Receipt through the NTS Cash Receipt system when a purchaser requests one, including online pharma/nutraceutical payment rails. An anti-fraud digital audit trail (acceptance numbers, issuer registration, and transaction reconciliation) is mandatory for all cross-border e-commerce export claims.

      
NTS audits export zero-rating by matching customs export declarations, bills of lading, and bank foreign-currency receipt records. If export proceeds are not received within the 180-day statutory foreign-exchange collection period or the export documents are false, the VAT zero-rating is revoked retroactively, uncollected 10% VAT is assessed with statutory interest, and the taxpayer is liable for additional taxes and unfaithful tax invoice penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent customs declarations are punishable under the Customs Act with criminal penalties and fines, and tax evasion referrals to the prosecutor are made under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea employs a largely liberalised but notification-heavy exchange-control framework. Current-account payments and receipts are freely convertible for documented trade, service, and dividend flows; no prior BOK approval is required for genuine business transactions executed through registered foreign-exchange banks. Capital-account transfers are regulated: inward direct equity investment must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act through KOTRA/Invest Korea, and outward remittances of invested capital or distributable profits require the foreign-invested company report number, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and the foreign-exchange bank's transaction screening. A mandatory currency surrender requirement does not exist, but exporters must repatriate proceeds to a domestic foreign-exchange bank within the statutory collection period and evidence the underlying export. Dividend and capital repatriation are permitted once corporate tax and withholding obligations are settled, and every transfer must be transmitted through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Banks also require Real-Name Financial Transaction Act verification for the Korean-won operating account. Undocumented e-commerce merchant receipts routed through third-party platform payment pools face heightened scrutiny and should never be used as substitute treasury inflows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Korea does not impose a fixed percentage conversion-to-won surrender ratio. The statutory collection rule requires export proceeds to be received within six months from the export declaration date, unless the foreign-exchange bank approves an extension for capital goods or project-based supply contracts. The full foreign-currency receipt may then be credited to, and retained in, a domestic foreign currency deposit account without mandatory conversion. All export receipts must be reconciled to the export invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration. For nutraceutical e-commerce sales settled through overseas payment gateways, the platform settlement statements and gateway payout reports must be preserved to prove collection within the period and to support the current-account classification of the revenue.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), operating as the central bank and the designated foreign-exchange reporting authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, together with the Financial Supervisory Service and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit for AML enforcement

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Very Low; not viable as a primary treasury rail. A foreign fintech EMI that is not licensed as a domestic electronic financial transaction provider cannot open a Korean won demand-deposit account, cannot make mandatory FEIS foreign-exchange declarations, cannot issue a compliant Real-Name verified corporate account, and cannot process tax payments, LC issuance, or import-export settlement. Such EMIs may only be used as an ancillary collection access point for international consumer card transactions if the aggregate funds are immediately swept into a domestic licensed-bank account and all platform-settlement detail is available to the bank for transaction monitoring. Using an EMI as the main operating or liquidity account would create unregulated cross-border money flows and trigger likely KoFIU STR filings and bank relationship termination.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Offshore shareholding and opaque ultimate beneficial ownership in a newly formed LLC filing as a licensed nutraceutical e-commerce operator
        
Root cause: Offshore shareholding and opaque ultimate beneficial ownership in a newly formed LLC filing as a licensed nutraceutical e-commerce operator

        
Defense evidence: Provide certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles of association, share register, directors' register, signed UBO declaration, source of wealth for each shareholder, 12 months original bank statements showing accumulated wealth, tax residence certificates, reference letters from the precedent banks, and a board resolution approving the Korean AML compliance officer.

      

      
      

        High-risk product classification for dietary supplements and nutraceuticals, which Korean banks map to fraud and health-claim reputation risk
        
Root cause: High-risk product classification for dietary supplements and nutraceuticals, which Korean banks map to fraud and health-claim reputation risk

        Cross-border revenue settlement pattern involving offshore payment processors, marketplace stores, and prepaid/card rails creating potential round-tripping or trade-based money laundering risk

Defense evidence: Include independent screening reports from reputable providers, passport copies, local criminal record certificates, confirmation of no past FSS/KoFIU enforcement, a comprehensive tax lien search, court litigation check, directorship history, and a written risk remediation statement explaining any past regulatory or criminal finding and the entity's mitigating controls.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All inbound capital must be routed as a direct SWIFT MT103 from the corporate investor's licensed bank into the LLC's Foreign Currency Demand Deposit or Capital Escrow Account held at a Korean foreign-exchange bank. The remittance instructions must include the Foreign Investment Promotion Act (FIPA) report number obtained through KOTRA/Invest Korea and the purpose code for direct equity participation. Funds must be received before bank accounts are activated; the Korean bank will complete source-of-funds checks and issue a certificate of receipt of foreign exchange. This certificate, together with the FIPA filing and foreign investor's report, constitutes the capital verification required to fund registered share capital and to support future dividend/exit remittance. Third-party funding or aggregate platform proceeds are prohibited for capital-account injection.For operating trade payments, use SWIFT MT103 through the Korean bank for intercompany royalties, supplier settlement, logistics, and tax payments, attaching the invoice, proforma contract, MFDS import or approval certificate if applicable, and a completed FEIS foreign-exchange transaction report. For high-value nutraceutical raw-material purchases, use a confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700/MT710) with draft terms, quality certificates, and B/L as negotiating documents. For short-cycle or lower-value open-account purchases, documentary collections with documents against payment or documents against acceptance are acceptable, provided the bank has a signed underlying sale/purchase agreement and any required clinical or regulatory evidence. All outbound disbursements must be aligned with the company's stated business plan to avoid current/capital account misclassification.Deliverable FX forward contracts up to 12 months with licensed foreign-exchange banks, executed only against underlying export or import exposure reflected in invoices and contractsPlain-vanilla OTC FX options purchased (call/put) to hedge forecast nutraceutical cross-border sales/costs, subject to the FSS underlying-risk ratio limitsCross-currency swaps (USD/KRW) for intercompany loans or long-term working capital facilities, with the loan agreement, BOK reporting confirmation, and FIPA documentation attachedNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where KRW deliverability is not available, generally limited to offshore non-resident structures; not recommended as the primary in-country hedging rail for the LLC

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
Progressive schedule under the Corporate Tax Act: 9% on taxable base up to KRW 200 million; 19% on KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% on KRW 20 billion to KRW 300 billion; 24% above KRW 300 billion.

22% aggregate for cash dividends remitted to a non-resident shareholder: 20% corporate income withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where intercompany transactions with a foreign related party exceed KRW 10 billion per transaction type in a fiscal year; Master File is mandatory where total intercompany cross-border transactions exceed KRW 50 billion. All reporting entities must disclose transactions with specially related parties in the corporate tax return and maintain contemporaneous TP documentation at lower thresholds upon audit demand.

      
Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the international tax framework applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and the Principal Purpose Test. A Korean LLC must actually carry on management, employees, premises, board decisions, and economically significant functions (CIGA) in Korea to defend its entitlement to treaty benefits and to avoid re-characterization of royalty/dividend flows by the NTS.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Cross-Border Digital Sales Omission
        
Statutory sanction: NTS assesses the omitted corporate tax plus local surcharge, imposes 20% non-filing additional tax under Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2, 10% under-reporting additional tax under Article 47-3 (increased for serious fraud), daily late-payment surcharge of 0.022%, and refers the matter to the public prosecutor's office under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available when the LLC is abusively used as a cover; directors may be jointly and severally liable under the Commercial Act (Article 401), subjected to civil restitution for unpaid taxes, NTS travel bans/blacklisting for foreign residents, asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment of up to two years (or higher terms for aggravated large-scale fraud) with fines of up to twice the amount of tax evaded.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Abuse / Sham Cross-Border Royalties and Management Fees
        
Statutory sanction: The NTS re-determines the arm's length amount under Article 5 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, disallows the excess as a non-deductible expense, imposes 20% additional tax on the unpaid corporate tax, and may apply a 10% penalty for failure to submit TP documentation; if fraudulent, criminal referral under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act follows.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false intercompany contracts expose themselves to personal liability under the Commercial Act (Article 401) and to criminal charges of aiding and abetting tax evasion; the corporate veil can be pierced where the LLC is functionally a payment conduit for shareholders, resulting in asset garnishment, travel blacklisting, disqualification of directors, and imprisonment.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / No Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied because the recipient is not the beneficial owner or lacks substance under the Principal Purpose Test (OECD MLI and Korean DTA protocols); the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 4-3 empowers NTS to disregard the hybrid arrangement and apply 22% withholding on deemed dividends plus additional tax up to 40% in fraud cases.

        
Director personal liability: The NTS may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable as the de facto entrepreneurs; secondary liability under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 39 can be imposed on directors that transfer assets or strip the company, and directors face civil restitution, travel bans/blacklisting, criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and imprisonment in severe abuse cases.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating the Home Tax Service (HomeTax) electronic portal at hometax.go.kr; Korea Customs Service (KCS) via UNI-PASS administers export declaration control for indirect-tax substantiation.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-owned Yuhan Hoesa: (1) complete incorporation registration at the local court Commercial Registry and, where foreign equity investment criteria apply, file a foreign direct investment notification with a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank or Invest KOREA; (2) within 20 days of commencing business, file the Business Operator Registration application with the competent District Tax Office administered by the NTS, using HomeTax or an NTS-ready agent, accompanied by the corporate registration certificate, articles of incorporation, foreign investment notification, proof of place of business, and documents identifying the ultimate foreign shareholder; (3) the NTS will issue the statutory Business Registration Certificate, whose unique Business Operator Registration Number also functions as the corporate TIN and VAT ID; VAT registration is embedded in this business registration and does not require a separate VAT form; (4) for a foreign non-resident seller that is not establishing a domestic entity but is nevertheless required to collect Korean VAT, the standard route is Non-Resident Business Operator Registration filed with the NTS through HomeTax, including designation of a Korean domestic tax representative under the VAT Act, a power of attorney, foreign business registration evidence, and a domestic address and account arrangement; the NTS then issues a VAT ID for the non-resident business, subject to the same electronic invoice and reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification

Available election forms
None. Korean tax law is not a check-the-box environment: a Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily taxed as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and cannot elect pass-through treatment or a different indirect-tax entity status. No hybrid mismatch election is available in the VAT and GST regime.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No general turnover registration threshold: VAT registration is mandatory from business commencement for a domestic Yuhan Hoesa LLC. The law uses a KRW 80,000,000 annual taxable turnover ceiling for simplified taxpayer treatment of qualifying small individual businesses, but a corporate e-commerce retailer is outside that simplified category. A foreign non-resident supplier supplying goods or electronic and digital services to Korea is also subject to mandatory simplified VAT registration from its first taxable supply, meaning no economic turnover threshold applies.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating is available under the Value-Added Tax Act export provisions where (1) the goods are sold to a foreign purchaser, including an overseas individual on an e-commerce marketplace, and are physically removed from Korean customs territory; (2) the export is covered by a formal export declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS; (3) the seller retains proof that title and risk passed to the foreign buyer outside Korea, supported by the export invoice, parcel or airway bills, and logistics tracking; (4) the sales proceeds are denominated in foreign currency and received through a Korean licensed foreign exchange bank or otherwise evidenced by a Foreign Exchange Transaction Certificate; and (5) for qualifying offshore services, including digital content and gaming services supplied to non-resident customers, the place of supply and consumption is outside Korea. Consumer-electronics parcel exports qualify where the export declaration is in the exporting Korean entity's name and the documented shipment leaves Korea within the permitted export period.

Under the VAT Act and the NTS electronic invoice system, every VAT-registered supplier must issue a statutory tax invoice for each taxable supply. Taxpayers required to issue e-tax invoices must transmit the invoice data to the NTS HomeTax National e-Tax Invoice System using the certified HomeTax XML and API interface or an approved third-party e-invoicing agent. South Korea does not currently operate a real-time per-invoice fiscal-clearance model and does not impose anti-fraud digital stamps; instead, the HomeTax system receives e-tax invoice data promptly after issuance and automatically reconciles it against VAT returns, customs export declarations in UNI-PASS, card-payment data, and bank foreign-exchange reports. Failure to issue, delayed issuance, or false issuance of an e-tax invoice gives rise to penalty tax under the VAT Act, and a zero-rated export invoice will be rejected if it cannot be matched to a valid UNI-PASS export declaration.

      
NTS and Korea Customs audit export zero-rating by cross-matching UNI-PASS export clearances, HomeTax zero-rated e-tax invoices, VAT refund claims, and bank-reported foreign exchange receipts. If the exporter cannot produce valid export clearance or the export proceeds are not repatriated within the six-month statutory foreign-exchange settlement period, unless a permitted extension is obtained, the supply is reclassified as domestic taxable supply. NTS will then assess the 10% VAT, charge statutory late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, impose VAT penalty tax for false or unissued tax invoices and failure to report, and retroactively disallow input VAT credits previously claimed in connection with the purported export. Submission of forged export or foreign-exchange documents may additionally trigger penalties and criminal sanctions under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, the Customs Act, and the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Korean won is fully convertible for current-account activities: trade in goods, services, freight, insurance, commissions and ordinary business expenses can be settled freely through domestic foreign exchange banks. The capital account, however, is managed: non-resident equity contributions must be reported under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and any conversion of foreign currency into KRW must take place at a licensed domestic bank. Korea does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio on non-resident trade proceeds; export receivables can remain in foreign currency, but they must be collected within the six-month statutory settlement period and be traceable in the Foreign Exchange Information System. Profit and dividend remittances from a Yuhan Hoesa are legal, yet the paying bank will require audited or reviewed financial statements, proof that the equity interest is registered on the Korean registry, withholding tax clearance, and a copy of the board/shareholder resolution approving the distribution. Practical hurdles are the reporting discipline around foreign direct investment, the need to maintain complete trade documents in Korean or English, and the sensitive treatment of payments that exceed a plausible retail-electronics turnover; the external auditor's report and source-of-funds evidence will determine whether a dividend is remitted without further regulatory escalation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds must be collected within six months from the date of export declaration or the date the goods are loaded, whichever is applicable; banks may extend the deadline by up to six months for justifiable commercial reasons if the extension is requested and recorded before expiry. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage to KRW; the exporter may retain the receipts in a foreign-currency trade account at a domestic foreign exchange bank. However, the underlying obligation is a collection-and-repatriation duty: proceeds must enter a licensed Korean bank account, be reported in the Foreign Exchange Information System, and be matched to the export customs declaration. Receipts routed through unlicensed overseas digital accounts or held abroad beyond the statutory period are treated as a foreign exchange regulatory violation and may attract penalties.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting as the foreign exchange authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Low for primary treasury clearing. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut and Payoneer are not licensed foreign exchange banks in Korea and are legally barred from opening domestic won settlement accounts or providing Korean IBAN-equivalent clearing rails. They cannot service the Yuhan Hoesa as a primary bank or liquidity provider. Their permissible use is limited to supplementary consumer collection channels, for example card or wallet receipts outside Korea, and all such proceeds must ultimately be swept by SWIFT into a licensed Korean bank account using compliant purpose codes. Using an unlicensed EMI for corporate disbursements creates AML exposure, foreign exchange reporting failures, and a refusal risk from the Korean financial intelligence unit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For a Yuhan Hoesa receiving non-resident equity, the foreign investor must file a foreign investment report with MOEF/KOTRA or through a licensed domestic bank before remittance. The Korean entity opens an Investment Capital Escrow Account with a Tier-1 commercial bank; the investor remits foreign currency by SWIFT MT103/202 using the foreign direct investment purpose code, the domestic bank converts the funds into KRW and credits the escrow account, and after share issuance the company files a post-closing foreign investment report to obtain the Foreign Investment Enterprise Certificate. The bank records the inward remittance in the Foreign Exchange Information System, verifies the capital purpose, and produces a capital verification confirmation; the funds must not be released to unrelated parties before statutory capital verification is completed.For imports of consumer electronics for resale, the Yuhan Hoesa can pay via SWIFT MT103 through its designated foreign exchange bank after presenting the proforma/commercial invoice and customs declaration; for larger settlements, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit in sight or usance form may be issued under the bank's import facility. For exports, foreign buyers remit via SWIFT MT103/202 to the company's export proceeds account, and if a letter of credit is used, the Korean advising bank negotiates the documents and settles the proceeds. Documentary collections on a documents-against-payment or documents-against-acceptance basis are acceptable for established counterparties, but the company must maintain consistent transport and customs documentation to satisfy the Korean bank's trade-reporting obligations. All settlements must be classified under the correct Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose codes and reported through the bank's BOK reporting module.Plain-vanilla foreign exchange forward contracts and FX swaps permissible for underlying trade receivables and payables, with notional capped at the actual exposure and maturity aligned to the settlement date.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted for KRW-USD exposure where physical KRW settlement is impractical, but only through an authorized domestic foreign exchange bank with documentary proof of real commercial exposure; synthetic layering or speculative rolling is prohibited.Cross-currency swaps and interest-rate swaps allowed only for long-term capital or financing obligations, limited to the notional of the underlying Korean entity debt or equity, and any restructuring must be reported to the relationship bank and the Bank of Korea where applicable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for the annual Local File; KRW 50 billion threshold for the Master File and KRW 1 trillion consolidated group revenue for the CbC Report; disclosure of related-party transactions with the NTS is also required if the aggregate exceeds KRW 100 million.

      
Economic substance scope: Korean tax authorities apply substance over form and may disregard shell corporate vehicles under the Framework Act and international tax abuse doctrines; for e-commerce, the Core Income-Generating Activities that create business profits must be performed and managed in Korea if Yuhan Hoesa is a Korean resident; a company with no Korean business substance, real employees, local management decisions or core retail activities will fail economic substance scrutiny, and related-party payment arrangements aimed at stripping Korean profits will be recharacterized.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and False Corporate Filing
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 3 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, evasion of corporate tax is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years or a criminal fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; the NTS will also assess additional tax of 40% of the fraudulent under-reporting plus daily late-payment interest and impose a public disclosure as a high-value tax delinquent.

        
Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is merely a façade, holding directors personally liable for civil restitution of evaded tax and penalties; responsible directors may be subject to asset seizure, travel ban/blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 6, the tax authority may disregard the legal form, reattribute profits and impose a 20%-40% penalty on the adjusted tax base plus criminal referral for abuse of tax treaty.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a sham structure lose limited liability protection under the Korean Commercial Act, becoming jointly and severally liable for the taxes and penalties; Korean courts may issue exit bans and order arrest or imprisonment for directors who transferred assets abroad to frustrate collection.

      

      
      

        Cross-Border Withholding Tax and Repatriation Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes, the withholding agent is liable for the uncollected withholding tax, an additional tax equal to the unpaid withholding amount plus a 10%-50% penalty for willful failure, and daily overdue interest; criminal prosecution for tax evasion also applies.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who direct the non-withholding or authorize false invoices are jointly liable for the withheld corporate tax amounts; personal bank accounts of directors may be attached, and directors may be blacklisted with travel restrictions and face criminal imprisonment for fraud in connection with the repatriation of profits.

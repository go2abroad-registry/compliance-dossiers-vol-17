Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax System (hometax.go.kr). Korea Customs Service (UNI-PASS) is the cognizant agency for processing export customs declarations.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity obtains a Korean Business Registration Number (BRN) by filing an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office having jurisdiction over its domestic place of business. If no domestic place of business exists, the foreign entity registers under the simplified foreign business operator procedure, available through the tax office designated for non-resident persons; foreign suppliers of electronic services must complete this registration before making their first taxable supply to Korean consumers. The required submission package includes the entity's certificate of incorporation and articles, foreign business registration or equivalent licence evidence, identity documentation of the responsible person, a power of attorney appointing a domestic tax representative when applicable, and bank account details. The NTS issues a 10-digit BRN, which doubles as the VAT registration number, corporate tax identification number and employer withholding account.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
None (KRW 0) for general VAT registration; any person making taxable supplies in Korea must register within 20 days of business commencement. An annual taxable turnover of KRW 80,000,000 or less permits simplified taxable person status. Foreign suppliers of electronic services to Korean non-business consumers must register from the first taxable supply.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating eligibility follows Article 21 of the Value-Added Tax Act. The 0% rate applies to exported goods evidenced by a valid export customs declaration and to services consumed outside Korea, including qualifying international transportation, logistics and export-support services. Digital services supplied by a Korean business to a foreign business or to a foreign recipient where the place of consumption is outside Korea may be zero-rated; supplies of electronic services to consumers in Korea are subject to the 10% VAT rate and cannot be zero-rated.

Korean VAT is administered through the mandatory Electronic Tax Invoice (ETI) regime on the HomeTax System. Registered businesses must issue electronic tax invoices for taxable supplies and transmit the ETI data to the NTS by the day following the date of invoice issuance. The NTS does not operate a pre-issuance digital stamping approval model; instead it applies near-real-time cross-referencing of ETI data with VAT returns, customs declarations, cash receipts and foreign-exchange settlement records. Machine-to-machine filing through the HomeTax integration interfaces is available for certified accounting/ERP platforms, and late or non-transmission of ETIs triggers surcharge penalties.

      
The NTS reconciles zero-rated export claims against Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS declaration data, HomeTax ETI records and bank foreign-exchange settlement reports. Failure to maintain the complete document bundle or to repatriate proceeds within the statutory 180-day period results in reversal of the zero rate, assessment of 10% VAT on the supply value, plus interest and additional tax for under-reporting. Fraudulent export invoices, fictitious exports and intentional evasion are treated as tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, exposing the taxpayer to additional tax of up to 40% of the tax amount, fines and criminal imprisonment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea operates a highly open capital account with full current account convertibility and a liberalized capital account framework, subject to notification and reporting obligations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree. The Korean won is freely convertible for most legitimate business purposes, but banks and foreign exchange dealers must report transactions to the Bank of Korea and the Korea Customs Service through the Foreign Exchange Information System. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds in the traditional sense, but Korean resident enterprises must repatriate foreign currency receivables within a statutory period, generally no later than the due date of the underlying trade contract and in any case not exceeding one year from the date of export customs clearance, unless an extension is granted by the designated foreign exchange bank. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted after a corporate income tax withholding and a foreign exchange transaction notification; the remitting bank verifies the audited financial statements, board resolution, tax payment certificates, and the foreign currency purchase declaration. A practical hurdle is that the bank may require a certificate of tax payment and a certificate of no delinquent local taxes from the relevant tax office, and for amounts above KRW 100,000 equivalent there is an automatic reporting obligation. In practice, profit remittances require a corporate seal certificate, a board resolution, and a copy of the foreign investment report if the shareholder is a foreign investor. Capital account transactions such as equity injections, loans, and guarantees are subject to a prior or post-reporting regime depending on the amount and counterparty; foreign investors investing more than KRW 100 million must file a foreign investment report with a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Overall, the won is convertible on both current and capital accounts, but documentation and reporting discipline are essential, and the central bank retains macroprudential measures such as forward position limits for banks and a foreign exchange stability levy on non-deposit foreign currency liabilities.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under Article 21 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and related regulations, Korean exporters must repatriate foreign currency export proceeds into Korea through a designated foreign exchange bank within the period specified in the underlying trade contract; absent contractual terms, the proceeds must be collected and repatriated within one year from the date of export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to KRW; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a domestic foreign currency account or convert to KRW at market rate. However, if the export proceeds remain uncollected after the due date, the exporter must obtain a extension from the designated foreign exchange bank, and unresolved overdue receivables may be reported to the Korea Customs Service and trigger administrative penalties.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Employee Stock Ownership Trust with multiple beneficiaries may obscure ultimate beneficial ownership of the trust corpus if the beneficiary list is not fully disclosed and the ESOP sponsor holds a discretionary role.
        
Root cause: Employee Stock Ownership Trust with multiple beneficiaries may obscure ultimate beneficial ownership of the trust corpus if the beneficiary list is not fully disclosed and the ESOP sponsor holds a discretionary role.

        
Defense evidence: Provide (1) the full trust deed and ESOP plan documents specifying each beneficiary's beneficial interest and vesting schedule; (2) a certified register of participants with resident numbers or passport copies for each employee-beneficiary; (3) an organizational chart showing the sponsor company, trustee, and beneficiaries with percentage economic entitlements; (4) a board resolution from the ESOP sponsor confirming that the trust holds no assets beyond qualifying employee shares and cash reserves, and that the trust is not acting as a nominee for undisclosed third parties.

      

      
      

        Capital injection from foreign parent or venture investors into the Korean ESOP trust may trigger beneficial ownership filing under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and banks may flag unexplained foreign exchange credit as potential circumvention of Korean restrictions on equity-equivalent instruments.
        
Root cause: Capital injection from foreign parent or venture investors into the Korean ESOP trust may trigger beneficial ownership filing under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and banks may flag unexplained foreign exchange credit as potential circumvention of Korean restrictions on equity-equivalent instruments.

        
Defense evidence: Submit (1) the foreign investment report (Hanguk-uigoe-tuja-sing-go-seo) filed with the designated foreign exchange bank, including the investor's underlying identity and source of funds; (2) audited financial statements of the foreign parent, or for individuals, three months of bank statements and salary/tax documents proving legitimate source of wealth; (3) a notarized undertaking confirming that the inbound wire is classified under Royalty/Services vs. Equity or a trust settlement purpose code, not a disguised loan; (4) a legal opinion from a Korean law firm confirming compliance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act notification thresholds.

      

      
      

        ESOP trust may be classified as a 'money laundering high-risk' structure if the trustee has authority to distribute cash proceeds to employees without individual vesting verification, creating layering risk.
        
Root cause: ESOP trust may be classified as a 'money laundering high-risk' structure if the trustee has authority to distribute cash proceeds to employees without individual vesting verification, creating layering risk.

        
Defense evidence: Provide (1) a detailed written AML/CFT policy of the ESOP trust including beneficiary due diligence procedures before any distribution; (2) a KYC/AML questionnaire completed by the trustee and all beneficial owners holding more than 10% economic interest; (3) a sample beneficiary payout ledger with vesting triggers and tax withholding calculations; (4) an independent external audit confirmation that distributable cash is never paid to shell entities or non-beneficiaries; (5) written confirmation from the appointed Korean compliance officer that suspicious transaction reports are filed with the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit (KoFIU) within 3 business days where applicable.

      

      
      

        Use of cross-border digital EMI payment instruments to fund or distribute ESOP benefits without a domestic regulated bank intermediary may indicate an effort to avoid Korean FX reporting obligations.
        
Root cause: Use of cross-border digital EMI payment instruments to fund or distribute ESOP benefits without a domestic regulated bank intermediary may indicate an effort to avoid Korean FX reporting obligations.

        
Defense evidence: Deliver (1) a written commitment letter that all inbound ESOP funding and outbound employee distributions will be routed exclusively through the licensed Korean commercial bank; (2) the complete SWIFT MT103 history of prior inbound capital injections if any; (3) a copy of the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) reporting confirmation from the bank showing that prior transactions were properly reported; (4) a board resolution mandating the closure of any non-compliant third-party payment accounts and the appointment of the local bank as the sole treasury gateway.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital injection from foreign shareholders, venture investors, or the parent sponsor of the ESOT must be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire to the company's designated foreign currency account held at a licensed Korean commercial bank. The remitter must reference the proper Foreign Exchange Transactions Act purpose code, typically code 908022 (personal income) for dividends, but for ESOP trust capital the suitable code is generally 906000 or 60001 (capital inflow for establishment/expansion) depending on the bank's classification. Upon receipt, the bank requests the company to submit the foreign investment report, a certificate of incorporation, a board resolution, and evidence of the investor's authority. If the injection is classified as a foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, a prior report to the designated bank is required and the bank issues a Foreign Investment Certificate. For trust capital that is not a foreign direct investment but simply a funding of the employee benefit plan, the bank may classify it under service/trade current account or under capital transactions requiring a simple post-report within 30 days. Capital verification is performed by submitting the bank's receipt confirmation to the appointed certified public accountant (Ga-in) who issues the subscribed capital verification certificate (Jabon-geumsu-jeungmyeong-seo) required for registration filings. All inbound amounts above USD 50,000 must be accompanied by documentation declaring the ultimate source of funds and the transaction purpose in the FEIS system.Outbound trade disbursements for ESOP trust operations include employee payout distributions, dividend remittances to parent companies, share purchase payments, and trust administration fees. For employee distributions in KRW, the company issues direct payroll files through the domestic financial network (Korea Financial Telecommunications & Clearings Institute - KFTC) and reports to the National Pension Service and National Tax Service. For cross-border disbursements to non-resident beneficiaries, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for cash payments, with pre-approval from the bank's international remittance team and a remittance-specific Foreign Exchange Transactions Act declaration. Trade settlements related to any ESOP-related share purchase or redemption are executed via Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) only if the share purchase is transacted through a physical goods framework, which is atypical; for share transfers the typical rail is a direct MT103 with an underlying share sale and purchase agreement. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are irrelevant to ESOP trust cash flows and are only used if the trust is incidentally financing trade of the sponsor company; in that case, a Korean bank would issue or confirm an L/C in accordance with UCP600 and the bank's internal trade finance policies. For any outflow exceeding USD 100,000, the company must submit an FX transaction notification and the bank will assess whether the transfer is permitted under the current or capital account rubrics.Korean won forward contracts available from domestic licensed commercial banks for underlying eligible FX exposure arising from ESOP dollar-denominated share purchases and dividend remittancesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with offshore counterparties where the underlying exposure is a non-KRW asset, but Korean corporates are subject to BOK macroprudential limits and must execute hedging only with banks or brokerages that hold a Korean FX brokerage licenseCurrency swaps (KRW/USD and KRW/EUR) for notional conversion of capital injections or share redemption proceeds, provided the swap transact at a minimum of one-year maturity to comply with BOK risk management guidelinesPlain vanilla foreign exchange forwards traded onshore with a designated foreign exchange bank, with no synthetic or cryptocurrency-based derivatives permitted, and with documentation evidencing a real commercial or treasury risk hedging need
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to nil for primary corporate account opening for a Korean resident entity. Fintech electronic money institutions and offshore EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut Business do not have banking licenses in South Korea and are generally barred from providing local KRW IBAN-equivalent settlement accounts to Korean-domiciled companies under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. They may be used for ancillary cross-border payment initiation in limited corridors, but the Korean corporate treasury must maintain its primary settlement and regulatory reporting relationship with a licensed domestic commercial bank or a domestic fintech with a bank partnership under the 'Innovation Financial Services Act' sandbox. The recommended approach is a full-service domestic bank account with SWIFT correspondent access, and use of licensed domestic fintechs only for payroll or ESOP distribution disbursements to employee beneficiaries through domestic payroll rails.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
25% (national corporate income tax; progressive brackets apply: 10% up to KRW 200 million, 20% for KRW 200-20 billion, 22% for KRW 20-300 billion, 25% for excess over KRW 300 billion; local income tax surcharge of 10% on corporate tax liability effectively adds an aggregate headline rate of up to 27.5%)

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
22% (20% corporate income tax + 2% local income tax) on outbound dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; no additional tax on liquidation proceeds treated as dividends under the Corporate Tax Act; if the Korean company is listed on KRX, the rate for foreign portfolio investors is 22%, unless reduced under a DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File: KRW 10 billion (approx.) per fiscal year aggregate intercompany transactions with a foreign related party, or KRW 5 billion for cross-border transactions with substantial transfer pricing risk as designated by the NTS; the Master File threshold is KRW 100 billion in aggregate intercompany revenue; simplified Local File / summary schedules required for transactions below KRW 3 billion but above KRW 1 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: Although South Korea has no general Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to offshore jurisdictions, the National Tax Service applies the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and the CIT Law's controlled foreign company (CFC) rules (Article 79 of the Corporate Tax Act). A Korean ESOP trust's core income-generating activities (CIGA) – equity administration, dividend collection, shareholdings, and beneficiary communications – must have a physical presence (office, staff, software) in Korea. The trust must maintain a separate bank account, board/committee minutes, and an independent trustee to avoid being deemed a sham. If a non-resident trust attempts to book profits without any Korean substance, the NTS may recharacterize the trust's income as the beneficiary's personal income or as a concealed Korean PE, subjecting the trust to 22% withholding plus 41% penalty for unjust tax adjustments.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Taxation – Fictitious Loss Booking and Sham Trust Structure
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes (fictitious transaction / substance-over-form) and Article 34 (5) of the Corporate Tax Act, the NTS disallows all management fee deductions and assesses 41% unjust tax adjustment penalty (Article 47-3 of the Framework Act) on the underpaid tax, plus 0.022% daily overdue rate; criminal referral under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (imprisonment up to 2 years or fine up to KRW 100 million).

        
Director personal liability: The NTS pierces the corporate veil under Article 14 (2) of the Framework Act if the trust structure is used as a sham to evade tax; directors are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties up to 100% of the assessed amount; civil asset restitution includes attachment of personal accounts, real estate, and shares; the director's name is added to the NTS 'non-payment blacklisting' which prohibits exit (travel ban) and blocks credit/banking; criminal imprisonment can extend to 3 years for egregious fraud causing over KRW 500 million in tax evasion.

      

      
      

        Tax Evasion – Failure to Withhold and Report Outbound Dividends
        
Statutory sanction: Withholding failure triggers a penalty of 10% of the undistributed withholding amount (Article 47-5 of the Framework Act), plus a collection-of-unpaid-tax liability against the trustee personally (Article 76 (1) of Corporate Tax Act); if the trust later defaults, the NTS may issue a summary notice to the trustee, and any additional tax due is subject to interest at 0.022% daily; criminal liability under Article 12 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act (up to 2 years in prison or fine of up to KRW 100 million).

        
Director personal liability: The director/board member who signed the distribution resolution is deemed 'the responsible person' for withholding; the NTS imposes a secondary liability on personal assets for the withheld tax amount plus penalties; a personal travel ban is automatically imposed when the unpaid tax exceeds KRW 100 million (Article 6 of the National Tax Collection Act); in criminal cases, the director may face up to 4 years' imprisonment if fraudulently causing a loss of national tax revenue exceeding KRW 150 million.

      

      
      

        AML & Beneficial Ownership – Concealing Beneficiary Identity
        
Statutory sanction: Violates Article 2 of the Act on Real Name Financial Transactions and Confidentiality (giving false beneficial owner info – fine up to KRW 50 million plus 5-year imprisonment); also breaches Article 4 of the Proceeds of Crime Act (money laundering offense – up to 5 years in prison); the NTS will annul the trust's tax exempt treatment and re-attribute profits to the concealed beneficiary at a 30% deemed-disposition rate.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved false filings are personally liable for any unjustly reduced amount of tax; the court may order restitution of all trust assets to the corporate estate; the director is prohibited from holding directorship in any Korean financial institution for 5 years; travel bans and asset freezing orders (under the Specified Financial Transactions Information Act) apply immediately upon criminal indictment.

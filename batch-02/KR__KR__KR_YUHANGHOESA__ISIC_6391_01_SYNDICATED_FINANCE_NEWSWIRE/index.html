Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act; the paid-up capital is the amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation and the contribution schedule. All cash contributions must be paid in full to a domestic Korean bank account established in the company's intended name before the commercial registration application is filed. This is a capital-payment verification requirement, not a blocked escrow or locked capital account rule; funds are releasable to the company upon completion of incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS); electronic filing and registration are administered through the HomeTax online portal (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

Under Article 8 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act, a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC (or any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Korea) must apply for business registration with the competent district tax office within 20 days from commencing business. The procedure is: (1) complete incorporation under the Korean Commercial Act and obtain the court registration certificate; (2) where foreign ownership is involved, complete the foreign investment report required by the Foreign Investment Promotion Act; (3) file an Application for Business Registration with the National Tax Service through the HomeTax portal or at the district tax office having jurisdiction over the place of business, attaching the articles of incorporation, court registration certificate, lease or office location proof, representative identification, and, if applicable, a power of attorney for the Korean tax representative; (4) the NTS assigns a 10-digit Business Registration Number that serves as both the corporate TIN and VAT ID and issues the Business Registration Certificate. A foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business that supplies electronically supplied services to Korean customers may use the simplified Foreign Entrepreneur Business Registration procedure via HomeTax, must appoint a tax representative in Korea, and receives a Foreign Entrepreneur Business Registration Number for VAT filing and electronic tax invoice issuance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration exemption threshold exists under Korean law: any person starting a business that supplies taxable goods or services in Korea must register for VAT. A simplified taxable person status applies to an individual business with annual gross revenue not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but this is not an exemption from TIN/VAT registration; it only changes reporting frequency and calculation. Foreign suppliers of electronically supplied services are also required to register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Korean VAT Act zero-rates the supply of goods exported from Korea and the supply of services to a non-resident or to a foreign country when the services are actually used outside Korea and the consideration is received in foreign currency. For the ISIC 6391 financial newswire API, zero-rating is available on B2B services supplied to an overseas syndicated finance customer, provided the customer is a non-resident or foreign permanent establishment, the API is actually delivered and consumed offshore, and payment is repatriated in foreign exchange. B2C digital services to consumers located in Korea remain taxable at 10% VAT. For physical goods embedded in the syndicated finance bundle, zero-rating further requires a Korea Customs Service export declaration.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Customs Declaration (electronic export clearance record issued through the UNI-PASS system) with outward cargo manifest
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for any physical content/hardware components
  • Bill of lading or air waybill showing export from Korea
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence: bank remittance statement or Foreign Exchange Transaction Report / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Zero-rated electronic tax invoice issued through NTS HomeTax
  • Export contract, purchase order, or service agreement with the overseas syndicated finance customer
  • Offshore service consumption and location evidence for digital API services (customer domicile certificate, API subscriber records, access logs, and foreign-country usage data)

VAT-registered businesses are required to issue tax invoices for all B2B supplies. Electronic tax invoices are mandatory through the NTS HomeTax e-tax invoice system using NTS-compatible XML/API transmission; the e-invoice data is sent to the National Tax Service for validation and cross-matching between buyer and seller returns, operating as a real-time/continuous fiscal clearance mechanism. The NTS assigns an e-invoice number and maintains the transmission record as the anti-fraud control; no separate third-party physical fiscal stamp is required. B2C point-of-sale transactions are covered by the credit/debit card approval system and the cash receipt tax deduction program for real-time fiscal reporting.

If the zero-rated export claim is selected for audit and the required customs, foreign currency receipt, invoice, and offshore consumption documentation is incomplete or inconsistent, the NTS will rescind the 0% rate, assess VAT at 10% on the transaction, and impose Korean tax penalties. The standard foreign-exchange repatriation requirement is 180 days, subject to extension only under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; failure to repatriate within this period (or a permitted extension) allows the NTS to treat the export as a domestic supply and issue a retrospective VAT assessment with penalty tax. Penalties include underreporting penalty tax of 10% for negligent understatement and up to 40% for intentional/fraudulent understatement, plus additional VAT Act penalties for non-issuance or false issuance of tax invoices. Where export documents or foreign remittance evidence are forged or fabricated, the NTS may file a criminal complaint under the Act on the Punishment of Tax Evaders and the Customs Act; the statutory assessment period is extended from 5 years to 10 years for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberal current-account regime: the Korean won is freely usable for trade settlements, service payments, dividends, royalties, and ordinary operational remittances, provided the underlying transaction is documented and reported to the FXIS. Capital-account transactions are not fully liberalized. Inbound foreign direct investment must be notified under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act, and cross-border loans, securities transactions, and certain financial derivatives require post-transaction reporting or, in some cases, prior authorisation from the Ministry of Economy and Finance or Bank of Korea. Export proceeds must be repatriated through a licensed foreign exchange bank within the prescribed receipt period, which is generally six months from the export declaration date; however, no mandatory blanket surrender percentage is currently applied, and the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a licensed account or sell it into KRW. Profit remittance hurdles are mainly documentary: a bank will require the foreign investor certificate, audited financial statements, a board-approved dividend resolution, corporate tax clearance, and the correct FXIS purpose code before converting accumulated retained earnings into foreign currency and remitting them offshore. In practice, standard current-account flows clear efficiently, but treasury teams must map every capital inflow and outflow to the correct FX transaction code and preserve underlying contracts for matching.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Regulations, exporters must repatriate export proceeds within the statutory receipt deadline, which is generally six months from the date of the export declaration. The proceeds must be paid into an account at a licensed foreign exchange bank or sold to that bank for KRW. There is no fixed statutory ratio requiring a specific percentage of export proceeds to be converted into KRW; the mandatory surrender ratio is effectively 0%. The receiving bank must register the receipt in the Foreign Exchange Information System, and the exporter must maintain the invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, and settlement evidence for post-transaction verification. Delayed repatriation without adequate justification triggers regulatory review and potential enforcement penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK), together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MoEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; day-to-day foreign exchange controls are administered by licensed domestic foreign exchange banks through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FXIS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not legally permitted to serve as the primary treasury rail for this Korean entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed domestic financial institutions can open KRW accounts, receive foreign currency, and execute FX conversions for resident legal entities. An offshore EMI may only act as a supplementary revenue-collection gateway for foreign client card payments, and it must route settlement funds through a licensed partner bank. Capital injection, dividend remittance, trade settlement, and FX hedging must clear through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. Therefore, fintech EMIs are rated 'do not use' for statutory corporate banking; Stripe or similar gateways can collect API subscription revenue but those funds must be periodically swept into the Korean bank account to remain fully compliant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must be remitted by inbound SWIFT MT103 through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. The credit should be directed to a capital escrow account or a designated foreign-invested company operational account. The remitter must include the Korea Foreign Exchange Information System transaction purpose code corresponding to inbound direct investment capital subscription and reference the company name, Business Registration Number, board resolution, and capital contribution schedule. The receiving bank will verify the foreign-investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before releasing funds. Paid-in capital must be confirmed by a Korean certified public accountant and recorded in the commercial registry. Any temporary shareholder loan should be separately documented and reported as a current account loan to keep the equity and debt components clean.Outbound trade and operational settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfer, SWIFT MT700/MT710 for commercial letters of credit, and documentary collections through SWIFT MT400/MT750 where appropriate. The Korean foreign exchange bank will perform a post-transaction matching exercise using the underlying invoice, customs declaration, transport or service delivery evidence, and the settlement instruction. Where payments are made to sanctioned or restricted jurisdictions, the bank will apply targeted screening, so treasury must pre-validate all counterparty names, addresses, and ownership data before issuance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not legally permitted to serve as the primary treasury rail for this Korean entity. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, only licensed domestic financial institutions can open KRW accounts, receive foreign currency, and execute FX conversions for resident legal entities. An offshore EMI may only act as a supplementary revenue-collection gateway for foreign client card payments, and it must route settlement funds through a licensed partner bank. Capital injection, dividend remittance, trade settlement, and FX hedging must clear through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank. Therefore, fintech EMIs are rated 'do not use' for statutory corporate banking; Stripe or similar gateways can collect API subscription revenue but those funds must be periodically swept into the Korean bank account to remain fully compliant.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% national corporate income tax for taxable income over KRW 300 billion, plus a 10% local income tax surtax on the national tax amount, producing an aggregate top marginal rate of 26.4%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% aggregate statutory final withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax), unless reduced under an applicable bilateral Double Tax Treaty (DTA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion of same-category foreign related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File preparation; KRW 50 billion of total foreign related-party transactions triggers mandatory Master File preparation. Foreign related-party transactions above KRW 100 million must also be reported in the Schedule of International Transactions attached to the corporate income tax return.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the distinct Abuse of Law Doctrine, substance-over-form principle under Article 14 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, beneficial ownership requirements, and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under the International Tax Adjustment Act all apply with equal force. For a syndicated finance newswire LLC, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must include contract negotiation, API pricing, editorial curation, subscriber onboarding, revenue billing, bank settlement, data hosting, and risk management within Korea. If the Korean entity is only a mailbox while the directors, pricing decisions, and assets sit abroad, the tax authorities will pierce the arrangement and recompute the Korean taxable base.

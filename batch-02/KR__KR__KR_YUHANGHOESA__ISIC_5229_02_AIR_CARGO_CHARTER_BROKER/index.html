Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. South Korea does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Yuhan Hoesa (LLC). However, the Articles of Incorporation must specify the total authorized capital and the initial paid-in amount. For foreign investors, the Foreign Investment Promotion Act requires a minimum foreign investment of KRW 100 million (approximately USD 75,000) to qualify for foreign investment incentives and automatic approval under the Foreign Investment Notification system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Korean Commercial Act, a separate legal person subject to Korean corporate income tax on worldwide income, with no transparent or pass-through classification for Korean tax purposes.

Available election forms

None - Korea has no check-the-box election for entity classification and no hybrid mismatch entity election. A Yuhan Hoesa is mandatorily treated as a corporation. VAT simplified taxpayer treatment may apply when annual taxable turnover is KRW 80 million or less, but that is an indirect tax compliance simplification, not an income tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary exemption threshold for VAT registration; any business operator with a Korean taxable supply nexus must register from the commencement of business. A reduced-compliance simplified taxable person status applies where annual taxable turnover is KRW 80 million or less, but this threshold is not a registration exemption and does not apply to a foreign entity transactionally placed as a general taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where goods are exported under an export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, and for qualifying services supplied to non-residents, including international air cargo transportation, freight forwarding, and cargo charter brokerage, when the place of supply is outside Korea or the service directly supports cargo exported from Korea. The entity must establish through the charter agreement, commercial invoice, air waybill, and foreign exchange settlement that the underlying cargo is transported internationally, and that the service is supplied to a foreign principal or a Korean business in connection with an export transaction. Services performed wholly or partly in the domestic Korean market, including domestic cabotage air movement, import-related warehousing, or brokerage of domestic transportation, are outside the export zero-rating provision and are taxable at 10%.

  • KCS export declaration acceptance record (electronic Export Declaration in UNI-PASS)
  • Air Waybill (MAWB/HAWB) evidencing international carriage of the cargo from Korea to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and charter-party or forwarding contract between the Korean broker and the foreign principal
  • Packing list, cargo manifest, and, where applicable, certificate of origin
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank evidencing receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for supplies made to other business operators through the NTS e-tax invoice system on HomeTax. Tax invoices must be issued electronically by the day following the taxable supply date through HomeTax or an approved API-connected ERP system; the NTS receives the electronic tax invoice data in near real time. Korea does not operate a per-transaction fiscal stamp clearance model, but the NTS applies data matching and compliance analytics across HomeTax, foreign exchange settlement records, and the Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS system to detect missing or manipulated invoices, omitted supply, and anti-fraud anomalies.

Export zero-rating is retrospective and may be clawed back where the supporting documentation is absent, unreliable, mismatched, or created solely to obtain an export VAT refund. If zero-rating is denied, the NTS assesses output VAT at the standard 10% rate on the relevant supply, adds statutory late-payment interest under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and imposes penalty taxes as prescribed by the Value-Added Tax Act, including penalties for false or unfaithful tax invoices. Failure to collect export proceeds within the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline, or without an approved extension, can cause denial of zero-rating and liability under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including fines or imprisonment. Fraudulent export VAT refunds are additionally prosecuted under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the statutory audit lookback periods allow the tax authority to reach back up to 10 years for fraudulent tax evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a predominantly liberalised yet closely monitored foreign exchange regime. The Korean won is free for current account transactions such as trade in goods and services, freight and charter hire payments, and ordinary business expenses, which are settled through licensed foreign exchange banks without prior approval. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment, cross-border loans, and outbound direct investment, remain subject to mandatory reporting, declaration, or prior approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. For an air cargo charter broker in the form of a Yuhan Hoesa, the key friction points are the generation, retention, and repatriation of export service proceeds, as well as the statutory requirement to receive export-related foreign currency within a defined period. While there is no blanket surrender-and-convert obligation compelling firms to exchange all foreign currency into Korean won, companies are required to collect export proceeds into a domestic foreign exchange bank account or, subject to reporting, may hold them offshore. Profit remittances by foreign shareholders are permitted once corporate tax obligations and capital verification are satisfied, but the bank must receive a standard tax clearance certificate and a foreign exchange report confirming the remittance purpose. In practice, convertibility is robust for genuine trade and investment flows, but any deviation from the declared business purpose, round-tripping structures, or undocumented use of overseas accounts will trigger immediate rejection or enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Korean exporters and service providers must collect and repatriate export-related foreign currency receipts within six months from the date of the export declaration or, for cross-border services, completion of the service. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Korean won; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange bank account, but any non-reporting of offshore retention or delayed repatriation beyond the statutory timeline without prior approval constitutes a violation of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (South Korea) alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance, jointly administering the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. As a direct matter of Korean law, fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMIs do not possess the statutory authority to open bank accounts that can receive export proceeds, effect capital verification, or engage in foreign exchange clearing on behalf of a domestic Korean legal entity. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act confines licensed foreign exchange business to registered banks and authorised brokerages. Offshore EMIs operating purely on a payment-institution licence in another jurisdiction cannot clear Korean won or access the local settlement infrastructure. Accordingly, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks for all regulatory capital and trade settlement flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Yuhan Hoesa must be routed through the designated Tier-1 domestic bank under an official foreign investment declaration submitted to the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The remitting shareholder should use a SWIFT MT103 wire with a correct foreign exchange purpose code, typically the code for direct equity investment or paid-in capital. The receiving domestic bank verifies the declaration, issues a Foreign Exchange Bank Certificate of Confirmation, and records the capital in the company's capital verification ledger. All KRW conversion, if required, is executed at the applicable market rate through the licensed bank. Cash injections through unauthorised channels or fintech wallets are prohibited and will be deemed non-compliant.The primary clearing rails for air cargo charter settlements are SWIFT MT103 for direct cash payments to airlines, handling agents, and fuel suppliers; commercial letters of credit through MT700 or MT710 when the beneficiary bank requires documentary assurance; and documentary collections where the air carrier presents title documents and charter invoices. KRW-denominated obligations may clear through the local Korean Won Settlement System, but all cross-border portions must be processed via authenticated SWIFT messages. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. As a direct matter of Korean law, fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMIs do not possess the statutory authority to open bank accounts that can receive export proceeds, effect capital verification, or engage in foreign exchange clearing on behalf of a domestic Korean legal entity. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act confines licensed foreign exchange business to registered banks and authorised brokerages. Offshore EMIs operating purely on a payment-institution licence in another jurisdiction cannot clear Korean won or access the local settlement infrastructure. Accordingly, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks for all regulatory capital and trade settlement flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File must be prepared when the amount of intra-group cross-border transactions with foreign related entities exceeds KRW 5 billion (about USD 3.8 million) during a fiscal year. For transactions in the range of KRW 1 billion to KRW 5 billion, simplified documentation is still required if the taxpayer's total revenue exceeds KRW 10 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Korean tax authorities apply the substance-over-form principle and the international tax abuse rule to deny treaty benefits when an entity does not have the required economic substance. For an air cargo charter broker, the Korean entity must have adequate personnel, an office, and a real decision-making capability in treasury, charter negotiation, and invoicing; if not, the Korean entity may be disregarded as a "sham", and profits are reattributed to its beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 4(1) of the Punishment for Tax Evasion Act imposes up to 7 years imprisonment and a fine equal to not less than 2 times but not exceeding 3 times the amount of corporate tax evaded.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director has made a false statement in the tax return. The director is personally liable for the fine, and the director's assets are subject to attachment. A travel ban (departure prohibition) will be issued by the National Tax Service while the case is under investigation. The director may also be personally disqualified for 5 years from holding any corporate office.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Article 12(2) of the Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs (LCITA) allows an up to 30% penalty on the under-recalculated taxable income in addition to re-assessed tax, and a 10% surcharge on the difference.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's transfer pricing penalty only if the failure was due to gross negligence or willful intent. In addition, the courts may order the director to reimburse the company for the amount of fines under the Korean Commercial Act, and a criminal charge of tax fraud may be filed.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Article 49(2) of the Framework Act on International Tax Affairs empowers the tax authority to ignore the entity and reassess income to the beneficial owner, applying a penalty of 40% of the unreported offshore income.

Director personal liability: This triggers lifting the veil: the director becomes personally liable for the reassessed corporate taxes and penalties to the extent the corporate assets are insufficient. Remedies also include a court order prohibiting the director from leaving the country, and under the Tax Fraud Punishment Act, potential imprisonment for organising the sham structure.

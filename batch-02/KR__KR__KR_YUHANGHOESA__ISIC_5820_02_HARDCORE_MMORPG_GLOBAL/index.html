Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea through the Home Tax Service (HTS) online portal (hometax.go.kr); export declarations are filed at the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS electronic customs system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity wishing to operate a Korean Yuhan Hoesa must register the domestic entity with the competent district tax office within 20 days of business commencement. The process includes: (1) filing an Application for Business Registration with the district tax office via HTS or in person; (2) submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, certificate of foreign registration or evidence of foreign entity existence, foreign investment notification certificate (if applicable), domestic business place lease or ownership proof, identification of the representative director, and a power of attorney for the local tax agent if one is appointed; (3) receiving the 10-digit Business Registration Number (business entity registration number) that functions simultaneously as the corporate TIN and VAT identification number. A foreign non-resident corporation without a domestic place of business that is liable for Korean VAT on electronic services to Korean customers must instead complete the NTS simplified foreign business operator registration through a tax representative and receive a separate foreign business registration number for VAT filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW: There is no general turnover registration threshold for a domestic Yuhan Hoesa; every business operator must register with the NTS before commencing business. A simplified taxpayer regime may apply only to certain small individual businesses with annual taxable supplies not exceeding KRW 80,000,000, but this is an administrative simplification and does not create an exemption from VAT registration. Non-resident electronic service suppliers without a fixed place of business may use the NTS simplified foreign business registration procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Value-Added Tax Act applies to goods exported from Korea and to services supplied to a foreign recipient that are performed or used wholly outside Korea and paid for in foreign currency. For an MMORPG publisher, zero-rating is available for revenue derived from non-Korean users where the game is consumed outside Korea, the contract is with the foreign end user or foreign platform, the app-store territory reports and settlement statements identify foreign jurisdictions, and the foreign currency proceeds are remitted to Korea. Supplies to users in Korea (including digital goods sold through Korean app-store storefronts to local customers) are domestic supplies subject to 10% VAT and cannot be zero-rated. The zero-rating claim must be supported by export and foreign exchange evidence at the time of filing.

  • Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS electronic Export Declaration (full export clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) signed by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign Exchange Bank settlement/receipt confirmation (bank-issued foreign exchange receipt confirmation, equivalent to an Inward Remittance Confirmation)
  • App store platform settlement statements and territory-specific sales reports (for digital game exports)

Electronic tax invoicing is mandatory through the NTS Home Tax Service (HTS) e-tax invoice system. A VAT-registered supplier must issue an e-tax invoice for each taxable supply and transmit the e-tax invoice data to NTS electronically using HTS-approved API integration or the HTS portal by the statutory filing deadline; paper tax invoices are not generally permitted. Cash receipts for consumer sales are processed through the NTS cash-receipt system and transmitted to NTS at the point of sale. There is no separate physical anti-fraud fiscal stamp, but the NTS continuously reconciles e-tax invoices, cash receipts, credit card settlement data, and customs data to enforce VAT compliance. For MMORPG sales through Apple App Store and Google Play, the responsible VAT invoice issuer is determined by the contract structure: if the app store is the supplier/reseller, the platform handles Korean VAT; if the Yuhan Hoesa is the supplier and the store only processes payment, the Yuhan Hoesa must issue e-tax invoices and report the supplies through HTS.

The NTS cross-matches the UNI-PASS export declaration data, VAT return zero-rated entries, and foreign exchange bank receipt records. If any statutory document is absent, inconsistent, or fabricated, the 0% rate is revoked and VAT is assessed at the standard 10% rate on the gross supply. Additional tax penalties are imposed under the Framework Act on National Taxes and the Value-Added Tax Act, generally 20% of the underpaid tax for negligent underreporting and up to 40% for intentional or fraudulent underreporting, plus daily interest for late payment. A false zero-rating claim can also trigger criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and any refund previously claimed is clawed back with interest from the original refund date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Current account transactions, including software royalties, influencer payments, subscriber revenues, and international settlement of digital content, are generally freely convertible through designated foreign exchange banks with reporting obligations. Capital account transactions are subject to a higher level of administrative scrutiny; foreign direct investment in the Korean LLC must be declared to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or a delegated bank, and the subscribed capital must be deposited into a domestic temporary account to obtain a foreign-investment registration certificate. Dividend distributions and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permissible but require evidence of audited net profits, tax clearance on Korean corporate tax and dividend withholding tax, board payment resolution, and the absence of outstanding obligations. Foreign currency proceeds from digital MMORPG sales, including Apple App Store and Google Play payouts, are usually received in USD and can be retained in foreign currency bank accounts or sold on exchange. Surrender requirements are minimal; no blanket income conversion is mandatory. However, the Ministry of Economy and Finance can impose temporary conversion ceilings or use safety clauses under IMF-style early warning conditions. Special attention should be paid to split treatment between goods-related trade settlements and services payments in the automated reporting system to avoid enforcement penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, exporters of digital content and software must obtain settlement of export proceeds within 180 days from the date of export; the original due date is 90 days with a single 90-day extension upon application to the designated bank. Export receipts may be credited to a foreign currency account without any statutory conversion ratio for ordinary service and software exports; this means the mandatory surrender/convertibility ratio is 0% at standard operating conditions. In a financial imbalance emergency, the Ministry of Economy and Finance, via the Bank of Korea, may impose a temporary mandatory conversion requirement up to 30% of export receipts, with prior public notice. The exporter must file a monthly reporting of outstanding export claims and may be required to transfer all settlement foreign currency to an onshore foreign currency account maintained with a designated foreign exchange bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (The Central Bank of the Republic of Korea), acting as the foreign exchange information administrator under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act delegated by the Ministry of Economy and Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI viability is Low for primary treasury operations. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank payment service provider must obtain a license from the Financial Services Commission to handle Korean won deposits or receive electronic payments in Korea. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, and Payoneer are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Korea and cannot legally operate a Korean won business account for an onshore LLC. Their utility is limited to receiving inbound cross-border royalty or payout settlement from Apple, Google, or foreign publishers in USD or EUR, and even that channel must be routed through a domestic intermediary bank subject to foreign exchange reporting. We recommend using a domestic Tier-1 bank for all statutory capital, payroll, and corporate disbursement accounts; an offshore EMI may be used after approval as a supplementary collection pocket, but not for capital verification, tax filing, or FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital contributions, the Korean LLC must open a temporary receipt account at a designated foreign exchange bank and file a foreign-investment report to the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, or the bank, before remittance. The investor should send the wire via SWIFT MT103 using the foreign exchange purpose code for direct equity investment by a non-resident in a domestic company, with the foreign investment report number in the remittance information field and the Korean LLC as the beneficiary. After the capital arrives, the bank will issue a Foreign Investment Capital Verification Report and a Foreign-Invested Enterprise registration certificate; the temporary capital escrow account is then converted into the operating account. For software revenue settlement, Apple and Google payouts enter via SWIFT MT103 with the service-related foreign exchange code for software royalties, not as capital. All cross-border flows must be filed in the Bank of Korea automated reporting system within the prescribed deadline.Outbound payments for MMORPG developers, contractors, license fees, and marketing vendors should be initiated onshore through SWIFT MT103 for services and royalties, using the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act service payment purpose code. For physical merchandise such as boxed game copies or peripherals, a commercial letter of credit is acceptable when the transaction is trade-backed; for digital content, an L/C is rarely used. Documentary collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) are possible but not recommended for recurring royalty payments because digital transactions lack a transport document trail; SWIFT MT103 with invoice and contract is the standard clearing rail. For payouts from Apple and Google, each daily settlement file should be matched to the player-facing tax records and to the revenue reports in App Store Connect and Play Console. All outbound won wires must clear through the Bank of Korea Won-based electronic settlement system, while USD and major currency wires clear through the onshore treasury foreign currency settlement network to correspondent banks.Deliverable forward exchange contracts with an onshore Bank of Korea licensed foreign exchange bank, available only when the Korean LLC can evidence the underlying USD/KRW trade, royalty, or service exposure and the contract maturity is aligned with the corresponding transaction date.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted for hedging the same underlying exposure when the settlement must remain in Korean won or the onshore bank cannot deliver foreign currency; NDFs must be booked with an onshore entity and are limited to real-demand exposure documentation under the Financial Supervisory Service principle of legitimate hedging.Cross-currency swaps are permissible for long-term foreign currency funding or long-dated license receivables where the notional is matched to the USD revenue stream from Apple App Store or Google Play, and anti-abuse rules prohibit swap-only speculation; the swap must be tied to a specific balance sheet asset or liability.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI viability is Low for primary treasury operations. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, a non-bank payment service provider must obtain a license from the Financial Services Commission to handle Korean won deposits or receive electronic payments in Korea. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, and Payoneer are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Korea and cannot legally operate a Korean won business account for an onshore LLC. Their utility is limited to receiving inbound cross-border royalty or payout settlement from Apple, Google, or foreign publishers in USD or EUR, and even that channel must be routed through a domestic intermediary bank subject to foreign exchange reporting. We recommend using a domestic Tier-1 bank for all statutory capital, payroll, and corporate disbursement accounts; an offshore EMI may be used after approval as a supplementary collection pocket, but not for capital verification, tax filing, or FX hedging.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File preparation and filing are required for cross-border related-party transactions when the aggregate annual value exceeds KRW 10 billion; below that threshold, contemporaneous TP documentation and set-the-price policies remain advisable as evidence under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a free-zone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the Korean tax authorities apply a rigorous economic-substance doctrine through judicial anti-abuse principles and the Adjustment of International Taxes Act: the Korean entity must demonstrate actual employees, servers, gameplay operations, contract enforcement capability, and decision-making in Korea as the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the MMORPG platform; an offshore shell that bills all profits away from Korea without real activity will be disregarded, and profits will be reattributed to the Korean Yuhan Hoesa

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3(1): imprisonment for up to 10 years or a fine up to 5 times the tax evaded; and an administrative 40% fraudulent underreporting surcharge under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-3; the unpaid corporate tax and surcharge are immediately collectible by the National Tax Service

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors are jointly and severally liable to the Republic of Korea and the company under the Commercial Act Articles 389(3) and 399 for unpaid corporate tax and damages; the National Tax Service may seize personal bank accounts, real property, and personal passports, issue a travel ban (blacklisting) and denial of Korean tax clearance, and impose criminal imprisonment of the responsible director for up to 10 years
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act Article 17(1) and recharacterization of the shell structure; the tax authority will reallocate income to the Korean entity and impose a 20% aggregate penalty on the adjusted transaction; unpaid withholding tax is reassessed at the statutory 22% plus daily overdue interest

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or participated in the sham arrangement are personally liable for the unpaid non-resident withholding tax and penalties; the director may face a travel ban under the National Tax Collection Act, asset attachment, criminal liability for breach of trust under the Commercial Act Article 356, and imprisonment for up to 7 years if fraud against the company is established
Transfer Pricing Documentation and False Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: Under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act Article 13, the National Tax Service may adjust the intercompany price to an arm's-length value and impose an additional transfer pricing penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted income (10% if voluntarily corrected but late); failure to submit documentation separately attracts a penalty of 5% of the transaction value or KRW 100 million, whichever is greater

Director personal liability: Personal liability arises under the Commercial Act for directors who knowingly signed false tax returns and transfer pricing documentation; the National Tax Service can designate the director as a serious non-compliant taxpayer, impose a travel ban, order civil restitution for the additional tax and fines, and pursue criminal charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act for willful falsification of documents, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to 10 times the false amount

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. There is no blocked escrow account requirement under Korean law; however, before the registry will accept the incorporation filing, the full amount of the capital stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be deposited with a licensed bank in the Republic of Korea and confirmed by a Certificate of Paid-in Capital Deposit. For foreign-invested capital exceeding KRW 100 million, the investor must file a foreign investment report with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy or its designated receival agency, and any foreign exchange transaction must be made through a bank authorized for foreign exchange business.

Funding & unlock path: The warehouse and office must obtain a Fire Safety Completion Inspection under the Fire Suppression and Fire-Fighting Facility Installation, Maintenance, and Safety Management Act, including certification of fire extinguishers, smoke detectors, emergency exits, and sprinkler systems where required by building area and racking height. A structural loading clearance is required if the warehouse uses high-density pallet racking. The company must also obtain a workplace safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Act from the Ministry of Employment and Labor, covering chemical substance safety data sheets, personal protective equipment, emergency response plans, and worker training before commencing API handling operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). Customs administration is vested in the Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity with no domestic place of business must appoint a domestic tax representative under Article 109 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and file a business registration application with the jurisdictional district tax office via HomeTax. Required supporting documents include: (i) executed power of attorney to the domestic tax representative, (ii) a certified copy of the foreign company charter or articles of incorporation and foreign business registration certificate, (iii) proof of foreign tax residency, (iv) evidence of a Korean fixed place of business (e.g., lease agreement or branch address registration), and (v) identity documents of the tax representative and ultimate beneficial owner. The NTS issues the Business Registration Certificate (Saeupja Deungrokjeung) containing the ten-digit business registration number, which serves simultaneously as the corporate tax identification number and the VAT registration number. Foreign entities deriving only royalty, service, or dividend income without a domestic fixed place normally do not require business registration and instead are subject to simplified withholding tax procedures; however, any import-then-export wholesale operation involving physical inventory in Korea mandates business registration before customs clearance. Applications are screened against the Korea Customs Service and local licensing requirements; for a pharmaceutical API wholesale entity, submission of the statutory pharmaceutical wholesaler license is additionally required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 10,000,000 (aproximately USD 8,000) of taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year; a new business engaging in taxable supplies must apply for VAT business registration within 20 days from the date operations commence. Where a foreign entity lacks a domestic place of business, VAT registration is required through a tax representative if it makes a taxable supply from a Korean fixed establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 22(1) of the Value-Added Tax Act, zero-rated VAT applies to exported goods and qualified services supplied to non-residents. For a pharmaceutical API wholesale exporter, the statutory criteria are: (i) the goods must be physically moved out of South Korea and delivered overseas under a customs export declaration accepted by the Korea Customs Service, (ii) the exporter must hold a definitive foreign exchange payment evidence recognized by a licensed Korean bank, and (iii) the export declaration number must be reconciled with the VAT return. The supply becomes zero-rated at the time the export is declared, irrespective of the sale price; however, input VAT incurred on purchase of locally sourced APIs or business expenses is creditable, and any excess input VAT after the zero-rated sales is refundable under the accelerated export refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Export Entry) accepted by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS, bearing the export declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical conveyance of goods outside South Korea
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds certificate demonstrating receipt of payment in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice in English or Korean describing the pharmaceutical APIs and their H.S. classification
  • Packing List
  • Statutory pharmaceutical wholesale export approval/license and any certificate of analysis required by the destination country

All VAT invoices for business-to-business supplies must be issued as electronic tax invoices (e-tax invoices) through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system or a certified ERP integration application programming interface. The e-tax invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS at the time of issuance, implementing near-real-time fiscal reporting. No separate manual clearance stamp or physical anti-fraud fiscal marking is required; however, each e-tax invoice carries a unique NTS-issued electronic invoice number, and the two fiscal reporting cycles for VAT returns (half-yearly, with interim filings) are mandatory. Failure to transmit e-tax invoices on time or incorrect data submission exposes the taxpayer to penalty tax under Article 32 of the Value-Added Tax Act, ranging from 1% to 3% of the invoice value depending on the violation.

The NTS cross-matches each zero-rated VAT return against the Korea Customs Service export declaration database and bank foreign exchange receipt records. If zero-rating is claimed without a valid export declaration, or without the goods having actually left Korean customs territory, the NTS will recharacterize the supply as a domestic taxable supply, levy the standard 10% VAT, and impose penalty tax: (i) 20% of the relevant tax amount for negligent understatement under the Framework Act on National Taxes, (ii) 40% for fraudulent or egregious misstatement, plus statutory interest computed on a daily basis from the original return due date. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 540-day statutory settlement period under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act are separately subject to KCS administrative fines and may trigger cancellation of the export declaration, with retrospective VAT clawback and penalties. Where patterns of fabricated export declarations or forged FIRCs are detected, the case is referred to the Supreme Prosecutors' Office for tax evasion fraud with potential imprisonment of up to five years, fines up to KRW 100,000,000, and additional punitive charges under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The South Korean won (KRW) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, and for most capital account transactions, subject to reporting and declaration requirements under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds; exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic foreign currency accounts or overseas accounts, but must repatriate proceeds within a statutory period (generally one year from the export date, with extensions possible on application). Profit remittance for foreign investors, including dividends, is permitted after settlement of corporate tax liabilities and is subject to a 20% withholding tax (or treaty-reduced rate), with supporting documents such as audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board minutes for dividend declaration, and a remittance purpose report. For capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment (FDI) capital injection and loans, prior declaration or registration is required with the Bank of Korea via a foreign exchange bank, but there is no general licensing bottleneck. Cross-border payments must be routed through authorized foreign exchange banks; use of virtual assets or crypto-assets is strictly regulated. South Korea does not impose a dual-rate system or currency controls that prevent full convertibility; rather, the regime relies on transaction transparency, reporting thresholds, and AML/KYC compliance. A pharmaceutical API wholesale entity will require typical trade finance documentation, including invoices and transport documents, to support the underlying transaction and avoid anti-money-laundering scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion into KRW. Exporters must repatriate foreign currency receipts within one year from the date of exportation (the 'collection period') unless an extension is granted by a foreign exchange bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, and funds may be held in Foreign Exchange Accounts, Foreign Exchange Settlement Accounts, or overseas accounts. However, the export letter of credit, bill of exchange, or shipping documents must be reported to the designated foreign exchange bank within 15 days of export approval, and actual settlement must be completed through an authorized bank to demonstrate compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act administered by the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. South Korean law under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree restricts foreign exchange business to authorized foreign exchange banks. Fintech EMIs such as Payoneer, Wise, and Airwallex do not hold a Korean foreign exchange bank license and are legally barred from directly offering foreign exchange settlement or proprietary cross-border clearing to Korean entities. They can only operate as payment facilitators or technology providers in partnership with licensed domestic banks, under the bank's compliance and reporting framework. For a high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale entity requiring LC and comprehensive bank confirmation, relying on a fintech EMI without direct Korean settlement rails is not viable; the statutory corporate banking relationship must be with a licensed tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injection by a foreign shareholder into the Korean LLC, the compliant routing is as follows: (1) the non-resident investor opens a Foreign-investment Registration via the investee company's central bank designated foreign exchange bank, submitting an application for registration with the Bank of Korea. (2) The remittance is executed via SWIFT MT103 including a purpose of payment/code such as 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Subscription' or 'Capital Increase', referencing the registered investment number. (3) The receiving bank credits a temporary capital escrow account or a Foreign Exchange Purchase Account, and after receiving the wire, the bank issues a Certificate of Acceptance of Foreign Investment and completes a cap verification exchange transaction. (4) The capital is transferred to the LLC's operating account only after submission of the Certificate of Receipt of Foreign Investment, proof of business registration, and a settlement disclosure form to the Tax Office. (5) For foreign exchange affairs, a 'Report of Payment for Foreign Debt' may also be required if the injection is structured as shareholder loan. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low. South Korean law under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree restricts foreign exchange business to authorized foreign exchange banks. Fintech EMIs such as Payoneer, Wise, and Airwallex do not hold a Korean foreign exchange bank license and are legally barred from directly offering foreign exchange settlement or proprietary cross-border clearing to Korean entities. They can only operate as payment facilitators or technology providers in partnership with licensed domestic banks, under the bank's compliance and reporting framework. For a high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale entity requiring LC and comprehensive bank confirmation, relying on a fintech EMI without direct Korean settlement rails is not viable; the statutory corporate banking relationship must be with a licensed tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion

Economic substance scope: South Korea has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to offshore financial centers, but applies an economic substance and substance-over-form doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in the Framework Act on National Taxes (Article 17) and the International Tax Adjustment Act (Articles 4 and 6). A Korean wholesale API distributor must demonstrate real business purpose and substance: physical premises, licensed pharmaceutical wholesale staff, actual purchase and sales contracts, inventory custody and risk-bearing, local management decisions, and independent financial capacity. A pure shell company or conduit lacking such substance may be recharacterized, its income attributed to the economic owner, and treaty benefits denied under the principal purpose test or limitation-on-benefits provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: National Tax Service reassessment with 20% additional tax for false returns on the underreported amount; late payment surcharges of 0.022% per day; criminal liability under the Punishment for Tax Evaders Act with imprisonment of up to 5 years for aggravated tax fraud or a fine not exceeding two times (serious cases up to five times) the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the statutory representative are jointly and severally liable for tax debts where corporate assets are dissipated or where the director personally directed the evasion; courts may impose travel bans, asset seizure in the director's name, and imprisonment in aggravated cases.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority adjusts the transaction price to the arm's length amount, increases taxable income, imposes a 10% additional penalty for underreporting on the resulting deficiency, and may deny deduction of the overstated expenses; statutory interest and anti-abuse penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation face personal secondary liability when the company cannot pay the additional tax assessment; severe cases may lead to criminal liability for aiding tax evasion.
Shell company, treaty abuse, and disguised profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the payment as a dividend, triggering 22% withholding tax; denial of DTA benefits under the principal purpose test; imposition of a 20% additional tax on unreported dividends; recovery of unpaid withholding tax with interest and penalties; possible criminal referral for willful tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who are the ultimate beneficiaries of the recharacterized payments may be personally liable for the unpaid withholding taxes; the corporate veil may be pierced where the entity is used as a sham, subjecting directors to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
AML, sanctions, and pharmaceutical licensing violations

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Financial Transactions Reports Act and Foreign Exchange Transactions Act can lead to imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to KRW 50 million; the competent pharmaceutical regulator may revoke the wholesale license and impose administrative monetary penalties; sanctioned business may be suspended and assets subject to freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal fines and imprisonment, asset freezing, and debarment from licensed pharmaceutical activities; the limited liability shield is pierced where the company is a mere instrumentality of illegal conduct, exposing directors to civil damages and personal monetary penalties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act. A Yuhan Hoesa may be incorporated with no statutory minimum paid-up capital, provided there is at least one member and one director. All capital stated in the articles of incorporation, if any, must be deposited into a temporary corporate bank account in the company's name before registration is completed. No blocked escrow account requirement exists. Foreign investors must route contributions through a foreign exchange bank and, where applicable, file a Foreign Investment report under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) via the HomeTax System electronic portal (hometax.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business in South Korea must appoint a tax representative (a resident individual or local corporation) registered with the NTS. The representative must apply to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the representative's business location or the Seoul National Tax Service for non-residents for a Business Registration Certificate using the standard application for business registration. The application must confirm the foreign entity's constitutional documents, foreign tax identification number, and proof of a Korean bank account used for tax payments. Upon approval, the NTS issues a Business Registration Number (SINBRI) which serves as the corporate tax ID and value-added tax ID. The foreign entity is then required to comply with all periodic filing, e-invoice, and payment obligations through the tax representative or through a fixed place of business if one is established.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity without a domestic place of business in South Korea must appoint a tax representative (a resident individual or local corporation) registered with the NTS. The representative must apply to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the representative's business location or the Seoul National Tax Service for non-residents for a Business Registration Certificate using the standard application for business registration. The application must confirm the foreign entity's constitutional documents, foreign tax identification number, and proof of a Korean bank account used for tax payments. Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not maintain a check-the-box or hybrid entity classification election analogous to the US Treasury regulations. A Yuhan Hoesa is always treated as a taxable domestic corporation, subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act and to the Value-Added Tax Act as a supply business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0. Any person independently supplying taxable goods or services in South Korea must register for VAT before commencing business, irrespective of expected turnover. However, a non-resident engaged only in exports or qualifying cross-border services with no domestic taxable supply may not be required to obtain a Korean VAT registration unless the supply is subject to Korean VAT under the reverse charge or has a fixed establishment in Korea. The annual turnover threshold for simplified taxation for small individual entrepreneurs is KRW 80,000,000, but this does not exempt registration for corporations or foreign entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% is applied to the export of goods when the goods are taken out of South Korea under an export declaration, and to services supplied to a place outside South Korea where the benefit of the services is consumed outside Korea. Qualifying services include international transport, services physically rendered outside Korea, and digital or consulting services provided to foreign non-resident clients with no taxable domestic use. The exporter must keep full documentary evidence of the exporter's identity, the customer's identity, the export contract, delivery terms, and proof that the goods actually left Korea. For offshore digital services supplied by a foreign business to a Korean customer, the domestic customer may be subject to reverse-charge VAT; exports by Korean suppliers must meet the place-of-supply rules and supporting foreign exchange documentation to claim 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate) prepared and lodged through UNI-PASS and confirmed by Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual shipment out of Korea
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Confirmation (including Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate or FIRC-equivalent issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank) evidencing collection of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Export sales invoice matched to the customs declaration and the underlying purchase order or contract

All VAT-registered businesses are required to issue and transmit electronic tax invoices through the HomeTax System for all business-to-business supplies of taxable goods or services. The NTS operates a real-time anti-fraud clearance mechanism in which every e-tax invoice must be transmitted to HomeTax within one day of the supply date. Cash receipts are mandatory for retail supplies exceeding KRW 100,000, with mandatory real-time reporting. Tax invoices for exports must be issued and the export details reported in the electronic taxation of exports. There is no fiscal stamping or government pre-clearance of individual VAT invoices; instead the NTS uses continuous validation, data matching against customs declarations, and automatic cross-checks to detect suppressed or false invoices.

The National Tax Service will deny zero-rating and retroactively assess VAT at 10% plus statutory interest if the export is later determined to be fictitious, unsubstantiated, or not economically real. The attraction or wrongful receipt of VAT refunds on unverified zero-rated exports triggers penalty surcharges under the Framework Act on Taxes: up to 40% of the unpaid or evaded tax for fraudulent claims, or up to 20% for negligent unfaithful filing. Additional penalties apply for failure to issue electronic tax invoices, failure to transmit them within the statutory deadline, and foreign exchange law violations for unrepatriated export proceeds. The NTS may refer egregious cases to the public prosecutor's office for criminal tax evasion proceedings, resulting in imprisonment and additional fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized yet strictly monitored foreign exchange system under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won (KRW) is generally convertible for current account transactions, including consultancy fees, dividends, and trade-related payments, but capital account operations such as equity investment in a local entity, acquisition of domestic debt instruments, or cross-border loans are subject to filing or reporting requirements with the designated foreign exchange bank, and in certain cases, prior approval from the MOEF/BOK. A statutory surrender requirement exists for export of goods and services: all export proceeds must be received in foreign currency and either sold to a domestic bank within the prescribed period or deposited into a foreign currency account; service exporters, including management consultancy firms, must adhere to a six-month collection and settlement deadline unless an extension is granted. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permissible upon presentation of audited financial statements and tax clearance, but anti-money-laundering (AML) screening and beneficial ownership declarations add a two-to-four-week bank compliance cycle. In practice, repatriation of capital gains and dividends requires a foreign exchange certificate of transaction (performance confirmation) issued by the receiving bank, and any hedging instrument must be tied to an underlying real demand to avoid speculative classification. The overall regime is best described as current-account open, capital-account managed, with high transparency and strong penalties for non-reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, export proceeds (including consultant service invoices billed to overseas clients) must be collected within six months (or one year for projects approved by the MOEF) from the date of export. There is no mandatory forced conversion to KRW; if the exporter maintains a domestic foreign-currency account, the receipts can be retained, but the bank must be notified and the transaction reported to the Korea Customs Service via the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). Any extension beyond the statutory period requires an ex-ante or ex-post approval depending on the reason, such as delayed payment due to inspection or litigation. For KRW settlement needs, the exporter may sell the foreign currency to a domestic bank at the prevailing market rate with no prior approval required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) and the Korea Customs Service (KCS) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not viable as primary banking providers for a Korean registered entity. The Korean financial regulatory framework does not confer passporting rights to foreign EMIs and any entity performing money transfer services, payment gateway operations, or FX intermediation in KRW without a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act (EFTA) and the Real Name Financial Transaction Act is committing an unlicensed foreign-exchange activity. A statutory Korean corporation must maintain its transactional banking relationship with a licensed domestic bank for capital escrow, operational KRW settlement, and trade-related fees. Offshore EMIs can only be used incidentally for employee perks or outbound small-value credit card top-ups, but they cannot act as the treasury rails for corporate disbursements or inbound capital. The compliance risk of routing client consultancy fees through an unlicensed EMI triggers debanking by Korean counterparties and potential criminal liability under FETA Article 8; therefore the treasury blueprint forbids such routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Yuhan Hoesa must be wired from the remitting shareholder via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code compliant with the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS), typically '821101 - Acquisition of equity shares' for paid-in capital or '821201 - Long-term loan' for shareholder subordinated debt. The remittance must be received at a licensed foreign exchange bank before the filing of the foreign-investment report is completed; the bank will issue a 'certificate of foreign direct investment' after funds are credited and the Korea Trade Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) acknowledgment is obtained. For capital verification under the Commercial Act, a certified public accountant (CPA) statement must reference the bank receipt and the foreign exchange register number. The exchange bank will independently check that the remitter matches the UBO name on the shareholder register, the purpose code matches the statutory scope, and no mixing occurs with third-party strings. All incoming wires must avoid using personal remittance codes or omitting the corporate registration number, as that leads to suspension and possible conversion penalty.Outbound treasury operations will use three primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for operational fees, licensing royalties, and management consultancy invoices where the beneficiary invoice contains a clear economic rationale; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) when the Korean entity purchases software licenses or digital content with a physical deliverable, enabling the bank to verify shipment and invoice; and (3) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for smaller trade contracts with trusted counterparties, reducing bank charges but preserving traceability. For every outbound payment above USD 5,000, the Korean bank requires a supporting invoice, contract, and the appropriate FEIS purpose code (e.g., '321201 - Management fees' for cross-border management consulting, or '321302 - R&D services' if applicable). Cross-border disbursements in KRW are not permitted as a default; settlements must be effectuated in USD, EUR, or a fully convertible currency, and the Korean bank will convert the underlying KRW deposit at the rate published by the Seoul Money Brokerage Services on the value date. Any payment to a tax haven jurisdiction or a blacklisted bank is automatically held for manual AML review, so the treasury team must maintain a counterparty whitelist in the KYC system.Onshore forward FX contracts with maturities up to 12 months for hedging real trade receivables or payables, requiring a confirmed invoice and a bank-approved underlying exposure form under FETA guidelines.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for off-shore entities hedging KRW exposure; the Korean Yuhan Hoesa itself cannot access the onshore NDF market, but its parent may use NDFs offshore to synthetically hedge the subsidiary's net profit repatriation, provided the contract is executed with a global bank that has no onshore KRW position.Currency swaps with a maturity exceeding 12 months and associated with long-term intercompany loans from the parent to the Yuhan Hoesa, subject to MOEF filing when the swap notional exceeds the debt-to-equity safe harbor threshold (currently 150% under the Korean tax code).Zero-cost collars and vanilla options on USD/KRW with a maximum tenor of 12 months, solely for hedging the forecasted annual consulting fee receipts from US and European clients, requiring board-approved hedging policy and monthly valuation reporting to the bank's Treasury Division.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not viable as primary banking providers for a Korean registered entity. The Korean financial regulatory framework does not confer passporting rights to foreign EMIs and any entity performing money transfer services, payment gateway operations, or FX intermediation in KRW without a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act (EFTA) and the Real Name Financial Transaction Act is committing an unlicensed foreign-exchange activity. A statutory Korean corporation must maintain its transactional banking relationship with a licensed domestic bank for capital escrow, operational KRW settlement, and trade-related fees. Offshore EMIs can only be used incidentally for employee perks or outbound small-value credit card top-ups, but they cannot act as the treasury rails for corporate disbursements or inbound capital. The compliance risk of routing client consultancy fees through an unlicensed EMI triggers debanking by Korean counterparties and potential criminal liability under FETA Article 8; therefore the treasury blueprint forbids such routing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% progressive corporate income tax rate (10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million; 20% on the next KRW 200 million; 22% on the next KRW 280 billion; 25% above KRW 300 billion) plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national corporate tax, resulting in a combined marginal headline rate of 27.5% for large taxable income brackets

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% on the dividend payment to a non-resident shareholder, composed of 20% corporate income tax withheld under Article 98 of the Corporate Income Tax Act plus 2% local income tax surcharge; this rate applies before any Double Tax Agreement (DTA) reduction, with withholding and remittance due on the 10th day of the month following the dividend distribution

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year-end plus 3 months for the annual tax return (e.g., a December 31 fiscal year-end is due on March 31 of the following year); corporations with an advance tax year-end must file within 3 months of the appointed closing date; quarterly advance corporate tax payments are due within 2 months after the end of each six-month advance payment period on June 30 and December 31, meaning an interim return must be filed by August 31 and February 28 respectively

Deadline: · Frequency:

Failure to file a corporate income tax return by the statutory deadline triggers: (i) a blanket non-filing penalty of 20% of the unpaid tax, (ii) an understatement penalty ranging from 10% to 30% of the understated tax depending on egregiousness (gross understatement 30%); (iii) daily overdue surcharge of 0.022% per day on the unpaid tax using a 365-day annual rate; (iv) a false tax invoice penalty of 2% of the invoice amount; and (v) criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act if the tax evaded exceeds KRW 300 million (imprisonment up to 7 years or fine up to 3 times the evaded tax). Corporate directors and compliance officers are personally liable for the unpaid taxes plus penalties where they intentionally concealed cross-border income or failed to submit required transfer pricing documents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Articles 6(5) and 7 of the International Tax Adjustment Act and Enforcement Decree provisions, a taxpayer engaged in international related-party transactions must prepare a Local File if the annual intercompany transaction value exceeds KRW 5 billion, and a Master File if its consolidated annual revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and total international related-party transactions exceed KRW 5 billion; in addition, a short-form local report is required for transactions exceeding KRW 1 billion, and a data submission deadline of 60 days upon NTS request (not less than 30 days) applies for the ordinary documentation; failure to submit contemporaneous TP documentation may result in a 20% penalty of the claimed adjustment amount and a discretionary portion from the NTS in determining the arm's length price

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply the specific OECD Economic Substance Requirements for zero-tax jurisdictions; instead, the national doctrine of substance-over-form, reinforced by the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) in Article 6(2) of the International Tax Adjustment Act and the 'effective management and control' test, allows the tax authority to recharacterize transactions or deny a claimed treaty benefit where the Korean resident company or the foreign owner lacks the functional profile (people, premises, core income-generating activities) expected of a true intermediary; for a cross-border advisory firm such as ISIC 7020, the NTS will scrutinize whether the Korean entity is the principal engaged in the CIGA (negotiating contracts, billing clients, bearing economic risks, and employing substantive senior consultants) rather than an instrument of a foreign principal; if substance is deemed insufficient, the income is reattributed to the non-resident principals, the treaty withholding exemptions are denied, and the corporate veil of the Yuhan Hoesa is pierced for WHT and criminal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion through omission of cross-border income and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Violates Articles 9 and 10 of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act and Article 14 of the same Act: the sanction is imprisonment for up to 7 years or a fine of up to 3 times the amount of the tax evaded; the NTS imposes an additional understatement penalty ratio of 30% and a non-filing penalty of 20% of the unpaid tax, and the unpaid tax is increased by a daily arrears surcharge of 0.022%

Director personal liability: The corporate form is pierced: the representative director is jointly and severally liable for the entire unpaid tax and penalties, as an 'owner-employee' responsible for the deliberate false return; the director's personal assets are subject to seizure, a travel ban (blacklisting) is issued by the Ministry of Justice Communications Office, and repatriation of the director or funds is prohibited; criminal prosecution leads to a Korean jail term; in contrast to the limited liability of the Yuhan Hoesa, the court treats the director as co-conspirator for the fraudulent scheme, and civil restitution order forces full reimbursement of the foreign-diverted funds with interest
Transfer pricing manipulation, false documentation, and fraudulent intercompany payments

Statutory sanction: Article 12(1) and Article 6(3) of the International Tax Adjustment Act authorize the NTS to disallow the non-arm's length deduction and impose an adjustment of the taxable income; the penalty is a 30% surcharge on the adjusted amount for false TP reporting; an additional 3-year limitation extension applies; if the scheme exceeds KRW 300 million, criminal sanctions apply under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act Article 11(1) with imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine up to 3 times the amount dodged; foreign withholding tax at 22% on the fictitious payment is also reclaimed and imposed on the Korean payor as withholding agent

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner-directors of the Korean entity are treated as joint offenders with the non-resident recipient; courts order a piercing so that the directors' personal financial records are in scope, and any transfer to personal accounts is clawed back; the director is liable for the entire unpaid tax and penalties without any limited-company cap; travel bans are issued against the director because the NTS has classified the case as a large delinquent tax matter; criminal custodial sentence of up to 7 years is sought, and the court may additionally order compensation to the Korean tax administration for lost foreign tax credit and improperly deducted service fees
Shell company misuse and treaty benefit abuse in dividend and royalty restructuring

Statutory sanction: Article 4 and Article 6(2) of the International Tax Adjustment Act plus the framework of Article 172-1 of the Corporate Income Tax Act permit denial of all DTA benefits and application of the 22% domestic WHT; the NTS imposes an additional 15% penalty under the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act for under-withholding of non-resident tax; the entire withholding tax uncollected is re-assessed to the Korean legal entity and its directors; criminal prosecution under Article 10(1) of the Punishment of Tax Offenses Act carries imprisonment up to 5 years or fine equal to the evaded tax; the anti-abuse ruling also triggers a 40% negligence surcharge for severe omission

Director personal liability: If the director approved or knew of the sham arrangement, the court pierces the Yuhan Hoesa veil: the director is personally liable for the WHT, penalties, and all interest; NTS blacklists the director's passport, imposes a travel ban, and registers the director's name on the public delinquent taxpayer list; the director's personal bank accounts and real estate are attached; if the director is a foreign national residing outside Korea, the Korean court issues a mutual legal assistance request to enforce the criminal fine and restitution order, and extradition may be pursued; the director's civil liability also includes a non-dischargeable obligation to the Korean government that survives personal bankruptcy under Korean insolvency law

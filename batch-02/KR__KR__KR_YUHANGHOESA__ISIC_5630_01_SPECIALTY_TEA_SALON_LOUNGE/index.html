Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea - HomeTax online portal (https://www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS for export declaration processing

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity commencing activity in Korea must, within 20 days of business commencement, file an Application for Business Registration (사업자등록 신청) with the competent district tax office that has jurisdiction over its Korean place of business or, in the absence of a Korean place of business, through a duly appointed tax representative. The application may be submitted online through HomeTax or physically at the district tax office. Required attachments include the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, foreign business registration certificate or equivalent foreign tax registration evidence, the entity's foreign tax identification number, proof of a Korean place of business (ownership deed or lease agreement), identity and authorization documents for the domestic tax representative, and apostilled or consular legalized constitutional documents. Upon acceptance, NTS issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which functions as both the corporate taxpayer identification number and the VAT registration number. A foreign corporation with no Korean business establishment and no VAT obligations may instead apply for a Foreigner Tax Code through the competent district tax office for purposes of domestic withholding and income tax compliance. A tax representative is mandatory for a foreign non-resident entity with Korean-source tax obligations and no fixed place of business in Korea.

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0; VAT registration is mandatory for every person carrying on an enterprise in Korea, with registration required within 20 days of business commencement. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exported goods when a Korea Customs Service export declaration has been accepted and the goods physically leave Korea within the statutory period. Qualifying services supplied to non-residents, including offshore digital content services, are zero-rated when the recipient is a non-resident, the place of supply is outside Korea, and consideration is received in foreign currency or through foreign exchange settlement from the non-resident customer. For specialty tea and cultural ceramic goods sold to overseas consumers, each export shipment must be traceable to an accepted customs export declaration or an equivalent e-commerce export parcel record. The exporter must retain documentation evidencing actual shipment, export payment, and foreign exchange receipt in order to claim the corresponding input VAT credit or refund on the periodic VAT return.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration generated through UNI-PASS, referencing the Export Declaration Number and export value
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, buyer, HS code, quantity, and total value
  • Main carriage document evidencing physical export: Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or postal/parcel dispatch certificate for e-commerce export
  • Foreign exchange receipt record issued by a Foreign Exchange Bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, proving repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • VAT export invoice or electronic tax invoice reflecting the 0% VAT rate and cross-referencing the export declaration number

All VAT-registered businesses issuing tax invoices must issue electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) through an NTS-compliant system and transmit them to HomeTax no later than the day following issuance. Electronic tax invoices must include the NTS-issued business registration numbers of both parties, supply date, taxable value, VAT, and a sequential electronic tax invoice number. Credit card sales data and cash receipt data are automatically transmitted to NTS for cross-matching. Korea does not use real-time fiscal digital stamping or continuous transaction control; instead NTS performs near-real-time validation and reconciliation through HomeTax. Licensed e-invoicing service providers must integrate with NTS HomeTax APIs and comply with the statutory electronic tax invoice serial-number standards.

NTS cross-checks zero-rated export claims against customs declarations, UNI-PASS records, Foreign Exchange Information System data, and bank remittance records. If the exporter fails to produce a valid customs export declaration and foreign exchange receipt evidence, NTS denies zero-rating, assesses the supply at the standard 10% VAT rate, and imposes additional tax under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including up to 40% of the understated tax for dishonest or fraudulent underreporting, plus overdue unpaid-tax additions. Intentional fabrication of export documentation or fraudulent VAT refund claims may trigger criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, and the statutory assessment limitation period is extended to 10 years in cases of fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a fundamentally liberalized convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Korean won is treated as a current-account convertible currency for trade, services, royalties, dividends, and loan interest, while capital-account transactions are open but subject to structured reporting rather than blanket pre-approval for standard foreign direct investment. Non-resident investors may own 100% of a Yuhan Hoesa and may remit dividends and disposed proceeds after corporate tax filings, tax clearance certificates, and external audit confirmation. However, treasury must manage several operational constraints: all foreign exchange transactions must route through an authorized foreign exchange bank; significant cross-border payments require documentary reasoning for settlement and settlement reporting under the Foreign Exchange Information System. There are no mandatory forced surrender conversion ratios for tea salon import payments, but export proceeds must be received within one year from the export declaration date. Account balances in Korean won cannot be transferred offshore without an underlying trade or investment; speculative won trading is prohibited, and the Bank of Korea and Korea Customs Service monitor volatility via macro-prudential limits, including FX liquidity guidance applied to banks. Profit remittance hurdles include the need to evidence corporate tax clearance, audited retained earnings, and compliance with the Foreign Investment Promotion Act registration if the entity enjoys foreign-investment protection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports of specialty tea, ceramics, or lounge-adjacent merchandise must be settled in foreign currency within one year of the export declaration date. Exporters may retain proceeds in an authorized foreign currency account or convert them to Korean won; no fixed statutory conversion ratio exists. If export proceeds are not collected by the one-year deadline, the exporter must obtain approval from the Korea Customs Service or the relevant foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and late settlement is subject to reporting penalties and potential loss of export incentive treatment.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea, operating with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as the central foreign exchange policy authority, and the Korea Customs Service as co-administrator of the Foreign Exchange Information System under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMI viability is rated Very Low. In South Korea, only licensed foreign exchange banks authorized by the Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea may clear Korean won trade settlements, process foreign direct investment capital injection, and file Foreign Exchange Information System reports. Offshore digital EMIs cannot maintain a Korean won corporate settlement account, cannot provide local IBAN-equivalent clearing rails, and are not eligible to act as the receiving institution for paid-in capital verification. While certain domestic fintech licences exist under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act for money transfer business, these products must partner with a commercial bank for domestic settlement and cannot replace a tier-one treasury banking relationship. For KYC robustness, the entity should not use EMIs for capital injections or trade disbursements.

For KYC robustness, the entity should not use EMIs for capital injections or trade disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% national corporate income tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 20 billion, with progressive 10% (first KRW 200 million) and 20% (KRW 200 million to 20 billion) brackets, plus 10% local income tax surtax on the assessed national tax (effective top rate 24.2%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surtax) on outbound dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders; DTA may reduce the rate to 5-15% depending on shareholding and treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File in Korea are required when international related-party transactions exceed KRW 10 billion in the preceding business year and total group revenue equals or exceeds KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report is due if consolidated turnover exceeds KRW 1 trillion; a specialty tea salon will nearly always be below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not use UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form principle under the Framework Act on National Taxes and BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test require the Korean tea salon and any foreign shareholder to perform genuine Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA)-local storefront, inventory, employees, and decision-making; lack of substance will deny treaty benefits and can recharacterize the Korean branch under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Cash Sales Suppression)

Statutory sanction: Article 85(1) of the Punishment for Tax Evaders Act: imprisonment up to 3 years or a fine of up to 3 times the evaded tax; in flagrant cases exceeding KRW 50 million in omitted tax, the Director must file a criminal complaint with the Public Prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for evaded tax plus 20% wrongful-reduction surcharge; a travel ban is issued against the responsible director, the corporate veil is pierced to attach personal bank accounts, and criminal imprisonment of 1-3 years may be imposed if the board knowingly approved suppressed revenue schedules.
Transfer Pricing Fabrication (Fake Royalty Fees)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, the National Tax Service re-determines the arm's length amount, adds 10% TP adjustment penalty, and imposes neglect/understatement surcharges of up to 20% of the underpaid corporate tax; the unilateral relief cannot be used as a defense.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute the phantom service agreement are jointly and severally liable under the commercial manager's duty for unjustified corporate outflow; assets may be attributed to the director personally, the director may be blacklisted from exiting Korea, and criminal liability under Article 85 of the Punishment for Tax Evaders Act applies if the false fee was knowingly used to shift profits.
Shell Company Treaty Abuse (False DTA Withholding Claim)

Statutory sanction: Article 5-2 of the International Tax Adjustment Act denies treaty benefits retroactively; the Korean company is assessed the 20% difference plus 10% underreporting penalty and monthly overdue interest; the tax treaty's Mutual Agreement Procedure is inapplicable to the sham entity.

Director personal liability: Korean directors who allowed the false declaration are subject to piercing of the corporate veil under the company behind the company principle, personal civil liability for the denied withholding tax plus penalties, a Korean travel ban (blacklisting) during investigation, and up to 3 years of imprisonment for aiding false official documents.

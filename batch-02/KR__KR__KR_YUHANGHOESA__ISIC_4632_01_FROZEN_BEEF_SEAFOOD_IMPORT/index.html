Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Korean Commercial Act; no mandatory statutory minimum paid-in capital exists for forming a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the proposed paid-in capital of KRW 50,000,000 must be irrevocably deposited into a domestic provisional collection account in the corporate name before court registration, and the bank-issued certificate of capital deposit must be submitted with the registration application. For wholesale import companies, a practical minimum working capital of KRW 30,000,000 is recommended for food import business reporting and foreign exchange bank due diligence; there is no blocked escrow account requirement under general Korean company law, but a Korean bank may place a temporary frozen hold on the account until the court registration is certified.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Act Firefighting System Completion Inspection Certificate, construction use approval or certificate of occupancy for the cold storage premises, workplace safety approval from the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency, and, where an ammonia refrigeration unit contains more than 2,000 kg of refrigerant, a Process Safety Management report and safety inspection certificate from the Ministry of Employment and Labor. Under the Framework Act on Civil Defense, the Company must maintain an emergency evacuation plan, participate in municipal civil defense drills, and keep a designated disaster prevention manager for the licensed cold-chain facility.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of South Korea, with electronic self-service operations through the Home Tax Service (HTS) web portal.

E-government filing portal

The non-resident foreign entity must first appoint a domestic tax representative in South Korea for tax administration. The tax representative, or the entity through a representative, files a business registration application with the competent district tax office where the Korean place of business is located. The application must be accompanied by: (i) corporate existence proof from the home jurisdiction (certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration extract), (ii) the foreign entity's articles of incorporation and board resolution authorizing Korean business operations, (iii) a power of attorney appointing the domestic tax representative, (iv) lease or ownership documentation for the Korean place of business, and (v) identification documents of the appointed representative. The NTS then issues a corporate tax registration number (legal entity identification number) and VAT business registration number in one integrated business registration certificate. This registration satisfies both withholding and VAT obligations and must be completed before commencing any import, wholesale, or taxable supply activity in Korea.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for all business operators conducting taxable supplies; there is no general exemption threshold. A natural-person sole business with annual taxable turnover not exceeding KRW 80,000,000 may be treated as a Simplified Taxpayer, but a Yuhan Hoesa corporation is registered as a General Taxpayer regardless of revenue level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from South Korea to a foreign purchaser are zero-rated under the Korean Value Added Tax Act, provided that the goods are actually exported through customs and prove delivery to a foreign destination. For the importer/wholesaler's onward export business, zero-rating also applies where frozen beef or seafood products are re-exported after being imported and stored, as long as the customs export declaration is made in the entity's name and the foreign buyer receives the goods outside Korea.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export clearance Bill of Entry)
  • Transportation document showing actual carriage to the foreign destination (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or purchase order from the overseas buyer
  • Foreign exchange transaction evidence confirming receipt of payment from abroad (Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency receipt confirmation)
  • Customs proof of re-export in bond, where applicable, for imported frozen meat/seafood moved into export logistics zones

Korean VAT invoices must be issued in electronic form through a certified ERP or e-invoice system and transmitted to the NTS Home Tax Service electronic invoice system. The NTS operates a real-time electronic invoice clearance infrastructure that records the invoice number, supplier and purchaser registration numbers, supply amount, VAT amount, and digital signature. Mandatory electronic tax invoices apply to General Taxpayer suppliers and to all corporations. The system includes automated data matching, anti-fraud digital stamping through the NTS issuance number, and cross-checks with customs import/export declarations and corporate tax returns.

Unsubstantiated export zero-rating is reversed by the NTS on audit, with the VAT otherwise chargeable assessed as tax payable, plus additional tax for failure to file and interest. Deliberate submission of false export declarations or forged foreign-currency receipt certificates triggers criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The NTS may claw back input VAT credits attached to the zero-rated export and impose penalty tax of up to 10% of the fraudulently claimed amount, in addition to refund recapture, for fraudulent or unverified zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with liberal current account convertibility; however, the Korean won does not have unrestricted offshore delivery. A resident corporation that imports frozen beef/seafood must make payments via licensed foreign exchange banks and provide an import declaration and trade documents. Capital account transactions are subject to reporting and registration: inbound capital contribution to a Korean LLC must be reported to the Korea Trade-Investment Promotion Agency (KOTRA) or a designated foreign exchange bank, with foreign direct investment notification before the funds arrive. Once the notified funds land, they can be credited to a foreign-currency capital account; conversion to KRW is lawful but the details are captured by the bank and the BOK. Dividend remittances require audited financial statements, tax settlement, and corporate residency evidence for tax treaty application; there is no permanent block, but the bank verifies that the dividend has been booked in retained earnings in compliance with the Korean Commercial Act. The KRW is not an offshore deliverable currency, so funds can be used only domestically or via permitted financial services. The BOK retains authority to apply prudential macro measures even under liberalization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exported from Korea, the exporter must receive the foreign currency proceeds within 18 months from the date of export clearance unless an extension is approved under Article 40 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Decree. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring a fixed percentage of export proceeds to be converted into KRW on delivery; however, the entire export proceeds must be repatriated and deposited into an authorized foreign exchange bank account, not left offshore, unless prior MOEF/BOK approval is given. The exporter must maintain the underlying trade documentation for at least five years. Since this particular Yuhan Hoesa is structured as an import company, the rule is relevant for any future export turnover or for re-export of processed frozen goods.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), as the central bank of the Republic of Korea, functions as the core FX authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) retains policy-level oversight, with designated foreign exchange banks licensed by the Bank of Korea to execute regulated FX business.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. A foreign fintech EMI without a Korean banking or e-money license is legally barred from servicing a Korean corporate entity for cross-border trade and capital transactions. To act as a treasury clearing rail, the EMI must obtain a financial business license from the Financial Services Commission and a foreign exchange dealer designation from the MOEF/BOK. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must first file a foreign direct investment notification with KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and obtain the FDI registration certificate. The capital contribution must then be remitted in the shareholder's own name via SWIFT MT103 from a qualified bank account, with the wire narrative containing the Korean entity name, the bank's FDI reference number, and the statutory foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment equity. Upon receipt, the domestic Korean bank will credit the foreign investment capital account, record the transaction in the BOK foreign exchange information system, and permit conversion to KRW. Capital verification is performed by the external auditor who reconciles the inward SWIFT amount, FDI registration certificate, bank accounting receipt, share issuance resolution, and paid-in capital entries in the Korean LLC's financial statements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when the Korean entity's cross-border related-party transaction amount reaches KRW 50 billion in the prior fiscal year; Master File is required when the multinational consolidated group revenue reaches KRW 100 billion and the Korean entity engages in related-party transactions; the annual International Transaction Report must be attached to the corporate tax return when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 500 million. Separate transfer pricing documentation must be maintained in Korean or English and submitted within 45 days of request by the National Tax Service.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations and Core Income-Generating Activities analysis apply through Korea's substance-over-form doctrine in the Framework Act on National Taxes and NTS anti-abuse case law. For a wholesale importer of frozen beef and seafood, the Korean entity must prove that it performs procurement negotiation, order acceptance, cold-chain inventory risk, temperature management, sales contracting, insurance, and foreign exchange risk management in Korea with substantive staff and premises. If the Korean entity is a mere invoicing shell and these CIGA are conducted by a foreign related party, the National Tax Service will reallocate income, deny treaty benefits, impose withholding tax on constructive dividends, and apply penalties for violation of the arm's length principle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3 and Framework Act on National Taxes Articles 47-2, 47-3, and 47-5: fraudulent understatement attracts 40% additional tax of the evaded amount, criminal fines up to three times the evaded tax, and imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Korean Commercial Act Article 401: directors who willfully or maliciously participate in the evasion are personally and jointly liable for the company's tax damage and restitution of diverted corporate assets. The National Tax Service may impose a travel ban and blacklist the responsible directors as delinquent taxpayers, preventing departure from Korea until tax restitution is secured; a criminal conviction on tax fraud carries a custodial sentence of up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Law for Coordination of International Tax Affairs Article 11 permits the National Tax Service to adjust taxable income to the arm's length amount; unfaithful return penalties of 10-40% may be layered on the adjusted tax, and non-production of transfer pricing documentation triggers separate documentation penalties and evidentiary sanctions, including disallowance of the taxpayer's comparability evidence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the fraudulent intercompany pricing are personally liable for the civil restitution of the adjusted corporate tax under Commercial Act Article 401 and may be made co-obligors where they transferred funds or assets abroad to avoid collection. NTS operates an asset freeze, travel ban, and public blacklisting for directors of significant delinquent TP assessments; criminal sanctions for false international transaction reports can include imprisonment for up to 5 years and substantial fines.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service applies the OECD BEPS MLI principal purpose test and Korean general anti-abuse doctrine to deny the treaty benefit, recharacterize the intermediary as a transparent agent of the Korean entity, assess the 22% combined withholding tax on constructive dividends or royalties, and impose a 40% fraudulent return penalty for omission.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrated the letterbox structure and directed payments through sham intermediaries are subject to piercing of the corporate veil: they are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, withholding tax, and interest. The NTS will blacklist the directors, issue an asset seizure and travel ban, and refer the case for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act; imprisonment for willful, habitual evasion can reach 10 years, and civil asset restitution will be ordered to recover the diverted corporate profits.

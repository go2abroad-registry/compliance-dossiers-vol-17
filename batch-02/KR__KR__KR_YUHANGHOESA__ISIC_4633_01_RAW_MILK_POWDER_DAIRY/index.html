Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr). The Korea Customs Service (KCS) UNI-PASS system is the complementary customs clearance portal for exports, but NTS administers both VAT and corporate income tax registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the entity has a domestic place of business in Korea. A Yuhan Hoesa incorporated under commercial law has a registered office in Korea, so it is a domestic business operator. Step 2: File the Business Registration Application with the competent district tax office, either in person or through the HomeTax portal, before commencing taxable activities. Step 3: Attach the articles of incorporation, corporate registration certificate, representative identity document, lease agreement or evidence of the domestic place of business, and evidence of foreign investment if any non-resident owns shares. Step 4: NTS examines the application and issues the 10-digit Business Registration Number, which also functions as the VAT identification number, and issues the Business Registration Certificate. Step 5: Register for the electronic tax invoice system through HomeTax or an approved Value Added Network (VAN) provider so that zero-rated export e-Tax Invoices and domestic supply invoices can be transmitted to NTS. Step 6: If the entity is managed by a non-resident without a Korean address, appoint a local tax representative for NTS correspondence and VAT/corporate tax filings. Step 7: Use the issued Business Registration Number for KCS export clearance, foreign exchange bank settlement, and all subsequent VAT returns and corporate income tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None. Korea does not impose a general turnover registration threshold for VAT. Every business operator making or intending to make taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register with NTS before commencing business. A cross-border commodity trading entity such as the Yuhan Hoesa must register even if its only supplies are zero-rated exports, because the Business Registration Number and VAT filing channel are necessary to claim export zero-rating and input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of goods physically exported from the Republic of Korea is eligible for zero-rated VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act. The Korean exporter must obtain an accepted export declaration from the Korea Customs Service, evidence that the goods left Korea through transport documents, and hold the commercial export documentation in the name of the registered Korean entity. The export proceeds must be received in accordance with the Foreign Trade Act and foreign-exchange trade settlement rules, and the seller must report the export as zero-rated in its VAT return with a corresponding zero-rated e-Tax Invoice. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating applies only when the service is supplied to a non-resident and is actually used or consumed in a foreign jurisdiction, evidenced by the service contract, foreign payment, and foreign-use evidence; for this dairy commodity entity, goods export zero-rating is the primary applicable channel.

  • Korean Customs Service electronic Export Declaration Confirmation and clearance sheet
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the whole and skimmed milk powder from Korea
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Yuhan Hoesa to the overseas buyer
  • Original zero-rated export e-Tax Invoice
  • Purchase order or export contract stating the Incoterms, price, ISIC 4633 product description, and delivery terms
  • Foreign Exchange Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or Export Proceeds Settlement Certificate showing receipt of export payment
  • Packing list and weight certificate for the dairy commodity shipment
  • Certificate of origin or phytosanitary/dairy export certificate as required by the destination country

Electronic tax invoices are mandatory for business-to-business supplies in Korea. The e-Tax Invoice must be issued and transmitted to NTS through HomeTax or an approved VAN provider no later than the day following the tax invoice issuance date. Korea does not use real-time fiscal stamping or an anti-fraud digital clearance platform analogous to a mandatory government API stamping regime; instead, NTS performs post-clearance matching of e-Tax Invoice data with Korea Customs Service export declarations and foreign-exchange bank settlement records. Domestic e-Tax Invoice transmission is required for B2B supplies, and foreign non-resident sellers with a Korean business registration can fulfill reporting through the HomeTax foreign-user channel.

NTS and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims by matching export declaration filings, e-Tax Invoices, and foreign-exchange bank settlement records. If the exporter cannot produce the export declaration, transport documents, or foreign-exchange receipt evidence, NTS disallows zero-rating and assesses 10% VAT on the supply plus additional tax for late or insufficient declaration, up to 40% of the unreported or underreported tax liability, plus interest. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated zero-rating claims are subject to criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment up to two years or fines up to two times the evaded tax, and the statutory assessment period for false or fraudulent returns is extended. Excess VAT refunds obtained through an invalid export zero-rating claim are clawed back with a 40% unjustified refund additional tax plus interest calculated from the original refund date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalized current and capital account system, but all cross-border foreign exchange transactions must be reported through authorized foreign exchange banks. The Korean won is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade in goods and services, and profit remittances may be freely repatriated after settlement of tax obligations, subject to submission of the prescribed foreign exchange application. For capital account transactions such as foreign direct investment, borrowing, and portfolio investment, prior reporting or notification to the Ministry of Economy and Finance or Bank of Korea is required under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds; companies may maintain foreign currency accounts and choose to convert at market rates. However, all receipts from exports must be collected through a designated bank and the underlying trade must be proved with shipping documents. The convertibility risk is minimal for legitimate trade, but foreign investors must comply with the reporting timelines to avoid administrative penalties. Profit remittance hurdles are mainly paperwork-oriented: audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board resolutions must be presented to the bank at the time of dividend or royalty payment. Overall, the regime is transparent and non-restrictive, but the operational burden of real-time foreign exchange position reporting and the requirement for physical in-branch validation for corporate verification add friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to Korean export proceeds. Exporters must receive payment through a domestic authorized foreign exchange bank and report the receipt within 30 days from the date of payment. The proceeds must be received within 180 days from the date of shipment, unless an extension is obtained from the authorized bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) have no practical viability for cross-border corporate accounts in South Korea. Korean banking law and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require that foreign exchange business be conducted only by licensed banks; non-banking fintech companies may provide payment tools but cannot maintain proprietary foreign currency accounts or process trade settlements without a licensed bank partner. Since our entity is a dairy commodity trader requiring SWIFT and LC rails, reliance on an EMI would create unnecessary regulatory friction and higher risk. The suitable architecture is a direct relationship with a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or loan proceeds must be reported as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The sending bank should wire funds via SWIFT MT103/202 to the Korean bank under the trader's name with the appropriate foreign exchange purpose code (e.g., Equipment and goods purchase or paid-in capital). The Korean bank will issue a Certificate of Foreign Currency Deposit, which is later used for capital verification by the court registral authority and tax office. For each inbound remittance, the treasurer must file a Foreign Exchange Receipts Report within 15 business days.For purchases of whole and skimmed milk powder from overseas suppliers, preferential clearing rails are: (1) Direct wire via SWIFT MT103 for small payments or pre-payment with full compliance documentation; (2) Documentry Letter of Credit (L/C) for large and regular shipments, typically irrevocable and confirmed at the beneficiary's bank; (3) Documentary collections (DA/DP) suitable for trusted supplier relationships. The Korean bank will require the underlying trade contract, import declaration, and invoice. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) have no practical viability for cross-border corporate accounts in South Korea. Korean banking law and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act require that foreign exchange business be conducted only by licensed banks; non-banking fintech companies may provide payment tools but cannot maintain proprietary foreign currency accounts or process trade settlements without a licensed bank partner. Since our entity is a dairy commodity trader requiring SWIFT and LC rails, reliance on an EMI would create unnecessary regulatory friction and higher risk. The suitable architecture is a direct relationship with a Tier-1 Korean commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 100,000,000 per related-party cross-border transaction triggers contemporaneous Local File preparation under International Tax Coordination Act (ITCA); annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceeding KRW 50,000,000,000 require a Master File; Country-by-Country reporting applies where group revenue is KRW 1 trillion or more.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the OECD-style substance-over-form doctrine: under ITCA Art. 4 the NTS may disregard arrangements made primarily to avoid Korean tax; there is no separate named 'Economic Substance Regulation', but a Korean PE must carry on real business operations in Korea, employ personnel, maintain local books and control assets to withstand an anti-avoidance attack; CFC rules tax Korean shareholders on low-tax offshore subsidiary income unless the foreign entity carries on substantial economic activity and has adequate substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 20% of the unpaid tax (40% for fraudulent/intentional misreporting) under Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 47(2); criminal prosecution under Punishment of Tax Offenses Act Art. 3(1): imprisonment up to 2 years or a fine not exceeding 30% of the evaded tax; up to 5 years' imprisonment and 30% fine if evaded tax exceeds KRW 300 million; forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Under Commercial Act Art. 399 and Corporate Tax Act Art. 19, directors are jointly and severally liable for company tax if they engaged intentionally or with gross negligence; personal asset attachment and criminal fines apply; the court may order a travel ban, passport revocation and must transfer offenders to immigration control; responsible director can be condemned to prison for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's-Length Violation

Statutory sanction: NTS adjustment under International Tax Coordination Act (ITCA) Art. 7 re-computing taxable income based on arm's-length price; penalty of 0.03% per day of underpayment and additional tax if misreporting appears in the return; for missing documentation, a statutory negligence penalty up to KRW 50 million per year and an upward tax re-determination; non-compliance may lead to criminal warning escalation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the pricing structure can be jointly liable for NTS-imposed tax deficiencies; if documentation omission enables tax evasion, the director may be prosecuted as an accomplice to tax fraud; denial of DTA benefits may result in assessment eviction; failure to pay a final assessment triggers travel ban/blacklisting under the National Tax Collection Act.
Anti-Money Laundering / Economic Substance (Shell Company Structure)

Statutory sanction: Under the Act on Report and Use of Specific Financial Transaction Information (ARTUSFI) Art. 23: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine up to KRW 50 million for violation of reporting/disclosure duties; forfeiture and confiscation of proceeds; the entity's license may be suspended; additionally, under the Commercial Act, an insolvent shell trading company may lose limited liability protection if the corporate veil is pierced.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face individual criminal liability (imprisonment up to 5 years); civil liability to creditors and the state for damages caused by misleading business conduct; Korean courts may pierce the veil to hold directors personally accountable for unpaid taxes and debts; blacklisting of the director by financial institutions and travel restrictions imposed by NTS/immigration authorities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea via the HomeTax System (www.hometax.go.kr) for VAT and corporate tax registration and electronic filing; Korea Customs Service (KCS) through UNI-PASS (www.unipass.kr) for export and import customs clearance and customs data exchange.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-owned Korean Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company), the representative director must file the Application for Business Registration with the competent district tax office or through the HomeTax System before commencing business. If the foreign investor has no domestic place of business for tax purposes, a domestic tax representative must be appointed under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Required documentation includes the certificate of incorporation, articles of incorporation, constitutional documents of the foreign parent, board resolutions establishing the Korean entity, passport or proof of identity of the appointed representative, evidence of the Korean business address (lease agreement or utility bill), and proof of bank account opening. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit business registration number which is simultaneously the value-added tax identification number, corporate tax reference number, and employer withholding number for payroll taxes. The Business Registration Certificate is the primary evidence of VAT registration for issuing tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation; a Korean Yuhan Hoesa is a domestic corporation under the Commercial Act and Corporate Tax Act, taxed in its own name on worldwide income, with non-resident shareholders subject to Korean dividend withholding where applicable; no pass-through treatment is available.

        
Available election forms
None. The Republic of Korea does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election; a Yuhan Hoesa is always treated as a taxable corporation under the Corporate Tax Act. Hybrid mismatch rules under Korean tax law apply to cross-border arrangements to prevent double non-taxation or deduction/no-inclusion outcomes.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
10%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No turnover threshold for registration; all persons supplying taxable goods or services in Korea are required to register under Article 5 of the Value-Added Tax Act. A simplified VAT compliance regime is available only to individual proprietors with prior-year annual turnover below KRW 80,000,000; corporations, including Yuhan Hoesa, are subject to the standard VAT registration and filing regime regardless of revenue.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under the Value-Added Tax Act, export of tangible goods is zero-rated where the goods are actually shipped out of Korea and an export declaration is accepted by the Korea Customs Service. Qualifying offshore services, including certain B2B digital services supplied to non-residents, are zero-rated when rendered to a foreign purchaser for use solely outside Korea and consideration is received in foreign currency from abroad. The zero rating is available only upon complete documentary substantiation showing the physical export or the offshore supply and the actual receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign exchange.

Electronic Tax Invoice issuance through the National Tax Service HomeTax system is mandatory for taxpayers with prior-year annual turnover of at least KRW 100,000,000, and such electronic tax invoices must be transmitted to the NTS within one day after issuance. Where export zero-rating is claimed but the required customs declaration, transport documentation, foreign exchange remittance evidence, or contract records are missing, unverified, or fraudulent, the NTS may disallow the zero rating and reassess VAT at the standard 10% rate plus interest. KCS may also impose separate customs penalties for false export declarations and the exporter may be placed under stricter customs inspection and trade compliance monitoring.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
South Korea maintains a liberalised current account regime for goods, services, and ordinary business income. The Korean won is freely convertible for settlement of trade and franchise royalty flows, but every transaction must be reported through an authorised foreign exchange bank using the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN). Current account items such as bakery ingredient imports, franchise royalty fees, and central kitchen equipment leases do not require prior ministerial approval or central bank surrender; the legal basis is the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. Capital account flows, including inbound equity injection for the Yuhan Hoesa, require prior notification to the Korean authorities through KOTRA/FEIN, and the receiving bank reports the actual remittance to BOK/FEIN. There are no material restrictions on profit remittance if the company has complied with corporate tax filing and withholding obligations under the Korea-China tax treaty; however, banks may request evidence of underlying commercial substance to avoid suspicious transaction reporting. Export proceeds are not subject to forced conversion but must be credited within defined collection periods. Overall, Korea is a low-friction, high-reporting administrative convertibility jurisdiction rather than a strict licence-controlled foreign exchange system.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations, an exporter must collect export proceeds within six months from the date of export; extensions may be granted through the relevant domestic foreign exchange bank. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; 0% of export proceeds must be surrendered, but the foreign currency receivable must be receipted into a local domestic foreign exchange bank account and reported to BOK/FEIN using the applicable export purpose code. Failure to collect within the statutory period triggers a bank-led investigation and possible regulatory penalty.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK), acting under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act with the Ministry of Economy and Finance as policy authority; the BOK operates the Foreign Exchange Information Network (FEIN) and supervises authorised foreign exchange banks.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore fintech digital banks and electronic money institutions cannot act as a primary local KRW treasury bank or hold customer deposits in South Korea. The Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and the Electronic Financial Transactions Act mandate that local currency deposit-taking, foreign exchange brokerage, and final settlement be performed by a bank licensed in Korea. Foreign EMIs may only provide payment-orchestration software or front-end invoicing over a licensed Korean bank's account; direct account onboarding for the Yuhan Hoesa is legally barred. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must use domestic Tier-1 Korean commercial banks for all KRW clearing, POS acquiring, and remittance rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The non-resident shareholder should file a foreign investment notification with KOTRA under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and obtain the Foreign Investment Notification Certificate. Capital funds are then transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the Korean bank's capital escrow account using the FEIN foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment in the acquisition of shares of a domestic Korean company. The Korean bank verifies the FEIN declaration, credits the proceeds to the escrow account, and releases funds into the operating KRW account only after the company completes capital verification with a Korean certified public accountant. All inbound funds must be reported to BOK/FEIN within the prescribed period, and the wire narrative should state that the transfer is a Korean-won capital remittance under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.The Yuhan Hoesa will use SWIFT MT103 for franchise royalty, ingredient import, and service payments, accompanied by the appropriate FEIN purpose code and withholding tax clearance certificate. Local bakery POS revenues are cleared through a domestic acquiring bank; inventory payables and local central kitchen invoices are settled through real-time KRW interbank transfers. For large central kitchen equipment purchases from China, ICC UCP 600 letters of credit will be issued by the Korean bank and advised through the beneficiary's bank; documentary collections are handled under ICC URC 522. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
9% for the first KRW 200 million of taxable income; 19% for the tranche between KRW 200 million and KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion; plus a 10% local income tax surcharge, producing an effective statutory blended headline rate of 9.9%, 20.9% or 23.1% depending on the bracket.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
20% national withholding tax on gross dividends paid to a non-resident plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax, making the total statutory effective dividend withholding tax rate 22%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion in annual foreign related-party transaction amount triggers mandatory Local File preparation; KRW 100 billion in annual group revenue triggers Master File preparation; KRW 1 trillion of group consolidated revenue triggers Country-by-Country Report filing; even below these thresholds, Yuhan Hoesa must report all cross-border related-party transactions in the annual International Transaction Statement where the transaction amount exceeds the statutory disclosure threshold (commonly KRW 100 million per transaction type).

      
Economic substance scope: Korea is not a UAE-style ESR jurisdiction, but the NTS enforces a substance-over-form doctrine through Korean GAAR, the International Tax Adjustment Act, and the beneficial-ownership requirement in each DTA. Yuhan Hoesa must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Korea, including centralized bakery production, menu development, franchisee selection, store management, financial control, and significant economic risk-taking; if these activities are performed by a foreign affiliate while the Korean entity is merely a legal shell, the NTS can deny DTA relief, disallow intercompany deductions, reattribute income under the arm's-length rules, and apply the Korean controlled-foreign-corporation rules.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Corporate Income Tax Understatement
        
Statutory sanction: Korean Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Article 3: imprisonment up to 5 years and/or a fine up to 5 times the evaded tax; the NTS will additionally impose a 20% non-filing/understatement penalty, late-payment interest at 0.03% per day, and a criminal referral for fraudulent tax evasion.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced; the representative director and the accounting director are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and for civil restitution of the evaded amounts; the NTS imposes a travel ban and overseas travel restriction (blacklisting); criminal conviction results in imprisonment plus personal financial penalties, and the directors may be barred from serving as directors of any Korean company.

      

      
      

        Shell Company, Treaty Shopping, and Sham Royalty Arrangement
        
Statutory sanction: Korean International Tax Adjustment Act Articles 3-2, 5, and 27 (anti-treaty-shopping, arm's-length adjustment, and controlled foreign corporation rules) authorize full disallowance of the sham royalty, upward adjustment of taxable income, denial of DTA benefits, and additional local income tax; the NTS imposes a 20% negligence penalty on the understated tax plus late-payment surcharges.

        
Director personal liability: The director who approved the sham contracts is personally liable under Korean Commercial Act Article 401 for damages to the company and creditors; the NTS places the director on a travel-blacklist and freezes personal assets; criminal charges for tax fraud may be brought because the arrangement was a deliberate false declaration; directors may be personally assessed for the unpaid withholding tax on recharacterized dividends.

      

      
      

        AML, Foreign Exchange Violation, and Unlicensed Cross-Border Payment Rails
        
Statutory sanction: Korean Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes fines up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment up to 3 years for unauthorized cross-border payments; customs authorities may impose additional customs duty plus evasion penalties on mispriced or undeclared imports of frozen pastry and flour; the Financial Services Commission/FIU can impose AML fines and criminal sanctions for failure to file suspicious transaction reports.

        
Director personal liability: The director is personally criminally liable for foreign-exchange and AML violations; the FIU/NTS blacklists the director, imposing immigration holds and travel bans; the court may pierce the corporate veil because the LLC was used as an instrument for fraud, allowing personal asset attachment and civil restitution; the bakery and franchise licenses can be revoked, and franchisees may sue directors individually for losses caused by hidden liabilities.

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for a Yuhan Hoesa established as a private equity buyout SPV in Korea. The National Tax Service and the municipal authorities require a physical address, accessible during business hours, where jurisdictional books, invoices, and board minutes are kept, and where the company is represented. Korean banks for KYC/AML will reject a virtual office because a branch visitation and inspection of the actual material business location is a mandatory part of the account opening procedure under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act. The Commercial Registration Act further requires the registered seat to be a real property location. Therefore, the SPV must lease or own a physical office in Korea, and the address must be evidenced by a lease agreement and utility contract.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the full subscription amount of each unit must be remitted to a restricted capital subscription account at a licensed Korean bank under the Act on Real Name Financial Transactions, and those funds must be blocked until the court issues the certified certificate of incorporation and the National Tax Service issues the Business Registration Certificate. Release of the deposited capital is prohibited prior to completion of those registrations, and any later change in capital must be registered with the court.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity such as a private equity buyout SPV, the requirement to register for Korean tax purposes depends on whether the SPV has a domestic source of income or a permanent establishment in Korea. A non-resident entity that acquires and holds Korean target shares typically has no Korean VAT registration obligation unless it makes taxable supplies in Korea (e.g., management or advisory services), but for completeness, the statutory procedure is: (1) If the SPV has a fixed place of business or a taxable activity in Korea, it must submit a Business Registration Application (사업자등록신청서) to the competent district tax office (세무서) within 20 days from the commencement of business. (2) The application must be accompanied by certified Korean translations of the foreign incorporation certificate, articles of incorporation, and a power of attorney identifying a Korean tax representative (국세대리인) if no Korean office exists. (3) After verification, the NTS issues a Business Registration Number (사업자등록번호), which serves as both the corporate TIN and the VAT registration number. (4) For non-resident digital service suppliers, a simplified electronic registration is available through HomeTax without requiring a physical domestic establishment. (5) The SPV must then file periodic VAT returns (only if it makes taxable supplies) and comply with e-Tax Invoice requirements through HomeTax.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Korean domestic tax law does not provide for US-style check-the-box elections for corporate entities. A Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company) is categorised as a domestic corporation and is subject to entity-level corporate income tax. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies. All business operators undertaking taxable supplies in Korea must register for VAT irrespective of annual revenue. Non-resident digital service providers are subject to simplified mandatory registration if they supply electronic services to Korean consumers; a de minimis net revenue threshold of KRW 100,000,000 per calendar year applies specifically to foreign digital platform operators under the amended VAT Act.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Korea qualify for 0% VAT under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act when the goods are actually exported and ownership has transferred to a foreign buyer. Specific services that are physically performed outside Korea, international transport services, and certain intermediary services directly related to exports also qualify for zero-rating. For a non-exporting PE SPV that merely holds investments, no zero-rating will apply unless it separately supplies exports or qualifying international services. Digital services supplied to offshore recipients may be zero-rated if they are consumed outside Korea and the service recipient is a non-resident, subject to NTS substance and documentation requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (수출신고필증 / Export Declaration Confirmation) issued by the Korea Customs Service
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) indicating shipment from Korea to the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (외환매매확인서 / Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation) issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Evidence of foreign buyer identity and overseas delivery (e.g., delivery order, port arrival notice)
  • Contract of sale evidencing FOB, CFR, CIF or other export terms

Korea operates a mandatory electronic tax invoice system. All VAT-registered persons issuing tax invoices must transmit them electronically to the National Tax Service through the HomeTax platform or via NTS-approved certified EDI/API connections. Electronic tax invoices (전자세금계산서) must contain all statutory data fields, be issued within ten days of the supply, and are transmitted to the NTS in near real-time (within one business day of issuance for most taxpayers). The system does not require fiscal stamping or a government real-time clearance seal, but tax invoices must bear a digital signature and are subject to automatic NTS data matching. Failure to comply triggers penalty surcharges of up to 2% of the tax invoice value.

The National Tax Service and Korea Customs Service jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Taxpayers must retain the full documentation bundle for at least five years from the filing date. If export documentation is found to be false, forged, or insufficient to prove physical export or receipt of foreign exchange proceeds, the NTS will reverse the zero-rated treatment and assess VAT at the standard 10% rate on the invoiced amount, together with a surcharge of up to 40% of the tax assessed for intentional violation or fraud, plus 0.025% statutory interest per day for late payment. In addition, an administrative penalty of up to 10% of the unreported zero-rated supply amount may be imposed, and criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act may be pursued in cases of wilful tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates a liberalized current account with full convertibility for trade-related receipts and payments, while the capital account is subject to a declaration or reporting regime. The Korean won is an onshore convertible currency for residents but is not freely deliverable offshore for speculative purposes; offshore trading is concentrated in non-deliverable forwards (NDFs). For a foreign-invested Yuhan Hoesa LLC, inbound equity capital may be injected as foreign direct investment under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act once the investor files the required FDI notification with the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy through KOTRA or a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital contributions can be converted into KRW, and dividends or dispositions can generally be remitted abroad after supporting evidence is presented to the bank, including audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and proof of FDI registration. There is no general Central Bank surrender obligation for FDI proceeds, and no minimum capital is required for the target LLC, which reduces initial conversion complexity. Restrictions remain where the transaction involves gambling, money laundering, tax evasion, or designated financial crimes; banks must confirm the ultimate beneficial ownership and source of funds before opening and maintaining treasury rails. Overall, Korea is assessed as a high-convertibility jurisdiction with process-driven FIU and bank surveillance rather than blanket exchange controls. Profit repatriation is feasible if documentary compliance is maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received through a licensed Korean foreign exchange bank within the statutory export settlement window, which follows the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act procedures and generally requires collection within one year from the export declaration date unless an extension is approved by the bank or MOEF in exceptional circumstances. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0 percent for ordinary export receipts: no surrender to the Bank of Korea is imposed. Exporters are permitted to retain 100 percent of export proceeds in a designated Foreign Currency Deposit Account and may convert to KRW only as needed for business operations, provided the transaction is recorded in the bank's foreign exchange systems.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree; BOK supervises licensed foreign exchange banks and MOEF administers FX licensing and capital transaction declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for a Korean domestic entity. Offshore digital electronic money institutions are not licensed to accept deposits or issue domestic Korean won account numbers for onshore treasury operations. Under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a fintech EMI would need a domestic FSC license and a partnership with an existing bank to offer payment services; it cannot replace a commercial bank account for FDI capital verification, statutory currency settlements, or corporate wire routing. Use of an offshore EMI is therefore only permissible as a secondary operational rail, never as the primary regulated corporate account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity for the Korean PE buyout SPV must be routed as a direct foreign investment remittance. The non-resident investor must first file an FDI notification with KOTRA or the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy through a designated foreign exchange bank, obtain the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, and then wire funds via SWIFT MT103 to the opening bank's designated FDI settlement account. The wire must include the correct FX purpose code for capital acquisition, the beneficiary entity name exactly as registered in Korea, and the reference 'Foreign Direct Investment in KR LLC / Yuhan Hoesa'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% top marginal rate (taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion); progressive: 9% up to KRW 200 million, 19% over KRW 200 million, 21% over KRW 20 billion, 24% over KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% corporate tax + 2% local surtax) on non-resident dividends; treaty-reduced to 5%-15% under relevant DTAs (e.g., Korea-US 10%; Korea-UK 15%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when foreign related-party transactions aggregate at least KRW 10 billion per fiscal year; Master File mandatory for groups with KRW 100 billion revenue and KRW 50 billion related-party transactions; SPV must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation as soon as audit risk is apparent

Economic substance scope: Korea has no formal ESR statute comparable to UAE/Cayman, but retains GAAR under Framework Act on National Taxes and substance-over-form; for a PE buyout SPV, Core Income-Generating Activities must be managed in Korea: contracts executed, board decisions made, risks borne, and payroll/office substance; absence leads to treaty shopping challenge, denial of DTA benefits, deemed income assessment

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Punishment of Tax Evasion Act Article 9: imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to 5 times the evaded tax; Corporate Tax Act Article 110 administrative surcharge of 20% plus daily arrearage charge of 0.025%

Director personal liability: Directors held jointly and severally liable for tax debt and penal surcharges under Corporate Tax Act Article 4; civil asset restitution; travel ban (blacklisting) issued by MOEF; imprisonment applied for intentional concealment
Shell Company / Corporate Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Commercial Act Article 171 and Framework Act on National Taxes allow veil piercing and revocation of limited liability; administrative retroactive tax assessment with interest and KRW 30 million fine for sham entity under Criminal Act Article 233

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow owners jointly liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; personal assets frozen; travel ban; criminal imprisonment up to 7 years for fraud
AML / Foreign Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Transactions Act violation: fine up to KRW 50 million and imprisonment up to 3 years; Financial Intelligence Unit civil penalty up to KRW 100 million for unreported suspicious transactions

Director personal liability: FIU imposes passport revocation and travel ban for up to 6 months; asset seizure of directors' personal property; criminal conviction disqualifies from future directorship and carries up to 3 years imprisonment

Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under National Tax Service and Korean banking practice, a registered office cannot be a mere post-office box or pure mail-forwarding address. A serviced office or hybrid virtual office is acceptable for the Yuhan Hoesa only if the provider is a licensed commercial premises operator, the lease or service agreement is with the Korean entity, the address can receive legal notices, and NTS inspectors can physically access the location. For an electronic B2B cross-border marketplace, Korean banks uniformly reject mail-only virtual office addresses for corporate account opening because they require face-to-face branch interviews, verification of business operations, and proof of exclusive desk space. The National Tax Service will also reject a VAT registration if the address cannot support the issuance of tax invoices or actual business administration. A virtual mailbox is therefore impermissible as the sole registered office, but a serviced office with dedicated desk space and local staff is permissible if disclosed to and accepted by the NTS and the chosen bank.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa under the Korean Commercial Act. The stated capital in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid into a corporate subscription bank account before registration; no blocked escrow account is mandated by law. However, the Registry may request a bank balance confirmation or remittance certificate issued by a recognized foreign-exchange bank. Non-resident shareholders must comply with Foreign Investment Promotion Act reporting requirements before contribution. Registration tax is variable and is calculated on the contributed capital, typically at 0.4% plus local education surtax, so no single official state filing fee amount can be stated; hence the value zero is used for fixed fee purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration has no KRW turnover threshold in Korea; registration is obligatory within 20 days of business commencement or taxable nexus. A simplified taxable person status is available only to certain small individual suppliers with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but a Yuhan Hoesa LLC cannot use it. Foreign non-resident businesses making taxable supplies to Korean customers must register through NTS's foreign business operator procedure as soon as a regular taxable supply nexus is established.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Article 21 of the Korean Value-Added Tax Act to: (i) goods exported outside Korea, subject to an export customs declaration in UNI-PASS; (ii) services rendered outside Korea for qualifying offshore consumers; (iii) cross-border international carriage, brokerage and intermediary services where the service recipient is a non-resident and the service is actually used overseas; and (iv) supplies paid for in foreign currency to a foreign buyer, evidenced by bank remittance. A Korean B2B cross-border marketplace or store may zero-rate its intermediary commission and platform services only when the contract, delivery, use and payment evidence prove the service is supplied to a foreign business and satisfied outside the Korean customs territory.

  • Export Declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service via UNI-PASS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for export movement
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt certificate confirming export proceeds repatriation
  • Signed cross-border sales contract or purchase order from the non-resident buyer
  • Proof of foreign business registration or tax ID of the overseas buyer

Korea operates a mandatory national electronic tax invoice (e-tax invoice) system through NTS HomeTax or authorized ASPs. Every VAT-registered business must issue an e-tax invoice through HomeTax for taxable supplies to other registered businesses no later than the day after the supply, and the NTS receives the e-tax invoice data electronically at issuance. No pre-clearance revenue stamp is used; compliance is enforced through NTS data matching, digital certificate authentication, and mandatory e-tax invoice numbers. Foreign non-resident registered vendors must also issue e-tax invoices using the NTS foreign business operator channel.

NTS and Korea Customs Service can cross-check export declarations, e-tax invoices and bank FX records during post-audit. If export zero-rating is not substantiated, NTS recharacterizes the supply as taxable, assesses VAT at 10% retroactively, and imposes additional tax plus non-deductible penalties under the Value-Added Tax Act and the Framework Act on National Taxes. Fraudulent or false zero-rating or refund claims constitute tax evasion under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, exposing the entity to criminal imprisonment and penal surcharges. Any unpaid VAT and clawback amount may become immediately collectible with interest and additional charge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a largely liberalised current account and capital account regime, but the won is not a freely internationally deliverable currency outside a narrow onshore window. Current account transactions, including trade-related payments, services, and transfers, are generally convertible upon submission of a valid customs declaration and supporting underlying trade contract through a domestic foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections, intercompany loans, and acquisition of Korean equities or bonds, are subject to a notification or reporting framework under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. A foreign-invested entity may open a won denominated account without prior approval, but documentary evidence of foreign direct investment must be filed with the Foreign Exchange Information System within 30 days of the capital remittance. Profit remittances by a non-resident owned Korean LLC are permitted only after the completion of statutory external audit, tax clearance, and submission of certified financial statements evidencing distributable reserves under the Korean Commercial Act. In practice, a formal verification certificate from a licensed certified public accountant is required by the remitting bank. The largest operational hurdle for a B2B marketplace operator is the prohibition on settling intercompany and domestic B2B transactions through offshore dwelling accounts; all revenue generated by the Korean resident entity from Korean-source value-added services must be received into a domestic won account. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds, but Korean customs and the BOK require individual trade settlement reports for all receivables through the Foreign Exchange Information System. A foreign company wishing to repatriate dividends must demonstrate that the underlying foreign invested enterprise status was properly registered with KOTRA or the receiving financial institution, and that the transaction is reported under the code for direct investment dividend remittance. Without this evidence, remittance will be blocked by the bank's compliance department.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory surrender requirement for export foreign currency receipts in Korea. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange bank account as long as each receipt is reported to the Foreign Exchange Information System via the bank within the designated business day. However, the Customs Act requires the importer or exporter to file a finalised export declaration before the associated payment is credited, and the foreign exchange bank must cross-match the SWIFT remittance reference with the KCS customs declaration number. Failure to report an export receipt within the customary reporting window, generally the banking business day following receipt, will trigger a fine and repeated failures will render the entity ineligible for simplified export clearing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) and the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) acting through the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; the Bank of Korea administers the Foreign Exchange Information System together with the Korea Customs Service for trade settlement reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for serving the Korean domestic won clearing and settlement requirements of a Korean resident LLC. Fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex can provide only limited ancillary functionality without a local e-money license. In Korea, any entity receiving funds on behalf of merchants is subject to the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and must obtain a hypothetical e-money and prepaid issuance license from the Financial Services Commission unless the receivable is a direct remittance to the corporate's own name at a licensed bank. A non-bank EMI cannot perform a primary won clearing function for Korean-sourced payments because all domestic B2B payments in Korea settle through the Bank of Korea Won Clearing System, which is accessible only to domestic banks and licensed financial institutions. An offshore EMI may, however, be used as a payment collection front for non-Korean buyers when the order is contractually structured as an offshore sale by the foreign parent, but that does not satisfy the Korean entity's requirement to declare export sales. The legally viable architecture is to use a domestic Tier-1 bank as the primary account and use an EMI solely for foreign currency receivables from overseas marketplace buyers, with those proceeds later remitted to the Korean bank via a designated foreign currency account. Korean financial regulators generally require marketplaces that facilitate settlement between sellers and buyers to hold a for-performance guarantee, and any operation of an internal clearing house within the Korean platform may require a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must distinguish platform merchant settlement, which should be routed through tier-one bank trust accounts or a licensed payment gateway, from the Korean entity's own operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial equity contribution by the overseas shareholder should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the Korean LLC's designated foreign currency or won capital escrow account at a domestic trading bank. The remittance reason should be exactly 'FDIP investment' or 'FDI subscription proceeds' with a supplementary purpose code aligning with the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System code for direct investment. The capital must be credited to the Korean side before the statutory FDI report is filed, generally within 30 days from the date of payment. A foreign exchange bank requires the following dossier: certificate of incorporation and proof of good standing of the overseas investor, notarised board resolution, tax and financial records of the investor to evidence source of funds, the Korean LLC's registration certificate, and the capital subscription contract. Once the bank verifies the inward remittance, it will issue a certificate of foreign direct investment and register the inflow in the Korean Foreign Exchange Information System. Capital verification is performed by the licensed foreign exchange bank, and where required, a certified public accountant issues a capital contribution completion report. If the foreign parent later converts owed royalties into equity, a separate and more stringent debt-equity swap filing must be made. For pure inbound equity, the entities must avoid the use of cryptocurrency wrappers, cash deposits, or an inflow from a high-risk jurisdiction without a preceding licensing statement, because the bank's foreign exchange and AML team will require the settlement of the MT103 through a correspondent bank that has direct or indirect account relationship with the Korean bank.The Korean LLC, acting as a B2B wholesaler or marketplace operator, should use the following clearing hierarchy: (1) for high-confidence receivable-backed supplier settlements, SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the Korean bank, with field 70 including the invoice number, customs declaration number, and purpose code for trade imports; (2) for high-value, repetitive contract shipments with an Asian counterparty, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit advised through the Korean bank, subject to UCP600, with a fixed payment tenor and transport document package; (3) for relationship-based but small-ticket high-volume B2B transactions, documentary collections (DP/DA) through the correspondent banking channel, though the Korean bank will likely require complete sellers' invoice databases and tax withheld receipts. The Korean entity's own platform settlement payouts to domestic sellers should never be made from the main trading account directly if the platform holds buyer funds on behalf of merchants; instead, a separate escrow or trust account structure with a licensed payment institution or a bank's settlement service must be established to avoid breaching the financial business licensed distinction.Onshore bank-level forward foreign exchange contracts (FX forwards) for eligible trade related underlying, with a maturity no longer than 12 months and require a real trade invoice and customs declaration.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) conducted only with a Korean financial institution or a licensed offshore bank where counterparty has a Korean FX business license; however, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, a Korean resident company is generally restricted from entering into NDFs except for permitted hedging of real import/export exposure.Currency swaps for capital account flows are atypical for a simple B2B marketplace and require prior Foreign Exchange Information System notification if the counterparty is a non-resident financial institution; a plain cross-currency swap for senior investment inflows may be advised only through a major Korean commercial bank with MOEF prior approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for serving the Korean domestic won clearing and settlement requirements of a Korean resident LLC. Fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex can provide only limited ancillary functionality without a local e-money license. In Korea, any entity receiving funds on behalf of merchants is subject to the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and must obtain a hypothetical e-money and prepaid issuance license from the Financial Services Commission unless the receivable is a direct remittance to the corporate's own name at a licensed bank. A non-bank EMI cannot perform a primary won clearing function for Korean-sourced payments because all domestic B2B payments in Korea settle through the Bank of Korea Won Clearing System, which is accessible only to domestic banks and licensed financial institutions. An offshore EMI may, however, be used as a payment collection front for non-Korean buyers when the order is contractually structured as an offshore sale by the foreign parent, but that does not satisfy the Korean entity's requirement to declare export sales. The legally viable architecture is to use a domestic Tier-1 bank as the primary account and use an EMI solely for foreign currency receivables from overseas marketplace buyers, with those proceeds later remitted to the Korean bank via a designated foreign currency account. Korean financial regulators generally require marketplaces that facilitate settlement between sellers and buyers to hold a for-performance guarantee, and any operation of an internal clearing house within the Korean platform may require a license under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must distinguish platform merchant settlement, which should be routed through tier-one bank trust accounts or a licensed payment gateway, from the Korean entity's own operating account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when aggregate cross-border intercompany transactions with a foreign related party exceed KRW 1 billion in a business year; Master File obligations apply to Korean entities belonging to multinational groups with Korean annual revenue exceeding KRW 100 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations follow the OECD threshold as adopted under Korean law.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the substance-over-form principle and the economic substance requirements embedded in the Adjustment of International Taxes Act and OECD BEPS standards. The Korean Yuhan Hoesa must exercise real decision-making, assume risk through local employees with actual authority, and perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Korea. A letter-box entity lacking economic substance may be disregarded, with income reallocated to the foreign parent and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The National Tax Service may make a deemed arm's length adjustment, disallow excessive deductions, impose an underreporting additional tax equal to 20% of the unpaid corporate tax, levy international transaction penalties, and prosecute under the Tax Evasion Punishment Act with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham intercompany agreements are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and additional tax where corporate assets are insufficient; personal criminal exposure includes imprisonment, confiscation of gains, and a travel ban and blacklisting for collection enforcement.
Failure to Withhold on Cross-Border Dividend, Interest, and Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: The Korean payer as statutory withholding agent becomes liable for the unpaid withholding amount, additional tax of 20% of the unwithheld amount, daily delinquency surcharges, and criminal prosecution for intentional failure to withhold.

Director personal liability: The representative director and responsible finance director may be personally pursued for the unremitted withholding tax under the secondary liability rule in the National Tax Collection Act; intentional omission exposes them to imprisonment, personal asset seizure, and outward remittance restrictions.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Arrangement for Treaty Shopping or PE Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Korean tax authorities may disregard the entity on substance-over-form grounds, treat the foreign parent as having a Korean permanent establishment, reallocate all platform profit to the Korean source income, impose corporate income tax and local surcharge, and deny double tax treaty benefits under the principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly file false annual returns and financial statements for a fictitious corporate form face civil restitution of misappropriated assets, joint and several liability for tax deficiencies, travel bans and blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion or fraud.

None under the Commercial Act for a Yuhan Hoesa; the articles of incorporation may fix paid-in capital at zero, though any declared capital must be fully paid before registration. National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic portal (www.hometax.go.kr) and the UNI-PASS customs system for cross-border verification. For foreign non-resident entities, the competent local district tax office under the NTS is the registration authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 - no mandatory turnover exemption threshold. Any person, including a foreign non-resident entity, making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register for VAT. A simplified taxpayer filing regime exists for individual small businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80,000,000, but it is not a registration exemption and does not apply to a Yuhan Hoesa LLC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under Article 21 of the Korean VAT Act when the goods are removed from Korean customs territory and exported under a valid export declaration filed by the VAT-registered seller. Qualifying cross-border services rendered or used outside Korea and entered into with non-residents may also qualify for 0% VAT if the service is performed overseas and paid in foreign currency. The taxpayer must be the actual exporter, the transaction must be substantiated by statutory export documentation, and the export proceeds must be repatriated to Korea in foreign currency within the statutory settlement period.

  • UNI-PASS export declaration receipt or export declaration certificate evidencing customs clearance from Korea
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving delivery to Almomani destination outside Korean customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Foreign exchange bank certificate of receipt / inward remittance certificate evidencing actual repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Standard packing list and shipping documents required by Korea Customs Service

All VAT-registered businesses, including Korean LLCs, must issue tax invoices electronically through the NTS mandatory electronic tax invoice system and transmit issuance details to HomeTax no later than the day after issuance. The system operates through NTS-approved e-invoice APIs and ERP integrations; there is no separate physical fiscal stamp in Korea, but every B2B tax invoice is centrally recorded in near-real time. B2C sales in qualifying retail transactions must be reported through the national cash-receipt system, and credit card or cash-receipt transaction data is transmitted directly to the NTS for anti-fraud matching.

The NTS may deny or claw back the 0% VAT rate if the statutory export documentation is incomplete, the foreign exchange is not received within the 180-day repatriation deadline, or the export declaration is found to be false or diverted to domestic consumption. In such cases, the NTS will assess 10% VAT on the undisclosed supply together with statutory additional taxes under the Framework Act on National Taxes, including up to 40% underreporting penalties for dishonesty, interest, and late-payment surcharges. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims using fabricated export declarations or sham foreign-currency receipts are subject to criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act and the Customs Act, with retrospective reassessment within the extended ten-year statute of limitations for concealed or fraudulent transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Korean won is a free-floating advanced-economy currency fully convertible for current-account trade and e-commerce transactions, but the capital account remains subject to declaratory and documentary controls. For Yuhan Hoesa, an online maternity and infant-supplies retailer, KRW received from domestic sales is freely usable within the Republic of Korea. Foreign currency receipts from offshore customers must be collected and remitted into a registered foreign currency account at an authorized Korean bank, with the underlying export or e-commerce sale reported through the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS). The Bank of Korea supervises a delegated foreign-exchange-bank system, requiring every inbound wire for equity, loans, or trade to be routed through an authorized earner bank with the correct purpose code. Profit remittance for foreign shareholders requires prior proof of the Foreign Direct Investment report, corporate tax clearance from the National Tax Service, evidence of paid-in capital subscription execution, and a bank remittance application filed before the transfer. There is no compulsory KRW conversion of export proceeds; the treasury may retain foreign currency in designated foreign currency deposit accounts without surrender. The regime presents tolerable but non-negligible friction for capital account movements, and all external borrowings, security provisions, and guarantees above statutory thresholds require prior MOEF reporting or approval, although pure current-account trade flows face no substantive convertibility blockade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under FETA enforcement notices, export proceeds must be collected within 6 months from the date of export declaration issuance (airway bill or bill of lading). No mandatory conversion ratio to KRW exists; a 0% surrender obligation applies, and the exporter may hold the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Deposit Account at a domestic authorized bank. All repatriated proceeds must be matched against the shipment records and declared via FEIS to the bank within 30 days of receipt. Where collection exceeds 180 days, written justification and an MOEF extension are mandatory to avoid penalties. For low-ticket e-commerce sales, the bank may accept a consolidated export ledger with peripheral transactional evidence rather than a per-parcel customs submission.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act (FETA) and the Foreign Investment Promotion Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - Offshore digital financial institutions and EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex are not licensed under the FETA or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act to hold Korean-won balances or issue local IBAN-style accounts for a Korean resident legal entity. These EMIs can only act as third-party remittance channels through partnerships with authorized Korean banks; the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its primary corporate current account and its foreign currency deposit accounts with a domestic Tier-1 bank for FEIS reporting, foreign exchange confirmation, and tax record integration. EMIs may be used as auxiliary collection escrows from end-consumers, but settlement must be promptly swept to the domestic Korean bank account via SWIFT MT103 with a proper export declaration reference. The legal risk of using an unlicensed EMI for core treasury deposits is severe: bank account freezing, criminal non-processing, and denial of tax credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign equity contribution to Yuhan Hoesa must be preceded by a Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) report filed with a domestic commercial bank under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act. The investing bank will open a dedicated Capital Deposit Account in foreign currency as an escrow virtual account. The foreign investor then instructs its remitting bank to send an SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code corresponding to inward foreign investment (e.g., code 72xxx) and referencing the FDI notification number. The receiving Korean bank will match the MT103 against the FEIS record, issue a certificate of inward remittance, and deposit the funds into the escrow account. After the paid-in capital verification is completed by the statutory auditor and the Korean entity's commercial registration is electronically updated through the court registration system, the funds can be released to the operational KRW account at the prevailing spot rate and thereafter used for business operating expenses. No unauthorized local utilization of the escrow before approval is permissible.For trade settlements related to the sale and procurement of maternity and infant supplies, the primary rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments to overseas suppliers and freight forwarders when the trade document package is self-contained; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for high-value or structured transactions requiring bank certification, particularly for bulk manufacturing orders from Taiwan, China, or Southeast Asia; and (iii) Documentary Collections with MT400/MT430 for moderate-trust counterparties requiring presentation of transport documents through the banking channel. Every outbound payment, regardless of rail, must be declared on the Foreign Exchange Declaration form and reported through FEIS to the remitting Korean bank. For disbursements above KRW 2 million or USD equivalent, the bank will also require the relevant pro-forma invoice, purchase order, bill of lading, and customs export/import matching data. Payments must not be sent to anonymous-beneficiary or shell accounts; the beneficiary account should reside in a cleared jurisdiction.Forward exchange contracts (vespa-style spot/forward up to 12 months) with authorized Korean banks, using the underlying export and import invoices as the economic exposure basis; speculative uncovered forwards are prohibited for Yuhan Hoesa because they exceed current-account autonomy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally prohibited for an onshore domestic entity; NDFs are available only to offshore counterparties in the global broker market and are not permissible for the treasury of a Korean resident LLC.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) are permissible solely for matching external debt or foreign-investment principal obligations, with mandatory prior FEIS registration and MOEF approval for tenors exceeding three years; currency options are not commonly allowed without confirmation of a physically settled underlying trade.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - Offshore digital financial institutions and EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex are not licensed under the FETA or the Electronic Financial Transactions Act to hold Korean-won balances or issue local IBAN-style accounts for a Korean resident legal entity. These EMIs can only act as third-party remittance channels through partnerships with authorized Korean banks; the Yuhan Hoesa must maintain its primary corporate current account and its foreign currency deposit accounts with a domestic Tier-1 bank for FEIS reporting, foreign exchange confirmation, and tax record integration. EMIs may be used as auxiliary collection escrows from end-consumers, but settlement must be promptly swept to the domestic Korean bank account via SWIFT MT103 with a proper export declaration reference. The legal risk of using an unlicensed EMI for core treasury deposits is severe: bank account freezing, criminal non-processing, and denial of tax credit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 48 of the Framework Act on National Taxes: up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine not exceeding KRW 100 million; supplemented by Article 50 penalty adding 40% of the evaded tax amount; local income tax surcharge penalties apply; the NTS can also levy a 3% monthly interest on unpaid principal and a travel ban or overseas emigration authorization refusal.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest under Article 89 of the Local Tax Act and general tort liability; the NTS may freeze bank accounts, seize real estate, and blacklist the director's passport until the outstanding assessment is settled; criminal conviction can lead to pre-trial arrest and up to 5 years' imprisonment, with forced recovery against personal assets through the National Tax Collection Act.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: NTS may recharacterize the entity under general anti-avoidance rules of Article 53 of the Corporation Tax Act and Art. 18 of the Individual Tax Law equivalent, disregard the legal structure, assess CFC income against the Korean directors or shareholders, and impose a 2% per month overdue interest charge (up to 12 months) plus a 40% unfair under-reporting penalty under the Framework Act.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all taxes arising from the disregarded entity, including CFC-attributed income, because the acts were performed for a sham owner; the director's personal foreign bank accounts can be discovered via Financial Action Task Force automatic exchange, and the NTS may refer the case to the Korean Supreme Prosecutors' Office for fraud; prison term up to 5 years and a fine, plus personal civil liability for the benefit received; travel restrictions and a global asset freeze order may be issued.
Money Laundering and AML

Statutory sanction: Article 6 of the Act on the Reporting of Certain Financial Transactions 2006 (KFIU) requires reporting every foreign exchange transaction above KRW 5 million; failure is punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine up to KRW 20 million; any false declaration may constitute unlawful foreign exchange transfer under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act carrying similar penal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if they condone or omit AML oversight; maximum prison term 3 years for non-reporting, or up to 5 years when combined with tax fraud; civil confiscation of the underlying proceeds under Article 48 of the Act on Aggravated Punishment for Specific Economic Crimes; directors may be stripped of capacity to serve as officers of any Korean financial institution; the travel ban imposed by the NTS under Article 7 of the National Tax Collection Act prevents directors from leaving Korea until financial obligations are secured.

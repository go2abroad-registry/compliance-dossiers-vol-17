A virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected for this high-risk PSP and payment gateway industry by Korean tax authorities, the Commercial Registration Court, and licensed banks. The National Tax Service and financial institutions require a physical registered office evidenced by a lease in the corporate name, with actual operational substance, personnel, books and records, and visible management. A serviced office with dedicated employees and physical infrastructure may be accepted, but a bare virtual mailbox is not.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for incorporation of a Yuhan Hoesa. However, the amount stated in the Articles of Incorporation must be fully paid in cash or in kind before completion of the incorporation registration, and proof of payment, such as a bank certificate of deposit or an appraisal report for in-kind contribution, must be submitted to the registry. No blocked escrow account is mandatory under the Commercial Act for a non-public Yuhan Hoesa. If a non-resident member acquires capital contributions equal to at least 10 percent of the company, a foreign investment notification must be filed under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act before or within the statutory period after such acquisition. Separately, the Financial Services Commission may impose a prudential minimum net capital threshold before granting or registering the electronic financial business authorization for a payment gateway.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense fire clearance for industrial-scale facilities is not applicable. No structural load inspection is required unless server racks, heavy equipment, or physical storage installations create a floor-loading risk, in which case a structural engineer's certification and local building inspection will be necessary.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online electronic portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must first appoint a domestic tax representative (Napse Nae-Gwalli-in) unless it has a fixed place of business in South Korea. The foreign entity must complete an Application for Business Registration (Saeopja Deungrok Shincheong) with the relevant district tax office having jurisdiction over the domestic place of business or, in the absence of one, the tax representative's registered address. Upon approval, the NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number (Saeopja Deungrok Beonho), which serves simultaneously as the corporate Tax Identification Number, the Value-Added Tax identifier, and the e-invoice reference key. The same number is used for corporate income tax and VAT administration, and the entity must file an incorporation tax report with the NTS and, if applicable, register with the local customs office for export activities.

Default tax classification

None. South Korea does not provide check-the-box elections, pass-through classifications, or hybrid mismatch elections for a Yuhan Hoesa (limited liability company). A Korean LLC is mandatorily treated as a domestic corporation subject to entity-level corporate tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and no elective flow-through treatment is available.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

KRW 0 / no general turnover threshold — VAT registration is mandatory for any business activity in South Korea, including cross-border merchants acquiring or clearing card transactions for Korean or foreign merchants where VAT registration nexus is established

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported tangible goods and for internationally supplied services, including qualifying cross-border payment processing services supplied to non-resident merchants where the service is consumed or effectively used outside South Korea and the consideration is received in foreign currency. The statutory basis is the zero-rating provision of the Value-Added Tax Act, which requires proof that the goods were physically exported or that the services were supplied to a non-resident for use outside Korea, evidenced by export declarations, foreign exchange receipt confirmations, and underlying commercial contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Declaration Certificate / Saechul Shin-go Piljeung)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Foreign Exchange Transaction Confirmation (OEgukwan Geo-rae Jeungmyeong), equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate / FIRC
  • Bank Statement evidencing foreign currency repatriation from the overseas buyer or merchant
  • VAT electronic tax invoice issued at 0% and transmitted through HomeTax

Electronic tax invoices (Jeonja Saeupgyesanseo) are mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses and must be issued through the NTS HomeTax system. The e-invoice must be submitted to the NTS by the deadline prescribed under Article 32 of the VAT Act, with real-time or near-real-time electronic transmission for self-issued invoices. API-based bulk submission and authenticated machine-readable transmission through HomeTax are required for high-volume PSP operators. The system applies digital serial numbers, electronic signatures, and NTS approval numbers for anti-fraud integrity; failure to issue or timely transmit e-invoices triggers surcharges, penalties, and denial of input VAT credits.

The NTS may deny zero-rating if the exporter or service supplier cannot substantiate physical export, foreign currency settlement, and non-resident service use within the statutory six-month foreign exchange repatriation period. False or unverified zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT reassessment at 10%, plus additional understatement penalties up to 10% or 20% under the Framework Act on National Taxes, e-invoice non-compliance surcharges, and criminal sanctions for intentional fraudulent tax refund claims. Repeat or large-scale schemes can attract aggravated penalties and director liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a hybrid convertibility architecture. The Korean won is fully convertible for most current-account transactions, including service payments, dividends, royalties, and trade settlements, provided a licensed foreign exchange bank obtains the statutory transaction purpose code. Capital-account flows such as equity injections, loan investments, intercompany lending, acquisition of foreign real estate, and derivatives are subject to prior notification or approval under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The Bank of Korea and the Ministry of Economy and Finance operate a 24-hour reporting system for every cross-border receipt and payment, and the government can impose temporary measures during financial instability. There is no mandatory parity, surrender, or centralised auction mechanism; exporters may retain one hundred percent of proceeds in foreign currency deposit accounts. However, real-world convertibility is not absolute: non-resident Korean won is inaccessible offshore, and banks require commercial documentation before converting received funds. Profit repatriation through dividends is permitted after audited accounts, withholding tax settlement, and foreign exchange bank notification. Therefore, a cross-border payment gateway must deploy robust treasury controls, evidence each transaction with a clean audit trail, and pre-clear any capital-account movement to avoid freezing or penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article 21 of the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree, an exporter must collect export proceeds within 18 months from the export declaration date and repatriate them to Korea through a licensed foreign exchange bank unless an extension is granted by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; one hundred percent of export proceeds may be held in a foreign currency deposit account or converted into Korean won at the exporter's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea, acting jointly with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and delegated foreign exchange banks under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI licences, such as those issued under the UK Electronic Money Regulations, EU PSD2, or Hong Kong MSO regimes, have no passporting recognition in South Korea. A Yuhan Hoesa incorporated in Korea and performing payment gateway or merchant acquiring functions must be registered as an electronic financial business operator under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act and must report to the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit as a money service business. Offshore EMIs cannot independently open KRW clearing accounts or establish direct correspondent membership with domestic banks without licensing a local branch or entering into a formal partnership with a licensed domestic bank. Consequently, fintech EMI reliance is ranked very low viability for primary treasury operations; all in-country liquidity and clearing must sit with licensed Korean banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be remitted from the ultimate foreign shareholder's bank account via SWIFT MT103 using the South Korean foreign direct investment purpose code associated with bank-established capital accounting. The remitter name must match the registered shareholders' registry, and third-party remittances are prohibited. Funds must arrive in a dedicated capital escrow account at a licensed foreign exchange bank. Upon receipt, the foreign exchange bank will issue a certificate of foreign capital receipt and a capital verification certificate. The Yuhan Hoesa must then file the paid-in capital with the local registry court and complete registration before any operational disbursement may occur. Every outbound remittance must be filed with the correct foreign exchange purpose code and is subject to underlying-contract review by the bank's trade and treasury desk. Turnover: A certified independent CPA audit is compulsory if: (1) the entity holds an electronic financial business license as a payment gateway / PSP under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, requiring an FSS-registered auditor regardless of asset size; or (2) total assets at the immediately preceding fiscal year-end equal or exceed KRW 12,000,000,000 (approximately USD 9.2 million); or (3) annual operating revenue equals or exceeds KRW 100,000,000,000 (approximately USD 76.9 million) for a non-listed non-SME entity; or (4) the entity has 100 or more regular employees and total assets exceed KRW 10,000,000,000 (approximately USD 7.7 million), to the extent adopted in the Enforcement Decree of the Act on External Audit of Stock Companies; or (5) members holding at least 5% of the membership interests validly request an audit in accordance with the Korean Commercial Code and the External Audit Act. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% top marginal corporate income tax rate; progressive brackets of 10% on taxable income up to KRW 200 million, 20% on KRW 200 million to 20 billion, 22% on KRW 20 billion to 300 billion, and 25% above KRW 300 billion

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% combined domestic statutory rate on outbound dividends to non-resident corporations: 20% corporate income tax withholding under Article 98 of the Corporate Tax Act plus 2% local income tax surcharge (10% of the national withholding tax) under the Local Tax Act. Treaty rates may reduce the dividend WHT to 0%-15%, depending on the residence jurisdiction and qualifying shareholding level

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a cross-border payment gateway enterprise, related-party international transactions of KRW 5 billion or more in a category require the preparation and retention of Local File documentation; the Group Master File is required when the Korean IT/PSP group annual revenue reaches KRW 100 billion; CbC reporting is triggered at KRW 1 trillion consolidated group revenue; separate report of international related-party transactions through HomeTax is required when aggregate cross-border transactions exceed KRW 500 million

Economic substance scope: Korea does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but applies substance-over-form, OECD permanent establishment attribution, and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. For PSP entities, the Core Income-Generating Activities - signing merchants, transaction authorization, treasury management, compliance and anti-money laundering oversight, and software ownership - must be carried out in Korea; if contracts, personnel and decisions are booked offshore while the economic activity is in Korea, the tax authority may disallow the intercompany profit shift, re-attribute income to the Korean branch/P.E., and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Transfer Pricing Sham

Statutory sanction: Under Article 4 of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 50 million; under the Framework Act on National Taxes, a 40% fraudulent under-reporting penalty, 20% unfaithful return penalty, and 0.03% daily late-payment interest; the National Tax Service can re-determine the Korean tax base using TNMM/Pay-Split, ban the transfer pricing document deduction and issue adjustment notice

Director personal liability: Under Article 401 of the Korean Commercial Act, directors who intentionally or negligently violate the law are jointly and severally liable to third parties for damages; the corporate veil may be pierced because the shell agreement is a 'fictitious facade' and the Korean entity is a mere instrumentality; directors may face civil attachment of personal assets, passport validity warning and departure blacklist from the National Tax Service, and debarment from acting as officers in any licensed financial institution, plus criminal imprisonment if they signed the false tax return and beneficiary documentation.
Withholding Tax Evasion and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Payer is jointly liable as withholding agent for the unpaid 22% statutory tax plus 2% local surcharge, with a 20%-40% failure penalty under Article 47-2 of the Framework Act on National Taxes and 0.03% daily overdue interest; the treaty benefit is denied from the beginning under the PPT; National Tax Service imposes additional withholding plus local income tax; the Financial Services Commission can also issue a precaution fine up to KRW 50 million for failure to report the non-resident transaction under the Act on Reporting and Use of Certain Financial Information

Director personal liability: Directors who overdraw outbound distribution without proper tax withholding are personally liable to the company under Article 399 of the Commercial Act and to creditors/claimants under Article 401; Korean courts can assert 'lifting of corporate veil' where the foreign shareholder is a dummy and funds are in fact controlled by Korean beneficial owners; each director faces criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years, accumulation of civil restitution for unpaid withholding, a travel ban based on NTS tax-charged unpaid list, and possible revocation of Korean executive IP for financial services.
Unlicensed Payment Business / AML and Trade Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 51(1) of the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine up to KRW 500 million, in addition to license revocation and administrative corrective orders by the Financial Services Commission; violation of the Financial Transaction Report Act for not filing STRs triggers criminal disposition up to imprisonment for 3 years or a criminal fine up to KRW 60 million, plus an administrative fine up to KRW 200 million; the Financial Supervisory Service may order disgorgement of transaction profits

Director personal liability: Directors and officers bear personal civil liability for damages suffered by merchants, banks and platform customers; under Article 401 of the Commercial Act, they are jointly liable; under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, the responsible director's personal assets can be attached, and the director is automatically disqualified from serving on any licensed Korean PSP or bank; the Financial Supervisory Service and Korean Immigration can impose a travel ban and blacklist, and the Korea Financial Intelligence Unit can issue a sanctions 'red flag' notice for suspected money laundering; repeated violations can increase imprisonment to 10 years.

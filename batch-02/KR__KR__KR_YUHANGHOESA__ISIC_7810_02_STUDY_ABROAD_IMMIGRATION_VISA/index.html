Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Yuhan Hoesa under Korean law. The members may fix the capital at any amount in the Articles of Incorporation. No blocked escrow account is statutorily required, but contributed funds must be deposited into a domestic capital subscription account in the Company's name and held there until the court registration is completed, after which funds may be used for business operations.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea; online portal: HomeTax (www.hometax.go.kr) for registration, VAT returns, and e-Tax Invoices; Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS (www.unipass.customs.go.kr) for export declarations and export documentation.

E-government filing portal

For a Korean Yuhan Hoesa (domestic corporation) with foreign shareholders, the tax identity is the 10-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by NTS. Procedure: (1) complete Korean Commercial Registry incorporation and obtain the certificate of incorporation; (2) open a corporate bank account and report any non-resident equity investment to a designated foreign exchange bank under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act; (3) within 20 days of business commencement, file a Business Registration Application with the competent NTS district tax office, attaching the articles of incorporation, certificate of incorporation, domestic lease agreement, identification of the domestic representative and foreign shareholders, and the foreign investment remittance certificate; (4) NTS issues the BRN, which functions as the corporate tax TIN and VAT registration number simultaneously; (5) if a foreign non-resident entity has no Korean place of business but supplies or repays VAT-connected Korean-source services, it must separately file a Non-Resident Business Registration through HomeTax, appoint a domestic tax representative for tax collection and refund purposes, and obtain a separate BRN; (6) the same BRN must be referenced on e-Tax Invoices, VAT returns, corporate tax returns, and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. There is no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election in Korea. A Yuhan Hoesa is automatically an entity-level taxpayer subject to Korean corporate tax on worldwide income. A qualifying wholly-owned Korean domestic subsidiary may elect group consolidated taxation under the Corporate Tax Act; the simplified VAT regime based on the KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover threshold is available only to individual entrepreneurs and not to a corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold. Any person, including a non-resident foreign entity, making taxable supplies of goods or services in Korea must register as a VAT entrepreneur within 20 days of commencing business. A simplified taxable threshold of KRW 80,000,000 annual turnover is available only to individual small businesses and is not available to a Yuhan Hoesa corporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Act, exported goods are zero-rated once the export declaration is accepted by Korea Customs Service and the goods depart Korea. Export services are zero-rated only if expressly recognized as exported services under the VAT Act and Presidential Decree. For study-abroad placement and immigration visa consulting, zero-rating applies when the Yuhan Hoesa supplies the service to a non-resident counterparty, the service is performed and consumed outside Korea, and consideration is received in foreign currency. Commission income from offshore schools, overseas immigration authorities, or non-resident principals can qualify. Fees charged to Korean resident students or to applicants for Korean visas are taxable at 10%. Qualifying digital services that are delivered offshore to non-residents and paid in foreign currency may also be zero-rated. Excess input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable under the VAT Act.

  • For goods: Korea Customs Service Export Declaration Certificate issued through UNI-PASS, bill of lading or air waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and a foreign exchange bank certificate proving receipt of foreign currency export proceeds.
  • For zero-rated study-abroad and immigration advisory services: written contract with the non-resident counterparty, commercial invoice denominated in foreign currency, foreign currency receipt certificate issued by a designated Korean foreign exchange bank, service delivery documentation such as admission letters, visa approvals, placement confirmations or service completion reports, and a signed client statement confirming the service was performed and consumed offshore.
  • Where the Yuhan Hoesa acts as an intermediary for overseas schools or immigration authorities, retain the agency agreement, the principal's invoice, and the onward foreign currency remittance evidence to substantiate the offshore supply.
  • Digital service exports: retain the user subscription or service contract, server or delivery location evidence confirming overseas use, payment gateway records, foreign currency receipts, and a technical delivery report where applicable.

Every VAT taxpayer must issue a Korean electronic tax invoice (e-Tax Invoice) for taxable B2B supplies and transmit it operationally to the NTS through HomeTax no later than one day after the supply date. B2C cash transactions of KRW 10,000 or more in cash-receipt-mandatory businesses also require real-time cash receipt transmission. Korea does not operate a physical fiscal stamp or real-time clearance device regime; anti-fraud control is achieved through NTS pre-validation of the Business Registration Number on every e-Tax Invoice, mandatory electronic archive retention, and cross-matching of e-Tax Invoices, credit card data, cash receipts, customs declarations, and foreign exchange receipts. Approved ERP vendors and e-invoice service providers transmit through HomeTax EDI/API interfaces.

Export zero-rating is heavily audited by NTS. If the complete substantiation bundle is not preserved, or the export consideration is not received in foreign currency within the statutory 180-day period without an approved extension from a designated foreign exchange bank, the zero-rate is retroactively clawed back and the supply is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply. NTS will then assess VAT at 10%, together with statutory additional taxes for under-reporting and late payment under the Framework Act on National Taxes, and, in cases of fabricated export documentation or false e-Tax Invoices, criminal liability under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, including imprisonment or fines of up to several times the evaded tax, and a discretionary 40% fraud penalty. Failure to repatriate proceeds may also trigger penalties under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, including administrative fines, export restriction orders, and compliance sanctions. NTS continuously cross-matches export declarations, e-Tax Invoices, corporate bank accounts, and foreign exchange receipts to identify overclaimed zero-rated supplies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Korea maintains a liberalized foreign exchange market with statutory reporting obligations rather than blanket surrender requirements. The won is convertible for current account transactions, including fees for study-abroad placement and immigration visa services, subject to documentary evidence and settlement through authorized foreign exchange banks. Capital account transactions, including inbound direct investment in a Yuhan Hoesa, require declaration and registration under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but there is no fixed lower-layer notification threshold for equity subscribed by foreign investors. Profit remittances are permitted once corporate taxes, withholding taxes, and external audits are satisfied; the branch/company must present audited financial statements and a tax clearance certificate in order to remit dividends abroad. Foreign currency proceeds may be retained in designated foreign currency deposit accounts, and there is no statutory mandate to convert export or service receipts into won at a fixed ratio. However, any conversion into KRW must be executed with a licensed foreign exchange bank at market rates, and all cross-border settlement events must be transmitted to the Korea Federation of Banks for statistical and AML surveillance. In practice, the greatest hurdles are documentary: a Korean bank will require full underlying contracts, capital verification certificates, and an explanatory memorandum for each significant inbound wire before treating the funds as cleared operating capital. The absence of minimum paid-in capital does not exempt the entity from enhanced due diligence, and perceived mismatches between the corporate purpose, remitter profile, and client geography will attract further scrutiny. Overall, the Korean FX regime is best characterized as regulatory liberalization with intensive ex post reporting rather than hard administrative rationing of foreign exchange.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea, operating in coordination with the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Korea Federation of Banks' Foreign Exchange Information System under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

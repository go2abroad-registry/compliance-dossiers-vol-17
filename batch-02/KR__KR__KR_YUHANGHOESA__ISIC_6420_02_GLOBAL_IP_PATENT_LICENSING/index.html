Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an IP HoldCo engaged in patent sub-licensing, Korean banks and the National Tax Service reject a mail-only virtual address. The Commercial Act and the Registration of Place of Business rules require the registered head-office to be an actual place of business with a lease agreement, office signboard and installed communication equipment. Although some registered agents offer virtual business addresses, corporate account opening and VAT registration are routinely declined unless the tenant can produce the original lease and undergo a bank site visit. A serviced office with dedicated lockable room, lease directly in the company's name, and a physically present representative is the minimum acceptable substance; an unregistered postal drop is wholly impermissible.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Yuhan Hoesa; the initial capital schedule is set forth in the Articles. Funds must be deposited into a temporary subscription account with a licensed Korean bank and may be converted to the corporate operating account after registration. No blocked escrow account is mandated by law; however, if the investor is a foreign national or entity, the inward contribution must be reported in a Foreign Investment Report before or within the grace period prescribed by the Foreign Investment Promotion Act.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — no civil-defense fire permit, structural load inspection or workplace-safety approval is required for a patent/trademark licensing holding company without a factory, warehouse or assembly line. If the Company leases commercial office space, it must obtain from the building owner a valid fire-safety certificate, install fire extinguishers and evacuation signage, and apply general duties under the Occupational Safety and Health Act for office personnel (electrical safety, sanitation and emergency procedures). The Seoul Fire Department standard special fire safety management certification is not required unless the future business changes to physical operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service of the Republic of Korea (NTS) via the HomeTax electronic tax portal

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity or a newly formed Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC), the business operator must register for tax purposes by filing an application for business registration with the competent district tax office or through the NTS HomeTax online portal. The application requires submission of the articles of incorporation or foreign company registration certificate, proof of the domestic place of business or domestic tax representative appointment, power of attorney, identification of the legal representative, and, where applicable, foreign investment reporting documentation. The NTS issues a 10-digit Business Registration Number which functions as the Corporate Income Tax ID and VAT ID. Registration must be completed within 20 days of business commencement; upon approval, the registration certificate is issued and the same number is used for VAT returns, electronic tax invoice issuance, corporate tax filings, and customs clearance documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. South Korea does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election. A Yuhan Hoesa is taxed as a standalone domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act. Limited optional consolidated corporate filing is available for wholly-owned Korean subsidiaries only under strict statutory conditions, and hybrid mismatch rules apply under domestic tax law to neutralise deduction with no inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold applies. Any person engaged in a business supplying goods or services in South Korea is mandatorily required to register for VAT regardless of turnover level. Small businesses with annual taxable turnover below the statutory simplified taxable person threshold (generally KRW 80 million) may be subject to simplified VAT assessment procedures, but VAT registration remains mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying services are statutorily zero-rated under the Value Added Tax Act. For IP and patent licensing, zero-rating applies when the IP is supplied to a non-resident licensee for use outside South Korea, and the supply is not made to a permanent establishment in Korea. Claiming the 0% rate requires demonstrable foreign use of the licensed rights, receipt of consideration in foreign currency from the non-resident licensee, and contemporaneous documentation evidencing the cross-border nature of the transaction.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement Certificate

South Korea mandates the issuance of electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax e-invoice system. Electronic invoices are transmitted to the NTS in near real time using API/EDI integrations; the NTS validates and assigns an approved electronic invoice number with a digital security seal. There is no pre-clearance fiscal authorisation model, but anti-fraud controls are implemented through mandatory e-invoice issuance deadlines, invoice matching, digital time-stamping, and mandatory cash receipts for qualifying B2C transactions.

The National Tax Service may audit zero-rated export claims and, if the supply is not fully substantiated or foreign currency repatriation is not evidenced within the statutory period, will reassess VAT at 10% on the supply value with retroactive effect, together with additional tax penalties, interest, and surcharges. Fraudulent or false zero-rating claims may trigger criminal penalties through tax evasion prosecution, including fines or imprisonment, and the taxpayer bears the burden of proving export delivery, foreign currency settlement, and foreign use of the IP rights.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains current account convertibility for normal trade and service transactions, including patent royalties, trademark licensing, and other IP-related commercial flows, under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. By contrast, capital account transactions such as foreign direct investment, overseas deposits, loans, securities acquisition, and profit repatriation are subject to mandatory declaration or prior approval through a designated foreign exchange bank. The Korean won is not freely convertible outside the jurisdiction; settlement must be routed through licensed local banking channels, and every remittance must be supported by real underlying trade or service evidence. Foreign investors are not generally prohibited from repatriating profit, but dividend remittances require certified earnings allocation by the board of directors, proof of distributable statutory earnings, tax withholding clearance, and an approved foreign exchange report. Repatriation of invested capital upon equity disposal or liquidation also requires retrospective filing through the custodian bank. During periods of severe market volatility, Korean authorities may impose emergency controls on foreign exchange positions, payment periods, and surrender requirements. Practically, the onboarding and transaction-reporting burden is substantial, and all IP royalty flows must be documented with purpose codes and supported by contracts to avoid administrative fines and foreign exchange transaction penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters, including IP licensors earning foreign currency royalties, must collect all proceeds from foreign counterparts through a designated foreign exchange bank within one year from the date of the export declaration or the provision of services, unless an extension is granted by the authorities. There is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring foreign currency to be exchanged into KRW; however, 100% of the invoiced foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into Korea and either deposited into a foreign currency account or converted to KRW through the settlement bank. The actual receipt and settlement must be reported in a timely manner under the Bank of Korea's balance-of-payments reporting framework; failure to adhere to the one-year collection timeline or to repatriate the full invoice amount may result in administrative sanctions and customs penalties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Korea (BOK) in conjunction with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the licensing and oversight authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its Enforcement Decree; operational FX supervision is delegated to the Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service, with all reporting executed through designated foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and multi-currency wallet providers are effectively barred from acting as the principal foreign exchange clearing counterparty or account-holding institution for a Korean-resident entity because they do not hold a foreign exchange business entity license from the Korean authorities and cannot execute Foreign Exchange Transactions Act reports. A fintech EMI can only function as a technical interface or front-end aggregator wrapped around a fully licensed onshore bank, and even then the legal account relationship and SWIFT-clearing responsibility must remain with a local commercial bank. For this global IP patent licensing vehicle, the prudent and compliance-safe approach is to open a multi-currency operational account directly with a tier-1 Korean bank and treat EMIs as supplementary payment dashboards rather than core treasury rails. Therefore, the viability rating for standalone offshore EMI onboarding is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from the foreign parent company must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to a designated foreign exchange bank in Korea with the correct balance-of-payments purpose code for cross-border direct equity investment. The bank will register the transaction under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and issue a foreign currency deposit certificate evidencing receipt of the cross-border proceeds. Following the share allotment, a licensed Korean accountant must perform capital verification against the bank's SWIFT advice, the foreign currency deposit certificate, the board resolution, and the updated corporate registry. The Korean company must also file the required foreign investment report through the designated bank to avoid classification as unauthorized borrowing or irregular remittance. All funds should be held in a defined Capital Escrow Account until the statutory foreign investment registration or shareholder notification has been completed.For trade settlements and royalty-related disbursements from Korea, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers covering licensing fees and intercompany disbursements. For physical goods embedded in the licensing model, a commercial letter of credit issued by a Korean bank or confirmed by an overseas correspondent bank is acceptable, together with transport and customs documents. Documentary collections are permissible for lower-risk trade flows but carry greater non-payment and documentary compliance risk. All outbound disbursements must be reported to the designated bank under the Bank of Korea's foreign exchange reporting framework, with the correct BOP purpose code for royalty payments or goods trade, and supported by the relevant license agreement, tax invoice, customs declaration, or board-approved intercompany settlement statement.Deliverable onshore FX forward contracts executed with a domestic tier-1 bank against real underlying IP royalty exposure, up to traditionally 100 percent of the documented underlying exposure, with settlement on the maturity date in KRW or the convertible foreign currency.Currency options and cross-currency interest rate swaps used solely for hedging balance sheet foreign exchange exposures arising from the patent licensing operation and cross-border intercompany loans, subject to the bank's internal derivative eligibility assessment.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not an available hedging substitute for the Korean-resident entity in the domestic market; NDFs are generally reserved for non-resident counterparties in the offshore market, and domestic hedging must be executed through licensed onshore derivative products or permitted over-the-counter instruments booked by the designated bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

21% national corporate income tax (progressive: 9% up to KRW 200 million; 19% for KRW 200 million to KRW 20 billion; 21% above KRW 20 billion), plus a 10% local income tax surcharge on the national tax (resulting in a maximum combined effective headline rate of 23.1%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

22% (20% national withholding tax plus 2% local income tax surcharge) on gross dividends paid by a Korean corporation to a non-resident corporation, unless reduced under an applicable bilateral double tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: KRW 10 billion per fiscal year for intercompany transactions with foreign related parties triggers mandatory Local File and Master File submissions; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more; documentation must be filed within 45 days after the annual tax return due date.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the substance-over-form doctrine under Article 4 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, judicial anti-avoidance rules, CFC legislation under the International Tax Adjustment Act Article 6, and treaty anti-abuse provisions mandate that an IP HoldCo must show actual personnel, office premises, decision-making, risk control, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) within Korea to benefit from DTA relief and arm's-length transfer pricing; a mere paper entity or passive dormant recipient of royalties from foreign licensees will be ignored for Korean tax purposes, with income imputed to the actual shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 9(1) of the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act, a person who fails to report taxable income in excess of KRW 300 million may be imprisoned for a term of 2 to 5 years and fined up to 3 times the evaded amount; additionally, the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 47-2 imposes a 40% negligence penalty on under-reported taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized or failed to prevent the evasion are subject to piercing of the corporate veil under the Framework Act on National Taxes Article 39, making them jointly and severally liable for the full unpaid corporate tax and fines; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment, forfeiture of personal assets, and a travel ban (blacklisting) by the National Tax Service and the Ministry of Justice.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Statutory TP Penalty

Statutory sanction: Under Article 11 of the International Tax Adjustment Act, failure to submit transfer pricing documentation triggers a penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted amount, and unauthorized royalty deductions may be denied in full; the payer may additionally face a 20% penalty for under-declaration of taxable income under the Framework Act on National Taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the amount of tax underpaid in proportion to their control, with civil asset restitution reaching the entire Benami-omitted sums; a wilful TP violation constitutes tax evasion for criminal purposes, leading to personal fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years, in addition to professional disqualification as a continuing director under the Commercial Act.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse / Treaty Abuse and AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Korea's substance-over-form doctrine under Article 4 of the Framework Act on National Taxes allows the tax authority to disregard the entity and deny treaty benefits; Article 6 of the International Tax Adjustment Act imposes CFC taxation on the undistributed passive income of the shell entity; and under the Real Name Financial Transactions Act, failure to disclose true beneficial ownership exposes the company to a statutory fine and amendment of the AML registration, while a false TRC claim may trigger fraud penalties and extension of the statute of limations.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in the shell arrangement are personally liable for restitution of all unpaid withholding tax and local surcharge amounts plus interest; the courts can pierce the veil to execute against personal assets; directors also face travel blacklisting, a civil extended liability under Commercial Act Article 399 for damages to other shareholders, and criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for organised evasion or abuse of the cross-border payment system.

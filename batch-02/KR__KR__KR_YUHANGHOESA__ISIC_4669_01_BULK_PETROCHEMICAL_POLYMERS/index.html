Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, with electronic filing and registration via the HomeTax portal (https://www.hometax.go.kr); border and customs tax operations are administered by the Korea Customs Service through the UNI-PASS portal (https://unipass.customs.go.kr).

E-government filing portal

For the Korean Yuhan Hoesa (LLC) engaged in bulk petrochemical polymer wholesale distribution: (1) Prepare the entity's foreign investor ownership documents, articles of incorporation, domestic lease evidence for the principal place of business, and representative identity documents; no statutory paid-in minimum capital is required for a Yuhan Hoesa. (2) Submit the business registration application to the district tax office having jurisdiction over the principal place of business, either in person or through HomeTax, before commencing taxable business operations. (3) The NTS will issue the Korean Business Registration Certificate, which contains the 10-digit Business Registration Number; this number serves simultaneously as the national TIN and the VAT identification number for the LLC. (4) If the foreign parent has no Korean permanent establishment and is itself acting as a non-resident supplier of goods or services into Korea, the non-resident entity must appoint a domestic tax representative under the Framework Act on National Taxes and apply through the jurisdictional district tax office for a non-resident business and VAT registration number; this is a separate TIN from the local LLC's number. (5) Where cross-border trading is performed, the LLC must also register with the Korea Customs Service in UNI-PASS to obtain a customs clearance business identification number for import and export declarations. (6) The LLC must file VAT returns electronically through HomeTax and issue electronic tax invoices in the NTS e-invoice system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC is classified as a domestic corporation under the Corporate Tax Act and is subject to entity-level corporate tax on worldwide income; it is not treated as a pass-through entity. Foreign shareholders are separately taxable on dividends or remittances, and the local LLC remains the registered taxpayer for corporate and VAT purposes.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; Korea does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a Korean Yuhan Hoesa LLC. There are no optional hybrid mismatch entity classification elections under the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, and anti-hybrid rules apply. Only general Korean tax elections are available, such as VAT simplified taxable status for eligible small businesses below KRW 80 million annual turnover, which is not applicable to an institutional petrochemical wholesale enterprise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No KRW threshold: registration is mandatory upon commencement of taxable business activity in Korea. A local Yuhan Hoesa with a Korean place of business has VAT nexus and must obtain a Business Registration Number. The simplified taxable regime is available only for eligible small businesses with annual taxable turnover below KRW 80 million and is not consistent with bulk petrochemical wholesale trading; therefore the general VAT registration and invoicing regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules qualify for zero-rated VAT under the Value-Added Tax Act if the goods are actually exported under a valid export declaration filed with the Korea Customs Service, title and risk pass to a non-resident purchaser, the goods depart Korea, the sale is supported by commercial documentation, and export proceeds are received in foreign exchange and repatriated to Korea in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. The zero-rated treatment also entitles the registered exporter to claim a quick refund of remaining input VAT credits attributable to zero-rated export sales through the VAT return. For this ISIC 4669 wholesale goods transaction, physical customs clearance is the core zero-rating evidence.

  • Customs export declaration (Outward Clearance / Export Bill of Entry) approved by the Korea Customs Service through UNI-PASS
  • Bill of Lading (ocean carriage) or Air Waybill evidencing export movement out of Korea
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign buyer describing the bulk petrochemical polymers and the agreed Incoterms
  • Packing list and vessel or containerized shipment loading records
  • Sales contract or purchase order between the Korean Yuhan Hoesa and the non-resident purchaser
  • Electronic tax invoice issued at zero rate through the NTS electronic tax invoice system
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Acquisition Certificate issued by a Korean foreign exchange bank, confirming receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the statutory repatriation period
  • UNI-PASS export clearance data report or Korea Customs Service-issued export confirmation statement

Registered general VAT taxpayers must issue and transmit electronic tax invoices through the NTS HomeTax electronic tax invoice system for all business-to-business taxable supplies. Each electronic tax invoice must contain the supplier and customer business registration numbers, supply value, VAT amount, and item description, and must be submitted to the NTS within the statutory e-invoice transmission deadlines. Korea does not operate a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping regime comparable to Latin American or Southern European models; instead, the NTS receives e-invoice data in near real time and reconciles it against VAT returns and Korea Customs Service UNI-PASS export and import declarations. For exports, the electronic tax invoice is issued at zero rate, and customs export declaration data provides the clearance evidence. Import VAT is assessed and collected by the Korea Customs Service at the border at 10% and is recoverable as input VAT by the registered LLC subject to ordinary input tax requirements.

The NTS and Korea Customs Service will scrutinize the customs export declaration, transport documents, electronic tax invoice, and foreign exchange receipt evidence to verify that the goods left Korea and that proceeds were repatriated. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine physical export, with false documentation, or where export proceeds are not received within the 180-day foreign-exchange settlement period, the NTS may disallow the zero rate and reassess output VAT at 10% on the supply value, together with statutory interest and additional tax for false tax invoices and failure of electronic invoice obligations under the Value-Added Tax Act. Fraudulent cases may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Punishment of Tax Evaders Act. Retrospective clawback may reach all open tax years under the Framework Act on National Taxes, with the assessment limitation period extended for false or fraudulent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea maintains a liberalised current account with full convertibility for trade-related settlement, but capital account transactions remain subject to the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act and its subordinate regulations. For the subject entity engaged in wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules, import payments for physical polymer cargoes are classified as current account transactions requiring only a genuine underlying trade contract and standard bank documentation. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are likewise treated as current account items for dividends, but the bank will require evidence of corporate tax clearance, audited financial statements, and a dividend resolution; remittance is generally permitted without prior central bank approval, yet the paying Korean bank must file an investment registration certificate and monitor the transaction under its internal anti-money laundering policy. Capital account inflows such as foreign parent loans and equity injections require a preliminary declaration of acquisition or loan agreement on the Foreign Exchange Information System (FEIS) through a designated foreign exchange bank. Convertibility of the Korean won is broadly achievable, but currency mismatches can arise because polymer trading contracts are often invoiced in US dollars while local operating costs are incurred in KRW. Since Korea applies a real demand principle for FX forwards, the entity must demonstrate a firm underlying trade transaction to hedge this mismatch. Surrender obligations were substantially liberalised, so export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts, but collection must occur within the statutory settlement period. In practical underwriting, the bank will review trade flow consistency, invoice authenticity, bill of lading dates, and payment matching to distinguish genuine trade operations from disguised capital movements. The absence of any surrender ratio lowers friction, but strict reporting under FEIS remains mandatory for each trade transaction. Overall, South Korea offers high FX flexibility for operating cash flow, with only narrow regulatory barriers around capital account declarations, non-resident borrowing, and speculative FX positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and settled within six months from the date of export declaration, unless an extension is separately approved by the Minister of Economy and Finance or delegated to the Bank of Korea; there is no mandatory conversion ratio into Korean won, and proceeds may be held in a domestic foreign currency account at a foreign exchange bank, but all receipts must be reported to the foreign exchange bank and traceable to the underlying export customs clearance.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central bank, with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) exercising foreign exchange policy authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very Low - the Financial Services Commission and the Bank of Korea do not recognise offshore fintech EMI or electronic money institutions as licensed banks for corporate cross-border settlement. A foreign EMI cannot open a regulated KRW account or execute FX declarations under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Using such an entity for petrochemical trade settlements would create severe regulatory risk and likely lead to frozen funds, blocked FEIS reporting, and bank rejection of the underlying transactions. The only compliant approach is to use a traditional Korean commercial bank or a locally licensed digital bank chartered under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act, and even then the digital institution would need support for LC operations and trade-based AML screening, which most Korean digital-only banks do not provide.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File preparation and submission is required for a multinational enterprise group with annual consolidated revenue of KRW 100 billion or more; a Local File is required for the Korean entity when its intercompany transactions in the relevant category reach KRW 5 billion in a fiscal year, and a Country-by-Country Report is required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion. Even below these thresholds, contemporaneous TP documentation is mandatory if the NTS issues an adjustment or requests evidence of arm's-length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Korea applies the OECD arm's-length principle and allows the NTS to recharacterize transactions conducted by a Korean entity that lacks economic substance, including circular transactions, shell companies, and excessive management fee arrangements. Although Korea has separate Economic Substance Regulations only for special-purpose entities in tax-haven jurisdictions under the International Tax Adjustment Act, a wholesale LLC with substantial inventory trading must evidence substance through local employees, offices, contracts, and CIGA functions; failing this may expose the entity to permanent establishment attribution adjustments and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under Article 10 of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, conviction carries imprisonment for up to 5 years or a fine of up to 2 times the evaded tax; additionally, the NTS imposes a 40% negligence penalty plus a 20% understatement penalty and daily late-payment surcharges under the Framework Act on National Taxes. Repeat offenders face aggravated imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Korean courts will pierce the corporate veil if the director uses the LLC as a sham to evade taxes, commingles assets, or strips profits to foreign shell entities. The director is personally liable for the full amount of unpaid tax, and under Article 10(2) of the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act, a responsible director can be sentenced to imprisonment of up to 5 years; the foreign parent's director may also be held personally liable if the Korean LLC is found to be an alter ego.
Transfer Pricing and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The NTS may reassess taxable income under Articles 4 and 5 of the Adjustment of International Taxes Act using the TNMM or profit split, impose a 20% adjustment penalty on the additional tax, and disallow all excessive fees. Failure to submit the Local File within the statutory 60-day request window triggers a penalty of up to KRW 30 million per document, and the denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test exposes the transaction to full 22% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Under the principle of substance-over-form and the doctrine of serious negligence, a Korean resident director who signs the return without performing due diligence on the intercompany pricing is jointly liable for the CIT deficiency plus penalties; where the director intentionally structured the shell to divert profits, criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to 3 years and a travel ban or blacklisting by the NTS preventing departure from Korea.
Withholding Tax and Cross-Border Remittance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Article 11 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and Article 30 of the Framework Act on National Taxes, the payer remains liable for the withheld amount plus a 10% penalty for failure to withhold, plus 0.75% per month on the unpaid withholding until payment. Falsification of treaty documents constitutes document forgery and tax fraud, leading to fines up to KRW 200 million and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and the Criminal Act.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the remittance instruction or approves the false withholding return is personally and criminally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and may be subject to a travel ban, passport revocation, and asset seizure by the NTS. In egregious cases, the Korean court may order personal restitution to the NTS of the full grossed-up dividend amount and impose imprisonment of up to 5 years; the director is also disqualified from serving as a company officer in Korea.

Therefore, a virtual office/commercial mailbox is not viable as the primary registered office, but may be used as a secondary address if the registered office lease is authentic and a physical workspace exists in the territory.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

National Tax Service (NTS) of the Republic of Korea, operating through the HomeTax electronic tax portal (https://www.hometax.go.kr); Korea Customs Service (KCS) administers export declarations and customs clearance.

E-government filing portal

A Korean Yuhan Hoesa obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number by filing a Business Registration Application with the NTS through HomeTax within 20 days from the date of business commencement. Non-resident shareholders must appoint a Korean tax representative when there is no resident director with authority over tax matters. The application must be accompanied by the certificate of corporate registration issued by the local court registry, articles of incorporation, power or powers of attorney, foreign shareholder passport or ID authentication, and evidence of the Korean place of business. The NTS then issues a 10-digit business registration number that serves as the corporate tax TIN, VAT ID, and withholding account identifier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Korean Yuhan Hoesa obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number by filing a Business Registration Application with the NTS through HomeTax within 20 days from the date of business commencement. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No exemption threshold for real business VAT registration in Korea. Any person supplying taxable goods or services in Korea, including a Yuhan Hoesa, must register for VAT from business commencement. A simplified VAT filing status exists only for individual sole proprietors whose annual taxable turnover is below KRW 80,000,000; it is not available to an LLC or corporate entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to exported goods when a valid Korea Customs Service export declaration is filed and physical export is confirmed. Zero rating also applies to exported services supplied to a non-resident or foreign customer under the international services provisions of the VAT Act, provided the service, here UI/UX design subscription deliverables, is actually used or consumed outside Korea, the consideration is received in foreign currency or through an approved foreign-exchange settlement method, and the contract clearly evidences the cross-border supply. The design subscription service qualifies when the customer is located outside Korea and no Korean place of business derives benefit from the design services.

  • Korea Customs Service Export Declaration (Export Clearance Certificate) for any goods shipments included in the design subscription hardware or printed deliverables, if applicable
  • Signed UI/UX design subscription contract or work order with the non-resident customer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of any exported tangible goods
  • Foreign exchange bank Confirmation of Receipt of Export Proceeds (inward remittance receipt certificate issued by a Korean foreign-exchange bank)
  • Zero-rated E-Tax Invoice issued through HomeTax showing the application of the 0% VAT rate

All general VAT taxpayers must issue E-Tax Invoices (electronic VAT invoices) for taxable supplies. The E-Tax Invoice data must be transmitted to the NTS HomeTax system in electronic form, either through the HomeTax web portal or approved API or ASP integration, no later than the day following issuance. NTS validates each invoice against the counterparty TIN and stores the issuance in the NTS database; there is no additional adjudication stamp or real-time government clearance API beyond this digital transmission requirement. Paper tax invoices are allowed only in limited exceptional cases, and the cash receipt system applies to B2C supplies where no tax invoice is required.

NTS and Korea Customs Service audit zero-rated export records with strict documentary scrutiny. If the foreign-currency consideration is not repatriated to a Korean foreign-exchange bank within the 180-day statutory collection period, unless an authorized extension is granted, or if the export declaration, service contract, E-Tax Invoice, or bank receipt cannot be produced, the effective zero-rating is denied. In that case the NTS re-assesses VAT at the standard 10% rate from the date of supply, adds VAT penalty surcharges and statutory interest for unfiled or unpaid tax, and may claw back input VAT previously claimed on related costs. Fraudulent documentation or intentionally fabricated export transactions can lead to criminal penalties under the Punishment of Tax Evasion Act and aggravated surcharges; entity directors may also face criminal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

South Korea operates an OECD-compliant, largely liberalised foreign exchange regime under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, but distinguishes strictly between current account and capital account transactions. Current account receipts and payments, including software subscriptions, royalties, and design service fees, are freely convertible and remittable through authorised foreign exchange banks without prior government approval in most cases, although banks retain mandatory reporting obligations to the Korea Customs Service for tracking purposes. Capital account conversions, including inbound foreign direct investment into a Yuhan Hoesa, remain subject to prior FDI notification to the Ministry of Economy and Finance via a foreign exchange bank, and the capital contribution must be routed through a designated foreign currency escrow or capital verification account at a licensed Korean bank. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds, meaning a Korean entity may retain foreign currency in an onshore foreign currency deposit account; however, all export proceeds must be collected through an authorised FX bank and reported within statutory deadlines. Profit remittance by a foreign shareholder requires proof of certified invested capital, an FDI investment notification certificate, audited distributable reserves, corporate tax clearance, and in some cases a board resolution, but is not subject to any actual profit repatriation tax withholding beyond the statutory 22% dividend withholding tax treaty rate applicable after relevant treaty relief. Overall, KRW is convertible on current account, while capital account flows need registration, reporting, and bank-level documentary underwriting, making Tier-1 Korean banks indispensable as the clearing and custody layer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods and services must be received through an authorised foreign exchange bank, generally within 18 months from the date of export declaration under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations. South Korea does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio, so exporters may keep the full amount in a foreign currency account or convert to KRW at their business discretion. If settlement is not completed within the statutory timeline, the exporter must submit a written explanation of the delay and obtain a grace period from the responsible foreign exchange bank or obtain approval for the applicable settlement extension.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of Korea (BOK) as the central monetary authority, together with the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MOEF) as the principal foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low and not permitted as a principal treasury settlement rail. Global fintech EMIs such as Wise or Stripe are not licensed domestic banks in South Korea and cannot maintain corporate KRW settlement accounts, issue capital verification certificates, or act as the receiving bank for inbound FDI notification and reporting. Such providers may technically be used as payment collection gateways for subscription clients, but all underlying funds must settle through a licensed Korean bank. Therefore the treasury blueprint requires a Tier-1 domestic bank as the primary counterparty and treats fintech EMIs strictly as supplementary front-end billing tools, never as the legal clearing entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital for the Yuhan Hoesa must be transferred by MT103 from the investor's commercial bank to a currency account in the name of the Korean entity at the chosen Tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must clearly carry the foreign exchange purpose code for FDI capital acquisition and state 'investment in KR_YUHANGHOESA' in the beneficiary description. Prior to transfer, the investor must file an FDI notification through the foreign exchange bank, after which the bank will issue a Foreign Capital Verification Certificate when funds are received and matched to the notified transaction. Only then may the capital be moved into the operating KRW account for use in ISIC 7410 design subscription operations. No capital disbursement is legally permitted before the bank has processed the FDID notification and the MOEF has issued final acknowledgment.Outbound service fees, software licensing royalties, global partner payments, and profit remittances should be executed through SWIFT MT103 messages with bank-to-bank instructions cleared via the sending Korean bank's corresponding network. For domestic KRW payments, the appropriate rail is the Korea Financial Telecommunications and Clearings Institute (KFTC) clearing system. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not standard for UI/UX design subscriptions and should be avoided except when a foreign counterparty requires them for large enterprise design contracts; in that case, they would be handled as clauses in an export LC through the same Korean clearing bank. All outbound service payments must carry correct FX purpose codes and, when remitting dividends or trademark royalties, require corporate tax clearance and supporting board resolutions to prevent bank-side holds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

24% maximum marginal Corporate Income Tax on taxable income exceeding KRW 300 billion; 9% on the first KRW 200 million; 19% on the next KRW 19.8 billion; 21% on the next KRW 280 billion; plus 10% local income tax surcharge (aggregate top statutory burden 26.4%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding under Corporate Tax Act Art. 98(1) plus 2% local income tax surcharge (22% aggregate) on dividends remitted to a non-resident company; DTA rates are typically 5% for qualified substantial shareholdings and 10%-15% for portfolio dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The annual International Related-Party Transaction Report is required when cross-border related-party transactions exceed KRW 300 million per fiscal year; contemporaneous Master File and Local File obligations are generally mandatory when total revenue exceeds KRW 100 billion and related-party international transactions exceed KRW 50 billion; any taxpayer with controlled transactions, regardless of revenue, must still maintain an arm's length study and internal comparability evidence.

Economic substance scope: Korea does not maintain a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation; however, the substance-over-form doctrine (Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 14), CFC rules (ITAA Art. 56-79), and Korean tax treaty PE definitions require actual employees, local management, contracts, performance, bank accounts, and Core Income-Generating Activities inside Korea. A Korean Yuhan Hoesa that merely receives UI/UX subscription fees and instantly transfers them offshore is a shell for tax purposes and will be recharacterized as a sham or deemed PE of the foreign owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Filing

Statutory sanction: Framework Act on National Taxes Art. 47-3 plus Punishment of Tax Evaders Act Art. 3: 40% fraudulent underreporting penalty, daily 0.022% overdue surtax, criminal fine up to 3 times the evaded tax, and imprisonment up to 5 years, extended to 10 years for habitual evasion or where evaded tax exceeds high statutory thresholds.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, approve, or knowingly file false returns are treated as joint tax debtors; courts pierce the corporate veil where the Korean LLC is an alter ego of foreign principals; directors may be placed on the National Tax Service heavy-default blacklist, subjected to exit bans/travel bans, passport revocation, asset seizure, and personal criminal imprisonment for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ITAA Arts. 5 and 6 permit revenue uplift and expense denial; underreporting penalties up to 40% plus daily surcharges are applied; failure to submit required TP documentation is an administrative fine under ITAA Art. 11; the National Tax Tribunal will disregard the transaction as a sham when no economic substance supports the service fee.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign controlled transactions and transfer Korean assets abroad may be personally liable for corporate tax debt if the scheme renders the Korean entity insolvent; Korean tax authorities may issue property consignment orders, freeze bank accounts and vehicles, impose travel bans, and prosecute directors for fraudulent documentation and evasion.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Foreign Exchange / AML Veil-Piercing

Statutory sanction: Corporate Tax Act Art. 98(2) and the National Tax Collection Act permit immediate collection of unpaid WHT from the withholding agent; the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act imposes declaration requirements and monetary penalties for unlawful outward remittance; aggravated cross-border conduct may trigger AML/FT sanctions and criminal fines up to 100% of the unlicensed amount.

Director personal liability: Nominee and straw directors remain statutorily liable for withheld taxes; the corporate veil is pierced when the Yuhan Hoesa has no separate board, capitalization, or books, making both the foreign beneficial owner and the local director jointly liable; personal sanctions include asset attachment, travel ban/blacklisting, personal recovery of unpaid WHT, and imprisonment up to 5 years with fines up to 3 times the tax evaded.
